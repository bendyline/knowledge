# Fossil track



A reverse ichnite of the impression of *Jialingpus yuechiensis*, on display at the [Paleozoological Museum of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoological_Museum_of_China)

A **fossil [track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_track)** or **ichnite** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) "*ἴχνιον*" (*ichnion*) – a track, trace or footstep) is a fossilized [footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footprint). This is a type of [trace fossil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_fossil). A **fossil trackway** is a sequence of fossil tracks left by a single organism. Over the years, many ichnites have been found, around the world, giving important clues about the behaviour (and foot structure and stride) of the animals that made them. For instance, multiple ichnites of a single species, close together, suggest 'herd' or 'pack' behaviour of that species.

Combinations of footprints of different species provide clues about the interactions of those species. Even a set of footprints of a single animal gives important clues, as to whether it was [bipedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biped) or [quadrupedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadruped). In this way, it has been suggested that some [pterosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterosaur), when on the ground, used their forelimbs in an unexpected quadrupedal action.

Special conditions are required, in order to preserve a footprint made in soft ground (such as an [alluvial plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain) or a formative [sedimentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock) deposit). A possible scenario is a sea or lake shore that became dried out to a firm mud in hot, dry conditions, received the footprints (because it would only have been partially hardened and the animal would have been heavy) and then became silted over in a flash storm.

The first ichnite found was in 1800 in Massachusetts, US, by a farmer named Pliny Moody, who found 1-foot (31 cm) long fossilized footprints. They were thought by Harvard and Yale scholars to be from "Noah's Raven".[^cite_note-1]

A famous group of ichnites was found in a limestone quarry at [Ardley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardley,_Oxfordshire), 20 km Northeast of [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), [England](9316.md), in 1997. They were thought to have been made by *[Megalosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalosaurus)* and possibly *[Cetiosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetiosaurus)*. There are replicas of some of these footprints, set across the lawn of [Oxford University Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Museum_of_Natural_History) (OUMNH).

A creature named *[Cheirotherium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirotherium)* was, for a long time and still may be, only known from its fossilized trail. Its footprints were first found in 1834, in [Thuringia](31130.md), [Germany](11867.md), dating from the Late [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic) [Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_\(geology\)).

The largest known dinosaur footprints, belonging to [sauropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropoda) and dating from the early [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) were found to the north of [Broome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broome,_Western_Australia) on the [Dampier Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dampier_Peninsula), [Western Australia](33613.md), with some footprints measuring 1.7 m.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The 3D digital documentation of tracks has the benefit of being able to examine ichnite in detail remotely and distribute the data to colleagues and other interested personnel.[^cite_note-4]

## Fossil trackways

Many fossil trackways were made by [dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosauria), early [tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod), and other [quadrupeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadruped) and [bipeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biped) on land. Marine organisms also made many ancient trackways (such as the trails of [trilobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilobite) and [eurypterids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurypterid) like *[Hibbertopterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibbertopterus)*).

Some basic fossil trackway types:

1. footprints

2. tail drags

3. belly drag marks – (e.g., tetrapods)

   [^cite_note-5]

4. chain of trace platforms – (example: *[Yorgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorgia)*)

5. body imprint – ([Monuron trackway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monuron_trackway?action=edit\&redlink=1), insect)



Specialized marine trace trackway, [Yorgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorgia), from the [Ediacaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediacaran) of northern Russia

The majority of fossil trackways are [foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot) impressions on land, or subsurface water, but other types of creatures will leave distinctive impressions. Examples of creatures supported, or partially supported, in a [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water) environment are known. The fossil "[millipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millipede)-type" genus *[Arthropleura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropleura)* left its multi-legged/feet trackways on land.

### Hominid trackways

#### Africa

##### Tanzania

See also: [Laetoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laetoli)



Laetoli Site, February 2006

Some of the earliest trackways for human ancestors have been discovered in [Tanzania](30118.md).[^cite_note-\:1-6] The [Laetoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laetoli) trackway is famous for the [hominin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominin) footprints preserved in [volcanic ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash). After the footprints were made in powdery ash, soft rain cemented the ash layer into [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff), preserving the prints.[^cite_note-\:1-6] The hominid prints were produced by three individuals, one walking in the footprints of the other, making the original tracks difficult to discover. As the tracks lead in the same direction, they might have been produced by a group – but there is nothing else to support the common reconstruction of a nuclear family visiting the waterhole together.

##### South Africa

In [South Africa](17416221.md), two ancient trackways have been found containing footprints, one at Langebaan and one at Nahoon. Both trackways occur in calcareous eolianites or hardened sand dunes. At Nahoon, trackways of at least five species of vertebrates, including three hominid footprints, are preserved as casts.[^cite_note-7] The prints at [Langebaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langebaan) are the oldest human footprints, dated to approximately 117,000 years old.[^cite_note-8]

#### Australia

##### New South Wales

Twenty six human fossil trackways have been found in the Willandra Lakes area adjacent to Lake Garnpung, consisting of 563 human footprints from 19,000 to 20,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Willandra-9]

### Early Tetrapod

The earliest land creatures (actually land-marine coastal-riverine-marshland) left some of the first terrestrial trackways. They range from tetrapods to proto-reptilians and others.

A possible first connection of a trackway with the vertebrate that left it was published by Drs. Sebastian Voigt and David Berman and Amy Henrici in the 12 September 2007 issue of *[Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology](10659584.md)*. The paleontologists who made the connection were aided by unusually detailed trackways left in fine-grained [Lower Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian) mud of the Tambach Formation in central Germany, together with exceptionally complete fossilised skeletons in the same 290-million-year-old strata. They matched the two most common trackways with the two most common fossils, two reptile-like herbivores known as *[Diadectes absitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadectes)* (with the trackway pseudonym *Ichniotherium cottae*) and *[Orobates pabsti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orobates)* (with the trackway pseudonym of *Orobates pabsti*).[^cite_note-10]

The Permo-[Carboniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous) of [Prince Edward Island](23071.md), Canada contains trackways of tetrapods and stem-reptiles.[^cite_note-11] Macrofloral and [palynological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palynology) information help date them.

Ireland hosts late Middle [Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian) tetrapod trackways at three sites on [Valentia Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentia_Island) within the Valentia Slate Formation.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

The earliest fossil trackway of primitive tetrapods in Australia occurs in the [Genoa River Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa,_Victoria), Victoria, dating from the [Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian) 350 million years ago.[^cite_note-14]

### Dinosaur trackways



[Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) dinosaur trackway&#x20;

Dinosaurs lived on the continents before [grasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass) evolved (the "Age of the Grasses" evolved with the "Age of the Mammals"); the dinosaurs lived in the [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic), [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic), and [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) and left many trackways, both from plant-eaters and the meat-eaters, in various layers of mud and sand.

With scientific analysis, dinosaur specialists are now analyzing tracks for the ***walking-speeds***, or *sprint*-***running speeds*** for all categories of dinosaurs, even to the large plant eaters, but especially the faster 3-toed meat hunters. *Evidence of* ***herding***, as well as ***pack hunting*** are also being investigated.

#### Bolivia

- [Cal Orck'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cal_Orck'o), Cretaceous park, [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre), Bolivia

- From an investigation that began in the 1960s, it wasn't until December 2025 that paleontologists from Loma Linda University in California discovered 16,600 footprints left by theropods, the dinosaur group that includes the [Tyrannosaurus rex](30467.md). After six years of regular field visits, this finding sets the highest number of theropod footprints ever discovered in the world. The footprint sizes indicated that giant creatures roughly 10 meters (33 feet) tall, while also finding tiny theropod footprints the size of a chicken, 32 centimeters (1 foot) tall at the hip. Interestingly enough, the scientist who found it in 1960 wanted to prove to kids that those prints weren't made by a monster, but by a dinosaur.

#### Africa

##### Namibia



Dinosaur trace fossil of Otjihaenamparero

In north-central Namibia there is a dinosaur trackway in sandstone on what is now the private farm [Otjihaenamparero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otjihaenamparero). Larger footprints are of a [ceratosauria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceratosauria) and smaller ones of [syntarsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coelophysis). The prints are believed to be around 190 million years old.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

##### Zimbabwe

In the Lower Zimbabwe Rift Valley there is a trackway in 140 Ma rose-coloured [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) of Chewore Area. The small footprint size, with both [manus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manus_\(zoology\)) and [pes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pes_\(zoology\)), implies that it is a trackway of a juvenile, a probable [carnosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnosaur).[^cite_note-18]

#### North America



Probable *Dilophosaurus* footprint from Red Fleet State Park, northeastern Utah

The western regions of North America, especially the western border of the [Western Interior Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Interior_Seaway), are common for dinosaur trackways. [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) has dinosaur trackways from the Late Cretaceous, 65 ma.[^cite_note-19] (A model example of this 3-toed Wyoming trackway was made for presentation.)[^cite_note-20]



Theropod and sauropod tracks under water in the Paluxy River

In the [United States](3434750.md), [dinosaur](8311.md) footprints and trackways are found in the [Glen Rose Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Rose_Formation), the most famous of these being the [Paluxy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paluxy_River) site in [Dinosaur Valley State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Valley_State_Park). These were the first [sauropoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropoda) footprints scientifically documented, and were designated a US [National Natural Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Natural_Landmark) in 1969. Some are as large as about 3 feet across. The prints are thought to have been preserved originally in a [tidal flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_flat) or a [lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon).[^cite_note-Lockley-21] There are tracks from two types of dinosaur. The first type of tracks are from a [sauropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropoda) and were made by an animal of 30 to 50 feet in length, perhaps a [brachiosaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosaurid) such as *[Pleurocoelus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleurocoelus)*,[^cite_note-Lockley-21] and the second tracks by a [theropoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theropoda), an animal of 20 to 30 feet in length, perhaps an *[Acrocanthosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrocanthosaurus)*. A variety of scenarios was proposed to explain the tracks, but most likely represent twelve sauropods "probably as a herd, followed somewhat later by three theropods that may or may not have been stalking – but that certainly were not attacking."[^cite_note-Lockley-21]

#### Asia

##### China

The Gansu dinosaur trackway located in the Liujiazia National Dinosaur Geopark in Yanguoxia, China contains hundreds of tracks including 245 dinosaur, 350 theropod, 364 sauropod and 628 ornithopod tracks among others.[^cite_note-Gansu-23]

##### Thailand

The [Phu Pha Man National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phu_Pha_Man_National_Park) in [Thailand](30128.md) contains one of the oldest dinosaur tracks to have been discovered in [Asia](689.md).[^cite_note-24] Discovered in January 2024, paleontologists from the [Department of Mineral Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mineral_Resources_\(Thailand\)) have dated the tracks to around 225–220 million years old (the late Triassic period).[^cite_note-25] The track contains traces of a variety of dinosaurs including theropods, sauropods, and archosaurs.[^cite_note-26]

#### Australia

The [Lark Quarry Trackway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lark_Quarry_Dinosaur_Trackways) in [Queensland](59051.md) contains three-toed tracks made by a herd of [ornithopod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithopoda) dinosaurs crossing a river. It was once believed they respresented a large predator chasing a mixed flock of small ornithopods and theropods, but this was contested in 2011.[^cite_note-\:0-27]

#### Europe

##### Portugal

- [Lagosteiros Natural Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagosteiros_Natural_Monument), in [Sesimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesimbra), with [theropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theropod) and [ornithopod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithopod) tracks from the [Early Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Cretaceous).

- [Aire Range Dinosaur Tracks Natural Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aire_Range_Dinosaur_Tracks_Natural_Monument) near [Torres Novas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_Novas). In 1994 about 20 [sauropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropod) trackways from the [Middle Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Jurassic) were discovered in the *Pedreira do Galinha* quarry.

##### England

In a [Bathonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathonian) [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) quarry site at [Ardley, Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardley,_Oxfordshire) more than 40 sets of footprints, with some trackways reaching up to 180 metres in length, were discovered in 1997. These prints are largely only preserved in replicas and photographic records due to quarrying and subsequent restoration. Theropod tracks have been tentatively assigned to the [ichnogenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichnogenus) [Megalosauripus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalosauripus), possibly from [Megalosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalosaurus#Later_finds_of_Megalosaurus_bucklandii). The sauropod tracks could be grouped into *wide-gauge* trackways, associated with early [titanosauria](1139347.md) or [brachiosauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosauridae), and *narrow-gauge* ones, possibly from [Cetiosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetiosaurus#Later_species) or a basal [diplodocoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplodocoid).[^cite_note-28] In 2024 at nearby [Dewars Farm Quarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewars_Farm_Quarry),[^cite_note-29] five new extensive [Middle Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Jurassic) trackways dating back about 166 Ma were discovered, with evidence of more in the neighbourhood. The longest continuous trackway measured more than 150 metres. Four of the trackways were made by large [sauropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropods), most likely again *Cetiosaurus*. The fifth one was identified as made by the carnivorous [theropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theropod) *Megalosaurus*, based on its distinctive three-toed feet with claws. One area of the site shows the carnivore and herbivore tracks crossing over, suggesting the predator followed up on the steps of the sauropod.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

A newly described dinosaur track assemblage from the Middle Jurassic **Burniston Footprint Bed in Yorkshire** provides rare evidence of different dinosaur species occupying the same environment at roughly the same time. Researchers Romano, Lomax and Taylor identified a large herbivorous ornithopod footprint alongside numerous smaller theropod tracks, some of which appear to overlap the larger print. The findings suggest they may have interacted and offer valuable insights into dinosaur behaviour, sociality and community structure in Jurassic Britain.[^cite_note-32]

### Mammal trackways

Mammal trackways are among the least common trackways. Mammals were not often in mud, or riverine environments; they were more often in forestlands or grasslands. Thus the earlier tetrapods or proto-tetrapods would yield the most fossil trackways. The [Walchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walchia) forest of [Brule, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brule,_Nova_Scotia) has an example of an [in situ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_situ) Walchia forest, and tetrapod trackways that extended over some period of time through the forest area.

#### United States

A 1.5km-long Late Pleistocene Age trackway of human (child and adult) fossilized footprints, as well as [mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammoth) and [giant ground sloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_ground_sloth) tracks have been found at [White Sands National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_footprints) near [Alamogordo, New Mexico](125944.md).[^cite_note-Las_Cruces_Sun_News-33][^cite_note-QSR-34]

#### Australia

A recent [marsupial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsupial) trackway site at [Colac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colac,_Victoria) in the [Australian](4689264.md) state of [Victoria](4689460.md) contains marsupial trackways as well as kangaroo and wallaby tracks.[^cite_note-35]

### Pterosaur trackways

#### France

[Pterosaur Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterosaur_Beach) was, at the end of the [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) era, a [mudflat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudflat), flooded at high tide, on a marine lagoon in a [gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf) that opened on the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) between [Bordeaux](4097.md) and the island of [Oléron](85564.md). On it, animals foraged for food.[^cite_note-36] The site has hundreds of fossilized trackways.[^cite_note-37]
