**Muslims** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): المسلمون, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Muslimūn*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'submitters \[to [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Islam)]')[^cite_note-14] are people who are adherents of [Islam](6037917.md), a [monotheistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism) religion belonging to the [Abrahamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions) tradition. They consider the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), the foundational [religious text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_text) of Islam, to be the verbatim word of the [God of Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Abrahamic_religions) (or *[Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah)*) as it was revealed to [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad), the last [Islamic prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_prophet).[^cite_note-15] Alongside the Quran, Muslims also believe in [finality of prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_the_Prophets) as well as previous [revelations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_holy_books), such as the *[Tawrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawrat)* ([Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah)), the *[Zabur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabur)* ([Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms)), and the [*Injil*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injeel) ([Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel)). These earlier revelations are associated with [Judaism](15624.md) and [Christianity](5211.md), which are regarded by Muslims as earlier versions of Islam.[^cite_note-16] The majority of Muslims also follow the teachings, traits and practices attributed to Muhammad (*[sunnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah)*) as recorded in traditional accounts ([hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith)).[^cite_note-17]

With an estimated population of 2 billion followers, Muslims comprise around 26% of total global population.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-pewresearch.orgReligion-20] In descending order, the percentage of people who identify as Muslims on each continental landmass stands at:[^cite_note-comps.world-21] 45% of [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Africa), 25% of [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Asia) and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Oceania) collectively,[^cite_note-pewaspa-22] 6% of [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Europe),[^cite_note-peweur-23] and 1% of the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Americas).[^cite_note-pewame-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Additionally, in subdivided geographical regions, the figure stands at: 91% of the [Middle East–North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MENA#Religion),[^cite_note-pewmena-28][^cite_note-pewmuslim22-29][^cite_note-30] 90% of [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Central_Asia),[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-central_asia_ii-32][^cite_note-central_asia_ii-32] 65% of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus#Demographics),[^cite_note-cia.gov-33][^cite_note-eurasianet.org-34][^cite_note-Armenia_information-35][^cite_note-News.am-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] 42% of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Southeast_Asia),[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Yusuf-40] 32% of [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_South_Asia),[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] and 42% of [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md#Religion).[^cite_note-pewssa-43][^cite_note-pewmuslim32-44]

While there are several [Islamic schools and branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_schools_and_branches), as well as [non-denominational Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslims), the two largest denominations are [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) (87–90% of all Muslims)[^cite_note-Sunni-eb-45] and [Shia Islam](26961.md) (10–13% of all Muslims).[^cite_note-Shia-6][^cite_note-\:0-46][^cite_note-Pew_2009-7] By sheer numbers, South Asia accounts for the largest portion (31%) of the global Muslim population.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] By country, [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Indonesia) is the largest in the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world), holding around 12% of all Muslims worldwide;[^cite_note-Islam_by_country-49][^cite_note-pew2015countries-50] with [Pakistan](23235.md) having the second largest number of Muslims in the world after Indonesia.[^cite_note-51] Outside the Muslim-majority countries, [India](14533.md) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_China) are home to the largest (11%) and second-largest (2%) Muslim populations, respectively.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-pewmuslim122-53][^cite_note-54] Due to high [Muslim population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_population_growth), Islam is the [fastest-growing religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_of_religion) in the world.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-USNewsLippman2-57] Muslims have [experienced persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Muslims) of varying severity, especially in China, India, some parts of Africa, and Southeast Asia.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

## Etymology

See also: [Islam § Etymology](6037917.md#Etymology)

The word *muslim*[^cite_note-62] or *moslem*[^cite_note-64] is the [active participle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_participle) of the same verb of which *islām* is a [verbal noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbal_noun), based on the [triliteral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triliteral) *[Š-L-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-L-M)* "to be whole, intact".[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] A female adherent is a ***muslima*** (مسلمة; also [transliterated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) as *muslimah*).[^cite_note-67] The plural form in Arabic is *muslimūn* (مسلمون) or *muslimīn* (مسلمين), and its feminine equivalent is *muslimāt* (مسلمات).

### In English

The ordinary word in English is "Muslim". For most of the 20th century, the preferred spelling in English was "**Moslem**", but this has now fallen into disuse. That spelling and its pronunciation were opposed by many Muslims in English-speaking countries because the "s" was often pronounced with a z sound. This made the word more closely match the Arabic triliteral *[ẓ-l-m](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Appendix\:Arabic%20roots/%D8%B8%20%D9%84%20%D9%85)* (ظ-ل-م), which has negative meanings and includes the Arabic word for "the oppressor".[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-vanishingmoslems-69] In the United States, the [Associated Press](18935732.md) (AP) instructed news outlets to switch to the spelling "Muslim" in 1991, making it the most common spelling thereafter.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] The last major newspaper in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) to use the spelling "Moslem" was the *[Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mail)*, which switched to "Muslim" in 2004.[^cite_note-vanishingmoslems-69]

The word ***Mosalman***, ***Mussulman***, or ***Musulman*** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): مسلمان, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *mosalmân*, alternatively *musalmān*) is a common equivalent for *Muslim* used in [Central](6742.md) and [South Asia](21566765.md). In English it was sometimes spelled Mussulman and has become [archaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaism) in usage; however, cognates of this word remain the standard term for "Muslim" in various other European languages. Until at least the mid-1960s, many English-language writers used the term *[**Mohammedans**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammedan)* or ***Mahometans***.[^cite_note-72] Although such terms were not necessarily intended to be [pejorative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pejorative), Muslims argue that the terms are offensive because they allegedly imply that Muslims worship [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) rather than [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Islam).[^cite_note-73] Other obsolete terms include *Muslimite*[^cite_note-74] and *Muslimist*.[^cite_note-75] In medieval Europe, Muslims were commonly called [Saracens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracens).

### In Islam

The Muslim philologist [Ibn al-Anbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_al-Anbari) said:

> a Muslim is a person who has dedicated his worship exclusively to God, for just as we say in Arabic that something is ‘*salima*’ to a person, meaning that it became solely his own, so in the same way ‘*Islām*’ means making one's religion and faith God's alone.[^cite_note-76]

In several places in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), the word *muslim* conveys a universal meaning, beyond the description of the followers of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad), for example:[^cite_note-77]

> "Abraham was neither a Jew, nor a Christian, but he was a true Muslim \[مُسۡلِمࣰا], and he was not a polytheist." – Quran 3\:67 [^cite_note-78]
>
> "Then when Jesus perceived their disbelief he said, 'Who will be my helpers of God.' The disciples said 'We will be the helpers of God; we believe in God and bear witness that we are Muslims \[مُسۡلِمُونَ].'" – Quran 3\:52 [^cite_note-79]

[Quranic studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quranic_studies) scholar [Mohsen Goudarzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohsen_Goudarzi) has argued that in the Quran the word *[dīn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%ABn)* means "[worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worship)", the *[islām](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isl%C4%81m)* means "[monotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism)" and the *muslim* means "monotheist".[^cite_note-80]

Until the 8th century, the term *muslim* was more inclusive, including anyone who was considered to be submitting to God—e.g. Christians and Jews—and the term *mu'min* was instead used to refer to believers in Islam as a distinct religion.[^cite_note-81]

To become a Muslim and to convert to Islam, it is essential to say the *[Shahada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahada)* in front of Muslim witnesses,[^cite_note-82] one of the [Five Pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Pillars_of_Islam), a [declaration of faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_faith) and trust that professes that there is [only one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawhid) [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Islam) *([Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah))* and that [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) is God's messenger.[^cite_note-83] It is a set statement normally recited in Arabic: *ašhadu ʾan-lā ʾilāha ʾillā-llāhu wa ʾašhadu ʾanna muħammadan rasūlu-llāh* (أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله وأشهد أن محمداً رسول الله) "I testify that there is no god \[worthy of worship] except Allah, and Muhammad is the messenger of Allah."[^cite_note-84]

### In Sunni Islam

In [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), the shahada has two parts: *la ilaha illa'llah* (there is no god but [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah)), and *Muhammadun rasul Allah* ([Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) is the messenger of God),[^cite_note-LS-85] which are sometimes referred to as the first *shahada* and the second *shahada*.[^cite_note-Cornell9-86] The first statement of the shahada is also known as the *[tahlīl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahlil)*.[^cite_note-87]

### In Shia Islam

In [Shia Islam](26961.md), the shahada also has a third part, a phrase concerning [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali), the first [Shia Imam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamah_\(Shia_doctrine\)) and the fourth [Rashid caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun) of [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam): وعليٌّ وليُّ الله (*wa ʿalīyyun walīyyu-llāh*), which translates to "Ali is the *[wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali)* of God".[^cite_note-88]

### In Quranism

In [Quranist Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quranism), the shahada is the testimony that there is no god but [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah) (*la ilaha illa'llah*). Quranists believe adding Muhammad or other messengers in the declaration of faith contradicts the Quran and leads to idolization alongside God.[^cite_note-89]

### Five Pillars of Islam

Main article: [Five Pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Pillars_of_Islam)

The religious practices of Muslims are enumerated in the [Five Pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Pillars_of_Islam): the declaration of faith (*[shahadah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahadah)*), daily prayers (*[salah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salat)*), almsgiving (*[zakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat)*), fasting during the month of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) (*[sawm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawm)*), and the pilgrimage to [Makkah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) (*[hajj](23487999.md)*) at least once in a lifetime.[^cite_note-WSU-90][^cite_note-CIA-91]

## In Islamic theology

Main article: [Prophets and messengers in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophets_and_messengers_in_Islam)

The majority of [theological traditions of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schools_of_Islamic_theology) accept that works do not determine if someone is a Muslim or not. God alone would know about the belief of a person. Fellow Muslims can only accept the personal declaration of faith. Only the [Khawārij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharijites) developed an understanding of Muslim identity based mainly on the adherence to liturgical and legal norms.[^cite_note-92]

When asked about one's beliefs, it is recommended for one to say the *[Istit̲h̲nāʾ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inshallah)*, for example, "*in-sha'allah* I am Muslim a believer" (so God will, I am Muslim), since only God knows the future of a person.[^cite_note-93] Among [Ash'arites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash'arism), it is also seen as a sign of humility and the individual's longing to improve, because the creature has no assurance of their own state (of belief) until the end of life.[^cite_note-94]

The [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an) describes many prophets and messengers within [Judaism](15624.md) and [Christianity](5211.md), and their respective followers, as Muslim. Some of those that were mentioned are: [Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_\(Bible\)), [Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah), [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_in_Islam), [Ishmael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishmael_in_Islam), [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob), [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_view_of_Moses), and [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_view_of_Jesus) and his [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles) are all considered to be Muslims in the Quran.[^cite_note-95] The Quran states that these men were Muslims because they submitted to God, preached His message and upheld His values, which included praying, charity, [fasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasting) and pilgrimage. Thus, in Surah 3\:52 of the Qur'an, [Jesus' disciples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disciples_of_Jesus_in_Islam) tell him: "We believe in God; and you be our witness that we are Muslims (*wa-shahad be anna muslimūn*)." In Islamic belief, before the Quran, God had given the *[Tawrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawrat)* ([Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah)) to the prophets and messengers among the [Children of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELang201598-96] the *[Zabur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabur)* ([Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms)) to [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David) and the *[Injil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injil)* ([Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel)) to Jesus, who are all considered important [Muslim prophets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophets_of_Islam).[^cite_note-Books_of_Islam-97]

## Demographics

Main article: [Islam § Demographics](6037917.md#Demographics)

See also: [Islam by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_by_country)



World Muslim population by percentage (2012)

According to Pew estimates, as of 2020, Muslims made up about 25.6% of the global population, or roughly 2 billion people. The growth is mainly due to Muslims having a younger average age and higher birth rates—two key drivers of natural population increase.[^cite_note-98] The most populous Muslim-majority country is Indonesia, home to 12.7% of the world's Muslims,[^cite_note-Distrib-99] followed by Pakistan (11.0%), Bangladesh (9.2%), Nigeria (5.3%) and Egypt (4.9%).[^cite_note-Islam_by_country-49] About 20% of the world's Muslims live in the Middle East and North Africa. Non-majority India contains 10.9% of the world's Muslims.[^cite_note-Distrib-99][^cite_note-100] [Arab Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims) form the [largest ethnic group among Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_contemporary_ethnic_groups) in the world,[^cite_note-101] followed by [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslims),[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] and [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Muslims).[^cite_note-104]

Over 87–90% of Muslims are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni).[^cite_note-Sunni-4][^cite_note-Sunni_Islam-5] The second largest sect, [Shia](26961.md), make up 10–13%,[^cite_note-Shia-6][^cite_note-\:0-46][^cite_note-Pew_2009-7] whereas other movements such as the [Ahmadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya), [Quranism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quranism), [Ibadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadism), collectively count for 1% per cent.[^cite_note-ahmadi-8][^cite_note-105] While the majority of the population in the [Middle East](19323.md) identify as either Sunni or Shia, a significant number of Muslims identify as [non-denominational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslims).[^cite_note-106]

With about 2 billion followers (2025), over a quarter of [earth's population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population),[^cite_note-107] Islam is the [second-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups) and the [fastest-growing religion in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_of_religion),[^cite_note-108] primarily due to the young age and high [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate) of Muslims,[^cite_note-pew-109] with Muslims having a rate of 3.1 compared to the world average of 2.5. According to the same study, [religious switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_conversion) has no impact on the Muslim population, since the number of people who [embrace Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convert_to_Islam) and those who [leave Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Islam) are roughly equal.[^cite_note-Global_Islam-110][^cite_note-pew-109] According to a 2020 Pew study, about 1% of adults raised Muslim leave the faith, while a similar share convert to Islam, resulting in low levels of religious switching both into and out of Islam.[^cite_note-111]

As of 2010, 49 countries in the world had Muslim majorities, in which Muslims comprised more than 50% of the population.[^cite_note-Global_Islam-110] In 2010, 74.1% of the world's Muslim population lived in countries where Muslims are in the majority, while 25.9% of the world's Muslim population lived in countries where Muslims are in the minority.[^cite_note-Global_Islam-110] A Pew Center study in 2010 found that 3% of the world's Muslim population lives in non-Muslim-majority [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries).[^cite_note-Global_Islam-110] [India's Muslim population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India) is the world's largest Muslim-minority population in the world (11% of the world's Muslim population).[^cite_note-Global_Islam-110] Followed by Ethiopia (28 million), China (22 million), Russia (16 million) and Tanzania (13 million).[^cite_note-pew-109] Sizeable minorities are also found in [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Americas) (5.2 million or 0.6%), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Australia) (714,000 or 1.9%) and parts of [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Europe) (44 million or 6%).[^cite_note-Global_Islam-110] According to a 2020 Pew study, 79% of the world's Muslim population live in Muslim-majority countries, while 21% reside in countries where Muslims are a minority.[^cite_note-112]

A Pew Center study in 2016 found that Muslims have the highest number of adherents under the age of 15 (34% of the total Muslim population) of any major religion, while only 7% are aged 60+ (the smallest percentage of any major religion). According to the same study, Muslims have the highest [fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate) (3.1) of any major religious group.[^cite_note-Pew2016-113] The study also found that Muslims (tied with [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus)) have the lowest average levels of [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) with an average of 5.6 years of schooling, though both groups have made the largest gains in educational attainment in recent decades among major religions.[^cite_note-Pew2016-113] About 36% of all Muslims have no formal schooling,[^cite_note-Pew2016-113] and Muslims have the lowest average levels of [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education) of any major religious group, with only 8% having [graduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree) and [post-graduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-graduate) degrees.[^cite_note-Pew2016-113]

## Culture

Main article: [Islamic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_culture)

Muslim culture or Islamic culture are terms used to describe the cultural practices common to Muslims and historically Islamic people. The early forms of Muslim culture, from the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate) to early [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad) period, were predominantly [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians), and [Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant). With the rapid expansion of the Arab [Islamic empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate), Muslim culture has influenced and assimilated much from the [Indonesian](14579.md), [Pakistani](28171281.md) ([Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Muslims), [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns), [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_Muslims), [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis)), [Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga-Jamuni_tehzeeb), [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis), [Nigerian](21383.md), [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_culture), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_culture), [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_people), [Caucasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_people), [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people), [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people), and [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) cultures.
