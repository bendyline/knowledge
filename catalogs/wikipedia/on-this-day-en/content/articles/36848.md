The **Economic Community of West African States** (**ECOWAS**; also known as **CEDEAO** in French and Portuguese) is a regional [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_union) and [economic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_union) of twelve countries of [West Africa](67393.md). Collectively, the present and former members comprise an area of 5,114,162 km<sup>2</sup> (1,974,589 sq mi) and have an estimated population of over 424.34 million.[^cite_note-5]

Considered one of the pillar [regional blocs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Economic_Communities) of the continent-wide [African Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Economic_Community) (AEC), the stated goal of ECOWAS is to achieve "collective [self-sufficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-sustainability)" for its member states by creating a single large trade bloc by building a full economic and trading union. Additionally, ECOWAS aims to raise [living standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) and promote [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development).[^cite_note-6] The union was established on 28 May 1975, with the signing of the [Treaty of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lagos),[^cite_note-7] with its stated mission to promote economic integration across the region. A revised version of the [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) was agreed and signed on 24 July 1993 in [Cotonou](56647.md), the largest city in Benin.[^cite_note-8]

ECOWAS's published principles include equality and inter-dependence of member states, solidarity, self-reliance, cooperation and harmonization of policies, nonaggression, promotion of [human rights](13831.md), economic and [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice), and democratic governance.[^cite_note-9]

Notably among ECOWAS's protocols and plans are the ECOWAS Free Movement of Persons, Residences and Establishment Protocol and the Ecotour Action Plan 2019–2029. The *Free Movement of Persons Protocol* permits citizens the right to enter and reside in any member state's territory,[^cite_note-\:4-10] and the *Ecotour Action Plan* aims to develop and integrate the tourist industry of each member state.[^cite_note-\:6-11]

ECOWAS also serves as a [peacekeeping](84617.md) force in the region, with member states occasionally sending joint [military forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_forces) to intervene in the bloc's member countries at times of political instability and unrest.[^cite_note-deployment_to_liberia_2003_08_06_janes_defense_weekly-12][^cite_note-yahoomilint-13]

In 2024, the [military governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_Belt) of [Niger](21373.md), [Burkina Faso](3470.md), and [Mali](19127.md) jointly announced their withdrawal from the bloc, after having been suspended following respective military takeovers in these countries.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The withdrawal took effect on 29 January 2025.[^cite_note-16] The three later went on to form the [Alliance of Sahel States](74840743.md), with the end goal of establishing a federation.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Member states

ECOWAS currently has 12 member states: five [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language)-speaking, five [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)-speaking, and two [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language)-speaking. All current members joined the community as founding members in May 1975, except [Cape Verde](18962637.md) which joined in 1977.[^cite_note-HDM-19][^cite_note-Odeyemi_2020_97–123-20]

[Morocco](19291.md) officially requested to join ECOWAS in February 2017.[^cite_note-21] The application was endorsed in principle at the Summit of Heads of State in June 2017.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Odeyemi_2020_97–123-20] However, Morocco's bid for membership was stalled as West African economic actors feared goods imported through Morocco's free trade agreements would flood the market of states within ECOWAS.[^cite_note-23]

### States that have withdrawn or been suspended

Arabic-speaking [Mauritania](18778516.md) was one of the founding members of ECOWAS in 1975 and decided to withdraw in December 2000.[^cite_note-HDM-19] Mauritania signed a new associate-membership agreement in August 2017.[^cite_note-28]

[Mali](19127.md) was suspended from ECOWAS on 30 May 2021, following its [second military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) within nine months.[^cite_note-29] [Guinea](12176.md) was also suspended on 8 September 2021, shortly after a [military coup](68651369.md) took place in the country.[^cite_note-leaders_due_in_guinea_2021_09_09_reuters-30][^cite_note-leaders_suspend_guinea_2021_09_08_bbc-31] Sanctions were placed on both countries on 16 September.[^cite_note-32] On 10 January 2022, Mali announced its decision to close its borders and recalled several ambassadors with ECOWAS in response to sanctions imposed for deferring elections for four years.[^cite_note-33] On 28 January 2022, [Burkina Faso](3470.md) was suspended from ECOWAS following a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2022_Burkina_Faso_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-34] [Niger](21373.md) was suspended from ECOWAS after the [2023 coup d'état](74454838.md) and threatened with military intervention if President [Mohamed Bazoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Bazoum) was not restored to office,[^cite_note-The_Point-35][^cite_note-36] causing the [Nigerien crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Nigerien_crisis). Additionally, ECOWAS closed all land and air borders between other member states and Niger and instituted a no-fly zone on all commercial flights to and from Niger. The suspension removed all commercial and financial transactions and froze Niger's assets in ECOWAS central banks.[^cite_note-The_Point-35] On 16 September 2023, Niger, Mali, and Burkina Faso formed a military alliance, the [Alliance of Sahel States](74840743.md) (AES), following ECOWAS's threat to intervene to restore civilian rule in Niger.[^cite_note-37] On 21 November 2023, Niger's military government asked the ECOWAS regional court to order the lifting of sanctions imposed on the country.[^cite_note-\:7-38] Until the coup, aid from countries like the United States and international organizations like ECOWAS accounted for almost half of Niger's annual budget. Following Niger's ECOWAS suspension, Niger's neighbours closed their borders to the country and 70 percent of its electricity, coming from Nigeria, was cut off.[^cite_note-\:7-38] While these sanctions and consequences that have followed have affected the individuals and economy of Niger, the government is not backing down. As a result of the suspension, children have not been able to attend school due to lack of supplies, and businesses are shutting down due to rising costs. Further, the ECOWAS lawyer pointed out that the Nigerien government is not recognized by ECOWAS and therefore does not have the power to ask the regional court for a removal of these sanctions.[^cite_note-\:7-38]

On 28 January 2024, [Niger](21373.md), [Mali](19127.md), and [Burkina Faso](3470.md) announced via a joint statement that they were withdrawing from ECOWAS "without delay".[^cite_note-39] The three nations, all of which are currently ruled by military juntas, accused ECOWAS of implementing "inhumane" sanctions in order to reverse the coups in each nation.[^cite_note-40] Under the ECOWAS protocol, immediate withdrawal is not possible, and the three member states could remain in the bloc for up to a year. ECOWAS said in a statement that "Burkina Faso, Niger and Mali remain important members of the Community and the Authority remains committed to finding a negotiated solution to the political impasse."[^cite_note-41]

On 24 February 2024, ECOWAS announced that it was lifting some sanctions against Niger, Mali and Burkina Faso. For Niger this included the border closures, the freezing of central bank and state assets, the suspension of commercial transactions, and the no-fly-zone for commercial flights to and from Niger. However, the political sanctions and targeted sanctions would remain in force. The communiqué said this was done for humanitarian reasons, but it was seen as a gesture of appeasement to dissuade the three junta-led states from withdrawing from the bloc. ECOWAS also lifted sanctions on Guinea and Guinea-Bissau.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

In December 2024, ECOWAS heads of state of the member countries met to finalize the decision regarding the withdrawal of Mali, Niger and Burkina Faso, united under the AES.[^cite_note-45] President of Senegal, Basirou Diomaye Faye, stated on 8 December 2024 that he was continuing to discuss with the three countries remaining in ECOWAS, while maintaining the Alliance of Sahel States, which he recognized as a security response in the Sahel region.[^cite_note-46] On 12 December 2024, ECOWAS President Bola Tinubu confirmed the political will of ECOWAS leaders to reintegrate the three countries from the Alliance of Sahel States.[^cite_note-47] On 15 December 2024, the Conference of Heads of State of ECOWAS adopted an exit transition period for Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali, which begins on 29 January 2025 and ends on 29 July 2025. During this transition period, ECOWAS has indicated that any exit would be reversible.[^cite_note-48] The AES rejected the proposal. Celebrations were held in the three countries to mark the formal exit on 29 January.[^cite_note-49] ECOWAS noted the withdrawal, while calling for the continuance of existing arrangements for the free movement of people and goods, including requesting its own members still accept documents from the departing countries.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

The Foreign Ministers of the Alliance of Sahel States met on 26 January 2025, in Ouagadougou "in anticipation of future talks with ECOWAS", The Ministers reached a consensus on the overall approach to future negotiations with ECOWAS, in the best interest of the Sahelian populations. From 29 January 2025, begins the beginning of a six-month "transition period" after the official separation with the three Sahelian countries. The three Sahelian countries rejected any possibility of reversing their decision.[^cite_note-52]

In June 2025, the heads of state of ECOWAS met to finalize the formalities for the countries' exit from the alliance of Sahel states.[^cite_note-53]

On 29 November 2025 [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md) was suspended by ECOWAS following a [coup d'état](81695397.md).[^cite_note-54] On 28 January 2026, [Guinea](12176.md) had its suspension lifted following the [2025 Guinean presidential election](79968772.md).[^cite_note-55]

## History

ECOWAS was formed initially from the region's former French, British and Portuguese colonies, and independent Liberia, following post-colonial independence throughout the region (particularly in the 1960s and 1970s). At independence, many African states were challenged in increasing economic development.[^cite_note-\:3-56] Because these states could not address problems individually, there was a need for a regional approach and thus ECOWAS was founded.[^cite_note-\:3-56] ECOWAS was formed to provide regional economic cooperation, but has since evolved to include political and military cooperation, as well.[^cite_note-57]

The union was established on 28 May 1975, with the signing of the [Treaty of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lagos), with its stated mission to promote economic integration across the region. A revised version of the treaty was agreed and signed on 24 July 1993 in Cotonou.[^cite_note-Odeyemi_2020_97–123-20] Considered one of the pillar [regional blocs](307255.md) of the continent-wide [African Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Economic_Community) (AEC), the stated goal of ECOWAS is to achieve "collective [self-sufficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-sustainability)" for its member states by creating a single large trade bloc by building a full economic and trading union.[^cite_note-58]

ECOWAS also serves as a [peacekeeping](84617.md) force in the region, with member states occasionally sending joint military forces to intervene in the bloc's member countries at times of political instability and unrest.[^cite_note-\:3-56] ECOWAS facilitates peacekeeping through systematic collaboration with civil society, cooperation with development policies, and other activities with the goal to meet sub-regional security challenges.[^cite_note-\:3-56] It has played an important role in monitoring transitional election in West Africa, and these mediation efforts have even been recognized within and outside the continent of Africa.[^cite_note-\:3-56] In recent years these included interventions in [Ivory Coast in 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ivorian_Civil_War), [Liberia in 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Liberian_Civil_War), [Guinea-Bissau in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Guinea-Bissau_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), [Mali in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mali_conflict), [The Gambia in 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECOWAS_military_intervention_in_the_Gambia),[^cite_note-deployment_to_liberia_2003_08_06_janes_defense_weekly-12][^cite_note-yahoomilint-13][Guinea-Bissau in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Guinea-Bissau_coup_attempt), and [Benin in 2025](81783113.md).[^cite_note-59] Since its creation, ECOWAS has sent peacekeeping forces at least seven times.[^cite_note-60]

In 2011, ECOWAS adopted its development blueprint for the next decade, *Vision 2020*, and, to accompany it, a Policy on Science and Technology ([ECOPOST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECOWAS_Policy_on_Science_and_Technology_\(ECOPOST\))).[^cite_note-61] However, it has had trouble achieving the goals outlined in the policy.[^cite_note-62]

Covering a region known as a "[coup belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_belt)", ECOWAS, since the 1990s, has attempted to defend the region's shift towards democracy against authoritarian attacks. According to the BBC, since 1990, 78% of the 27 coups in sub-Saharan Africa have taken place in former French colonies. This has led some to question whether French influence in Africa has a destabilising effect.[^cite_note-63] The transition governments in Mali and Burkina Faso cancelled military agreements that allow for French troops to operate on their territory, and in the case of Mali, removed French as an official language.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] However, the group has been cited for mild and ineffective responses in the early 2020s, when three member countries experienced military [coups d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coups_d'%C3%A9tat) – two in Mali, one in Guinea, and two in Burkina Faso.[^cite_note-leaders_due_in_guinea_2021_09_09_reuters-30][^cite_note-leaders_suspend_guinea_2021_09_08_bbc-31][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] When a fourth member, Niger, experienced a [coup d'état in July 2023](74454838.md), ECOWAS was vocal in its condemnation and raised [the possibility of military action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerien_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%932024\)) if the deposed president was not reinstated by 7 August 2023.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Due to the Nigerien military's refusal to restore civilian rule, ECOWAS activated its standby force composed of all other members except for Mali, Burkina Faso, Guinea and Cape Verde.

On 6 July 2024, the military leaders of Niger, Mali, and Burkina Faso signed a new pact to form a confederation, a political union of [sovereign states](23604120.md). The confederation's stated goal is to provide mutual defense, pool resources to build energy and communications infrastructure, establish a [common market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_market), implement a [monetary union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_union) under proposed currency *the Sahel*, allow [free movement of persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_movement_of_persons), enable [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation), and invest in agriculture, mines and energy sectors, with the end goal of [federalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) into a single [sovereign state](23604120.md). The move is seen as a strong move away from ECOWAS, which has been pressing for a return to civilian rule. On 29 January 2025, Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger formally withdrew from ECOWAS after providing the required 1-year notice.[^cite_note-Odeyemi-72]

Within 18 hours of [a thwarted coup in Benin in 2025](81783113.md), a joint ECOWAS deployment of Nigerian, Sierra Leonean, Ivorian, and Ghanaian soldiers were sent to aid the Beninese government.[^cite_note-73]

## Structure

### Overall

ECOWAS consists of two operating institutions to implement policies: the ECOWAS Commission and the ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development (EBID) – formerly known as the Fund for Cooperation, until it was renamed in 2001.[^cite_note-74]

In addition, ECOWAS includes the following institutions: ECOWAS Commission, Community Court of Justice,[^cite_note-75] Community Parliament,[^cite_note-auto-76] ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development (EBID),[^cite_note-auto-76] West African Health Organisation (WAHO),[^cite_note-77] and the Inter-Governmental Action Group against Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing in West Africa (GIABA).[^cite_note-78]

ECOWAS includes two sub-regional blocks:

- The [West African Economic and Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Economic_and_Monetary_Union) (also known by its French-language acronym UEMOA) is an organisation of eight, mainly French-speaking, states within ECOWAS which share a [customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_union) and [currency union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_union).

  [^cite_note-\:0-79]

  &#x20;Established in 1994 and intended to counterbalance the dominance of English-speaking economies in the bloc (such as Nigeria and Ghana), members of UEMOA are mostly former territories of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa). The currency they all use is the [CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc), which is [pegged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_exchange-rate_system) to the [euro](9472.md).

  [^cite_note-\:0-79]

- The [West African Monetary Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Monetary_Zone) (WAMZ), established in 2000, comprises six mainly English-speaking countries within ECOWAS which plan to work towards adopting their own common currency, the [eco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco_\(currency\)).

  [^cite_note-\:0-79]

ECOWAS operates in three co-official languages—French, English, and Portuguese.[^cite_note-\:0-79]

### Expanded ECOWAS Commission

For the third time since its inception in 1975, ECOWAS is undergoing institutional reforms. The first was when it revised its treaty on 24 July 1993; the second was in 2007 when the Secretariat was transformed into a Commission. As of July 2013, ECOWAS now has six new departments (Human Resources Management; Education, Science and Culture; Energy and Mines; Telecommunications and IT; Industry and Private Sector Promotion). Finance and Administration to Sierra Leone has been decoupled, to give the incoming Ghana Commissioner the new portfolio of Administration and Conferences.[^cite_note-84]

### Community Court of Justice

Main article: [ECOWAS Court](45006378.md)

ECOWAS Community Court of Justice was created by a protocol signed in 1991 and was later included in Article 6 of the Revised Treaty of the Community in 1993.[^cite_note-Court-85] However, the Court did not officially begin operations until the 1991 protocol came into effect on 5 November 1996. The jurisdiction of the court is outlined in Article 9 and Articles 76 of the Revised Treaty and allows rulings on disputes between states over interpretations of the Revised Treaty. It also provides ECOWAS Council with advisory opinions on legal issues (Article 10). Like its companion courts, the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) and [East African Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Court_of_Justice), it has jurisdiction to rule on fundamental [human rights](13831.md) breaches.[^cite_note-Court-85]

### Sporting and cultural exchange

ECOWAS nations organise a broad array of cultural and sports events under the auspices of the body, including the [CEDEAO Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEDEAO_Cup) in [football](10568.md), the [2012 ECOWAS Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_ECOWAS_Games) and the [Miss CEDEAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_CEDEAO?action=edit\&redlink=1) [beauty pageant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_contest).[^cite_note-86]

The Community Heads of State and Government adopted [African Traditional Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutte_Traditionnelle) as the Community sport, and through its specialised agency in charge of youth and sports development, the [Ouagadougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouagadougou)-based ECOWAS Youth and Sports Development Centre (EYSDC), has consistently organised the yearly ECOWAS African Wrestling Tournament mainly in [Dakar](44251.md) (Senegal) and [Niamey](57061.md) (Niger) based on a harmonized African wrestling code.

The Community, through the EYSDC, also organized 2 editions of ECOWAS International Cycling tour, taking close to 100 riders from all member states, from [Lagos](85232.md) to [Accra](56824.md) and then from Lagos to [Abidjan](21371767.md). In addition to the sports and well-being objective of the tour, the race also served to demonstrate and put into practice ECOWAS protocol on free movement of goods and persons.

In 2019, the EYSDC instituted ECOWAS Abuja International Marathon. The first edition brought together international marathoners from West Africa, [Kenya](188171.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md) and [Cameroon](5447.md).

Similarly, the Community, through its specialised agency, promotes regional sports development by offering sponsorship to regional sports federations and specialized disciplines such as the West African Deaf Sports Union (WADSU), the West African Liaison Office of the International Council for Military Sports (WALO-CISM), the Region 2 of the African Athletics Federation, and the [West African University Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_University_Games) (WAUG), among others.

### Youth

The ECOWAS Youth Policy Strategic Plan of Action (SPAO) is a 10-year plan that aims to promote youth development and empowerment in the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS). The SPAO was adopted in 2016 and is based on the pillars of education and training, employment and entrepreneurship, health and well-being, peace and security, and governance and participation.[^cite_note-\:1-87][^cite_note-\:2-88]

The SPAO identifies a number of challenges facing youth in ECOWAS, including high unemployment rates, lack of access to education and training, and poor health outcomes. The plan sets out a number of strategies to address these challenges, including investing in education and training, creating jobs and supporting entrepreneurship, improving access to health care, promoting peace and security, and strengthening youth participation in governance.[^cite_note-\:1-87][^cite_note-\:2-88]

### West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA)&#x20;

Main article: [West African Economic and Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Economic_and_Monetary_Union)

See also: [CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc) and [Central Bank of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_West_African_States)

Formed in 1994 on the basis of earlier arrangements whose roots lie in the colonial era of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa), the West African Economic and Monetary Union, often referred to by its French acronym UEMOA, brings together eight West African states of which seven were French colonies until the late 1950s. The member countries use the [West African CFA franc](3791898.md) as their currency and share common institutions including the [Central Bank of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_West_African_States), [Banking Commission of the West African Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_Commission_of_the_West_African_Monetary_Union), [Financial Markets Authority of the West African Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Markets_Authority_of_the_West_African_Monetary_Union), and (together with other African countries of the [Franc Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franc_Zone)) [Regional Insurance Control Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Insurance_Control_Commission).

### West African Monetary Zone

See also: [Eco (currency)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco_\(currency\))

Formed in 2000, the West African Monetary Zone (WAMZ) is a group of six countries within ECOWAS that plan to introduce a common currency called the [eco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco_\(currency\)).[^cite_note-89] The six member states of WAMZ are [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia), [Ghana](12067.md), [Guinea](12176.md), [Nigeria](21383.md) and [Sierra Leone](27308.md) who founded the organisation together in 2000 and [Liberia](17791.md) who joined on 16 February 2010. Apart from Guinea, which is francophone, they are all English-speaking countries. Along with [Mauritania](18778516.md), Guinea opted out of the [CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc) currency shared by all other former French colonies in [West](67393.md) and [Central Africa](539606.md).

The WAMZ attempts to establish a strong stable currency to rival the CFA franc, whose [exchange rate is tied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_exchange-rate_system) to that of the euro and is guaranteed by the [French Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%A9sor_public). The eventual goal is for the CFA franc and eco to merge, giving all of West and Central Africa a single, stable currency. The launch of the new currency is being developed by the [West African Monetary Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Monetary_Institute) based in [Accra](56824.md), Ghana.

With the exit of Mali, Niger and Burkina Faso from the body, two structural options for a single currency could emerge: the "Sahel" for the AES and the "Eco" for the ECOWAS member countries.[^cite_note-90]

### The Free Movement of Persons, Residence and Establishment Protocol

In May 1979, ECOWAS adopted a Free Movement of Persons, Residence and Establishment Protocol, which permits citizens to enter, reside, and establish economic activities in the territory of member states.[^cite_note-\:4-10] There were three phases of implementation to achieve the goals of the protocol. Over the course of five years, Phase I eliminated the need for visas for stays of up to 90 days within the ECOWAS territory.[^cite_note-\:4-10] Phase II attempted to extend residency to citizens in host ECOWAS states to seek income-earning employment after obtaining an ECOWAS residence card. Phase II also required member states to grant migrant workers equal treatment in areas such as employment, participation, social and cultural activities, and in certain cases of job loss, re-employment, and training.[^cite_note-\:4-10] Phase III centered on the facilitation and establishment of business through the right of citizens to manage economic activities in countries other than their country of origin. However, this right has not been fully established in the ECOWAS region.[^cite_note-\:4-10] While these three phases promoting freedom of movement within the ECOWAS region is more advanced than in any other regional grouping in Africa, only the first phase has been fully implemented by all ECOWAS countries.[^cite_note-\:4-10] The complete implementation of the 90-day visa-free window enhanced human mobility in the region, creating positive effects on trade and economic development.[^cite_note-93]

In December 2000, the [ECOWAS passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECOWAS_passport) was introduced as a common passport that functions as an international travel document, and member states are currently in the process of implementing a joint visa for non-ECOWAS citizens.[^cite_note-94] Additionally, ECOWAS has worked to ease the movement of people transported in private and commercial vehicles by implementing policies that enable vehicles to enter and reside in a State for up to ninety days. Most ECOWAS states have instituted an ECOWAS brown card, which provides prompt, fair, and immediate compensation for any motor accident which occurs outside a motorist's home-country.[^cite_note-95]

While monitoring committees exist to ensure all three phases of the protocol are successfully implemented, their work is vague and has not been credited with effective and efficient production of data.[^cite_note-\:4-10] The largest challenges assosicated within the implementation of the protocol occur due to lack of commitment and enforceability. More so, there is a lack of access to readily available migrant information in the ECOWAS region. This poses a barrier to freedom of movement as immigration officials in member states are unaware that individuals who hold valid travel documents can enter their country freely. Therefore, West African migrants, who are entitled to enter through regular channels, leave their countries without proper travel documents and enter other countries illegally. This illegal and irregular entry poses a barrier towards gaining reliable travel statistics.[^cite_note-\:4-10]

For example, Francophone countries in the region have issued national identity cards that can be used similarly to a passport. These cards permit citizens to cross borders after presenting their identity cards. However, Anglophone countries have only just begun distributing a similar form of identification. Consequently, immigration officials in Anglophone countries commonly reject Francophone national identity cards and do not permit Francophone citizens to cross into their borders. Further, these structural barriers are exasperated between different social classes. Middle-class individuals typically experience a smoother border-crossing process than working-class individuals and impoverished citizens who do not have travel documents and are not fluent in the language of the countries they are crossing into.[^cite_note-96]

## Tourism

In 2019, ECOWAS unveiled its Ecotour Action Plan 2019 – 2029. It focuses on tourism heritage protection and development and on the development of standards, regulations, and control systems.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] The plan includes five programs for implementation, and detailed mechanisms for monitoring and evaluation. [Ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) is not specifically developed, yet it has been mentioned that the program has the opportunity to create linkages between institutions and stakeholder collaboration to suit ecotourism projects that prioritize community, biodiversity, and socioeconomics.[^cite_note-101] The Ecotour Plan prioritizes local development, especially in generating skilled and unskilled jobs for marginalized individuals, and aims to make the ECOWAS region a first-class tourist destination in Africa.[^cite_note-\:6-11] During its creation, ECOWAS ministers also called on ecotourism programs to protect threatened biodiversity in the [Guinean Forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_Forests_of_West_Africa), which span into seven ECOWAS member states. Similar to the Free Movement of People Protocol, Ecotour aims to integrate aviation and ground transportation. ECOWAS hopes that this regional approach will allow states to fight against [pandemics such as COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_West_Africa) to restore tourism and ecosystems. Ecotour works to create increasing returns to its members' economies by lowering transport costs, developing hospitality training centers and creating a more integrated use of digital technology.[^cite_note-102]

As of March 2023, Council members mentioned that phases one and two of the Ecotour Action Plan have come to an end and that the community is moving into phase three and four, which focuses on the development of tourist accommodations establishments, and a proposal for a regional mechanism to enforce tourist regulations. By the end of phase five, ECOWAS hopes to have unified accommodations in hotels, ecolodges, motels, [apart hotels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apart_hotel), and hostels. In April 2023, tourism experts met to amend the new text for tourist accommodations in the ECOWAS region.[^cite_note-103] This phase is critical to the success of the Ecotour plan as the lack of a regulatory system has been a barrier to the development of the tourism sector, despite its ability to increase member states' economies.[^cite_note-104] During this conference, ministers improved the tourism industry by adopting standards for hotel services. Massandjé Toure-Liste, the ECOWAS Commission's Commissioner for Economic Affairs and Agriculture, pointed out the improvements in the tourist sector due to the [African Continental Free Trade Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Continental_Free_Trade_Area), a trade agreement signed by 44 members of the [African Union](10634933.md) which creates a single market for goods and services. Toure-Liste praised the trade area for providing development opportunities, economic growth, and boosting regional integration.[^cite_note-105]
