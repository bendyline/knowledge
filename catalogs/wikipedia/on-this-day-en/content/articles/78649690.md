The **Baniyas Refinery** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مصفاة بانياس, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Miṣfāh Bāniyās*) is an [oil refinery](195137.md) located north of [Baniyas](30871895.md), Syria. It is the largest oil refinery in Syria.[^cite_note-\:0-1] It is owned by the Baniyas Oil Company, which is managed by the [Ministry of Oil and Mineral Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Oil_and_Mineral_Resources_\(Syria\)).[^cite_note-2] Of its four refining units, two process [light crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_crude), originally configured for Euphrates Valley crude, and two process [heavy crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_crude), originally configured for [Suwayda](3096933.md) crude.[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-4] [Pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipelines) connect the refinery to oil fields in [Deir ez-Zor](7445749.md) and [Al-Hasakah](1429110.md).[^cite_note-\:2-5] The refinery houses a [thermal power station](2549543.md)[^cite_note-\:3-6] that runs on [fuel oil](392547.md) and has an installed capacity of 30 MW.[^cite_note-\:1-3]

During the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), the refinery processed up to 125,000 barrels per day imported from [Iran](14653.md) by [oil tankers](15923603.md),[^cite_note-\:2-5][^cite_note-\:1-3] which docked at Baniyas oil terminal.[^cite_note-7] Following the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) in December 2024, it was forced to temporarily halt operations due to the cessation of Iranian imports.[^cite_note-\:0-1] In September 2025, the refinery received deliveries of imported equipment as part of a modernisation project.[^cite_note-8]

Several incidents have occurred at the facility. In 2019, an explosion during site maintenance killed a worker.[^cite_note-9] In August 2021, a fuel leak from the refinery power plant caused an [oil spill](158705.md) along the Syrian coast.[^cite_note-\:3-6]
