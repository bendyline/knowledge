**Jonas Gahr Støre** (Norwegian: [\[ˈjùːnɑs gɑː‿ˈʂtø̂ːrə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Norwegian);[^cite_note-2] born 25 August 1960) is a Norwegian politician who has served as the [prime minister of Norway](220458.md) since 2021. He has been leader of the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Norway\)) since 2014. He served under prime minister [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg) as [minister of foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Norway\)) from 2005 to 2012 and as [minister of health and care services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Health_and_Care_Services) from 2012 to 2013. Støre has been a member of the [Storting](172897.md) for [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_\(Storting_constituency\)) since 2009.

Støre was born in Oslo, grew up in [West End Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_and_West_End_of_Oslo) and underwent naval officer training at the [Royal Norwegian Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Naval_Academy). He studied [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) at [Sciences Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po) in Paris from 1981 to 1985 and [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics). As a student in Paris, he was active in the efforts to support the [Jewish refuseniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusenik) in the Soviet Union.

Støre was a career [special adviser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_adviser_\(Norway\)) and director-general in the Prime Minister's Office from 1989 to 1997, serving under prime ministers [Jan Syse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_P._Syse), [Gro Harlem Brundtland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro_Harlem_Brundtland), and [Thorbjørn Jagland](192081.md). He became known as a protégé of Brundtland in the 1990s, and her mentorship inspired him to become a member of the Labour Party in 1995. In 1998, he followed Brundtland to the [World Health Organization](33583.md), where he became her chief of staff. Støre was state secretary and chief of staff in the Prime Minister's Office in the [first government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Stoltenberg_cabinet) of [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg); the government was inspired by the British [New Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Labour) project. He later served as secretary-general of the [Norwegian Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Red_Cross) from 2003 to 2005.[^cite_note-stbio-3] Like his political mentors Brundtland and Stoltenberg, Støre is mostly associated with the business-friendly right-wing of the Labour Party. He became a household name for Norwegians during his tenure as foreign minister, when he was voted the most popular member of the cabinet several times.[^cite_note-4]

Despite the Labour Party receiving 1% fewer votes and losing a seat, the centre-left won a majority in the [2021 Norwegian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Norwegian_parliamentary_election). As the leader of the largest party, Støre was the clear favourite for the role of prime minister. Two days after the resignation of [Erna Solberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erna_Solberg) and [her government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solberg_cabinet) on 12 October 2021, Støre was appointed as prime minister by King [Harald V](66069.md), leading a [minority government](271434.md) with the [Centre Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Party_\(Norway\)).[^cite_note-5] In early 2025, the Centre Party left the coalition. Støre led the Labour Party to victory in the [2025 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Norwegian_parliamentary_election) and has continued to lead a Labour minority government since.

## Background

### Family

Born in Oslo, Støre is the son of ship broker Ulf Jonas Støre (1925–2017) and librarian Unni Gahr (1931–2021).[^cite_note-Jonas_Gahr_Støre-6] He grew up in the [Ris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ris,_Norway) neighbourhood in [West End Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_Oslo).[^cite_note-7] Støre is a multi-millionaire, with a fortune of around [NOK](64149.md) 60,000,000 (approx. [US\$](18717338.md)7,100,000 in 2016).[^cite_note-8] He owns a large part of the family company Femstø. Most of the family fortune comes from the 1977 sale of Norwegian company [Jøtul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B8tul), which was run by his maternal grandfather Johannes Gahr.[^cite_note-9] Støre's paternal grandfather was prominent business executive [Jonas Henry Støre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Henry_St%C3%B8re), the CEO and chairman of explosives manufacturer [Norsk Sprængstofindustri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsk_Spr%C3%A6ngstofindustri). Støre's great-grandfather Paul Edvart Støre was a [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Norway\)) mayor and deputy member of the Norwegian parliament from [Levanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levanger_\(town\)), and the family were affluent farmers in [Trøndelag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%B8ndelag) in the 19th century.[^cite_note-10]

Støre married [Marit Slagsvold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marit_Slagsvold), a sociologist and [minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_\(Christianity\)) in the state [Church of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Norway) in 1988.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-nettavisen-12][^cite_note-13] They have three sons, who attended [Oslo Waldorf School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Waldorf_School).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Støre is a professed [Christian](18337522.md) and a member of the state church.[^cite_note-17]

### Education and early career

Støre attended Slemendal Primary School, Ris Lower Secondary School and [Berg School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_videreg%C3%A5ende_skole) in Oslo. He then underwent naval officer training at the [Royal Norwegian Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Naval_Academy) from 1979 to 1980 before serving as a sub-lieutenant in the Royal Norwegian Navy from 1980 to 1981.[^cite_note-18] He later studied political science for five years at [Sciences Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Institute_of_Political_Studies) in Paris.[^cite_note-Jonas_Gahr_Støre-6] He started studying for a PhD at The London School of Economics, but dropped out after a few weeks.

In 1986, Støre was briefly a teaching fellow in the [Harvard Negotiation Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Negotiation_Project) at [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School).[^cite_note-19] From 1986 to 1989 he was a researcher at the [Norwegian School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_School_of_Management), working on the *Scenarier 2000* project with sociologist [Andreas Hompland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Hompland) and economist Petter Nore.[^cite_note-Stortinget-20]

## Career in public administration

Støre applied for a position as a political advisor on foreign affairs for the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Norway\)) in 1988. He was offered the job but subsequently turned it down.[^cite_note-adressa.no-21] In 1989, Støre became special adviser in the Prime Minister's Office of [Gro Harlem Brundtland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro_Harlem_Brundtland).[^cite_note-adressa.no-21] Brundtland's mentorship inspired him to become a member of the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Norway\)) in 1995, when he also became a director-general (*ekspedisjonssjef*) in the Prime Minister's Office. From 1998, he was executive director (Chief of Staff) in the [World Health Organization](33583.md) under the leadership of [Gro Harlem Brundtland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro_Harlem_Brundtland).[^cite_note-Stortinget-20]

Støre was executive chairman of the ECON Analyse [think tank](37101.md) from 2002 to 2003, and secretary general of the Norwegian [Red Cross](15487.md) from 2003 to 2005.

## Political career

### Chief of staff

Støre was State Secretary and Chief of Staff in the Prime Minister's Office in the [first government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoltenberg's_First_Cabinet) of Jens Stoltenberg from 2000 to 2001. The government was inspired by the [British Labour Party](19279158.md)'s [New Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Labour) agenda and oversaw the most widespread [privatization](24661.md) in Norwegian history.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### Foreign minister



Støre and [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg) with US President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) during the NATO Summit in April 2008

After the [2005 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Norwegian_parliamentary_election), Støre was appointed foreign minister in Jens Stoltenberg's government, serving in that position until 2012. When he joined the cabinet he was perceived as part of a group of "West End executives" and confidants of Stoltenberg that represented a shift to the right.[^cite_note-dagsavisen-24] Nevertheless, numerous polls showed that Støre was the most popular member of the Stoltenberg government.[^cite_note-25] However, he was also subject to criticisms in 2010 when he and [Anne-Grete Strøm-Erichsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne-Grete_Str%C3%B8m-Erichsen), minister of health, accepted expensive rugs from Afghan politicians.[^cite_note-26]

In 2006, Støre voiced concern over the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War). Støre called [Israel](9282173.md)'s reaction "totally unacceptable" and referred to it as "a dangerous escalation," while also condemning [Hezbollah](13919.md)'s attack on Israeli soldiers.[^cite_note-27]

Støre criticized the expansion of influence of the [G20](2206608.md) in response to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), calling it "sorely lacking in legitimacy" and comparing it to the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna).[^cite_note-28]

#### Assassination attempts

Main articles: [2008 Kabul Serena Hotel attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kabul_Serena_Hotel_attack) and [2011 Norway attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Norway_attacks)

On 14 January 2008, a [suicide bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bomber) struck the [Serena Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Hotel) in [Kabul](16826.md), Afghanistan, where Støre was staying.[^cite_note-29] Støre was unhurt in the incident, which killed six people including Norwegian journalist [Carsten Thomassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsten_Thomassen_\(journalist\)).[^cite_note-30] [United Nations Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) stated that Støre was the target of the attack,[^cite_note-31] but this claim was rejected by a [Taliban](30635.md) spokesperson.[^cite_note-32] Støre cancelled the rest of his visit to Afghanistan the day after the attack.[^cite_note-vg506423-33]

On 22 July 2011, Støre was one of the main targets of [Anders Behring Breivik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Behring_Breivik)'s attacks.[^cite_note-34]

### Minister of Health and Care Services

On 21 September 2012, Jens Stoltenberg commenced a cabinet reshuffle, and moved Støre to head the Ministry of Health and Care Services. He was succeeded as foreign minister by [Espen Barth Eide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espen_Barth_Eide).

### Member of Parliament

In the 2009 general election in Norway, Støre was elected to the Norwegian parliament, the [Stortinget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stortinget), representing [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_\(Storting_constituency\)). He has been re-elected since.

As a member of parliament Støre nominated one of the two recipients of the 2021 [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md), [Maria Ressa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Ressa).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

### Leader of the Labour Party

On 14 June 2014, he was elected leader of [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Norway\)), succeeding [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg), who had been appointed [Secretary General of NATO](22194433.md). He also became Leader of the Opposition. Støre led the party into the [2017 Norwegian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Norwegian_parliamentary_election), but the red-green coalition lost by four seats needed for a majority of 85 seats. His party was criticised for taking victory for granted.[^cite_note-37]

In August 2017, Støre received criticism when it was revealed that workers in a building company hired to do work on his holiday home had not paid taxes or [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAT).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In late 2017, several women alleged that the party deputy leader [Trond Giske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trond_Giske) had behaved inappropriately against them. Initially, Støre stated that Giske hadn't behaved as such after an internal discussion regarding the matter. In early January 2018, Giske decided to resign as deputy leader as a result of the allegations, while reasoning from the party was also given that he had breached rules of sexual misconduct.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Giske's successor, [Bjørnar Skjæran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rnar_Skj%C3%A6ran), was nominated on 19 March 2019. Following the nomination, Støre praised Skjæran, saying he would be the clear voice from the North.[^cite_note-44]

Støre led the party into the [2021 Norwegian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Norwegian_parliamentary_election), this time securing the red-green coalition a majority with 89 seats (85 being needed for a majority), defeating the blue-blue coalition led by incumbent prime minister [Erna Solberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erna_Solberg).[^cite_note-45] Pre-government negotiations began on 23 September in [Hurdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurdal), after the Centre Party became open to working with the Socialist Left Party. On 29 September, the Socialist Left Party withdrew from negotiations, notably citing disagreements on issues such as petroleum and welfare. Støre expressed disappointment and said he had hoped for a different outcome, but added he respected the party's decision.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] The Labour Party and Centre Party began government negotiations later that same day.[^cite_note-48] On 8 October, Støre and Vedum announced that the new government's platform would be presented on 13 October and that they were ready to form a government the day after, on 14 October.[^cite_note-49] After [Eva Kristin Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Kristin_Hansen) was nominated as the Labour Party's candidate for [President of the Storting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Storting), Støre presented the proposal in the Storting on 9 October, and the vote to confirm her happened in writing. Hansen was confirmed with 160 votes in favour and 8 abstaining.[^cite_note-50]

Støre was appointed prime minister on 14 October 2021, following his party's victory in the parliamentary election held in September. He formed a minority government, with the Labour Party and Centre Party. The Centre Party withdrew from government in late January 2025, leaving Støre in charge of the first Labour minority government in 25 years.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Støre led his party to re-election at the [2025 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Norwegian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-53] He announced that the Labour Party would continue as a minority government, and that he would invite the other red-green parties to conversations about the cooperation in the upcoming parliamentary term.[^cite_note-54]

### Domestic policy

Støre made his government's ascension declaration on 18 October, reiterating major parts of the government platform. These included "to combat social dumping and carry out a major clean-up in Norwegian working life to secure permanent, full-time positions with Norwegian pay and working conditions", to remove general access to temporary hiring, to limit hiring, to fight work-related crime, and to stop the release of the taxi industry. As regards industrial policy, Støre commented that "to succeed, we must ensure that Norwegian natural resources, including reasonably renewable energy, remain a competitive advantage for Norwegian industry", while also announcing a green industry initiative where the state would participate to get new industry up and running. Regarding regional policy, Støre said that the government would put forward a case to the [Storting](172897.md) to separate [Troms and Finnmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troms_og_Finnmark) once again, and doing the same for [Viken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viken_\(county\)), if the county council should wish to do so. In regard to transport, Støre stated that "the prices on national and county road ferries shall be halved during the four-year period", while also reiterating the promise of free ferries between islands and communities without a road connection to the mainland. This also included ferry connections carrying under 100 000 passengers annually.[^cite_note-55] Støre also announced that his government would act to reduce the increased electricity prices, and indicated that this could be in place within a short timespan.[^cite_note-56]

In a parliamentary session a week after assuming office, Støre defended the idea of increasing the sales tax on more expensive electric cars. He said that the money earned could be used to construct new charging stations. He further said that his government would initiate discussions with the [European Union](9317.md) about how to strengthen their cooperation to reach the climate goals.[^cite_note-57] [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Norway\)) leader [Erna Solberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erna_Solberg) also called on Støre to clarify his government's strategy to tackle [COVID-19](63030231.md), following a recent spike in hospital admissions. Støre responded that his government was following the COVID-19 situation and would inform the Storting and public if necessary. He also notably said: "Something must have happened since Thursday until today that gives cause for concern when one now wants a briefing from the Prime Minister in the Storting." He also said that the government had not received any messages from health authorities that would give cause for deep concern.[^cite_note-58]

On 12 November, Støre announced that his government would be introducing new national COVID-19 measures. These would include allowing municipalities to introduce COVID-19 passports; offering everyone over the age of 18 a booster vaccine dose; tightening the rules on who is in need of testing; and introducing new rules for unvaccinated health personnel. He also stated that the government was considering more extensive use of the COVID-19 certificate, and that they planned for everyone over the age of 18 to be offered a third vaccine dose next year.[^cite_note-59]

On 29 November, Støre, minister of finance [Trygve Slagsvold Vedum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Slagsvold_Vedum) and [Socialist Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Left_Party_\(Norway\)) leader [Audun Lysbakken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audun_Lysbakken) presented their re-negotiated state budget for 2022. Støre called it a "good budget \[that] has become better". He reiterated that the budget showed that it was "ordinary people's turn". The negotiations had originally begun on 15 November; the party leaders had been called in the weekend before the budget presentation.[^cite_note-60]

On 2 December, Støre, his government and health officials announced measures against the [SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Omicron_variant). Some of the measures were reiterated from previous COVID-19 measures, notably that people should keep distance between themselves, and wearing masks on public transport. In addition, the measures called for people to wear masks in malls and shops and while in contact with health services; not to hug or give handshakes; and for businesses to work from home in a manner that would not affect essential services.[^cite_note-61]

On 7 December, Støre and his government put forward further COVID-19 measures. Of the new measures, Støre said: "We should have sincerely hoped that we were done with the corona pandemic now and hoped for a long time that Christmas could be as before and as normal, but now the situation with infection is still so serious that we must take new measures." He further stated that the danger of overloading the health service and the spread of the omicron variant made new measures necessary. The measures covered the following: distance and social contact, leisure activities, schools, kindergartens and after-school programs, labour and ventilation.[^cite_note-62] Two days later, Støre didn't rule out that further measures could be announced, although they could perhaps include a few adjustments to already presented measures.[^cite_note-63]

On 13 December, Støre and his government announced further measures to battle COVID-19 and the omicron variant. The measures were said to be to prevent hospitals being overwhelmed and to protect socially critical functions. He stressed that the situation had become more critical and that the measures were put in place to maintain control of the pandemic. He also stressed that it was important to shield children, young people, and vulnerable groups, and that the measures would be noticed in people's everyday life.[^cite_note-64]

In early January 2022, Støre said that COVID-19 measures should not be in place any longer than necessary. He also stated that having a yellow clearance level in upper secondary schools was a target for them, and eventually getting it to green by more testing. He emphasised that the country was not in lockdown but had strict measures that understandably had ramifications for people.[^cite_note-65]

On 13 January, the government announced the lifting of a few COVID-19 measures, and a few changes to others. Støre warned that it might be necessary to adjust the measures to be stricter in the future, and said that a new evaluation would be made in early February.[^cite_note-66]

On 12 February, Støre and his government announced the lifting of almost all measures against COVID-19, notably social distancing and mandatory mask wearing. Remaining measures included practising good hand and coughing hygienes, following the vaccine program, and getting tested for possible symptoms. Støre reiterated that the pandemic was not over, citing the current situation in the midst of an ongoing omicron wave.[^cite_note-67]

On 20 April, Støre issued an apology to the queer community, marking the 50th anniversary of the abolishment of anti-homosexual legislation in Norway. He stated: "119 people were criminalized and punished for their love life. They had to go through trials, convictions and imprisonment. They faced public shame and condemnation. Through legislation, but also through a network of sanctions, we as a nation and society clearly stated that we did not accept queer love. The government wants to apologize for that."[^cite_note-68]

On 16 June, following the [Standing Committee on Foreign Affairs and Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs_and_Defence)'s vote on Swedish and Finnish NATO membership, Støre and all opposition parties criticised the [Red Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Party_\(Norway\)) for voting against approval, describing it as indicative of a lack of solidarity. Støre specifically said: "The Red Party's view expresses an attitude fundamentally lacking in solidarity".[^cite_note-69]

In early August, Støre stated that his government would "do what's necessary" to combat the exorbitant electricity prices and added that a maximum price was one of several measures that was being considered. He also stated that another measure was to limit how much power companies can pay for electricity, while also no measures would be "undividedly positive".[^cite_note-70]

Following the [Norwegian Directorate of Fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Directorate_of_Fisheries)'s decision to euthanise [Freya the Walrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freya_\(walrus\)), Støre expressed support for the decision. Regarding the massive response to the walrus's death, he said: "I am not surprised that this has led to many international reactions. Norway is a maritime nation, sometimes we have to make unpopular decisions. I myself have been in discussions about minke whales and seals. These reactions are not new".[^cite_note-71]

In September, he said that the state budget for 2023 could be "disappointing", noting that several promises from the Hurdal platform would have to be put on hold.[^cite_note-72]

On 3 November, in preparation for the [2022 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference), Støre announced that his government would be increasing the climate goal from 50% to 55%. His environment minister, [Espen Barth Eide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espen_Barth_Eide), added that this change would include every sector and climate gases.[^cite_note-73]

In early December, controversy arose when it was announced that Kristoffer Thoner would be appointed as an advisor to the Prime Minister's Office. The issue was Thoner's previous experience from [McKinsey & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_&_Company) where his customer lists were confidential. Despite this, Støre expressed confidence in Thoner. McKinsey would later declassify Thoner's customer lists at the request of the Prime Minister's Office.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

On 2 March, after his energy minister apologised on behalf of the government to the Sami people for human rights violations with wind farms placed at Fosen in [Trøndelag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%B8ndelag), Støre also expressed his apology.[^cite_note-76]

A week after the Fosen protests ended, Støre visited the [Sámi Parliament of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1mi_Parliament_of_Norway) and held a speech to the assembly. He talked about combating harassment against Sami people and pledged that his government would follow up on the ruling for Fosen. The youth league of the [Norwegian Sámi Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_S%C3%A1mi_Association) warned that protests would resume unless the government saw to handle the issue, with their leader, Elle Nystad, stating that it would serve as a reminder that an apology isn't sufficient unless the case is followed up on.[^cite_note-77]

On 28 March, Støre and his government announced that they would be lowering the basic interest tax for aquaculture to 35%, which will come into force from 1 January 2024.[^cite_note-78]

In September, he and higher education minister [Sandra Borch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Borch) announced that the government would invest 1 billion NOK in research of [artificial intelligence](1164.md) and digital technology. The investment was praised by both AI researchers and the opposition.[^cite_note-79]

In March 2024, Støre announced that Norway would be able to reach the goal of spending at least 2% of GDP on defence by the beginning of July that same year, rather than after two years, as previously predicted.[^cite_note-80] The goal originated as a non-binding agreement among [NATO](21133.md) members and states that each member state should aspire to spend at least 2% of their GDP on defence. Støre has praised this aim, though many commentators see it as conspicuous that it has received far more support and traction after [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and others have in effect made threats against those countries which fail to reach it.

#### Energy

In an October 2021 interview with the *[Financial Times](136566.md)*, Støre said that if Norway were to halt its petroleum industry, it would only contribute to a further struggle to reach the climate goals. He went on to say "we should develop towards a transition \[from fossil fuels to green energy], not close it \[the fossil fuels industry] down". Støre further added that it was a paradox that Norway, as Western Europe's largest petroleum producer, also spent significant funds on investment in green technology such as electric cars, [carbon capture and storage](3679268.md) and offshore wind power. Regarding the country's petroleum fund, he said it was political, but was driven by professionals whose aim was to gain much in return for an acceptable risk. He also emphasised that it was up to the government and Storting to set the boundaries for the fund.[^cite_note-81]

On 27 October, Støre announced a decrease in the fuel tax to be implemented in the 2022 state budget, while expressing concern about the increasing electricity costs. He said the revised budget would be presented on 8 November, adding: "We also want a policy that cuts taxes that hit ordinary people. Both in terms of taxes on fuel and electricity, we will look at this in our additional budget." He also said that the financial situation of municipalities should be strengthened so they could offer better welfare services.[^cite_note-82]

On 5 December, Støre expressed hope that a plan to deal with the ongoing electricity price crisis would be figured out before Christmas. He stated that the solution might involve additional money, and emphasised that the scheme had to work and that the government would make sure that it did. He added that the purpose of the plan was to help the most vulnerable, and that it had to be done in a fail-safe way and would not apply to everyone in Norway.[^cite_note-83]

At a press conference held on 11 December, Støre, alongside Vedum and minister of petroleum and energy [Marte Mjøs Persen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marte_Mj%C3%B8s_Persen), announced a new security scheme to battle the rising electricity prices. In particular, if the average market price of power were to exceed 70 øre per kilowatt hour for a month, the state would compensate half of the price above this level. The scheme would last throughout December and through March 2022. Støre notably said: "We present a security scheme that will meet the situation that ordinary people experience with extraordinary electricity costs."[^cite_note-84]

On 8 January 2022, Støre and Vedum said that the electricity support would be increased from 55 to 80% until at least March. He stated that the government would stand up against the electricity crisis in the next years should it happen again, and also defended the government's scheme, saying: "The scheme is good, because it allows us to quickly and accurately compensate all the country's consumers, by directly providing a subsidy to the electricity bill." Støre added that the government expected the prices to drop come spring, as they usually did, and that a similar scheme could be necessary should the prices rise again during future winters.[^cite_note-85]

At an unexpected press conference held on 28 September, Støre and finance minister [Trygve Slagsvold Vedum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Slagsvold_Vedum) announced that the government would be taking in 33 billion NOK from the power producers and aquaculture industries. Støre stated: "For this government, it is not relevant to cover the costs with large cuts, that is an alternative, we do not choose that. We have so far placed great emphasis on protecting the welfare state. For this government, it is not relevant to increase taxes for ordinary people". The aquaculture industry went out against the possibility of a basic interest tax for aquaculture and expressed that it would "shut off the lights along the coast".[^cite_note-86]

Støre was asked at his first question time of 2023 in the [Stortinget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stortinget) about Minister for Higher Education [Ola Borten Moe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ola_Borten_Moe)'s criticism of his own government's energy investment in hydrogen. Støre responded by saying that Borten Moe was simply wrong. Talking to the newspaper *[Aftenposten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftenposten)* afterwards he added that in a \[«imagined»] world with no challenges from climate change, it wouldn't have been necessary to bring hydrogen into the energy mix.[^cite_note-87]

Støre and his ministers, [Trygve Slagsvold Vedum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Slagsvold_Vedum) and [Terje Aasland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terje_Aasland), announced on 15 February 2023 that the electricity support scheme would be expanded until 2024. Other changes also included an hourly based calculation rather than monthly, and an increase from 70 to 90% per kilowatt hour, including during the summer months.[^cite_note-88]

In August, Støre and his government announced that they would allow electrification of the [Melkøya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melk%C3%B8ya) power development plant, which would ensure that it could stay operational until 2040. Støre further argued that the measure would cut emissions by 850 000 tons.[^cite_note-89]

In late January 2025, he and energy minister [Terje Aasland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terje_Aasland) announced a new line of electricity actions. This included a fixed price, a VAT cut, scrapping plans for new overseas cables until 2029 and a rejection of one of the four EU directives, the latter of which was part of the reason coalition partner the [Centre Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Party_\(Norway\)) withdrew from government.[^cite_note-90]

In August 2026, he and energy minister [Terje Aasland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terje_Aasland) presented a series of reforms to the energy sector. This notably included simplifying procedures for onshore wind power projects, hydropower and power grid developments, and municipalities right to accepting or rejecting projects would be enshrined in law. The reforms are scheduled to come into effect by the end of the parliamentary period (late 2029) and into the 2030s.[^cite_note-91]

#### Response to terrorism

Following the [Kongsberg attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongsberg_attack) a day before he assumed office, Støre and then-[Minister of Justice and Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Justice_and_Public_Security) [Emilie Enger Mehl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilie_Enger_Mehl) visited the city the day after, where they put down flowers in memory of the victims. Støre said that the visit was also intended to show solidarity with the people of Kongsberg.[^cite_note-92]

Following the [2022 Oslo shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Oslo_shooting) during the Pride Festival, Støre said that "we know that queer people are the targets of hate, threats and violence. The hate against trans people is particularly severe. We will not accept that in Norway."[^cite_note-93]

#### Youth affairs

In April 2026, Støre and his government announced that they would implement an age restriction for social media, setting at 16. The proposal would only allow children to utilise social media from 1 January the year they turn 16. Støre argued that this was necessary to not cause an uneven divide in access to social mediums in school classes. Furthermore, that the restriction was meant to help protect children from harmful content and to regulate their time spent on different platforms. The [Norwegian Data Protection Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Data_Protection_Authority) was critical of the government's proposal, but primarily in regards to them continuing to allow tech companies to uphold age verification of children, citing concerns for privacy policies not being properly enforced.[^cite_note-94]

#### Asia

Støre visited China in September 2024 to mark 70 years of diplomatic relations between Norway and China. He also met with President [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) and premier [Li Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Qiang). He highlighted that China is important partner in trade, artificial intelligence and climate change, but also noted that it was important to secure Norwegian interests. He also showed doubts about reiterating the importance of human rights violations, citing when China cut diplomatic ties back in 2010, further arguing that restarting the countries' former relationship would be challenging.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

During a visit to China in July 2026, Støre called on China to use its [influence over Russia](30964882.md) to promote negotiations to end the [Russo-Ukrainian War](70149799.md). He stated that China's close ties with Russia provided Beijing with an opportunity to encourage [peace talks](70255183.md), while also asserting that the relationship limited China's economic and political cooperation with Europe.[^cite_note-98]

#### Baltics

During a state visit by Lithuanian President [Gitanas Nauseda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitanas_Nauseda) in February 2023, Støre didn't rule out Nauseda's request for deploying Norwegian fighter jets in the country. Støre also remarked that a possible deployment would have to be discussed with other NATO countries who have stationed troops in Lithuania.[^cite_note-99]

Alongside defence minister [Bjørn Arild Gram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rn_Arild_Gram), Støre announced in July during a visit to Norwegian troops in Lithuania, that their stay would be extended by a year through 2024.[^cite_note-100]

#### Nordics

At a [Nordic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Council) meeting in [Copenhagen](5166.md) on 3 November 2021, Støre said that he wanted to adjust relations with Sweden following the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), while also defending the decision of the [previous government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solberg's_Cabinet) to close the border during the pandemic. Swedish politicians from the [Christian Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democrats_\(Sweden\)) and [Moderate Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderate_Party_\(Sweden\)) criticised the move, calling it "pandemic nationalism". Støre emphasised that people carry infection and it could appear in a line of people. He also stated that it was a difficult decision to close borders.[^cite_note-101]

Støre called Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdogan)'s opposition to Swedish and Finnish NATO membership "surprising". He further commented on Erdogan's claim that "Scandinavia is like a guest house for terrorist organisations", calling it a "completely baseless claim".[^cite_note-102]

During his semi-annual speech in late June 2023, Støre condemned burning of the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) and other holy books and flags, referring to the recent burning of the former in Sweden. He expressed that he thought of such actions as hate crime, but noted that it would ultimately be up to the courts and justice system to determine whether what expressions are within the boundaries of freedom of expression or not.[^cite_note-103]

Støre with President of the European Commission [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) in April 2025

#### Ukraine

Following the [2022 Ukraine cyberattack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukraine_cyberattack), Støre warned "we need to be more alert" and emphasised that Norway also had to be more alert to hybrid attacks and to consider them a threat to society. He said: "I am concerned that we as individuals and companies and institutions must be aware that this is part of our preparedness." He went on to say that the issue of cyber security would be looked at by the new Defence Commission, to be headed by former justice minister [Knut Storberget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knut_Storberget).[^cite_note-111]

Following the deployment of Russian troops to Eastern Ukraine, Støre expressed concerns about the situation and also condemned the actions by Russia. He also criticised Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md)'s speech where Russia recognised the [Donetsk](42425747.md) and [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) people's republics as independent countries, and [Sergei Lavrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Lavrov)'s further comments.[^cite_note-112]

On 27 February, Støre and members of his cabinet announced that Norway would be freezing the [Government Pension Fund of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Pension_Fund_of_Norway)'s investment in Russia. A process to pull the Oil Fund out of the country over time was also initiated.[^cite_note-113]

On 31 March, Støre had an hour-long phone call with Putin after taking the initiative to do so with Nordic and European allies and the United States. He described the conversation as "possible to reach out" to Putin, and urged him to end the [invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). He also urged him to open humanitarian corridors in [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol) and that the war required a negotiated solution.[^cite_note-114]

Støre visited Kyiv on 1 July, where he met with Ukrainian President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md), and announced that Norway would be contributing with 10 billion NOK in aid. The money would notably go to humanitarian aid, reconstruction and support to crucial infrastructure, such as schools and hospitals. Støre also visited the ruins of the city of [Yahidne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahidne,_Chernihiv_Oblast), which he described as "getting an insight into hell on earth".[^cite_note-115]

On 30 September, Støre condemned the Russian annexation of [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md), [Zaporizhzhia Oblast](494936.md), [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) and [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md). He also confirmed that Norway had accepted assistance from other allied countries to tighten security in the Norwegian sector. Regarding the annexations, he stated: "Putin has announced a few hours ago that Russia is incorporating four Ukrainian regions as part of Russia. The annexation of the four regions is without legitimacy. The so-called referendums were carried out under military occupation and are in violation of international law".[^cite_note-116]

At a press conference on 20 October, accompanied by justice minister [Emilie Enger Mehl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilie_Enger_Mehl) and defence minister [Bjørn Arild Gram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rn_Arild_Gram), Støre warned that the war in Ukraine was reaching a new and dangerous phase. He also warned that the security situation in Europe had become more tense in the wake of the [Nord Stream pipeline sabotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nord_Stream_pipeline_sabotage).[^cite_note-117]

In early May 2023, he attended a summit in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki), Finland, with Ukrainian President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) and other Nordic leaders.[^cite_note-118]

In February 2024, Støre announced that his government would provide 75 million NOK in support for Ukraine and stated that Norway would continue to support the country.[^cite_note-119]

Støre was one of the participants at the European-led Ukraine peace summit in March 2025. Despite the American absence at the summit, he argued that the United States should take part in any peace negotiations and that they would be the guarantor for security as a foundation for a peace settlement in the [war in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-120]

Støre with US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in April 2025

#### Middle East

On 24 November 2021, after [NRK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRK) journalists Halvor Ekeland and Lokman Ghorbani were detained in [Qatar](8238258.md) and released after 30 hours, Støre criticised the country, saying: "The arrest of NRK's journalists in Qatar is unacceptable. On 24 September 2022, Støre condemned the [Iranian regime](50913538.md) for its treatment of women and its hijab mandate in the wake of the [death of Mahsa Amini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Mahsa_Amini); stating: "My deepest condolences to Mahsa Amini's family in Iran and her relatives in Norway. It shows how much is at stake, and how much he feels he has to defend. Having a football president who invites to a party by scolding large parts of the world is not a good start to a sporting event". Following the start of the [Gaza war](74998519.md), Støre expressed his condemnation and called for the conflict to end. He also iterated that Israel has the right to defend itself as well as encouraging de-escalation.[^cite_note-130] On 10 November 2023, Støre stated that Israel's actions in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) violated international laws of war.[^cite_note-131]

On 22 May 2024, Støre announced that Norway would be recognising [Palestine](241405.md) as an independent country, joining [Spain](26667.md) and [Ireland](147575.md) in doing the same. The move received widespread support in parliament, while Israel decided to recall their ambassador to both Ireland and Norway as a result.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]



Støre at the [Gaza peace summit](81324529.md) in [Sharm El Sheikh](419335.md), Egypt, 13 October 2025

In October 2024, Støre called the proposed Israeli draft bills that would seek to ban the [UNRWA](32270.md) from Israel "a direct catastrophe" that could affect vulnerable Palestinians. He further stated that Norway would be putting pressure on Israeli allies like the [United States](3434750.md) and [Germany](11867.md), but would also attempt to appeal to other countries as well.[^cite_note-134]

In March 2025, Støre said in a statement that the renewed [Israeli attacks on the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2025_Israeli_attacks_on_the_Gaza_Strip) were "a great tragedy" for the people of Gaza.[^cite_note-135]

Støre was among the attendees of the [2025 Gaza peace summit](81324529.md) in [Egypt](8087628.md) and also expressed support for the peace plan put forward by US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md). ## Political positions

Like his political mentors [Gro Harlem Brundtland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro_Harlem_Brundtland) and [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg), Støre is associated with the business-friendly right-leaning faction of the Labour Party.[^cite_note-dagsavisen-24]

### Foreign policy

#### Middle East

During Støre's tenure as Foreign Minister, Norway was one of the first governments of the [Western world](21208200.md) to recognise the [Palestinian](241405.md) government.[^cite_note-138] In 2011 it became known that Støre had been in direct contact with Hamas leader [Khaled Meshaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Meshaal).[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] Former Conservative Prime Minister [Kåre Willoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A5re_Willoch) has also emphasized that dialogue with Hamas is important.[^cite_note-144] Støre has hailed the humanitarian work of [Mads Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mads_Gilbert) and [Erik Fosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Fosse) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), and both Støre and former Conservative Prime Minister [Kåre Willoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A5re_Willoch) wrote endorsements for their book *Eyes in Gaza* on the [Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%932009\)).[^cite_note-145] Støre wrote that Gilbert and Fosse "have shown great courage and acted in accordance with the best medical traditions, namely by helping the oppressed".[^cite_note-vg10-146][^cite_note-nordlys-gilbert-kritikk-147] Støre has condemned Israeli [occupation of Palestinian land](2781576.md) as contrary to international law.[^cite_note-148] In 2015, Støre said a Labour government would [recognise the State of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_the_State_of_Palestine).[^cite_note-149]

#### China

In a 2015 [BBC](19344654.md) interview, Støre was criticized by Geir Lundestad, the Nobel Prize Committee Secretary, for trying to dissuade the [Norwegian Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee) from awarding the prize to Chinese dissident [Liu Xiaobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Xiaobo) in 2010, for fear it would strain Norway's business relationship with Beijing. The Nobel committee, chaired by former prime minister [Thorbjørn Jagland](192081.md), ignored the warnings and honored Liu.[^cite_note-150] Subsequently, Støre ruled out apologising to China over the award.[^cite_note-151]

#### Libya

As foreign minister, Støre oversaw Norway's participation in the 2011 NATO-led [military intervention in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya). Norway's participation in the intervention was controversial on the far–left, and the communist party [Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_\(Norway\)) reported him to the police for alleged war crimes.[^cite_note-152]

## Other activities

He is a member of the board of trustees for the [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group).[^cite_note-153]

Støre is a supporter of the English [Premier League](11250.md) club [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.).[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]
