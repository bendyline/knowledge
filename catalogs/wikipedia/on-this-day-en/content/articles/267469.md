**Surigao del Sur** ([Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_language): *Probinsya nan Surigao del Sur*; [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Habagatang Surigao*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Timog Surigao*), officially the **Province of Surigao del Sur**, is a [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga) [region](216786.md) in [Mindanao](20546.md). Its capital is [Tandag City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandag_City) while [Bislig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislig) is the most populous city in the province. Surigao del Sur is situated at the eastern coast of Mindanao and faces the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea) to the east.

## Etymology

See also: [List of Philippine provincial name etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_provincial_name_etymologies)

There are two hypotheses on the original meaning of "Surigao" among linguists, depending on the original root word. If the root word was taken to be *sulig* ("sprout" or "spring up"), then Surigao may have derived from suligao ("spring water"), likely referring to the Surigao River (known as "Suligaw" in [Mandaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaya_language)) that empties at the northern tip of the island of [Mindanao](20546.md).[^cite_note-sur3-5][^cite_note-sur1-6] Early historical accounts record the name of the river as Suligao, Surigao, or Zurigan.[^cite_note-taw2-7]

Another possibility is that it is derived from Visayan surogao or *suyogao*, meaning "water current". From *suyog* (also *sulog* or surog), "current"; cf. [Sinulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinulog), [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu), and [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taus%C5%ABg_people) (Suluk).[^cite_note-8]

## History

Further information: [Surigao (province)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\))



An old map showing the current territories of the province as part of the [historical province of Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\))

### Early history

In precolonial times, the region of Surigao was inhabited by the [Visayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_people) [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_people) people in the coastal areas, as well as [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad_people) groups in the interiors like the [Mandaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaya), [Mansaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansaka), [Mamanwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamanwa) and [Manobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manobo).[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-SurigaodelSur-9]

### Spanish colonial era

During the [Spanish Occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1521%E2%80%931898\)) in 1860, six military districts were created in Mindanao, with Surigao and Agusan forming the "East District". In 1870, the district was renamed to "Distrito de Surigao".

### American colonial era

In 1901, Distrito de Surigao became [chartered province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\)). Agusan became an [independent province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_\(province\)) in 1907 during the [American era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)), when it was separated from Surigao.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-SurigaodelSur-9]

### Philippine independence

#### Foundation

Surigao del Sur was created as the 56th Philippine province on June 19, 1960, through *[Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) 2786*, separated from its mother province, [Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\)), on September 18, 1960.[^cite_note-Chan_Robles-RA2786-10]

At the time of its inception, it was classified as 4th Class province with an annual income of over [₱](227672.md)300,000.00. Seven years later, because of rapid increase of revenue collection particularly from the logging ventures, it has been reclassified as Ist Class B and in 1980 as Ist Class A with an estimated annual income of around ₱13,000,000. It has been reclassified as 2nd Class with a revenue adding up to ₱315,888,300.63.

Recaredo B. Castillo was the appointed first governor and subsequently elected governor while Vicente L. Pimentel was the first elected congressman.

The province was formed with 13 municipalities. Six more were added, raising the number to 19 with [Tandag](877402.md) as the capital. Two of its municipalities have been elevated to cities; the first was [Bislig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislig) City.

### Contemporary

The capital town [Tandag](877402.md) became a component city by virtue of *Republic Act No. 9392* which sought to convert the town into a city. The law was ratified on June 23, 2007. However, the cityhood status was lost twice in the years 2008 and 2010 after the [LCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Cities_of_the_Philippines) questioned the validity of the cityhood law. The cityhood status was reaffirmed after the [court](1335534.md) finalized its ruling on February 15, 2011 which declared the cityhood law constitutional.

House Bill No. 2336 of the 19th Congress seeks to separate the southern part of the province to be formed as the new Province of *Surigao Oriental*. Once the bill is signed into law it will be approved and ratified in a referendum.

## Geography



[Tandag Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandag_Airport)

Surigao del Sur is located along the northeastern coast of [Mindanao](20546.md) facing the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea) between 125°40' to 126°20' east longitudes and 7°55' and 9°20' north latitudes. It is bounded on the northwest by the province of [Surigao del Norte](267461.md), on the southeast by [Davao Oriental](296986.md), on the west and southwest by [Agusan del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Norte) and [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur).

Situated west is the [Diwata Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Diwata), isolating the province from the rest of Mindanao. To the east lies the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea). The [Mindanao Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao_Deep), one of the deepest trenches in the world, is situated a few kilometers east of the coastline.[^cite_note-OoCiies-SurigaodelSur-11][^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-SurigaodelSur-9]

### Land area



Ricefield in Cantilan

The land area of the province is 4,932.70 square kilometres (1,904.53 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-12] representing 27.75 percent of the total land area of Caraga Administrative Region and about 5.14 percent and 1.74 percent of the total land area of Mindanao and Philippines, respectively. The province is elongated in shape, extending from the northeastern portion at Carrascal to the southernmost municipality of Lingig. It is approximately 300 kilometres (190 mi) in length and 50 kilometres (31 mi) at its widest point which runs from [Cagwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagwait,_Surigao_del_Sur) to [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel,_Surigao_del_Sur).

Municipal-wise, San Miguel has the biggest land area accounting for 11.31% (558 square kilometers) of the total provincial land area while [Bayabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayabas,_Surigao_del_Sur) has the smallest constituting only about 2.39% (117.84 square kilometers).[^cite_note-nscb-12]

Of the 5,230.50 square kilometres (2,019.51 sq mi) land, only 1,703.72 square kilometres (657.81 sq mi) or 32.22 percent are classified as alienable and disposable (A and D) while 3,583.523 square kilometres (1,383.606 sq mi) or 67.78 percent are forest land. Tagbina has the biggest share of alienable and disposable land with 234.21 square kilometres (90.43 sq mi) or about 56.51 percent of its land area followed by Hinatuan with 202.52 square kilometres (78.19 sq mi) or 63.56 percent of its land area.



The Britania Group of Islands

Of the 3,583.523 square kilometres (1,383.606 sq mi) of forest land, 636.076 square kilometres (245.590 sq mi) are protection forest, 2,582.43 square kilometres (997.08 sq mi) production forest, 12.68 square kilometres (4.90 sq mi) are non-forest agriculture and 352.337 square kilometres (136.038 sq mi) are for non-forest mining. As of today, the province still has vast areas of remaining old growth and mossy forest.

### Climate

The province falls under Type II [climate of the Philippines](18906679.md), characterized by rainfall distributed throughout the year, although there is a distinct rainy season which begins from the month of November and ends in March. However, the climatic behavior of the province for the past few years has shown variations wherein the onset of the rainy seasons no longer occurs on the usual time. Months with low rainfall are from July to October with September as the driest month. Wet months are from November to June with January as the wettest month.

Surigao del Sur is one of the top 20 most vulnerable provinces to [climate change in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-13]

The population of Surigao del Sur in the 2024 census was 648,858 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–Caraga-15] with a density of 130 inhabitants per square kilometre or 340 inhabitants per square mile.

The province is home to the [Kamayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamayo_people), [Agusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_people), and the eponymous [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_people) peoples, with the latter ethnolingustically related to the Visayan ethnic groups (though geographically native in Mindanao). Their dances are showcased in the local festival, "[Sirong Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirong_Festival)", held especially during the town fiesta of [Cantilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilan). The Sirong Festival depicts the early Christianization of the early Cantilan inhabitants where the natives tried to defend their land against Visayan invaders. Other residents in the province include the migrants (and their native-born descendants) from [Ilocandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocandia), [Cagayan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley), [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region), [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon), [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon), [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro), [Marinduque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinduque) and [Bicolandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region) in [Luzon](69375.md) and [Panay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panay), [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros), [Central](47863267.md) and [Western Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Visayas), as well as Chinese and white Europeans (eg. Spaniards and white Americans) and their native-born descendants.

The indigenous people of the province were largely Christianized during the early times of the Spanish conquest.

### Languages

The [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_language) and [Tandaganon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_language#Tandaganon) languages are spoken in most parts of Surigao del Sur (except in [Bislig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislig) and the towns of [Barobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barobo), [Hinatuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinatuan), [Lingig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingig) and [Tagbina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagbina), where most of the inhabitants speak [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) and their native [Kamayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamayo_language), a different language but one distantly related to Surigaonon, is spoken by the rest of the population), with the Cantilangnon dialect, a northern variety of Surigaonon that is very much similar to the dialect of Surigaonon as spoken in Surigao del Norte, being spoken mostly in the five northern municipalities of the province, namely [Carrascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrascal,_Surigao_del_Sur), [Cantilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilan,_Surigao_del_Sur), [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid,_Surigao_del_Sur), [Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen,_Surigao_del_Sur) and [Lanuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanuza,_Surigao_del_Sur) (or the municipal cluster called [Carcanmadcarlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcanmadcarlan)); these municipalities were once under a single Municipality of Cantilan before December 10, 1918.

Tagalog and English are also widely spoken throughout the province and used as the primary languages of education, business, and administration.

Coastal fishing in Cantilan

Surigao del Sur is one of the suppliers of rice, bananas and other tropical fruits. Copper, chromite and silver are also found here. Marine and aquaculture are abundant in the province, being primary livelihoods of the inhabitants as the province is well known for producing seafood and sea by-products.

### Mineral resources

Surigao del Sur is endowed with metallic minerals such as [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [gold](12240.md), [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite), [cobalt](24580536.md), [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) and lead zinc, as well as non-metallic ([limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) and [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar), clay diatomite/bentomite and coarse/fine aggregates). There are small and large scale mining activities in the province. Corporations operating in a large scale are the Marc Ventures Mining Development Corporation at Carrascal and Cantilan, operating in an area of 4,799 hectares (47.99 km<sup>2</sup>) within the [Diwata Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Diwata).[^cite_note-EJATLASOrg-Marcventure-28][^cite_note-ManilaTimes-DENR-29] The \[TP Construction and Mining Corporation, also in Carrascal, focuses on gold and nickel mining in an area of 35.64 square kilometres (13.76 sq mi) and 48.6916 square kilometres (18.80 sq mi), respectively. The Carac-an Development Corporation, also in Carrascal, has an area of 506.3764 square kilometres (195.51 sq mi). Small scale mining activities are found in the municipalities of Barobo, Carmen and San Miguel.

## Tourism

Bislig's main tourist attraction is the [Tinuy-an Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinuy-an_Falls),[^cite_note-MB-CaragaTouristArrivals-30] known as the little "[Niagara Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Falls) of the Philippines". It is a white water curtain that flows in three levels about 55 metres (180 ft) high and 95 metres (312 ft) wide.[^cite_note-SunStar-Tinuy-anFalls-31] Its unique natural formation once appeared in the International Travel Magazine. It is also known as the widest waterfall in the Philippines.

[Surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing) in Surigao del Sur is widely known and has been one of the local tourist attractions. This [extreme sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_sport) is often practiced in [Cantilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilan) and [Lanuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanuza,_Surigao_del_Sur).[^cite_note-MindaNews-Surfing-32] [Skimboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skimboarding) is also found in several municipalities, attracting tourists.
