An indirect presidential election was held in [Myanmar](19457.md) on 3 April 2026,[^cite_note-1] following the [2025–26 Myanmar general election](69631013.md). [Min Aung Hlaing](38278640.md), the country's military leader since the 2021 coup, won the election, while [Nyo Saw](72867108.md) and [Nan Ni Ni Aye](58423397.md) were elected as vice presidents.[^cite_note-Update-2] The winners were inaugurated on 10 April.

The election was the first since the [2018 Myanmar presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Myanmar_presidential_election) and the [2021 Myanmar coup d'état](66567997.md).[^cite_note-Nikkei-3] The nomination process for the [Presidential Electoral College](47097201.md) began on 30 March, and the election was held at the [Pyidaungsu Hluttaw](29739324.md), chaired by Speaker [Aung Lin Dwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aung_Lin_Dwe).[^cite_note-exgeneralbehind-4]

## Process

The [Presidential Electoral College](47097201.md) appoints the president, consisting of the [Pyithu Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyithu_Hluttaw) committee (elected MPs only), the [Amyotha Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyotha_Hluttaw) committee (elected MPs only), and the [Tatmadaw](20394.md) committee (serving military personnel appointed to both houses by the [commander-in-chief of Defence Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief_of_Defence_Services)). Each committee nominates one candidate.[^cite_note-5] [Aung Lin Dwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aung_Lin_Dwe) presided over the election in his capacity as [Speaker of the Pyidaungsu Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Pyidaungsu_Hluttaw).[^cite_note-exgeneralbehind-4]

## Potential candidates

Military ruler [Min Aung Hlaing](38278640.md) was expected by most analysts to be a candidate and likely winner, though he declined to reveal his intentions publicly before being nominated.[^cite_note-Nikkei-3][^cite_note-6] He was expected to step down as Commander-in-Chief before the nomination process began on March 30, as presidential candidates are constitutionally barred from concurrently holding another office,[^cite_note-Reuters-7] and did so on 30 March, with the NDSC appointing [Ye Win Oo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_Win_Oo) to replace him.[^cite_note-BBC-8]

## Candidates

The committees of the Pyithu Hluttaw and Amyotha Hluttaw convened on March 30, 2026, to decide on the nominations. The selection was made through a vote the following day, March 31, 2026.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Results

On 30 March 2026, Nan Ni Ni Aye was nominated for the vice presidency by the Amyotha Hluttaw[^cite_note-17] and was elected on 31 March with 117 votes,[^cite_note-18] becoming the first woman to hold the office in the country's history.[^cite_note-19] Also [Nyo Saw](72867108.md) and [Min Aung Hlaing](38278640.md) were also elected to vice-presidency, before one of them becomes president.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

## Inauguration



Min Aung Hlaing, Nyo Saw, and Nan Ni Ni Aye taking the oath of office at the Pyidaungsu Hluttaw, administered by Speaker Aung Lin Dwe, on 10 April 2026

On 10 April 2026, Min Aung Hlaing was inaugurated as president at the Pyidaungsu Hluttaw, alongside his two vice presidents.[^cite_note-25] That same day, he established a [new Union Government of Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Min_Aung_Hlaing_cabinet), appointing many individuals who had served in his previous ministries.[^cite_note-26]

## Political position of the candidates

[Min Aung Hlaing](38278640.md) is reportedly a supporter of [Burmese Way to Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_Way_to_Socialism),[^cite_note-27] while formerly supporting [privatization](24661.md).[^cite_note-28] The political positions of both [Nyo Saw](72867108.md) and [Nan Ni Ni Aye](58423397.md) are not known. They are members of the [Union Solidarity and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Solidarity_and_Development_Party), which supports Min Aung Hlaing and his [military junta](216815.md).[^cite_note-Irrawaddy-29][^cite_note-nikkei-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-ISEAS\:1-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]
