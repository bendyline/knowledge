**Drone warfare** is the use of [military robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_robot) and unmanned systems in [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warfare). The unmanned systems may be [remote controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telerobotics) by a pilot or have varying levels of [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_robot) during their mission. Types of unmanned systems and platforms include [unmanned combat aerial vehicles](337755.md) (UCAV) or weaponized commercial [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) (UAV), [unmanned surface vehicles](7808557.md) (USV) or [unmanned underwater vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_underwater_vehicle) (UUV), and [unmanned ground vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_ground_vehicle) (UGV).[^cite_note-1] [SUAVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniature_UAV) and [MAVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_air_vehicle) are man-portable UAVs and can be deployed for low-altitude, short-range support operations. Autonomous drones such as [autonomous aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_aircraft) and [LAWS](36973733.md) utilize [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_applications_of_artificial_intelligence) or pre-programmed algorithms. As of 2019, the following nations have been identified as having operational UCAVs:[^cite_note-2] [China](40602914.md), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aerial_vehicles_in_the_French_Armed_Forces), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aerial_vehicles_in_the_Hellenic_Armed_Forces), [India](14533.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Poland](22936.md), [Russia](25391.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), [the United States](3434750.md), [the United Kingdom](31717.md), and [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-the_guardian-5]&#x20;

Drones are an expendable [force multiplier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_multiplication) that may supplement–or reduce the necessity for–live personnel during operations. Military applications of drones range from reconnaissance tasks, *kamikaze* missions, logistical support, [bomb disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_ordnance_disposal), [training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_drone) and [medical evacuation](146346.md) to [electronic warfare](82272.md), [anti-air](146640.md), [anti-armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare), and [anti-personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-personnel_weapon) roles.[^cite_note-6] However drones are typically tasked with intelligence, [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance), [target acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_acquisition), and [reconnaissance](146911.md) ([ISTAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_surveillance_and_reconnaissance_aerial_vehicle)) missions, facilitating direct attacks on targets as part of a [kill chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_chain_\(military\)) or through [manned-unmanned teaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned-unmanned_teaming).[^cite_note-7] Aerial drone attacks can be conducted via purpose-built UCAVs that launch [ordnance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_ordnance) during a **drone strike**, by [loitering munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loitering_munitions) and [one-way attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_attack_drone) drones laden with a warhead that explodes on impact, or by civilian UAVs weaponized to deploy munitions or crash into a target.[^cite_note-Agence_France-Presse-2017-8] Larger drones can serve a "mothership" role by deploying sub-drones or by providing electronic warfare capabilities such as [radio relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_relay).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Heavy-lift drones may be used to [airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlift) supplies or evacuate wounded personnel across a battlefield.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Multiple drones can operate and attack simultaneously in a [drone swarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarm_robotics).[^cite_note-BBCthwarts-13]&#x20;

The [early years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_unmanned_aerial_vehicles) of the 21st century saw most drone strikes being [conducted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlefield_UAVs_of_the_United_States) by the US military using [air-to-surface missiles](1544116.md) against ground targets within countries such as [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_drone_strikes_in_Afghanistan), [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_intervention_in_Libya_\(2015%E2%80%932019\)), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strikes_in_Pakistan), [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_intervention_in_Somalia_\(2007%E2%80%93present\)), [Syria](43683196.md), and [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_airstrikes_on_Yemen) during the [war on terror](13425800.md).[^cite_note-14] Drone warfare evolved and proliferated quickly in the 2010s and 2020s, with countries such as [Azerbaijan](746.md),[^cite_note-The_Economist-15] China, [Iran](69129130.md), Israel, Russia, Turkey and Ukraine utilizing drones with increased ubiquity. Militant groups, such as the [Islamic State](9087364.md) and the [Houthis](17240332.md), and organized crime groups such as [Mexican cartels](82533550.md) likewise have used drones for attacks against adversaries and for logistical purposes.&#x20;

The [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) is "widely recognised as the world's first drone war"[^cite_note-auto-16] due to the large scale and high intensity of drone attacks, and the role of this experience in evolving the tactics of [modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_warfare) [conventional warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_warfare).[^cite_note-o268-17][^cite_note-f993-18][^cite_note-g513-19] Both armies have used a multitude of UAVs, including long-range fixed-wing drones and short range multirotor [FPV drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FPV_drone).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Ukraine became the first country to create a military branch exclusively dedicated to drone warfare—the [Unmanned Systems Forces](76045069.md)[^cite_note-auto1-22]—in June 2024, with Russia following soon with its [own Unmanned Systems Forces](81580694.md) in November 2025.[^cite_note-TASS12Nov25-23] The Russo-Ukrainian war demonstrated how drones have disrupted traditional military doctrines in a manner similar to how [gunpowder revolutionized warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_warfare), making them a "decisive" factor in all future conflicts.[^cite_note-24]&#x20;

## Commercial UCAVs



Weaponizing of [DJI Phantom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI_Phantom) commercial [videography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_videography) UAVs by the [Islamic State](9087364.md)

A **commercial UCAV** is any commercially-produced UAV that is modified aftermarket to carry such weapons as [guided bombs](3587974.md), [cluster bombs](79745.md), [incendiary devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incendiary_device), [air-to-surface missiles](1544116.md), [air-to-air missiles](320568.md), [anti-tank guided missiles](37517.md) or other types of [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition), [autocannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocannon) and [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_gun).[^cite_note-urlTurkey_is_getting_military_drones_armed_with_machine_guns_|_New_Scientist-25] These weaponized civilian drones may proceed to fire munition or a missile, drop explosives and crash into or [detonate above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_burst) vulnerable targets.[^cite_note-Agence_France-Presse-2017-8] Payloads could include explosives such as [hand grenades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_grenade), [mortar](324499.md) [shells](337004.md) and [IEDs](265112.md) or other dangerous materials such as shrapnel, chemical, radiological or biological hazards. These relatively cheap drones are also used for non-attack roles. For example, [multirotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirotor) FPV drones have been used extensively by both armies for [aerial reconnaissance](20338498.md) and [artillery spotting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_spotting) in the [Russo-Ukraine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukraine_War).[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

[Anti-UAV systems](146640.md#Anti-UAV_defenses) are being developed by states to counter the threat posed by commercial UCAVs.[^cite_note-27] According to James Rogers, an academic who studies drone warfare, "There is a big debate out there at the moment about what the best way is to counter these small UAVs, whether they are used by [hobbyists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobbyist) causing a bit of a nuisance or in a more sinister manner by a [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) actor."[^cite_note-28] This problem has split modern counter-UAS (C-UAS) development into two primary vectors: kinetic interception and [electronic warfare](82272.md) (EW) suppression. Kinetic solutions, which include automated laser systems, specialized net-interceptors, and rapid-fire munitions, offer definitive physical neutralization but often suffer from poor cost-to-damage ratios against low-cost commercial hardware. Conversely, non-kinetic electronic countermeasures focus on jamming [global navigation satellite systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_navigation_satellite_system) (GNSS) and severing radio frequency control links to trigger automated emergency landing sequences.[^cite_note-29]&#x20;

Key figures in the deal-making said the United States was aware of the arrangements and held off on drone attacks against the armed group, which was created by Osama bin Laden in 1988."[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]&#x20;

Under Biden, drone strikes reportedly decreased.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] A [Biden administration](65732174.md) [drone strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2021_Kabul_drone_strike) in [Kabul](16826.md), Afghanistan in August 2021 killed 10 civilians, including seven children.[^cite_note-45] Later, a drone strike killed [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) leader [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri).[^cite_note-46]&#x20;

In 2021, the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md)'s [Naval Forces Central Command](9880750.md) established Task Force 59 to further integrate armed and unarmed drones and [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_applications_of_artificial_intelligence) into maritime operations, particularly in the [Fifth Fleet](418867.md)'s [area of operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_of_operations).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-GARC-48] Task Force 59 helped expand the navy's procurement and deployment of [unmanned surface vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_surface_vessel), such as the [Saildrone Explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saildrone_\(company\)) and the [T38 Devil Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=T38_Devil_Ray\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-corsair-49]&#x20;

By mid-2025, reports emerged that the U.S. military was lagging behind in evolving its drone warfare capabilities, particularly its production and deployment of first-person view UAVs akin to those seen during the [Russo-Ukrainian war](72261128.md#Drones). A July 2025 memo by defense secretary [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md) urged military leadership to accelerate the adoption of drones among troops.[^cite_note-50] In September 2025 the U.S. envoy to [Ukraine](31750.md), [Keith Kellogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Kellogg), stated Ukraine had overtaken the U.S. in drone technology amid the Russo-Ukrainian war, as battlefield experience prompted continuous innovation in drones.[^cite_note-51] Defense industry executive Steve Siomi, the president of robotics defense company [Allen Control Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Allen_Control_Systems\&action=edit\&redlink=1), said China had overtaken the U.S. in responding to drone proliferation and argued that drone adoption made warfare more affordable for all combatants, including America's rivals.[^cite_note-52]&#x20;

In July 2025, the U.S. military revealed the [LUCAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_Uncrewed_Combat_Attack_System) [one-way attack drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_attack_drone) which was promoted as a low-cost counter to enemy drone warfare, costing \$35,000 per unit. LUCAS utilizes technology [reverse engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering) from [Iran](14653.md)'s [HESA Shahed 136](71747103.md).[^cite_note-53] During the [2026 Iran War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_War), the U.S. Navy deployed the [Global Autonomous Reconnaissance Craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Global_Autonomous_Reconnaissance_Craft\&action=edit\&redlink=1) (GARC) drone speedboat[^cite_note-GARC-48] and the autonomous Corsair USV, which successfully rescued two pilots after Iranian forces shot down their [Apache helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_helicopter).[^cite_note-corsair-49] Three Corsair USVs were also used to strike an Iranian sea port in [Bandar Abbas](464348.md), becoming the first-ever combat use of *kamikaze* sea drones by the U.S. military.[^cite_note-54]&#x20;

#### Effects

See also: [Public opinion about the United States drone attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_opinion_about_the_United_States_drone_attacks), [Civilian casualties from the United States drone strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_casualties_from_the_United_States_drone_strikes), and [PlayStation mentality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_mentality)

Scholarly opinions are mixed regarding the efficacy of drone strikes, particularly [targeted killings](33928336.md) conducted by UCAVs such as [MQ-1 Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Atomics_MQ-1_Predator) and [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md) drones. Some studies support that [decapitation strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapitation_strike) to kill a terrorist or insurgent group's leadership limits the capabilities of these groups in the future, while other studies contradict this. Drone strikes are successful at suppressing militant behavior, though this response is in anticipation of a drone strike rather than as a result of one. Data from the U.S. and Pakistan's joint counter-terrorism efforts show that militants will cease communication and attack planning to avoid detection and targeting.[^cite_note-55]&#x20;

Proponents of UCAV drone strikes assert that such strikes are largely effective in targeting specific combatants.[^cite_note-Byman-2013-56] Some scholars argue that drone strikes reduce the amount of [civilian casualties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_casualties_from_U.S._drone_strikes) and territorial damage when compared to other types of military force like large bombs.[^cite_note-Byman-2013-56] Military alternatives to drone strikes, such as raids and interrogations, can be extremely risky, time-consuming, and potentially ineffective. However relying on drone strikes does not come without risks, as U.S. drone usage sets an international precedent on [extraterritorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritorial_operation) and [extrajudicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing) killings.[^cite_note-Byman-2013-56]&#x20;

U.S. UCAVs regularly deploy the [AGM-114 Hellfire](38151.md) missile, which has the [R-9X](38151.md#Variants) variant that utilizes a [kinetic warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy_weapon) with pop-out blades instead of an explosive warhead, greatly reducing collateral damage while targeting specific people.[^cite_note-57]&#x20;

### Israel

Israel utilized drones in warfare since 1973, starting with usage in the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War) to conduct surveillance missions and counter Egyptian and Syrian [surface-to-air missile](182664.md) systems.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Israel uses drones extensively in warfare for surveillance and targeted attacks on enemies. In June 2025 it was reported the [Mossad](27619007.md) set up drone bases inside Iran as part of the preparations for war between the sides.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]&#x20;

Israel had become a leading developer and manufacturer of military drones by the 2020s, with drones composing of 9% of Israel's weapons exports by 2021.[^cite_note-79]&#x20;

### Myanmar

See also: [Myanmar Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Civil_War)

Drones revolutionized the [Myanmar civil war](13299430.md), with both the military junta and resistance forces (PDFs/EAOs) using them for surveillance and, primarily, to drop bombs, acting as a crucial, low-cost air force for rebels.&#x20;

The [Tatmadaw](20394.md) uses more sophisticated drones, including Chinese-made CH-3 and CH-4, along with [Shahed-136](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahed-136) "suicide" drones. They use these for surveillance and to guide artillery strikes, increasingly employing "kamikaze" or "suicide" drones that detonate on impact with targets. The military has countered the rebels by strengthening its electronic warfare capabilities, using for example, drone guns, and heavy jamming to disrupt rebel drone operations.&#x20;

Resistance groups commonly modify commercial agricultural drones for payloads, Initially, rebel forces used small, off-the-shelf quadcopters, but have shifted to larger, custom-made, or modified fixed-wing drones to drop bombs and deliver ordnance, with thousands of attacks documented. Groups like the [Karenni Nationalities Defense Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenni_Nationalities_Defense_Force) (KNDF) maintain dedicated units to build, repair, and 3D-print drones, often using techniques found on YouTube. Drones have been crucial for resistance fighters for taking over military bases, disrupting troop movements, and operating as a "homemade air force".&#x20;

October 2021 and June 2023, over 1,400 drone attacks were verified, with, in some cases, thousands of bombs dropped on military bases in a single month. Due to Chinese restrictions on exports of dual-use items, resistance groups have found it more challenging to acquire drones, shifting towards acquiring parts rather than pre-assembled units. The warfare is increasingly shifting toward faster, more durable FPV (First Person View) drones for precise, faster attacks.&#x20;

### Non-state actors

#### Hamas drone attacks

On 7 October 2023, [Hamas](13913.md) launched [incursions of southern Israel](74998317.md), using commercial drones to bomb Israeli guard towers before breaching the [border wall](478337.md). Videos of Israeli troops and a [Merkava IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkava_IV) tank being taken out by drones surfaced on the internet.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] In 2025, there was an increase in drone usage by Hamas in the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-82]&#x20;

#### Houthi drone attacks

Main articles: [Houthi–Saudi Arabian conflict](47695166.md), [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md), and [Houthi attacks on commercial vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_attacks_on_commercial_vessels)

On 25 March 2022, the [Houthis](17240332.md) launched coordinated drone and missile strikes on multiple Saudi energy facilities, including sites in Jeddah and Ras Tanura. The attacks temporarily disrupted oil production and caused fires at Aramco facilities.[^cite_note-83]&#x20;

Between late 2023 and July 2024, during the [Gaza War](74998519.md), the Houthis carried out over 100 attacks on commercial ships in the Red Sea and Bab al-Mandab Strait. These attacks used drones, missiles, and fast boats, targeting vessels linked to Israel, the U.S., and its allies.&#x20;

#### Islamic State drone attacks

Small drones and quadcopters have been used for strikes by the [Islamic State](9087364.md) in Iraq and Syria. A group of twelve or more have been piloted by specially trained pilots to drop munitions onto enemy forces. They have been able to evade ground defense forces.[^cite_note-ISISDrones-84]&#x20;

During the [battle for Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mosul_\(2016%E2%80%932017\)), the Islamic State was able to kill or wound dozens of Iraqi soldiers by dropping light explosives or 40-millimeter grenades from numerous drones attacking at the same time. Drone strikes were also used to destroy military supplies. Drone footage released by the Islamic State showed bombs being dropped on an ammunition facility located in [Deir ez-Zor](1429146.md), Syria, an area of contested control between the Islamic State and the Syrian government at the time.[^cite_note-85]&#x20;

In 2017, [FBI](11127.md) Director [Christopher Wray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Wray) stated at a Senate hearing that "We do know that terrorist organizations have an interest in using drones ... We have seen that overseas already with some frequency. I think that the expectation is that it is coming here imminently."[^cite_note-ISISDrones-84]&#x20;

Drone expert [Brett Velicovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Velicovich) discussed the dangers of the Islamic State utilizing off-the-shelf drones to attack civilian targets, claiming in an interview with [Fox News](11121.md) that it was only a matter of time before ISIS extremists use of drones to strike civilian targets would become more prevalent and sophisticated.[^cite_note-86]&#x20;

The overall success rate for drone strikes used by the Islamic State is unclear. The Islamic State may have used drones as a way to gather footage for propaganda purposes rather than for their military value.[^cite_note-87]&#x20;

### Thailand

Main article: [2025 Cambodia–Thailand border conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cambodia%E2%80%93Thailand_border_conflict)

On 24 July 2025, the [Royal Thai Armed Forces](30136.md) launched coordinated drone strikes against the [Royal Cambodian Armed Forces](2867590.md) in the disputed border area near [Ta Muen Thom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prasat_Ta_Muen_Thom) and [Ta Krabey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta_Krabey). Using FPV drones, quadcopters, and one-way [kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze) UAVs, Thai forces targeted Cambodian command posts, ammunition depots, artillery positions, and communication lines. Analysts described the campaign as one of the most prominent uses of commercial multirotor drones in a state-on-state conflict since the war in Ukraine, challenging assumptions that such drones are ineffective in dense jungle terrain.[^cite_note-Telegraph2025-89][^cite_note-RUSI-90]&#x20;

## Europe

### Russo–Ukrainian war (2022–present)

Further information: [Aerial warfare in the Russo-Ukrainian war § Drones](72261128.md#Drones)

See also: [Human safari (terror campaign)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_safari_\(terror_campaign\)), [Operation Spiderweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Spiderweb), and [Women drone operators in the Ukrainian military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_drone_operators_in_the_Ukrainian_military)



In the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md), use of Russian drones (unmanned aerial vehicles) increased about tenfold from early 2024 through summer 2025.

The [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) that began in 2022 is recognized as "the world's first drone war"[^cite_note-auto-16] due to both Russian and Ukrainian armies heavily integrating drones into daily combat and reconnaissance tasks, including for long-range bombing attacks and supporting ground assaults and offensives. In the early months of the war, Ukrainian forces extensively used the Turkish-made [Bayraktar TB2](62445596.md) UCAV to conduct strikes against Russian forces. Russian forces meanwhile launched waves of Iranian [HESA Shahed 136](71747103.md) drones during the [October 2022 missile strikes on Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2022_missile_strikes_on_Ukraine).[^cite_note-92] Ukraine and Russia have since developed their own [one-way attack drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_attack_drone) for both tactical and strategic-level attacks. The main roles of drones in the war, however, are in reconnaissance and [artillery spotting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_spotting). The ubiquity of drones spurred the development of anti-drone tactics; for example Russian sources claimed to have used a "Stupor anti-drone rifle" to jam the radio controls of Ukrainian drones.[^cite_note-93] In 2024, Ukraine became the first country in the world to create a [military branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) dedicated to drone warfare, known as the [Unmanned Systems Forces](76045069.md),[^cite_note-auto1-22] Russia followed suit in 2025 with the creation of the [Unmanned Systems Forces of Russia](81580694.md).[^cite_note-FDD12Nov25JohnHardie-94]&#x20;

Small commercial drones in Ukraine were first used during the [Donbas War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas_War), where [Ukrainian volunteer units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_volunteer_battalions) employed commercially available unmanned aerial vehicles for reconnaissance and surveillance. By 2016, volunteers were acquiring and modifying consumer drones for military use, using them to photograph enemy positions and provide real-time aerial imagery to Ukrainian forces.[^cite_note-95] One notable example was [Aerorozvidka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerorozvidka), a volunteer organization founded in 2014 that helped equip and support [Ukrainian volunteer battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_volunteer_battalions) with small commercial UAVs for aerial reconnaissance and surveillance, which were later adopted by the Ukrainian Armed Forces. This experience later contributed to the expansion of Aerorozvidka's drone operations, where it developed more advanced unmanned systems,[^cite_note-bpla.in.ua_27.11.2017-96] such as the [R18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerorozvidka_R18), an armed octocopter developed by the organization from 2017 and tested in combat in 2019.[^cite_note-ShoTam_22.06.2022-97] [Pro-Russian Separatist forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas) likewise began employing commercially available UAVs for battlefield reconnaissance, with some drones subsequently being adapted to carry and drop improvised munitions.[^cite_note-98] The experience gained during the conflict demonstrated the military potential of inexpensive commercial UAVs and contributed to their wider adoption during the [Russian Invasion of Ukraine in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Invasion_of_Ukraine).&#x20;

On 13 October 2022, the first recorded instance of an unarmed drone-on-drone combat encounter occurred above the [Donetsk](562951.md) region of Ukraine. A Ukrainian [DJI Mavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI_Mavic) quadcopter was recorded ramming a Russian drone of the same model, resulting in the latter crashing towards the surface below.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] Another instance of this aerial ramming tactic occurred on 24 November 2022, this time with the Russian DJI Mavic being recorded plummeting towards the ground after a collision with a Ukrainian drone.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] On 9 May 2023, a Russian conscript surrendered to (or rather via) a Ukrainian drone.[^cite_note-104] The average HESA Shahed 136 drone is worth about \$20,000. An [IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T) missile is worth about \$430,000 each in comparison. From 13 September until 17 October, open source information suggests that Ukraine has had to spend \$28.14 million on defending against these drones.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]&#x20;

Since at least September 2022, Ukraine has used black [naval drones](7808557.md), equipped with the [Starlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlink_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) satellite internet system, to carry attacks on the Russian [Black Sea fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) at the [Sevastopol Naval base](59166849.md).[^cite_note-\:11-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-\:23-109][^cite_note-\:19-110] The naval drones were at first assumed to be for reconnaissance, but appear to carry munitions and act as a bomb.[^cite_note-\:33-111] With experts noting that the sensors on the front of the naval drone could be used as a [laser range finder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_rangefinder) to help in targeting.[^cite_note-\:33-111] In late October 2022, seven of these drones were used to mount a [successful drone attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_drone_attack_on_the_Sevastopol_Naval_Base) on the Sevastopol Naval base.[^cite_note-\:11-107][^cite_note-112]&#x20;

On 13 October 2022, a Ukrainian [MiG-29](185653.md) became the first manned plane to go down during combat due to a drone. The pilot is claimed to have destroyed a [Shahed-136](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahed-136) drone with his cannon. The blast is believed to have brought the plane down and hospitalised the pilot.[^cite_note-113]&#x20;

In September 2023, Ukrainian troops were reportedly using cardboard drones with GoPro cameras for aerial reconnaissance.[^cite_note-114]&#x20;

Since the start of the war, approximately 30 companies in Ukraine have emerged to mass-produce drones for the war effort. The [Ukraine government](1372585.md) Ministry of Digital Transformation initiated the "Army of Drones" project and attempted to purchase up to 200,000 drones in 2023, aiming to deploy relatively cheap drones against large advantages Russia has had in military equipment. In 2023, they also sponsored several competitions where the "dozens of drone developers that have sprung up all over Ukraine" are invited to make simulated attacks on ground targets, or chase [fixed-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft) drones, or even participate in drone [dogfight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogfight) competitions.[^cite_note-cnn20230603-115] One new model that has been successful is the "[Baba Yaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baba_Yaga_\(aircraft\))" hexacopter, which can carry "44 pounds of payload".[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]&#x20;

From early 2024, Ukraine has started deploying manned aircraft, such as the [Aeroprakt A-22 Foxbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroprakt_A-22_Foxbat) and [Yakovlev Yak-52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-52), which uses snipers or machine guns to shoot down Russian drones.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] Ukrainian drones have also been given a variety of improvised modifications for the express purpose of attacking and countering Russian drones; these include an instance in July 2024 where an FPV drone with a stick mounted to it was used to attack and eventually destroy a [ZALA 421-16E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZALA_421-16) reconnaissance drone through repeated [aerial ramming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_ramming).[^cite_note-121] In 2024 [drones controlled through a tether with optical fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_optic_drone) became commonplace, thus avoiding radio jamming.[^cite_note-122]&#x20;

On 31 July 2024, a Russian [Mi-8 helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-28) was downed over occupied Donetsk, by a Ukrainian FPV drone, the first time a helicopter in combat was destroyed by a drone. The Mi-8 was believed to have been attacked near the ground either during landing or take off.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]&#x20;

In February 2025, Russian authorities reportedly discovered a plot in which a shipment of FPV drone headsets loaded with explosives were sent to Russian soldiers. Each headset had 10-15 grams of explosives and were programmed to detonate on activation. Officials compared it to the [2024 Lebanon electronic device attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Lebanon_electronic_device_attacks) by Israel.[^cite_note-125] Subsequent reports claimed 8 Russian FPV pilots lost their eyesight due to explosions between 4 and 7 February.[^cite_note-126]&#x20;

On 1 June 2025, Ukraine carried out [Operation Spiderweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Spiderweb). Dozens of FPV drones were smuggled into Russia in trucks, and were then launched from the trucks to strike Russian airbases, including [Belaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belaya_air_base) and [Olenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olenya_airbase), destroying multiple Russian strategic bombers.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]&#x20;

Fire at the [Moscow Oil Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Oil_Refinery) on June 18, 2026

Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian oil refineries have caused a [fuel crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Russian_fuel_crisis) in Russia.[^cite_note-129]&#x20;

Nevertheless, an article by a Ukrainian journalist published by the [Atlantic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Council) in December 2025 states: "The Kremlin strategy has focused on mass producing a limited range of models for use on the battlefield and in the bombardment of Ukrainian cities. This methodical approach has paid dividends. By the end of 2024, it was already becoming clear that the drone war was turning in Russia's favor. This trend has only intensified over the past year".[^cite_note-auto-16]&#x20;

The largest drone strike of the war was on 7 September 2025, when Russia launched 810 drones and 13 missiles at Ukraine, of which 747 drones and four missiles were intercepted, while 54 drones struck targets.[^cite_note-130]&#x20;

On 9 September 2025, over a dozen Russian drones [breached Polish airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Russian_drone_incursion_into_Poland), prompting a NATO [Quick Reaction Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_Reaction_Alert) and Poland invoking [Article 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty#Article_4) of the NATO treaty. At least four of the drones were confirmed shot down. On 18 September Polish and Ukrainian officials announced that the two countries would establish joint military training and production programs, as the Ukrainian military had emerged as a major pioneer in drone warfare in the Russo-Ukrainian war.[^cite_note-131]&#x20;

By 2026, some Ukrainian media analysis framed long-range drone and missile-drone strikes as part of a broader shift from tactical drone use near the front line toward attacks on Russian logistical infrastructure, including fuel supplies, transport nodes, ports, warehouses, repair facilities and air defence systems.[^cite_note-132] Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces began the [middle strike campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_strike_campaign) in early April 2026 to degrade Russian logistics in [Russian-occupied territories of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine) heavily utilizing one-way attack drones.[^cite_note-Sleiman-133] Around this time, Russia reportedly began mass deployment of AI-enabled autonomous versions of its "Molniya" one-way attack drone, exhibiting a reduced [radar cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross_section), smaller [IR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) signature and a negligible electronic emissions due to not needing to communicate with a UAV operator.[^cite_note-134]&#x20;

## Anti-drone warfare

See also: [Interceptor drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interceptor_drone), [Directed-energy weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed-energy_weapon), [Electronic warfare](82272.md), and [Anti-aircraft warfare](146640.md)



Metal [anti-drone mesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-drone_mesh) protecting gas infrastructure in [Moscow](19004.md), November 2025



[German soldier](288188.md) displays a Dronebuster [counter-UAV weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed-energy_weapon), December 2025

As drone warfare evolved, anti-drone warfare also matured. Drones can be intercepted kinetically (projectiles, missiles, barriers or another drone) or non-kinetically ([directed-energy weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed-energy_weapon), hacking, or communications jamming).[^cite_note-140] Buildings, vehicles, roads, [weapons platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_platform), [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare) and fortifications can be modified with materials such as wire mesh, netting, metal caging or chain-link fencing to defend against *kamikaze* drone attacks.[^cite_note-141] [Decoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dummy) may be used to mislead and divert incoming drone attacks or deceive enemy drone surveillance.[^cite_note-142]&#x20;

Aerial drones can be shot down from the ground using [anti-drone guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed-energy_weapon), [anti-aircraft guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_anti-aircraft_guns), missile interceptors, [laser beams](4714516.md) and occasionally [small arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_arms_and_light_weapons) fire (depending on the drone's altitude). UAVs can be intercepted air-to-air by [interceptor drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interceptor_drone), helicopters, fighter aircraft or by otherwise physically obstructing the drone's flight path. Electronic countermeasures include signal jamming, spoofing and hi-jacking, often by means of in-flight hacking. Larger UAVs that share similar flight profiles to manned aircraft, such as the [MQ-1 Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-1_Predator), remain prone to being shot down via more traditional methods such as [MANPADs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MANPAD) or [SAMs](182664.md).[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] During the Russo-Ukrainian war, soldiers began shooting down approaching UAVs using [shotguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_of_shotguns_against_drones), machine guns and rifle fire.[^cite_note-145] [Trainer aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainer_aircraft) such as the [Yak-52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-52) were repurposed to intercept enemy UAVs.[^cite_note-146]&#x20;

Surface drones such as UGVs and USVs can also be neutralized kinetically or non-kinetically. A UGV may be vulnerable to [terrain obstacles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_obstacles) as simple as a [ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_trench) or [booby trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booby_trap) and can be attacked by shorter-range weapons such as [land mines](18172.md), [anti-armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare) weaponry and small arms fire. USVs can be neutralized by various [anti-surface warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-surface_warfare) methods including [naval mines](22102.md), weapons mounted on defending ships and aircraft, and obstacles on the water's surface. [Underwater drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_underwater_vehicle) may be countered by [underwater warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_warfare) methods ranging from underwater nets to more sophisticated [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed_warfare) and [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) tactics.&#x20;

[Anti-drone mesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-drone_mesh), netting or wooden logs[^cite_note-147] can be used to protect vehicles, buildings, roads, seaports and weapons and radar systems against *kamikaze* drone attacks or to obfuscate targeting.[^cite_note-148] [Reactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactive_armour), [spaced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaced_armour), [slat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slat_armor) or [improvised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvised_vehicle_armour) [armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_armour) can also be applied to vehicles to defend against *kamikaze* drone threats.&#x20;
