The **South Africa national soccer team** represents [South Africa](17416221.md) in men's international [soccer](10568.md) and is run by the [South African Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Football_Association), the governing body for [soccer in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_in_South_Africa). Nicknamed **Bafana Bafana** (lit. "Young Men" in [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_language)), the team plays at various stadiums around the country. The team, a member of both [FIFA](11049.md) and the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF), is one of the most well-supported national sides in Africa,[^cite_note-5] and are recognised by their traditional green and gold kits.

Having played their first match in 1906, South Africa returned to the world stage in 1992, after sixteen years of being banned from [FIFA](11049.md), and forty years of effective suspension due to the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) system.[^cite_note-6] South Africa have qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) four times: [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2026](36581929.md), and qualifying as hosts of the 2010 tournament, becoming the first African nation to do so. Despite defeating [France](250197.md) 2–1 in their final game of the group stage, they failed to progress from the first round of the tournament, becoming the first host nation in the history of the FIFA World Cup to exit in the group stage, followed by [Qatar](1111350.md) in 2022.[^cite_note-7] As of August 2026, the team is ranked 54th in [FIFA](11049.md) International Rankings.

South Africa's most significant achievement came in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_African_Cup_of_Nations) when they hosted and won the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) (AFCON). They reached the final again in 1998 but were defeated by [Egypt](8087628.md). Later in 2000, they earned a bronze medal (third place) by defeating [Tunisia](30188.md) (2-2 a.e.t, 4-3 on penalties) and in 2023 earned another bronze medal (third place) by defeating [DR CONGO](1030243.md) (0-0 a.e.t; 6-5 on penalties)

### Pre-1992

Football first arrived in South Africa through colonialism in the late nineteenth century, as the game was popular among British soldiers.[^cite_note-8] From the earliest days of the sport in South Africa until the end of apartheid, organised soccer was affected by the country's system of racial segregation. The all-white Football Association of South Africa (FASA) was formed in 1892, while the South African [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_Africans) Football Association (SAIFA), the South African [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_speaking_peoples_of_South_Africa) Football Association (SABFA) and the South African [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black) Football Association (SACFA) were founded in 1903, 1933 and 1936 respectively.



The South African team that toured South America in 1906. They played 12 matches with only one defeat.

In 1903 the SAFA re-affiliated with [the English Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) after the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War) between the British Empire and the Boer state. There was a plan to play a tournament held in Argentina, with South Africa and [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) as guest teams, but it was not carried out. Nevertheless, South Africa traveled to South America in 1906 to play a series of friendly matches there.[^cite_note-before-9]

South Africa played [a total of 12 matches in South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_football_clubs_tours_to_South_America), winning 11 with 60 goals scored and only 7 conceded. Opponents included [Belgrano A.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrano_Athletic_Club), the [Argentina national team](454699.md), a combined [Liga Rosarina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Rosarina_de_Football), [Estudiantes (BA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estudiantes_de_Buenos_Aires) and [Quilmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilmes_Atl%C3%A9tico_Club).[^cite_note-10] The only team that defeated South Africa was Argentina's [Alumni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumni_Athletic_Club) 1–0 at [Sociedad Sportiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociedad_Sportiva_Argentina) stadium in Buenos Aires, on 24 June, although the South Africans would take revenge on 22 July, defeating them 2–0.[^cite_note-iwa-11] The players were exclusively white, civil servants, government employees, bankers and civil engineers. Seven of the 15 players were born in South Africa and 8 originated from England and Scotland.[^cite_note-before-9]

South Africa was one of four African nations to attend FIFA's 1953 congress, at which the four demanded, and won, representation on the FIFA executive committee.[^cite_note-Goldblatt493-12] Thus the four nations (South Africa, [Ethiopia](187749.md), [Egypt](8087628.md) and [Sudan](27421.md)) founded the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) in 1956,[^cite_note-Goldblatt493-12] and the South African representative, Fred Fell, sat at the first meeting as a founding member. It soon became clear however that South Africa's constitution prohibited racially mixed teams from competitive sport, and so they could only send either an all-black side or an all-white side to the planned [1957 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_African_Cup_of_Nations). This was unacceptable to the other members of the Confederation, and South Africa was disqualified from the competition, however, some sources say that they withdrew voluntarily.[^cite_note-13]

At the second CAF conference in 1958, South Africa were formally expelled from CAF. The all-white FASA were admitted to [FIFA](11049.md) in the same year, but in August 1960 it was given an ultimatum of one year to fall in line with the non-discriminatory regulations of FIFA. On 26 September 1961 at the annual FIFA conference, the South African association was formally suspended from FIFA. [Sir Stanley Rous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Rous), president of [The Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) of England and a champion of South Africa's FIFA membership, was elected FIFA President a few days later. Rous was adamant that sport, and FIFA in particular, should not embroil itself in political matters and against fierce opposition, he continued to resist attempts to expel South Africa from FIFA. The suspension was lifted in January 1963 after a visit to South Africa by Rous in order to investigate the state of soccer in the country.[^cite_note-14]

Rous declared that if the suspension were not lifted, soccer there would be discontinued, possibly to the point of no recovery. The next annual conference of FIFA in October 1964 took place in Tokyo and was attended by a larger contingent of representatives from African and Asian associations and here the suspension of South Africa's membership was re-imposed. In 1976, after the [Soweto uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soweto_uprising), they were formally expelled from FIFA.[^cite_note-15]

In 1991, when the apartheid system was beginning to be demolished, a new multi-racial South African Football Association was formed, and admitted to FIFA – and thus finally allowing South Africa to enter the qualifying stages for subsequent World Cups.[^cite_note-16]

### 1992–2000

After spending nearly two decades in international isolation, the South African national team played its first game on 7 July 1992, beating [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) 1–0 at [Kings Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park_Stadium) in [Durban](313650.md).[^cite_note-17]

The team entered the [1994 Cup of Nations qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) and was placed in Group 5, along with [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_national_football_team), [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team). They won one game, away to Mauritius, and drew with Mauritius and Zimbabwe at home. The team finished third in the group and failed to qualify.

For the [1994 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_first_round) South Africa was placed in Group D, along with [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team), [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team). Libya withdrew from the qualifiers. South Africa finished second in the group, and failed to qualify for the next stage of qualifying.

In 1996, a mere five years after readmission, South Africa achieved their finest moment when they hosted and won the [1996 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_African_Cup_of_Nations), in the process of reaching 16th in the [FIFA rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings).[^cite_note-18]

The shock firing of AFCON-winning coach [Clive Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Barker_\(soccer\)) did little to slow momentum as they followed their 1996 success with a second-place finish at the [1998 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) behind Egypt under [Jomo Sono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomo_Sono). The team had earned their first appearance at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), qualifying for [France '98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup). Under head coach [Philippe Troussier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Troussier), South Africa suffered a 3–0 defeat to hosts [France](250197.md) in their World Cup debut, and drew against [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and [Asian](715249.md) juggernauts [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) 1–1 and 2–2 respectively on their way to an early exit.

### 2000s

Under newly hired coach [Trott Moloto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trott_Moloto), South Africa started the decade off strongly, finishing third in the [2000 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Africa_Cup_of_Nations); despite this, another coaching change was made. Portuguese [Carlos Queiroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Queiroz) was hired as the man to take South Africa to the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup). South Africa qualified for the [2002 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and were eliminated in the quarter-finals against hosts [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team). In the run-up to the World Cup, in which South Africa had easily qualified as winners of [Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_CAF_final_round#Group_E), Queiroz left his post as head coach amid increasing behind-the-scenes political wrangling.

Former legend Jomo Sono was brought back in a caretaker capacity to lead South Africa in Korea/Japan. Placed in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B) along with [Spain](609825.md), [Paraguay](804025.md) and debutants [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team), South Africa earned a 2–2 draw against Paraguay in their opening game with a last-gasp penalty from [Quinton Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinton_Fortune) and followed that up with their first-ever victory at the finals with a 1–0 win over Slovenia. South Africa headed into their final game against Spain second in the group and three points clear of Paraguay and Slovenia. In arguably the game of the group, in which five goals were scored inside the hour, South Africa twice came from a goal down in an agonising 3–2 defeat to the Spanish. In the other game, which kicked off at the same time, Paraguay found themselves beating Slovenia 3–1, with Paraguay's goal difference enough for them to surpass South Africa.

South Africa would go through 4 head coaches between the 2002 and [2006 FIFA World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup): [Ephraim Mashaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephraim_Mashaba) (2002–2004), [April Phumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Phumo) (2004), [Stuart Baxter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Baxter) (2004–2005), [Ted Dumitru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Dumitru) (2005–2006) and caretaker [Pitso Mosimane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitso_Mosimane) (2006). None of them were able to match the success seen in the late 1990s and early 2000s, as South Africa failed to progress beyond the group stages in either the [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) or [2006 Africa Cups of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), failing to register a single goal in the latter, with South Africa failing to qualify for the World Cup. After being placed in [Group 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_CAF_second_round#Group_2) for qualifying, South Africa missed the tournament in Germany by finishing behind [Ghana](1029620.md).



South Africa hosting the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) against Uruguay

Former [World Cup winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) and [Brazil national team](149286.md) coach [Carlos Alberto Parreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Parreira) was approached for the job, and he accepted. He signed a R100 million contract covering four years. His term as manager started 1 January 2007 targeting the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md), which South Africa had been awarded the right to host. He resigned as coach in April 2008 due to family reasons. [Joel Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Santana) was hand-picked by Parreira to succeed him and he was signed to coach the team until 2010.[^cite_note-19]

The World Cup hosts failed to qualify for the [2010 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and Parreira would return in time for the [2009 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) – also hosted by South Africa. The national team would see an improvement in performance, holding its own against eventual world champions Spain and perennial powerhouse Brazil, the latter requiring an 88th-minute goal to overcome South Africa in the semi-finals. South Africa would ultimately finish 4th, following a thrilling 3–2 loss to the Spanish in the third-place match.

As World Cup hosts, South Africa was placed in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_A), along with [Mexico](808402.md), [Uruguay](679782.md) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_final) runners-up [France](250197.md). South Africa played the first game, which opened the World Cup 2010 tournament with a 1–1 draw against Mexico after taking the lead through a long-range [Siphiwe Tshabalala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siphiwe_Tshabalala) strike. A heavy 3–0 defeat in the second group game against Uruguay and a Mexican win over France left South Africa with a mountain to climb in order to progress beyond the group stage. Despite defeating France, elimination in the group stage made South Africa become the first host to fail to progress beyond the first round of the World Cup.

Following the conclusion of the World Cup and the expiration of his contract, Carlos Alberto Parreira announced his retirement from coaching. As part of his contract, Parreira had also drawn up a plan of recommendations extending as far as soccer development at grassroots level that SAFA was encouraged to review in the hopes of reviving the fortunes of the national football team.

### 2010s

South Africa failed to qualify for the [Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) finals after the rules were misread.[^cite_note-20] They ultimately played for and achieved a draw at home in [Nelspruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelspruit) against Sierra Leone in a game they needed to win, when news about Niger trailing in Egypt was received, leading to the qualification of Niger at their expense, and also at Sierra Leone's. They then celebrated at the end as if they had qualified, making it the second time they would be so embarrassed in 4 years after qualifying as a lucky 3rd and last best runners-up for the [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification#Group_11) edition, with [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) catapulting over South Africa as Group winners after a 3–1 win in [Cape Town](6653.md) – and thereby gaining an automatic qualifying ticket on the head-to-head record with the South Africans erroneously thinking goal difference would be the primary tie-breaker.[^cite_note-21]

The [SABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Broadcasting_Corporation) also announced that the team had qualified and the [SAFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Football_Association) president [Kirsten Nematandani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Nematandani) then congratulated the team on TV before realisation dawned.[^cite_note-22] [SAFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Football_Association) said they would appeal to [CAF](715265.md) but the appeal was later withdrawn.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

South Africa continued to disappoint into 2012, opening the year with a lacklustre 0–0 draw away to [2012 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) co-hosts [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea_national_football_team). This would be the start of a run that would see *Bafana Bafana* rack up 6 successive draws to start the year. The run of draws included 1–1, 0–0 and 1–1 draws at home to [African champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team), Ghana and [Senegal](1017436.md) respectively in [International Friendlies](977090.md). Following a 1–1 draw at home to [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia_national_football_team) in a [2014 World Cup qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_CAF_second_round), head coach Pitso Mosimane was fired.[^cite_note-25] The draw increased South Africa's streak to 7 games without a win. Mosimane was replaced as head coach by [Steve Komphela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Komphela) on an interim basis while SAFA interviewed potential candidates to fill the post on a permanent basis.

In his first game in charge as interim head coach, Komphela oversaw yet another uninspired performance in South Africa's second World Cup qualifier against [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana_national_football_team) as they drew yet again, extending their winless run to 8 games. *Bafana Bafana* finally tasted victory when they defeated [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon_national_football_team) 3–0 at home in a friendly, ending their winless streak.

Following the [crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_Civil_War) in [Libya](17633.md) throughout 2011, CAF made the decision to move the [2013 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) from Libya – who were the original hosts – to South Africa. Libya was then given the right to host the [2017 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) of the tournament, which was originally awarded to South Africa.[^cite_note-26] The 2013 hosts were seeded and drawn in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_Group_A) along with [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team), [Morocco](1024865.md) and tournament debutants [Cape Verde](1205575.md).[^cite_note-27]

South Africa faced Group B runners-up [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team) in the quarterfinals, being eliminated from the tournament 3–1 on penalties.

They received a [First round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_CAF_first_round) bye when the [qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) process for the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) began on 30 July 2011. For the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_CAF_second_round) of the qualifying process, South Africa was placed in Pot 1 as a top-seeded nation for the draw from where they were drawn into Group A, along with neighbours [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana_national_football_team), the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic_national_football_team) and [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia_national_football_team).

On 19 June 2013 it was reported in the South African press that Ethiopia had fielded an ineligible player against South Africa, who would possibly win 3–0 as a result.[^cite_note-28] Despite this, Ethiopia were never punished, and South Africa failed to qualify for the tournament. &#x20;

### 2020s

Under [Molefi Ntseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molefi_Ntseki), South Africa failed to qualify for the [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations). [Molefi Ntseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molefi_Ntseki) was promptly sacked and [Hugo Broos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Broos) was announced as his replacement.

[Qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) began with South Africa in a group with Ghana, Zimbabwe, and Ethiopia. South Africa got off to a promising start, beating a weakened Ghana side 1–0 at home on Matchday 2 and going to the top of the group. South Africa remained at the top of the group until the final matchday when they lost 0–1 away to Ghana after a dubious penalty was awarded to Ghana in the first half. South Africa ultimately finished second to Ghana on goals scored, narrowly missing out on qualification for the final 10-team playoff round to qualify for the World Cup. South Africa qualified to the 2023 Africa Cup of Nations after beating Liberia 2–1.[^cite_note-35]

The [2023 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) was held in January 2024 in Côte d'Ivoire. South Africa was drawn into a group with [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team), [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team), and [Tunisia](1029953.md). They started their campaign with a 2–0 loss against Mali, followed by a 4–0 win against Namibia, and 0–0 draw against Tunisia. They would finish second in their group and qualify for the knockout stages. In the Round of 16, South Africa would pull off a shocking upset and defeated [Morocco](1024865.md), a team many had said would win the entire competition, and who also placed fourth at the [2022 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Cup) just a year earlier. In the quarter finals, Bafana Bafana would play against [Cabo Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabo_Verde_national_football_team), which ended in a 0–0 draw and went to penalties, in which South Africa would win the shootout 2–1. Captain [Ronwen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronwen_Williams) would receive global attention after saving 4 Cabo Verdean shots in the shootout, as well as for his standout performances earlier in the match and against Morocco. In the semi-finals, South Africa would play against rivals [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team). The match would end 1–1, and South Africa would fall to the Super Eagles 4–2 on penalties. In the third place match, South Africa would face [DR Congo](1030243.md), who Bafana would defeat 6–5 on penalties. It was South Africa's highest finish at AFCON since the 2000 edition of the tournament, where they also finished third.

In qualifying for the 2026 World Cup, South Africa were drawn into a group with Nigeria, [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_national_football_team), [Rwanda](1145307.md), [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team), and [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho_national_football_team). Early on, Nigeria would lead the group though Bafana remained close behind them, as did Benin. As the qualifying campaign progressed, Nigeria would slowly start to capitulate, and South Africa's results would start picking up, eventually taking the top spot in the group in 2024. Despite being docked three points in qualifying after fielding an ineligible player, South Africa qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) in October 2025 after defeating Rwanda 3–0, ending a 16 year gap since their last appearance, the longest in team history and their first successful qualifying campaign since 2002.[^cite_note-36]

On 5 December 2025, South Africa were drawn into Group A of the [2026 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Cup), playing against [Mexico](808402.md), [South Korea](1018627.md), and the [Czech Republic](729739.md).

In December 2025, South Africa competed in the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md), hosted by [Morocco](19291.md), and finished 2nd in Group B, after 2 wins (2–1 win against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team) and 3–2 win against [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team)) and a 1–0 loss against [Egypt](1022953.md), who would ultimately finish 4th at the tournament. South Africa had a short knockout journey, as the lost their Round of 16 tie against [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team), 2–1, and exited the tournament. As of 19 January 2026, South Africa gained one spot in the [FIFA Men's Rankings](719095.md), climbing to 60th. In their AFCON win against Angola, South Africa ended a 21-year wait for an opening match victory.

In June 2026, South Africa began World Cup group play with a 2–0 defeat against Mexico at [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md) in the tournament's opening fixture. They would then draw Czechia 1–1 at [Mercedes-Benz Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_Stadium) thanks to a late penalty from [Teboho Mokoena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teboho_Mokoena_\(soccer,_born_1997\)), and beat South Korea 1–0 at [Estadio BBVA](17130610.md) via a goal from [Thapelo Maseko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thapelo_Maseko), qualifying for the knockout stages for the first time in the country's history. South Africa were eliminated from the 2026 FIFA World Cup at [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) after a 1–0 defeat to [Canada](723688.md) in the round of 32, making them the first team eliminated from the knockout stage. After the tournament, Hugo Broos announced his retirement from football, ending his tenure as South Africa's manager. He stepped down as the longest tenured manager in the country's footballing history.

Shortly after the conclusion of Bafana Bafana's World Cup run, the country was rocked by the news of the sudden death of [Jayden Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayden_Adams), a midfielder for [Mamelodi Sundowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamelodi_Sundowns) who had played for the national team just weeks earlier. Tributes from across the football world came in from various footballing associations, including those from [Canada](723688.md) and [Mexico](808402.md) among other nations, footballers, and publications sending condolences and paying their respects. [FIFA](11049.md) held a [moment of silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_of_silence) in Adams' honour during the quarter-finals with [Norway](203277.md) vs. [England](9904.md) and [Argentina](454699.md) vs. [Switzerland](681862.md) on 11 July.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

Following the departure of Hugo Broos, Pitso Mosimane was appointed head coach in August 2026.[^cite_note-40]

On 30 September 2026, South Africa marked their 500th match since readmission with a 5–0 victory over [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_national_football_team) in the [2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers](81670241.md). The result extended South Africa’s overall record since readmission to 236 wins, 125 draws and 139 losses.[^cite_note-41]

## Team image

### Nickname

"[Bafana Bafana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafana_Bafana)" is a nickname given to the national side by its fans. It is [Nguni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguni_language) and translates literally as "the boys, the boys". Its actual meaning in Nguni language is, "Go boys! Go boys!", though others take the double use of "Bafana" to mean "all of the boys"---the team and management, as well as the spectators; togetherness, as in the African concept of [ubuntu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubuntu_\(philosophy\)).

In July 1992, at Kings Park stadium in Durban, fans shouted "Bafana Bafana" \[GO BOYS GO BOYS] when South Africa was on the verge of beating Cameroon and after the game. A group of three journalists, from South Africa newspaper *[The Sowetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sowetan)*, then began to use the name in print when referring to the team. The name was initially used only informally, as SAFA felt that it was not commercially viable, and that it was degrading to refer to a team of men as boys.[^cite_note-42]

Standton Woodrush Ltd applied to trademark the name in 1993. The trademark was granted but was for clothing only. In 1994, the team's technical sponsor at the time, Kappa, applied for the trademarks "Bafana" and "Bafana Bafana" in class 25. These were granted and subsequently passed on to SAFA. In 1997, SAFA filed for the trademark "Bafana Bafana" for all goods and services, in all classes.

SAFA then applied to have Standton Woodrush Ltd removed from the trademarks register, on the grounds that SAFA was the rightful owner of the name. The case was dismissed on the ground that SAFA was not an interested person within the meaning of this term as used in the Trade Marks Act No. 94 of 1993, which provides that only an interested party has legal standing to seek relief.

SAFA made another application to gain ownership of the trademark, with the second application going to the Appeal Court. The court again found that SAFA did not have the necessary grounds for ownership of the name in all classes. The court held that an intention to use a mark does not create a preference for registration, and that the proprietor of a trademark need not be its originator.

In 2011 SAFA paid R5 million to Standton Woodrush Ltd for rights to the name "Bafana Bafana", in class 25 and all other classes.[^cite_note-43]

### Home stadiums



First game of the 2010 FIFA World Cup, South Africa vs Mexico at Soccer City

The South Africa national football team does not use a single stadium as its home stadium for matches. Instead, they use a variety of stadiums throughout South Africa to host matches.

The largest venue used is the 94,700-seater [Soccer City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FNB_Stadium) in [Soweto](28573.md), outside [Johannesburg](58893.md). Other often used venues include the [Atteridgeville Super Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atteridgeville_Super_Stadium), outside [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria), the [Royal Bafokeng Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bafokeng_Stadium), outside [Rustenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustenburg), the [Free State Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_Stadium) in [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein) and [Moses Mabhida Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Mabhida_Stadium) in [Durban](313650.md).

Matches have also been hosted at [Ellis Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_Park_Stadium) in Johannesburg, [Kings Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park_Stadium) in Durban, [Cape Town Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_Stadium) in [Cape Town](6653.md), [Nelson Mandela Bay Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela_Bay_Stadium) in [Gqeberha](2013184.md), [Loftus Versfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loftus_Versfeld_Stadium) in Pretoria, [Orlando Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Stadium) in Soweto, and [Mbombela Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbombela_Stadium) in [Mbombela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbombela).

The team has also hosted matches outside of South Africa. In preparation for the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), the team hosted a friendly in [Baiersbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiersbronn), Germany. As a warm-up for the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), the team hosted a friendly at [Mong Kok Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mong_Kok_Stadium) in [Hong Kong](13404.md). In 2006 the team hosted a Nelson Mandela Challenge match at [Griffin Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Park), in London, England. During their preparations for the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md), the team hosted two matches in Germany. The matches were played at the [BRITA-Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRITA-Arena) in [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden) and the [Stadion am Bieberer Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_am_Bieberer_Berg) in [Offenbach am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenbach_am_Main).

### Kits and crest

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [South Africa national soccer team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/South%20Africa%20national%20soccer%20team%20kits).

Throughout the years, Bafana Bafana has employed multiple logos to represent the team. Notably, they often utilise two primary logos simultaneously on their jerseys.

#### Protea Emblem

The prominent [Protea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protea_cynaroides) emblem, a [national symbol of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_South_Africa), particularly in the post-apartheid era, graces the right side of the jersey. This signifies the team's connection to the broader South African sporting landscape.

#### South African Football Association (SAFA) Badge

Located on the left side of the jersey, the SAFA badge typically features a star above it, commemorating the team's triumph in the [1996 Africa Cup of Nations.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_African_Cup_of_Nations_final)

#### Tertiary Emblem

A less frequently used yet noteworthy emblem was introduced approximately a decade ago. This distinctive logo depicts two stars embodied as footballers on the pitch, offering a symbolic representation of the team's aspirations and achievements.

#### Sponsors

When the team returned to international soccer in 1992, their kit was supplied by an Italian clothing company, [Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa_\(company\)). The team wore Kappa kits up to and during the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-44]

Following the 1998 World Cup, the kits for all South African national football teams were supplied by German sports apparel company [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas). The sponsorship deal is believed to be worth Rs 14 million per year.[^cite_note-45] Their contract with the [South African Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Football_Association) ran until the end of 2010.

On 7 June 2011, SAFA announced [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_AG) as its new technical sponsor and unveiled Bafana Bafana's new kit.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

In October 2013, [ABSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absa_Group) announced its resignation as a sponsor of SAFA and the South African national team as of the end of their contract in December 2013. This was followed a week later by the announcement from [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_AG), that they too would step down as technical sponsors of Bafana Bafana.[^cite_note-48]

From March 2014 until June 2020, the official kit provider was the American sporting apparel company [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) from the international friendly match against [Brazil](149286.md).

From June 2020 to early 2026, the official kit provider was French sporting apparel company [Le Coq Sportif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Coq_Sportif).[^cite_note-49]

The team now has one main sponsor, [Castle Lager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SABMiller), a brand owned by South African Breweries. Smaller sponsors and suppliers include [Energade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Brands) and [Tsogo Sun Hotels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsogo_Sun).[^cite_note-50]

In 2022, Banxso became the official online trading partner for the team.[^cite_note-51]

In 2026, [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) was signed as the next kit provider for the South African National Team just in time for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), returning for the first time since the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md).

### Media coverage

The [South African Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Football_Association) owns the rights to national team matches played in South Africa. SAFA currently has a contract with the [South African Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Broadcasting_Corporation), to broadcast these matches. It is up to the SABC to procure the rights to matches played outside of the country.

The SABC usually shows most national team games, though they have often had problems with broadcasting weekday matches. They have previously used delayed live footage,[^cite_note-52] and sometimes not shown matches.[^cite_note-53]

Most large tournaments in which the team play, such as the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) and [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md), have separate broadcast rights. [SuperSport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperSport_\(South_African_broadcaster\)) purchase the pay TV rights to these tournaments, thus during these tournaments, they also show South African matches. The SABC usually holds the free to air rights to these tournaments, with the notable exception being the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup). Those matches were shown by [e.tv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.tv).[^cite_note-54]

## Competitive record

### FIFA World Cup

Main article: [South Africa at the FIFA World Cup](40808567.md)

South Africa has made four appearances in the [World Cup](11370.md), making it past the first round once. The team's first attempt to [qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_first_round#Group_D) was for the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), two years after they had been readmitted to FIFA. Their first appearance was in [France 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup). Despite a 3–0 drubbing to [France](250197.md) in their opening game, they went on to draw against [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team); the team finished [third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C) and thus exited the tournament. [Korea/Japan 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) saw them eliminated in the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B) again after drawing to [Paraguay](804025.md) and beating [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team) 1–0 for their first-ever World Cup win. The team finished third in their group, losing out to Paraguay on goal difference.

Hosting the [2010 World Cup](168079.md), they famously upset [France](250197.md) 2–1 and drew 1–1 to [Mexico](808402.md), but lost 0–3 to [Uruguay](679782.md). They failed to advance to the round of 16, and became the first host country to fail to advance past the group stage. This would begin a long gap between appearances at the tournament for Bafana Bafana, but eventually ended after they qualified for the 2026 edition of the tournament, and made it to the round of 32 for the first time.[^cite_note-59]
