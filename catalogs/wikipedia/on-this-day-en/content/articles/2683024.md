**Panda diplomacy** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 熊猫外交) is the practice of sending [giant pandas](12713.md) from [mainland China](55079.md) to other countries as a tool of [diplomacy](28165911.md) and wildlife conservation.[^cite_note-1] From 1941 to 1984, the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) and then the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC) gifted pandas to other countries. Since 1984, they have been leased rather than gifted due to a PRC policy change.

## History

### Pre-1950s

While there are few ancient records of the [giant panda](12713.md), "During the Manchu dynasty, skins of this animal \[bei-shung, presumed to be the panda] were sent as tribute to the government of China by the aborigines of western Szechuan and eastern Tibet", according to [David Crockett Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Crockett_Graham).[^cite_note-Morris-2]<sup>: 25 </sup>[^cite_note-AnimalKingdom2-3]<sup>: 19 </sup>



[Madam Chiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soong_Mei-ling) and [John Tee-Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tee-Van) of the Bronx Zoo, U.S., with the baby Panda in Madam Chiang's yard in Chongqing, November 9, 1941

The first instance of panda diplomacy in the modern era was arranged by [Soong Mei-ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soong_Mei-ling) (Madame Chiang) in 1941 during the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War). China was under siege by Japan, the U.S. had been sending aid to the [Kuomintang](16903.md) (Nationalist Government) in China, and Madame Chiang wanted a dramatic way of saying thank you. There had been previous pandas sent to the U.S., including one named Su Lin sold to the Brookfield Zoo in Chicago by [Ruth Harkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Harkness) in 1937, a second one named Mei-Mei brought back by Harkness in 1938 and also sold to the Brookfield Zoo,[^cite_note-Croke-4] one named Pandora sent to the Bronx Zoo by [David Crockett Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Crockett_Graham) in 1938, and a second named Pan sent to the Bronx Zoo in 1939.[^cite_note-Morris-2]<sup>: 92 </sup> Besides the two live Pandas sent to the Bronx Zoo, Graham had also collected several skins and skeletons that were sent to the Smithsonian.[^cite_note-Nicholls-5]<sup>: 55 </sup>

In the summer of 1941, Madame Chiang enlisted [David Crockett Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Crockett_Graham) to capture a live panda. Eventually, two were caught. After spending some time at Graham's house in Chengdu, they were brought to [Chongqing](171752.md) for a formal handover to a representative of the Bronx Zoo. William J. Dunn, a CBS radio reporter, was in Chongqing at the time and was enlisted to emcee the ceremony, which would air on both radio [XGOY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XGOY?action=edit\&redlink=1), "The Voice of China", and CBS Radio. To ensure the program aired during prime time in the U.S., it originated from Chongquing at 4 a.m. local time.[^cite_note-Dunn-6]<sup>: 59–60 </sup> [Annalee Whitmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annalee_Whitmore_Fadiman), then working as publicity manager for United China Relief, interviewed the participants and wrote the transcript.[^cite_note-Lascher-7]<sup>: 198–200 </sup> The broadcast was to include Madame Chiang, her sister [Soong Ai-ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soong_Ai-ling) (Madame Kung), [David Crockett Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Crockett_Graham), and [John Tee-Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tee-Van) from the Bronx Zoo. The plan was to transmit the XGOY signal to an RCA communications centre in [Manila](184334.md) and then on to [San Francisco](49728.md); however, on the morning of November 9, 1941, the engineers were unable to confirm reception from Manila. The broadcast began as planned, but atmospheric conditions prevented the broadcast from reaching the United States.[^cite_note-Dunn-6]<sup>: 60 </sup>

The pandas were flown to [Hong Kong](13404.md) under cover of night and from there to the [Philippines](23440.md) on [Pan Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am)'s Hong Kong Clipper. From there, they took a circuitous six-week route by ship to San Francisco. Unfortunately, while they were en route, [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor) was bombed;[^cite_note-Morris-2]<sup>: 95–97 </sup> and, when they arrived in San Francisco in late December 1941, front-page news was all about war. While the pandas did get attention, they weren't at the top of the news across the nation as had been hoped.[^cite_note-8] The bears were officially received by the Bronx Zoo on December 30, 1941, and five months later, following a national contest, they were named Pan-dee and Pan-dah by 11-year-old Nancy Lostutter of Columbus, Indiana.[^cite_note-9]

### Post-1950s

While the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) used giant pandas for diplomatic means as early as 1941,[^cite_note-10] the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) began to use panda diplomacy more prominently in the 1950s and has continued the practice into the present day. Between 1957 and 1983, 24 pandas were given as gifts to 9 nations as gestures of friendship. These nations included the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the [Democratic People's Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_People's_Republic_of_Korea), the [United States of America](3434750.md), and the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-\:0-11]



U.S. First Lady [Pat Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Nixon) viewing the [Beijing Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Zoo) panda exhibit in February 1972

When [President Nixon visited China in 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_visit_by_Richard_Nixon_to_China), [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong) promised to send two pandas to an American zoo. In exchange, [Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) gave two [musk oxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk_oxen) to the Chinese as a gift. The mutual gifts illustrated the growing diplomatic relationship between China and the United States at the time.[^cite_note-20160204nytimes-12] Despite the long history of panda diplomacy, the arrival of the pandas in 1972 marked the first time a panda had been in the United States in over twenty years.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

Upon the pandas' arrival in April 1972, First Lady [Pat Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Nixon) donated them to the [National Zoo in Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoological_Park_\(United_States\)), where she welcomed them in an official ceremony. Over 20,000 people visited the pandas the first day they were on display, and an estimated 1.1 million visitors came to see them the first year they were in the United States.[^cite_note-Magnier-13] The pandas were wildly popular and China's gift was seen as an enormous diplomatic success, evidence of China's eagerness to establish official [relations with the U.S.](277880.md)[^cite_note-Byron-14] It was so successful that British Prime Minister [Edward Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Heath) asked for pandas for the United Kingdom during a visit to China in 1974. Pandas Chia-Chia and Ching-Ching arrived at the [London Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Zoo) a few weeks later.[^cite_note-Magnier-13] The pandas gifted to the UK would later be the inspiration for the logo of the [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund).[^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Mexico



One of the gifted pandas at Chapultepec Zoo, July 24, 1978

Mexico and the PRC established [formal relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Mexico_relations) on February 14, 1972.[^cite_note-Cerda_Dueñas-15] On September 10, 1975,[^cite_note-16] two pandas, Pe Pe (male) and Ying Ying (female), were gifted to Mexico as a sign of friendship by Mao Zedong following the April 1973 state visit to China by President [Luis Echeverría](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Echeverr%C3%ADa). The pandas were delivered by Vicepremier of the State Council [Chen Yonggui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Yonggui) and received by First Lady [María Esther Zuno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Esther_Zuno).[^cite_note-Cerda_Dueñas-15] They were housed at [Chapultepec Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapultepec_Zoo). The zoo became the first institution outside China to successfully breed pandas.

Although China shifted from panda gifts to a policy of high-priced loans in 1984, Mexico has retained ownership of the locally born pandas since their lineage traces to gifted pandas.[^cite_note-17]

In 1980, Pe Pe and Ying Ying had Xen Li, the first panda born outside China, however, she did not survive into adulthood. In 1983 the couple had Liang Liang. In 1985 Ying Ying gave birth to twins, an unnamed cub and Xiu Hua. In 1987, Ying Ying gave birth to another set of twins, Ping Ping and Shuan Shuan. In 1981 Ying Ying gave birth to [Tohui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohui), who in turn mothered [Xin Xin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xin_Xin_\(giant_panda\)) (with Chia Chia, a gifted panda originally at the London Zoo). Xin Xin is the last living panda not owned by China.[^cite_note-18]

### Post-1984

In 1984, China's leader [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) modified the policy, such that subsequent pandas would be [leased](635681.md), instead of gifted, beginning with China presenting two pandas to Los Angeles during the 1984 Olympic Games for \$50,000 per month per panda. Short-term loans of the pandas for exhibition purposes became increasingly common. The loans were typically for 100 to 200 days with fees ranging from \$300,000 to \$500,000. In 1988, concerns over the welfare of the pandas resulted in lawsuits in the United States challenging the importation criteria.[^cite_note-19880531NYT-19]

The leasing practice was again modified by China in 1991 in favor of long-term leases. China began to offer pandas to other nations only on ten-year lease. The standard lease terms include a fee of up to US\$1 million per year and a provision that any cubs born during the lease period be the property of the People's Republic of China. The [gifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gift) of two pandas to Hong Kong in 2007 was ostensibly an exception to this lease model, but can be seen as outside of the spectrum of panda diplomacy.[^cite_note-\:2-20]

After the [2008 Sichuan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Sichuan_earthquake) that severely damaged many facilities, 60 pandas required new housing. The majority were given to nations that had favourable trade agreements with China or those that supplied China with necessary resources, such as uranium from Australia.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Taiwan

Main article: [Tuan Tuan and Yuan Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuan_Tuan_and_Yuan_Yuan)



Giant panda at the [Taipei Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Zoo)

In 2005, [Lien Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lien_Chan), Chairman of the [Kuomintang](16903.md), the then opposition party in [Taiwan](25734.md), visited [mainland China](55079.md). As part of the talks between Lien and the CCP, two pandas (later named Tuan Tuan and Yuan Yuan, meaning "reunion" in Chinese) were offered as a gift to the people of Taiwan. The offer was opposed by the Republic of China (ROC) government of Taiwan, then led by the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party) (DPP), which opposes [unification with the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_unification). The gift of pandas was seen as an attempt by the CCP to draw the ROC government into its "[united front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_front_in_Taiwan)". While several zoos in Taiwan made bids to host the pandas, the ROC government raised objections, ostensibly because pandas were not suited to the Taiwanese climate and Taiwan did not have the expertise to rear pandas successfully. It was widely understood, however, that these were pretexts underlaid by political considerations by the DPP-led government to maintain its distance from the PRC government.[^cite_note-21]

Another technical issue is a dispute over the applicability of the [Convention on the International Trade of Endangered Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES) (CITES). In 1998, China offered the Republic of China two giant pandas in exchange for wartime peace. The PRC insisted that a transfer from mainland China to Taiwan was a domestic transfer, not subject to CITES, while the ROC government disputed this and would not accept the pandas without CITES procedures.[^cite_note-Spencer-22] On March 11, 2006, the ROC formally rejected the offer, with President [Chen Shui-bian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Shui-bian) explaining in his weekly newsletter, "A-bian (Chen's nickname) sincerely urges the Chinese leaders to leave the giant pandas in their natural habitat because pandas brought up in cages or given as gifts will not be happy."[^cite_note-Spencer-22]

Following a change of government in Taiwan, in July 2008, the ROC government led by the Kuomintang stated that it would accept the gift of two four-year-old giant pandas.[^cite_note-23] In December 2008, the government approved the import of pandas under the terms of "species of [traditional herbal medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_herbology)."[^cite_note-24] Tuan Tuan and Yuan Yuan arrived at [Taipei Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Zoo) later in the same month. In response to the transfer, the CITES Secretariat stated that the transfer of the two pandas was a matter of "internal or domestic trade", and so was not required to be reported to CITES.[^cite_note-25] The ROC quickly issued a rebuttal to the CITES statement and insisted that the country-to-country transfer protocols be respected. The ROC also noted that such procedures would not have been needed if it had been an internal/domestic transfer.[^cite_note-26] The ROC further noted that Taiwan is not a CITES signatory and is therefore not obligated to report to the CITES Secretariat its acceptance of the two pandas.[^cite_note-27]

### United States

See also: [Animal diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_diplomacy)

In the 1970s, the Nixon administration sought to improve [U.S.–China relations](277880.md). Shortly after Nixon visited China in 1972, Beijing sent two pandas, named [Ling-Ling and Hsing-Hsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ling-Ling_and_Hsing-Hsing). The female died in 1992 from heart disease and the male was euthanized in 1999 after developing end-stage kidney disease. China has leased out subsequent pandas to the U.S.; however, most of these leases expired by 2024, with the [National Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoological_Park_\(United_States\)) returning three giant pandas to China in November 2023.[^cite_note-28]

In the 1980s, the growing popularity of short-term loans was concerning to conservationists. In 1984 and 1985, three zoos used short-term loans: the [Los Angeles Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Zoo), the [San Francisco Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Zoo) and the [Toronto Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Zoo). By 1987 and 1988, pandas on short-term loans were sent to five zoos: the [Bronx Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Zoo), [Busch Gardens Tampa Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busch_Gardens_Tampa_Bay), the [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo), the [Toledo Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo_Zoo_&_Aquarium), and, in conjunction with the 1988 Winter Olympics, the [Calgary Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Zoo). Similar loans were in the planning stages at five other zoos – [Zoo Atlanta](920796.md), the [Columbus Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus_Zoo_and_Aquarium), [Omaha's Henry Doorly Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha's_Henry_Doorly_Zoo_and_Aquarium), Portland's [Oregon Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Zoo), and Seattle's [Woodland Park Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_Park_Zoo) – as well as [Walt Disney World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_World) and the [Michigan State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Fair).[^cite_note-19880531NYT-19]

The [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund) and the [American Association of Zoological Parks and Aquariums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Zoos_and_Aquariums) filed suit in 1988 to force the [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service](75289.md) (FWS) to cancel the [Toledo Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo_Zoo_&_Aquarium)'s display of breeding-age pandas, which they alleged were solely for commercial purposes and in violation of the [U.S. Endangered Species Act](532055.md) and the [Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES) (CITES).[^cite_note-19880531NYT-19] The injunction was denied and the pandas arrived for a 100-day stay which was later extended. However, Chinese wildlife officials were insulted by the dispute, including the FWS questioning how China used the money it received for the loans.[^cite_note-29]

In 1993, the FWS issued a moratorium on the importation of pandas to the U.S. To ensure that imported pandas would contribute to the welfare of pandas in the wild in China, the FWS expanded the criteria to obtain an import permit: the import must not be for commercial purposes, it must benefit wild populations through scientific research or propagation, it must not jeopardize the wild populations, and the importer must have facilities and the expertise to care for the pandas. The requirement that the zoo ensure that China will channel more than half of its loan fee into conservation efforts for wild pandas and their habitat was a result of the World Wildlife Fund lawsuit.[^cite_note-\:2-20] The criteria also makes it unlikely a permit would be granted for a short-term loan. With the new policy in place, the moratorium was lifted in 1998.[^cite_note-30]

In 2007, PRC officials used panda diplomacy in an attempt to scuttle an agricultural trade deal between Nebraska and Taiwan.[^cite_note-\:3-31] After six years of formally pursuing a panda project and raising \$55 million to cover the costs, officials at the [Henry Doorly Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha's_Henry_Doorly_Zoo_and_Aquarium) in Omaha, Nebraska were told they were at the top of the waiting list. Just before the state's governor was to leave on a trip to finalize the \$400 million trade deal with Taiwan, the Chinese Embassy asked the zoo director to use his influence to have the governor abandon the deal or the zoo would be dropped to seventh or eighth place on the waiting list. The director refused and he was told China was no longer looking for panda loan partners. While the Chinese Embassy has said they have no record of the phone call, the zoo did not receive the pandas. It would be 2024 before a zoo in the United States did.[^cite_note-32]

In the late 2010s and early 2020s, with U.S.–China relations straining, China began declining to renew panda leases for U.S. zoos.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo) pandas returned to China in 2019, followed by pandas at the [Memphis Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_Zoo) and [National Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoological_Park_\(United_States\)) in Washington, D.C., in 2023.[^cite_note-35] The Memphis pandas, Ya Ya and Le Le, became a rallying point for Chinese calls to repatriate the bears after accusations of poor living conditions circulated on Chinese social media sites and the sudden death of Le Le in February 2023. A joint team of American and Chinese scientists concluded Ya Ya was suffering from skin disease due to genetic components and fluctuating hormones.[^cite_note-36] The [Zoo Atlanta](920796.md) pandas, the last giant pandas remaining in the United States, were returned to China in late 2024.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Scholars, including [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) political economist [Ho-fung Hung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-fung_Hung), have questioned whether a deterioration in U.S.–China relations starting in the late 2010s brought an end to panda diplomacy between the two countries.[^cite_note-39]

In a 2023 *[Washington Post](102226.md)* opinion piece, Lonnie G. Bunch III and Ellen Stofan, secretary and undersecretary, respectively, of the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution), called the return of the [National Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoological_Park_\(United_States\))'s pandas "a lesson in cultural diplomacy". They wrote: "Pandas are a vital source of cultural diplomacy—using the arts, science and history to help nations find common ground with the hopes of building on our shared humanity to create a more peaceful world. The pandas were a bridge between the American people and the Chinese people." They concluded: "If we can save this iconic species, then surely, we can work together to tackle some of our greatest challenges, including climate change and preservation of ecosystems around the world."[^cite_note-40]

In February 2024, the China Wildlife Conservation Association announced plans to send a new pair of pandas to the [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo) as early as summer 2024, nearly five years after the Zoo returned its pandas to China.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] These pandas, who are named Yun Chuan and Xin Bao, arrived in late June 2024 and debuted to the public on August 8, 2024.[^cite_note-43]

Following a campaign that included then-Mayor [London Breed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Breed) traveling to China the previous year, in April 2024, she announced from Beijing that the [San Francisco Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Zoo) had been selected to receive pandas. The timeline for their arrival depends on the completion of a panda enclosure at the zoo.[^cite_note-\:3-31][^cite_note-44] In May 2026, Mayor [Daniel Lurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Lurie) reaffirmed City Hall’s commitment to the panda project while also saying it could not be directly funded by the city budget. Cost estimates that include updating the habitat were over \$25 million.[^cite_note-45]

In May 2024, the [National Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoological_Park_\(United_States\)) announced that two pandas named Bao Li and Qing Bao would become part of the collection by the end of the year.[^cite_note-46] The pandas arrived on October 15, 2024, and were quarantined for 30 days to reduce the risk of their passing parasites or diseases to other animals. This also gave them time to get acclimated to their new environment. After some preview days in mid-January with more limited audiences present, the pandas made their public debut on January 24, 2025.[^cite_note-47]

In April 2026, the China Wildlife Conservation Association announced that two pandas born in 2020, Ping Ping (male) and Fu Shuan (female), would be sent to [Zoo Atlanta](920796.md) under a 10-year conservation partnership agreement signed in 2025.[^cite_note-48] On September 24, 2026, during a [state visit to the United States](84169290.md) where he met with President Trump, Chinese leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) confirmed that China will send the pandas to Zoo Atlanta as an "envoy of friendship."[^cite_note-49] The zoo announced that the pandas arrived on September 27, 2026.[^cite_note-50]

### Other nations



Mao Sun at [Copenhagen Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Zoo) in 2025, one of two giant pandas sent to Denmark in 2019

[^cite_note-51]

[^cite_note-52]

Other countries recognize the significance of pandas as diplomatic symbols, emblematic of the state of relations with China. During a visit by then [Chinese Leader](262766.md) [Hu Jintao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Jintao) to Japan in May 2008, China announced the lease of two pandas to Japan. The leader was quoted as saying "Giant pandas are very popular among the Japanese, and they are a symbol of the friendly [ties between Japan and China](13106128.md)."[^cite_note-53] Treatment of the pandas is likewise associated with the relevant foreign policy. For example, in 1964, British diplomats worried that a transfer of a panda from a London Zoo to Moscow would worsen [Sino–Soviet relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_relations).[^cite_note-bbc4508873-54] In January 2006, U.S. Deputy Secretary of State [Robert Zoellick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Zoellick) was photographed hugging a five-month-old panda cub during his visit to [Sichuan](65185.md). The Chinese media widely broadcast the image and it was purportedly interpreted as a sign that Zoellick supported better relations between China and the United States.[^cite_note-washingtonpost2006012500-55]

On April 16, 2014, China planned to send a pair of pandas named Fu Wa and Feng Yi to Malaysia to mark [their 40-year diplomatic ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Malaysia_relations) but were postponed following the [MH370](42142305.md) tragedy.[^cite_note-56] The two pandas later arrived at [Kuala Lumpur International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur_International_Airport) on May 21, and were placed at the [National Zoo of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoo_of_Malaysia).[^cite_note-news2420140521-57][^cite_note-20140521thestar-58] In 2018, Finland agreed to care for two giant pandas following their endorsement of the [one-China policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-China_policy).[^cite_note-59] They were sent home in November 2024 due to their maintenance cost of €1.5 million a year and lack of government funding.[^cite_note-60] Two pandas, Cai Tao and Hu Chun, arrived in [Jakarta](16275.md) in 2017 to be placed in [Taman Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Safari) in [Bogor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogor) as part of the 60th anniversary celebrations of [China–Indonesia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Indonesia_relations).[^cite_note-scmp2113229-61]



Chinese leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) presented two pandas to [Moscow Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Zoo) at a ceremony with [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) on June 5, 2019.

In April 2019, after Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) visited Beijing, two pandas were sent to Russia's [Moscow Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Zoo). On June 5, 2019, during an official state visit which emphasized the strengthening ties between the two countries, [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) [General Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) unveiled the pandas to the public. Putin said they were a "gesture of particular respect and trust" and that "we greatly value this gesture of friendship." Ru Yi, a male born in 2016, and Ding Ding, a female born in 2017, were leased to the zoo for 15 years as part of a joint research project.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

In December 2023, the only giant pandas in the UK were sent back to China. The pandas, Tian Tian and Yang Guang, were sent to the [Edinburgh Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Zoo) in December 2011 on a 10-year lease as China expressed a commitment to "take Chinese-Scottish relations to a new level".[^cite_note-64] For the two years beyond the original lease, the annual fee paid to China was reduced by half to \$500,000. There were research gains in panda fertility, husbandry, and veterinary care though there were no successful pregnancies. In November 2023, as the elderly pandas were scheduled to return, a memorandum was signed by China's Giant Panda National Park and the UK's Loch Lomond and the Trossachs National Park to collaborate on conservation efforts. The deputy director of Giant Panda National Park said the partnership will "further strengthen the cooperation between China and the UK in terms of resources, capabilities, willingness, and determination".[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]



Xiao Xiao and Lei Lei at Ueno in 2022

At the end of 2025, following the onset of a [diplomatic crisis between China and Japan](81642186.md) regarding potential Japanese intervention in a Taiwan Strait conflict, it was announced that twin pandas from the Tokyo [Ueno Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ueno_Zoo) would return to China at the end of January 2026, a few weeks earlier than expected. The pandas, Xiao Xiao and Lei Lei, were born at Ueno in 2021. Their parents, Shin Shin and Ri Ri, by then considered elderly, had been returned to China in September 2024. Japan had previously hosted pandas continuously since the normalization of relations between the PRC and Japan in 1972.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

## Practicalities

Keeping pandas is very expensive. Besides the "rent" payable to China, obtaining enough bamboo is very expensive. A panda typically consumes only fresh bamboo, eating 40 kilograms (88 lb) of it every day.[^cite_note-\:1-69] It was reported in 2011 that Edinburgh Zoo spent \$107,000 per year to feed its two pandas.[^cite_note-independent6258681-70] This caused the zoo to ask for bamboo donations, as well as for local gardeners to start growing bamboo.[^cite_note-20110117deadlinenews-71] During the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the supply of bamboo added to cost considerations. Owing to the difficulty of securing a consistent and adequate supply of fresh bamboo, Calgary Zoo returned their pair of pandas ahead of schedule, to join their progeny back in China.[^cite_note-\:1-69] Due to the high upkeep costs, pandas have been considered a [white elephant gift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_elephant).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

In 2003, China sent Thailand a pair of pandas, [Chuang Chuang and Lin Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuang_Chuang_and_Lin_Hui), to [Chiang Mai Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Mai_Zoo). Chuang Chuang was put on a diet in 2007 due to obesity and died in September 2019 as a result of heart failure.[^cite_note-\:12-74][^cite_note-20191009theguardian-75]
