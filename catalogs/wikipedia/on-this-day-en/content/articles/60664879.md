**An Se-young** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 안세영; [Hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja): 安洗瑩; born 5 February 2002) is a South Korean [badminton](3956.md) player, who won the gold medal at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in the [women's singles event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-og24-1] She was named 2019's Most Promising Player of the Year and 2023's Female Player of the Year by the [BWF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_World_Federation).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] She won the gold medals at the [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BWF_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [2026 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BWF_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), making history as the first Korean women's singles player to win the World Championships title.[^cite_note-wc23-4][^cite_note-wc26-5] She then clinched the women's singles gold medal at the [2022 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-ag22-6] An was also a part of South Korea's gold medal winning teams at the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) and [2026 Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) as well at the [2022 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_team).[^cite_note-uc22-7][^cite_note-agt22-8]

In 2018, An was selected to join the national team and became the first junior high school student on the South Korean national team. She was part of the national junior team that won the mixed team title at the [2017 Asian Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Badminton_Asia_Junior_Championships).[^cite_note-ajct17-9] An later represented her country at the [2018 Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) in Bangkok and [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2018_Asian_Games) in Jakarta, helping the team win a bronze medal in the former event.[^cite_note-uc18-10][^cite_note-agwt18-11] In 2019, she clinched her first BWF World Tour title at the Super 300 [New Zealand Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_New_Zealand_Open_\(badminton\)), beating the 2012 Olympic gold medalist [Li Xuerui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Xuerui) in the final.[^cite_note-nz19-12]

## Career

An Se-young entered her first international competition when she was only 13 years old, participating in the 2015 [Asian Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_Asia_Junior_Championships#Youth_Championships_\(U%E2%80%9317_&_U%E2%80%9315\)) where she finished as quarter-finalist in the U15 girls' singles and doubles. An won her first international junior title at the U15 Korea Junior Open in 2015. An increasingly dominated the 2016 U15 junior tournaments, winning the girls' singles title at the Jakarta Open Junior International, Jaya Raya Junior Grand Prix, Asian Junior Championships, and Korea Junior Open; she also won the women's doubles title at the Jaya Raya Junior Grand Prix and the Korea Junior Open.

In 2017, An Se-young competed in the U17 and U19 competitions, where she managed to win the U17 Korea Junior Open, but at the [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Badminton_Asia_Junior_Championships) and [World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_BWF_World_Junior_Championships), she was unable to win any medals in the individual event. Meanwhile, in the mixed team, An succeeded in helping her team win the Asian junior mixed team title and also won a bronze medal at the World Junior Championships.[^cite_note-ajct17-9] At the end of the year, An, 15 years old, was selected for the national senior team, becoming the first ever middle school student to join the South Korean national team.[^cite_note-13]

She was then entrusted to strengthen the Korean team at the [2018 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2018_Asian_Games), but did not manage to win any medals in the individual or team events.[^cite_note-ag18-14][^cite_note-agt18-15] In the 2018 Indonesia International Challenge, An managed to reach the final round.[^cite_note-inaic18-16] She then won her first senior international title at the 2018 [Irish Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Open_\(badminton\)), beating compatriot [Kim Ga-eun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Ga-eun_\(badminton\)) in the final.[^cite_note-irl18-17]

An Se-young won her maiden World Tour title at the [2019 New Zealand Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_New_Zealand_Open_\(badminton\)), beating the 2012 Olympic gold medalist [Li Xuerui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Xuerui) of China in the final.[^cite_note-nz19-12] Her breakthroughs continued by winning the [Canada Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Canada_Open_\(badminton\)),[^cite_note-can19-18] [Akita Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Akita_Masters),[^cite_note-aki19-19] [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_\(badminton\)),[^cite_note-fra19-20] and [Korea Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Korea_Masters).[^cite_note-korm19-21] The continuously improving performance she displayed in 2019 brought her into the women's singles top 10 in the BWF world rankings.[^cite_note-19mpp-22] In recognition of her achievements, the BWF awarded her the 2019 [Most Promising Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Awards#Eddy_Choong_Most_Promising_Player_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-19mpp-22][^cite_note-23]

Due to COVID-19, An only participated in five tournaments in 2020, with her best result being runner-up at the [Thailand Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Thailand_Masters_\(badminton\)),[^cite_note-tham20-24] and together with the national team winning a silver medal at the [Asian Women's Team Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Badminton_Asia_Team_Championships).[^cite_note-awtc20-25] In 2021, in her debut at the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she was eliminated in the quarter-finals by [Chen Yufei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Yufei).[^cite_note-og20-26] An then made her first final in a Super 1000 tournament, the [Denmark Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Denmark_Open), but she was unable to finish the match, and had to settle for runner-up to [Akane Yamaguchi](37094638.md).[^cite_note-den21-27] At the Indonesia badminton festival held in Bali, An managed to win all three tournaments after in the final she beats Yamaguchi in the [Indonesia Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Indonesia_Masters),[^cite_note-inam21-28] [Ratchanok Intanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchanok_Intanon) in the [Indonesia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Indonesia_Open),[^cite_note-inao21-29] and [P. V. Sindhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._V._Sindhu) in the [World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BWF_World_Tour_Finals).[^cite_note-wtf21-30]



An at the [2022 German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_German_Open_\(badminton\))

In 2022, An reached five finals in the BWF World Tour, winning the [Korea Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Korea_Open_\(badminton\)),[^cite_note-kor22-31] [Malaysia Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Malaysia_Masters),[^cite_note-masm22-32] and the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_\(badminton\));[^cite_note-aus22-33] and also finished as runners-up in the [All England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_All_England_Open) and [Japan Opens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Japan_Open_\(badminton\)).[^cite_note-ae22-34][^cite_note-jpn22-35] She also claimed the bronze medals in the women's singles at the [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Badminton_Asia_Championships) and [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-ac22-36][^cite_note-wc22-37] Together with the South Korean women's team, she clinched the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup).[^cite_note-uc22-7]

An marked huge milestones for Korean badminton in 2023. She became the first ever Korean women's singles to win the World Championships title in [2023 BWF World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BWF_World_Championships),[^cite_note-wc23-4] and was the first Korean women's singles to win the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2022_Asian_Games) in 29 years.[^cite_note-ag22-6] She also won the gold medal in the women's team at the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_team).[^cite_note-agt22-8] In the BWF World Tour, she claimed eight titles out of ten finals,[^cite_note-38] and topped the women's singles ranking on 1 August 2023.[^cite_note-rank1-39]

In the first half of 2024 season, An played seven individual tournaments, won 3 titles in the [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Malaysia_Open_\(badminton\)), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_\(badminton\)), and the [Singapore Opens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Singapore_Open_\(badminton\)),[^cite_note-sgp24-40] and also became finalist in the [Indonesia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indonesia_Open).[^cite_note-inao24-41] An clinched the gold medal in the women's singles final at the [Paris 2024 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics), defeating China's [He Bingjiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Bingjiao) 2–0, marking South Korea's first gold in this event since [Bang Soo-hyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang_Soo-hyun)'s victory in 1996.[^cite_note-42] Following her triumphant gold medal victory at the Paris 2024 Olympics, An Se-young entered a period of both reflection and recovery. After a brief hiatus to address long-standing injury concerns and navigating internal discussions with the Badminton Korea Association (BKA),[^cite_note-poy24-43] she made a highly anticipated return to the international circuit at the [Denmark Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Denmark_Open), where she impressively reached the final and secured the runner-up position.[^cite_note-den24-44] Continuing her post-Olympic momentum, she showcased her unwavering resilience by clinching the title at the [China Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_China_Masters).[^cite_note-chnm24-45] She concluded her competitive season by finish as the semi-finalist in the [World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BWF_World_Tour_Finals) in December. An was honored as the BWF Female Player of the Year for the second year in a row.[^cite_note-poy24-43]

### BWF World Tour (39 titles, 11 runners-up)

The BWF World Tour, which was announced on 19 March 2017 and implemented in 2018,[^cite_note-72] is a series of elite badminton tournaments sanctioned by the [Badminton World Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_World_Federation) (BWF). The BWF World Tour is divided into levels of World Tour Finals, Super 1000, Super 750, Super 500, Super 300, and the BWF Tour Super 100.[^cite_note-73]
