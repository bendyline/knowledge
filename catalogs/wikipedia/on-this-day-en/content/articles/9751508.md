**Terrorism in Indonesia** refer to acts of [terrorism](30636.md) that take place within [Indonesia](14579.md) or attacks on [Indonesian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_people) or interests abroad. These acts of terrorism often target the [government of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Indonesia) or foreigners in Indonesia, most notably [Western](21208200.md) visitors, especially those from the [United States](3434750.md) and [Australia](4689264.md).

In June 2015, Indonesia was taken off the [Financial Action Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force) [blacklist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacklist) of 'Non-Cooperative Countries or Territories' (NCCTs) due to Indonesia no longer being non-cooperative in the global fight against [money laundering](19390.md) and terrorist financing. That gives Indonesia the same status as other major economies in the [G20](2206608.md).[^cite_note-61]

## Suspects

Traditionally [militias](20622.md) politically opposed to Indonesian government interests have been held responsible for terrorist attacks in Indonesia. [Separatist](286527.md) and violent [rebel movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_movement) operating in Indonesia, such as the [Darul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Islam_\(Indonesia\)), the [Communist Party of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Indonesia), [Fretilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fretilin) ([East Timorese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor) independence [militia](20622.md) during the [Indonesian occupation of East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_occupation_of_East_Timor)), the [Free Aceh Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Aceh_Movement), and the [Free Papua Organisation](541409.md) were often held responsible for terrorist attacks, such as bombings and shooting. Recent terrorism in Indonesia can in part be attributed to the [al-Qaeda](1921.md)-affiliated [Jemaah Islamiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemaah_Islamiyah) [Islamist](15012.md) [terrorist group](63578.md) and/or [Islamic State](9087364.md).

Indonesia has worked with other countries to apprehend and prosecute perpetrators of major bombings linked to militant [Islamism](15012.md).[^cite_note-62] Since 2003, a number of 'western targets' have been attacked. Victims have included both foreigners — mainly Western [tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Indonesia) — as well as Indonesian [civilians](145804.md). Terrorism in Indonesia intensified in 2000 with the [Philippine consulate bombing in Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_consulate_bombing_in_Jakarta) and [Jakarta Stock Exchange bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Stock_Exchange_bombing), followed by four more large attacks. [The deadliest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Bali_bombings) killed 202 people (including 164 international tourists) in the [Bali](4147.md) resort town of [Kuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuta,_Bali) in 2002.[^cite_note-63] The attacks, and subsequent travel warnings issued by other countries, severely damaged Indonesia's [tourism industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Indonesia) and foreign investment prospects.[^cite_note-64] However, after the capture and killing of most of its key members and leaders, most notably [Imam Samudra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Samudra), [Amrozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amrozi), [Abu Dujana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dujana_\(Jemaah_Islamiah\)), [Azahari Husin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azahari_Husin), and [Noordin Mohammad Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noordin_Mohammad_Top), the [terrorist cells](14558092.md) in Indonesia have become less significant.

Since 2011, terrorist attacks seemed to shift from targeting foreign Western interests and residents to attacking Indonesian police officers. The Indonesian Police had success in cracking down on terrorist cells, and in retaliation a new terrorist cell, identified as the "Cirebon Cell", began targeting police officers. On 15 April 2011 a [suicide bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cirebon_bombing) detonated an [improvised explosive device](265112.md) in a [mosque](19894.md) in a police compound in the city of [Cirebon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirebon), West Java, during [Friday prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_prayer). The bomber was killed and at least 28 people were injured.[^cite_note-globe-65][^cite_note-Arnaz-66] The same cell was also suspected of being involved in two more attacks in [Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surakarta), the suicide bombing of a church on 25 September 2011, and a shooting targeting police on 17 August 2012. However these attacks were not as well-prepared and high scaled as previous attacks organised by Jemaah Islamiyah.

Although the number of terrorist attacks seem to have reduced in both number and scale, some terrorist hotspots such as [Poso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poso), Central Sulawesi, remain. the Poso region was previously marred by religious violence between [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Indonesia) and [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Indonesia) in the area. On 16 October 2012, Police discovered two corpses of [murdered](20171.md) police that had been missing for three days in Tamanjeka village, Poso Regency, Central Sulawesi. The victims went missing during an investigatory mission to a suspected terrorist training ground in a forest the Poso area.[^cite_note-BBCPoso-67]

Similar attacks targeting the Indonesian authorities, especially police officers, have also occurred in [Papua](15199.md), however these are not linked with Islamist terrorist cells, but rather with the Papuan [separatist](286527.md) movement [Organisasi Papua Merdeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisasi_Papua_Merdeka). On 8 April 2012, a Trigana Air PK-YRF airplane was shot at by unidentified gunmen during a landing approach on Mulia airstrip, Puncak Jaya, [Papua](15199.md). A Papua Pos journalist, Kogoya (35), was killed in this shooting.[^cite_note-BBCPapua2-68] On 27 November 2012, three policemen stationed at the remote Pirime police post, Jayawijaya, Papua, were killed in an attack by a group of unidentified men. Police suspected the Papua separatist movement was behind the attack.[^cite_note-BBCPapua1-69]

In 2025, after [Jakarta school bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_school_bombing), [Neo-Nazi](54361.md) terrorism activities become another challenge to the Indonesian security because their capability in mobilizing children and teenagers as their terror actors.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] On 30 December 2025, Police also uncovered 5 individuals that manned an unnamed Neo-Nazi network, which planned to indoctrinate 110 children and teenagers from 23 provinces by recruiting them through the [Terrorgram](72327036.md) circle "[True Crime Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Crime_Community)" and violent games as result of the follow up and investigation of the bomber's internet circles.[^cite_note-IBTimes1-59][^cite_note-DetikNews31122025-60]

## Political and community responses

Subsequent bombings in the centre of Jakarta, in which all but one victim were ordinary Indonesians, shocked the public and brought swift responses from the Indonesian security forces. Even the most reluctant politicians had to admit that the evidence pointed to a small group of Islamist agitators. The Jakarta bombings and legal prosecutions helped shift public opinion away from the use of extremist [Islamic political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_political_violence), but also increased the influence of intelligence bodies, the police and military whose strength had diminished since 1998.[^cite_note-72]

Political factors clouded Indonesian responses to the "war on terror"; politicians were at pains not to be seen to be bowing to US and Australian opinion. Even the term "Jemaah Islamiyah" is controversial in Indonesia as it means "Islamic community/congregation", and was also the subject of previous "New Order" manipulation.[^cite_note-73]

## Effects

The attacks, and subsequent travel warnings issued by other countries including the United States[^cite_note-Travel_Warning:_Indonesia-74] and Australia,[^cite_note-SMH-75] severely damaged [Indonesia's tourism industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Indonesia) and foreign investment prospects. Bali's economy was a particularly hard hit, as were tourism based businesses in other parts of Indonesia. In May 2008, the United States government decided to lift its warning.[^cite_note-SMH-75] In 2006, 227,000 Australians visited Indonesia and in 2007 this rose to 314,000.[^cite_note-SMH-75]

## Counter-terrorism

[Detachment 88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detachment_88) is the Indonesian [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) squad, and part of the [Indonesian National Police](5187883.md). Formed after the [2002 Bali bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Bali_bombings), the unit has had considerable success against the [jihadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadi) terrorist cells linked to the [Central Java](453584.md)-based [Islamist](15012.md) movement [Jemaah Islamiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemaah_Islamiah).[^cite_note-SMH-75]

Within three months after the 2002 Bali bombing, various militants, including the attack's mastermind [Imam Samudra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Samudra), the notorious 'smiling-bomber' [Amrozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amrozi), and many others were apprehended.[^cite_note-76] Imam Samudra, Amrozi, and Amrozi's brother [Ali Ghufron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Ghufron) were executed by firing squad on 9 November 2008.

On 9 November 2005, bomb expert and senior player in [Jemaah Islamiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemaah_Islamiyah), Malaysian former university lecturer [Dr. Azahari Husin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azahari_Husin), along with two other militants were killed in a raid on a house in [Batu, East Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu,_East_Java).[^cite_note-77]

The police forces uncovered JI's new command structure in March 2007 and discovered a weapons depot in Java in May 2007. [Abu Dujana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dujana_\(Jemaah_Islamiah\)), suspected leader of JI's military wing and its possible emir, was apprehended on 9 June 2007.[^cite_note-78]

By May 2008, Indonesian police had arrested 418 suspects, of which approximately 250 had been tried and convicted. According to sources within Detachment 88, the JI organisation had been "shrunk", and many of its top operatives had been arrested or killed.[^cite_note-SMH-75]

On 17 July 2009, [two blasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Jakarta_bombings) ripped two Jakarta hotels, JW Marriott and Ritz-Carlton, killing seven.[^cite_note-79] It was the first serious attack for the country in five years. The police stated that it was committed by a more radical splinter group of JI, led by the man then dubbed as the most wanted terrorist in Southeast Asia, [Noordin Mohammad Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noordin_Mohammad_Top). [Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noordin_Mohammad_Top) was killed in a raid two months later on 17 September 2009, in [Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surakarta), Central Java.[^cite_note-thejakartaglobe-80] All members of his cell were either killed or captured, including the recruiter and field coordinator of the attack, [Ibrohim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrohim?action=edit\&redlink=1), killed on 12 August 2009,[^cite_note-81] and the one said to be his successor, [Syaifudin Zuhri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syaifudin_Zuhri?action=edit\&redlink=1), killed on 9 October 2009. After Top, many believed that terrorism in Indonesia had run out of charismatic leaders, and grew insignificant. According to South East Asian terrorism expert and director of the South East Asia [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group), Sidney Jones, Top's death was "a huge blow for the extremist organizations in Indonesia and the region".[^cite_note-82]

On 9 March 2010, [Dulmatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulmatin), a senior figure in the militant group [Jemaah Islamiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemaah_Islamiyah) (JI) and one of the most wanted terrorists in Southeast Asia was killed in a police raid in [Pamulang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamulang), South Tangerang by [Detachment 88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detachment_88).[^cite_note-83]

In May 2018, The House of Representatives (DPR) and the government agreed to ratify the draft revision of Law Number 15 of 2013 concerning the Eradication of Criminal Acts of Terrorism (the Anti-terrorism Bill) into law.[^cite_note-84] Previously, police have to wait for members of the suspected terror organisation to commit terrorism, in order to arrest them. The new anti-terrorism law gave the government the authority to identify and submit an organisation as a terrorist organisation to the court. In this new law, the membership to a terrorist cells or terrorist organisation groups, that have been established by the court, is enough to arrest and persecute a terrorist suspect, thus enabled the authority to perform preemptive measures prior to the terrorist attacks. Since then, the JAD ([Jamaah Ansharut Daulah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaah_Ansharut_Daulah)), JI ([Jemaah Islamiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemaah_Islamiyah)), has been submitted as active terrorist groups in Indonesia, which membership to either of these organization will be prosecuted immediately by Indonesian law.[^cite_note-85]
