The **Islamic Jihad Movement in Palestine** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حركة الجهاد الإسلامي في فلسطين, *Ḥarakat al-Jihād al-Islāmi fi Filastīn*), commonly known simply as **Palestinian Islamic Jihad** (**PIJ**),[^cite_note-2] is an [Islamist](15012.md) [Palestinian](23267.md) [paramilitary](146730.md) organization formed in 1981.[^cite_note-3]

PIJ formed as an offshoot of [Egyptian Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Islamic_Jihad) and was influenced ideologically in its formation by the [Islamic Revolution](347268.md) in Iran. It is a member of the [Alliance of Palestinian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Palestinian_Forces), which rejects the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) and whose objective is the establishment of a sovereign [Islamic](2646438.md) [Palestinian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_statehood).[^cite_note-WIIJ-4] It [calls for the military destruction of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calls_for_the_destruction_of_Israel) and rejects a [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution).[^cite_note-ANS-5] The organization's financial backing has historically come mainly from [Iran](14653.md), [Syria](7515849.md) and [Hezbollah](13919.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Since 2014, PIJ has seen its power steadily increase with the backing of funds from Iran.[^cite_note-8] PIJ has notably participated in the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md) (2023–present), fighting alongside [Hamas](13913.md) and other [allied Palestinian factions](75192468.md).[^cite_note-\:2-9][^cite_note-\:3-10][^cite_note-\:4-11]

The armed wing of PIJ is [Al-Quds Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds_Brigades) (also known as "Saraya"), also formed in 1981, which is active in the [West Bank](33209.md) and the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), with its main strongholds in the West Bank being the cities of [Hebron](38577.md) and [Jenin](50997.md). Its operations have included [suicide bombings](18835454.md), attacks on Israeli civilians, as well as the firing of rockets into Israel, targeting civilians. PIJ has been designated a [terrorist organization](63578.md) by the United States,[^cite_note-12] the European Union,[^cite_note-13] the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-14] Canada,[^cite_note-15] Australia,[^cite_note-ANS-5] New Zealand,[^cite_note-NZ-r1373-terrorlist-16][^cite_note-17] Israel,[^cite_note-18] and Japan.[^cite_note-19]

PIJ is considered by Israel the most extreme Palestinian militant organization in its methods and its ideology.[^cite_note-ReichGoldberg-20]

In 2026, PIJ militants consented to the [Board of Peace](81274522.md)'s disarmament plan for Gaza.[^cite_note-21]

## History and background



Ramadan Abdullah Mohammad Shallah was wanted by the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI) for conspiracy to conduct the affairs of the designated international [terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organization) known as the "Palestinian Islamic Jihad".

### Establishment

PIJ was formally established in [Gaza](12047.md) in 1981 by six Palestinian activists, most notably: [Fathi abd al-Aziz Shaqaqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fathi_Shaqaqi), a [Rafah](584373.md)-based physician, [Shaykh Abd al-Aziz Awda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_Al_Aziz_Awda), an Islamic preacher from the Jabaliyya refugee camp, as well as [Ramadan Shalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_Shalah), Bashir Moussa. Based in [Egypt](8087628.md), Shaqaqi and Awda were originally members of the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md). Their views on the necessity of the destruction of Israel led them in 1979 to establish the [Egyptian Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Islamic_Jihad) branch Egyptian Islamic Jihad-Shaqaqi Faction and conduct militant operations out of Egypt.[^cite_note-Wiktorowicz2004-22] After the [assassination of Anwar Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Anwar_Sadat), the Egyptian president the Shaqaqi faction was expelled from Egypt due to Egypt uncovering the groups connections to the perpetrators.[^cite_note-\:6-23] Shaqaqi and Awda returned to Gaza where they formally established PIJ,[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-Marlowe-25] from where it continued its operations.

The aim of the organization was the establishment of a sovereign [Islamic](2646438.md) [Palestinian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_statehood) within the geographic borders of [pre-1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_State_of_Palestine) [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine). Completely rejecting the political process, the organization professes that its goals can only be achieved through military means.[^cite_note-CFR-26][^cite_note-Palestinian_Islamic_Jihad-27] PIJ began its armed operations against Israel in 1984.[^cite_note-auto-28] Palestinian Islamic Jihad only announced its existence publicly on December 11th 1987, but its existence was already widely known.[^cite_note-29]

### Intifada era

During the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) PIJ would use both terrorism and civil resistance, initially the PIJ had only 300 members but they had far more ideological supporters which gave them disproportionate influence for their size. Early in the Intifada it seemed likely that the group would merge with the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) largely due to their mutual initial disapproval of violence at the time. But this never happened because the [UNLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_National_Leadership_of_the_Uprising) disliked the PIJ and prevented the merger, though the groups still cooperated periodically. In the first month of the Intifada it was very active in organizing and calling for protests but after the start of 1988 the groups decreased in activity and was crippled in March 1988 when the IDF arrested many of the leaders and members. It took the group until [Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn) of 1988 to regroup and after it did it took on a more militant approach and ended its relationship with the PLO.[^cite_note-\:03-30]

In 1988, its leaders were exiled by Israel to [Lebanon](17771.md). While in Lebanon, the group received training, support and other backing from [Hezbollah](13919.md) and [its backers in Iran](75617077.md), and developed a close relationship with the organization. In 1990, the headquarters of PIJ moved to the [Syrian](7515849.md) capital, [Damascus](8914.md), where it continues to be based,[^cite_note-auto-28] with offices in [Beirut](37428.md), [Tehran](57654.md), and [Khartoum](17320.md). PIJ masterminded several [suicide attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_suicide_attacks) in Israel.[^cite_note-hjournal-31] From the late 80's to mid 90's PIJ had a rivalry with Hamas as they competed for sway over more radical Palestinians, though as time went on they would grow closer.[^cite_note-32]

By 1993 PIJ had reached its strongest point yet, but the establishment of the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) and the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) marked a severe downturn in the groups fortunes. Renewed hope in peace among Palestinian led to a crash in the popularity of PIJ, which continued to advocate a violent approach, while security co-operation between Israel and the PA led to many PIJ members and leader being killed or imprisoned, by the late 1990's the movement had been nearly wiped out. The [Second Intifada](71717.md) though allowed for a resurgence in the group, growing lawlessness and a breakdown in Israel-PA cooperation eased military pressure while the Palestinian populace had shifted to a more violent mentality. But as Israeli counterinsurgency efforts picked up steam the Second Intifada came to an end the group was once again but on the defensive, being temporarily eliminated from the West Bank by 2005.[^cite_note-\:01-33]

### Post-Intifadas

During the [2006 Palestinian legislative elections](38945340.md) and subsequent [Battle of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)), PIJ believed that internal division would weaken the Palestinian movement and that Palestinian violence's only legitimate target was Israel and so worked with the [PFLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) and [DFLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) to mend ties between [Fatah](11104.md) and [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-\:1-34]

In February 2012, the [Hamas government in the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas_government_in_the_Gaza_Strip) distanced itself from PIJ. During the [March 2012 Gaza–Israel clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2012_Gaza%E2%80%93Israel_clashes), which followed the Israeli assassination of [Popular Resistance Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Resistance_Committees) leader, [Zuhir al-Qaisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuhir_al-Qaisi), who bragged of kidnapping [Gilad Shalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilad_Shalit), PIJ and PRC opened attacks on Israel. Hamas refrained from joining PIJ and PRC in attacking Israel.[^cite_note-IBZ_14Mar2012-35] Over a hundred [Palestinians](23267.md) were killed or injured in the ensuing violence, but no Hamas targets were killed.[^cite_note-Tab-36] The subsequent ceasefire was negotiated between Israel and the militant groups, not Hamas.

In May 2015, London-based Arabic newspaper *[Asharq al-Awsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asharq_al-Awsat)* reported that Iran had stopped funding PIJ due to the group's neutrality over the [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen), throwing PIJ into a severe financial crisis. Iran had expected PIJ to condemn the intervention led by Saudi Arabia, Iran's chief regional rival. Palestinian newspaper *[al-Quds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds_\(newspaper\))* reported that Iran began supporting an offshoot of PIJ called [as-Sabirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabireen_Movement) (Arabic for "the patient ones"), headed by Islamic Jihad veteran Hisham Salem.[^cite_note-37]

Under the former leadership of Ramadan Shalah, the group would receive approximately US\$70 million per year from the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps of Iran.[^cite_note-38]

In 2021 the [Jenin Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenin_Brigades), an umbrella group of various militant groups in [Jenin](50997.md), of which PIJ is a part of would be founded. In 2022 the [Tulkarm Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulkarm_Brigade), [Nablus Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Brigade), [Tubas Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubas_Brigade), [Nur Shams Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Shams_Brigade), [Qabatiya Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qabatiya_Brigade) would be founded as similar local umbrella groups. And in 2023 the [Ramallah Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramallah_Brigade) would be founded, which unlike the other brigades only consisted of PIJ.

In 2026, PIJ militants agreed to the [Board of Peace](81274522.md) disarmament plan for Gaza.[^cite_note-39]

### United States PIJ interactions

On 20 February 2003, [University of South Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Florida) [computer engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_engineering) professor [Sami Al-Arian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_Al-Arian) was arrested after being indicted on a terrorism-related charge. [U.S. attorney general](44002.md) [John Ashcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ashcroft) alleged at a [press conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_conference) that Al-Arian was the [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American) head of the Palestinian Islamic Jihad. On 6 December 2006, Sami Al-Arian was sentenced to 57 months in prison, pursuant to a [plea bargain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plea_bargain).[^cite_note-40] In November 2006 he was found guilty of civil contempt for refusing to testify before a federal [grand jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_jury) and served 21 months in prison on that conviction. On 27 June 2014, the US Federal Government dropped all charges against Al-Arian.[^cite_note-41] On 23 January 1995, the founder of PIJ, [Abd Al Aziz Awda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_Al_Aziz_Awda), was indicted under United States law and added in 2006 to the United States [FBI Most Wanted Terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_Most_Wanted_Terrorists) list.[^cite_note-42]

On June 30th 2026 Catherine Beth Washburn the leader of [Direct Action Movement for Palestinian Liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Action_Movement_for_Palestinian_Liberation?action=edit\&redlink=1) an anti-Zionist [domestic extremist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism_in_the_United_States) group was arrested for donating \$30,116 to PIJ. She expressed [extremist](428002.md), [antisemitic](1078.md), and anti-Zionist views.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

## Ideology, motives and beliefs

Main article: [Ideology of Palestinian Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology_of_Palestinian_Islamic_Jihad)

### Goals and Methods

In a series of [by-laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-law) PIJ identified it's aims as such:[^cite_note-\:5-45]

1. The [Islamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam) of [Palestinian identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_identity)

2. The instigation of general popular revolution amongst Palestinians against Israel

3. The realization of the Islamic unity through collective jihad

4. The [destruction of Israel](40229800.md)

The group also identified its methods as such:[^cite_note-\:5-45]

1. The creation of a "state of terror, instability, and panic in the souls of Zionists and especially the groups of [settlers](15123.md), forcing them to leave their homes"

2. The creation of a "psychological barrier between Jews and the Muslim Palestinian people” and the “conviction that [coexistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_process) is impossible.”

### Anti-Zionism

Palestinian Islamic Jihad's ideological foundations lie in a synthesis of [Palestinian Nationalism](47246228.md), which it viewed as too [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularity), and Jihadi Islamism, which it viewed as not focused enough on Palestine, combining the two into a Islam focused Palestinian Nationalism.[^cite_note-\:01-33] Yehudit Barsky, a Middle East [counterterrorism](83392.md) specialist, explains that the PIJ views itself as “revolutionary vanguard" who have taken up the responsibility of purifying the Islamic world by eliminating all western influence within it. They believe that Israel is the primary western outpost in the Muslim World through which it disseminates its anti-islamic influence. In these goals the PIJ believes that terrorism and the intentional killing of civilians are acceptable practices and that "Jihad is the solution to liberate Palestine and topple the Infidel regimes".[^cite_note-\:5-45]

In his 2007 book *Facts and Stances* PIJ’s second secretary-general [Ramadan Abdallah Shallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_Shalah), explained the three reasons Palestine is the groups primary focus in what it views as an international Islamic struggle.[^cite_note-\:01-33]

> the religious and legal dimension, which presents Palestine in the religion as a blessed land in the text of the Qur’an This dimension also shows the inevitability of confrontation between us and the [Jews](25955086.md), and this is what is confirmed by the [Qur’an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) and the [sunna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah), in particular by surat al-isra’...
>
> The second is the historical dimension that places the [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist_as_a_pejorative) offensive within the context of war, the Jews as historical enemies, and their cunning against the religion since the emergence of the \[*[Arabic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script)*] until the present day. it also includes the Western enemies since the [Battle of Muta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mu'tah) and [Tabuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_Tabuk) via the [crusader wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) down to the inculcation of the Zionist entity into the heart of the [umma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah), Palestine. That is, the Zionist entity as the spearhead of the Western project, and as the culmination of its recent attack on the Islamic East, which began as the [campaign of Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Egypt_and_Syria) in the late eighteenth century...
>
> The third is the de facto dimension, appearing through what "Israel" represents in current reality as a colonialist base and military barracks to protect Western and American interests in the region, and through what is caused by its presence and role in the region. "Israel" is thus a threat not only to Palestine but also to the whole umma.[^cite_note-\:01-33]

&#x20;In 2009 interview with [Al-Jazeera](30204052.md), Ramadan Abdullah Shallah rejected the [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution), said "the one-state solution is better than the two-state solution" and accused the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) of being complicit with the Israeli occupation.[^cite_note-46]



PIJ's founder [Ramadan Shalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_Shalah) (right) and Hamas leader [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal) in Tehran, Iran, 14 April 2006

In a 2002 book, [Yonah Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonah_Alexander) wrote that PIJ [calls for the destruction of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calls_for_the_destruction_of_Israel), rejects the option of a peaceful settlement with Israel, and advocates the use of guerrilla tactics to achieve that aim. Israel's eradication is viewed as an essential prerequisite for addressing the challenges facing the Muslim world. It envisions laying the groundwork for a future scenario in which a significant Islamic Arab army engages in military confrontation to achieve Israel's destruction. The PIJ has been accountable for some of the deadliest [suicide attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_suicide_attacks) in Israel.[^cite_note-\:0-47]

Unlike Hamas, which engages in unofficial talks with Israel in matters such as ceasefire negotiations and prisoner exchanges. PIJ absolutely and entirely rejects any negotiations with Israel.[^cite_note-\:6-23]

### Islamic Fundamentalism

The group is a Sunni Jihadist movement but includes other religious beliefs.[^cite_note-JihadisminPIJ-48] PIJ is a proponent of [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam)-[Shia](26961.md) unity and reconciliation, believing that the divisions between the two sects are only a minor issue to be ignored to focus on the conflict against Israel and the West.[^cite_note-\:5-45]

After the destruction of Israel, PIJ ideology calls for the restoration of an Islamist [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) in the style of the [Rashidun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate) and [Umayyad Caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate). Some PIJ thinkers have said that this caliphate would then go on to destroy the West, but the group as a whole has not adopted this idea. PIJ ideas for after the destruction of Israel are much less developed compared to other aspects of their beliefs.[^cite_note-\:5-45]

## Organization

Palestinians Islamic Jihad is governed by a council of 15 members headed by a secretary-general whom the council elects. 9 of the council members are elected from within the organization reportedly every four years, 2 are appointed by the secretary-general, and 4 are chosen by the other 11 members.[^cite_note-\:6-23]

PIJ leadership has been based in Syria since 1989, though rumors exist that the leadership relocated to Iran in 2012, though this is denied by the group. A US report claims that PIJ leadership is dispersed throughout the middle east, though with many being based in Lebanon.[^cite_note-\:6-23]

The groups military wing, the [al-Quds Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds_Brigades) is divided into geographically based [brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade) each of which is overseen by a regional staff command. The brigades are then further subdivided into cells each of which is operated by a single member of the brigades regional staff command.[^cite_note-\:6-23]

The Palestinian Islamic Jihad pledges that armed resistance will continue as long as Israel [maintains the occupation](2781576.md) and conducts military operations against Palestinians.[^cite_note-Rudolph-49] Nonetheless, it states "the decision to end resistance belongs to that of the people". PIJ has employed suicide bombing as a tactic in realization that Palestinian military capabilities are outmatched by Israeli sophisticated weaponry.[^cite_note-Rudolph-49] Its objectives are to "maximum\[ize] amount of loss to the Israeli military".[^cite_note-Rudolph-49] PIJ states that their policy is that targeting innocent civilians is prohibited.[^cite_note-Rudolph-49] However, it allows for targeting Israeli civilians in response to Israeli targeting of Palestinian civilians. Nevertheless, PIJ states that in such scenarios they prioritize targeting the Israeli military over targeting civilians. Finally, PIJ states they reject any use of force against non-Israelis.[^cite_note-Rudolph-49] PIJ has also [deployed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel) its own rocket, similar to the Qassam rocket used by Hamas, called the [al-Quds rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds_rocket).

Three of the attackers were killed.

### Social services

Islamic Jihad also control dozens of religious organizations in the [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories) that are registered as NGOs and operate [mosques](19894.md), schools, and medical facilities that offer free services.[^cite_note-p69-75] Like other Islamic associations, these are heavily scrutinized by the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) who have shut some of them down.[^cite_note-p69-75] In one Islamic Jihad kindergarten graduation, children dressed up in military uniforms, waved guns, shouted anti-Israel slogans, and spoke of blowing themselves up to kill "Zionists".[^cite_note-Yedioth_Ahronot-76][^cite_note-77]

Islamic Jihad also operates dozens of [summer camps](926264.md) for children. They have opened up 51 summer camps which attracted approximately 10,000 children in 2010.

> We teach the children the truth. How the Jews persecuted the prophets and tortured them. We stress that the Jews killed and slaughtered Arabs and Palestinians every chance they got. Most important, the children understand that the conflict with the Jews is not over land, but rather over religion. As long as Jews remain here, between the \[Jordan] river and the \[Mediterranean] sea, they will be our enemy and we will continue to pursue and kill them. When they leave we won't hurt them.[^cite_note-78]

### Funding

According to an [Australian](4689264.md) government report, PIJ's primary source of funding is Iranian support, but the groups also received support from [Ba'athist Syria](57134800.md) and receives donations from Palestinians. Iran has used its support for the PIJ as leverage against Hamas, when Hamas refused to support Ba'athist Syria in the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md) Iran sent two million dollars of aid to PIJ instead of Hamas.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-\:6-23]

### Child Soldiers

PIJ has used minors for its operations. On 29 March 2004, 15-year-old Tamer Khuweir of [Rifidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifidia?action=edit\&redlink=1), a suburb of the Palestinian city of [Nablus](233369.md) in the West Bank, was captured by Israeli forces as he planned to carry out a suicide mission. His older brother claimed he was brainwashed and demanded the Palestinian Authority investigate the incident and arrest those responsible for it.[^cite_note-80]
