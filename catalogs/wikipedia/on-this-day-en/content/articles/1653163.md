In [diplomacy](28165911.md), an **attaché** (French pronunciation: [\[ataʃe\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/92/Fr-attach%C3%A9.ogg/Fr-attach%C3%A9.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/92/Fr-attach%C3%A9.ogg/Fr-attach%C3%A9.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a person who is assigned ("to be attached") to the diplomatic or administrative staff of a higher placed person or another service or agency. Although a [loanword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanword) from [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), in English the word is not modified according to [gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_grammar#Gender).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

An attaché is normally an official, who serves either as a [diplomat](82433.md) or as a member of the support staff, under the authority of an [ambassador](68651.md) or other head of a [diplomatic mission](8970.md), mostly in [intergovernmental organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_organizations) or [international non-governmental organisations or agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_non-governmental_organisation). Attachés monitor various issues related to their area of specialty (see examples below) that may require some action. To this end, attachés may undertake the planning for events to be attended, decisions which will be taken, managing arrangements and agendas, conducting research, and acting as a representative of the interests of their state when necessary, to the types of organizations mentioned above, and also to [national academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_academy) and to industry.

Sometimes an attaché has special responsibilities or expertise. Examples include a [cultural attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_attach%C3%A9), customs attaché, police officer attaché, labor attaché, legal attaché, liaison officer attaché, [military/defense attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_attach%C3%A9), [press attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicity#Publicists), [agricultural attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_attach%C3%A9), commercial attaché, maritime attaché and [science attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_attach%C3%A9).

## Military attaché

Main article: [Military attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_attach%C3%A9)

Typically, a military attaché serves on the diplomatic staff of an embassy or consulate while retaining a military commission.

## Science attaché

Main article: [Science attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_attach%C3%A9)

A science attaché advises on issues pertaining to science and technology.[^cite_note-3]

## Health attaché

A health attaché advises on global health issues and may serve multiple functions.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] A "diplomat who collects, analyzes, and acts on information concerning health in a foreign country or countries and provides critical links between public health and foreign affairs stakeholders."[^cite_note-6] Earlier known more as *Medical Attaché. Health attaches are the missing link for global diplomacy.*[^cite_note-7]

## Holy See

The title is also used in reference to diplomacy and in the hierarchical administration of the [Catholic Church](606848.md), specifically in the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia), in cases where a priest, usually in the diplomatic corps of the Holy See or else released for service to the [Holy See](13393.md), serves in a [nunciature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunciature) in a given country or to an international or [intergovernmental organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_organization). Especially in the latter cases, the official often provides a particular expertise in the service of the Church, thus, legal or otherwise.
