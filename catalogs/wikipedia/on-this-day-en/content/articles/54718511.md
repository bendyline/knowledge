**Bang Si-hyuk** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 방시혁; born August 9, 1972), also known as "**Hitman**" **Bang**, is a South Korean [music executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_executive), record producer and songwriter who is the co-founder of [JYP Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JYP_Entertainment),[^cite_note-JYPEnt-1] founder of record label [Big Hit Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Hit_Music), and [chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_\(officer\)) of [HYBE Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybe_Corporation).&#x20;

One of the 50 [wealthiest people in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_Korean_billionaires_by_net_worth) according to *[Forbes Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*, Bang is the only billionaire in the South Korean entertainment industry. As of April 2026, his reported net worth is an estimated \$1.25 billion, according to [*Forbes* 2026 Korea's 50 Richest List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes).[^cite_note-2]

## Early life and education

Bang was born on August 9, 1972[^cite_note-3] to Bang Geuk-yoon, former president of the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Corporation at the Korea Social Insurance Research Institute,[^cite_note-4] and his wife, Choi Myung-ja.[^cite_note-Today's_Korea-5] Bang has a younger sister.[^cite_note-6]

Bang developed a passion for music from an early age, having grown up in a musical home, but was discouraged from pursuing a career in the industry by his parents.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-KoreaHerald2017-8] Bang formed a band with his friends while in middle school and wrote and performed songs he composed.[^cite_note-Today's_Korea-5][^cite_note-9] He attended [Kyunggi High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyunggi_High_School) before graduating from [Seoul National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_National_University) with a degree in [aesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics).[^cite_note-BSH-10]

## Career

Bang debuted as a composer while in college.[^cite_note-BSH-10] In 1994, he won the [Yoo Jae-ha Music Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoo_Jae-ha_Music_Contest) and began his career in earnest writing and producing songs.[^cite_note-11] He met [Park Jin-young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.Y._Park) in 1997, and the pair often partnered together as a songwriting duo. Bang joined [JYP Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JYP_Entertainment),[^cite_note-JYPEnt-1] becoming a composer, arranger and producer at the company.[^cite_note-KoreaHerald2017-8][^cite_note-JoongAng.20100330-12][^cite_note-13] One of their early successes was the first-generation group [g.o.d](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.o.d).[^cite_note-JoongAng.20100330-12] Some of g.o.d's most famous songs [arranged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrangement) by Bang include "[One Candle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Candle)" and "[Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_\(g.o.d_song\))". Bang's stage name "Hitman" originated from this period, when g.o.d enjoyed success as one of the country's best-selling and most popular boy bands of the early 2000s, earning himself and Park a reputation as "hit makers".[^cite_note-JoongAng.20100330-12][^cite_note-NPR-14] Besides g.o.d, Bang has produced or composed for many other artists, including veterans [Im Chang-jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Im_Chang-jung) and [Park Ji-yoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Ji-yoon), singer-actor [Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_\(entertainer\)), groups [Wonder Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Girls), [2AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2AM_\(band\)), and [Teen Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teen_Top), and R\&B singer [Baek Ji-young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baek_Ji-young).[^cite_note-JoongAng.20100330-12][^cite_note-NPR-14][^cite_note-15]

Nearly four years after joining JYP Entertainment, Bang left JYP and founded his own company, [Big Hit Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybe_Corporation), where he continued to write, compose and produce for its artists.[^cite_note-NPR-14][^cite_note-16] Under the Big Hit label, he signed up [RM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RM_\(musician\)), at age 15, who would go on to lead the boy band [BTS](39862325.md).[^cite_note-\:0-17] He co-wrote six songs for BTS in their 2016 album *[Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wings_\(BTS_album\))*. Its success garnered Bang the Best Producer Award at the [Mnet Asian Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAMA_Awards) and the Songwriter Award at the [Melon Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melon_Music_Awards) that year. In June 2018, he was named one of [*Variety*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))'s International Music Leaders due to BTS' achievements.[^cite_note-18]

In April 2019, [Bloomberg](3057255.md) estimated Bang's worth to be about \$770 million.[^cite_note-19] The BigHit [initial public offering](63879.md) (IPO) filed in October 2020 was the largest seen in South Korea in three years and skyrocketed Bang's net worth to \$2.8 billion,[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] making him the only billionaire in South Korea's entertainment industry and the sixth-richest person in the country.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The success of the IPO, together with the commercial success of BTS that year, Bang's steps into diversification with apps like [Weverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weverse), and acquisitions of companies like [Source Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_Music) and [Pledis Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pledis_Entertainment) among others, landed him on the 2020 edition of [*Variety*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))'s *Variety500*, an annual index of the 500 most influential business leaders in the global media industry.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

In June 2021, *[Forbes Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* published its annual list of [Korea's 50 Richest People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_Korean_billionaires_by_net_worth). One of only two newcomers to the ranking, Bang was listed as the 16th richest person in South Korea.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] On July 1, following an organizational restructuring, [Hybe](41697032.md) announced Bang's resignation as CEO of the company to return his focus to music production. He was replaced by Park Ji-won, but retained his position as Chairman of the Board of Directors.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] According to the *Bloomberg* Billionaires Index, Bang is worth an estimated \$3.2 billion as of July 2021.[^cite_note-31]

Bang was featured on the cover of the April 2022 issue of *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine, together with BTS, following the release of the outlet's annual list of the 100 Most Influential Companies in March.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Later that month, he received an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) in Business from [Seoul National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_National_University)—he is the first figure in the entertainment industry to be awarded such—in recognition of his "contribution to the development and innovation of the nation's cultural and entertainment industry" and for his "role in turning K-pop into a global pop music culture".[^cite_note-34]

### Fraud investigation

In December 2024, South Korea's [Financial Supervisory Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Supervisory_Service) (FSS) launched an investigation into allegations that, ahead of Hybe's 2020 [IPO](63879.md), Bang entered into profit-sharing agreements with private equity funds without proper public disclosure to the market.[^cite_note-35] Those funds, linked to Bang, then purchased BigHit shares after inducing early investors to sell without informing them of the upcoming IPO. The funds subsequently sold those shares for a profit circumventing the IPO lock-up period.[^cite_note-\:1-36] Hybe denied any legal violation, stating that "the shareholder agreement in question" had been reviewed by IPO underwriters during the listing process and deemed not mandatory for disclosure.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

On July 16, 2025, the [Financial Services Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Services_Commission_\(South_Korea\)) (FSC) formally referred Bang and three other executives to prosecutors on suspicion of unfair trading related to the IPO.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] A week later, on July 24, police raided Hybe's headquarters in Seoul as part of the investigation. Hybe stated it was fully cooperating with authorities and expressed its intent to prove compliance with all laws and regulations.[^cite_note-41]

In August 2025, Bang voluntarily returned to South Korea to cooperate with the investigation.[^cite_note-42] On August 6, 2025, he sent a company-wide message to employees expressing regret over the concern caused, stating he had already offered a comprehensive explanation to the FSS, and stating that he was "temporarily putting aside urgent work and business meetings in order to prioritize cooperating with the authorities’ investigation process".[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] He was set to be questioned on September 15, 2025.[^cite_note-45]

In December 2025, the [Seoul Southern District Court](79100371.md#List_of_high_courts) approved the [Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Police_Agency)'s request in November to provisionally seize his shares in Hybe worth 156.8 billion won (approx. US\$118 million).[^cite_note-46]

On December 18, 2025, it was announced that the FSS's special judicial police launched an investigation into Bang, along with carrying out raids on his home and offices.[^cite_note-47]

In April 2026, South Korean police sought an arrest warrant for Bang.[^cite_note-48] In September, the police referred the case to Seoul Southern District prosecutors after obtaining a court order to freeze 263 billion won (approx. US\$193 million) of profit that Bang had gained from the scheme.[^cite_note-\:1-36]

## Philanthropy

In October 2022, Bang donated 5 billion won (approximately \$3.5 million) to the Community Chest of Korea.[^cite_note-49] On February 17, 2023, Bang donated 200 million won through Save the Children to help in the [2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Turkey%E2%80%93Syria_earthquakes).[^cite_note-50] In March 2025, Bang donated 5 billion won (approx. US\$3.7 million) to [Seoul National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_National_University) for the construction of a new Cultural Center.[^cite_note-51]
