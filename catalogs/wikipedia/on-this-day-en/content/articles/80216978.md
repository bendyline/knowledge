**Muhammad Abd al-Karim al-Ghamari** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): محمد عبد الكريم الغماري; c. 1979–1984 – 28 August 2025) was a Yemeni militant who served as the [Chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_General_Staff) of the [Houthi](17240332.md)-led faction of the [Yemeni Armed Forces](34252.md), holding the rank of Major General.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-4]

## Early life

Al-Ghamari was born in 1979 or 1984 in Izla Dhaen, [Washhah district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washhah_district), [Hajjah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjah_Governorate), [Yemen](350939.md).[^cite_note-5] He studied at the Hussain Badr al‑Din al‑Houthi Institute in 2003.[^cite_note-\:5-6]

## Career

Al-Ghamari's first military activity was in 2004, when he oversaw the first Houthi explosives-planting team.[^cite_note-\:3-7] After the death of [Hussein al-Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_al-Houthi) that year, he became responsible for relocating "al-Muhajiroin", the name for fighters from outside the [Saada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada) area, to Saada's [Bani Mu'adh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bani_Mu'adh?action=edit\&redlink=1) area, where they received training to be integrated into the Houthi military. He merged the group's tribal components, which it called the "People's Committees", into their [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Yemen\)). He later promoted the construction of ammunition and explosives manufacturing facilities and oversaw the recruitment of explosives experts into the militia.[^cite_note-\:4-8]

Al-Ghamari fought in all six wars against the Yemeni government between 2004 and 2010.[^cite_note-\:5-6] In 2005, al-Ghamari established the "Death Brigades" and the "Hussein Brigades". He was appointed as a supervisor in [Hajjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjah) in 2007, and that same year he participated in the establishment and arming of the militia's first suicide units.[^cite_note-\:6-9][^cite_note-\:4-8] He was arrested by the Yemeni government in 2008.[^cite_note-\:4-8] He served as a field commander against government forces in [Maran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maran,_Yemen?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Razamat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razamat?action=edit\&redlink=1), and in 2011 he was a commander during the [battle of Saada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Saada).[^cite_note-\:6-9] He also participated in the 2014 [battle of Sanaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sanaa_\(2014\)).[^cite_note-\:5-6]

Between 2005 and 2009, he traveled to Syria and southern Lebanon, where he was trained by military officers supervised by [Hezbollah](13919.md) and Iran.[^cite_note-\:3-7] During a visit to [Tehran](57654.md) in 2009, he learned how to use rocket and artillery shells.[^cite_note-\:5-6] In 2012, he visited [Beirut](37428.md)'s southern suburbs, where he received ideological and military training by Hezbollah.[^cite_note-\:4-8]

### Chief of Staff

Al-Ghamari was appointed as chief of staff by [Supreme Political Council](51279323.md) head [Saleh Ali al-Sammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleh_Ali_al-Sammad). He also took responsibility for defense, oversaw Ministry of Defense and military activities, and oversaw the theft and refurbishment of [Yemeni Armed Forces](34252.md) missiles in coordination with Iranian specialists.[^cite_note-\:4-8]

He was a part of the Houthis' Jihad Office, which oversaw the militia's military operations.[^cite_note-10]

Al-Ghamari coordinated military campaigns, including cross-border missile/drone strikes in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), the [UAE](69328.md), and latterly at [Israel](9282173.md). He led offensives in [Marib province](3604391.md) (2021–22), which threatened nearly a million internally displaced people.[^cite_note-\:2-11] During the [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md), al-Ghamari commanded missile and drone attacks against Israel as well as the [attacks on international shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_attacks_on_commercial_vessels) in the [Red Sea](26191.md).[^cite_note-12]

## Sanctions & international status

He was sanctioned by the [U.S. Treasury](53667.md) in May 2021 as a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Designated_Nationals_and_Blocked_Persons_List) for coordinating offensive operations,[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-\:1-13] and added to [UN Security Council’s sanctions list](31956.md) in November 2021 for threatening Yemeni peace and stability and cross-border aggression.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-\:2-11]

## Assassination

On 14 June 2025, the [IDF](39237.md) stated that it had targeted al-Ghamari.[^cite_note-14] An Israeli official later stated that he had been wounded in the attack, though this was denied by the Houthis.[^cite_note-15] Some Israeli media reports had initially claimed that al-Ghamari was targeted while he was meeting other Houthi leaders; however, the [Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Public_Broadcasting_Corporation) later stated that he was at a [khat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khat)-chewing party.[^cite_note-16]

On 28 August 2025, the IDF [again targeted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28_August_2025_Israeli_attacks_on_Yemen) al-Ghamari. Two months later, on 16 October, the Houthis confirmed that al-Ghamari had died, alongside several associates and his 13-year-old son.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] That same day, the IDF confirmed that al-Ghamari was killed on 28 August.[^cite_note-19]

After his death was announced, the Houthis held a funeral ceremony for al-Ghamari and his son in Sanaa on 20 October 2025.[^cite_note-20]
