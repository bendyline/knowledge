# Basketball scorekeeping



A [basketball scoreboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoreboard#Basketball)

**Basketball scorekeeping** is the practice of tracking the [statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_statistics) of a [basketball](3921.md) game throughout the duration of play. Points serve as the measure of scorekeeping, determining the ongoing score, and are accumulated by making [field goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_\(basketball\)) and [free throws](1098087.md).[^cite_note-how-1] The team with the most points when the game ends is declared the winner.

## History

The first method of basketball scorekeeping came about in 1891 when [James Naismith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Naismith), the inventor of basketball, published his original 13 rules of the game.[^cite_note-2] According to Rule 13, the winning team was decided by who scored the most number of goals.[^cite_note-3] The very first basketball game ended with a score of 1–0, equating a single point to each goal.[^cite_note-4] By 1896, the scoring system changed, representing a goal—now called a field goal—as 2 points, and free throws as 1 point.[^cite_note-5]

Decades later, the three-pointer emerged into a professional league in 1961, awarding the shotmaker an apt 3 points, when the [American Basketball League (ABL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_League_\(1961%E2%80%931962\)) painted the first official three-point line.[^cite_note-6] After the ABL folded in 1962, years passed before the NBA finally gave it a one-year trial in 1979, which they ultimately kept.[^cite_note-7] By 1986, the NCAA adopted it, but each major conference had different distances for their three-point lines.[^cite_note-8] And a year later, in 1987, the [National Federation of State High School Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Federation_of_State_High_School_Associations) (NFHS) implemented the three-point line across high schools.[^cite_note-9]

## Scoring

When a player successfully shoots the [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_\(ball\)) through the opponents hoop, their team is awarded between 1 and 3 points. This point value is determined by three primary shot types:



Free throw being shot by LA-Lafayette's Makayia Hallmon against the Texas-Arlington Mavericks in 2022

### [Free throw](1098087.md) (or foul shot)

**1 point** is scored for a team when its player makes an unguarded shot from the free throw line. Free throws are awarded from shooting fouls, [personal fouls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_foul_\(basketball\)) that exceed the [team foul limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonus_\(basketball\)), or [technical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_foul) and [flagrant fouls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagrant_foul) committed against a player.[^cite_note-10] Referees are solely responsible for calling fouls that can result in free throw opportunities, during which the game clock is stopped. It should also be noted that a free throw is not a field goal.[^cite_note-11] Typically, free throws are tracked as a separate statistic in official scorekeeping.

### [Two-point field goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_\(basketball\))

**2 points** are scored for a team when its player makes a field goal from anywhere inside the three-point line.[^cite_note-how-1] [Layups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layup), [slam dunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slam_dunk), post shots, and mid-range [jump shots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jump_shot_\(basketball\)) all award the same number of points despite their differences in distance and difficulty. Unlike free throws, the game clock does not stop when a field goal is scored, as the ball is considered in play.[^cite_note-12] Additionally, if the scoring player is fouled in the act of shooting, a made free throw turns the two-point field goal into a [three-point play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-point_play).[^cite_note-13]

### [Three-point field goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-point_field_goal)

**3 points** are scored for a team when its player makes a field goal from anywhere outside the three-point line.[^cite_note-how-1] This shot is worth an extra point compared to a standard two-point field goal because it is generally more difficult to convert and relatively less common to shoot.[^cite_note-14] The shooter must not be touching the three-point line before when attempting the shot. They are often recorded as their own statistic on the scoresheet, while also contributing to the overall field goal totals.[^cite_note-15] Additionally, if the scoring player is fouled in the act of shooting, a made free throw turns the field goal into a [four-point play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-point_play).[^cite_note-16]
