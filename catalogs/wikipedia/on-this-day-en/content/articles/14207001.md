The **Type 10** (10式戦車, *Hitomaru-shiki sensha*) is a Japanese [fourth generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_main_battle_tanks_by_generation) [main battle tank](26595097.md) produced by [Mitsubishi Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Heavy_Industries) for the [Japanese Ground Self Defense Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Ground_Self_Defense_Force). It entered service in 2012. Compared with other [currently-serving main battle tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_modern_equipment_of_the_Japan_Ground_Self-Defense_Force#Vehicles) in the JGSDF, the Type 10 is better equipped to deal with [anti-tank weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare).[^cite_note-type1003-3]

## History

The Type 10 started as the TK-X (MBT-X) project, intended to complement and eventually replace the [Type 74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_74_tank) and [Type 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_90_Ky%C5%AB-maru) tanks in service with the JGSDF.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIchinohe2011-4] Development began in the 1990s, and production started in 2010–2011. A prototype was revealed on February 13, 2008 at the Technology Research and Development Institute (TRDI) in [Sagamihara, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagamihara).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIchinohe2011-4] Emphasis was placed on the design's command and control capabilities.[^cite_note-type10tkx01-5]

On 4 January 2014, sources revealed that [Turkey](11125639.md) was interested in signing a joint development deal of tank engines based on the Type 10's engine. The Type 10 tank boasts high mobility, including a backward movement speed of 70 km/h (43 mph).[^cite_note-6] The engine was to power the Turkish [Altay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altay_\(main_battle_tank\)) tank. However, negotiations broke down, and the deal was "off the agenda" by March 2014. Provided reasons included Japan's stringent arms export ban laws, Turkey's intention to export the Altay themselves, and Japan's reluctance to license the engine.[^cite_note-7]

In May 2026, the Philippines has reportedly looked into the Type 10 due to delays with delivering the [Sabrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrah_light_tank).[^cite_note-dsa-8] Potentially, it can be used to either complement or replace the Sabrahs.[^cite_note-dsa-8]

On April 21, 2026, a Type 10 shell exploded during a live-fire exercise at the Hijudai Training Area in Oita Prefecture, leaving three JGSDF tank crew members dead and another injured.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Authorities have launched an investigation into the cause of the accident.

## Design

### Development history

In the early 2000s, the JGSDF recognized a need to field a fourth generation tank in order to compete on a modern battlefield. [C4I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C4I) (Command, Control, Communication, Computing and Intelligence) capability was considered a high priority in designing the Type 10.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIchinohe2011-4]

A Ministry of Defense assessment found that C4I upgrades to existing Type 74 and Type 90 tanks were not feasible, primarily due to a lack of internal space. Thus, development of a completely novel main battle tank was deemed necessary.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIchinohe2011-4]

### Armor

The use of [modular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_design) components significantly improves the side armor compared to the Type 90. The [commander's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_commander) panoramic sight was moved to the right, and is mounted higher compared to the Type 90, giving the commander a wider [field of view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_of_view).

The vehicle's armor consists of removable sections, allowing operators to balance weight and protection depending on the mission profile. The Type 10 weighs 40 tonnes (44 short tons) in its base configuration, 44 tonnes (49 short tons) in standard configuration, and 48 tonnes (53 short tons) fully loaded. The prototype featured in 2008 at TRDI weighed 44 tonnes.[^cite_note-type1001-11]

The Type 10 weighs less than its predecessor, the Type 90. The composite armor weight was increased, however, from 1,380 to 1,940 kg (3,040 to 4,280 lb) for the turret, and from 1,249 to 2,680 kg (2,754 to 5,908 lb) for the hull.

In a penetration resistance test, [APFSDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armour-piercing_fin-stabilized_discarding_sabot) rounds were fired at the hull from a distance of 250 m (270 yd).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Each armor module and [mantlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_mantlet) achieved the required performance specified in "Type 10 tank GV-Y120001E".

Protection against 120mm [kinetic energy penetrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy_penetrator) is limited to the front of the hull, turret, and gun mantlet, with the exception of the lower [glacis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacis).

The top armor can effectively counter [explosively formed penetrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosively_formed_penetrator) and related threats.[^cite_note-14]

### Electronics and mechanics

The Type 10 tank is equipped with a [C4I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C4I) system (command, control, communication, computer & intelligence) known as "10NW". This system integrates into the JGSDF network and enables real-time data sharing between tanks. It works alongside the Field Communication System (FiCS)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIchinohe2011-4] and the Regiment Command Control System (ReCS).[^cite_note-type1003-3]

According to design documentation, the C4I system enables the following capabilities:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDefense_Industrial_Production_Committee2014-15]

- [Platoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platoon)-level [automatic target recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_target_recognition) and target synchronization

- Real-time commands from a [platoon leader](4838780.md)

- Real-time vehicle [telemetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemetry)

- [Fire control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system) integration

- Daytime and [night vision cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night-vision_device) mounted around the turret, providing 360° coverage

### Armament

Whereas the Type 90 was equipped with the [Rheinmetall Rh-120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinmetall_Rh-120) smoothbore main gun (as on the [German](11867.md) [Leopard 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_2)), the Type 10 uses a novel 120mm gun developed by [Japan Steel Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Steel_Works).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIchinohe2011-4] The gun can fire the newly developed Type 10 [APFSDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armour-piercing_fin-stabilized_discarding-sabot) round.[^cite_note-18] It can also fire the JM33 APFSDS (a variant of the German [DM33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/120%C3%97570mm_NATO#Armour-Piercing_Fin-Stabilized_Discarding_Sabot_\(APFSDS\)) shell, produced domestically under license) as well as [120mm NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/120%C3%97570mm_NATO) ammunition. The Type 10 holds 14 rounds in the autoloader, 2 behind the gunner, 6 rounds in the ready ammunition storage, and 14 in reserve, with a total of 36 rounds carried on board.[^cite_note-19]

The gunner has access to a [Type 74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumitomo_Type_62#Type_74) [coaxial machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxial_machine_gun) chambered in [7.62×51mm NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7.62%C3%9751mm_NATO). The Type 74 is a heavier, vehicle-mounted variant of the [Sumitomo Type 62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumitomo_Type_62) machine gun.

A [M2 Browning .50 caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_Browning) machine gun chambered in [12.7x99mm NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12.7x99mm_NATO) is affixed to a [pintle-mount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pintle-mount) on the roof, and can be crewed by the commander.

### Strategic transportation

The predecessor of the Type 10, the Type 90, was deployed only in [Hokkaido](58092.md) due to road and bridge weight limitations in mainland Japan. For this reason, weight was a priority in design, as the Type 10 needed to be capable of deploying anywhere in Japan. Size and weight reductions made the Type 10 six tonnes lighter than the Type 90. Approximately 84% of bridges in Japan can accommodate the Type 10, compared to only 65% for the Type 90, and \~40% for other NATO tanks.[^cite_note-20]

### Development

The development costs as of 2008 are approximately [¥](34392.md)48.4 billion (equivalent to ¥54.2 billion or [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD)358.3 million in 2024)[^cite_note-inflation-JP-21]. The projected cost per unit was approximately [¥](34392.md)700 million (equivalent to ¥784.3 million or [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD)5.2 million in 2024)[^cite_note-inflation-JP-21].[^cite_note-type1001-11]

The [Japanese Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Japan\)) formally acknowledged the Type 10 in December 2009.

In 2010, the Japanese Ministry of Defense placed a [¥](34392.md)12.4 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)113 million) order for thirteen Type 10 tanks.[^cite_note-22]

The Type 10 entered service in January 2012,[^cite_note-23] with production continuing at a steady rate. As of 2020, there were 76 units in service, with plans to order 12 more that year.[^cite_note-24]
