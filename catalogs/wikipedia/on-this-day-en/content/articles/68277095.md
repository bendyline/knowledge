**Jacques Tilly** (born 27 June 1963) is a German sculptor and illustrator, known for his politically satirical sculptures that adorn [floats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Float_\(parade\)) in protests and parades,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-4] and is considered the premier carnival float designer in Germany.[^cite_note-\:3-5]

## Early life

Jacques Tilly was born in 1963 in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf), and attended the Comenius-Gymnasium high school.[^cite_note-6] He studied graphic design at the [University of Duisburg-Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Duisburg-Essen), graduating in 1994.[^cite_note-\:5-7]

Tilly is an atheist[^cite_note-8] and a humanist, and is an advisory member of the [Giordano Bruno Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giordano_Bruno_Foundation).[^cite_note-\:5-7]

## Artistic career



Jacques Tilly's sculpture of the [Three wise monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_wise_monkeys) one year after the [2021 flood in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_European_floods#Germany) in the [Ahr Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahr_Valley).



A Tilly sculpture of Vladimir Putin, commemorating the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). The inscription reads: *"Choke on it!!!"*.

Tilly is known for his [papier-mâché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papier-m%C3%A2ch%C3%A9) sculptures that are featured on parade floats,[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-9] most notably those in [Rosenmontag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenmontag), during [Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_in_Germany,_Switzerland_and_Austria) in Düsseldorf,[^cite_note-\:4-10][^cite_note-\:2-11] for which he has made sculptures since 1983.[^cite_note-\:5-7] The sculptures often have overt political messaging to them, and frequently include [caricatured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caricature) depictions of politicians, world leaders, and religious figures, especially those on the [far-right](221220.md).[^cite_note-\:3-5][^cite_note-\:2-11][^cite_note-\:1-12] These themes gained increased attention during the late 2010s, with some of Tilly's sculptures featuring U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md), U.K. Prime Minister [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May), Polish Deputy Prime Minister [Jarosław Kaczyński](637440.md), and German Chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel).[^cite_note-\:4-10][^cite_note-\:2-11][^cite_note-\:1-12] Other topics of note include [Brexit](41688778.md) and the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-13] In addition to German parades and festivals, the sculptures have also appeared at protests and political events abroad, including one in front of the House of Parliament in London.[^cite_note-14]

Tilly is also an illustrator, and has designed official [postcards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcard), calendars, and portraits for several German cities.[^cite_note-\:3-5][^cite_note-15]

In 2025 Tilly was sued by Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), whom he had portrayed in various works. According to the [Basmanny District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basmanny_District) court website, the artist is accused of "Defamation of the Russian army".[^cite_note-16] Tilly was found guilty in March 2026 by a Moscow court and sentenced [in absentia](582826.md) to 8 years and 6 month in prison, a Euro 20,000 fine and a 4 year prohibition of activity.[^cite_note-17]
