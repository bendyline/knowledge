Presidential elections were held in [Djibouti](17207794.md) on 10 April 2026.[^cite_note-1] Incumbent fifth term president [Ismaïl Omar Guelleh](392341.md) was re-elected to a sixth term, having been president since 1999.[^cite_note-PreliminaryResults1-2] Originally ineligible for a sixth term due to an age limit of 75, a constitutional amendment was passed in 2025 that allowed him to run again.[^cite_note-3] Most of the opposition parties either boycotted the election or decided to support Guelleh.[^cite_note-BlocNational-4][^cite_note-Hared-5]

## Background

[Ismaïl Omar Guelleh](392341.md), [president of Djibouti](384276.md) since 1999, was re-elected five times, most recently [in April 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Djiboutian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-6] While scrapping term limits in the [2010 constitutional reform](51114502.md), Guelleh also imposed an age limit of 75, meaning he was originally term-limited in 2026. Despite this, he had expressed interest in running again.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Alexis Mohamed, a senior advisor to Guelleh, resigned on 22 September 2025, citing [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding) and [nepotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepotism).[^cite_note-Mohamed1-9] Mohamed criticised any amendment that would change term limits, stating that he could not endorse any constitutional change that would allow Guelleh to run again.[^cite_note-Mohamed2-10]

On 26 October 2025, all 65 members of the [National Assembly](2584439.md) unanimously passed a bill lifting age limits for the presidency, enabling Guelleh to run for a sixth term.[^cite_note-11] National Assembly president [Dileita Mohamed Dileita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dileita_Mohamed_Dileita) defended the amendment, stating that the change was essential to maintaining stability.[^cite_note-TermLimitLifted-12] The amendment, which was later ratified by the parliament on 2 November 2025, was criticised by the president of the Djiboutian League for Human Rights (LDDH), Omar Ali Ewado, stating that the change prepared Djibouti for a presidency for life under Guelleh.[^cite_note-Ewado-13] On 8 November 2025, Guelleh officially announced that he would be a candidate.[^cite_note-14]

Similar to the 2021 election, major opposition coalitions such as the Bloc for National Salvation (BSN) — a coalition created in May 2024 bringing together the [Republican Alliance for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Alliance_for_Democracy) (ARD) and the [Movement for Democratic Renewal and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Democratic_Renewal_and_Development) (MRDD) — did not field a candidate against Guelleh.[^cite_note-BlocNational-4] The [Union for Democracy and Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_Union_for_Democracy_and_Justice), led by Ilaya Ismail Guedi Hared, also chose to support Guelleh while passing the constitutional amendment that lifted the age limit.[^cite_note-Hared-5]

## Electoral system

The president is elected through a [two-round system](25932.md) for a five year term. A candidate who gets the majority of votes in the first round is duly elected; if this is not achieved, a second round is organised between the two candidates who received the most votes in the first. Whoever receives the most votes in the second round is then elected.[^cite_note-Constitution1-15]

To be eligible for the presidency, a candidate must be exclusively of Djiboutian nationality, currently have [civil and political rights](37071.md), be at least forty years of age and have resided continuously in the country for five years — except in the case of a mission carried out on behalf of the State or an international organization — at the time of submission of the candidacy.[^cite_note-Constitution2-16] Each candidate is also required to pay a financial deposit of five million [Djiboutian francs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_franc) to the fund of the National Paymaster.[^cite_note-CandidatePay-17]

## Candidates

On 1 March 2026, the Ministry of the Interior announced that the filing of candidacies would be officially opened on 2 March, giving the chance for any potential candidate to file by 11 March.[^cite_note-OpenCandidacies-18] On 9 March, incumbent President Ismail Omar Guelleh submitted his candidacy for a sixth term as the candidate of the [Union for the Presidential Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Presidential_Majority) (UMP) coalition, which includes the presidential party, the [People's Rally for Progress](622709.md) (RPR).[^cite_note-GuellehCandidate-19] On 10 March, the candidate for [Unified Democratic Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Democratic_Centre?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CDU), [Mohamed Farah Samatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Farah_Samatar?action=edit\&redlink=1), also submitted his candidacy.[^cite_note-SamatarCandidate-20] Samatar, a former member of the RPP, was seen as a largely unknown candidate.[^cite_note-SamatarFrance24-21] Former presidential advisor Alexis Mohamed stated that he was unable to pursue his candidacy because he had no security guarantees if he decided to return to Djibouti from abroad.[^cite_note-AlJazeeraElection-22] The Constitutional Council confirmed the two candidates on 12 March.[^cite_note-ConstitutionalCouncil-23]

With the Ministry of the Interior ratifying the two candidacies on 18 March,[^cite_note-InteriorRatify-24] the only two candidates on the ballot were Guelleh and Mohamed Farah Samatar of the CDU.[^cite_note-25]

## Campaign

After the campaign period officially began on 27 March 2026,[^cite_note-Africa24-26] CDU candidate Mohamed Farah Samatar launched his campaign at [Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadjoura) at a rally, presenting an alternative vision for the country under the slogan "Another Djibouti is possible".[^cite_note-AlJazeeraElection-22] After stressing the need for political renewal and citizen involvement, Samatar held a second rally in [Obock](203894.md).[^cite_note-GuellehvsSamatar1-27] Samatar further campaigned on cracking down on corruption, cutting wasteful government expenditures, and boosting social spending.[^cite_note-GuellehvsSamatar2-28]

Incumbent president [Ismail Omar Guelleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Omar_Guelleh) also began campaigning on 27 March at an initial rally in [Dikhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikhil), followed by [Ali Sabieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Sabieh) on 28 March.[^cite_note-GuellehvsSamatar1-27] After one day of rest, Guelleh resumed his national tour at [Boul'aos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boul'aos), followed by a set of three rallies at [Arta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arta,_Djibouti), Obock, and Tadjoura, before closing at the [El Hadj Hassan Gouled Aptidon Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hadj_Hassan_Gouled_Aptidon_Stadium).[^cite_note-GuellehvsSamatar3-29] The campaign for Guelleh reportedly peaked on 6 April, with a pro-Guelleh rally in [Balbala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balbala).[^cite_note-GuellehvsSamatar4-30] At the closing rally at Gouled Stadium on 9 April, Guelleh outlined his key priorities including accelerating economic growth, expanding job opportunities, improving public services and infrastructure, while also maintaining national stability.[^cite_note-GuellehvsSamatar5-31]

### Conduct

Figures from the Ministry of the Interior revealed that 256,467 people, including 162,833 in [Djibouti City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_City), were eligible to vote across 712 polling stations nationwide.[^cite_note-Africa24-26][^cite_note-France24Registered-32] On 7 April, the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Djibouti\)) announced that 67 international observers would be deployed during the election from the [African Union](10634933.md), the [Intergovernmental Authority on Development](1921406.md), the [Arab League](52625.md) and the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md).[^cite_note-Observers-33] Voting took place from 06\:00 am to 06\:00 pm [EAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa_Time) (UTC+03\:00), with some polling stations opening late and morning turnout being low.[^cite_note-ElectionDayDjibouti1-34] Some polling stations in the capital Djibouti closed around 7\:00 pm EAT (1600 [GMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMT)), an hour after the official end of voting.[^cite_note-ElectionDayDjibouti2-35] Voter numbers picked up in the late afternoon at polling stations in the capital, but no queues formed; [AFP](471981.md) observers reported that turnout at visited polling stations ranged between 36 to 58 percent, but it exceeded 90 percent at one where soldiers were voting.[^cite_note-ElectionDayDjibouti2-35]

## Results

When 6% of all votes were counted,[^cite_note-PartialResults1-36] an [AFP](471981.md) tally of early partial results covering 38 polling stations in [Djibouti City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_City) indicated that incumbent president [Ismaïl Omar Guelleh](392341.md) was leading with 96.47% of the vote, while CDU candidate Mohamed Farah Samatar followed second with 3.52% of the vote.[^cite_note-PartialResults2-37]

Later preliminary figures published by the Ministry of the Interior revealed that Guelleh won re-election to a sixth term in office, winning 97.81% of the vote, while Samatar took 2.19% of the vote.[^cite_note-PreliminaryResults1-2] Turnout in the election was reportedly 80.33%.[^cite_note-PreliminaryResults2-38] The results were confirmed by the Constitutional Council on 21 April, which showed that Guelleh was reelected with 97.01% of the vote, followed by Samatar with 2.99%.[^cite_note-39]

## Reactions

During the release of early results, President Guelleh declared himself re-elected in a social media post ahead of the official results.[^cite_note-PresidentReaction-40] Election officials noted that the vote was peaceful, while supporters of Guelleh celebrated and offered congratulations.[^cite_note-APDjibouti-41] [IGAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGAD) executive secretary [Workneh Gebeyehu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workneh_Gebeyehu) reiterated IGAD's continued commitment to working with [Djibouti](17207794.md) in advancing regional cooperation, peace, stability, and shared prosperity.[^cite_note-Gebeyehu-42] [African Union](10634933.md) commission chairperson [Mahamoud Ali Youssouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamoud_Ali_Youssouf) also congratulated President Guelleh, giving his best wishes for a successful term in office.[^cite_note-Youssouf-43]
