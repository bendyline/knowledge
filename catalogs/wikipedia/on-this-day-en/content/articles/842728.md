**Angeles** (locally [\[ˈaŋhɛlɛs\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog)), officially the **City of Angeles** ([Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language): *Ciudad ning Angeles*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lungsod ng Angeles*), is a [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Legal_classification) in the [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) region of the [Philippines](23440.md). It has a population of 483,452 people.[^cite_note-5]

Angeles is the largest city of [Pampanga](255602.md). While politically independent from the province, it is usually grouped by the [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority) therewith for statistical purposes.

Angeles City is the urban core of [Metro Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Clark),[^cite_note-PDP_2015-6] an urban area in Pampanga. This area, also known as Metro Angeles, is considered the industrial and residential heartland of Central Luzon.[^cite_note-LSDF_2015-7] Metro Clark-Angeles is also identified as a regional center by the national government.[^cite_note-PDP_2015-6]

### Spanish period



Pantaleon de Miranda House along Santo Rosario St.

In 1796, the *[gobernadorcillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobernadorcillo)* or town head of [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga), *[Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_\(honorific\))* Ángel Pantaleón de Miranda, and his wife, *[Doña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_\(honorific\))* Rosalía de Jesús, along with some followers, staked out a new settlement, which they named *Culiát* because of the abundance of vines (*[Gnetum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnetum) indicum*)[^cite_note-8] of that name in the area. The new settlers cleared the woodland and cultivated the area for rice and sugar farming. Don Ángel built his first house with light materials at the northwest corner of the intersection of Sapang Balen and the road going towards the town of [Porac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porac). It was later donated to the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines) and became a cemetery called "Campo Santong Matua" (today the site of Nepomuceno Coliseum).[^cite_note-9]

On May 12, 1812, the new settlers tried to make Culiat a self-governing town but the friars resisted the move, led by Fray José Pometa. Ten years later, on February 11, 1822, Don Ángel filed a petition for the township of Culiat to secede from San Fernando, but it was denied. This was followed by another petition within the same year, jointly signed by Don Ángel, his son-in-law, Mariano Henson, and the latter's father, Severino Henson. He donated 35 hectares (86 acres) for the construction of the first Catholic church, a convent and a primary school while Doña Agustina Henson de Nepomuceno, the niece of who would become the first *_gobernadorcillo_* of Angeles in 1830, Don Ciriaco de Miranda, gave land for the new public market. Don Ángel paid the complete amount required by law just for the secession of Culiat from San Fernando. There were only 160 taxpayers then but the law required that it should have at least 500 taxpayers.[^cite_note-10]

Located some 10 miles (16 km) north of Pampanga's capital, Culiat became a barrio of San Fernando for 33 years and on December 8, 1829, became a separate municipality. The newly autonomous town was renamed "*El Pueblo de los Ángeles*" in honor of its patron saints, the Holy Angels, and the name of its founder, Don Ángel, coinciding with the rise of new *[barrios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay)* such as Santo Cristo (as the *[población](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poblacion)* or town proper), Cutcut, Pampang, and Pulong Anunas. The progressive barrios developed some new industries such as a sugar mill and a wine distillery. The transition of Angeles from a jungle clearing to a barrio, to a town and finally to a city took 168 years and in all that time, it survived locusts' infestations, wars, epidemics, volcanic eruptions and typhoons to become one of the fast rising towns in the country. When it received its first official municipal charter, the town contained some 661 people, 151 houses and an area of 38.65 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-TouristCenter-11][^cite_note-Andro-12]

On May 7, 1899, General [Emilio Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Aguinaldo) transferred the seat of the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic) to Angeles. It then became the site of celebrations for the first anniversary of Philippine independence, which was proclaimed a year earlier in [Kawit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawit), [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite). Events included a parade, led by the youngest ever Filipino generals, [Gregorio del Pilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorio_del_Pilar) and [Manuel Tinio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Tinio), with General Aguinaldo viewing the proceedings from the Pamintuan Residence, which was the Presidential Palace from May to July 1899 (and later was the [Central Bank of the Philippines](1276593.md) office in Central Luzon, before its ownership passed to the [National Historical Commission of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Commission_of_the_Philippines)). Aguinaldo's sojourn was short, however, for in July of this same year he transferred his government to the province of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) following Angeles' occupation by the American forces.[^cite_note-13]

### American period

On August 10, 1899, U.S. forces began the attack on Angeles confident in capturing it in a few days. However, the Filipino Army defending the town refused to give in so easily and fiercely fought back and for three months, they battled the Americans in and around the town. It was only after the battle on November 5, 1899, that the town finally fell into American hands. The Battle of Angeles was considered to be the longest in the history of the [Filipino-American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino-American_War) in Pampanga. This led to the establishment of an American camp in Barrio Talimundoc (in what is now Lourdes Sur), located next to the railroad station, in order to establish control over the central plains of Luzon. In January 1900, General [Frederick D. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_D._Grant) organized the first U.S. Civil Government in Angeles by appointing an *alcalde* or municipal mayor, beginning American rule over Angeles.[^cite_note-14]

In 1902, the [United States Army](32087.md) studied relocating their post from Barrio Talimundoc to a fertile plain in Barrio [Sapang Bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapang_Bato), which supposedly had better grass for their horses. A year after that, U.S. President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) signed an executive order on September 1, establishing 7,700 acres (31 km<sup>2</sup>) of land in Sapang Bato as [Fort Stotsenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Stotsenburg) (which later would expand to 156,204 acres (632.14 km<sup>2</sup>) in 1908 to become [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base)). It was centered on what would in later years become Clark Air Base's parade ground.[^cite_note-15]

The Americans quickly commandeered Holy Rosary Parish Church and converted it into an army hospital, with the choir loft served as a dental clinic. The *[convento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy_house)*, which now houses Holy Family Academy, was the barracks for medical officers and enlisted men. The [sacristy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacristy) was the only portion where Angeleños could hear [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)). When the Americans finally vacated the church in 1904 and relocated to Fort Stotsenburg, [parish priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_priest) Rev. Vicente Lapus listed a total of [US\$](18717338.md)638 for portions of the church destroyed, looted church items and treasures, and arrears on rentals.

### World War II

Hours after the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), Japan attacked the Philippines, targeting the American military presence, as well as the Philippine Army, and taking over the civilian government. During the Japanese occupation in the country, 57,000 Filipino and American [prisoners of war](25008.md) passed the town of Angeles. They were forced to join the [Bataan Death March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan_Death_March), going to [Camp O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_O'Donnell) in [Capas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capas), Tarlac. Angeleños showed their sympathy by handing them food, milk, boiled eggs, rice cakes, cigarettes, and water. Angeleños followed them up to the train station in Dau railway station in [Mabalacat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabalacat) to give moral and spiritual support, and even helped the escapees.

War historians considered the bombing of Fort Stotsenburg on December 8, 1941, at 12\:30 p.m. as one of the most destructive air raids in [World War II](32927.md), because almost all the American war planes were wrecked on the ground. In thirty minutes, the air might of the United States in the Far East was completely destroyed.

On the early morning of New Year's Day 1942, the first Japanese troops entered Angeles; they would occupy it until January 1945. During the Japanese invasion, another type of local government was set up on January 22, 1942. During the Japanese occupation, Clark Air Base then became a major center for staging Japanese air operations. Japanese aircraft flying out of Clark participated in the [Battle of Leyte Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte_Gulf), considered to be the largest naval battle of the Second World War and possibly the largest naval battle in history.[^cite_note-Morison1956-16][^cite_note-Woodward1947-17]

Clark Air Base was recaptured by the Americans in January 1945, after three months of fierce fighting in the Philippines. After three years of atrocities committed by Japanese forces, the town and the rest of the Philippines were finally liberated by the combined United States and Philippine Commonwealth troops in 1945. The building of the general headquarters of the [Philippine Commonwealth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth_Army) and [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) was situated in Angeles from January 1945 to June 1946, during and after World War II.

### Philippine independence

#### Cityhood

Main article: [Cities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines)

After World War II, the Philippines gained independence from the United States on July 4, 1946, but then would be tied to a neo-colonial relationship. The "Treaty of General Relations" signed on independence day itself signified the Americans' withdrawal and surrender of possession, control and sovereignty over the Philippines, except the use of their bases. It was followed by the Philippine-American Military Bases Agreement on March 14, 1947, allowing the U.S. to maintain territorial integrity and sovereignty over Clark Air Base and [Subic Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic_Bay) for the next 44 years. Clark occupied 63,103 hectares and served as the tactical operational U.S. air force installation in the entire Southeast Asian region that had the capacity to accommodate the U.S. military transport planes, which served the entire Western Pacific.

Through the years, although Fort Stotsenburg continued to expand to become what would eventually be known as Clark Air Base, Angeles, despite its proximity to the American camp, did not progress fast and remained fairly small until the end of World War II. It was finally inaugurated on January 1, 1964, as a chartered city under Republic Act No. 3700 and then proceeded to enter a period of tremendous growth that resulted in its present position as the "Premier City in Central Luzon". It was through Mayor Rafael del Rosario's brainchild that Angeles became a city. He gained the distinction of being the last municipal mayor of Angeles. He was assisted in the preparation of the City Chapter by Attorney Enrique Tayag, a prominent resident of the town. Congresswoman [Juanita Nepomuceno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juanita_Nepomuceno) of the first district of Pampanga sponsored the bill in Congress, which was approved by then President [Diosdado Macapagal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Macapagal), the ninth Philippine president and a native of the province of Pampanga.[^cite_note-ClarktonHotel-18]

#### Mount Pinatubo eruption and Angeles today



Collapsed hangars at the [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) after the 1991 eruption of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo)

On June 15, 1991, Angeles was affected by [the cataclysmic eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Pinatubo) of nearby [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo), with up to 60,000 people being evacuated from the city. It was the second-largest volcanic eruption of the twentieth century and, by far, the largest eruption to affect a densely populated area. The province of [Pampanga](255602.md), Clark specifically, was badly hit and the agricultural lands, as well as other businesses, were covered by tons of [lahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar).[^cite_note-19] There were no casualties reported inside Clark two days from the initial eruption because the 18,000 personnel and their families were transported to [Guam](11974.md) and the [Subic Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic_Naval_Base) in [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales).

The eruption of Mount Pinatubo forced the leadership of the U.S. to prematurely abandon its military installation at Clark Air Base. This was in addition to a vote by the Philippine Senate in 1991 to no longer extend the [Laurel–Langley Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel%E2%80%93Langley_Agreement), which allowed the presence of U.S. military forces on Philippine territory, thus ending the long chapter of Filipino-American relations in the history of Angeles. The U.S. military never returned to Clark, turning over the damaged base to the Philippine government on November 26, 1991.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-GlobalSecurity-21][^cite_note-Coconuter-22]



[MacArthur Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Highway) at Angeles. The neighboring city [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga) is southbound of the highway.

In 1993, cleanup and removal of volcanic ash deposits began. The former base re-emerged as [Clark Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Special_Economic_Zone) (CSEZ) approved by then President [Fidel V. Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_V._Ramos) on April 3 of the same year. The airfield infrastructure was improved and destined to be the premiere airport in the country in the next five years and one of the most modern in Asia.[^cite_note-SunStarMillionth-23] The creation of CSEZ has helped to offset the loss of income and jobs previously generated by the presence of the U.S. base. Today, Angeles and Clark together form the hub for business, industry, aviation and tourism, as well as the entertainment and gaming center of [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon).[^cite_note-TravelPH-24]

Among the draws for tourists is the local dish [sisig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisig) which according to the Center for Kapampangan Studies, originated in this Angeles and has been on the menu since the 1730s. Pampanga is well known as the culinary center of the Philippines.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-ManilaTimesDonita-27]

In 2018, Angeles applied to be a UNESCO Creative City, while it also applied *sisig* into the UNESCO List of Intangible Cultural Heritage. The applications are currently being processed by UNESCO.[^cite_note-28]

A recent racial study showed that Angeles City, Pampanga; alongside Manila; and [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo), Zambales; was the locations of several concentrations of 250,000 [Amerasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerasians) (Mixed American-Asians) who were born as a result of the American colonization of the Philippines and the presence of US bases in the country.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Geography

It is bordered by [Mabalacat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabalacat) to the north, [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico,_Pampanga) to the east, [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga) to the southeast, [Bacolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolor) to the south, and [Porac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porac) to the southwest and west. Though the city administers itself autonomously from [Pampanga](255602.md), it is the province's commercial and financial hub.

Angeles is served by [Clark International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport) in [Clark Freeport Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_Zone).[^cite_note-31]

Angeles is 83 kilometres (52 mi) from Manila and 17 kilometres (11 mi) from the provincial capital, [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga).

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system, Angeles has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) that borders on a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) Aw/Am). Angeles experiences two distinct seasons: a dry season from November through April, with a wet season from May through October. From 1953 to 1991, the mean daily low was 22.6 °C and the mean daily high was 31.3 °C, with June being warmest and January and February being the coolest. The average annual rainfall is 2026.8 mm. Typhoons tend to approach from the east during the summer and fall. Many damaging storms struck the city, including [Typhoon Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Irma_\(1974\)) on November 28, 1974 (generally considered to be the strongest one); [Typhoon Rita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Typhoon_Rita_\(Kading\)) on October 27, 1978; [Typhoon Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Irma_\(1981\)) (the name was reused) on November 24, 1981; [Typhoon Ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ruby_\(1988\)) on October 25, 1988; and [Typhoon Yunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Yunya_\(Diding\)) on June 15, 1991, which coincided with the Mount Pinatubo blast. In July 1972, Central Luzon experienced a month of nearly continuous rain, resulting in ±2,440 millimetres (96 in) falling on the plain around Angeles.

#### Anunas



Korea Town of Angeles

Anunas is the [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) that houses the city's [Koreatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreatown), a chain of Korean establishments along the [Fil-Am Friendship Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_American_Friendship_Highway). Anunas is also identified as one of the growth centers of the city, focusing on light industries such as woodcarving and rattan craft.[^cite_note-34]

#### Balibago and Malabañas



Malabañas skyline

[Balibago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balibago) is the main entertainment district of Angeles. It contains [Casino Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Amusement_and_Gaming_Corporation) Angeles and the famous [Fields Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Avenue,_The_Philippines) tourist belt. The city's biggest mall, [SM City Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_City_Clark), is also situated in Barangay Malabañas.

#### Pampang and San Nicolas

These two barangays form the main public market district of the city. The Pampang Wet Market, San Nicolas Market, Friday Flea Market (locally referred to as Apu), Jumbo Jenra Angeles, Puregold Angeles, and the Angeles Slaughterhouse are found here. The Pampang Wet Market is the largest and most frequented wet market in the province of Pampanga. It also attracts people from nearby towns. Ospital Ning Angeles (ONA), City College of Angeles, Angeles City National High School are located in Pampang.

#### Pulung Maragul



Marquee Mall



Marquee Residences in Pulung Maragul (under construction)

Pulung Maragul is the [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) that houses the city's government complex, which includes the Angeles City Hall, the Angeles City Hall of Justice, and other government buildings. It is also the location of the Angeles Exit of the [North Luzon Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Luzon_Expressway) and [Marquee Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquee_Mall), Ayala's first mall in Central Luzon. Marquee Place and Marquee Residences later rose in Pulung Maragul as well, next to the mall.

#### Santo Rosario

Santo Rosario is the [poblacion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poblacion). It is home to most of Angeles' heritage and historical structures such as the [Holy Rosary Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Rosary_Parish,_Angeles) Church, Pamintuan Mansion which is privately owned by Maverick Pamintuan, Bale Herencia, and Museo ning Angeles (former City Hall building). [Holy Angel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Angel_University), Central Luzon's largest university in terms of population,[^cite_note-35] is also located here. Plans of declaring the [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) or parts of it a heritage zone are ongoing.[^cite_note-36]

#### Sapangbato

Sapangbato is the largest [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) in Angeles in terms of territory, with a total land area of 104,694 sq. meters and a population of 11,262. Located northwest of Angeles near [Clark Freeport Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_Zone), it is identified as the barangay in Angeles with the highest elevation of 750 feet above sea level. It is home to [Fort Stotsenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Stotsenburg), also known as the Parade Grounds of Clark. [apl.de.ap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apl.de.ap), member of the hip hop group [the Black Eyed Peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Eyed_Peas), hails from Sapangbato. The famous Puning Hot Springs of Barangay Inararo in [Porac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porac) are accessed through Sitio Target in Sapangbato.

### Religion

The majority of the population of Angeles is Catholic. At least two major festivals associated with the Catholic faith are held in October in the city. Commemorating the victory of the Spanish fleet over the Dutch Navy in 1646, the La Naval Fiesta is celebrated in honor of the [Our Lady of La Naval de Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_La_Naval_de_Manila) with adherents believing that her intercession was instrumental to the Spaniards' naval victory.[^cite_note-44] The Apu Fiesta involves devotees from all over Pampanga making a visit to the Apu shrine to venerate the image of Jesus Christ lying in the [sepulchre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepulchre) which is believed to be miraculous by believers.[^cite_note-45]

### Expatriate and immigrant community

Owing to the presence of the nearby U.S. Clark Air Force Base and consequent Freeport Zone,[^cite_note-46] many Americans chose to permanently settle in the area, particularly in the Balibago district, and thus Angeles became home to a large colony of expatriates. During the American colonial period (1898–1946), more than 800,000 Americans were born in the Philippines, and a large concentration of [Filipino mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_mestizo) or [Filipinos with American ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_of_American_descent) were located in this city.[^cite_note-TimePFCA-47][^cite_note-ACP-48][^cite_note-ExpPhi-49]

[SM City Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_City_Clark), the second [SM Supermall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_Supermalls) in [Pampanga](255602.md)

Being home of the former [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) (once the largest United States military facility outside the continental United States), it was significantly affected by the fallout from the eruption of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo) in 1991. The economy of Angeles was heavily dependent on the American base at that time.[^cite_note-TouristCenter-11]

In 1993, a full cleanup and removal of volcanic ash deposits began and the former U.S. base was transformed into the [Clark Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Special_Economic_Zone) (CSEZ).[^cite_note-SunStarMillionth-23] The creation of CSEZ has helped to offset the loss of income and jobs previously generated by the presence of the U.S. base in the city. Today, Angeles and Clark form the hub for business, industry, aviation, and tourism in the Philippines as well as a leisure, fitness, entertainment and gaming center of [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon).[^cite_note-59]

Angeles is home to an emerging technology industry. Its economy is based also on tourism and gambling. [Fields Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Avenue,_The_Philippines) forms the hub of the night life industry focused in Angeles.[^cite_note-60] With close proximity to an international airport in Clark Freeport, Angeles is visited by foreigners all year round.[^cite_note-61]



Al-fresco restaurants at the backside of [Marquee Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquee_Mall)

In the 2000s, the local government of Angeles rebranded the Fields Avenue tourist belt as a high-end destination with fine restaurants and luxury hotels and casinos.[^cite_note-CDC-62][^cite_note-RepackagingClark-63] The finishing of roads, such as the [Subic–Clark–Tarlac Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic%E2%80%93Clark%E2%80%93Tarlac_Expressway), has improved trade and transport.[^cite_note-TravelPH-24][^cite_note-AsiaTravel-64][^cite_note-GlobalPinoy-65] The project connects the industrial, transport and business hubs of Pampanga, Zambales, Bataan and Tarlac. The project is crucial to bolstering growth in Central Luzon.[^cite_note-SunStarPush-66][^cite_note-GovPhExpressway-67]

The city has [cottage industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_industry) producing [rattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattan) furniture, [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut), and [charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcoal) [briquettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briquette). It also has many thriving export businesses in handicrafts, metal crafts, toys, houseware and garments.[^cite_note-Wow-68] Apart from the Clark Freeport Zone, industrial areas include the Angeles Livelihood Village and the Angeles City Industrial Estate.[^cite_note-Positive-69]

Call centers present are e-Telecare,[^cite_note-70] CyberCity, Sutherland and IRMC. Other American IT industries are major employers as well.[^cite_note-ASM-71] The establishment of a number of shopping malls also fueled the city's economy, including [SM City Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_City_Clark), [Robinsons Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinsons_Malls), Jenra Grand Mall, Nepo Mall, Saver's Mall and the [Marquee Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquee_Mall), next to City Hall.[^cite_note-MTcomplex-72][^cite_note-InqHaven-73]

Angeles City houses numerous restaurants that are usually located near the malls and mostly in Nepo Quad which was newly renovated to cater the heightened needs of the population.

## Culture



Preparing for the Octoberfest 2009 along McArthur Highway in Balibago district

The city hosts a street party called the Tigtigan Terakan keng Dalan (lit. 'Music and Dancing on the Streets') every October which features musical performances from both amateur and better-known [OPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_Pilipino_Music) bands.[^cite_note-74] Through *Presidential Proclamation 696*, October 26, 2024 was declared a [special non-working day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Philippines) in celebration of its Tigtigan Terakan Keng Dalan Festival.[^cite_note-75]

The Sisig Festival, locally known as the Sadsaran Qng Angeles, festivities dedicated to the Kapampangan dish *[sisig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisig)*, used to be held every December.[^cite_note-76] It was halted in 2008 following the murder of [Lucia Cunanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucia_Cunanan), who was known for promoting the dish. The festival was revived as a one-day fiesta in April 2017 in association with the [Department of Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Tourism_\(Philippines\)).

## Tourism



Angeles Heritage District featuring [Museo ning Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_ning_Angeles) and [Santo Rosario Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Rosario_Church_\(Angeles\))



The Salakot Arch

Angeles is promoted as a [gastronomy tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culinary_tourism) and is billed as the "culinary capital" of the Philippines. The city is known as a hub for Kampampangan cuisine as well as for its pork [sisig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisig).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The city also has numerous historically and culturally significant tourist destinations including the Pamintuan Mansion, a heritage house which hosts a history and social studies museum, and the Holy Rosary Church, which is recognized by the [National Museum of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Philippines) as an [Important Cultural Property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Cultural_Property_\(Philippines\)). Angeles is also situated within the perimeter of the [Clark Freeport Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_Zone).[^cite_note-79]

### Sex tourism

One consequence of the historical presence of a US base (until 1991) is the prostitution industry in Angeles. From the early days of [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base), Fields Avenue in Balibago district became an area frequently visited by the U.S. servicemen. It is until today widely known as a center for prostitution and [sex tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_tourism).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] A [BBC](19344654.md) article characterized it as "the centre of the Philippines sex industry" and dubbed it "Sin City".[^cite_note-bbc-84] Elsewhere and in later years, Philippine travel publications have described it as the "Entertainment Capital of Central Luzon", "The Filipino [Las Vegas](47737.md)", and "Entertainment City".[^cite_note-Fontana-85][^cite_note-AllTravel-86]

## Government

### Local government

Main article: [Sangguniang Panglungsod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panglungsod)



Angeles City Hall



Hall of Justice

As a highly urbanized city in the province of Pampanga, government officials at the provincial level are not voted for by the city. The provincial government has no political jurisdiction over the local transactions of the city government. Residents of this city are not allowed to run for positions at the provincial level, except for congressional representation for the 1st District of Pampanga.

The City of Angeles is governed by a city mayor, designated as its local chief executive, and by a city council as its legislative body in accordance with the Local Government Code. The mayor, vice mayor, and the city councilors are elected directly in polls held every three years.

Barangays are also headed by elected officials: [Barangay Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay_Captain), [Barangay Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Barangay), whose members are called [Barangay Councilors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay_Kagawad). The barangays have SK federation which represents the barangay, headed by SK chairperson and whose members are called SK councilors. All officials are also elected every three years.
