**Idaho** ([/ˈaɪdəhoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f7/En-us-Idaho.ogg/En-us-Idaho.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f7/En-us-Idaho.ogg/En-us-Idaho.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [*EYE-də-hoe*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a landlocked [state](18618239.md) in the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) and [Mountain West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_states) subregions of the [Western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States). It borders [Montana](19978.md) and [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) to the east, [Nevada](21216.md) and [Utah](31716.md) to the south, and [Washington](13015878.md) and [Oregon](26811621.md) to the west; the state shares a small portion of the [Canada–United States border](630260.md) to the north with the Canadian province of [British Columbia](3392.md). Idaho's [state capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capital_\(United_States\)) and largest city is [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho). With an area of 83,569 square miles (216,440 km<sup>2</sup>), Idaho is the [14th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) state by land area. The state has a population of approximately two million people; it ranks as the [13th-least populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) and the [seventh-least densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density) of the [50 U.S. states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_US_states).

For thousands of years, and prior to European colonization, Idaho had been inhabited by [natives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States). In the early 19th century, Idaho was considered part of the [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country), an area which was disputed between the U.S. and the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). Idaho officially became [a U.S. territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) with the signing of the [Oregon Treaty of 1846](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty), but a separate [Idaho Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Territory) was not organized until 1863, instead being included for periods in [Oregon Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Territory) and [Washington Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Territory). The state was eventually [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) on July 3, 1890, becoming the [43rd state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union).

Forming part of the Pacific Northwest (and the associated [Cascadia bioregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascadia_\(bioregion\))), Idaho is divided into several distinct geographic and climatic regions. The state's north, the relatively isolated [Idaho Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Panhandle), is closely linked with [Eastern Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington), with which it shares the [Pacific Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone)—the rest of the state uses the [Mountain Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Time_Zone). The state's south includes the [Snake River Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River_Plain) (which has most of the population and agricultural land), and the southeast incorporates part of the [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin). Idaho is quite mountainous and contains several stretches of the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains). The [United States Forest Service](42652.md) holds about 38% of Idaho's land, the highest proportion of any state.[^cite_note-8]

Industries significant for the state economy include manufacturing, agriculture, mining, forestry, science and technology, and tourism. Idaho has been a predominantly Republican state since statehood, with the [Republican Party](32070.md) dominating in both state and national elections; [abortion](765.md) is severely restricted and the state retains the [death penalty](5902.md), including methods like the [firing squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firing_squad). The state contains the [Idaho National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_National_Laboratory). Idaho's agricultural sector supplies many products, but the state is best known for its [potato crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), which comprises around one-third of the nationwide yield. Its [official state nickname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._state_nicknames) is the "Gem State".[^cite_note-9]

## Etymology

In the early 1860s, when the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) was considering organizing a new territory around [Pikes Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikes_Peak) in the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains), the name "Idaho" was popular among the Congressional committee, as they believed it to be derived from a [Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone_language) term meaning "gem of the mountains."[^cite_note-10] Realizing in January 1861 the name was quite possibly a fabrication and not Native American at all,[^cite_note-Idaho_State_Digital_Atlas-11] the U.S. Congress ultimately decided to name the area [Colorado Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Territory) when it was created. But, by the time this decision was made in February 1861, the town of [Idaho Springs, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Springs,_Colorado) had already been named after their early frontrunner for a name, Idaho.

In 1863, the [Territory of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Idaho) was formed, though "Montana" was nearly its name.[^cite_note-Ellis-12]

More than a decade after the name Idaho was first discussed for the eventual Colorado Territory, [George M. Willing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Willing), a politician, claimed that he had been inspired by a little girl named *Ida* and that he coined the name in Washington at the time of the Congressional committee when he had been posing as an elected delegate to Congress.[^cite_note-Ellis-12]

The same year Congress created Colorado Territory, a county called [Idaho County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_County,_Idaho) was created in eastern [Washington Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Territory). The county was named after a [steamship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat) named *Idaho*, which was launched on the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) in 1860. It is unclear whether the steamship was named before or after the legitimacy of the Native American origins of "Idaho" came into question. Regardless, part of Washington Territory, including Idaho County, was used to create [Idaho Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Territory) in 1863.[^cite_note-Idaho_State_Digital_Atlas-11] Idaho Territory would later change its boundaries to the area that became the U.S. state.[^cite_note-13]

## History

Main article: [History of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Idaho)

Humans may have been present in the Idaho area as long as 14,500 years ago. Excavations at [Wilson Butte Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Butte_Cave) near [Twin Falls](110784.md) in 1959 revealed evidence of human activity, including arrowheads, that rank among the oldest dated artifacts in North America. [American Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) peoples predominant in the area included the [Nez Percé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nez_Perce_people) in the north and the Northern and Western [Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone) in the south.[^cite_note-natgeo-14]

A Late [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic) site was identified at Cooper's Ferry in western Idaho near the town of [Cottonwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottonwood,_Idaho) by archaeologists in 2019. Based on evidence found at the site, first people lived in this area 15,300 to 16,600 years ago, predating the [Beringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beringia) land bridge by about a thousand years. The discoverers emphasized that they possess similarities with tools and artifacts discovered in [Japan](15573.md) that date from 16,000 to 13,000 years ago.[^cite_note-natgeo-14][^cite_note-science-15] The discovery also showed that the first people might not have come to [North America](21139.md) by land, as previously theorized. On the contrary, they probably came through the water, using a [Pacific](23070.md) coastal route.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

> The most parsimonious explanation we think is that people came down the Pacific Coast, and as they encountered the mouth of the Columbia River, they essentially found an off-ramp from this coastal migration and also found their first viable interior route to the areas that are south of the ice sheet.[^cite_note-science-15]

An early presence of French-Canadian trappers is visible in names and [toponyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponym): *Nez Percé, Cœur d'Alène, Boisé, Payette*. Some of these names appeared prior to the [Lewis and Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark) and [Astorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fur_Company) expeditions, which included significant numbers of French and [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis) guides recruited for their familiarity with the terrain.[^cite_note-17]

The Lewis and Clark expedition crossed Idaho in 1805 on the way to the Pacific, and in 1806, on the return trip, largely following the [Clearwater River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater_River_\(Idaho\)) in both directions, making it one the last states settled by [European Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans). The first non-indigenous settlement was [Kullyspell House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kullyspell_House), established on the shore of [Lake Pend Oreille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Pend_Oreille) in 1809 by [David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thompson_\(explorer\)) of the [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company) for fur trading.[^cite_note-auto2-18][^cite_note-19] In 1812 [Donald Mackenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Mackenzie_\(explorer\)), working for the [Pacific Fur Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fur_Company) at the time, established a post on the lower Clearwater River near present-day Lewiston. This post, known as "MacKenzie's Post" or "Clearwater", operated until the Pacific Fur Company was bought out by the North West Company in 1813, after which the post was abandoned.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-auto2-18] The first organized non-indigenous communities within the present borders of Idaho were established by [Mormon pioneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_pioneers) in 1860.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The first permanent, substantial [incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation) community was Lewiston, in 1861. Early in its history, Idaho saw a large influx of [Chinese immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Americans), who by 1870 made up about 28.5% of the territory's population.[^cite_note-23]

Idaho, as part of the [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country), was claimed by both the United States and Great Britain until the United States gained undisputed jurisdiction in 1846. From 1843 to 1859, present-day Idaho was under the de facto jurisdiction of the [Provisional Government of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_Oregon). When Oregon became a state in 1859, what is now Idaho was situated in what remained of the original Oregon Territory, designated as the Washington Territory. Between 1849 and the creation of the [Idaho Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Territory) in 1863, parts of present-day Idaho were included in the [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Territory), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Territory), and [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Territory) Territories. The new Idaho territory included present-day Idaho, [Montana](19978.md), and most of [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming).&#x20;

Idaho achieved statehood in 1890, following a difficult start as a territory, including the transfer of the territorial capital from [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho) to [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho),[^cite_note-24] [disenfranchisement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disenfranchisement) of [Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon) [polygamists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygamist) upheld by the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) in 1890,[^cite_note-25] and a federal attempt to split the territory between Washington Territory, which gained statehood in 1889, a year before Idaho, and the state of [Nevada](21216.md) which had been a state since 1864.[^cite_note-26]

Idaho was one of the hardest hit of the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) states during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression).[^cite_note-27] Prices plummeted for Idaho's major crops: in 1932 a bushel of potatoes brought only ten cents compared to 1919 for \$1.51, while Idaho farmers saw their annual income of \$685 in 1929 drop to \$250 by 1932.[^cite_note-28]

Between 1991 and 2002, Idaho expanded its commercial base to include the science and technology sector which accounted for over 25% of its [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) in 2001.[^cite_note-29]

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Idaho enacted statewide crisis standards of care as COVID-19 patients overwhelmed hospitals.[^cite_note-30] The state had one of the lowest vaccination rates in the country as of mid-October 2021.[^cite_note-31]

Idaho shares a border with six U.S. states and one Canadian province. The states of [Washington](13015878.md) and [Oregon](26811621.md) are to the west, [Nevada](21216.md) and [Utah](31716.md) are to the south, and Montana and Wyoming are to the east. Idaho also shares a short border with the Canadian province of [British Columbia](3392.md) to the north.[^cite_note-32]



Shoshone Falls

The landscape is rugged, with some of the largest unspoiled natural areas in the United States. For example, at 2.3 million acres (930,000 ha), the [Frank Church-River of No Return Wilderness Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Church-River_of_No_Return_Wilderness_Area) is the largest contiguous area of protected wilderness in the continental United States. Idaho is a [Rocky Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) state with abundant natural resources and scenic areas. The state has snow-capped mountain ranges, rapids, vast lakes and steep canyons. The waters of the [Snake River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River) run through [Hells Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hells_Canyon), the deepest gorge in the United States. [Shoshone Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone_Falls) falls down cliffs from a height greater than [Niagara Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Falls).[^cite_note-33]

By far, the most important river in Idaho is the Snake River, a major tributary of the Columbia River. The Snake River flows from [Yellowstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone) in northwestern [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) through the [Snake River Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River_Plain) in southern Idaho before turning north, leaving the state at [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho) before joining the Columbia in [Kennewick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennewick). Other major rivers are the [Clark Fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Fork_\(river\))/[Pend Oreille River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pend_Oreille_River), the [Spokane River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_River), and, many major tributaries of the Snake River, including the [Clearwater River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater_River_\(Idaho\)), the [Salmon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon_River_\(Idaho\)), the [Boise River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_River), and the [Payette River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payette_River). The Salmon River empties into the Snake in Hells Canyon and forms the southern boundary of Nez Perce County on its north shore, of which Lewiston is the county seat. The Port of [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho), at the confluence of the [Clearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater_River_\(Idaho\)) and the Snake Rivers is the farthest inland [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) on the West Coast at 465 [river miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_mile) from the Pacific at [Astoria, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Oregon).[^cite_note-34]

The vast majority of Idaho's population lives in the Snake River Plain, a valley running from across the entirety of southern Idaho from east to west. The valley contains the major cities of [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise), [Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian,_Idaho), [Nampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nampa,_Idaho), [Caldwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldwell,_Idaho), [Twin Falls](110784.md), [Idaho Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Falls), and [Pocatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocatello). The plain served as an easy pass through the Rocky Mountains for westward-bound settlers on the [Oregon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Trail), and many settlers chose to settle the area rather than risking the treacherous route through the [Blue Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mountains_\(Pacific_Northwest\)) and the [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range) to the west. The western region of the plain is known as the [Treasure Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Valley), bound between the [Owyhee Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owyhee_Mountains) to the southwest and the [Boise Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_Mountains) to the northeast. The central region of the Snake River Plain is known as the [Magic Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Valley).



Edna Lake, Sawtooth Mountains

Idaho's highest point is [Borah Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borah_Peak), 12,662 ft (3,859 m), in the [Lost River Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_River_Range) north of [Mackay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackay,_Idaho). Idaho's lowest point, 710 ft (216 m), is in [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho), where the [Clearwater River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater_River_\(Idaho\)) joins the [Snake River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River) and continues into Washington. The [Sawtooth Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawtooth_Range_\(Idaho\)) is often considered Idaho's most famous mountain range.[^cite_note-35] Other mountain ranges in Idaho include the [Bitterroot Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitterroot_Range), the [White Cloud Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Cloud_Mountains), the [Lost River Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_River_Range), the [Clearwater Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater_Mountains), and the [Salmon River Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon_River_Mountains).

[Salmon-Challis National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon-Challis_National_Forest) is located in the east central sections of the state, with Salmon National Forest to the north and Challis National Forest to the south. The forest is in an area known as the Idaho Cobalt Belt, which consists of a 34 miles (55 km) long [geological formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_formation) of [sedimentary rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock) that contains some of the largest [cobalt](24580536.md) deposits in the U.S.[^cite_note-36]

Idaho has two [time zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_the_United_States), with the dividing line approximately midway between Canada and [Nevada](21216.md). Southern Idaho, including the [Boise metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_metropolitan_area), [Idaho Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Falls,_Idaho), [Pocatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocatello,_Idaho), and [Twin Falls](110784.md), are in the [Mountain Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Time_Zone). A legislative error ([15 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_15_of_the_United_States_Code) [ch. 6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-6) §264) theoretically placed this region in the [Central Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Time_Zone), but this was corrected with a 2007 amendment.[^cite_note-37] Areas north of the [Salmon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon_River_\(Idaho\)), including [Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene,_Idaho), [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho), [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho), and [Sandpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandpoint,_Idaho), are in the [Pacific Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone), which contains less than a quarter of the state's population and land area.[^cite_note-38]

### Climate



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Idaho, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](42778673.md)

Idaho's [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) varies widely. Although the state's western border is about 330 miles (530 km) from the Pacific Ocean, the maritime influence is still felt in Idaho; especially, in the winter when cloud cover, [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity), and [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) are at their maximum extent. This influence has a moderating effect in the winter where temperatures are not as low as would otherwise be expected for a northern state with predominantly high elevations.[^cite_note-39] In the panhandle, moist air masses from the coast are released as precipitation over the [North Central Rockies forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_Rockies_forests), creating the [North American inland temperate rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_inland_temperate_rainforest).[^cite_note-40] The maritime influence is least prominent in the state's eastern part where the precipitation patterns are often reversed, with wetter summers and drier winters, and seasonal temperature differences are more extreme, showing a more semi-arid [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate).[^cite_note-climate_idaho_wrcc-41]

Idaho can be hot, although extended periods over 98 °F (37 °C) are rare, except for the lowest point in elevation, [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho), which correspondingly sees little snow. Hot summer days are tempered by the low relative humidity and cooler evenings during summer months since, for most of the state, the highest [diurnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day) difference in temperature is often in the summer.[^cite_note-climate_idaho_wrcc-41] Winters can be cold, although extended periods of bitter cold weather below zero are unusual. Idaho's all-time highest temperature of 118 °F (48 °C) was recorded at [Orofino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orofino,_Idaho) on July 28, 1934; the all-time lowest temperature of −60 °F (−51 °C) was recorded at [Island Park Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Park_Dam) on January 18, 1943.

Idaho [population pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_pyramid)

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) determined Idaho's population was 1,900,923 on July 1, 2021, a 21% increase since the [2010 US census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_US_census).[^cite_note-PopEstUS-46]

Idaho had an estimated population of 1,754,208 in 2018, an increase of 37,265 from the prior year and an increase of 186,626 (11.91%) since 2010. This included a natural increase since the last census of 58,884 (111,131 births minus 52,247 deaths) and an increase due to net [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration) of 75,795 people into the state. There are large numbers of Americans of English and German ancestry in Idaho. [Immigration](15051.md) from outside the United States resulted in a net increase of 14,522 people, and migration within the country produced a net increase of 61,273 people.

According to the American Immigration Council, in 2018, the top countries of origin for Idaho's immigrants were Mexico, Canada, the Philippines, China, and Germany.[^cite_note-auto1-47]

Idaho's population increased by 17.3% from 2010 to 2020, the second fastest rate of growth of any state that decade.[^cite_note-48]

Nampa, about 20 miles (30 km) west of downtown Boise, became the state's second-largest city in the late 1990s, passing Pocatello and Idaho Falls. Nampa's population was under 29,000 in 1990 and grew to over 81,000 by 2010. Located between Nampa and Boise, Meridian also experienced high growth, from fewer than 10,000 residents in 1990 to more than 75,000 in 2010, and is now Idaho's third-largest city. Growth of 5% or more over the same period has also been observed in [Caldwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldwell,_Idaho), [Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene,_Idaho), [Post Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Falls,_Idaho), and Twin Falls.[^cite_note-49]

From 1990 to 2010, Idaho's population increased by over 560,000 (55%). The [Boise metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_metropolitan_area) (officially known as the Boise City-Nampa, ID, Metropolitan Statistical Area) is Idaho's largest. Other metropolitan areas, in order of size, are [Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene,_Idaho), [Idaho Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Falls,_Idaho), [Pocatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocatello,_Idaho) and [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho).[^cite_note-50]

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report to Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 1,998 [homeless](19394651.md) people in Idaho.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

The majority of Idaho's population is of European descent. Most of Idaho's white residents trace their ancestry to the United Kingdom, Germany, Ireland, France, Italy, or Poland. There are also small numbers of Native Americans, Asians, and African Americans in the state.[^cite_note-62]

In 2018, the top countries of origin for Idaho's immigrants were [Mexico](3966054.md), [Canada](5042916.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), [China](5405.md), and [Germany](11867.md).[^cite_note-auto1-47]

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Idaho) and [Idaho locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_locations_by_per_capita_income)

In 2025, the [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) (state [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP)) for Idaho was \$135.5 billion and the state's per capita personal income was \$64,846.[^cite_note-84] Idaho had the 39th highest [GDP per capita in the United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_GDP) in 2025.[^cite_note-85]

In 2025, [small businesses](276495.md) made up 99.2% and employed 56.0% of the state's work force.[^cite_note-86] As of May 2025, the state's unemployment rate was 3.6%.[^cite_note-87] Total employment in Idaho in 2024 was 965,824.

Idaho is the top potato producing state in the United States and almost one-third of the nation's potatoes are grown in the [Snake River Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River_Plain), a belt of low-lying land that extends across southern Idaho.[^cite_note-88]

Important industries in Idaho are food processing, lumber and wood products, machinery, chemical products, paper products, electronics manufacturing, silver and other mining, and tourism. The world's largest factory for barrel cheese, the raw product for [processed cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processed_cheese), is in [Gooding, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gooding,_Idaho). It has a capacity of 120,000 metric tons per year of barrel cheese and belongs to the [Glanbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glanbia) group.[^cite_note-89]

As Idaho neared statehood, mining and other extractive industries played a significant role in its economy. Although the state's reliance on mining has diminished over time, Idaho remains renowned as "The Gem State" due to its production of seventy-two varieties of precious and semi-precious stones. Idaho is a leading national producer of potatoes, trout, Austrian winter peas, and lentils. The state's primary industries include manufacturing, agriculture, food processing, timber, and mining. Tourism is another way that Idaho capitalizes on its natural resources. The same tracts of wilderness that attracted [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway) to the region in the early 1960s, continue to attract outdoor enthusiasts with camping, hunting, fishing, as well as whitewater kayaking and rafting, and skiing.[^cite_note-90]

Idaho introduced a [state gambling lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Lottery) in 1989, which has contributed over \$1.2 billion to [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district), [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), and state owned buildings as of July, 2025.[^cite_note-91]

### Taxation

Tax is collected by the [Idaho State Tax Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_State_Tax_Commission).[^cite_note-92]

The state personal [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) is a flat 5.8%. Idahoans may apply for state [tax credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_credit) for taxes paid to other states, as well as for donations to Idaho state educational entities and some nonprofit youth and rehabilitation facilities.[^cite_note-93]

The state [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) is 6% with a very limited, selective local option up to 6.5%. Sales tax applies to the sale, rental or lease of tangible personal property and some services. Food is taxed, but [prescription drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescription_drug) are not. Hotel, [motel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motel), and [campground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campground) accommodations are taxed at a higher rate (7% to 11%). Some jurisdictions impose local option sales tax.[^cite_note-94]

The sales tax was introduced at 3% in 1965, easily approved by voters,[^cite_note-csrv-95] where it remained at 3% until 1983.[^cite_note-stxhis-96]

## Energy

See also: [List of power stations in Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Idaho)

Idaho has a regulated electricity market, with the [Idaho Public Utilities Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Public_Utilities_Commission) regulating the three major utilities of [Avista Utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avista), [Idaho Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Power), and [Rocky Mountain Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PacifiCorp).[^cite_note-97]

Idaho consumes almost four times more energy than it produces. In 2022, renewable energy sources accounted for 75% of the total electricity generated in the state, the fourth-highest share of renewable electricity for any state.[^cite_note-98] In 2022, half of Idaho's utility-scale (1 megawatt or larger) electricity generating capacity is at hydroelectric power plants, 25% from natural gas, 17% of the state's total in-state electricity net generation came from wind facilities, 4% from solar and 1% from geothermal.[^cite_note-99] Washington State provides most of the natural gas used in Idaho through one of the two major pipeline systems supplying the state.

Idaho has an upper-boundary estimate of development potential to generate 44,320 GWh/year from 18,076 MW of wind power, and 7,467,000 GWh/year from [solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_in_Idaho) using 2,061,000 MW of photovoltaics (PV), including 3,224 MW of rooftop photovoltaics, and 1,267,000 MW of [concentrated solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_solar_power).[^cite_note-100] Idaho had 973 MW of installed wind power as of 2020.[^cite_note-101]

### Ports

The Port of Lewiston is the farthest inland Pacific port on the west coast. A series of dams and locks on the Snake River and Columbia River facilitate barge travel from Lewiston to Portland, where goods are loaded on ocean-going vessels.[^cite_note-104]

## Law and government



The [Idaho State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_State_Capitol) in [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho)

### State constitution

See also: [Idaho Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Constitutional_Convention)



Logo for the State of Idaho.

The Constitution of Idaho is roughly modeled after the national Constitution, with several additions. The constitution defines the form and functions of the state government, and may be amended through [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite). The state constitution presently requires the state government to maintain a [balanced budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanced_budget).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-105]

### Idaho Code and Statutes

All of Idaho's state laws are contained in the Idaho Code and Statutes. The code is amended through the legislature with the governor's approval. Idaho still operates under its original (1889) state constitution.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-105]

Idaho has one of the [strictest abortion laws in the nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_law_in_the_United_States_by_state).[^cite_note-106] In April 2023, Idaho became the first state to restrict interstate travel for abortion services.[^cite_note-\:2-107][^cite_note-\:3-108] Nearly all abortions are banned and private citizens can sue abortion providers. The Idaho Supreme Court has ruled there is no constitutional right to abortion. A federal judge ruled in 2022 that doctors cannot be punished for performing an abortion to protect a patient's health.[^cite_note-109] The state abortion laws have led to an outmigration of physicians who specialize in maternal/fetal care.[^cite_note-110]

The constitution of Idaho provides for three branches of government: the executive, legislative, and judicial branches. Idaho has a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) legislature, elected from 35 legislative districts, each represented by one senator and two representatives.

Since 1946, statewide elected constitutional officers have been elected to four-year terms. They include: [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Idaho), [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Idaho), [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_Idaho), [Idaho state controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_state_controller) (Auditor before 1994), Treasurer, [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Attorney_General), and Superintendent of Public Instruction.

Last contested in 1966, the office of Inspector of Mines was originally an elected constitutional position. Afterward, it was an appointed position and ultimately done away with entirely in 1974.

Idaho's government has an [alcohol monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage_control_state); the [Idaho State Liquor Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_State_Liquor_Division).

#### Executive branch

Further information: [List of governors of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Idaho), [Lieutenant Governor of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Idaho), and [Secretary of State of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_Idaho)

The governor of Idaho serves a four-year term and is elected during what is nationally referred to as midterm elections. As such, the governor is not elected in the same election year as the president of the United States. The current governor is [Republican](32070.md) [Brad Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Little), who was first elected in 2018 and re-elected in 2022. Idaho is one of 13 states that has no term limits for its governor.[^cite_note-111]

#### Legislative branch

Main article: [Idaho Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Legislature)



Chamber of the House of Representatives in 2018

Idaho's [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_\(United_States\)) is part-time. Because of this, Idaho's legislators are considered "citizen legislators", meaning their position as a legislator is not their main occupation. However, the session may be extended if necessary, and often is.

Terms for both the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Senate) and [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_House_of_Representatives) are two years. Legislative elections occur every even-numbered year.

Both of Idaho's state legislative chambers have been continuously controlled by Republicans since 1960. However, Democratic legislators are routinely elected from Boise, Pocatello, [Blaine County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine_County,_Idaho), and the northern Panhandle.

#### Judicial branch

Main article: [Courts of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Idaho)

The highest court in Idaho is the [Idaho Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Supreme_Court). There is also an intermediate [appellate court](643.md), the [Idaho Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Court_of_Appeals), which hears cases assigned to it from the Supreme Court. The state's District Courts serve seven judicial districts.[^cite_note-112]

After the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), many Midwestern and Southern [Democrats](5043544.md) moved to the [Idaho Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Territory). As a result, the early territorial legislatures were solidly controlled by Democrats. In contrast, most of the territorial governors were appointed by [Republican](32070.md) presidents and were Republicans. This led to sometimes-bitter clashes between the two parties, including a range war with the Democrats backing the sheepherders and the Republicans backing the cattlemen, which ended in the ["Diamondfield" Jack Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Davis_\(prospector\)) murder trial. In the 1880s, Republicans gained prominence in local politics.

In 1864, Clinton DeWitt Smith removed the territorial seal and the state constitution from a locked safe and took them to Boise. This effectively moved the capital from where they were stored ([Lewiston, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho)) to the current capital, Boise.[^cite_note-114]

Since Idaho's statehood, the Republican Party has typically been the dominant party in the state. At one time, Idaho had two Democratic parties, one being the mainstream and the other called the Anti-Mormon Democrats, lasting into the early 20th century. In the 1890s and early 1900s, the [Populist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist_Party_\(United_States\)) enjoyed prominence, while the Democratic Party maintained a brief dominance in the 1930s during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression). Since World War II, most statewide-elected officials have been Republicans, though the Democrats did hold the majority in the House (by one seat) in 1958 and the governorship from 1971 to 1995.

Idaho's congressional delegation has also been generally Republican since statehood. Several Idaho Democrats have had electoral success in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md) over the years, but the [Senate](24909346.md) delegation has been a Republican stronghold for decades. Several Idaho Republicans, including current Senators [Mike Crapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Crapo) and [Jim Risch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Risch), have won reelection to the Senate, but only [Frank Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Church) has won reelection as a Democrat. Church's [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_United_States_Senate_election_in_Idaho) victory was the last win for his party for either Senate seat, and [Walt Minnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Minnick)'s 2008 victory in the [1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho's_1st_congressional_district) was the last Democratic win in any congressional race.

In modern times, Idaho has been a reliably Republican state in presidential politics. It has not supported a Democrat for [president](24113.md) since [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election_in_Idaho). Even in that election, [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) defeated [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater) in the state by fewer than two percentage points, compared to a landslide nationally. In the [2004 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election), Republican [George W. Bush](3414021.md) carried Idaho by a margin of 38 percentage points and with 68.4% of the vote, winning in 43 of 44 counties. Only [Blaine County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine_County,_Idaho), which contains the [Sun Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Valley,_Idaho) ski resort, supported [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry), who owns a home in the area. In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election) [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s 36.1 percent[^cite_note-115] showing was the best for a Democratic presidential candidate in Idaho since [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election). However, Republican margins were narrower in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election) and [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election).

In the 2006 elections, Republicans, led by gubernatorial candidate [Butch Otter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._L._Otter), won all the state's constitutional offices and retained both of the state's seats in the House. However, Democrats picked up several seats in the Idaho Legislature, notably in the Boise area.[^cite_note-116]

Idaho retains the [death penalty](5902.md). Pending the outcome of a legal challenge on a bill passed on March 20, 2023, authorized methods of execution include the [firing squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firing_squad).[^cite_note-117] [Abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Idaho) is severely restricted in Idaho.[^cite_note-118]

### K–12

See also: [List of school districts in Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Idaho), [List of high schools in Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Idaho), and [List of colleges and universities in Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Idaho)

As of January 2020, the State of Idaho contains 105 [school districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district)[^cite_note-119] and 62 [charter schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_school).[^cite_note-120] The school districts range in enrollment from two to 39,507 students.[^cite_note-121]

Idaho school districts are governed by elected school boards, which are elected in November of odd-numbered years,[^cite_note-122] except for the [Boise School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_School_District), whose elections are held in September.[^cite_note-123]

### Colleges and universities



The Jacob Spori Building at [Brigham Young University-Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University-Idaho) in Rexburg



[Idaho State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_State_University) in [Pocatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocatello,_Idaho)



[University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho) [Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho_Arboretum_and_Botanical_Garden) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho)

The Idaho State Board of Education oversees three comprehensive universities.[^cite_note-124] The [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho) was the first university in the state (founded in 1889). It opened its doors in 1892 and is the [land-grant institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university) and primary research university of the state. [Idaho State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_State_University) in Pocatello opened in 1901 as the Academy of Idaho, attained four-year status in 1947 and university status in 1963. [Boise State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_State_University) is the most recent school to attain university status in Idaho. The school opened in 1932 as Boise Junior College and became Boise State University in 1974. [Lewis-Clark State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis-Clark_State_College) in [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho) is the only public, non-university four-year college in Idaho. It opened as a [normal school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_school) in 1893.[^cite_note-125]

Idaho has four regional community colleges: [North Idaho College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Idaho_College) in [Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene,_Idaho); [College of Southern Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Southern_Idaho) in [Twin Falls](110784.md); [College of Western Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Western_Idaho) in [Nampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nampa,_Idaho), which opened in 2009, [College of Eastern Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Eastern_Idaho) in [Idaho Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Falls,_Idaho), which transitioned from a [technical college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_college) in 2017.

Private institutions in Idaho are [Boise Bible College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_Bible_College), affiliated with congregations of the [Christian churches and churches of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_churches_and_churches_of_Christ); [Brigham Young University-Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University-Idaho) in [Rexburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rexburg,_Idaho), which is affiliated with the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and a [sister college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_college) to [Brigham Young University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University); The [College of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Idaho) in [Caldwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldwell,_Idaho), which still maintains a loose affiliation with the Presbyterian Church; [Northwest Nazarene University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Nazarene_University) in [Nampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nampa,_Idaho); and [New Saint Andrews College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Saint_Andrews_College) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho), of [Reformed Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Christian) theological background. [McCall College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCall_College) is a non-affiliated two-year private college in [McCall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCall,_Idaho) that was founded in 2011 and later opened in 2013.

## Health

### Ban on COVID-19 vaccines

In October 2024, a health department in Idaho voted 4–3 to stop providing [COVID-19 vaccines](63319438.md) to residents in six counties. Opposite mainstream healthcare providers' and epidemiologists' pleas against the decision were more than 290 public comments, many of which called for an end to vaccine mandates or taxpayer funding of the vaccines, neither of which was happening in the district.[^cite_note-126]

### Hantavirus

In 2026, the irrigation system (irrigation canals) around Boise is causing rats from California to migrate into the state. There is an influx of rats in Idaho and there is a concern that the [hantavirus](416945.md) could spread to the local population.[^cite_note-127] There has been evidence of deer rats and other species of rats carrying the hantavirus in northern and central Idaho.[^cite_note-128]

## In popular culture
