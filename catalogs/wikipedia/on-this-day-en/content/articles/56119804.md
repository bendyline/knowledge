The **insurgency in Cabo Delgado** is an ongoing [Islamist](15012.md) [insurgency](589841.md) in [Cabo Delgado Province](448193.md), [Mozambique](19301.md), mainly fought between militant [Islamists](15012.md) and [jihadists](7826589.md) attempting to establish an [Islamic state](2646438.md) in the region, and Mozambican security forces.[^cite_note-CTC_2021-1] Civilians have been the main targets of [terrorist attacks](566821.md) by Islamist militants.[^cite_note-48] The main insurgent faction is [Ansar al-Sunna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Sunna_\(Mozambique\)), a native extremist faction with tenuous international connections. From mid-2018, the [Islamic State](9087364.md)'s [Central Africa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_%E2%80%93_Central_Africa_Province) has allegedly become active in northern Mozambique as well,[^cite_note-confirm-49] and claimed its first attack against Mozambican security forces in June 2019.[^cite_note-IS_first_attack-28] In addition, bandits have exploited the rebellion to carry out raids.[^cite_note-jazeera-9] As of 2020, the insurgency intensified, as in the first half of 2020 there were nearly as many attacks carried out as in the whole of 2019.[^cite_note-50]

Ansar al-Sunna (English: "Supporters of the [Tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah)") is known locally as [al-Shabaab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shabaab_\(Mozambique\)) but they are not formally related to the better known [Somali al-Shabaab](8726463.md).[^cite_note-51] Some of the militants are known to speak [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), the official language of Mozambique, however others speak [Kimwane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimwani_language), the local language, and [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language), the [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) language spoken north of that area in the [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region_\(Africa\)). Reports also state that members are allegedly mostly Mozambicans from [Mocimboa da Praia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moc%C3%ADmboa_da_Praia), [Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma,_Mozambique), and [Macomia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macomia_District) districts, but also include foreign nationals from [Tanzania](30118.md) and [Somalia](27358.md).[^cite_note-52]

## Background

Ansar al-Sunna, also known by its original name *Ahlu Sunnah Wa-Jamo* (translated: "adepts of the prophetic tradition"), was initially an Islamic religious movement in the northern districts of Cabo Delgado[^cite_note-Former_Policemen-30] which first appeared around 2015. It was formed by followers of the radical [Kenyan](188171.md) cleric [Aboud Rogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboud_Rogo), who was killed in 2012. Thereafter, some members of his movement settled down in Kibiti, [Tanzania](30118.md), before moving into Mozambique.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWest20185-53]

Ansar al-Sunna claims that [Islam as practised in Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Mozambique) has been [corrupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_Islam) and no longer follows [the teachings of Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith). The movement's members consequently entered traditional mosques with weapons in order to threaten others to follow [their own radical beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Islam).[^cite_note-Former_Policemen-30] The movement is also [anti-Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians),[^cite_note-auto6-54] [anti-Animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_traditional_African_religion), and [anti-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Western_sentiment), and has tried to prevent people from attending hospitals or schools which it considers secular and anti-Islamic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWest20186-55][^cite_note-extremist_threat-56] This behavior alienated much of the local population instead of converting them to Ahlu Sunnah Wa-Jamo, so that the movement's members broke away and formed their own places of worship.[^cite_note-extremist_threat-56] Over time, the group became increasingly violent: it called an extreme form of *[Sharia](28840.md)* to be implemented in the country,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWest20186-55] no longer recognized the Mozambican government, and started to form hidden camps in [Macomia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macomia_District), [Mocímboa da Praia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moc%C3%ADmboa_da_Praia_District), and [Montepuez District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montepuez_District). There, Ansar al-Sunna militants were trained by ex-policemen, and ex-frontier guards who had been fired and held grudges against the government. The movement also contacted other Islamist militants in East Africa, and reportedly hired trainers from [Somalia](27358.md), [Tanzania](30118.md), and [Kenya](188171.md).[^cite_note-Former_Policemen-30] Some of the Ansar al-Sunna militants have also journeyed abroad to receive direct training by other militant groups.[^cite_note-extremist_threat-56]

The militants are not unified, but split into different cells which do not appear to coordinate their actions.[^cite_note-extremist_threat-56] By August 2018, the Mozambican police had identified six men as leaders of the militants in Cabo Delgado: Abdul Faizal, Abdul Raim, Abdul Remane, Ibn Omar, "Salimo", and Nuno Remane.[^cite_note-ringleaders-18] Ansar al-Sunna funds itself through [drug trafficking](218449.md) (primarily [heroin](14034.md)), [contraband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraband), and [ivory trade](26744317.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWest20186-55]

While religion does play a fundamental role in the conflict, analysts believe the most important factors in the insurgency are widespread social, economic, and political problems in Mozambique. Unemployment and especially youth unemployment are considered the main causes for locals to join the Islamist rebels. Increasing inequalities have led many young people to be easily [attracted by such a radical movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicalization),[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Former_Policemen-30][^cite_note-extremist_threat-56] as Ansar al-Sunna promises that its form of Islam will act as "antidote" to the existing "corrupt, elitist rule".[^cite_note-ringleaders-18] Most rebels belong to the [Mwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwani_language) and Makwa ethnic groups that are native to Cabo Delgado; civilians belonging to these peoples have expressed sympathy for the insurgents. In general, the province lacks infrastructure and the state is underrepresented, easing the spread of the insurgency.[^cite_note-rfi-25]

In an interview to [Aid to the Church in Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_to_the_Church_in_Need), in October 2024, the bishop of Pemba lamented what he described as a lack of solidarity with Cabo Delgado from the rest of the country. "Since I have been in [Pemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Pemba), I have found it difficult to mark 4 October as Peace Day. The truth is that we should be celebrating it as [Rome Peace Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambican_Civil_War) Day, because in Cabo Delgado peace does not exist. Cabo Delgado is part of Mozambique, therefore there is no peace in Mozambique. It is hard for me to listen to speeches where people say that we have peace, when there is no peace in the whole country. When I hear these speeches, I feel there is a certain lack of solidarity with the people of Cabo Delgado, as if this was their problem, and not the country's problem."[^cite_note-58]

Speaking to [Aid to the Church in Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_to_the_Church_in_Need) in June 2025, the bishop of Tete, in Northwest Mozambique, said that Mozambique needs international help to solve the crisis in Cabo Delgado.[^cite_note-59]

[Pope Francis](1687680.md) mentioned the conflict in Cabo Delgado on a number of occasions, and [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) mentioned it for the first time in August 2025.[^cite_note-60]

### 2017

- On 5 October, a pre-dawn raid targeted 3 police stations in the town of [Mocímboa da Praia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moc%C3%ADmboa_da_Praia). It was led by 30 armed members, who killed 17 people, including two police officers and a community leader. 14 of the perpetrators were captured. During this brief occupation of Mocímboa da Praia, the perpetrators stole firearms and ammunition and told residents that they reject state health and education, and refused to pay taxes. The group is said to be affiliated with [Al-Shabaab](8726463.md), the [Al Qaeda](1921.md)-affiliated Islamist extremist group situated and operating in mostly the southern regions of Somalia.

  [^cite_note-61]

- On 10 October, police detained 52 suspects in relation to the attack on 5 October.

  [^cite_note-62]

- On 21 October, a pre-dawn skirmish took place between the group and government forces in the fishing village of Maluku, approximately 30 kilometres (19 mi) from Mocímboa da Praia. As a result, many locals fled the village.

  [^cite_note-auto-63]

- On 22 October, further skirmishes occurred near Columbe village, about 16 kilometres (9.9 mi) south of an installation of [Anadarko Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadarko_Petroleum).

  [^cite_note-auto-63]

- On 27 October 2017, the Mozambican police confirmed the arrest of 100 more members of the group, including foreigners, in relation to the attack on 5 October.

  [^cite_note-64]

- On 24 November, in the northern Mozambican province of [Cabo Delgado](448193.md), the government ordered the closure of three mosques located in [Pemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemba,_Mozambique) and in the neighbourhoods of Cariaco, Alto Gigone and Chiuba, which were believed to have a connection with [Islamic fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_fundamentalism).

  [^cite_note-65]

- On 29 November, the group attacked the villages of Mitumbate and Maculo, injuring two and killing at least two people. The two deaths were by [decapitation](70802.md) and [death by burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_by_burning). According to local authorities, the terrorists also destroyed a church and 27 homes.

  [^cite_note-66]

- On 4 December, the district government of Moçímboa da Praia in northern Mozambique named two men, Nuro Adremane and Jafar Alawi, as suspected of organising the attacks by an armed group against the police in October. Both men were Mozambican nationals. The district government stated that both men studied Islam in [Tanzania](30118.md), [Sudan](27421.md) and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), where they allegedly also received military training.

  [^cite_note-67]

- On 17 December, a successful assassination attempt was committed on the National Director of Reconnaissance of the Police Rapid Intervention Unit.

  [^cite_note-68]

- On 26 December, Police Spokesman Inacio Dino announced the commencement of counter-insurgency operations in the forests surrounding Mutumbate, in Cabo Delgado province. Since the amnesty for surrendering expired, stated that 36 Tanzanian citizens would be targeted by the operations.

  [^cite_note-69]

- On 29 December, the independent Mozambican newspaper "O Pais" reported that Mozambican paratroopers and marines attacked the village of Mitumbate via air and sea, regarding it as a stronghold for the insurgents. The aftermath of the attack left 50 dead, including women and children, and an unknown number injured.

  [^cite_note-70]

### 2019



Satellite image of [Cyclone Kenneth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Kenneth) approaching [Mozambique](19301.md) on 25 April 2019.

- Sometime in January or early February 2019, security forces captured Abdul Rahmin Faizal, a suspected insurgent leader of Ugandan nationality.

  [^cite_note-presented-10]

- On 8 February, Islamist fighters attacked Piqueue village in Cabo Delgado, killing and dismembering seven men, and kidnapping four women.

  [^cite_note-90]

- After [Cyclone Kenneth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Kenneth) hit Mozambique on 25 April, resulting in much devastation, the rebels initially halted their attacks. On 3 May, however, they struck once again by destroying the village of Nacate, Macomia District, killing six civilians. In the following weeks, the Islamists increased their attacks, raiding and burning several villages such as Ntapuala and Banga-Vieja in Macomia District, as well as Ida and Ipho in Meluco District. They also carried out ambushes, and told locals to abandon their homes. At least two attacks targeted workers of [Anadarko Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadarko_Petroleum), a [United States](3434750.md)-headquartered [hydrocarbon exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration) company.

  [^cite_note-cyclone-91]

- On 4 June, ISIL claimed that its "[Central Africa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State's_Central_Africa_Province)" branch had carried out a successful attack on the Mozambican Army at Mitopy in the [Mocímboa da Praia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moc%C3%ADmboa_da_Praia_District).

  [^cite_note-IS_first_attack-28]

  &#x20;At least 16 people were killed and about 12 wounded during the attack. By this point, ISIL considered Ansar al-Sunna as one its affiliates, though how many Islamist rebels in Mozambique are actually loyal to ISIL remains unclear.

  [^cite_note-foothold-92]

## Insurgency as a maritime threat

In 2011, Northern Mozambique gained much international attention when large offshore gas fields containing up to 425 billion cubic meters of gas, were discovered.[^cite_note-305] With the goal of becoming a major international player in the energy sector, Mozambique's government has prioritized the region's economic attractiveness and has sent its army to ensure the safety of planned investments.[^cite_note-306][^cite_note-307] Foreign activities include a \$30 billion investment of US-based company Exxon Mobil and expected \$20 billion of Total Energies.[^cite_note-308] While Total Energies has stated that they will pause their investment to reassess the security situation in Northern Mozambique, the EU's planned reduction in Russian gas imports has made the project more likely.[^cite_note-309]

Terrorist activity is viewed as a risk to international LNG investment, with concerns raised about the kidnapping of foreign workers.[^cite_note-310] However, indigenous coastal communities and maritime facilities are also targets of violent activity: in 2021, the port town of Palma was attacked, killing dozens of civilians.[^cite_note-311] Maritime infrastructure has also been targeted, with the key port of Mocimboa da Praia being seized.[^cite_note-312]

Attacks on maritime infrastructure can be attributed to rising social inequality.[^cite_note-\:2-313] The exploitation of the region's natural resources, including the offshore gas fields, puts the native population at risk, as they face displacement and very little profit returns to the region due to high levels of corruption. Finding its root in the precarious economic situation and the political marginalization of the native population, local conflicts have been used to radicalize young men.[^cite_note-\:2-313][^cite_note-314]

Given the increase in extreme violent activities and simultaneously in military personnel, tensions are likely to rise as the region's economic exploitation continues. This also endangers the region's maritime environment. Conflicts on the land spill over to the sea, creating an unsafe environment for offshore investments.[^cite_note-315] Because Northern Mozambique is a major transnational drug trafficking hub, contested governance on land and sea facilitates maritime heroin smuggling.[^cite_note-316]

The deteriorating security situation has led to various international efforts in order to support Mozambique's maritime capabilities and ensure safety along the coast. Russia deployed the Wagner Group, a private security organization heavily linked to the Russian government[^cite_note-317] in Northern Mozambique but failed its mission to support counter-insurgency activities and withdrew in 2020.[^cite_note-Brookings-103][^cite_note-Oxford-104] Wagner Group was allegedly aided by the Russian fleet, though this has not been officially proven.[^cite_note-318] Mozambique has formed an arrangement with Italy to train its navy,[^cite_note-319] while Portugal, the former colonial power, has donated speedboats to the country.[^cite_note-320] Furthermore, Mozambique and India are cooperating on marine security issues, with India assisting in the development of the country's naval infrastructure and the training of personnel.[^cite_note-321] In addition, South Africa has dispatched naval patrol vessels, and Rwanda has increased its presence along the coast.[^cite_note-322] Currently, the UNODC is assisting Mozambique's naval capabilities by training the country's Navy, Maritime Authority, and maritime law enforcement officers, thereby improving domain awareness and port security.[^cite_note-323] However, mere capability training for law enforcement authorities is controversial, as corruption, power abuse and involvement in illicit economies is high in Mozambique's state authorities.[^cite_note-324][^cite_note-325]

## Limits on media freedom

There is a lack of access to reliable information in the region due to journalists being intimidated by government and military personnel. On 5 January 2019, Mozambican authorities unlawfully detained journalist Amade Abubacar, who had reported on the insurgency. He was subsequently subjected to [torture](47702.md), and only released on bail after 107 days in [detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detention_\(imprisonment\)).[^cite_note-326]

## War crimes

The rebels have committed extensive war crimes, targeting and murdering civilians on many occasions during the insurgency.[^cite_note-327]

In addition, Mozambican security forces were reportedly filmed as they tortured and murdered captured rebel fighters. [Amnesty International](18947898.md) said that the videos were genuine, while Mozambique's defense ministry spokesperson Omar Saranga argued that they were probably produced by the rebels themselves, using captured uniforms to produce propaganda against the government.[^cite_note-328]
