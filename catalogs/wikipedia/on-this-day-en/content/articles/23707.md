A **priest** is a [religious leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_leader) authorized to perform the sacred [rituals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual) of a religion, especially as a mediatory agent between humans and one or more [deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deity). They also have the authority or power to administer religious rites; in particular, rites of sacrifice to, and propitiation of, a deity or deities. Their office or position is the "**priesthood**", a term which also may apply to such persons collectively. A priest may have the duty to hear confessions periodically, give marriage counseling, provide prenuptial counseling, give spiritual direction, teach [catechism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism), or visit those confined indoors, such as the sick in hospitals and nursing homes.

## Description

According to the [trifunctional hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trifunctional_hypothesis) of prehistoric [Proto-Indo-European society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_society), priests have existed since the earliest of times and in the simplest societies, most likely as a result of [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_surplus#Neolithic) surplus and consequent [social stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification).[^cite_note-1] The necessity to read sacred texts and keep temple or church records helped foster literacy in many early societies. Priests exist in many religions today, such as all or some branches of [Judaism](15624.md), [Christianity](5211.md), [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto), and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism). They are generally regarded as having privileged contact with the [deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deity) or deities of the religion to which they subscribe, often interpreting the meaning of events and performing the rituals of the religion. There is no common definition of the duties of priesthood between faiths; but generally it includes mediating the relationship between one's congregation, [worshippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipper), and other members of the religious body, and its deity or deities, and administering religious [rituals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual) and rites. These often include blessing worshipers with prayers of joy at marriages, after a birth, and at [consecrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecrations), teaching the wisdom and [dogma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma) of the faith at any regular worship service, and mediating and easing the experience of grief and death at funerals – maintaining a spiritual connection to the [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife) in faiths where such a concept exists. Administering religious building grounds and office affairs and papers, including any religious library or collection of [sacred texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_text), is also commonly a responsibility – for example, the modern term for [clerical duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary) in a secular office refers originally to the duties of a [cleric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleric). The question of which religions have a "priest" depends on how the titles of leaders are used or translated into English. In some cases, leaders are more like those that other believers will often turn to for advice on spiritual matters, and less of a "person authorized to perform the sacred rituals." For example, [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy) in [Catholicism](606848.md) and [Eastern Orthodoxy](10186.md) are *priests*, as with certain synods of [Lutheranism](23371382.md) and [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism), though other branches of [Protestant Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), such as Methodists and Baptists, use *[minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_\(Christianity\))* and *[pastor](215153.md).* The terms *priest* and *priestess* are sufficiently generic that they may be used in an [anthropological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) sense to describe the religious mediators of an unknown or otherwise unspecified religion.

In many religions, being a priest or priestess is a full-time position, ruling out any other career. Many Christian priests and pastors choose or are mandated to dedicate themselves to their churches and receive their living directly from their churches. In other cases, it is a part-time role. For example, in the early [history of Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Iceland) the chieftains were titled *[goði](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go%C3%B0i)*, a word meaning "priest". As seen in the [saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saga) of [Hrafnkell Freysgoði](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrafnkels_saga), however, being a priest consisted merely of offering periodic sacrifices to the [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_mythology) gods and goddesses; it was not a full-time role, nor did it involve ordination.

In some religions, being a priest or priestess is by human election or human choice. In Judaism, the priesthood is inherited in familial lines. In a [theocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theocracy), a society is [governed](12229.md) by its priesthood.

## Etymology

The word "priest", is ultimately derived from Latin via [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) *[presbyter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyter)*,[^cite_note-2] the term for "elder", especially elders of Jewish or Christian communities in [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity). The Latin *presbyter* ultimately represents Greek πρεσβύτερος *presbúteros*, the regular Latin word for "priest" being *[sacerdos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_ancient_Roman_religion#sacerdos)*, corresponding to ἱερεύς *hiereús*.

It is possible that the Latin word was loaned into [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English), and only from Old English reached other Germanic languages via the [Anglo-Saxon mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_mission) to the continent, giving [Old Icelandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Icelandic) *prestr*, [Old Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swedish) *präster*, [Old High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_German) *priast*. Old High German also has the disyllabic *priester, priestar*, apparently derived from Latin independently via [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French) *presbtre*.

An alternative theory makes *priest* cognate with Old High German *priast*, *prest*, from [Vulgar Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulgar_Latin) \**prevost* "one put over others", from Latin *praepositus* "person placed in charge".[^cite_note-3]

That English should have only the single term *priest* to translate *presbyter* and *sacerdos* came to be seen as a problem in [English Bible translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Bible_translations). The *presbyter* is the minister who both presides and instructs a Christian congregation, while the *sacerdos*, offerer of [sacrifices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrifice), or in a Christian context the [eucharist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist), performs "mediatorial offices between God and man".[^cite_note-4]

The feminine English noun, *priestess*, was coined in the 17th century, to refer to female priests of the pre-Christian religions of classical antiquity. In the 20th century, the word was used in controversies surrounding the [women ordained in the Anglican communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordination_of_women_in_the_Anglican_Communion), who are referred to as "priests", irrespective of gender, and the term priestess is generally considered archaic in Christianity.

Webster's 1829 Dictionary stated "PRIEST, *noun* \[Latin proestes, a chief, one that presides; proe, before, and sto, to stand, or sisto.]" <https://webstersdictionary1828.com/Dictionary/priest>

In historical [polytheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheism), a priest administers the [sacrifice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrifice) to a deity, often in highly elaborate [ritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual). In the [Ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East), the priesthood also acted on behalf of the deities in managing their property.

Priestesses in antiquity often performed [sacred prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_prostitution), and in Ancient Greece, some priestesses such as [Pythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythia), priestess at [Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi), acted as [oracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle).

### Ancient Egypt

In [ancient Egyptian religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_religion), the right and obligation to interact with the [gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_deities) belonged to the [pharaoh](23294.md). He delegated this duty to priests, who were effectively bureaucrats authorized to act on his behalf. Priests staffed [temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_temple) throughout Egypt, giving offerings to the [cult images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_image) in which the gods were believed to take up residence and performing other rituals for their benefit.[^cite_note-10] Little is known about what training may have been required of priests, and the selection of personnel for positions was affected by a tangled set of traditions, although the pharaoh had the final say. In the [New Kingdom of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt), when temples owned great estates, the high priests of the most important cult—that of [Amun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amun) at [Karnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnak)—were important political figures.[^cite_note-11]

High-ranking priestly roles were usually held by men. Women were generally relegated to lower positions in the temple hierarchy, although some held specialized and influential positions, especially that of the [God's Wife of Amun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God's_Wife_of_Amun), whose religious importance overshadowed the [High Priests of Amun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Priest_of_Amun) in the [Late Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Period_of_Egypt).[^cite_note-12]

### Ancient Rome

See also: [College of Pontiffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Pontiffs)

In [ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) and throughout Italy, the ancient sanctuaries of [Ceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceres_\(mythology\)) and [Proserpina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proserpina) were invariably led by female *sacerdotes*, drawn from women of local and Roman elites. It was the only public priesthood attainable by Roman matrons and was held in great honor.

A Roman matron was any mature woman of the upper class, married or unmarried. Females could serve public cult as [Vestal Virgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestal_Virgin) but few were chosen, and then only from young maidens of the upper class.[^cite_note-13]

### Ancient Greece

- The [Pythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythia) was the title of a priestess at the very ancient temple of [Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi) that was dedicated to the [Earth Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Mother). She was widely credited for her [prophecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophecy). The priestess retained her role when the temple was rededicated to [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo), giving her a prominence unusual for a woman in the male-dominated culture of [classical Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greece).

- The [Phrygian Sibyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygian_Sibyl) was the priestess presiding over an [Apollonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo) [oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle) at [Phrygia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia), a historical kingdom in the [Anatolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) highlands.

## Abrahamic religions

### Judaism

Main article: [Kohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohen)

#### Historical

Main article: [Priesthood (ancient Israel)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_\(ancient_Israel\))

After [the departure of the Israelites from Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exodus), priests in [ancient Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Israel_\(united_monarchy\)) were required by the [Law of Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Moses) to be direct [patrileneal descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrilineal_descent) of [Aaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron), the elder brother of [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses). In Exodus 30\:22–25 [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JHWH) instructs Moses to make a [holy anointing-oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_anointing_oil) to consecrate the priests "for all of eternity". During the times of the two Jewish [Temples in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_in_Jerusalem), the Aaronic priests performed the daily and special [Jewish-holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_holiday) offerings and sacrifices within the temples; these offerings are known as the *[korbanot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korbanot)*.

In Hebrew, the word for "priest" is *[kohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohen)* (singular כהן *kohen*, plural כּהנִים *kohanim*), hence the family names [*Cohen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen_\(surname\)), *[Cahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahn)*, *[Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahn)*, *[Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohn)*, *[Kogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kogan)*, etc. Jewish families with these names belong to the [tribe of Levi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Levi) ([Levites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levites) – descended from [Levi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi), the great-grandfather of Aaron) and in twenty-four instances are called by scripture as such.[^cite_note-14] In Hebrew, the word for "priesthood" is *kehunnah*.

The Hebrew word *kohen* comes from the [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_root) KWN/KON כ-ו-ן 'to stand, to be ready, established'[^cite_note-\:0-15] in the sense of "someone who stands ready before God",[^cite_note-16] and has cognates in other [Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages), e.g. [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_language) [KHN 𐤊𐤄𐤍 "priest"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabnit_sarcophagus#Inscription) or [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) *kahin* كاهن "priest".

#### Modern Judaism

See also: [List of disqualifications for the Jewish priesthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_disqualifications_for_the_Jewish_priesthood)

Since the [destruction of the Second Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Second_Temple), and (therefore) the cessation of the daily and seasonal temple ceremonies and sacrifices, [kohanim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohanim) have become much less prominent. In traditional Judaism ([Orthodox Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) and to some extent, [Conservative Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Judaism)) a few priestly and [Levitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levitical) functions, such as the *[pidyon haben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pidyon_haben)* (redemption of a first-born son) ceremony and the [Priestly Blessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestly_Blessing), have been retained. Especially in Orthodox Judaism, kohanim remain subject to a number of restrictions concerning matters related to marriage and [ritual purity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumah_and_taharah).

[Orthodox Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) regard the kohanim as being held in reserve for a future [restored Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Temple). Kohanim do not perform roles of propitiation, sacrifice, or sacrament in any branch of [Rabbinical Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbinical_Judaism) or in [Karaite Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaite_Judaism). The principal religious function of any kohanim is to perform the [Priestly Blessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestly_Blessing), although an individual kohen may also become a [rabbi](51273.md) or other professional religious leader.

##### Beta Israel

The traditional [Beta Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Israel) community in Israel had little direct contact with other Jewish groups after the destruction of the temple and developed separately for almost two thousand years. While some Beta Israel now follow Rabbinical Jewish practices, the Ethiopian Jewish religious tradition ([Haymanot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymanot)) uses the word [Kahen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahen) to refer to a type non-hereditary cleric.

### Samaritanism

Aaronic Kohanim also officiated at the [Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan) temple on [Mount Gerizim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gerizim). The Samaritan kohanim have retained their role as religious leaders.

### Christianity



[Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) priest wearing [epitrachelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epitrachelion) (stole) and [epimanikia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epimanikia) (cuffs), [Mtskheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mtskheta), [Republic of Georgia](48768.md)

With the spread of Christianity and the formation of [parishes](23623.md), the Greek word ἱερεύς *hiereús* and Latin word *sacerdos*, which Christians had since the 3rd century applied to [bishops](4092.md) and only in a secondary sense to [presbyters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyter), began in the 6th century to be used for presbyters,[^cite_note-17] and is today commonly used for presbyters, distinguishing them from bishops.[^cite_note-18]

Apart from the ministerial priesthood, the general priesthood or the [priesthood of all believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_of_all_believers), is a [Christian](5211.md) doctrine derived from several passages of the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament). It is a foundational concept of [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism),[^cite_note-19] and the same concept is affirmed by the Catholic Church: in Jesus, "all the faithful are made a holy and royal priesthood".[^cite_note-po-20]<sup>: Paragraph 2 </sup>

Today, the term "priest" is used in the [Catholic Church](606848.md), [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy), the [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East), the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormons, LDS) and many branches of [Lutheranism](23371382.md) and [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism), to refer to those who have been [ordained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordained) to a ministerial position through receiving the [sacrament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrament) of [Holy Orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Orders), although "presbyter" is also used.[^cite_note-21] Since the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), non-sacramental denominations are more likely to use the term "[elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_\(Christianity\))" to refer to their pastors. The Christian term "priest" does not have an entry in the [Anchor Bible Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor_Bible_Series), but the dictionary does deal with the above-mentioned terms under the entry for "Sheep, Shepherd".[^cite_note-22]

In Western Christianity, priests are usually styled "[The Reverend](99176.md)".

#### Eastern Orthodoxy



[Married Eastern Orthodox priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_marriage) from [Jerusalem](16043.md) with his family (three generations), c. 1893

Main article: [Priesthood (Eastern Orthodox Church)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_\(Eastern_Orthodox_Church\))

In Orthodoxy, the normal minimum age is thirty (Can. 11 of Neocaesarea) but a bishop may dispense with this if needed. In neither tradition may priests marry after ordination. In the Catholic Church, priests in the Latin Church must be [celibate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_celibacy) except under special rules for married clergy converting from certain other Christian confessions.[^cite_note-23] Married men may become priests in [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), but cannot marry after ordination, even if they become [widowed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widower). In the view of the Eastern Orthodox Churches:

> By allowing married men to enter the priesthood, the Orthodox Church affirms the blessedness of marriage without lessening the affirmation of the blessedness of celibacy. St Clement of Alexandria writes: "Celibacy and marriage each have their own functions and specific services to the Lord", and so "we pay homage to those whom the Lord has favoured with the gift of celibacy and admire monogamy and its dignity" (The Stromata, Book 3). Marriage according to the will of Christ, and celibacy as a devotion to Christ, are two different spiritual paths, equally valid for a true living of the spiritual life. This is so for ordained clergy as it is for everyone else.[^cite_note-Toumbelekis2014-24]

Candidates for bishop are chosen only from among the celibate. Orthodox priests will either wear a [clerical collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_collar) similar to the above-mentioned, or simply a very loose black robe that does not have a collar. The garb of an Eastern Orthodox priest is generally the same as that of an [Eastern Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic) or [Eastern Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lutheran) priest.

#### Oriental Orthodoxy

In the [Oriental Orthodox Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches), there are different orders of ministry, each with different vestments.[^cite_note-Manna2019-25] The order of the priest is known as the *Kashisho*.[^cite_note-Manna2019-25]

In addition to the ministerial priesthood, Oriental Orthodoxy does affirm the [priesthood of believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_of_believers), which is called the *Ulmoyo*.[^cite_note-Manna2019-25]

#### Catholicism

Main article: [Priesthood in the Catholic Church](8655005.md)



A [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) priest celebrating [Mass](5684685.md)

&#x20;The [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) set out in 1965 a statement "in some depth" concerning the lives and ministry of priests in the Catholic Church, stating that:

> Priests by sacred ordination and mission which they receive from the bishops are promoted to the service of Christ the Teacher, Priest and King. They share in his ministry, a ministry whereby the Church here on earth is unceasingly built up into the People of God, the Body of Christ and the Temple of the Holy Spirit.[^cite_note-po-20]<sup>: Preface </sup>

Only men who meet certain requirements may become priests. In Catholicism, the [canonical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law) minimum age is twenty-five. Bishops may dispense with this rule and ordain men up to one year younger. Dispensations of more than a year are reserved to the [Holy See](13393.md) (Can. 1031 §§ 1, 4.) A Catholic priest must be [incardinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incardination_and_excardination) by his bishop or his major religious [superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_\(hierarchy\)) in order to engage in public ministry. [Secular priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_clergy) are incardinated into a [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese), whereas religious priests live the [consecrated life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecrated_life) and can work anywhere in the world that their specific community operates. Married men may become priests in the [Eastern Catholic Churches](214903.md), but cannot marry after ordination, even if they become widowed.

The most significant [liturgical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_liturgy) acts reserved to priests in these traditions are the administration of the [Sacraments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrament), including the celebration of the [Holy Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) or [Divine Liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Liturgy) (the terms for the celebration of the [Eucharist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist) in the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) and Byzantine traditions, respectively), and the [Sacrament of Reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrament_of_Reconciliation), also called [Confession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confession_\(religion\)). The sacraments of [Anointing of the Sick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anointing_of_the_Sick) ([Extreme Unction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_Unction)) and [Confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation_\(Catholic_Church\)) are also administered by priests, though in the Western tradition Confirmation is ordinarily celebrated by a [bishop](4092.md). In the East, Chrismation is performed by the priest (using oil specially [consecrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecration_in_Christianity) by a bishop) immediately after Baptism, and [Unction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unction) is normally performed by several priests (ideally seven), but may be performed by one if necessary. In the West, [Holy Baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism) may be celebrated by anyone. The Vatican catechism states that "According to Latin tradition, the spouses as ministers of Christ's grace mutually confer upon each other the sacrament of [Matrimony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_marriage)".[^cite_note-vatican-26] Thus marriage is a sacrament administered by the couple to themselves, but may be witnessed and blessed by a deacon, or priest (who usually administers the ceremony). In the East, Holy Baptism and Marriage (which is called "Crowning") may be performed only by a priest. If a person is baptized *in extremis* (i.e., when in fear of immediate death), only the actual threefold immersion together with the scriptural words[^cite_note-27] may be performed by a layperson or deacon. The remainder of the rite, and [Chrismation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrismation), must still be performed by a priest, if the person survives. The only sacrament which may be celebrated only by a bishop is that of [Ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordination) (*cheirotonia*, "Laying-on of Hands"), or [Holy Orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders_in_the_Catholic_Church).

In the Catholic Church, the term "assistant priest" (used liturgically) applies to a minister assisting the priest who is the main celebrant,[^cite_note-28] while in parish ministry, an "assistant priest" or "assistant pastor" ministers "under the authority of the pastor".[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Such priests are also known as [curates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curate#Catholic_church).

#### Lutheranism



A Lutheran priest of the [Church of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Sweden) prepares for the celebration of [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) in [Strängnäs Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Str%C3%A4ngn%C3%A4s_Cathedral)

Conservative Lutheran reforms are reflected in the theological and practical view of the ministry of the church. Much of European Lutheranism follows the traditional Catholic governance of deacon, priest, and bishop. The Lutheran archbishops of Finland, Sweden, etc. and Baltic countries are the historic national primates and some ancient cathedrals and parishes in the Lutheran church were constructed many centuries before the Reformation. Indeed, ecumenical work within the Moravian Church, Anglican Communion and among Scandinavian Lutherans mutually recognize the [historic apostolic legitimacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_succession) and [full communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_communion). Likewise in America, Lutherans have embraced the apostolic succession of bishops in the full communion with Moravians and Episcopalians. Most Lutheran ordinations are performed by a bishop.

The [Church of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Sweden) has a threefold ministry of bishop, priest, and deacon and those ordained to the presbyterate are referred to as priests.[^cite_note-31] In the [Evangelical Lutheran Church of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_of_Finland), ordained presbyters are referred to by various publications, including Finnish ones, as pastors,[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] or priests.[^cite_note-Sequeira2021-34][^cite_note-Doe2011-35] In the United States, denominations like the [Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Missouri_Synod) use the terms "reverend" and "pastor" interchangeably for ordained members of the clergy;[^cite_note-36] the [Lutheran Church - International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church_-_International), a Confessional Lutheran denomination of [Evangelical Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Catholic) churchmanship, uses the term "priest" for those ordained to the presbyterate, who are addressed as "Father".[^cite_note-37]

It is the doctrine the priesthood of all believers that [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther) adduces in his 1520 *[To the Christian Nobility of the German Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_the_Christian_Nobility_of_the_German_Nation)* in order to dismiss the medieval Christian belief that Christians were to be divided into two classes: "spiritual" and "temporal" or non-spiritual.

#### Anglican or Episcopalian



An [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) priest in choir dress

Main article: [Anglican ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_ministry)

The role of a priest in the [Anglican Communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion) and the [Free Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Church_of_England) is largely the same as within the [Catholic Church](606848.md) and [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity), except that [canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law) in almost every [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) province restricts the administration of [confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation) to the [bishop](4092.md), just as with [ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordination). Although Anglican priests who are members of [religious orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_religious_order) must remain [celibate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celibate) (although there are exceptions, such as priests in the [Anglican Order of Cistercians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Cistercians)), the [secular clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_clergy)—bishops, priests, and deacons who are not members of religious orders—are permitted to marry before or after ordination (although in most provinces they are not permitted to [marry a person of the same sex](92656.md)). The Anglican churches, unlike the Catholic or [Eastern Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christian) traditions, have allowed the ordination of women as priests (referred to as "priests" not "priestesses") in some provinces since 1971.[^cite_note-38] This practice remains controversial, however; a minority of provinces (10 out of the 38 worldwide) retain an all-male priesthood.[^cite_note-39] Most [Continuing Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuing_Anglican_Movement) churches do not ordain women to the priesthood.

As Anglicanism represents a broad range of theological opinion, its [presbyterate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterate) includes priests who consider themselves no different in any respect from those of the Catholic Church, and a minority who prefer to use the title *[presbyter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyter)* in order to distance themselves from the more sacrificial theological implications which they associate with the word *priest*.

While *priest* is the official title of a member of the presbyterate in every Anglican province worldwide (retained by the Elizabethan Settlement), the ordination rite of certain provinces (including the [Church of England](5955.md)) recognizes the breadth of opinion by adopting the title *The Ordination of Priests (also called Presbyters).* Even though both words mean 'elders' historically the term *priest* has been more associated with the "[High Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Church)" or [Anglo-Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Catholic) wing, whereas the term "[minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_\(Christianity\))" has been more commonly used in "[Low Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Church)" or Evangelical circles.[^cite_note-40]

#### Latter Day Saints

Main article: [Priesthood (Latter Day Saints)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_\(Latter_Day_Saints\))



1898 depiction of the Restoration of the Aaronic Priesthood

In the [Latter Day Saint movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement), the priesthood is the power and authority of God given to man, including the authority to perform [ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_\(Latter_Day_Saints\)) and to act as a leader in the church. A body of priesthood holders is referred to as a [quorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quorum_\(Latter_Day_Saints\)). Priesthood denotes elements of both power and authority. The priesthood includes the power [Jesus](1095706.md) gave his [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_apostles) to perform miracles such as the casting out of devils and the healing of sick ([Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke) 9\:1). Latter Day Saints believe that the [Biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible) miracles performed by [prophets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophet) and apostles were performed by the power of the priesthood, including the miracles of Jesus, who holds all of the keys of the priesthood. The priesthood is formally known as the "Priesthood after the Order of the Son of God", but to avoid the too frequent use of the name of deity, the priesthood is referred to as the Melchizedek priesthood ([Melchizedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchizedek) being the high priest to whom [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham) paid tithes). As an authority, the priesthood is the authority by which a bearer may perform ecclesiastical acts of service in the name of God. Latter Day Saints believe that acts (and in particular, [ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_\(Latter_Day_Saints\))) performed by one with priesthood authority are recognized by God and are binding in heaven, on earth, and in the afterlife.

There is some variation among the Latter Day Saint denominations regarding who can be ordained to the priesthood. In [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (LDS Church), all worthy males above the age of 12 can be ordained to the priesthood. However, prior to a [policy change in 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Revelation_on_Priesthood), the LDS Church did not ordain men or boys who were of black African descent. The LDS Church does not ordain women to any of its priesthood offices. The Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints (now the Community of Christ), the second largest denomination of the movement, began ordaining women to all of its priesthood offices in 1984. This decision was one of the reasons that led to a schism in the church, which prompted the formation of the independent [Restoration Branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_Branches) movement from which other denominations have sprung, including the [Remnant Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remnant_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter_Day_Saints).

### Mandaeism

A [Mandaean priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaean_priest) refers to an ordained religious leader in [Mandaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism). In [Mandaean scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaean_scriptures), priests are referred to as *Naṣuraiia* ([Naṣoraeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasoraean_Mandaeans)).[^cite_note-Secret_Adam-41] All priests must undergo lengthy ordination ceremonies, beginning with [tarmida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarmida) initiation.[^cite_note-Buckley_2002-42] Mandaean religious leaders and copyists of religious texts hold the title *Rabbi* or in Arabic '[Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh)'.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

All Mandaean communities traditionally require the presence of a priest, since priests are required to officiate over all important religious rituals, including [masbuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbuta), [masiqta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masiqta), birth and wedding ceremonies. Priests also serve as teachers, scribes, and community leaders.[^cite_note-Buckley_2002-42]

There are three types of priests in [Mandaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism):[^cite_note-Buckley_2002-42]

- *[rišama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishama)* "leader of the people"

- *[ganzibria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzibra)* "treasurers" (from Old Persian *ganza-bara* "id.," Neo-Mandaic *ganzeḇrānā*)

- *[tarmidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarmida)* "disciples" (Neo-Mandaic *tarmidānā*)

Priests have lineages based on the succession of *ganzibria* priests who had initiated them. Priestly lineages, which are distinct from birth lineages, are typically recorded in the [colophons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colophon_\(publishing\)) of many [Mandaean texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaean_texts). The position is not hereditary, and any Mandaean male who is highly knowledgeable about religious matters is eligible to become a priest.[^cite_note-Buckley2010-45]

### Hinduism

Main article: [Hindu priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_priest)

A [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) priest traditionally comes from the [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) community.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] Priests are ordained and trained before practicing. Their training consists of a precise memorisation of texts in a written or printed form and learning how to pronouciate and recite these texts from a guru.[^cite_note-48]

While only men have traditionally been ordained as priests, developments including feminism in India have led to the opening of training schools for women to become priests.[^cite_note-49]

### Zoroastrianism

Main article: [Mobad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobad)

A Zoroastrian priest are called a Mobad and they officiate the *[Yasna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasna)*, pouring libations into the sacred fire to the accompaniment of ritual chants. The Mobad also prepare drinks for the *[haoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haoma)* ritual.[^cite_note-50]

### Taoism



A fortune-telling [Taoist priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoist_priest) with a customer outside of Changchun Temple, [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan)

The [Taoist priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoist_priest) (道士 "master of the [Dao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism)" p. 488) act as interpreters of the principles of Yin-Yang [5 elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxing_\(Chinese_philosophy\)) (fire, water, soil, wood, and metal p. 53) school of ancient Chinese philosophy, as they relate to marriage, death, festival cycles, and so on. The Taoist priest seeks to share the benefits of meditation with his or her community through public ritual and liturgy (p. 326). In the ancient priesthood before the Tang, the priest was called *Jijiu* ("libationer" p. 550), with both male and female practitioners selected by merit. The system gradually changed into a male only hereditary Taoist priesthood until more recent times (p. 550,551).[^cite_note-54]

Shinto priest and priestess in Japan.

[Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) priest and priestess in Japan

The Shinto priest is called a *[kannushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannushi)* (神主; lit. "Master of the [kami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami)"), originally pronounced *kamunushi*, sometimes referred to as a *shinshoku* (神職). A kannushi is the person responsible for the maintenance of a Shinto shrine, or *[jinja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto_shrine)*, purificatory rites, and for leading worship and veneration of a certain *kami*. Additionally, *kannushi* are aided by another priest class, *[miko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miko)* (巫女; "shrine maidens"), for many rites. The maidens may either be family members in training, apprentices, or local volunteers.

*[Saiin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiin_\(priestess\))* were female relatives of the Japanese emperor (termed *saiō*) who served as High Priestesses in [Kamo Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamo_Shrine). *Saiō* also served at [Ise Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ise_Grand_Shrine). *Saiin* priestesses usually were elected from royalty. In principle, *Saiin* remained unmarried, but there were exceptions. Some *Saiin* became [consorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_consort) of the emperor, called *Nyōgo* in Japanese. The *Saiin* order of priestesses existed throughout the Heian and Kamakura periods.

### Iṣẹṣe

The [Yoruba people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) of West Africa practice an indigenous religion with a hierarchy of priests and priestesses that dates to AD 800–1000.[^cite_note-55] [Ifá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If%C3%A1) priests and priestesses bear the titles [Babalawo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babalawo) for men and [Iyanifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyanifa) for women.[^cite_note-google-56] Priests and priestesses of the varied [Orisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orisha) are titled Babaloriṣa for men and Iyaloriṣa for women.[^cite_note-google2-57] Initiates are also given an Oriṣa or Ifá name that signifies under which deity they are initiated. For example, a Priestess of [Osun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshun) may be named Osunyẹmi, and a Priest of [Ifá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If%C3%A1) may be named Ifáyẹmi. This traditional culture continues to this day as initiates from all around the world return to Nigeria for initiation into the priesthood, and varied derivative sects in the New World (such as Cuban [Santería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santer%C3%ADa) and Brazilian [Umbanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbanda)) use the same titles to refer to their officers as well.

### Afro-Latin American religions

In Brazil, the priests in the [Umbanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbanda), [Candomblé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candombl%C3%A9) and [Quimbanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quimbanda) religions are called *[pai-de-santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pai-de-santo)* (literally "Father of saint" in English), or "babalorixá" (a word borrowed from [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language) *bàbálórìsà*, meaning *Father of the [Orisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orisha)*); its female equivalent is the *[mãe-de-santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A3e-de-santo)* ("Mother of saint"), also referred to as "ialorixá" ([Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language): *iyálórìsà*).

In the [Cuban](5042481.md) [Santería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santer%C3%ADa), a male priest is called *Santero*, while female priests are called *Iyanifas* or "mothers of wisdom".[^cite_note-58]

## Neo-paganism

### Wicca



Wiccan priestess preaching in the United States

According to traditional [Wiccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicca) beliefs, every member of the religion is considered a priestess or priest, as it is believed that no person can stand between another and the divine. However, in response to the growing number of Wiccan temples and [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiccan_church), several denominations of the religion have begun to develop a core group of ordained priestesses and priests serving a larger laity. This trend is far from widespread, but is gaining acceptance due to increased interest in the religion.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

In modern Wicca, there is no one specific form of dress designated for the clergy. If there is, it is a particular of the denomination in question, and not a universal practice. There is a traditional form of dress, (usually a floor-length [tunic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunic) and a knotted cord [cincture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincture), known as the *cingulum*), which is often worn by worshipers during religious rites. Among those traditions of Wicca that do dictate a specific form of dress for its clergy, they usually wear the traditional tunic in addition to other articles of clothing (such as an open-fronted [robe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robe) or a [cloak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloak)) as a distinctive form of religious dress, similar to a [habit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_habit).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]
