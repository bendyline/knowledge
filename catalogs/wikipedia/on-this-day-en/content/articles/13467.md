**Hamburg**,[^cite_note-9] officially the **Free and Hanseatic City of Hamburg**,[^cite_note-constitution-10][^cite_note-11] is the [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population) in [Germany](11867.md) after [Berlin](3354.md) and [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_European_Union_by_population_within_city_limits) city in the [European Union](9317.md), with a population of over 1.9 million.[^cite_note-citypopulation_urban-3][^cite_note-12] The [Hamburg Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Metropolitan_Region) has a population of over 5.1 million and is the [tenth-largest metropolitan region by GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_EU_metropolitan_areas_by_GDP) in the European Union. At the base of the [Jutland Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland_Peninsula), the 110 km (68 mi)-long [Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) [estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) begins in Hamburg, at the confluence of the [Alster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alster) and [Bille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bille_\(Elbe\)). Hamburg is one of Germany's three city-states alongside Berlin and [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\)), and is surrounded by [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md) to the north and [Lower Saxony](18435.md) to the south. The [Port of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hamburg) is Germany's largest and Europe's [third-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_ports_in_Europe), after [Rotterdam](4410985.md) and [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Antwerp). The [local dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_German) is a variant of [Low Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Saxon).

The official name reflects [Hamburg's history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hamburg) as a member of the medieval [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) and a [free imperial city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). Before the 1871 [unification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany), it was a fully [sovereign](28791.md) [city state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_state), and before 1919 formed a civic republic [headed constitutionally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) by a class of hereditary [Grand Burghers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Burgher), or *[Hanseaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseaten_\(class\))*. Beset by disasters such as the [Great Fire of Hamburg in 1842](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_Hamburg), the [Allied fire-bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II) of the city in 1943, and [North Sea flood of 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_flood_of_1962), the city has managed to recover and emerge wealthier after each catastrophe. Major regional broadcaster [NDR](2917610.md), the printing and publishing firm [Gruner + Jahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruner_+_Jahr) and the newspapers *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)* and *[Die Zeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Zeit)* are based in the city. Hamburg is the seat of Germany's oldest [stock exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Stock_Exchange) and the [world's oldest merchant bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_banks_in_continuous_operation), [Berenberg Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berenberg_Bank). Media, commercial, logistical, and industrial firms with significant locations in the city include multinationals [Airbus](26220236.md), [Blohm + Voss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blohm_+_Voss), [Aurubis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurubis), [Beiersdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiersdorf), [Lufthansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Technik) and [Unilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilever).

Hamburg is also a major European [science, research, and education hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Hamburg), with several universities and institutions, including the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg), and the Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron Laboratory [DESY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DESY). The city enjoys a very high quality of living, being ranked 28th in the 2024 [Mercer Quality of Living Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_Quality_of_Living_Survey).[^cite_note-mercer2019-13] Hamburg hosts specialists in world economics and international law, including [consular and diplomatic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_in_Hamburg) such as the [International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tribunal_for_the_Law_of_the_Sea), the [EU-LAC Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU-LAC_Foundation), and the [UNESCO Institute for Lifelong Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Institute_for_Lifelong_Learning), multipartite international political conferences and summits such as [Europe and China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Summit:_China_meets_Europe) and the [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_G20_Hamburg_summit). Former German chancellors [Helmut Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Schmidt) and [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) were both born in Hamburg. The former [Mayor of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Hamburg), [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz), was chancellor from December 2021 until May 2025.

Hamburg is a major international and domestic [tourist destination](#Tourism). The *[Speicherstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speicherstadt)* and *[Kontorhausviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontorhaus_District)* were declared [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-14] Hamburg's rivers and canals are crossed by around 2,500 bridges, making it the city with the highest number of bridges in Europe,[^cite_note-15] and Hamburg is also home to [five of the world's 29 tallest churches](800972.md#Overview_-_church_buildings_%E2%89%A5_99_m_\(325_ft\)_-_country_and_city_list), making it the city with the greatest number of churches surpassing 100 metres (330 ft) worldwide. Aside from its rich architectural heritage, the city is also home to notable cultural venues such as the [Elbphilharmonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbphilharmonie) and [Laeiszhalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laeiszhalle) concert halls. It gave birth to movements like *[Hamburger Schule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_Schule)* and paved the way for bands including [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles_in_Hamburg). Hamburg is also known for [several theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_theatres_in_Hamburg) and a variety of musical shows. [St. Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Pauli)'s [Reeperbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reeperbahn) is among the best-known European red-light districts.

## History

Main article: [History of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hamburg)

For the extended chronology of events, see [Timeline of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Hamburg).

### Origins

[Claudius Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy) (2nd century AD) reported the first name for the vicinity as [Treva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treva).[^cite_note-16]

### Etymology

The name *Hamburg* comes from the first permanent building on the site, a castle which the Emperor [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) ordered constructed in AD 808. It rose on rocky terrain in a marsh between the [Alster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alster) and the [Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) rivers as a defence against [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) incursion, and acquired the name *Hammaburg*, *burg* meaning castle or fort. The origin of the *Hamma* term remains uncertain,[^cite_note-17] but its location is estimated to be at the site of today's [Hammaburgplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_Cathedral,_Hamburg).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### Medieval Hamburg



Hamburg in 1150 (painting from 1835)

In 834 AD, Hamburg was established as the seat of a [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric). Its first bishop, [Ansgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansgar), later known as the Apostle of the North, founded the city's early Christian institutions. Two years later, Hamburg was linked with [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Bremen) to form the [Bishopric of Hamburg-Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_administrators,_archbishops,_bishops,_and_prince-archbishops_of_Bremen).[^cite_note-verg15-20]

Hamburg faced repeated destruction in its early centuries. In 845, a fleet of 600 [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) ships sailed up the [River Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) and destroyed the town, which then had about 500 inhabitants.[^cite_note-verg15-20] In 1030, King [Mieszko II Lambert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mieszko_II_Lambert) of the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland) burned the city, and in 1201 and 1214 it was raided and occupied by [Valdemar II of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_II_of_Denmark). The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) reached Hamburg in 1350, killing at least 60% of its population, and the city also suffered several major fires in the medieval period.[^cite_note-21]

A major turning point came in 1189 when [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa) granted Hamburg the status of a [Free Imperial City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Imperial_City). The imperial charter included tax-free passage down the Lower Elbe to the North Sea (a form of [free-trade zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-trade_zone)). In 1265, an allegedly forged charter was presented to or by the *Rat* (council) of Hamburg.[^cite_note-verg26-22]

Hamburg's location between the [North Sea](21179.md) and [Baltic Sea](3335.md) trade routes helped it become a major port in Northern Europe. Its 1241 alliance with [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck) is regarded as the founding moment of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League). On 8 November 1266, a contract between [Henry III of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_England) and Hamburg's merchants allowed them to establish a *hanse* in London — the first recorded use of the word for the League's trading guild.[^cite_note-verg30-23]

In 1270, *Jordan von [Boitzenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boitzenburger_Land)*, solicitor to the [Senate of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Hamburg), wrote the *Ordeelbook*, the first description of civil, criminal and procedural law for a German city in the German language.[^cite_note-24] On 10 August 1410, civil unrest resulted in a negotiated compromise (German: *Rezeß*), considered the first [constitution of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Hamburg).[^cite_note-25]

On 25 February 1356 — the first day of spring in medieval reckoning — the *Matthiae-Mahl* feast for [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) cities was celebrated for the first time. It continues today as the world's oldest ceremonial meal.[^cite_note-mattmahl-26]

### Early modern period



Hamburg as a walled town, c. 1600

In 1529, the city embraced [Lutheranism](23371382.md), and it received [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Reformed_church) refugees from the [Netherlands](21148.md) and France.

When [Jan van Valckenborgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Valckenborgh) introduced a second layer to the fortifications to protect against the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) in the seventeenth century, he extended Hamburg and created a "New Town" (*Neustadt*) whose street names are still based upon the grid system of roads he introduced.[^cite_note-27]

From the autumn of 1696 to the spring of 1697, the [Company of Scotland trading to Africa and the Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_of_Scotland_Trading_to_Africa_and_the_Indies) was active in Hamburg. While it was unsuccessful in raising capital locally, it commissioned the construction of four vessels in the port. The *Caledonia*, a ship of 600 tons with 56 guns, and the *Instuaration* (later renamed the *St. Andrew*), a vessel of 350 tons, were launched in March 1697.[^cite_note-28]

Upon the dissolution of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1806, the [Free Imperial City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Imperial_City) of Hamburg was not incorporated into a larger administrative area while retaining special privileges ([mediatised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_mediatisation)), but became a [sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign) state with the official title of the *Free and Hanseatic City of Hamburg*. Hamburg was briefly annexed by [Napoleon I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I) to the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) (1804–1814/1815). Russian forces under [General Bennigsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levin_August,_Count_von_Bennigsen) finally freed the city in 1814. Hamburg re-assumed its pre-1811 status as a city-state in 1814. The [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) of 1815 confirmed Hamburg's independence and it became one of 39 sovereign states of the [German Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation) (1815–1866).

In 1842, about a quarter of the inner city was destroyed in the [Great Fire of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_Hamburg). The fire started on the night of 4 May and was not extinguished until 8 May. It destroyed three churches, the town hall, and many other buildings. 51 people were killed and an estimated 20,000 people were left homeless.[^cite_note-29] Reconstruction took more than 40 years.



Hamburg in 1811

After periodic political unrest, particularly during the [Revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848), Hamburg adopted in 1860 a semidemocratic constitution that provided for the election of the Senate, the governing body of the city-state, by adult taxpaying males. Other innovations included the separation of powers, the separation of Church and State, freedom of the press, of assembly and association. Hamburg became a member of the [North German Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_German_Confederation) (1866–1871) and of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) (1871–1918), and maintained its self-ruling status during the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) (1919–1933). [Hamburg acceded to the German Customs Union or Zollverein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Hamburg_to_the_German_Customs_Union_\(Zollverein\)) in 1888, the last (along with Bremen) of the German states to join. The city experienced its fastest growth during the second half of the 19th century when its population more than quadrupled to 800,000 as the growth of the city's [Atlantic](698.md) trade helped make it Europe's second-largest port.[^cite_note-30] The [Hamburg America Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_America_Line), with [Albert Ballin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ballin) as its director, became the world's largest transatlantic shipping company around the start of the 20th century. Shipping companies sailing to South America, Africa, India and East Asia were based in the city. Hamburg was the departure port for many Germans and Eastern Europeans to emigrate to the United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Trading communities from all over the world established themselves there.

A major [outbreak of cholera in 1892](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1881%E2%80%931896_cholera_pandemic#1892_outbreak_in_Hamburg) was badly handled by the city government, which retained an unusual degree of independence for a German city. About 8,600 died in the largest German epidemic of the late 19th century, and the last major cholera epidemic in a major city of the Western world.

### Second World War



Hamburg [Eilbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilbek) after the [1943 bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II); today around 25% of Hamburg's buildings are from before the Second World War

[^cite_note-31]

Hamburg was a *[Gau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gau_\(territory\))* within the [administrative division of Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_Nazi_Germany) from 1934 until 1945. The [bombing of Hamburg in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II) devastated much of the city and the harbour. On 23 July 1943, the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF) and [United States Army Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Force) firebombing created a [firestorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firestorm) which spread from the *Hauptbahnhof* (main railway station) and quickly moved south-east, completely destroying entire boroughs such as [Hammerbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammerbrook), [Billbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billbrook) and [Hamm South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamm,_Hamburg). Thousands of people perished in these densely populated working class boroughs. The raids, codenamed [Operation Gomorrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gomorrah) by the RAF, killed at least 42,600 civilians; the precise number is not known. About one million civilians were evacuated in the aftermath of the raids. While some of the boroughs destroyed were rebuilt as residential districts after the war, others such as Hammerbrook were entirely developed into office, retail and limited residential or industrial districts.

The [Hamburg Commonwealth War Graves Commission Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Commonwealth_War_Graves_Commission_Cemetery) is in the greater [Ohlsdorf Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohlsdorf_Cemetery) in the north of Hamburg.

At least 42,900 people are thought to have perished[^cite_note-kz-neuengamme-32] in the [Neuengamme concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuengamme_concentration_camp) (about 25 km, 16 mi outside the city in the marshlands), mostly from epidemics and in the [destruction of Kriegsmarine vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Cap_Arcona_\(1927\)) housing evacuees at the end of the war.

Systematic [deportations of Jewish Germans and Gentile Germans of Jewish descent](412990.md#Deportation_during_World_War_II) started on 18 October 1941. These were all directed to [ghettos in Nazi-occupied Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghettos_in_Nazi-occupied_Europe) or to [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps). Most deported persons perished in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust). By the end of 1942, the *Jüdischer Religionsverband in Hamburg* was dissolved as an independent legal entity and its remaining assets and staff were assumed by the [Reich Association of Jews in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Association_of_Jews_in_Germany) (District Northwest). On 10 June 1943, the [Reich Security Main Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Security_Main_Office) dissolved the association by a decree.[^cite_note-33] The few remaining employees not somewhat protected by a [mixed marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-miscegenation_laws#Nazi_Germany) were deported from Hamburg on 23 June to [Theresienstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienstadt), where most of them perished. About 7800 Hamburg Jews were murdered in the Holocaust, of the nearly 17 thousand who had lived in the city before [Hitler's rise to power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler's_rise_to_power).[^cite_note-34]

### Post-war history



Container Terminal at the Port of Hamburg

The city was surrendered to [British Forces](31730.md) on 3 May 1945, in the [Capture of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Hamburg),[^cite_note-35] three days after [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md)'s death. After the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Hamburg formed part of the [British Zone of Occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Zone_of_Occupation); it became a state of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in 1949.

The [North Sea flood of 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_flood_of_1962) caused the Elbe to rise to an all-time high, inundating one-fifth of Hamburg and killing more than 300 people.

The [inner German border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_German_border) – only 50 kilometres (30 mi) east of Hamburg – separated the city from much of its hinterland and reduced Hamburg's global trade. Since [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990, and the accession of several Central European and [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_region) countries into the [European Union](9317.md) in 2004, the [Port of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hamburg) has restarted ambitions for regaining its position as the region's largest deep-sea port for container shipping and its major commercial and trading hub.

## Geography

Hamburg is at a sheltered natural harbour on the southern fanning-out of the [Jutland Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland_Peninsula), between [Continental Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe) to the south and [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) to the north, with the [North Sea](21179.md) to the west and the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) to the northeast. It is on the [River Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) at its confluence with the [Alster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alster) and [Bille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bille_\(Elbe\)). The city centre is around the [Binnenalster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binnenalster) ("Inner Alster") and [Außenalster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Au%C3%9Fenalster) ("Outer Alster"), both formed by damming the Alster river to create lakes. The islands of [Neuwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuwerk), [Scharhörn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scharh%C3%B6rn), and [Nigehörn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeh%C3%B6rn), 100 kilometres (60 mi) away in the [Hamburg Wadden Sea National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Wadden_Sea_National_Park), are also part of the city of Hamburg.[^cite_note-36]

The neighbourhoods of [Neuenfelde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuenfelde), [Cranz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranz,_Hamburg), Francop and [Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finkenwerder) are part of the *[Altes Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altes_Land)* (old land) region, the largest contiguous fruit-producing region in Central Europe. [Neugraben-Fischbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neugraben-Fischbek) has Hamburg's highest elevation, the [Hasselbrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasselbrack) at 116.2 metres (381 ft) [AMSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level).[^cite_note-37] Hamburg borders the [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md) and [Lower Saxony](18435.md).

### Climate

Hamburg has an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*; [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dobk*), influenced by its proximity to the coast and maritime influences that originate over the Atlantic Ocean. The location in the north of Germany provides extremes greater than typical marine climates, but definitely in the category due to the prevailing westerlies.[^cite_note-38] Nearby wetlands enjoy a maritime temperate climate. The amount of snowfall has varied greatly in recent decades. In the late 1970s and early 1980s, heavy snowfall sometimes occurred,[^cite_note-39] the winters of recent years have been less cold, with snowfall just a few days per year.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

The warmest months are June, July, and August, with high temperatures of 20.1 to 22.5 °C (68.2 to 72.5 °F). The coldest are December, January, and February, with low temperatures of −0.3 to 1.0 °C (31.5 to 33.8 °F).[^cite_note-Deutscher_Wetterdienst-42] The annual extreme temperatures range from −29.1 °C (−20.4 °F) on 13 February 1940, to 40.1 °C (104.2 °F) on [20 July 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_European_heatwaves#July_heatwave_2), and the latter was measured at Hamburg-Neuwiedenthal Meteorological Station, on the same day, a high temperature record of 39.1 °C (102.4 °F) was recorded at [Hamburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Airport).[^cite_note-sklima-43]

On 31 December 2016, there were 1,860,759 people registered as living in Hamburg in an area of 755.3 km<sup>2</sup> (291.6 sq mi). The population density was 2,464/km<sup>2</sup> (6,380/sq mi).[^cite_note-Hamburger_Melderegister-48] The metropolitan area of the Hamburg region ([Hamburg Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Metropolitan_Region)) is home to 5,107,429 living on 196/km<sup>2</sup> (510/sq mi).[^cite_note-49]

There were 915,319 women and 945,440 men in Hamburg. For every 1,000 females, there were 1,033 males. In 2015, there were 19,768 births in Hamburg (of which 38.3% were to unmarried women); 6422 marriages and 3190 divorces, and 17,565 deaths. In the city, 16.1% of the population was under the age of 18, while 18.3% was 65 years of age or older. There were 356 people in Hamburg over the age of 100.[^cite_note-Statistikamt_Nord-50]

According to the Statistical Office for Hamburg and [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md), there were 631,246 residents with a migrant background, representing 34% of the population.[^cite_note-statistik-nord.de-51] Immigrants come from 200 countries. 5,891 people acquired German cititzenship in 2016.[^cite_note-statistik-nord.de1-52]

In 2016, there were 1,021,666 households, of which 17.8% had children under the age of 18; 54.4% of all households were made up of singles. 25.6% of all households were single parent households. The average household size was 1.8.[^cite_note-Haushalte_nach_Stadtteilen_in_Hamburg_am_31.12.2016-53]

### Portuguese community

Hamburg has the largest [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) community in Germany; the city is home to about 30,000 people of [Portuguese heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_diaspora). Many Portuguese sailors and merchants came to Hamburg beginning in the 15th century due to its [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hamburg). Since the 1970s, there has been a district in Hamburg called the [Portugiesenviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugiesenviertel?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Portuguese quarter) where many Portuguese people settled and which has a variety of Portuguese restaurants, cafes and shops that attract many tourists. There are several statues, squares and streets in Hamburg that are named after Portuguese historical figures. These include the [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama) statue on the Kornhaus bridge, which was suggested by Portuguese residents to bring visibility to the Portuguese community in Hamburg.[^cite_note-54]

### Afghan community

See also: [Afghans in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghans_in_Germany) and [Afghan diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_diaspora)

Hamburg's [Afghan](2235.md) community of about 50,000 people is the largest not only in Germany, but also in Europe. They first came to Hamburg in the 1970s before expanding during the [Afghan conflict](27576770.md) in the 1980s and 1990s where many Afghan migrants chose to live in Hamburg.[^cite_note-BPB-55] After 2015 the Afghan population almost doubled due to a new influx from the migrant crisis. There is an area in Hamburg behind the central station where many Afghan restaurants and shops are located. Many [carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpet) businesses in [Speicherstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speicherstadt) are operated by Afghan traders,[^cite_note-56] with Hamburg still a global leader in the trade of oriental rugs.[^cite_note-57]

### Language

See also: [Hamburgisch dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburgisch_dialect)

As elsewhere in Germany, [Standard German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German) is spoken in Hamburg, but as typical for northern Germany, the original language of Hamburg is [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German), usually referred to as *Hamborger Platt* (German *Hamburger Platt*) or *[Hamborgsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburgisch)*. Since large-scale [standardisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_language) of the German language beginning in earnest in the 18th century, various Low German-coloured dialects have developed (contact-varieties of German on Low Saxon substrates). Originally, there was a range of such [Missingsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missingsch) varieties, the best-known being the low-prestige ones of the working classes and the somewhat more bourgeois *Hanseatendeutsch* (Hanseatic German), although the term is used in appreciation.[^cite_note-58] All of these are now moribund due to the influences of Standard German used by education and media. However, the former importance of Low German is indicated by several songs, such as the [sea shanty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_shanty) [Hamborger Veermaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamborger_Veermaster), written in the 19th century when Low German was used more frequently. Many toponyms and street names reflect Low Saxon vocabulary, partially even in Low Saxon spelling, which is not standardised, and to some part in forms adapted to Standard German.[^cite_note-59]

According to the publication *Muslimisches Leben in Deutschland* ("*Muslim life in Germany*"), an estimated 141,900 Muslim migrants (from nearly 50 countries of origin) lived in Hamburg in 2008.[^cite_note-61] About three years later (May 2011) calculations based on census data for 21 countries of origin resulted in a figure of about 143,200 Muslim migrants in Hamburg, making up 8.4% percent of the population.[^cite_note-62] As of 2021, there were more than 50 mosques in the city,[^cite_note-63] including the [Ahmadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya) run [Fazle Omar Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazle_Omar_Mosque), which is the oldest in the city,[^cite_note-64] and which hosts the [Islamic Centre Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Centre_Hamburg).

A Jewish Community also exists.[^cite_note-65] As of 2022, around 2,500 Jews live in Hamburg.[^cite_note-66]

## Government

Further information: [Government of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Hamburg) and [List of mayors of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Hamburg)



Hamburg City Hall (front view)

The city of Hamburg is one of 16 [German states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany), therefore the [Mayor of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Hamburg)'s office corresponds more to the role of a [minister-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-president) than to that of a city mayor. As a [German state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany#Politics), it is responsible for public education, correctional institutions and public safety; as a municipality, it is additionally responsible for libraries, recreational facilities, sanitation, water supply and welfare services.

Since 1897, the seat of the government has been [Hamburg City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_City_Hall) (Hamburg Rathaus), with the office of the mayor, the meeting room for the Senate and the floor for the [Hamburg Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Parliament).[^cite_note-rathaus-67] From 2001 until 2010, the mayor of Hamburg was [Ole von Beust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_von_Beust),[^cite_note-usatoday2008-68] who governed in Germany's first statewide "black-green" coalition, consisting of the conservative [CDU Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDU_Hamburg) and the alternative GAL, which are Hamburg's regional wing of the [Alliance 90/The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_'90/The_Greens) party.[^cite_note-69] Von Beust was briefly succeeded by [Christoph Ahlhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Ahlhaus) in 2010, but the coalition broke apart on 28 November 2010.[^cite_note-70] On 7 March 2011 [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) ([SPD](60585.md)) became mayor. After the 2015 election the SPD and the [Alliance 90/The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_'90/The_Greens) formed a coalition.

### Boroughs

Main article: [Boroughs and quarters of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_and_quarters_of_Hamburg)



The 7 boroughs and 104 quarters of Hamburg

Hamburg is made up of seven boroughs (German: *Bezirke*), which are subdivided into 104 quarters (German: *Stadtteile*). There are 181 localities (German: *Ortsteile*). The urban organisation is regulated by the Constitution of Hamburg and several laws.[^cite_note-constitution-10][^cite_note-borough-71] Most of the quarters were former independent cities, towns or villages annexed into Hamburg proper. The last large annexation was done through the [Greater Hamburg Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Hamburg_Act) of 1937, when the cities [Altona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altona,_Hamburg), [Harburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harburg,_Hamburg), and [Wandsbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Wandsbek) were merged into the state of Hamburg.[^cite_note-greateract-72] The *Act of the Constitution and Administration of Hanseatic city of Hamburg* established Hamburg as a state and a municipality.[^cite_note-reichconst-73] Some of the boroughs and quarters have been rearranged several times.

Each borough is governed by a Borough Council (German: *Bezirksversammlung*) and administered by a Municipal Administrator (German: *Bezirksamtsleiter*). The boroughs are not independent municipalities: their power is limited and subordinate to the [Senate of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Hamburg). The borough administrator is elected by the Borough Council and thereafter requires confirmation and appointment by Hamburg's Senate.[^cite_note-borough-71] The quarters have no governing bodies of their own.



The part of the North Sea in this aerial picture is called the [Hamburg Wadden Sea National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Wadden_Sea_National_Park) and belongs administratively to the borough of Hamburg-Mitte. Some 50 people live here on the island Neuwerk (visible just above the centre).

Following the latest territorial reform of March 2008, the boroughs are [Hamburg-Mitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Mitte), [Altona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altona,_Hamburg), [Eimsbüttel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eimsb%C3%BCttel), [Hamburg-Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Nord), [Wandsbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandsbek), [Bergedorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergedorf), and [Harburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harburg,_Hamburg).[^cite_note-actareal-74][^cite_note-75]

### Architecture



Historicist Palmaille, [Altona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altona,_Hamburg). Nearly 25,000 buildings in Hamburg are from those [Gründerzeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCnderzeit) times.



The Marco-Polo-Centre (left) and former Unilever HQ Germany

Further information on a building type for school buildings in Hamburg: [Kreuzbau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreuzbau_\(Hamburg\))

Hamburg has architecturally significant buildings in a wide range of styles and just one skyscraper under construction (see [List of tallest buildings in Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Hamburg)). Churches are important landmarks, such as [St Nicholas'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Nikolai,_Hamburg), which for a short time in the 19th century was the world's tallest building. The skyline features the tall spires of the most important churches (*Hauptkirchen*) [St Michael's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michaelis_Church,_Hamburg) (nicknamed "Michel"), [St Peter's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Church,_Hamburg), [St James's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jacobi,_Hamburg) (*St. Jacobi*), and [St. Catherine's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catherine's_Church,_Hamburg) covered with copper plates, and the [Heinrich-Hertz-Turm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich-Hertz-Turm), the radio and television tower (no longer publicly accessible).



The Chilehaus with a typical brick expressionist façade

The many [streams, rivers, and canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Hamburg) are crossed by [some 2,500 bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bridges_in_Hamburg), more than London, [Amsterdam](844.md), and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) put together.[^cite_note-76] Hamburg has more bridges inside its city limits than any other city in the world.[^cite_note-77] The [Köhlbrandbrücke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6hlbrandbr%C3%BCcke), Freihafen Elbbrücken, [Lombardsbrücke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardsbr%C3%BCcke), and Kennedybrücke dividing Binnenalster from Aussenalster are important roadways.

The [town hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Rathaus) is a richly decorated Neo-Renaissance building finished in 1897. The tower is 112 metres (367 ft) high. Its façade, 111 m (364 ft) long, depicts the emperors of the Holy Roman Empire, since Hamburg was, as a Free Imperial City, only under the sovereignty of the emperor.[^cite_note-78] The [Chilehaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilehaus), a [brick expressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Expressionism) office building built in 1922 and designed by architect [Fritz Höger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_H%C3%B6ger), is shaped like an ocean liner.

Europe's largest urban development since 2008, the [HafenCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HafenCity), will house about 15,000 inhabitants and 45,000 workers.[^cite_note-79] The plan includes designs by [Rem Koolhaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rem_Koolhaas) and [Renzo Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Piano). The [Elbphilharmonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbphilharmonie) *(Elbe Philharmonic Hall)*, opened in January 2017, houses concerts in a sail-shaped building on top of an old warehouse, designed by architects *[Herzog & de Meuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzog_&_de_Meuron)*.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

The many parks are distributed over the whole city to make Hamburg a verdant city. The biggest parks are the *[Stadtpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Stadtpark)*, the [Ohlsdorf Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohlsdorf_Cemetery), and [Planten un Blomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planten_un_Blomen). The *[Stadtpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Stadtpark)*, Hamburg's "Central Park", has a great lawn and a huge water tower, which houses one of Europe's biggest [planetaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetarium). The park and its buildings were designed by [Fritz Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Schumacher_\(architect\)) in the 1910s.

### Parks and gardens

See also: [List of parks and gardens in Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_and_gardens_in_Hamburg)



A water-light concert at *Planten un Blomen* park

The lavish and spacious *[Planten un Blomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planten_un_Blomen)* park (Low German dialect for "plants and flowers") located in the centre of Hamburg is the green heart of the city. Within the park are various thematic gardens, the biggest Japanese garden in Germany, and the *[Alter Botanischer Garten Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter_Botanischer_Garten_Hamburg)*, which is a historic [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) that now consists primarily of [greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse).

The *[Botanischer Garten Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanischer_Garten_Hamburg)* is a modern [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) maintained by the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg). Besides these, there are many more parks of various sizes. In 2014 Hamburg celebrated a birthday of park culture, where many parks were reconstructed and cleaned up. Moreover, every year there are water-light-concerts in the *[Planten un Blomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planten_un_Blomen)* park, from May to early October.

## Culture



[Abel Seyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Seyler), one of the great theatre principals of 18th-century Europe, established Hamburg as a major centre of theatrical innovation in the 1760s.

From the 1760s, the theatre director [Abel Seyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Seyler)—the leader of the [Hamburg National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_National_Theatre) and subsequently the [Seyler Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyler_Theatre_Company)—established Hamburg as one of the leading European centres of theatrical innovation, promoting experimental productions and pioneering a new more realist style of acting, introducing Shakespeare to a German-language audience, and promoting the concept of a national theatre in the tradition of Ludvig Holberg, the [Sturm und Drang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturm_und_Drang) playwrights, and serious [German opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_opera).[^cite_note-82]

Today Hamburg has more than 40 theatres, 60 museums, and 100 music venues and clubs. With 6.6 music venues per 100,000 inhabitants, Hamburg has the second-highest density of music venues of Germany's largest cities, after Munich and ahead of Cologne and Berlin.[^cite_note-fazemag_20210920-83][^cite_note-clubstudy_2021-84] In 2005, more than 18 million people visited concerts, exhibitions, theatres, cinemas, museums, and cultural events, and 8,552 taxable companies (average size: 3.16 employees) were engaged in the culture sector, which includes music, performing arts, and literature.[^cite_note-85] The creative industries represent almost one-fifth of all companies in Hamburg.[^cite_note-creative_industry_report_2016-86] Hamburg has entered the [European Green Capital Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Capital_Award) scheme, and was awarded the title of European Green Capital for 2011.

### Theatres

See also: [List of theatres in Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_theatres_in_Hamburg)



The *Deutsches Schauspielhaus* in the St. Georg quarter

Theatres in the city include the state-owned *[Deutsches Schauspielhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Schauspielhaus)*, the [Thalia Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalia_Theatre_\(Hamburg\)), [Ohnsorg Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohnsorg-Theater), "Schmidts Tivoli", and the *[Kampnagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampnagel)*.[^cite_note-87]

The [English Theatre of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_English_Theatre_of_Hamburg), near the [U3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U3_\(Hamburg_U-Bahn\)) station Mundsburg, was founded in 1976 and is the oldest professional English-language theatre in Germany, with exclusively English-speaking actors in its company.

### Museums

See also: [List of museums in Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Hamburg)

Hamburg has several large museums and galleries showing classical and contemporary art, such as the [Kunsthalle Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthalle_Hamburg) with its contemporary art gallery (*Galerie der Gegenwart*), the *[Museum für Kunst und Gewerbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_f%C3%BCr_Kunst_und_Gewerbe_Hamburg)* (Museum of Art and Design), and the [Deichtorhallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deichtorhallen) (with the House of Photography and Hall of Contemporary Art). The [Internationales Maritimes Museum Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationales_Maritimes_Museum_Hamburg) opened in the HafenCity quarter in 2008. There are various specialised museums in Hamburg, such as the *[Archäologisches Museum Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch%C3%A4ologisches_Museum_Hamburg)* (Hamburg Archaeological Museum) in the [Harburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Harburg) borough, the [Hamburg Museum of Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Museum_of_Work) (*Museum der Arbeit*), and several museums of local history, such as the [Kiekeberg Open Air Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiekeberg_Open_Air_Museum?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*Freilichtmuseum am Kiekeberg*) at [Kiekeberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiekeberg) in the [Harburg Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harburg_Hills), just outside of Hamburg, in [Rosengarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosengarten,_Lower_Saxony). Two [museum ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_ship) near [St. Pauli Piers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Pauli_Piers) (*Landungsbrücken*) bear witness to the freight ship (*[Cap San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_San_Diego)*) and cargo sailing ship era (*[Rickmer Rickmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickmer_Rickmers)*).[^cite_note-88] In 2017 the Hamburg-built iron-hulled sailing ship *[Peking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_\(ship\))* returned to the city and was installed in the [German Port Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Port_Museum) in 2020. The world's largest model railway museum, [Miniatur Wunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniatur_Wunderland), with 15.4 km (9.57 mi) total railway length, is also situated near St. Pauli Piers in a former warehouse.

[BallinStadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BallinStadt), a memorial park and former emigration station, is dedicated to the millions of Europeans who emigrated to North and South America between 1850 and 1939. Visitors descending from those overseas emigrants may search for their ancestors at computer terminals.

### Music



The 110-metre-high (361-foot) Elbphilharmonie concert hall

[Hamburg State Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_State_Opera) is a leading opera company. Its orchestra is the [Philharmoniker Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philharmoniker_Hamburg). The city's other major orchestra is the [NDR Elbphilharmonie Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Elbphilharmonie_Orchestra). The main concert venue is the new concert hall [Elbphilharmonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbphilharmonie). Before it was the [Laeiszhalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laeiszhalle), *Musikhalle Hamburg*. The Laeiszhalle also houses a third orchestra, the [Hamburger Symphoniker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_Symphoniker). [György Ligeti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gy%C3%B6rgy_Ligeti) and [Alfred Schnittke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Schnittke) taught at the [Hochschule für Musik und Theater Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochschule_f%C3%BCr_Musik_und_Theater_Hamburg).[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

Hamburg is the birthplace of [Johannes Brahms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Brahms), who spent his formative early years in the city, and the birthplace and home of waltz composer [Oscar Fetrás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Fetr%C3%A1s), who wrote the "Mondnacht auf der Alster" waltz.

Since the German premiere of *[Cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cats_\(musical\))* in 1986, there have always been musicals running, including *[The Phantom of the Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phantom_of_the_Opera_\(1986_musical\))*, *[The Lion King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_King_\(musical\))*, *[Dirty Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_Dancing#Stage_adaptation)*, and *[Dance of the Vampires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_of_the_Vampires_\(musical\))*. This density, the highest in Germany, is partly due to the major musical production company [Stage Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_Entertainment) being based in the city.

In addition to musicals, opera houses, concert halls, and theatres, the cityscape is characterised by a large music scene. This includes, among other things, over 100 music venues, several annual festivals and over 50 event organisers based in Hamburg.[^cite_note-91] Larger venues include the [Barclaycard Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclaycard_Arena_\(Hamburg\)), the [Bahrenfeld harness racing track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabrennbahn_Bahrenfeld), and [Hamburg City Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Stadtpark).

Hamburg was an important centre of rock music in the early 1960s. [The Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles) lived and played in Hamburg from August 1960 to December 1962. They proved popular and gained local acclaim. Prior to the group's initial recording and widespread fame, Hamburg provided residency and performing venues for the band during the time they performed there. One of the venues they performed at was the [Star-Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star-Club) in St. Pauli.[^cite_note-92]

Pop musicians from Hamburg include [Udo Lindenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udo_Lindenberg), [Deichkind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deichkind), and [Jan Delay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Delay). The singer [Annett Louisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annett_Louisan) lives in the city.[^cite_note-93]

An important meeting place for Hamburg musicians from the 1970s to the mid-1980s was the jazz pub [Onkel Pö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onkel_P%C3%B6), which was originally founded in the Pöseldorf neighbourhood and later moved to Eppendorf. Many musicians who were counted as part of the "[Hamburg scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_scene?action=edit\&redlink=1)" met here. In addition to Udo Lindenberg, these included [Otto Waalkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Waalkes), Hans Scheibner and groups such as [Torfrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torfrock) and [Frumpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frumpy). One of the members of the band Frumpy was the Hamburg-born singer and composer [Inga Rumpf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inga_Rumpf).

Hamburg is the origin of the "[Hamburger Schule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_Schule)", a term used for alternative music bands like [Tocotronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocotronic), [Blumfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumfeld), [Tomte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomte_\(band\)) or Kante. The meeting point of the Hamburg School was long considered to be the [Golden Pudel Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Pudel_Club?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Altona's old town, near the Fischmarkt. Alongside clubs such as the Pal, the Moondoo or the Waagenbau, today the Pudel is a central location of the Hamburg electro scene. Artists of this scene include the DJ duo [Moonbootica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonbootica), Mladen Solomun, and [Helena Hauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Hauff).[^cite_note-94]

Hamburg is also home to many music labels, music distributors and publishers. These include [Warner Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Music_Group), [Kontor Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontor_Records), [PIAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIAS_Recordings), [Edel SE & Co. KGaA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edel_SE_&_Co._KGaA), Believe Digital, and Indigo. The high proportion of independent labels in the city, which include [Audiolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audiolith_Records), Dial Records, [Grand Hotel van Cleef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Hotel_van_Cleef), among others, is striking. Before its closure, the label [L'Âge d'or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Age_D'Or_\(record_label\)) also belonged to these.

In addition, Hamburg has a considerable [alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock) and [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) scene, which gathers around the [Rote Flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rote_Flora), a [squatted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting) former theatre located in the [Sternschanze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternschanze).

The city was a major centre for [heavy metal music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) in the 1980s. [Helloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helloween), [Gamma Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_Ray_\(band\)), [Running Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Wild_\(band\)), and [Grave Digger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grave_Digger_\(band\)) started in Hamburg.[^cite_note-95] The influences of these and other bands from the area helped establish the subgenre of [power metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_metal). The [industrial rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_rock) band [KMFDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMFDM) was also formed in Hamburg, initially as a performance art project.

In the late 1990s, Hamburg was considered one of the strongholds of the German hip-hop scene. Bands like [Beginner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beginner_\(band\)) shaped the city's hip-hop style and made it a serious location for the hip-hop scene through songs like "Hamburg City Blues". In addition to Beginner, German hip-hop acts from Hamburg include [Fünf Sterne Deluxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCnf_Sterne_deluxe), [Samy Deluxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samy_Deluxe), [Fettes Brot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fettes_Brot), and [187 Strassenbande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/187_Strassenbande).[^cite_note-96]

Hamburg has a highly active psychedelic trance community, historically housing record labels such as [Spirit Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Zone_Records).[^cite_note-97]

### Festivals and regular events



Annual *Hafengeburtstag* (Port Anniversary)

Hamburg is noted for several festivals and regular events. Some of them are street festivals, such as the [LGBT pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_pride) [Hamburg Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Pride) festival[^cite_note-98] or the Alster fair (German: *[Alstervergnügen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstervergn%C3%BCgen)*),[^cite_note-99] held at the *[Binnenalster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binnenalster)*. The *[Hamburger DOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_DOM)* is northern Germany's biggest funfair, held three times a year.[^cite_note-100] *[Hafengeburtstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafengeburtstag)* is a [funfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funfair) to honour the birthday of the port of Hamburg with a party and a ship parade.[^cite_note-101] The annual biker's service in [Saint Michael's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michaelis_Church,_Hamburg) attracts tens of thousands of [bikers](19876.md).[^cite_note-102] Christmas markets in December are held at the [Hamburg Rathaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Rathaus) square, among other places.[^cite_note-103] The *long night of museums* (German: *Lange Nacht der Museen*) offers one entrance fee for about 40 museums until midnight.[^cite_note-104] The sixth *Festival of Cultures* was held in September 2008, celebrating multi-cultural life.[^cite_note-105] The [Filmfest Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmfest_Hamburg) — a film festival originating from the 1950s *Film Days* (German: *Film Tage*) — presents a wide range of films.[^cite_note-106] The *[Hamburg Messe und Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Messe_und_Congress)* offers a venue for trade shows, such *hanseboot*, an international boat show, or *Du und deine Welt*, a large consumer products show.[^cite_note-107] Regular sports events—some open to pro and amateur participants—are the cycling competition [EuroEyes Cyclassics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroEyes_Cyclassics), the [Hamburg Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Marathon), the biggest marathon in Germany after Berlin,[^cite_note-108] the tennis tournament [Hamburg Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_German_Open), and equestrian events like the [Deutsches Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Derby).

Hamburg is also known for its music and festival culture. For example, the Reeperbahn alone has between 25 and 30 million visitors every year. In addition, there are over a million visitors to the annual festivals and major music events.[^cite_note-109] Hamburg's festivals include the Elbjazz Festival, which takes place 2 days a year (usually on the Whitsun weekend) in Hamburg's harbour and HafenCity.

For contemporary and experimental music, the "blurred edges" festival usually follows in May at various venues within Hamburg. In mid-August, the [MS Dockville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dockville) music and arts festival has run annually since 2007 in the [Wilhelmsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmsburg,_Hamburg) district.[^cite_note-110] This is followed at the end of September by the [Reeperbahn Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reeperbahn_Festival?action=edit\&redlink=1), which has been running since 2006. As Europe's largest club festival, it offers several hundred program points around the Reeperbahn in Hamburg over four days and is one of the most important meeting places for the music industry worldwide.[^cite_note-111] In November, the Überjazz Festival, which aims to expand the stylistic boundaries of the concept of jazz, starts every year at Kampnagel.[^cite_note-112]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Cuisine of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Hamburg)



Fried [plaice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaice), [Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finkenwerder) style

Original Hamburg dishes include *[Birnen, Bohnen und Speck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birnen,_Bohnen_und_Speck)* (green beans cooked with pears and bacon).[^cite_note-113] *Aalsuppe* ([Hamburgisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburgisch_dialect) *Oolsupp*) is often mistaken to be German for "[eel soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eel_soup)" (*Aal*/*Ool* translated 'eel'), but the name probably comes from the Low Saxon *allns* \[aˑlns], meaning "all", "everything and the kitchen sink", not necessarily eel. Today eel is often included to meet the expectations of unsuspecting diners.[^cite_note-114] There is *Bratkartoffeln* ([pan-fried potato slices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_fries)), *Finkenwerder Scholle* (Low Saxon *Finkwarder Scholl*, pan-fried plaice), *Pannfisch* (pan-fried fish with mustard sauce),[^cite_note-115] *[Rote Grütze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B8dgr%C3%B8d)* (Low Saxon *Rode Grütt*, related to Danish *[rødgrød](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B8dgr%C3%B8d)*, a type of summer pudding made mostly from berries and usually served with cream, like Danish *rødgrød med fløde*),[^cite_note-116] and *[Labskaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labskaus)* (a mixture of corned beef, mashed potatoes, and beetroot, a cousin of the Norwegian *lapskaus* and [Liverpool](18081.md)'s [lobscouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouse_\(food\)), all offshoots off an old-time one-pot meal that used to be the main component of the common sailor's humdrum diet on the high seas).[^cite_note-117]

*Alsterwasser* (in reference to the city's river, the [Alster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alster)) is the local name for a type of [shandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandy), a concoction of equal parts of beer and carbonated lemonade (*Zitronenlimonade*), the lemonade being added to the beer.[^cite_note-118]

A regional dessert pastry called "[Franzbrötchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franzbr%C3%B6tchen)" is similar in preparation to a [croissant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croissant), but includes a cinnamon and sugar filling, often with raisins or brown sugar [streusel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streusel). Ordinary bread rolls tend to be oval-shaped and of the French bread variety. The local name is *Schrippe* (scored lengthways) for the oval kind and, for the round kind, *Rundstück* ("round piece" rather than mainstream German *[Brötchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B6tchen)*, diminutive form of *Brot* "bread"),[^cite_note-119] a relative of Denmark's *rundstykke*. The cuisines of Hamburg and [Denmark](76972.md), especially of [Copenhagen](5166.md), have a lot in common. This also includes a predilection for open-faced sandwiches of all sorts, especially topped with cold-smoked or pickled fish.

The American [hamburger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger) may have developed from Hamburg's *[Frikadeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frikadeller)*: a pan-fried patty (usually larger and thicker than its American counterpart) made from a mixture of ground beef, soaked [stale bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staling), egg, chopped onion, salt, and pepper, usually served with potatoes and vegetables like any other piece of meat, not usually on a bun. The Oxford Dictionary defined a *Hamburger steak* in 1802: a sometimes-smoked and -salted piece of meat, that, according to some sources, came from Hamburg to America.[^cite_note-120] The name and food, "hamburger", has entered all English-speaking countries, and derivative words in non-English speaking countries.

There are restaurants which offer most of these dishes, especially in the [HafenCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HafenCity).

### Alternative culture

Hamburg has long been a centre of alternative music and counter-culture movements. The boroughs of [St. Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Pauli), [Sternschanze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternschanze), and [Altona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altona,_Hamburg) are known for being home to many radical left-wing and anarchist groups, culminating every year during the traditional May Day demonstrations.[^cite_note-121]

During the [2017 G20 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_G20_Hamburg_summit), which took place in Hamburg from 7–8 July that year, protestors clashed violently with the police in the [Sternschanze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternschanze) area and particularly around the Rote Flora. On 7 July, several cars were set on fire and street barricades were erected to prevent the police from entering the area. In response to that, the police made heavy use of water cannons and tear gas in order to scatter the protestors. However, this was met with strong resistance by protestors, resulting in a total of 160 injured police and 75 arrested participants in the protests.[^cite_note-122]

After the summit, however, the Rote Flora issued a statement, in which it condemns the arbitrary acts of violence that were committed by some of the protestors whilst generally defending the right to use violence as a means of self-defence against police oppression. In particular, the spokesperson of the Rote Flora said that the autonomous cultural centre had a traditionally good relationship with its neighbours and local residents, since they were united in their fight against gentrification in that neighbourhood.[^cite_note-123]

## Economy

The gross domestic product (GDP) of Hamburg was €119.0 billion in 2018, accounting for 3.6% of German economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was €59,600 or 197% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 132% of the EU average.[^cite_note-139] The city has a relatively high employment rate, at 88 percent of the working-age population, employed in over 160,000 businesses. The median gross salary in 2021 was €47,544, which is 9.29% higher than the median gross salary in Germany overall.[^cite_note-140]

The unemployment rate stood at 6.1% in October 2018 and was higher than the German average.[^cite_note-141]

### Banking

Hamburg has for centuries been a commercial centre of Northern Europe, and is the most important banking city of [Northern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Germany). The city is the seat of [Germany's oldest bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_banking), the [Berenberg Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berenberg_Bank), [M.M.Warburg & CO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.M.Warburg_&_CO), and [Hamburg Commercial Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Commercial_Bank). ### Port

Main article: [Port of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hamburg)



[Queen Mary 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Queen_Mary_2) at the [Port of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hamburg)

The most significant economic unit is the Port of Hamburg, which ranks third to [Rotterdam](26049.md) and [Antwerp](32149462.md) in Europe and 17th-largest worldwide, with transshipments of 8.9 million [twenty-foot equivalent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_unit) (TEU) of cargo and 138.2 million tons of goods in 2016.[^cite_note-143] International trade is also the reason for the large number of consulates in the city. Although situated 110 kilometres (70 mi) up the Elbe, it is considered a seaport due to its ability to handle large ocean-going vessels.[^cite_note-144]

### Industrial production

Heavy industry of Hamburg includes the making of steel, aluminium, copper and various large shipyards such as [Blohm + Voss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blohm_+_Voss).[^cite_note-145]

Hamburg, along with [Seattle](11388236.md) and [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), is an important location of the civil [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) industry. [Airbus](26220236.md), which operates the [Hamburg-Finkenwerder assembly plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Hamburg-Finkenwerder) in [Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finkenwerder), employs over 13,000 people.[^cite_note-146]

### HafenCity

Main article: [HafenCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HafenCity)



Western HafenCity area and Speicherstadt

The HafenCity is Europe's largest urban development project and is located in the [Hamburg-Mitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Mitte) district. It consists of the area of the Great Grasbrook, the northern part of the former Elbe island [Grasbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasbrook), and the warehouse district on the former Elbe island Kehrwieder and Wandrahm. It is bordered to the north, separated by the customs channel to Hamburg's city centre, west and south by the Elbe, and to the east, bounded by the upper harbour, [Rothenburgsort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothenburgsort). The district is full of rivers and streams and is surrounded by channels, and has a total area of about 2.2 square kilometres (0.85 sq mi).

HafenCity has 155 [hectares](24919513.md) (383 [acres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre)) in the area formerly belonging to the free port north of the Great Grasbrook. Residential units for up to 12,000 people are planned to be built on the site by around the mid-2020s, and jobs for up to 40,000 people, mainly in the office sector, should be created. It is the largest ongoing urban development project in Hamburg.

Construction work started in 2003, and in 2009 the first part of the urban development project was finished with the completion of the Dalmannkai / Sandtorkai neighbourhood – which is the first stage of the HafenCity project. According to the person responsible for the development and commercialisation of HafenCity, *HafenCity Hamburg GmbH*, half of the master plan underlying structural construction is already completed, whereas the other half is either under construction or is in the construction preparation stages.

### Tourism



City logo of Hamburg



[Neuer Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuer_Wall), one of Europe's most luxurious shopping streets

In 2017, more than 6,783,000 visitors with 13,822,000 overnight stays visited the city.[^cite_note-147] The tourism sector employs more than 175,000 people full-time and brings in [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue) of almost €9 billion, making the tourism industry a major economic force in the Hamburg Metropolitan Region. Hamburg has one of the fastest-growing tourism industries in Germany. From 2001 to 2007, the overnight stays in the city increased by 55.2% (Berlin +52.7%, [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) +33%).[^cite_note-148]

The area of [Reeperbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reeperbahn), in the quarter [St. Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Pauli), is Europe's largest red light district and home of strip clubs, brothels, bars, and nightclubs. The singer and actor [Hans Albers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Albers) is strongly associated with St. Pauli, and wrote the neighbourhood's unofficial anthem, "Auf der Reeperbahn Nachts um Halb Eins" ("[On the Reeperbahn at Half Past Midnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Reeperbahn_at_Half_Past_Midnight_\(song\))"), in the 1940s. [The Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles) had stints on the Reeperbahn early in their careers. Others prefer the laid-back neighbourhood *Schanze*, with its street cafés, or a barbecue on one of the beaches along the Elbe. Hamburg's zoo, the [Tierpark Hagenbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierpark_Hagenbeck), was founded in 1907 by [Carl Hagenbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Hagenbeck) as the first zoo with moated, barless enclosures.[^cite_note-149]

In 2016, the average visitor spent two nights in Hamburg.[^cite_note-statistik-nord1-150] The majority of visitors come from Germany. Most foreigners are European, especially from [Denmark](76972.md) (395,681 overnight stays), the United Kingdom (301,000 overnight stays), [Switzerland](26748.md) (340,156 overnight stays), [Austria](26964606.md) (about 252,397 overnight stays), and the [Netherlands](21148.md) (about 182,610 overnight stays).[^cite_note-statistik-nord1-150] The largest group from outside Europe comes from the United States (206,614 overnight stays).[^cite_note-statistik-nord1-150]

The *[Queen Mary 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Queen_Mary_2)* has docked regularly since 2004, and there were six departures planned from 2010 onwards.[^cite_note-151]

### Creative industries



*Der Spiegel* headquarters

Media businesses employ over 70,000 people.[^cite_note-152] The [Norddeutscher Rundfunk](2917610.md), which includes the television station [NDR Fernsehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Fernsehen), is based in Hamburg, including the news program *[Tagesschau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagesschau_\(German_TV_programme\))*, as are the commercial television station *[Hamburg 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_1?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, the Christian television station *[Bibel TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibel_TV)*, and the civil media outlet *[Tide TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide_TV?action=edit\&redlink=1)*. There are regional radio stations such as [Radio Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Hamburg). Some of Germany's largest publishing companies, [Axel Springer AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Springer_SE), [Gruner + Jahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruner_+_Jahr), and [Bauer Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauer_Media_Group), are located in the city. Hamburg has 54 hospitals. The [University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Medical_Center_Hamburg-Eppendorf), with about 1,736 beds, houses a large medical school. There are also smaller private hospitals. On 1 January 2011 there were about 12,507 hospital beds.[^cite_note-156] The city had 5,663 physicians in private practice and 456 pharmacies in 2010.[^cite_note-157]

Hamburg is a major transportation hub, connected to four [Autobahnen](1764276.md) (motorways) and the most important railway junction on the route to Scandinavia.

Bridges and tunnels connect the northern and southern parts of the city, such as the [old Elbe Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe_Tunnel_\(1911\)) (*Alter Elbtunnel*) or St. Pauli Elbtunnel (official name), which opened in 1911, today a major tourist sight, and the [Elbe Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe_Tunnel_\(1975\)) (*Elbtunnel*), the crossing of a [motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_7).[^cite_note-158]

[Hamburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Airport) is the oldest airport in Germany still in operation.[^cite_note-159] There is also the smaller [Hamburg Finkenwerder Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Finkenwerder_Airport), used only as a company airport for [Airbus](26220236.md). Some airlines market [Lübeck Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck_Airport) in [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck) as serving Hamburg.[^cite_note-160]

Hamburg's [licence plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Germany) prefix is "HH" (Hansestadt Hamburg; English: Hanseatic City of Hamburg), used between 1906 and 1945 and from 1956 onwards, rather than the single letter normally used for large cities since the introduction of Germany's current vehicle registration system in 1956, such as B for Berlin or M for Munich. "H" was Hamburg's prefix in the years between 1945 and 1947; it has used by [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover) since 1956.[^cite_note-161]

Hamburg is located in the area of the transport association [Hamburger Verkehrsverbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_Verkehrsverbund) (HVV).

### Public transport



A map of the transit rail lines in Hamburg

&#x20;Public transport by rail, bus, and ship is organised by the *[Hamburger Verkehrsverbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_Verkehrsverbund)* ("Hamburg transit authority") (HVV). Tickets sold by one company are valid on all other HVV companies' services. The HVV was the first organisation of this kind worldwide.[^cite_note-162]

Thirty-three mass transit rail lines across the city are the backbone of public transport.[^cite_note-Zahlen_{{!}}_HVV-Verbundbericht-163] The [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_S-Bahn) (commuter train system) comprises six lines and the [U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_U-Bahn) four lines – *U-Bahn* is short for *Untergrundbahn* (underground railway). Approximately 41 km (25 mi) of 101 km (63 mi) of the U-Bahn is underground; most is on embankments or viaduct or at ground level. Older residents still speak of the system as *Hochbahn* (elevated railway), also because the operating company of the subway is the *[Hamburger Hochbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_Hochbahn)*. The [AKN railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AKN_Eisenbahn) connects satellite towns in Schleswig-Holstein to the city. On some routes regional trains of Germany's major railway company [Deutsche Bahn AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn) and the regional *[metronom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metronom_Eisenbahngesellschaft)* trains may be used with an HVV ticket. Regional trains stop at various stations within city limits such as the four larger stations, [Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Hauptbahnhof), [Dammtor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Dammtor_station), [Altona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Altona_station), and [Harburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Harburg_station), as well as Hamburg Hasselbrook and Hamburg Bergedorf. The [tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Hamburg) was opened in 1866 and shut down in 1978.[^cite_note-tramway-atlas-germany-1996-164]

Gaps in the rail network are filled by more than 669 bus routes, operated by single-deck two-, three- and four-axle diesel buses.[^cite_note-Zahlen_{{!}}_HVV-Verbundbericht-163] Hamburg has no trams or [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus), but has hydrogen-fuelled buses. The buses run frequently during working hours, with buses on the [MetroBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrobus_\(Germany\)) routes running every ten minutes from 6 am to 9 pm. On special weekday night lines the intervals can be 30 minutes or more, while on normal days (Monday-Friday) normal buses stop running at night (some lines run 24 hours a day, every day of the year at least every half hour).[^cite_note-165]

There are eight ferry lines along the [Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe), operated by *[HADAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HADAG_Seetouristik_und_F%C3%A4hrdienst)*, that fall under the aegis of the HVV. While mainly used by citizens and dock workers, they can also be used for sightseeing tours.[^cite_note-166]

There are additionally several cruise terminals each suited to different sizes of ship[^cite_note-167] all of which feature power connections large enough to allow suitably equipped ships to plug in and turn off all their engines.[^cite_note-168]



An [Airbus A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321) on [final assembly line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line) 3 in the [Airbus Hamburg-Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Hamburg-Finkenwerder) plant

The international airport serving Hamburg, [Hamburg Airport Helmut Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association): HAM, [ICAO](14985.md): EDDH; formerly and still informally named Hamburg Fuhlsbüttel Airport), is the fifth biggest and oldest airport in Germany, having been established in 1912 and located about 5 miles (8 kilometres) from the city centre. About 60 airlines provide service to 125 destination airports, including some long-distance destinations like [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey) on [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines), [Dubai](211583.md) on [Emirates](142715.md), and [Tehran](57654.md) on [Iran Air](168635.md). Hamburg was a secondary hub for [Lufthansa](97733.md), which was at one time the largest carrier at the airport, but has subsequently moved all its routes except the ones to its primary hubs in [Frankfurt](10992.md) and [Munich](19058.md) to its subsidiary [Eurowings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurowings). Lufthansa also operates one of its biggest [Lufthansa Technik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Technik) maintenance facilities in Fuhlsbüttel. The second airport is located in [Hamburg-Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Finkenwerder), officially named [Hamburg Finkenwerder Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Finkenwerder_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association): XFW, [ICAO](14985.md): EDHI). It is about 10 km (6 mi) from the city centre and is a nonpublic airport for the [Airbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Operations_GmbH) plant. It is the second biggest Airbus plant, after [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), and the third biggest aviation manufacturing plant after [Seattle](11388236.md) and Toulouse; the plant houses the final assembly lines for [A318, A319, A320, A321](164933.md), and [A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) aircraft.[^cite_note-169]

Public transportation statistics

The average amount of time people spend commuting with public transit in Hamburg, for example to and from work, on a weekday is 58 min. 16% of public transit riders, ride for more than two hours every day. The average amount of time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is 11 min, while 11% of riders wait for over 20 minutes on average every day. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 8.9 km, while 21% travel for over 12 km in a single direction.[^cite_note-170]

### Utilities

Electricity for Hamburg and Northern Germany is largely provided by *[Vattenfall Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vattenfall)*, formerly the state-owned *Hamburgische Electricitäts-Werke*. Vattenfall Europe used to operate the [Brunsbüttel Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunsb%C3%BCttel_Nuclear_Power_Plant) and [Krümmel Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C3%BCmmel_Nuclear_Power_Plant), both taken out of service as part of the [nuclear power phase-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_Germany#Closures_and_phase-out). In addition, [E.ON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.ON) operates the [Brokdorf Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokdorf_Nuclear_Power_Plant), near Hamburg. There are also the coal-fired [Wedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedel_Power_Station), [Tiefstack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiefstack_Power_Station), and Moorburg [CHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Heat_And_Power) Plant, and the fuel-cell power plant in the HafenCity quarter. *VERA Klärschlammverbrennung* uses the [biosolids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosolids) of the Hamburg wastewater treatment plant; the *Pumpspeicherwerk Geesthacht* is a pump storage power plant and a solid waste combustion power station is *Müllverwertung Borsigstraße*.[^cite_note-171]

In June 2019, Hamburg introduced a law governing the phasing out of coal based thermal and electric energy production ("Kohleausstiegsgesetz").[^cite_note-172] This move was the result of negotiations between parliamentary parties and representatives of a campaign called *Tschuess Kohle] ("Goodbye Coal").* In 2020, the city's Ministry for Environment and Energy announced a partnership with [Namibia](21292.md) as a potential supplier of woody biomass, sourced through landscape maintenance in areas affected by [woody plant encroachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_plant_encroachment), to replace coal.[^cite_note-173]

The school system is managed by the Hamburg Authority of Schools, Families and Vocational Training (*Behörde für Schule, Familie und Berufsbildung*). The system had approximately 191,148 students in 221 primary schools and 188 secondary schools in 2016.[^cite_note-189] There are 32 public libraries in Hamburg.[^cite_note-190]

Nineteen universities are located in Hamburg, with about 100,589 university students in total, including 9,000 resident students.[^cite_note-191] Six universities are public, including the largest, the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg) (Universität Hamburg) with the [University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Medical_Center_Hamburg-Eppendorf), the [University of Music and Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochschule_f%C3%BCr_Musik_und_Theater_Hamburg), the [Hamburg University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_University_of_Applied_Sciences), the [HafenCity University Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HafenCity_University_Hamburg), and the [Hamburg University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_University_of_Technology). The city has also smaller private colleges and universities, including many religious and special-purpose institutions, such as the [Helmut Schmidt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Schmidt_University) (formerly the University of the Federal Armed Forces Hamburg).[^cite_note-192] Hamburg is home to one of the oldest international schools in Germany, the International School of Hamburg.
