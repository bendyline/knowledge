**Dhaka District** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): ঢাকা জেলা, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Bengali): *Ḍhākā jēlā*) is a [district](37210460.md) in central [Bangladesh](3454.md),[^cite_note-Banglapedia-5] and is the densest district in the nation. It is a part of the [Dhaka Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Division). [Dhaka](56656.md), the capital of Bangladesh, rests on the eastern banks of the [Buriganga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buriganga_River) which flows from the [Turag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turag_River) to the southern part of the district. The former [Dhaka city corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_City_Corporation) occupied only about a fifth of the area of Dhaka district until 2011 where the municipal corporation was fractionated and rearranged in [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_North_City_Corporation) and [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_South_City_Corporation) corporations due to being the economic, political and cultural centre of the district and also the country. Dhaka District consists of the upazillas of [Keraniganj](9495748.md), [Nawabganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabganj_Upazila,_Dhaka), [Dohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dohar_Upazila), [Savar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savar_Upazila) and [Dhamrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamrai_Upazila), alongside the city of [Dhaka](56656.md). Dhaka District is an administrative entity, and like many other cities, it does not cover the modern [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) which is [Greater Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Dhaka), which has spilled into neighbouring districts, nor does the conurbation cover the whole district, as there are rural areas within the district.

## Geography

Dhaka District shares borders with [Gazipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazipur_District) and [Tangail](1897981.md) to the north, [Munshiganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munshiganj_District) and [Rajbari](2274550.md) to the south, [Narayanganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayanganj_District) to the east and [Manikganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manikganj_District) to the west. The main rivers flowing through this district are [Padma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_river), [Kaliganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaleshwari_River), [Dhaleshwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaleshwari_River), [Ichamati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichamati_River), [Shitalakshya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shitalakshya_River), [Buriganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buriganga_River) and numerous smaller rivers including [Bangshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangshi_River), [Turag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turag_River), [Balu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balu_River), Elamjani, Alam, Bherujkha, Ramkrishnadi, Elisamari, Tulsikhali. Major lakes (*Bengali*: **বিল**) include Belai, Saldaher, Labandaher, Churain, Damsharan and Kiranjir Beel. The annual average temperature of the district is maximum 41.1 °C, minimum 11.5 °C; the average annual rainfall 1931mm.

## History

Main article: [History of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dhaka)



[Lalbagh Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalbagh_Fort) was developed by Shaista Khan.

The administrative Dhaka District was first established in 1772, but the existence of urbanised settlements in the area that is now Dhaka city—dates from the 7th century. The present-day [Savar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savar_Upazila) was the capital of the Sanbagh Kingdom during the seventh and eighth centuries. The city area of Dhaka was ruled by the [Buddhist](3267529.md) kingdom of [Kamarupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa) and the [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire) before passing to the control of the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty) in the 9th century. Many believe that the name of the city was derived after the establishment of the goddess [Dhakeshwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhakeshwari)'s temple by [Ballal Sena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballal_Sena) in the 12th century. Dhaka and its surrounding area were identified as *Bengalla* around that period. The town itself consisted of a few market centres like Lakshmi Bazar, Shankhari Bazar, Tanti Bazar, Patuatuli, Kumartuli, Bania Nagar and Goal Nagar. After the [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty), Dhaka was successively ruled by the [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) and [Afghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people) governors descending from the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) before the arrival of the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) in 1608.

The development of townships and significant growth in population came as the city was proclaimed the capital of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) under [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) rule in 1608. During Mughal rule the areas currently under Dhaka district were famous for their textile products—especially the [Muslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslin). Mughal [subahdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subahdar) [Islam Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Khan_I) was the first administrator of the city. Khan named the town "Jahangir Nagar" (*City of Jahangir*) in honour of the Mughal emperor [Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir), although this name was dropped soon after Jahangir's death. The main expansion of the city took place under the Mughal general [Shaista Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaista_Khan). The city then measured 19 by 13 kilometres (11.8 by 8.1 mi), with a population of nearly a million people. The city passed to the control of the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) in 1757 after the [Battle of Plassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plassey) and eventually to the [Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown), [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), in 1765 at the [Battle of Buxar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buxar). The city's population shrank dramatically during this period as the prominence of [Kolkata](47905.md) rose, but substantive development and modernisation eventually followed. A modern civic water supply system was introduced in 1874 and an electricity supply was launched in 1878. The [Dhaka Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Cantonment) was established near the city, serving as a base for [British Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) military personnel.



[Jatiyo Sangshad Bhaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatiyo_Sangshad_Bhaban) houses the national parliament.

During the abortive [Partition of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Bengal_\(1905\)) in 1905, Dhaka was declared to be the capital of the newly established state of [Eastern Bengal and Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal#First_partition,_1905%E2%80%931912), but Bengal was reunited in 1911. The rural areas under the present Dhaka district, especially [Dohar Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dohar_Upazila) were used for the production of [indigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigo).

Following the partition of Bengal in (1947) appending the partition of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) in 1947, Dhaka became the capital of [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal) as a part of the new [Muslim state](15012.md) of [Pakistan](23235.md), while the western part of Bengal with a majority Hindu population had become a part of the new and independent India, designated as [West Bengal](34040.md) with [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta) as state capital. Calcutta witnessed communal violence that left thousands of people dead. A large proportion of the city's [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Bangladesh) population departed for [India](14533.md), while the city received hundreds of thousands of [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Bangladesh) immigrants from Calcutta, India. The city's population rose dramatically in a very short period of time, which created severe shortages and infrastructural problems.[^cite_note-Dhaka2-6] As the centre of regional politics, Dhaka saw an increasing number of political strikes and incidents of violence. The adoption of [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) as the sole [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of [Pakistan](23235.md) led to protest marches involving large crowds. Known as the [language movement of 1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Language_Movement), the protests resulted in police firing which killed students who were demonstrating peacefully.[^cite_note-CALC-7] Throughout the 1950s and '60s, Dhaka remained a hotbed of political activity, and the demands for autonomy for the Bengali population gradually gained momentum.

The [1970 Bhola cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Bhola_cyclone) devastated much of the region, killing an estimated 500,000 people. More than half the city of Dhaka was flooded and millions of people were marooned. With public anger growing against ethnic discrimination and poor cyclone relief efforts from the central government, Bengali politician [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman) held a nationalist rally on 7 March 1971 at the [Race Course Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhrawardy_Udyan). An estimated one million people attended the gathering, leading to [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman)'s 26 March declaration of Bangladesh's independence.[^cite_note-CALC-7][^cite_note-REF-8] In response, the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) launched [Operation Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Searchlight), which led to the arrests, torture and killing of hundreds of thousands of people, mainly Hindus and [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_people) intellectuals.[^cite_note-SelectiveGenocide-9]

During the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) the [Pakistan](23235.md) army arrested and killed fourteen [Muktijoddhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muktijoddha) from [Dhamrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamrai_Upazila) Bazar. A mass grave created during the war still exists in the western side of Kalampur Bazar.[^cite_note-BPD-Dhamrai-10] The Pak army also burnt down many houses in Konakhola, Basta, Brahmankirtha, Goalkhali and Khagail Kholamora villages of [Keraniganj Upazila](9495748.md).

The fall of Dhaka city to the allied forces led by [Jagjit Singh Arora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagjit_Singh_Aurora) on 16 December marked the surrender of the Pakistani army. The post-independence period has seen a rapid and massive growth of the city population, attracting migrant workers from rural areas across Bangladesh.[^cite_note-Dhaka2-6] A real estate boom followed with [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) and the development of new settlements such as [Gulshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulshan_Thana), [Banani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banani_\(town\)) and [Motijheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motijheel_Thana).[^cite_note-Dhaka2-6]

During the Quota Reform Movement in Dhaka, students from a wide range of schools, colleges, and universities united to demand a fairer government job recruitment system. The protests became highly visible in front of major educational institutions, with students carrying banners, placards, and chanting slogans to draw attention to their cause. Schools such as Maple Leaf International School and Mastermind English Medium School became early centres of peaceful demonstration, where students organised sit-ins and small marches. The movement quickly spread to college campuses across the city, including Dhaka College, Notre Dame College, and others, while Dhaka University emerged as the heart of the protests. Students occupied central areas like the TSC (Teacher-Student Center) and Shahbagh intersections, forming human chains and holding long sit-ins that lasted for days.

On 1 July 2026, [Purbachal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purbachal) was incorporated into Dhaka District for administrative convenience. It previously straddled between [Rupganj Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupganj_Upazila) of [Narayanganj District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayanganj_District) and [Kaliganj Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliganj_Upazila,_Gazipur) of [Gazipur District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazipur_District).[^cite_note-11]

## Subdivisions



Dhaka District upazila geocode map



Subdistrict and City District map of Dhaka

The district does not cover all parts of Greater Dhaka, and Greater Dhaka does not include all parts of the district, which includes rural areas. The district consists of 57 thanas (50 in city corporations, 7 in upazilas), 5 upazilas, 86 unions, 974 mauzas, 1999 villages, 2 City Corporations, 129 City Wards, 855 City Mahallas, 3 paurashavas, 27 wards and 133 mahallas.[^cite_note-dhakaglance-14]

The five upazilas in Dhaka district are:

- [Dhamrai Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamrai_Upazila), suburban and rural

- [Dohar Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dohar_Upazila), suburban and rural

- [Keraniganj Upazila](9495748.md), suburban

- [Nawabganj Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabganj_Upazila,_Dhaka), suburban and rural

- [Savar Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savar_Upazila), suburban

The municipal area of Dhaka City is under the jurisdiction of the [Dhaka North City Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_North_City_Corporation) and [Dhaka South City Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_South_City_Corporation); for administrative purposes, the municipal area is divided into 92 wards.[^cite_note-Banglapedia-5] Tejgaon Development Circle is merged with the two city corporations.

## Demographics

According to the [2022 Census of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bangladeshi_census), Dhaka District had 4,035,241 households and a population of 14,734,701 with an average 3.62 people per household. Among the population, 2,231,856 (15.15%) inhabitants were under 10 years of age. The population density was 10,067 people per km<sup>2</sup>. Dhaka District had a literacy rate (age 7 and over) of 84.88%, compared to the national average of 74.80%, and a sex ratio of 865 females per 1000 males. Approximately 76.26% of the population lived in urban areas. The ethnic population was 27,137.[^cite_note-2022census-2][^cite_note-census2022-15]

In [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bangladesh_census), Islam was the largest religion in the Dhaka district with 94.65% of the population. Hinduism was the second-largest religion with 4.70% of the population. 0.52% of the population were Christians, while Buddhists were only 0.11% of the population. Although all communities are predominantly urban, a greater percentage of Hindus live in rural areas than in other communities. The district of Dhaka has more than 12 000 mosques, 3012 Hindu mandirs, 530 churches, 174 [Buddhist temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple), three pagodas and a synagogue. For a long time, Islam has been the majority religion in Dhaka, with only 1300 mosques, but more mosques were recently built, and the district is now called the City of Mosques.[^cite_note-19]

## Economy



Bashundhara City Shopping Complex

Dhaka is the commercial heart of Bangladesh.[^cite_note-britannica-20] The city has a large middle class population, increasing the market for modern consumer and luxury goods.[^cite_note-Dhaka2-6][^cite_note-CiE-21][^cite_note-CiE2-22] Many skilled workers are employed in the businesses and industries located in the Dhaka metropolitan area. The city has historically attracted many migrant workers.[^cite_note-EDemo1-23] [Hawkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_centre), peddlers, small shops, rickshaws transport, roadside vendors and stalls employ a large segment of the population[^cite_note-EDemo1-23][^cite_note-Rick-24]—rickshaw-drivers alone number as many as 400,000.[^cite_note-CiE2-22] Half the workforce is employed in household and unorganised labour, while about 800,000 work in the textile industry. Even so, unemployment remains high at 23%. According to *CityMayors Statistics*, Dhaka's GDP registered at \$52 billion in 2005 with an annual growth rate of 6.1%.[^cite_note-25] Its estimated GDP in the year 2020 is \$126 billion. The annual per capita income of Dhaka is estimated at \$550, although a large segment of the population lives below the poverty line, with many surviving on less than \$3 a day.[^cite_note-CiE2-22]

The main business districts of the city include [Motijheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motijheel), [Panthapath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthapath) and [Gulshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulshan_\(Dhaka\)). [Bashundhara City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashundhara_City) is a recently developed economic area that houses many high-tech industries and corporations and a shopping mall that is one of the largest in Southeast Asia, frequented daily by more than 25,000 people.[^cite_note-CiE2-22] The [Export Processing Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export_Processing_Zone) in Dhaka was set up to encourage the export of garments, textiles and other goods. The EPZ is home to 80 factories, which employ mostly women.[^cite_note-EPZ-26] The [Dhaka Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Stock_Exchange) is based in the city, as are most of the large companies and banks of Bangladesh, including the [Bangladesh Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Bank), [HSBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSBC), [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank) and the [Grameen Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Bank). Urban developments have sparked a widespread construction boom, causing new high-rise buildings and skyscrapers to change the city landscape.[^cite_note-CiE2-22] Growth has been especially strong in the finance, banking, manufacturing, [telecommunications](33094374.md) and services sectors, while tourism, hotels and restaurants continue as important elements of the Dhaka economy.[^cite_note-CiE2-22][^cite_note-EDemo1-23]

## Places of interest

The historic city of Dhaka is the capital of Bangladesh and lies on the banks of the [Buriganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buriganga) River in the central area of Bangladesh. The old town of Dhaka, south of the city centre, is the site of most of the tourist attractions, including the [Lalbagh Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalbagh_Fort), the Stat Mosque, and the [Ahsan Manzil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahsan_Manzil) Palace Museum.

Dhaka was predominantly a city of the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughals), whose governors and viceroys built several palace, mosques and katrias. One example of this time is the Aurangabad Fort, commonly known as **Lalbagh Fort**. Also in this area of Dhaka is the Ahsan Manzil Plance Museum, the [Bara Katra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bara_Katra), the Cotta Katra and several mosques of note.

The old European quarter lies just north of Dhaka's old town, which houses the presidential place and the National Museum, Dhaka's commercial and diplomatic regions are northeast of this zone. Dhaka Zoo and the Botanical Gardens are a short taxi ride into the suburbs.

Once famed for its [Muslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslin), Dhaka is now renowned for pink [pearls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearls) and a rich tradition of [handicrafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicrafts).&#x20;

The [Botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) is a park at [Mirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur_Thana) in Dhaka. It contains various species of plants. It is also a major tourist spot.

### Lalbagh Fort

Main article: [Lalbagh Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalbagh_Fort)

Built in 1678 by the Viceroy of Bengal, it contains a three-domed mosque, the tomb of Pari Bibi, the reputed daughter of Nawab Shaista Khan, the Audience Hall and the hammam of the Governor. Four Kilometres from the hotel.

### Ahsan Manzil Palace Museum

Main article: [Ahsan Manzil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahsan_Manzil)

A majestic place, it was once home to the [Nawab of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab_of_Dhaka), and houses 23 galleries displaying portraits, furniture and household articles.

### Jatiyo Smriti Soudho

[Jatiyo Smriti Soudho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatiyo_Smriti_Soudho) or National Martyrs' Memorial is a monument in Bangladesh. It is the symbol of the valour and the sacrifice of the martyrs of the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) of 1971, which brought the independence of Bangladesh from Pakistani rule. The monument is located in Savar, about 35 Kilometres north-west of the capital, Dhaka.

### Tara Mosque

Main article: [Star Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Mosque)

The Star Mosque, also known as Tara Masjid or Sitara Masjid, is a historic mosque located in the [Armanitola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armanitola) area of [Old Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dhaka), Bangladesh. It was originally constructed in the first half of the 19th century by Mirza Ghulam Pir (also referred to as Mirza Ahmed Jan), a local nobleman. At the time of its construction, the mosque featured a simple architectural design, devoid of the elaborate decorations seen today. The structure was commonly referred to as the Mosque of Mirza Ghulam Pir, in honour of its founder. In 1926, the mosque underwent significant renovation under the patronage of Ali Jan Bepari, a local businessman. During this renovation, decorative elements—including the prominent blue star motifs—were added to the mosque's interior and exterior surfaces. Following these modifications, the mosque became popularly known as the Star Mosque (Tara or Sitara Masjid). Today, it is recognised for its ornate aesthetic and distinctive star-themed embellishments, which have become emblematic of its architectural identity.[^cite_note-27]

### Satmasjid

Satmasjid Road, located in the heart of Dhaka’s Dhanmondi area, is one of the city’s most important educational corridors, connecting thousands of students, teachers, and families to schools and colleges every day. The road links residential neighbourhoods with major academic institutions, making it a vital route for daily school commutes and academic life. Over the years, Satmasjid Road has developed a strong reputation as a centre of quality education, especially for its vigorous English-medium school culture. Prominent English-medium schools along or near Satmasjid Road include **Maple Leaf International School, Mastermind English Medium School, Scholastica (Dhanmondi Campus), Academia School, Oxford International School, Daffodil International School, Sunshine Grammar School, Greenherald International School, and South Breeze School**. These institutions have attracted students from across the city, reinforcing the road’s identity as a hub of modern education. Historically, Satmasjid Road evolved alongside Dhanmondi’s transformation from a planned residential area into a major urban and educational centre, named after the historic Sat Gambuj Mosque (Sat Masjid) nearby. Today, the road not only serves as a physical connector but also as a symbol of Dhaka’s growing emphasis on education, diversity, and academic excellence.[^cite_note-28]

### National Museum

Main article: [Bangladesh National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_National_Museum)

The **Bangladesh National Museum**, located in Shahbag, Dhaka, is the country’s most important institution for preserving and showcasing its history, culture, and heritage. Established in 1913 as the Dhaka Museum and later renamed the National Museum, it plays a vital role in educating the public about Bangladesh’s rich past. The museum houses extensive collections covering archaeology, history, art, ethnography, natural history, and the Liberation War of 1971. Through its galleries, visitors can explore ancient artifacts, sculptures, manuscripts, traditional crafts, and exhibits that reflect the diverse cultural traditions of the people of Bangladesh. As a major cultural landmark in Dhaka, the National Museum serves not only as a centre of learning for students and researchers but also as a symbol of national identity and historical awareness.

**Dhakeshwari Temple**

Main article: [Dhakeshwari Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhakeshwari_Temple)

**Dhakeshwari National Temple** is a [Hindu temple](2247213.md) in [Old Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dhaka)..It is state-owned, giving it the distinction of being Bangladesh's 'National Temple'. The name "Dhakeshwari" means "Goddess of Dhaka". Dhaka is named after Goddess Dhakeshwari. It is the 'National Temple' of Bangladesh. The temple was built in the 12th century, 1100 A.D. by [Ballal Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballal_Sen), a king of the [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty), and it is said that the city [Dhaka](56656.md) was named after the Goddess.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]It is one of the [Shakta pithas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakta_pithas), where the [jewel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone) from the crown of the Goddess [Sati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sati_\(goddess\)) had fallen.Within the premises of the Dhakeswari there are temples of two types of architecture. The ancient one is of the Pancharatna Goddess Durga's which lost its actual look after the renovation work. Bradly Bird wrote this at the beginning of this century. Other than this there are four [Shiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) Temples.Dhakeshwari Temple in the 19th century is described by Ridoynath Majumder. He wrote that it was overgrown by jungle and in its north the Urdu road went westward towards [Pilkhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilkhana,_Bangladesh) and at its south west was [Mirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur_Thana) road. At its south there was a wood and Urdu bazar was at the east.In 1996, Dhakeshwari Temple was renamed Dhakeshwari Jatiya Mandir (National Temple) reflecting its position as the centre of [Hindu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_culture) and worship in Bangladesh.

**Ramna Kali Temple**

Main article: [Ramna Kali Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramna_Kali_Temple)

**Ramna Kali Temple** is a Hindu temple in [Dhaka](56656.md) beside the former Dhaka Racecourse, now [Suhrawardy Udyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhrawardy_Udyan).It was built in [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) period (early 17th century) by a mahant named Haricharan Giri.

**Armenian Church**

Main article: [Armenian Church, Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Church,_Dhaka)

The **Armenian Church**, also known as **Armenian Apostolic Church of the Holy Resurrection**, is a historically significant architectural monument situated in the [Armanitola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armanitola) area of [old Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dhaka), [Bangladesh](3454.md). The [church](6325.md) bears testimony to the existence of a significant [Armenian community in the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_community_of_Dhaka) in the 17th and 18th centuries.

### Bara Katra

See also: [Bara Katra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bara_Katra)

Bara Katra was built between [1644](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1644) and [1646](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1646) CE to be the official residence of Mughal prince [Shah Shuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Shuja_\(Mughal\)), the second son of the emperor [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan). The prince endowed it to his *[diwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwan_\(title\))* and the builder of the serai, Abul Qasim.
