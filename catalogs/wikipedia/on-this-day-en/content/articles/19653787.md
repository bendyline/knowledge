The **Caspian Sea** is the world's largest inland [body of water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_of_water), also described as the [world's largest lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_by_area) and usually referred to as a full-fledged [sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Economist_Caspian_sea_or_lake-3][^cite_note-4] An [endorheic basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endorheic_basin), it is situated in both [Europe](9239.md) and [Asia](689.md): east of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus), west of the broad [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) of [Central Asia](6742.md), south of the fertile plains of [Southern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia) in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), and north of the mountainous [Iranian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Plateau). It covers a surface area of 371,000 km<sup>2</sup> (143,244 sq mi) (excluding the highly saline lagoon of [Garabogazköl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garabogazk%C3%B6l) to its east), an area similar to [Japan](15573.md), with a volume of 78,200 km<sup>3</sup> (19,000 cu mi).[^cite_note-5] It has a [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity) of approximately 1.2% (12 g/L), about a third of the salinity of average [seawater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater). It is bounded by [Kazakhstan](16642.md) to the northeast, [Russia](25391.md) to the northwest, [Azerbaijan](746.md) to the southwest, [Iran](14653.md) to the south, and [Turkmenistan](198149.md) to the southeast. The name of the Caspian Sea is derived from the ancient [Iranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples) [Caspi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspians) people.

The lake stretches 1,200 km (750 mi) from north to south, with an average width of 320 km (200 mi). Its gross coverage is 386,400 km<sup>2</sup> (149,200 sq mi) and the surface is about 27 m (89 ft) below [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). Its main freshwater [inflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflow_\(hydrology\)), Europe's longest river, the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), enters at the shallow north end. Two deep basins form its central and southern zones. These lead to horizontal differences in temperature, salinity, and ecology. The [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed) in the south reaches 1,023 m (3,356 ft) below sea level, which is the third-lowest natural non-oceanic [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(geology\)) on Earth after [Baikal](56865.md) and [Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika) lakes.

The Caspian Sea is five times as big as [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior) and is home to a wide range of animal species. It is famous for its [caviar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caviar) and oil industries. Pollution from the oil industry and dams on rivers that drain into it have harmed its ecology. It is predicted that during the 21st century, the depth of the sea will decrease by 9–18 m (30–60 ft) due to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) and the process of [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification), leading to an [ecocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocide).[^cite_note-Dumont1995-6][^cite_note-TheConversation20201223-7][^cite_note-PrangeWilkeWesselingh2020-8]

## Etymology

The sea's name stems from [Caspi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspians), the ancient people who lived to the southwest of the sea in [Transcaucasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasia).[^cite_note-9] [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) (died circa AD 24) wrote that "to the country of the Albanians[^cite_note-10] belongs also the territory called Caspiane, which was named after the Caspian tribe, as was also the sea; but the tribe has now disappeared".[^cite_note-11] Moreover, the [Caspian Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Gates), part of [Iran](14653.md)'s [Tehran province](202698.md), may evince such people migrated to the south. The Iranian city of [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin) shares the root of its name with this common name for the sea. The traditional and medieval Arabic name for the sea was *Baḥr al-Khazar* ("[sea of the Khazars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_the_Khazars)"), but in recent centuries the common and standard name in the Arabic language has become بحر قزوين *Baḥr [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin)*, the Arabized form of Caspian.[^cite_note-12] In modern [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Каспи́йское мо́ре, *Kaspiyskoye more*.[^cite_note-LPIran-13]

Some Turkic ethnic groups refer to it with the Caspi(an) descriptor; in [Kazakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_Language) it is called Каспий теңізі, *Kaspiy teñizi*, [Kyrgyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_language): Каспий деңизи, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Kyrgyz): *Kaspiy deñizi*, [Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_language): *Kaspiy dengizi*. Others refer to it as the *[Khazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars) sea*: [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_language): *Hazar deňzi*; [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language): *Xəzər dənizi*, [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Hazar Denizi*. In all these the first word refers to the historical [Khazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars) [Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate#Turkic_khanates), a large empire based to the north of the Caspian Sea between the 7th–10th centuries.[^cite_note-14]

In Iran, the lake is called the *Mazandaran Sea* ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): دریای مازندران), after the historic [Mazandaran Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran_Province) at its southern shores.[^cite_note-15]

Old Russian sources use the *Khvalyn* or *Khvalis* Sea (Хвалынское море / Хвалисское море) after the name of [Khwarezmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarezmia).[^cite_note-16]

Among [Greeks](42056.md) and [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) in [classical antiquity](252905.md) it was the [Hyrcanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyrcania) Sea (Hyrcanium Mare).[^cite_note-17]

Renaissance European maps labelled it as the *Abbacuch Sea* (Oronce Fine's 1531 world map), *Mar de [Bachu](4566.md)* (Ortellius' 1570 map), or *Mar de Sala* (the [Mercator 1569 world map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercator_1569_world_map)).

It was also sometimes called the Kumyk Sea[^cite_note-18] and Tarki Sea[^cite_note-19] (derived from the name of the [Kumyks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumyks) and their historical capital [Tarki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarki)).

## Physical characteristics

### Formation



Separation of the [Paratethys Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratethys) from open seas formed a megalake, the basis of the Caspian Sea and other bodies of water in vicinity, resulting in confinements of oceanic faunas such as [cetaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacean) and [pinnipeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped).

The Caspian Sea is at its *South Caspian Basin*, like the [Black Sea](3386.md), a remnant of the ancient [Paratethys Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratethys). Its seafloor is, therefore, a standard oceanic [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) and not a continental [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) body.[^cite_note-Kaz’min_Verzhbitskii_2011_pp._131–140-20] It is estimated to be about 30 million years old,[^cite_note-21] and became landlocked in the [Late Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Miocene), about 5.5 million years ago, due to [tectonic uplift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_uplift) and a fall in [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). The Caspian Sea was a comparatively small endorheic lake during the [Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliocene), but its surface area increased fivefold around the time of the Pliocene-Pleistocene transition.[^cite_note-VanBaak2019-22] During warm and dry climatic periods, the landlocked sea almost dried up, depositing [evaporitic sediments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporite) like [halite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halite) that were covered by wind-blown deposits and were sealed off as an [evaporite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporite) [sink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sink_\(geography\)) when cool, wet climates refilled the basin. (Comparable evaporite beds underlie the [Mediterranean](19006.md).) Due to the current inflow of fresh water in the north, the Caspian Sea water is almost fresh in its northern portions, getting more [brackish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackish) toward the south. It is most saline on the Iranian shore, where the catchment basin contributes little flow.[^cite_note-23] Currently, the mean salinity of the Caspian is one third that of Earth's oceans. The [Garabogazköl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garabogazk%C3%B6l) lagoon, which dried up when water flow from the main body of the Caspian was blocked in the 1980s but has since been restored, routinely exceeds oceanic salinity by a factor of 10.[^cite_note-web1-24]

### Geography



Area around the Caspian Sea. Yellow area indicates the (approximate) drainage area.

The Caspian Sea is the largest inland body of water in the world by area and accounts for 40–44% of the total lake waters of the world,[^cite_note-irngaz-25] and covers an area larger than [Germany](11867.md). The coastlines of the Caspian are shared by [Azerbaijan](746.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Russia](25391.md), and [Turkmenistan](198149.md). The Caspian is divided into three distinct physical regions: the Northern, Middle, and Southern Caspian.[^cite_note-hooshang1-26] The Northern–Middle boundary is the [Mangyshlak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangyshlak_Peninsula) Threshold, which runs through [Chechen Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen_Island) and Cape Tiub-Karagan. The Middle–Southern boundary is the [Apsheron Threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsheron_Threshold), a [sill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_sill) of tectonic origin between the Eurasian continent and an oceanic remnant,[^cite_note-27] that runs through [Chilov Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilov) and Cape Kuuli.[^cite_note-dumont1-28] The Garabogazköl Bay is the saline eastern inlet of the Caspian, which is part of Turkmenistan and at times has been a lake in its own right due to the isthmus that cuts it off from the Caspian.

Differences between the three regions are dramatic. The Northern Caspian only includes the Caspian shelf,[^cite_note-kost1-29] and is very shallow; it accounts for less than 1% of the total water volume with an average depth of only 5–6 m (16–20 ft). The sea noticeably drops off towards the Middle Caspian, where the average depth is 190 m (620 ft).[^cite_note-dumont1-28] The Southern Caspian is the deepest, with oceanic depths of over 1,000 m (3,300 ft), greatly exceeding the depth of other regional seas, such as the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). The Middle and Southern Caspian account for 33% and 66% of the total water volume, respectively.[^cite_note-hooshang1-26] The northern portion of the Caspian Sea typically freezes in the winter, and in the coldest winters ice forms in the south as well.[^cite_note-30]

Over 130 rivers provide inflow to the Caspian, the [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River) being the largest. A second affluent, the [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River), flows in from the north, and the [Kura River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_\(Caspian_Sea\)) from the west. In the past, the [Amu Darya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amu_Darya) (Oxus) of Central Asia in the east often changed course to empty into the Caspian through a now-desiccated riverbed called the [Uzboy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzboy_River), as did the [Syr Darya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syr_Darya) farther north. The Caspian has several small islands, primarily located in the north with a collective land area of roughly 2,000 km<sup>2</sup> (770 mi<sup>2</sup>). Adjacent to the North Caspian is the [Caspian Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Depression), a low-lying region 27 m (89 ft) below [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). The [Central Asian](6742.md) [steppes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) stretch across the northeast coast, while the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Caucasus) hug the western shore. The [biomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomes) to both the north and east are characterized by cold, continental deserts. Conversely, the climate to the southwest and south are generally warm with uneven elevation due to a mix of highlands and [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range); the drastic changes in climate alongside the Caspian have led to a great deal of [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) in the region.[^cite_note-web1-24]

The Caspian Sea has numerous islands near the coasts, but none in the deeper parts of the sea. [Ogurja Ada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogurja_Ada) is the largest island. The island is 37 km (23 mi) long, with [Goitered gazelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goitered_gazelle) roaming freely on it. In the North Caspian, the majority of the islands are small and uninhabited, like the [Tyuleniy Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyuleniy_Archipelago_\(Kazakhstan\)), an [Important Bird Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_Area) (IBA).

### Climate

The climate of the Caspian Sea is variable, with the [cold desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_desert_climate) (BWk), [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) (BSk), and [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Dsa, Dfa) being present in the northern portions of the Caspian Sea, while the [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (Csa) and [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Cfa) are present in the southern portions of the Caspian Sea.

### Hydrology



Caspian Sea near [Aktau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktau), Mangystau Region, [Kazakhstan](16642.md)

The Caspian has characteristics common to both seas and lakes. It is often listed as the world's largest lake, although it is not [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater): the 1.2% salinity classes it with [brackish water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackish_water) bodies.

It contains about 3.5 times as much water, by volume, as all five of North America's [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) combined. The [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River) (about 80% of the inflow) and the [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River) discharge into the Caspian Sea, but it has no natural outflow other than by [evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation). Thus the Caspian [ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem) is a [closed basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_basin), with its own sea level history that is independent of the [eustatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eustasis) level of the world's oceans.

The sea level of the Caspian has fallen and risen, often rapidly, many times over the centuries. Some Russian historians, such as [Lev Gumilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Gumilev), claim that the rising of the Caspian in the 10th century caused the coastal towns of [Khazaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars) to flood, resulting in the Khazars losing approximately two-thirds of their territory.[^cite_note-31]

Over the centuries, Caspian Sea levels have changed in synchrony with the estimated discharge of the Volga, which in turn depends on rainfall levels in its vast catchment basin. Precipitation is related to variations in the amount of North Atlantic depressions that reach the interior, and they in turn are affected by cycles of the [North Atlantic oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_oscillation). Thus levels in the Caspian Sea relate to atmospheric conditions in the North Atlantic, thousands of kilometres to the northwest.[^cite_note-32]

The last short-term sea-level cycle started with a sea-level fall of 3 m (10 ft) from 1929 to 1977, followed by a rise of 3 m (10 ft) from 1977 until 1995. Since then smaller oscillations have taken place.[^cite_note-33]

The current projection is that climate change will cause water levels to decline by up to 21 m by 2100.[^cite_note-\:0-34]

### Environmental degradation

The [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River), the longest river in Europe, drains 20% of the European land area and is the source of 80% of the Caspian's inflow. Heavy development in its lower reaches has caused numerous unregulated releases of chemical and biological pollutants. The [UN Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Environment_Programme) warns that the Caspian "suffers from an enormous burden of pollution from oil extraction and refining, offshore oil fields, radioactive wastes from nuclear power plants and huge volumes of untreated sewage and industrial waste introduced mainly by the Volga River".[^cite_note-Nation-20190418-35]

The magnitude of [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) extraction and transport activity in the Caspian also poses a risk to the environment. The island of [Vulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulf) off [Baku](4566.md), for example, has suffered ecological damage as a result of the petrochemical industry; this has significantly decreased the number of species of marine birds in the area. Existing and planned oil and gas pipelines under the sea further increase the potential threat to the environment.[^cite_note-36]

The high concentration of [mud volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_volcano) under the Caspian Sea was thought to be the cause of a fire that broke out 75 kilometers from [Baku](4566.md) on July 5, 2021. The State oil company of Azerbaijan [SOCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCAR) said preliminary information indicated it was a mud volcano which spewed both mud and flammable gas.[^cite_note-37]



Area reductions arising from sea level decline scenarios for ecologically important areas in the Caspian Sea

It is calculated that during the 21st century, the water level of the Caspian Sea will decrease by 9–18 m (30–60 ft) due to the acceleration of evaporation due to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) and the process of [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification), causing an [ecocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocide).[^cite_note-Dumont1995-6][^cite_note-TheConversation20201223-7][^cite_note-PrangeWilkeWesselingh2020-8][^cite_note-38]

Under medium to high [emission scenarios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_scenario), a decline towards the high end, of 18 m (60 ft), could mean an area of up to 143,000 km<sup>2</sup> (55,000 sq mi) converting to land, a 37% reduction to current water area.[^cite_note-\:0-34] Under such a scenario all ecologically significant areas of the Caspian Sea would experience a large reduction in sea level.[^cite_note-\:0-34]

&#x20;On October 23, 2021, Kazakhstan President [Kassym-Jomart Tokayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassym-Jomart_Tokayev) signed the Protocol for the Protection of the Caspian Sea against Pollution from Land-based Sources in order to ensure better protection for the biodiversity of the Caspian Sea.[^cite_note-39]

In July 2025, the Caspian Sea reached its lowest recorded level, falling lower than 29 m (95 ft) below sea level.[^cite_note-40]

## Flora and fauna

### Flora



Iran's northern [Caspian Hyrcanian mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Hyrcanian_mixed_forests) are maintained by moisture captured from the Caspian Sea by the [Alborz Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz).

The rising level of the Caspian Sea between 1995 and 1996 reduced the number of habitats for rare species of aquatic vegetation. This has been attributed to a general lack of seeding material in newly formed coastal [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon) and water bodies.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Many rare and endemic plant species of Russia are associated with the [tidal areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertidal_zone) of the [Volga Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Delta) and [riparian forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riparian_forest) of the [Samur River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samur_River) delta. The shoreline is also a unique refuge for plants adapted to the loose sands of the [Central Asian Deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asian_northern_desert). The principal limiting factors to successful establishment of plant species are hydrological imbalances within the surrounding deltas, [water pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution), and various land reclamation activities. The water level change within the Caspian Sea is an indirect reason for which plants may not get established.

These affect aquatic plants of the Volga Delta, such as *[Aldrovanda vesiculosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldrovanda_vesiculosa)* and the native *[Nelumbo caspica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelumbo)*. Over a thousand plant species are found in the Samur River delta, including the unique temperate [broadleaf forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad-leaved_tree) and tropical [liana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liana) forests that date back to the [Tertiary period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_period).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] In 2018, the [Samursky National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samursky_National_Park) was created, protecting much of the Samur River delta.

Since 2019 [UNESCO](21786641.md) has admitted the lush [Hyrcanian forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyrcanian_forests) of [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran), Iran as World Heritage Site under category (ix).[^cite_note-45]

### Fauna



Most [tadpole gobies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadpole_goby) (*Benthophilus*) are found only in the Caspian Sea basin.

[^cite_note-Naseka2009-46]

The [Caspian turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_turtle) (*Mauremys caspica*), although found in neighboring areas, is a wholly freshwater species. The [zebra mussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebra_mussel) is native to the Caspian and [Black Sea](3386.md) basins, but has become an [invasive species](67554.md) elsewhere, when introduced. The area has given its name to several species, including the [Caspian gull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_gull) and the [Caspian tern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_tern). The [Caspian seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_seal) (*Pusa caspica*) is the only aquatic mammal [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(ecology\)) to the Caspian Sea, being one of very few [seal species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped) that live in inland waters, but it is different from [those inhabiting freshwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_seal) due to the hydrological environment of the sea. A century ago the Caspian was home to more than one million [seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_seal). Today, fewer than 10% remain.[^cite_note-Nation-20190418-35]

Archeological studies of [Gobustan Rock Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobustan_Rock_Art) have identified what may be oceanic species including [cetaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetaceans) from [baleen whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baleen_whale) to [dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin),[^cite_note-Gallagher2-47][^cite_note-48] and [auks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auk) most likely [Brunnich's Guillemot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thick-billed_murre),[^cite_note-Gallagher2-47][^cite_note-GH-49] although the rock art on [Kichikdash Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kichikdash_Mountain?action=edit\&redlink=1) which is assumed to depict either a [beaked whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaked_whale) or a [dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin),[^cite_note-Gallagher2-47][^cite_note-GH-49] it may represent the famous [beluga sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beluga_\(sturgeon\)) instead due to its size (430 cm in length). These [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) may suggest potential presences of oceanic faunas in the Caspian Sea presumably until the [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary) or even the [last glacial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_glacial_period) or [antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history) due to historic marine inflow between the current Caspian Sea and either the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean) or [North Sea](21179.md), or the [Black Sea](3386.md). This is supported by the existences of current endemic, oceanic species such as [lagoon cockles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerastoderma_glaucum) which was genetically identified to originate in the Caspian and Black Seas regions.[^cite_note-Gallagher2-47][^cite_note-50]

The sea's [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) (including associated waters such as rivers) has 160 native species and [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies) of fish in more than 60 [genera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genera).[^cite_note-Naseka2009-46] About 62% of the species and subspecies are [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism), as are 4–6 genera (depending on [taxonomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_\(biology\)) treatment). The lake proper has 115 natives, including 73 endemics (63.5%).[^cite_note-Naseka2009-46] Among the more than 50 genera in the lake proper, 3–4 are endemic: *[Anatirostrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatirostrum)*, *[Caspiomyzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspiomyzon)*, *[Chasar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponticola_bathybius)* (often included in *[Ponticola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponticola)*) and *[Hyrcanogobius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyrcanogobius)*.[^cite_note-Naseka2009-46] By far the most numerous families in the lake proper are [gobies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobies) (35 species and subspecies), [cyprinids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprinid) (32) and [clupeids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clupeid) (22). ## History

### Geology

A map of the Caspian Sea in the mid-1700s

A New and Accurate Map of the Caspian Sea by the Soskam Sabbus & [Emanuel Bowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Bowen), 1747



Caspian Sea (Bahr ul-Khazar), 10th century map by Ibn Hawqal

The main geologic history locally had two stages. The first is the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene), determined by [tectonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics) events that correlate with the closing of the [Tethys Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tethys_Ocean). The second is the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) noted for its [glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_period) cycles and the full run of the present Volga. During the first stage, the Tethys Sea had evolved into the [Sarmatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatians) Lake, that was created from the modern Black Sea and south Caspian, when the collision of the [Arabian peninsula](47858.md) with [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia) pushed up the [Kopet Dag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopet_Dag) and [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains), making lasting south and west limits to the basin. This [orogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orogeny) movement was continuous, while the Caspian was regularly disconnected from the [Black Sea](3386.md). In the late [Pontian stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontian_stage?action=edit\&redlink=1), a mountain arch rose across the south basin and divided it into the [Khachmaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khachmaz_District) and [Lankaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lankaran) Lakes (or early [Balaxani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaxan%C4%B1)). The period of restriction to the south basin was reversed during the Akchagylian—the lake became more than three times its size today and took again the first of a series of contacts with the Black Sea and [Aral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Sea). A recession of Lake Akchagyl completed stage one.[^cite_note-55]

### Early settlement nearby



The 17th-century [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) rebel and pirate [Stenka Razin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenka_Razin), on a raid in the Caspian ([Vasily Surikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Surikov), 1906)

The earliest hominid remains found around the Caspian Sea are from [Dmanisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmanisi) dating back to around 1.8 million years ago and yielded a number of skeletal remains of *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus)* or *[Homo ergaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_ergaster)*. More later evidence for human occupation of the region came from a number of caves in [Georgia](48768.md) and Azerbaijan such as Kudaro and [Azykh Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azykh_Cave). There is evidence for [Lower Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Palaeolithic) human occupation south of the Caspian from western Alburz. These are Ganj Par and [Darband Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darband_Cave) sites.

[Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) remains also have been discovered at a cave in Georgia. Discoveries in the Hotu cave and the adjacent [Kamarband cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotu_and_Kamarband_Caves), near the town of [Behshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behshahr), [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran) south of the Caspian in Iran, suggest human habitation of the area as early as 11,000 years ago.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Ancient Greeks focused on the civilization on the south shore—they call it the (H)yr(c/k)anian Sea ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Υρκανία θάλαττα,[^cite_note-58] with sources noting the latter word was evolving then to today's *Thelessa*: late [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): θάλασσα).[^cite_note-59]

[Hafiz-i Abru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz-i_Abru), a fourteenth century [Timurid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire) geographer, recorded that the destruction of the Oxus river dam and irrigation works diverted the river flow towards the Caspian Sea, which caused the Aral sea to nearly disappear.[^cite_note-Water_on_Sand_Environmental_Histories_of_the_Middle_East_and_North_Africa;_Alan_Mikhail-60][^cite_note-Silk_Road-61]

### Fossil fuel

The area is rich in [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels). Oil wells were being dug in the region as early as the 10th century to reach oil "for use in everyday life, both for medicinal purposes and for heating and lighting in homes".[^cite_note-SOCAR1-62] By the 16th century, Europeans were aware of the rich oil and gas deposits locally. English traders [Thomas Bannister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bannister) and Jeffrey Duckett described the area around [Baku](4566.md) as "a strange thing to behold, for there issueth out of the ground a marvelous quantity of oil, which serveth all the country to burn in their houses. This oil is black and is called [nefte](184342.md#Etymology). There is also by the town of Baku, another kind of oil which is white and very precious \[i.e., [petroleum](23195.md)]."[^cite_note-SOCAR2-63]

Today, oil and gas platforms abound along the edges of the sea.[^cite_note-64]

### Geography, geology and navigation studies

During the rule of [Peter I the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_the_Great), [Fyodor Soimonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Soimonov) was a pioneering explorer of the sea. He was a hydrographer who charted and greatly expanded knowledge of the sea. He drew a set of four maps and wrote *Pilot of the Caspian Sea*, the first lengthy report and modern maps. These were published in 1720 by the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^cite_note-65]

## Economy



Oil pipelines in the Caspian region. September 2002

Countries in the Caspian region, particularly [Azerbaijan](746.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md) and [Turkmenistan](198149.md), have high-value natural-resource-based economies, where the oil and gas compose more than 10 percent of their [GDP](12594.md) and 40 percent of their exports.[^cite_note-Kalyuzhnova_2008-66] All the Caspian region economies are highly dependent on this type of [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) wealth. The [world energy markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_market) were influenced by Azerbaijan and Kazakhstan, as they became strategically crucial in this sphere, thus attracting the largest share of [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) (FDI).

All of the countries are rich in solar energy and harnessing potential, with the highest rainfall much less than the mountains of central Europe in the mountains of the west, which are also rich in [hydroelectricity](381399.md) sources.

[Iran](14653.md) has high fossil fuel energy potential. It has reserves of 137.5 billion [barrels](594043.md) of [crude oil](23195.md), the fourth largest in the world, producing around four million barrels a day. Iran has an estimated 988.4 trillion cubic feet of [natural gas](22131.md), around 16 percent of world reserves, thus key to current paradigms in global energy security.[^cite_note-Kalyuzhnova_2008-66]

[Russia's economy](25705.md) ranks as the [twelfth largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) by [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP) and [sixth largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) by [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) in 2015.[^cite_note-67] Russia's extensive mineral and energy resources are the largest such reserves in the world,[^cite_note-68] making it the second leading [producer of oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_producer) and [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_producer) globally.[^cite_note-69]

Caspian [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral) states join efforts to develop infrastructure, tourism and trade in the region. The first [Caspian Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Economic_Forum) was convened on August 12, 2019, in Turkmenistan and brought together representatives of Kazakhstan, Russia, Azerbaijan, Iran and that state. It hosted several meetings of their ministers of economy and transport.[^cite_note-70]

The Caspian countries develop robust cooperation in the tech and digital field as part of the Caspian Digital Hub. The project helps expand data transmission capabilities in Kazakhstan as well as data transit capabilities between Asia and Europe. The project generated interest from investors from all over the world, including the UK.[^cite_note-71]

### Oil and gas

Oil production using drilling platform, on the offshore of Turkmenistan

Drilling platform "Iran Khazar" in use at a Dragon Oil production platform in the [Cheleken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazar,_Turkmenistan) field ([Turkmenistan](198149.md))

The Caspian Sea region presently is a significant, but not major, supplier of [crude oil](23195.md) to world markets, based upon estimates by [BP Amoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP-Amoco) and the U.S. [Energy Information Administration](1704673.md), [U.S. Department of Energy](62866.md). The region output about 1.4–1.5 million [barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrels_of_oil) per day plus natural [gas](23639.md) liquids in 2001, 1.9% of total world output. More than a dozen countries output more than this top figure. Caspian region production has been higher, but waned during and after the collapse of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). [Kazakhstan](16642.md) accounts for 55% and [Azerbaijan](746.md) for about 20% of the states' [oil output.](8082027.md)[^cite_note-72]



Caspian region oil and natural gas infrastructure, August 2013

The world's first offshore wells and machine-drilled wells were made in Bibi-Heybat Bay, near [Baku, Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku,_Azerbaijan). In 1873, exploration and development of oil began in some of the largest fields known to exist in the world at that time on the [Absheron Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absheron_Peninsula) near the villages of [Balakhanli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaxan%C4%B1), [Sabunchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabanchi,_Yanaulsky_District,_Republic_of_Bashkortostan), Ramana, and Bibi Heybat. Total recoverable reserves were more than 500 million tons. By 1900, Baku had more than 3,000 oil wells, 2,000 of which were producing at industrial levels. By the end of the 19th century, Baku became known as the "black gold capital", and many skilled workers and specialists flocked to the city.

By the beginning of the 20th century, Baku was the center of the international oil industry. In 1920, when the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) captured Azerbaijan, all private property, including oil wells and factories, was confiscated. Rapidly the republic's oil industry came under the control of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). By 1941, Azerbaijan was producing a record 23.5 million tons of oil per year – its Baku region output was nearly 72 percent of the Soviet Union's oil.[^cite_note-SOCAR1-62]

In 1994, the "[Contract of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_of_the_Century_\(Azerbaijan\))" was signed, heralding extra-regional development of the Baku oil fields. The large [Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline](397567.md) conveys Azeri oil to the Turkish Mediterranean port of [Ceyhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceyhan) and opened in 2006.

The [Vladimir Filanovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Filanovsky?action=edit\&redlink=1) oil field in the Russian section of the body of water was discovered in 2005. It is reportedly the largest found in 25 years. It was announced in October 2016 that [Lukoil](1027881.md) would start production from it.[^cite_note-73]

#### Transport

[Baku](4566.md) has the main moorings of all large vessels, such as oil tankers, in [Azerbaijan](746.md). It is the largest port of the Caspian Sea. The port (and tankers) have access to the oceans along the Caspian Sea–Volga–[Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)) [Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal), and the Don–[Sea of Azov](78019.md). A northern alternate is the [Volga–Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Baltic_Waterway) (a sea which has a connection to the [North Sea](21179.md) of the Atlantic, as the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea) does via the [White Sea-Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea%E2%80%93Baltic_Canal) canal). Baku Sea Trade Port and Caspian Shipping Company CJSC, have a big role in the sea transportation of Azerbaijan. The Caspian Sea Shipping Company CJSC has two fleets plus shipyards. Its transport fleet has 51 vessels: 20 tankers, 13 ferries, 15 universal dry cargo vessels, 2 Ro-Ro vessels, as well as 1 technical vessel and 1 floating workshop. Its specialized fleet has 210 vessels: 20 cranes, 25 towing and supplying vehicles, 26 passenger, two pipe-laying, six fire-fighting, seven engineering-geological, two diving and 88 auxiliary vessels.[^cite_note-74]

The Caspian Sea Shipping Company of Azerbaijan, which acts as a liaison in the Transport Corridor Europe-Caucasus-Asia (TRACECA), simultaneously with the transportation of cargo and passengers in the Trans-Caspian direction, also performs work to fully ensure the processes of oil and gas production at sea. In the 19th century, the sharp increase in oil production in [Baku](4566.md) gave a huge impetus to the development of shipping in the Caspian Sea, and as a result, there was a need to create fundamentally new floating facilities for the transportation of oil and oil products.[^cite_note-75]

### Political issues

Many of the islands along the Azerbaijani coast retain great geopolitical and economic importance for demarcation-line oil fields relying on their national status. [Bulla Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulla_Island), [Pirallahı Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirallah%C4%B1_Island), and [Nargin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nargin_\(island\)), which is still used as a former [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) base and is the largest island in the [Baku](4566.md) bay, hold oil reserves.

The collapse of the Soviet Union allowed the market opening of the region. This led to intense investment and development by international oil companies. In 1998, [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney) commented that "I can't think of a time when we've had a region emerge as suddenly to become as strategically significant as the Caspian."[^cite_note-76]

A key problem to further local development is arriving at precise, agreed demarcation lines among the five [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral) states. The current disputes along Azerbaijan's maritime borders with Turkmenistan and Iran could impinge future development.

Much controversy currently exists over the proposed [Trans-Caspian oil and gas pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Caspian_Oil_Transport_System). These projects would allow Western markets easier access to Kazakh oil and, potentially, Uzbek and Turkmen gas as well. Russia officially opposes the project on environmental grounds.[^cite_note-scuttle-77] However, analysts note that the pipelines would bypass Russia completely, thereby denying the country valuable transit fees, as well as destroying its current [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly) on westward-bound [hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) exports from the region.[^cite_note-scuttle-77] Recently, both Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan have expressed their support for the Trans-Caspian Pipeline.[^cite_note-78]

[Leaked U.S. diplomatic cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak) revealed that [BP](18998720.md) covered up a gas leak and [blowout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blowout_\(well_drilling\)) incident in September 2008 at an operating gas field in the Azeri-Chirag-Guneshi area of the Azerbaijan Caspian Sea.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

### Coastline

Five states are located along about 4,800 km (3,000 mi) of the Caspian coastline. The length of the coastline of these countries:[^cite_note-81]

- [Kazakhstan](16642.md) – 1,422 km (884 mi)

- [Turkmenistan](198149.md) – 1,035 km (643 mi)

- [Iran](14653.md) – 728 km (452 mi)

- [Azerbaijan](746.md) – 813 km (505 mi)

- [Russia](25391.md) – 747 km (464 mi)

### Negotiations

A 1931 treaty between [Iran](14653.md) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) stated that the sea belonged to both nations.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] In 2000, negotiations as to the [demarcation](13541266.md) of the sea had been going on for nearly a decade among all the states bordering it. Whether it was by law a sea, a lake, or an agreed hybrid, the decision would set the demarcation rules and was heavily debated.[^cite_note-84] Access to mineral resources ([oil](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md)), access for fishing, and access to [international waters](394357.md) (through Russia's [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga) river and the canals connecting it to the [Black Sea](3386.md) and [Baltic Sea](3335.md)) all rest on the negotiations' outcome. Access to the Volga is key for market efficiency and economic diversity of the [landlocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked) states of Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, and Turkmenistan. This concerns Russia, as more traffic seeks to use its inland [waterways](174851.md), which can become congested at certain points. If the body of water is, by law, a sea, many precedents and international treaties oblige free access to foreign vessels. If it is a lake there are no such obligations. Resolving and improving [environmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_\(biophysical\)) issues rests on the sea's status and [borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border) issue.



Caspian Sea shore, [Dagestan](751099.md)

All five Caspian littoral states maintain naval forces on the sea.[^cite_note-85] According to a treaty signed between Iran and the Soviet Union, the sea is technically a lake and was divided into two sectors (Iranian and Soviet), but the resources (then mainly fish) were commonly shared. The line between the two sectors was considered an international border in a common lake, like [Lake Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Albert_\(Africa\)). The Soviet sector was sub-divided into the four littoral republics' administrative sectors.

Russia, Kazakhstan, and Azerbaijan have bilateral agreements with each other based on [median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median) lines. Because of their use by the three nations, median lines seem to be the most likely method of delineating territory in future agreements. However, Iran insists on a single, multilateral agreement among the five nations (aiming for a one-fifth share). Azerbaijan is at odds with Iran over some of the sea's [oil fields](2910801.md). Occasionally, Iranian patrol boats have fired at vessels sent by Azerbaijan for exploration into the disputed region. There are similar tensions between Azerbaijan and Turkmenistan (the latter claims that the former has pumped more oil than agreed from a field, recognized by both parties as shared).

The Caspian littoral states' meeting in 2007 signed an accord that only allows ships bearing flags of littoral states to enter the sea.[^cite_note-86]

Negotiations among the five states ebbed and flowed, from about 1990 to 2018. Progress was notable in the fourth Caspian Summit held in [Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan) in 2014.[^cite_note-87]

### Caspian Summit

The Caspian Summit is a head of state-level meeting of the five littoral states.[^cite_note-summit-88] The fifth Caspian Summit took place on August 12, 2018, in the Kazakh port city of [Aktau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktau).[^cite_note-summit-88] The five leaders signed the 'Convention on the Legal Status of the Caspian Sea'.[^cite_note-signed-89]

Representatives of the Caspian littoral states held a meeting in the capital of Kazakhstan on September 28, 2018, as a follow-up to the Aktau Summit. The conference was hosted by the Kazakh Ministry of Investment and Development. The participants in the meeting agreed to host an investment forum for the Caspian region every two years.[^cite_note-90]

### Convention on the Legal Status of the Caspian Sea

Main article: [Convention on the legal status of the Caspian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_the_legal_status_of_the_Caspian_Sea)

The five littoral states build consensus on legally binding governance of the Caspian Sea through Special Working Groups of a Convention on the Legal Status of the Caspian Sea.[^cite_note-convlegalstatus-91] In advance of a Caspian Summit, the 51st Special Working Group took place in [Astana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astana) in May 2018 and found consensus on multiple agreements: Agreements on cooperation in the field of transport; trade and economic cooperation; prevention of incidents on the sea; combating terrorism; fighting against organized crime; and border security cooperation.[^cite_note-51agreement-92]

The convention grants jurisdiction over 24 km (15 mi) of territorial waters to each neighboring country, plus an additional 16 km (10 mi) of exclusive fishing rights on the surface, while the rest is [international waters](394357.md). The seabed, on the other hand, remains undefined, subject to bilateral agreements between countries. Thus, the Caspian Sea is legally neither fully a sea nor a lake.[^cite_note-Economist_Caspian_sea_or_lake-3]

While the convention addresses caviar production, oil and gas extraction, and military uses, it does not touch on environmental issues.[^cite_note-Nation-20190418-35]

## Transportation

Although the Caspian Sea is [endorheic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endorheic), its main tributary, the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), is connected by important shipping canals [with the Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal) (and thus the Black Sea) and [with the Baltic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Baltic_Waterway), with branch canals [to Northern Dvina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Dvina_Canal) and [to the White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea%E2%80%93Baltic_Canal).

Another Caspian tributary, the [Kuma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma_River_\(Russia\)), is also connected by [an irrigation canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma%E2%80%93Manych_Canal) with the Don basin.

Scheduled [ferry](50771.md) services (including [train ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_ferry))[^cite_note-94] across the sea chiefly are between:

- [Türkmenbaşy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrkmenba%C5%9Fy,_Turkmenistan) in [Turkmenistan](198149.md), (formerly [Krasnovodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrkmenba%C5%9Fy,_Turkmenistan)) and [Baku](4566.md)

- [Aktau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktau), Kazakhstan and Baku

- [Kuryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuryk), Kazakhstan and Baku

- Cities in Iran and Russia (chiefly for cargo.)

### Canals

As an [endorheic basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endorheic_basin), the Caspian Sea basin has no natural connection with the ocean. Since the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Period), traders reached the Caspian via a number of [portages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage) that connected the Volga and its tributaries with the [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)) (which flows into the [Sea of Azov](78019.md)) and various rivers that flow into the [Baltic Sea](3335.md). [Primitive canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Baltic_Waterway#History) connecting the Volga Basin with the Baltic were constructed as long ago as the early 18th century. Since then, a number of canal projects have been completed.

The two modern canal systems that connect the Volga Basin, and hence the Caspian Sea, with the ocean are the [Volga–Baltic Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Baltic_Waterway) and the [Volga–Don Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal).

The proposed [Pechora–Kama Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechora%E2%80%93Kama_Canal) was a project that was widely discussed between the 1930s and 1980s. Shipping was a secondary consideration. Its main goal was to [redirect some of the water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_river_reversal) of the [Pechora River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechora_River) (which flows into the Arctic Ocean) via the [Kama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River) into the Volga. The goals were both irrigation and the stabilization of the water level in the Caspian, which was thought to be falling dangerously fast at the time. During 1971, some [peaceful nuclear construction experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_nuclear_explosion) were carried out in the region by the USSR.

In June 2007, in order to boost his oil-rich country's access to markets, [Kazakhstan](16642.md)'s President [Nursultan Nazarbayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursultan_Nazarbayev) proposed a 700 km (435 mi) link between the Caspian Sea and the [Black Sea](3386.md). It is hoped that the "[Eurasia Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia_Canal)" ([Manych Ship Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manych_Ship_Canal)) would transform landlocked Kazakhstan and other [Central Asian countries](6742.md) into maritime states, enabling them to significantly increase trade volume. Although the canal would traverse Russian territory, it would benefit Kazakhstan through its Caspian Sea ports. The most likely route for the canal, the officials at the Committee on Water Resources at Kazakhstan's Agriculture Ministry say, would follow the [Kuma–Manych Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma%E2%80%93Manych_Depression), where currently a chain of rivers and lakes is already connected by an irrigation canal (the [Kuma–Manych Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma%E2%80%93Manych_Canal)). Upgrading the Volga–Don Canal would be another option.[^cite_note-95]
