**Rolpa** ([Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language): रोल्पा जिल्ला)[Listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/86/Rolpa.ogg/Rolpa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, is a "hill" district in [Lumbini Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumbini_Province) of [Nepal](171166.md). Rolpa district covers an area of 1,879 km<sup>2</sup> (725 sq mi) with population (2011) of 221,177. [Rolpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolpa_Municipality) town is the district's administrative center.

The various grievances of Rolpa's population made the district ripe for revolt. It became a "Maoist Stronghold" of the [Communist Party of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Nepal).[^cite_note-2] In May 2002 a major battle between Maoist guerrillas and the army was fought at Lisne Lekh near the Rolpa-Pyuthan border.

## History

The area of Rolpa District was under the Rukumkot ([Rukum District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rukum_District)) King before 15th century. Tuthansen (King of Salyankot) established a separate kingdom carving out some 18 villages from Rukumkot Kingdom. The new kingdom was named Gajulkot. These 18 villages were given to Tuthansen in dowry by Jayant Berma who was the king of Rukumkot. These ruins are still available to see in Rolpa District.[^cite_note-3]

Rolpa district was part of two different districts [Pyuthan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyuthan_District) and [Salyan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyan_District,_Nepal) during [Rana regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_regime). In 1962, Rolpa district established carving out parts of [Pyuthan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyuthan_District) and [Salyan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyan_District,_Nepal).[^cite_note-4]

### Maoist administration

During the [Nepali civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_civil_war), much of the Rolpa district came under the control of Nepal's Maoist revolutionaries.[^cite_note-Dispatches_from_the_people's_war_in-5] The installation of a Maoist administration in Rolpa involved revolutionary united front committees which settled local disputes, and many people in Rolpa began to use the Maoist legal administration over the central state's legal outposts.[^cite_note-Dispatches_from_the_people's_war_in-5] These adminsirations created by the Maoists in Rolpa were based on the 3-in-1 committee system, where 40% of the seats went to the party, another 40% to the population, and 20% to the Maoist armed forces.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-6] Gender was important to the Maoist administration in Rolpa, with Maoist forces reporting that if a crime mandated [corporal punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporal_punishment) if the crime was carried out by a woman against a man or woman, the punisher would have to be a woman. If it was between two men, both men and women would take part in the punishment, though in cases like spying punishment would be carried out by the armed forces.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-6]

The economy of the areas of the district under Maoist control were transformed. As money lenders often left following Maoist entry, the party had to assume control over moneylending practices. As a result, yearly interest rates charged on loans fell from 60-120% to 10-15%, with no interest in cases of those needing loans for grain or if they were sick. Money was also raised by the party administration through taxation, fines, and fees for registering land.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-6]

## Geography and climate

Rolpa is drained southward by the [Madi Khola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Rapti_River) from a complex of 3,000 to 4,000 meter ridges about 50 kilometers south of the [Dhaulagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaulagiri) [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya). This mountainous barrier historically isolated Rolpa by encouraging travelers between India and Tibet to detour to follow easier routes to the east or west, while east–west travelers found easier routes to the north through [Dhorpatan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhorpatan), or to the south through [Dang Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dang_Deukhuri_District) or along the [Mahabharat Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharat_Range).

## Demographics

At the time of the [2021 Nepal census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nepal_census), Rolpa District had a population of 234,793. 10.26% of the population is under 5 years of age. It has a literacy rate of 75.63% and a sex ratio of 1137 females per 1000 males. 35,376 (15.07%) lived in municipalities.[^cite_note-provincialreport-8]

Castes/ethnic groups in Rolpa district (2021)

[^cite_note-caste-9]

1. [Magar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magars) (42.8%)

2. [Chhetri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhetri) (35.2%)

3. [Kami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami_\(caste\)) (12.6%)

4. [Damai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damai) (3.64%)

5. [Dasnami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da%C5%9Ban%C4%81mi_Sampradaya) (1.54%)

6. [Bahun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahun) (0.96%)

7. Other Hill Janjati (1.09%)

8. Others (2.26%)

[Khas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khas_people) people make up a majority of the population with 55% of the population, of which Khas [Dalits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit) are 17% of the population. Hill Janjatis make up 44% of the population, of which [Magars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magars) are 43% and are the largest single community in the district.[^cite_note-caste-9]
