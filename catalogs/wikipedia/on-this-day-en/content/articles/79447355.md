The **2025 Georgian local elections** were held on 4 October to elect the bodies of [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Georgia_\(country\)) of [Georgia](48768.md).[^cite_note-3] 9 opposition parties, including two major opposition blocs [Coalition for Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_Change_\(Georgia\)) and [Unity – National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_%E2%80%93_National_Movement), boycotted the election due to their allegations of the [2024 Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_parliamentary_election) being rigged and the government thus lacking legitimacy,[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_8parties_pledge-4] although 11 other opposition parties, including two other major opposition blocs [Strong Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Georgia) and [For Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Georgia), took part in the election.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_8parties_pledge-4][^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_17registered-5]

## Background

### Electoral system

Main article: [Local government in Georgia (country)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Georgia_\(country\))

Executive Secretary of the ruling [Georgian Dream](35807631.md) party [Mamuka Mdinaradze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamuka_Mdinaradze) announced that the 2025 local elections would be conducted under the same rules as the [2017 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Georgian_local_elections). According to Mdinaradze, the Georgian Dream-led parliament would adopt the necessary amendments to the Election Code through an accelerated procedure.

The local government elections of 21 October 2017, were held with a 4% threshold. Voters elected officials for a four-year term, including:

- Mayors of 59 self-governing communities and four self-governing cities, including Tbilisi.

- 50 members of the [Tbilisi City Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi_City_Assembly) (Sakrebulo), with 25 elected in single-mandate majoritarian districts and 25 through proportional representation.

- 2,008 members of 63 assemblies in self-governing cities (excluding Tbilisi) and self-governing communities, with 945 elected proportionally and 1,063 through a majoritarian system.

For the [2021 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Georgian_local_elections), the electoral system was revised, altering the proportion of Sakrebulo members elected under proportional and majoritarian rules. Voters elected 2,068 members of 64 assemblies and 64 mayors, including five mayors of self-governing cities. These changes also led to adjustments in district boundaries and administration. A two-round system was introduced for local majoritarian districts, with a 40% threshold. Additionally, the electoral barrier was lowered to 2.5% in Tbilisi and 3% in other districts.[^cite_note-6]

### Boycott debate

See also: [2024–2025 Georgian protests](78322562.md) and [2024–2025 Georgian political crisis](78750834.md)

As of March 2025, none of the four opposition coalitions that crossed the electoral threshold in the [2024 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_parliamentary_election)[^cite_note-7] announced plans to participate in the local elections. They stated that they would only consider participation in the elections if the demands of the [2024–2025 Georgian protests](78322562.md) were met, including the holding of [snap parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Georgian_parliamentary_election) and the "release of all political prisoners".[^cite_note-8] In May and June 2025 [Lelo for Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelo_for_Georgia) urged the other opposition parties to consider "strategic approach to self-government elections", citing the big chance of winning the capital, [Tbilisi](42298.md), amidst the opposition victory in the capital in the 2024 parliamentary elections. The Lelo described this as a major potential blow to Georgian Dream.[^cite_note-kupr-9][^cite_note-gegel-10] Earlier, the [For Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Georgia) party also hinted at potential participation in the elections.[^cite_note-gakh-11] On the other hand, the [United National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_National_Movement) politician Zviad Kuprava referred to the 2025 local election as a "Russian special operation".[^cite_note-OCMedia_Kuprava_detained-12] On 30 June, eight opposition parties, Freedom Square, European Georgia, the United National Movement, Strategy Aghmashenebeli, Ahali, Girchi – More Freedom, Droa and Federalists signed a statement announcing the boycott of the local elections.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_8parties_pledge-4][^cite_note-13] [Strong Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Georgia) officially announced that it would take part in the election on 5 July,[^cite_note-lel-14] while on 14 July it announced an alliance with [For Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Georgia) opposition party for joint candidates.[^cite_note-lelgakh-15] Overall, 12 parties were registered for the local elections.[^cite_note-12part-16]

### Campaign

#### Georgian Dream

On 20 August 2025, Kaladze announced the [tram](30733.md) line project to connect [Didi Dighomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didi_Dighomi?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Didube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didube_\(Tbilisi_Metro\)) metro stations, marking a return of a tram system to Tbilisi after 35 years.[^cite_note-31]

#### Strong Georgia & For Georgia

On 18 September, the joint opposition candidate Irakli Kupradze met with the Mayor of [Brussels](3708.md) [Philippe Close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Close). They discussed the future Tbilisi-Brussels partnership, including joint infrastructure and educational projects. Philippe Close expressed full support for the candidacy of Kupradze.[^cite_note-32]

#### Georgian Dream

On 20 March, Prime Minister [Irakli Kobakhidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irakli_Kobakhidze) stated in an interview that [Kakha Kaladze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakha_Kaladze)'s candidacy for Tbilisi mayor had no alternative, adding, "We'll see what decision the party will make."[^cite_note-33] However, there were speculations that [Georgian Dream](35807631.md) might nominate [Noshrevan Namoradze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noshrevan_Namoradze?action=edit\&redlink=1), a businessman affiliated with the ruling party, as its candidate.[^cite_note-34] In the end, the party confirmed Kakha Kaladze as its nominee.[^cite_note-35]

#### Strong Georgia and For Georgia

On 14 July, [Strong Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Georgia) and [For Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Georgia) announced an alliance for a joint, non-partisan mayoral candidate.[^cite_note-lelgakh-15] [Merab Sepashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merab_Sepashvili?action=edit\&redlink=1), a renowned pop singer, was speculated as a potential candidate.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] [Aleko Elisashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleko_Elisashvili), one of the leaders of Strong Georgia, expressed readiness to run for mayor if there was public support, although he also denied rumors of his nomination by the alliance.[^cite_note-38] Additionally, [Giorgi Sharashidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgi_Sharashidze?action=edit\&redlink=1), a member of the For Georgia party, was considered as a candidate for Tbilisi mayor.[^cite_note-39]

Ultimately, the alliance jointly nominated [Irakli Kupradze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irakli_Kupradze?action=edit\&redlink=1), Secretary General of [Lelo for Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelo_for_Georgia), as their Tbilisi mayoral candidate. The announcement took place at a ceremony, where Kupradze was formally introduced by Elisashvili. The two parties contested the October 4 municipal elections under the shared slogan: "Together, let's take back our cities and villages".[^cite_note-40]

#### New Political Center – Girchi

[Alexandre Rakviashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Rakviashvili), one of the leaders of the New Political Center – Girchi, suggested that the party could field a joint candidate with the other opposition parties.[^cite_note-41] Later the party announced that it would select its Tbilisi mayoral candidate through internal primaries. "We are ready to take part in the local elections — for all positions, including holding primaries for the mayoral candidate of Tbilisi," said party leader [Vakhtang Megrelishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vakhtang_Megrelishvili).[^cite_note-42] On 26 August 2025, the party nominated [Iago Khvichia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iago_Khvichia) as its candidate for Tbilisi mayor.[^cite_note-43]

#### Conservatives for Georgia

In August, the [Conservatives for Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatives_for_Georgia) announced Zurab Makharadze as its candidate for Tbilisi mayor in the local elections. Makharadze said his campaign would focus on addressing what he called the country's most pressing issues, [mass migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_migration) and a severe [demographic crisis](720599.md). He proposed measures such as granting land plots to large families as part of a program to improve Georgia's demographic situation.[^cite_note-44]

### Debate

On 2 October 2025, the [Georgian Public Broadcaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Public_Broadcaster) hosted a [debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debate) between the Tbilisi mayoral candidates, including the Georgian Dream candidate [Kakha Kaladze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakha_Kaladze), the Strong Georgia candidate [Irakli Kupradze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irakli_Kupradze?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Girchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girchi) candidate [Iago Khvichia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iago_Khvichia) and others.[^cite_note-45]

## Allegations of Electoral Fraud

Like the [2024 Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_parliamentary_election) and [2024 Georgian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_presidential_election), local elections were marred by allegations of systemic electoral fraud.&#x20;

In their assessment of the lead up to the 4 October election, [Amnesty International](18947898.md) said that it had, "documented a sweeping campaign of repression, including politically motivated prosecutions of opposition figures, the silencing of independent media and civil society through restrictive laws and punitive measures, and the widespread arbitrary detention and ill-treatment of protesters."[^cite_note-50]

According to Tamaz Datunashvili of [Lelo for Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelo_for_Georgia), instances of voter bribery and carousel voting, as well as the obstruction of opposition observers, occurred at Tbilisi voting stations.[^cite_note-51]

The political intelligence company and poll aggregator, [Europe Elects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Elects) conducted an analysis of data from over 3,000 precincts reported by the Georgian Central Electoral Commission and found "that the local election results show serious signs of falsification and vote tampering — even while accounting for the specific peculiarities of this election."[^cite_note-52]

## Attempted uprising

Main article: [2024–2025 Georgian protests](78322562.md)

Starting in June 2025, opera singer [Paata Burchuladze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paata_Burchuladze) proposed protests on the 4 October local election day with the aim of "tak\[ing] over the government peacefully". [Levan Khabeishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levan_Khabeishvili) of [United National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_National_Movement) (UNM) expressed his support for the plan on 23 June, stating, "hundreds of thousands of Georgians must take to the streets and return power to the people" in the 4 October protest. On 20 August, Khabeishvili described the plan as a "peaceful overthrow, a peaceful revolution".[^cite_note-53] However, Khabeishvili was arrested for publicly offering police officers money in exchange of siding with protesters on 11 September.[^cite_note-54]

On 4 October 2025, twenty thousand people held a rally in Tbilisi and listened to a declaration by opera singer Paata Burchuladze. Following the declaration, an attempt was made to storm Orbeliani Palace, the presidential residence, but they were repelled by the security forces with water cannon and tear gas.[^cite_note-55]
