The **2021 Chicago White Sox season** was the club's 122nd season in [Chicago](6886.md) and 121st season in the American League, and their 1st season under manager [Tony La Russa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_La_Russa) since [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Chicago_White_Sox_season). The White Sox played their home games at [Guaranteed Rate Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaranteed_Rate_Field). On September 23, after a win against the [Cleveland Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Cleveland_Indians_season), the White Sox clinched the [American League Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League_Central) for the first time since the [2008 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Chicago_White_Sox_season) and became the first Major League team of the [2021 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Major_League_Baseball_season) to clinch their division. They finished the regular season with a 93–69 record, good enough for third in the American League and their most wins as a franchise since the [2005 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Chicago_White_Sox_season). By winning the American League Central, it secured the team their first back-to-back postseason appearance in franchise history after having clinched a wild card berth in the [previous season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Major_League_Baseball_season). They lost to the [Houston Astros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Houston_Astros_season) in the [2021 American League Division Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_American_League_Division_Series). Until the [2026 MLB Season](80882200.md), this was the last season the White Sox qualified for the MLB postseason.[^cite_note-1]

## Offseason

### Manager

On October 12, 2020, the White Sox fired manager [Rick Renteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Renteria). He had managed the team for 4 seasons, leading them to a playoff appearance in 2020.[^cite_note-2] On October 29, 2020, the White Sox announced that they would hire [Tony La Russa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_La_Russa), who managed the team from 1979 to 1986. La Russa, age 76, became the oldest person to manage an MLB team. La Russa had not managed a team since the [2011 St. Louis Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_St._Louis_Cardinals) who won the [World Series that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_World_Series).[^cite_note-3]

## Regular season

### City Connect jerseys

On May 28, the White Sox unveiled the City Connect jerseys. They wore the jerseys for the first time on June 5 against the Detroit Tigers. The jerseys are black with white stripes and the hat logo has a "CHI" instead of the "SOX" logo. The jerseys pay homage to the South Side of Chicago.[^cite_note-14]

### Achievements

- [Yermín Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerm%C3%ADn_Mercedes) became the first MLB player of the modern era to start a season 8-for-8. He picked up five hits on April 2 against the [Los Angeles Angels](1360083.md) becoming the first player in White Sox history to record five hits in their first start and the following day he picked up three hits in his first three at bats.

  [^cite_note-22]

- On April 14, [Carlos Rodón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Rod%C3%B3n) pitched the twentieth [no hitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_hitter) in White Sox history against the [Cleveland Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Indians). Rodón pitched eight perfect innings and retired the first batter he faced in the ninth inning before hitting Cleveland catcher [Roberto Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_P%C3%A9rez) in the foot. Pérez was the only base runner Rodón allowed. Rodón struck out 7 batters and threw 75 of 114 pitches for strikes.

  [^cite_note-23]

  [^cite_note-24]

- [Tony La Russa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_La_Russa) surpassed [John McGraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McGraw) for second all-time in [career wins as a manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Major_League_Baseball_managers_with_most_career_wins). This milestone in La Russa's career occurred as a result of the team's 3–0 victory over the Detroit Tigers on June 6.

  [^cite_note-25]

- The White Sox had four players selected to play for the [American League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League) in the [2021 MLB All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_MLB_All-Star_Game): [Tim Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Anderson_\(baseball\)), [Liam Hendriks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Hendriks), [Lance Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Lynn), and [Carlos Rodón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Rod%C3%B3n). During the game, Lynn pitched one shutout inning, where he gave up no hits, one walk, and struck out catcher [J. T. Realmuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._T._Realmuto) of the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(baseball\)). Hendriks pitched to close out the game for the American League and recorded the [save](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_\(baseball\)), becoming the first Australian-born baseball player to do so. Anderson was substituted into the game as a defensive replacement, but was not able to record an at-bat.

- On July 31, [Seby Zavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seby_Zavala) became the first player in MLB history to hit his first three career home runs in the same game. His first two were hit off of the [Cleveland Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Indians) starter [Triston McKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triston_McKenzie) (with one of them being a grand slam) and his third came off of reliever [Bryan Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Shaw_\(baseball\)).

  [^cite_note-26]

- On August 8 and 9, [Eloy Jiménez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eloy_Jim%C3%A9nez) became the first player in White Sox history to hit 2 Home Runs and 5 RBI's in back-to-back games. His two home runs on August 8 came off of [Chicago Cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cubs) pitchers [Zach Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Davies) and [Michael Rucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rucker_\(baseball\)) and his two homers on August 9 came off of [Minnesota Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Twins) pitcher [Beau Burrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beau_Burrows). It was the first time a Major League player had at least two home runs and five RBIs in back-to-back games since [Bryce Harper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_Harper) in 2015.

  [^cite_note-27]

- [José Abreu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Abreu_\(first_baseman\)) surpassed [Harold Baines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Baines) for the third most home runs in White Sox history. His 222nd career home run with the club came as a game-tying hit in the ninth inning against the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) off of [Chad Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Green_\(pitcher\)) on August 14.

  [^cite_note-28]

- On August 16, [Craig Kimbrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Kimbrel) recorded his 1000th career strikeout. He became the fastest pitcher in Major League history to reach that career milestone, doing it in just over 613 innings pitched. The strikeout was recorded against [Matt Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Chapman) of the [Oakland Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Athletics).

  [^cite_note-29]

- The White Sox pitching staff lead the American League in Complete Games (4), Hits allowed (1,205), Runs allowed (636), Earned Runs allowed (581), and Strikeouts (1588).

- The White Sox finished with the best home record in the American League, going 53-28 (a .650 win percentage) at [Guaranteed Rate Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaranteed_Rate_Field).

  [^cite_note-30]

- Closer [Liam Hendriks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Hendriks) completed the season with the most saves in the American League (38). This was the first time that a White Sox pitcher accomplished such a feat since [Bobby Thigpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Thigpen) in 1990. Hendriks also led Major League relievers in strikeouts (113) becoming the first White Sox pitcher to have 100 or more strikeouts in relief.

- [Dylan Cease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Cease) led the American League in K/9 with 12.3.

- The White Sox had 6 pitchers with 100 or more strikeouts. [Dylan Cease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Cease) (226), [Lucas Giolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Giolito) (201), [Carlos Rodón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Rod%C3%B3n) (185), [Lance Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Lynn) (176), [Liam Hendriks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Hendriks) (113), and [Michael Kopech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kopech) (103). It is the most pitchers to have 100 or more strikeouts in one season in franchise history.

## Awards and honors

[Yermín Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerm%C3%ADn_Mercedes) was named the AL Player of the Week on April 5 after going 9-for-14 with a .643 batting average, a 1.643 OPS, 1 home run, and 6 RBIs.[^cite_note-32] Mercedes was then named the Rookie of the Month in the month of April for the number of games played.

[Liam Hendriks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Hendriks) was named the AL Reliever of the Month in the month of May after not allowing an earned run in 11 2/3 innings pitched for the White Sox while striking out 19 batters and recording eight saves in eight opportunities.[^cite_note-33] Hendriks later earned AL Reliever of the Month honors again for September/October after going 8-for-8 in save opportunities while only allowing six hits, no walks, and striking out 21 batters in 13 2/3 innings.[^cite_note-34] For his efforts, Hendriks won the [Mariano Rivera AL Reliever of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Reliever_of_the_Year_Award) for the second year in a row while becoming the second White Sox player to win reliever of the year and the first since [Bobby Thigpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Thigpen) in 1990.[^cite_note-35]

[José Abreu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Abreu_\(first_baseman\)) was named the AL Player of the Month in August. Abreu batted .330/.382/.661 hitting 10 Home Runs and 25 RBI's with an OPS of 1.043.[^cite_note-36]

Despite not having a great season stat-wise, [Dallas Keuchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Keuchel) won his 5th [Fielding Bible Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielding_Bible_Award) for the pitcher position and passed former White Sox pitcher [Mark Buehrle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Buehrle) for most Fielding Bible Awards won by a pitcher since the Award was first handed out in 2006. Keuchel also won his 5th [Gold Glove Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Glove_Award). Keuchel had a career-high 12 defensive runs saved, most by a pitcher since Buehrle had 12 in 2012.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]
