A **minister** is a politician who heads a [ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)),[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] making and implementing decisions on policies in conjunction with the other ministers. In some jurisdictions the [head of government](37570.md) is also a minister and is designated the '[prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister)', '[premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier)', '[chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_minister)', '[chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor)' or other title.

In [Commonwealth realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm) jurisdictions which use the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system) of government—such as the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Australia](4689264.md)—ministers are usually required to be members of one of the houses of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) or [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature)—or to be appointed to one if not[^cite_note-3]—and are usually from the political party that controls a majority in the lower house of the legislature. These ministers continue to represent their [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) in parliament while being part of the government. In other jurisdictions with strict [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers)—such as [Belgium](3343.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Netherlands](21148.md),[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-a-5] [Philippines](23440.md), [Slovenia](27338.md), and [Nigeria](21383.md)—the holder of a cabinet-level post or other government official is not permitted to be a member of the legislature and must resign if chosen.

Depending on the administrative arrangements in each jurisdiction, ministers are usually heads of a [government department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)) and members of the government's ministry, [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)) and perhaps of a committee of cabinet. Some ministers may be more senior than others, and some may hold the title '[assistant minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Minister)' or '[deputy minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_minister)'. Some jurisdictions, with a large number of ministers, may designate ministers to be either in the inner or outer ministry or cabinet.

In some jurisdictions—such as [Hong Kong](13404.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the [United States](3434750.md)—holders of an equivalent cabinet-level post are called [secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_\(title\)) (e.g., the [Home Secretary](149104.md) in the United Kingdom, [Secretary of State](32293.md) in the United States). Some holders of a cabinet-level post may have another title, such as '[Attorney-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General)' or '[Postmaster-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmaster-General)'.

The term 'minister' also is used in diplomacy, for a diplomat of the [second class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_rank), such as in the title [Minister Plenipotentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_Plenipotentiary), ranking between an [Ambassador](68651.md) and a [Minister Resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_Resident).

The term minister comes from [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English), deriving from the [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French) word *ministre*, originally *minister* in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), meaning "servant, attendant", which itself was derived from the word '*minus*' meaning "less".[^cite_note-8]

Some ministers may hold multiple portfolios and lead several ministries simultaneously, while multiple ministers with separate portfolios may oversee a single ministry, or may also share both ministerial and deputy-ministerial portfolios in different ministries. Some ministers may be more senior than others, and some may hold the title "[assistant minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_minister)" or "[deputy minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_minister)". Some jurisdictions, with a large number of ministers, may designate ministers to be either in the inner or outer ministry or cabinet. A cabinet minister can sometimes be in charge of no ministry at all, and is then known as a "[minister without portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio)".
