***Skyscraper Live*** is a [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) special in which American climber [Alex Honnold](29469916.md) scaled the [Taiwan](25734.md) skyscraper [Taipei 101](203801.md) without the use of ropes.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The ascent was broadcast live on January 25, 2026, and was hosted by sports broadcaster [Elle Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_Duncan), along with professional wrestler [Seth Rollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rollins), climber [Emily Harrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Harrington), climber and climbing broadcaster Pete Woods and [YouTuber](57313961.md) [Mark Rober](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rober).[^cite_note-5]

*[Outside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outside_\(magazine\))* magazine claimed the stunt was the highest [free solo climb](1975031.md) of an urban structure,[^cite_note-\:1-6] though this record is disputed with [Dan Goodwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Goodwin)'s 1986 ascent of the [CN Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CN_Tower).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Background



[Alex Honnold](29469916.md) in 2023



[Taipei 101](203801.md) in 2025

[Taipei 101](203801.md) is 508 meters (1,667 ft) tall and was the tallest building in the world from 2004 to 2009. As of 2026, it is the [eleventh-largest skyscraper in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings) and the tallest building in Taiwan. It was last climbed by [Alain Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Robert) in 2004, over the course of four hours in heavy rain and with a safety rope, meaning it was not a [free solo](1975031.md) climb. Robert, who has climbed about 200 such buildings, rates it at 6 out of 10 on his difficulty scale.[^cite_note-NYT-9]

Honnold became famous in 2017 for his free solo climb of the [El Capitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Capitan) rock formation in [Yosemite National Park](48664.md), which was the subject of the Academy Award-winning documentary *[Free Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Solo)*. His climb of Taipei 101 was his first significant climb of a man-made structure.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

Honnold was 40 years old during this climb. He practiced climbing portions of the building to prepare for the live ascent, telling *[The New York Times](30680.md)* that he climbed it with ropes "two or three" times.[^cite_note-\:2-11] In advance of his climb, Honnold participated in a traditional Taiwanese blessing ceremony to pray for success.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

In an interview in advance of the event, Honnold told Adam Skolnick of *[The New York Times](30680.md)* that he would be paid "an embarrassingly small amount" for the climb, which the newspaper reported would be in the "mid-six figures".[^cite_note-\:2-11] *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* parodied the event the Saturday before it took place, with [Mikey Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikey_Day) playing Honnold.[^cite_note-12]

## Broadcast

The special aired live on January 25, 2026, one day later than originally scheduled due to inclement weather. Honnold began his climb at 9\:00 a.m. local time and reached the top of the spire one hour and 31 minutes later.[^cite_note-Yahoo_Sports-13][^cite_note-14] Plimsoll Productions was the lead producer.[^cite_note-15] The event used "standard live-TV production safety protocols", according to *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\))*.[^cite_note-16] The Taiwanese government and the [Taipei Film Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Film_Commission) were organizers of the event.

While climbing, Honnold appeared to be relaxed, smiling and waving to onlookers inside and outside the building, and making occasional commentary through a live microphone. Though he mentioned that he was tired, he completed the ascent without incident, arriving at the top of the spire after one hour, 31 minutes, and 34 seconds.[^cite_note-\:1-6] After it was complete, Honnold [rappelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abseiling) down to the 88th floor and exited through the interior of the building.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

In addition to footage of Honnold climbing, the broadcast included live commentary from the panel of hosts and clips of Honnold training and spending time with his family.[^cite_note-\:1-6] Netflix broadcast the climb on a [10-second delay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_delay) so that, in the case of Honnold falling, the broadcast could be stopped before viewers would see the fall.[^cite_note-17]

## Reception

Ed Power of *[The Telegraph](23797577.md)* gave the special three of five stars, on grounds that the broadcast was thrilling but monotonous, writing "*Skyscraper Live* draws you into a sort of Zen state, where you are both simultaneously riveted and slightly bored" and "There’s not much to see – and so, the audience will have found themselves in the weird position of both experiencing a pounding heart and occasionally stifling a yawn."[^cite_note-18] According to *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)*, many viewers were unhappy with the live commentary from the panel of hosts, saying it added little to the viewing experience and was distracting.[^cite_note-19]
