**John Vincent Pardon** (born June 1989) is an American mathematician who works on [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry) and [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology).[^cite_note-1] He is primarily known for having solved [Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhael_Gromov_\(mathematician\))'s problem on [distortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_factor) of knots, for which he received the 2012 [Morgan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Prize). He is a permanent member of the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Center_for_Geometry_and_Physics) in [Stony Brook, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook,_New_York). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](10859.md) in 2026.[^cite_note-auto-2]

## Early life

Pardon's mother, Joyce Eileen Maggio Pardon, was a math teacher. She introduced him to basic arithmetic, trigonometry, and calculus. His father, William Pardon, was a mathematics professor at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University).[^cite_note-eq-3]

Pardon was a three-time gold medalist at the [International Olympiad in Informatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympiad_in_Informatics), in 2005, 2006, and 2007.[^cite_note-4] In 2007, he placed second in the [Intel Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Science_Talent_Search) competition, with a generalization to [rectifiable curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifiable_curve) of the [carpenter's rule problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenter's_rule_problem) for polygons. In the project, he showed that every rectifiable [Jordan curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_curve) in the plane can be continuously deformed into a [convex curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_curve) without changing its length and without ever allowing any two points of the curve to get closer to each other.[^cite_note-5] He published this research in the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactions_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)* in 2009.

After high school, Pardon attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where, after his sophomore year, he began taking graduate-level mathematics classes.[^cite_note-eq-3] As a student there, he solved a problem in [knot theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_theory) posed by [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhael_Gromov_\(mathematician\)) in 1983 about whether every [knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_\(mathematics\)) can be [embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedding) into three-dimensional space with bounded [stretch factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_factor). He showed that on the contrary, the stretch factor of certain [torus knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torus_knot) could be arbitrarily large. His proof was published in the *[Annals of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annals_of_Mathematics)* in 2011, and it earned him the [Morgan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Prize) of 2012.[^cite_note-eq-3][^cite_note-clay-6][^cite_note-7]

In college, Pardon became fluent in Chinese.[^cite_note-\:0-8] He later recalled, "I was signing up for classes as a freshman in college, and I heard Chinese was pretty difficult, so I wanted a challenge."[^cite_note-\:1-9] He participated in a Chinese-language immersion program at Princeton and represented the university in an international debate competition in [Singapore](27318.md), broadcast on Chinese television. As a [cello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cello) player, he was a two-time winner of the Princeton Sinfonia concerto competition. He graduated in 2011 and was the [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian) of his class.[^cite_note-eq-3] He then went to [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) for his graduate studies. His accomplishments there included solving the three-dimensional case of the [Hilbert–Smith conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert%E2%80%93Smith_conjecture). He completed his Ph.D. in 2015, under the supervision of [Yakov Eliashberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakov_Eliashberg).[^cite_note-10] In 2015, he was also appointed to a five-year term as a [Clay Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Mathematics_Institute).[^cite_note-clay-6]

## Career

Starting in 2015, Pardon served a yearlong appointment as an assistant professor at Stanford University. In the fall of 2016, he became a full professor of [Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^cite_note-11] He is currently a permanent member of the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Center_for_Geometry_and_Physics) in [Stony Brook, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook,_New_York). In 2023, he proved the [Maulik–Nekrasov–Okounkov–Pandharipande (MNOP) conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulik%E2%80%93Nekrasov%E2%80%93Okounkov%E2%80%93Pandharipande_conjecture),[^cite_note-12] which posited an equivalence between two different curve enumeration invariants of [Calabi–Yau threefolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabi-Yau_manifold). He is currently working on a book about the foundations of [symplectic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_geometry).[^cite_note-\:0-8]

## Awards and honors

In 2017, Pardon received the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation)'s [Alan T. Waterman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_T._Waterman_Award) for his contributions to geometry and topology.[^cite_note-13]

He was elected to the 2018 class of [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Society).[^cite_note-14] Also in 2018 he was an [invited speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_International_Congresses_of_Mathematicians_Plenary_and_Invited_Speakers#2018,_Rio_de_Janeiro) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](627842.md) (ICM) in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md). In 2022 he was awarded the [Clay Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Research_Award).[^cite_note-15] In 2025, he was awarded the [New Horizons in Mathematics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Mathematics#New_Horizons_in_Mathematics_Prize).[^cite_note-16]

Pardon was awarded the [Fields Medal](10859.md) at ICM Philadelphia 2026 for "achievements in symplectic geometry including new approaches to virtual fundamental cycles, Fukaya categories of certain manifolds and counting holomorphic curves, and for his contributions to other areas of geometry and topology, including group actions on 3-manifolds and knot theory".[^cite_note-auto-2]

## Personal life

Pardon has two sons, Alexandros and Andreas. In the Fields Medal award video produced by the [Simons Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Foundation), Pardon said he quizzes them in Chinese: "I talk to them in Chinese a lot. We learn the characters. I read to them. I make them read to me."[^cite_note-17] His sister, Professor Mireille J. Pardon, is a noted medieval historian with a large TikTok following.[^cite_note-18] [^cite_note-19] [^cite_note-20] [^cite_note-21]
