The **Azawad Liberation Front** (**ALF**; [Tamashek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamashek_language): ⵜⴰⴶⴰⵉⵜ ⵢⵏ ⵢⵙⵢⵍⵢⵍⵓ ⴰⵣⴰⵓⴰⴷ; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جبهة تحرير أزواد; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Front de libération de l'Azawad*, [abbr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbr.) **FLA**) is a [separatist](286527.md) [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) organization in the region of [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad) in northern [Mali](19127.md). It was founded on 30 November 2024 and is led by [Tuareg](103054.md) military commander [Alghabass Ag Intalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alghabass_Ag_Intalla).[^cite_note-2]

The organization was formed after the dissolution of the [Strategic Framework for the Defense of the People of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Framework_for_the_Defense_of_the_People_of_Azawad), and included the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) (MNLA), the [High Council for Unity of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Council_for_the_Unity_of_Azawad) (HCUA), and the [Arab Movement of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Movement_of_Azawad) (MAA) and the [Imghad Tuareg Self-Defense Group and Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imghad_Tuareg_Self-Defense_Group_and_Allies) (GATIA). The organization was founded based on its demands for the [independence](62450.md) or [autonomy](191145.md) of [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad).

## History

The Azawad Liberation Front's formation was announced in a press release, following a meeting held between 26 and 30 November 2024 by approximately 180 officials and members of the [Strategic Framework for the Defense of the People of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Framework_for_the_Defense_of_the_People_of_Azawad) (CSP-DPA). The group declared its objective to be "the total liberation of [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad) and the formation of the Azawad Authority".[^cite_note-\:0-3] It adopted a new flag and called for the "self-determination" of Azawad.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-4]

The organization was formed from the merger of four movements of the CSP into a single entity — the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) (MNLA), the [High Council for the Unity of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Council_for_the_Unity_of_Azawad) (HCUA) and rebel fringes of the [Arab Movement of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Movement_of_Azawad) (MAA) and [Imghad Tuareg Self-Defense Group and Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imghad_Tuareg_Self-Defense_Group_and_Allies) (GATIA).[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:2-6][^cite_note-7]

The day after the creation of the FLA, seven or eight of its leaders were killed in a [Bayraktar TB2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_TB2) [drone strike](38212231.md) near [Tinzaouaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinzaouaten), including [Fahad Ag Almahmoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahad_Ag_Almahmoud), head of the rebel branch of GATIA, and Choguibe Ag Attaher, an [amenukal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amenukal) of the [Tuareg](103054.md) tribe of Idnanes.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:2-6][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] On 25 April 2026, the FLA [began offensives](83033878.md) against the Malian Army and claimed control of [Kidal](1463133.md) and parts of [Gao](1255721.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-aj2-11] The offensive was conducted in coordination with the [Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin](53401088.md) (JNIM), which launched attacks in other parts of the country.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

On 14 August, 82 Malian prisoners of war have been freed by FLA. Mohamed Elmaouloud Ramadane, a spokesperson for the FLA, said that the release was conducted for “strictly humanitarian” reasons.[^cite_note-14]

## Relations with other countries

Mali has often accused [Algeria](358.md) of supporting the Azawad Liberation Front, JNIM and other "terrorist groups" in the Sahel.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] [Mauritania](18778516.md) has also been accused by Bamako of providing safe haven for the FLA.[^cite_note-\:3-20] [Libya](17633.md) has been a major source of arms which are trafficked for and by Sahelian militants.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

Bamako has blamed French authorities for backing the FLA and facilitating meetings of their leadership in Europe. Mali-based sources have asserted that France may be supporting the FLA against Mali in retaliation for Mali expelling the French from their country and replacing them with [Russian forces](25709.md).[^cite_note-\:3-20]

Drone operators from the [Ukrainian Main Directorate of Intelligence](1406269.md) (HUR), whose country is at [war with Russia](70149799.md), have also embedded with the FLA to assist in fighting Russian troops. Such specialists remotely assist in coordinating the FLA's [drone warfare](38212231.md) operations, hoping to "put pressure on Russia and undermine the notion that Moscow can be a reliable ally" along with testing their own drone programme in desert environments. While the Ukrainian government denies "substantial cooperation with the rebels", a spokesman for the HUR has described the relationship as the FLA receiving "necessary information" on Russia.[^cite_note-24]

The FLA's leader, [Alghabass Ag Intalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alghabass_Ag_Intalla), has been reported to have close ties with the [Qatari royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari_royal_family), for whom he has arranged hunting trips in the [Sahara](325363.md).[^cite_note-\:5-25]
