&#x20; The **judiciaries of the United Kingdom** are the separate judiciaries of the three legal systems in [England and Wales](275187.md), [Northern Ireland](21265.md) and [Scotland](26994.md). The judges of the [Supreme Court of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom), the [Special Immigration Appeals Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Immigration_Appeals_Commission), [Employment Tribunals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_Tribunal), [Employment Appeal Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_Appeal_Tribunal) and the [UK tribunals system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_tribunals_system) do have a [United Kingdom](31717.md)-wide jurisdiction but judgments only apply directly to the jurisdiction from which a case originates as the same case points and principles do not inevitably apply in the other jurisdictions. In [employment law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_law), [employment tribunals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_tribunal) and the [Employment Appeal Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_Appeal_Tribunal) have [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) in the whole of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) (i.e., not in Northern Ireland).

There have been multiple calls from both Welsh academics and politicians however for a [Welsh justice system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Welsh_justice_system).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-\:1-3]

## Justices of the Supreme Court

The judges of the [Supreme Court of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom) are known as Justices of the Supreme Court, and they are also [Privy Counsellors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom). Justices of the Supreme Court are granted the courtesy title *Lord* or *Lady* for life.[^cite_note-4]

The Supreme Court is a relatively new Court being established in October 2009 following the [Constitutional Reform Act 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Reform_Act_2005). Formerly, the Highest Court of Appeal in the United Kingdom was the [House of Lords Appellate Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_functions_of_the_House_of_Lords) made up of [Lords of Appeal in Ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_of_Appeal_in_Ordinary), also known as Law Lords, which with other Lord Justices now form the Supreme Court. It also took over [devolution cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council#Domestic_jurisdiction) from the [Judicial Committee of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Such Law Lords were allowed to sit in the House of Lords and were members for life.

The Supreme Court serves as the highest court of appeal for all cases in [England and Wales](275187.md) and in [Northern Ireland](21265.md), but only for civil cases in [Scotland](26994.md).[^cite_note-7] The [High Court of Justiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justiciary) remains the [court of last resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_last_resort) in Scotland for criminal cases.[^cite_note-8]

The Supreme Court is headed by the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom) and [Deputy President of the Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_President_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom) and is composed of ten [Justices of the Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom).

The Justices do not wear any gowns or wigs in court, but on ceremonial occasions they wear black [damask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damask) gowns with gold lace without a wig.

## Tribunal Judiciary

The [UK tribunal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribunals_in_the_United_Kingdom) is part of the national system of [administrative justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_justice) with tribunals classed as [non-departmental public bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-departmental_public_bodies) (NDPBs).[^cite_note-9] Though it has grown up on an *ad hoc* basis since the beginning of the twentieth century, from 2007 reforms were put in place to build a unified system with recognised judicial authority, routes of [appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal) and regulatory supervision, and recognised legally qualified members of tribunals as members of the judiciary who are guaranteed continued [judicial independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_independence).[^cite_note-10] The UK tribunal system is headed by the [Senior President of Tribunals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_President_of_Tribunals).[^cite_note-ukpga2007c14-11]
