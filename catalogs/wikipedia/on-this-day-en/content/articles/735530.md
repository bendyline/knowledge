**Bashkortostan,**[^cite_note-14] officially the **Republic of Bashkortostan**,[^cite_note-16] sometimes also called **Bashkiria**,[^cite_note-17] is a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) of [Russia](25391.md) between the [Volga river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga) and the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains) in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). The republic borders [Perm Krai](1765550.md) to the north, [Sverdlovsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverdlovsk_Oblast) to the northeast, [Chelyabinsk Oblast](475230.md) to the east, [Orenburg Oblast](481957.md) to the south, [Tatarstan](67135.md) to the west and [Udmurtia](447145.md) to the northwest. It covers 143,600 square kilometres (55,400 square miles). It is the [seventh-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federal_subjects_of_Russia_by_population) federal subject in Russia and the most populous republic.[^cite_note-2010Census-18] As of 2025, it has a population of 4,046,094.[^cite_note-19] Its [capital](181337.md) and largest city is [Ufa](321112.md). Other major cities include [Sterlitamak](2177507.md), [Salavat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salavat,_Russia), [Neftekamsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neftekamsk), and [Oktyabrsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktyabrsky,_Bashkortostan,_Russia).

[Bashkortostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkiria_\(1917%E2%80%931919\)) was established on 28 November \[[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style) 15 November] 1917.[^cite_note-Юлдашбаев-20][^cite_note-Гумеров-21][^cite_note-22] On 20 March 1919, it was transformed into the [Bashkir ASSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkir_ASSR),[^cite_note-1987USSR-23] the first [autonomous republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republics) in the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR).[^cite_note-GSE-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] On 11 October 1990, it adopted the [Declaration of State Sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_State_Sovereignty_of_Bashkortostan). In the [Constitution of Bashkortostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_Bashkortostan) and [Constitution of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Russia), Bashkortostan is defined as a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-const-28]

## Name

See also: [Bashkirs § Ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs#Ethnonym)

The name *Bashkortostan* derives from the name of the [Bashkir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs) ethnic group, and is literally translated as "land of the Bashkirs".[^cite_note-29] While the [endonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym) having various theories on its meaning, the most prominent being a combination of [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) words *baş*, meaning 'head', 'chief', 'main', 'principal' and *qurt* meaning 'wolf' (one of the animals regarded as sacred to [Turkic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples));[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] the suffix [-stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-stan) is [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language), commonly used for many Eurasian territorial names.

The Russian name *Bashkiriya* first appeared at the end of the 16th century. Originally, it appeared in the forms *Bashkir land*, *Bashkir*, *Bashkirda* and *Bashkir horde*.

## History

Main article: [History of Bashkortostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bashkortostan)

### Early history

The first settlements in the territory of modern Bashkortostan date from the early [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) period, but the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) spurred an upsurge in the population of this territory.[^cite_note-32] When people of the [Abashevo culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abashevo_culture) started settling here, they possessed high skills in manufacturing bronze tools, weapons and decorations. They were the first to establish permanent settlements in the Southern [Urals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urals).

In the 10th century, [Al-Balkhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Zayd_al-Balkhi) wrote about Bashkirs as a people, divided into two groups, one of which inhabited the Southern Urals, while the other lived near the Danube River, close to the boundaries of Byzantium. His contemporary [Ibn-Ruste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Rustah) described the Bashkirs as "an independent people, occupying territories on both sides of the Ural mountain ridge between Volga, Kama, Tobol and upstream of Yaik River".

After the early-feudal [Mongolian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) had broken down in the 14th century, the territory of modern Bashkortostan became divided between the [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Khanate), the [Siberia Khanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia_Khanate) and the [Nogai Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogai_Horde). The tribes that lived there were headed by *bi* (tribal heads).&#x20;

### Within the Russian Tsardom and Empire

After [Kazan fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kazan) to [Ivan the Terrible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_IV_of_Russia) in 1554–1555, representatives of western and northwestern [Bashkir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs) tribes approached the [Tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar) with a request to voluntarily join [Muscovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia). The [Bashkir rebellion of 1662–1664](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkir_rebellion_of_1662%E2%80%931664) and the [Bashkir rebellion of 1704–1711](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkir_rebellion_of_1704%E2%80%931711) were primarily caused by the Russian government's violations of the terms and conditions previously agreed between the Bashkirs and the Russian authorities. Bashkirs were also largely involved in the [Pugachev Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pugachev_Rebellion) (their leader [Salawat Yulayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salawat_Yulayev), currently considered a national hero of the Bashkirs, was one of Pugachev's closest aides).&#x20;



The monument for the national hero of Bashkortostan, [Salawat Yulayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salawat_Yulayev), who led a rebellion against the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).

Starting from the second half of the 16th century, Bashkiria's territory began taking shape as a part of the Russian state. In 1798, the Spiritual Assembly of Russian Muslims was established, an indication that the tsarist government recognized the rights of Bashkirs, [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars), and other Muslim nations to profess Islam and perform religious rituals. [Ufa Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ufa_Governorate) (*[guberniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guberniya)*), with a center in [Ufa](321112.md), was formed in 1865—another step toward territorial identification.

### After the Russian Revolution of 1917

After the [Russian Revolution of 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917) were the All-Bashkir Qoroltays (conventions), which required a decision on the need to create a national federal republic within Russia. As a result, on 28 November 1917, the Bashkir Regional (Central) Shuro (Council) proclaimed the establishment of territorial and national autonomy in areas of Orenburg, Perm, Samara, and Ufa provinces with a predominantly Bashkir population.

In December 1917, delegates to the All-Bashkir (constituent) Congress, representing the interests of the population edge of all nationalities, voted unanimously for the resolution (Farman #2) of the Bashkir regional Shuro for the proclamation of national-territorial autonomy (of the Republic) Bashkurdistan. The congress formed the government of Bashkurdistan, the Pre-parliament—Kese-Qoroltay and other bodies of power and administration, and decisions were made on how to proceed.

In March 1919, based on the agreements of the Russian Government, the [Bashkir Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkir_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) was formed. During the Soviet period, Bashkiria was granted broad autonomous rights, the first among other Russian regions. The administrative structure of the Bashkir ASSR was based on principles similar to those of other autonomous republics of Russia.

On 11 October 1990, the Supreme Soviet of the Republic adopted the [declaration on state sovereignty of the Bashkir ASSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_State_Sovereignty_of_Bashkortostan).[^cite_note-33] On 25 February 1992, the Bashkir ASSR was renamed the Republic of Bashkortostan.[^cite_note-34]

On 31 March 1992, a [Federative Compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federative_Compact?action=edit\&redlink=1) "On separation of authorities and powers among federal organs of power of the Russian Federation and the organs of power of the Republic of Bashkortostan" was signed. On 3 August 1994,[^cite_note-asymmetries-35] a Compact "On separation of authorities and mutual delegating of powers among the organs of power of the Russian Federation and the organs of power of the Republic of Bashkortostan" was signed, granting the republic autonomy. This agreement was unilaterally abolished on 7 July 2005.[^cite_note-turner-36]

Major resources are [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves_in_Russia), [natural gas](22131.md), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), ferrous metal [ores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ores), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite), and more. Bashkortostan is a major source of materials used in non-ferrous [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy). The republic has considerablee deposits of [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) with a high degree of [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen).

Over one-third of the territory of Bashkortostan, or 62,000 square kilometres (24,000 sq mi), is wooded. The following types of trees dominate: [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch), [conifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifers), [lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilia), [oak](39696.md), and [maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple). Bashkortostan forests have special sanctuaries and national parks. They cover more than 10,000 square kilometres (3,900 sq mi).

The [Asselian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asselian) Age at the start of the [Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian) period of geological time is named after the [Assel River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assel_River) in Bashkortostan.[^cite_note-37]

The head of the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_Bashkortostan) of the Republic of Bashkortostan is called the [Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Republic_of_Bashkortostan) (before 1 January 2015 the title was "President"[^cite_note-39]). According to the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkortostan_Constitution), the Head of the Republic of Bashkortostan guarantees rights and liberties to the country's people and citizens, protects the economic and political interests of the Republic of Bashkortostan, and secures legitimacy, law, and order within its territory.

Since 11 October 2018, the [Head of the Republic of Bashkortostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Republic_of_Bashkortostan) has been [Radiy Khabirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiy_Khabirov). He was first appointed as acting head by [Russian president](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md). In 2019 he was elected after winning 82% of the vote in the [2019 Bashkir head election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bashkir_head_election). He was re-elected in the [2024 Bashkir head election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bashkir_head_election). Before his current role, [Radiy Khabirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiy_Khabirov) was the Head of [Krasnogorsk, Moscow Oblast](2399024.md). His predecessor was [Rustem Khamitov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustem_Khamitov), the leader after 19 July 2010. He resigned on 11 October 2018 ahead of the election because he decided to not run for reelection.[^cite_note-40]

The Republic's [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) is the [State Assembly—Kurultai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Assembly_of_Bashkortostan), popularly elected every five years. The one-chamber State Assembly has 110 deputies.



Bashkortostan's leader [Radiy Khabirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiy_Khabirov) with Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), 26 August 2019

The Republic's [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_Bashkortostan) was adopted on 24 December 1993. Article 1 of the Constitution stipulates that Bashkortostan is a sovereign state within Russia, it has state power beyond the limits of the authority of the Russian Federation and the powers of the Russian Federation concerning the aspect of the joint authority of the Russian Federation and the Republic of Bashkortostan. The Republic of Bashkortostan is a full-fledged subject of the Russian Federation on equal and agreed bases.

The relations of the Republic of Bashkortostan and the Russian Federation are based on the articles of the [Constitution of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Russian_Federation), the [Constitution of the Republic of Bashkortostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_Bashkortostan), the Federative Treaty (with amendments) and the Agreement on Separation of authorities and powers and mutual delegating of powers among the organs of state power of the Republic of Bashkortostan.

The judicial power of the republic is in the hands of the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bashkortostan), the Court of Appeals, [district courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_courts_of_Russia), and [justices of the peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate_\(Russia\)).

In full accord with universally recognized principles of international law, articles of the [European Charter of Local Self-Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Charter_of_Local_Self-Government) and the Constitution of the Russian Federation, the Republic of Bashkortostan ensures in its Constitution that local self-government is recognized and guaranteed within the republic's territory.

The Republic of Bashkortostan resolves all issues of administrative-territorial structure on its own. The list of districts and towns, municipalities, as well as the order of establishing, amending and changing borders of municipalities and their names, are stipulated by the Republic of Bashkortostan law "On administrative-territorial structure of the Republic of Bashkortostan and territory of municipalities".

The state has strong economic and cultural ties with its western neighbour, the Republic of [Tatarstan](67135.md).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

## Economy



Tyupkildy wind park



Sunflower field in [Ishimbaysky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishimbaysky_District). Bashkortostan has a developed agriculture

Bashkortostan is one of the Russian Federation's most developed regions in terms of its cross-regional output, the volume of industrial production, agricultural production, and investment in fixed assets.

The region's largest companies include [Bashneft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashneft), [Ufa Engine Industrial Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ufa_Engine_Industrial_Association?action=edit\&redlink=1) (part of [United Engine Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Engine_Corporation)), [Peton Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peton_Holding?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Bashkhim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkhim?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Ufaorgsintez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ufaorgsintez?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Beloretsk Iron and Steel Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beloretsk_Iron_and_Steel_Works).[^cite_note-regioncompanies-44]

The extraction of [crude oil](23195.md) in Bashkiria began in 1932. with major deposits first discovered in 1943. During the [Great Patriotic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Patriotic_War), Bashkiria became one of the major regions of the Soviet Union to accommodate plants and factories evacuated from Western Russia, as well as great masses of people, while also providing the country with weaponry, fuel, and foodstuffs. After the war, several industries developed further in Bashkiria, such as mining ([Bakr-Tay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakr-Tay_mine) and [Blyavinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blyavinsky_mine) copper mines), machine-building and oil-refining. Bashkiria's industry became a base for the further economic growth of all European outlying territories of Russia.

Bashkortostan has a diverse economy, including a large agricultural sector. But the republic's most important industry is chemical processing. Bashkortostan produces more oil than any other region of Russia, about 26 million tons annually, and provides 17% of the country's gasoline and 15% of its diesel fuel. Other important products manufactured in Bashkortostan include alcohols, pesticides, and plastics. In 2026, during the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), the agricultural grain harvest achieved record levels for a second year but had insufficient local storage, transport, and export options such that a surplus of over 2.5 million tons remained, and had to be covered in plastic and stored in the fields. Transport by road is virtually impossible and the large volume would require 640 trains, unprofitable in the current restricted export market due to the war.[^cite_note-45]

Bashkortostan's [gross regional product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_product) (GRP) in 2016 was 1.34 trillion rubles,[^cite_note-46] making the republic the subject with the ninth-highest GRP in Russia. The state had a positive [trade balance](4810.md), with \$13.7 billion exported and \$1.2 billion imported in 2013.[^cite_note-47] As much as 82.9% of enterprises in Bashkortostan are profitable,[^cite_note-48] higher than the nationwide average of 68.42%. Bashkortostan has been recognized as the region with the lowest economic risk.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

Bashkortostan is among the leaders in real estate development,[^cite_note-51] developed electric power industry[^cite_note-52] and tourism.[^cite_note-53]

Ufa was ranked by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* as among the best cities for business in Russia in 2013.[^cite_note-54]

[Islam](6037917.md) is adhered to by a majority of the nation's population[^cite_note-63] of [Bashkir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs) and [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) descent. The [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) of Bashkortostan follow the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) [Hanafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi) school of [Islamic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh).

Most ethnic Russians, Chuvash, and Ukrainians are [Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox). Most Mari are [Pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan). Non-religious people form a substantial part of any ethnic group in Bashkortostan. There are 13,000 [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) in the republic, with a historic [synagogue](48559.md) in [Ufa](321112.md), and a new Jewish Community Center built in 2008.[^cite_note-64]

According to a 2012 Sreda survey of 56,900 people,[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-61] 58% of the population of Bashkortostan are [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), 17% adhere to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), 3% are [unaffiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Christianity) generic [Christians](18337522.md), 1% are Orthodox Christian believers without belonging to any church or members of other Orthodox churches, and 2% are adherents of the [Slavic native faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_native_faith) (Rodnovery), the [Mari native religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_native_religion), Chuvash [Vattisen Yaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vattisen_Yaly) or [Tengrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengrism). In addition, 4% of the population declare to be "spiritual but not religious", 5% are [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), and 7% follow other religions or did not give an answer to the question.[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-61] Note, however, that this survey has been criticized as biased. It was conducted by the service "Sreda", which has ties to the Christian organizations.[^cite_note-65]

In 2010, there were over 1,000 mosques in Bashkortostan,[^cite_note-66] 200 Orthodox churches and 60 religious buildings of other confessions.[^cite_note-67]

### Education



School in Salavat

Main article: [Education in Bashkortostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Bashkortostan)

About sixty scientific organizations are active in the republic. Fundamental and applied scientific research is underway at 12 institutes of the Russian Academy of Sciences, 29 institutes of different branches of industry, as well as numerous design bureaus and organizations, universities, and colleges.

The country's system of popular education took shape over many centuries and reflects the Bashkir people's [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore), [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation) customs, and [traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradition). When [Islam](6037917.md) spread in Bashkiria in the 10th century, an educational system began to emerge gradually—primarily religious schools operated under the supervision of mosques (*_maktabeh_* and [madrasah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah)).

In addition, many institutions of higher education operate in the republic, including branches of 16 leading Russian universities and colleges. Specialists graduate with degrees in about 200 trades and [professions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profession).

Education is primarily in [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) and [Bashkir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkir_language).

## Culture



Bashkir State Academic Theater of Drama in Ufa

Bashkortostan is home to song and dance companies, a network of national theaters, museums, and libraries, and a number of annual folk festivals. The republic has seven Bashkir, four Russian, and two Tatar State Drama Theaters, a State Opera and Ballet Theater, a National Symphony Orchestra, "Bashkortostan" film studio, thirty philharmonic collectives, and the Bashkir State Folk Dance Ensemble.

The Bashkir School of Dance is well respected, with many students receiving international awards at competitions in Russia and other countries. World-renowned ballet dancer [Rudolf Nureyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Nureyev), as a child, was encouraged to dance in Bashkir folk performances, and began his dancing career in Ufa.

Bashkir literature is the literary tradition of the Republic of Bashkortostan.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

There are many museums in the Republic that chronicle the region's history. The [National Museum of the Republic of Bashkortostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Republic_of_Bashkortostan?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Bashkir Nesterov Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkir_Nesterov_Art_Museum), the [Museum of Archeology and Ethnography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Archeology_and_Ethnography?action=edit\&redlink=1) are the largest of them.
