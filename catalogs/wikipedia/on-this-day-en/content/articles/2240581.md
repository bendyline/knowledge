**Hazaribagh district** is one of the oldest districts of [Jharkhand](197225.md) state, [India](14533.md) and the district headquarters is located in [Hazaribagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaribagh) town. It is located in the north east part of [North Chotanagpur Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Chotanagpur_Division). The boundary of this district consists of districts of [Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaya_district) (BIHAR) and [Koderma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koderma_district) in the north, [Giridih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giridih_district) and [Bokaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokaro_district) in the east, [Ramgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramgarh_district) in the south and [Chatra](2503441.md) in the west.

## Etymology

The district is named after its headquarters, the town of Hazaribagh. The name, Hazaribagh consists of two [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) words, *hazar* meaning "one thousand", and *bagh* meaning "garden" - so, the literal meaning of Hazaribagh is 'a city of one thousand gardens'. According to [Sir John Houlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wardle_Houlton), a veteran British administrator, the town takes its name from the small villages of Okni and Hazari – shown in old maps as Ocunhazry. The last syllable in its name probably originated in a mango-grove, which formed a camping ground for troops and travellers marching along the ‘new military road’ from Kolkata to Varanasi, constructed in 1782 and the following years.[^cite_note-1]

## History

There are ancient Cave Paintings in Isko, Hazaribagh district which are from Meso-chalcolithic period (9,000-5,000 BC).[^cite_note-2] There is a group of megaliths found close to Barkagaon that is about 25 km from Hazaribagh town at Punkri Barwadih, which has been proven to date back to beyond 3000 BCE.[^cite_note-3]



Hazaribagh region in the center of Bihar and Orissa province, 1912 map of British India



The blue-shaded area (except [Dhanbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanbad_district) and part of [Bokaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokaro_district)) of current district map of Jharkhand, represents the historical Hazaribagh district from which other districts were carved out.

In 1872, Hazaribagh district had area of 7,021 square miles (18,180 km<sup>2</sup>) with 771,875 inhabitants. After that many area of the district are curved out as separate district, viz, On 6 December 1972, [Giridih district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giridih_district) was split from Hazaribagh.[^cite_note-Statoids-4] In 1999 this happened again with the creation of [Chatra](2503441.md) and [Koderma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koderma_district).[^cite_note-Statoids-4] Hazaribagh left [Bihar](442482.md) when [Jharkhand](197225.md) was formed on 15 November 2000.[^cite_note-Statoids-4] On 12 September 2007, yet another district was created from Hazaribagh's territory: [Ramgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramgarh_district).[^cite_note-Statoids-4]

## Economy

Coal is the major mineral found in this district. This significant coal deposit reserves of this district include Charhi, Kuju, Ghato Tand and Barkagaon of [North Karanpura Coalfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Karanpura_Coalfield). The coal mines are the main source of livelihood for the residents of this district. People of this district are known to be very hard working.

Patratu and Bhurkunda was also coal mines areas of Hazaribagh but it is now in Ramgarh district.

In 2006, the Indian government named Hazaribagh one of the country's 250 [most backward districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_India) (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-brgf-5] It is one of the 21 districts in Jharkhand currently receiving funds from the Backward Regions Grant Fund Programme (BRGF).[^cite_note-brgf-5]

According to the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India), Hazaribagh district has a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 1,734,495,[^cite_note-districtcensus-7] roughly equal to the nation of [The Gambia](65656.md)[^cite_note-cia-8] or the US state of [Nebraska](21647.md).[^cite_note-9] This gives it a ranking of 279th in India (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-districtcensus-7] The district has a population density of 488 inhabitants per square kilometre (1,260/sq mi).[^cite_note-districtcensus-7] Its [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) over the decade 2001-2011 was 25.75%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-7] Hazaribagh has a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 946 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) for every 1000 males,[^cite_note-districtcensus-7] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 69.75%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-7] 15.87% of the population lives in urban areas. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes make up 17.50% and 7.02% of the total population respectively.

At the time of the [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), 61.58% of the population in the district spoke [Khortha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khortha_language), 23.59% [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), 7.73% [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) and 3.48% [Santali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santali_language) as their first language. There is also a small population of [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) speaking community who are one of the early settlers of the region.[^cite_note-language-11]
