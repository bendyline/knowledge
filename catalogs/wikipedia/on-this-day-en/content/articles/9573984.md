# Multilateral treaty

A **multilateral treaty** or **multilateral agreement** is a [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) to which two or more [sovereign states](23604120.md) are parties.[^cite_note-1] Each party owes the same obligations to all other parties, except to the extent that they have stated [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservation_\(law\)). Examples of multilateral treaties include the [Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_Relating_to_the_Status_of_Refugees), the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md), the [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions), and the [Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court).

## Relationship to bilateral treaties

A [bilateral treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateral_treaty) is a treaty between two states. A bilateral treaty may become a multilateral treaty when additional new parties [succeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_of_states) or [accede](429650.md) to it. [Pope Francis](1687680.md) argues in his [encyclical letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclical_letter) *[Fratelli tutti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fratelli_tutti)* (2020) that "preference should be given to multilateral agreements between states, because, more than bilateral agreements, they guarantee the promotion of a truly universal [common good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_good) and the protection of weaker states.[^cite_note-2]

## Plurilateral treaties

A [plurilateral treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurilateral_treaty) is a special type of multilateral treaty. A plurilateral treaty is a treaty between a limited number of states with a particular interest in the subject of the treaty.[^cite_note-3] The primary difference between a plurilateral treaty and other multilateral treaties is that the availability of [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservation_\(law\)) is more limited under a plurilateral treaty. Due to the limited nature of a plurilateral treaty, the full cooperation of the parties to the treaty is required in order for the object of the treaty to be met. As a result, reservations to plurilateral treaties are not allowed without the consent of all other parties to the treaty. This principle is codified in [international law](8195726.md) by article 20(2) of the [Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Convention_on_the_Law_of_Treaties):

> When it appears from the limited number of the negotiating states and the object and purpose of a treaty that the application of the treaty in its entirety between all the parties is an essential condition of the consent of each one to be bound by the treaty, a reservation requires acceptance by all the parties.[^cite_note-4]

An example of a plurilateral treaty is the [Antarctic Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Treaty), signed on 1 December 1959.
