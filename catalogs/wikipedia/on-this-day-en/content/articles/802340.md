The **Anfal campaign**,[^cite_note-6] was a [counterinsurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterinsurgency) operation, described by many scholars and human rights groups as a [genocide](12441.md) or [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing), which was carried out by [Ba'athist Iraq](26215470.md) from February to September 1988 during the [Iraqi–Kurdish conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi%E2%80%93Kurdish_conflict) at the end of the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War). The campaign targeted rural [Kurds](17068.md)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7] because its purpose was to eliminate Kurdish rebel groups and [Arabize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabization) strategic parts of the [Kirkuk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkuk_Governorate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirmanjRafaat2021163-8] The [Ba'athist regime](26215470.md) committed atrocities on the local Kurdish population, mostly civilians.[^cite_note-\:4-9] While the primary targets were Kurds, other minorities in the north such as [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians) and [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmens) also fell victim to the Anfal campaign.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The Iraqi forces were led by [Ali Hassan al-Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hassan_al-Majid), on the orders of President [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein). The campaign's name was taken from the title of [the eighth chapter of the Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Anfal) (*al-ʾanfāl*).

In 1993, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) released a report on the Anfal campaign based on documents captured by Kurdish rebels during the [1991 uprisings in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_uprisings_in_Iraq); HRW described it as a genocide and estimated between 50,000 and 100,000 deaths. This characterization of the Anfal campaign was disputed by a 2007 [Hague](30269.md) court ruling, which stated that documentary evidence was not sufficient to establish the charge of genocide.[^cite_note-12] Although many [Iraqi Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Arabs) reject that there were any mass killings of Kurdish civilians during Anfal,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Interpretation_of_facts-13] the event is an important element constituting [Kurdish national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_national_identity).

Following the [Iraqi invasion of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_invasion_of_Iran) in 1980, the rival Kurdish opposition parties in Iraq—the [Patriotic Union of Kurdistan](70789.md) (PUK) and [Kurdistan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) (KDP) as well as other smaller Kurdish parties—experienced a revival in their fortunes. Kurdish fighters ([peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga)) fought a guerrilla war against the Iraqi government and established effective control over the Kurdish-inhabited mountainous areas of northern Iraq. As the war went on and Iran counterattacked into Iraq, the peshmerga gained ground in most Kurdish-inhabited rural areas while also infiltrating towns and cities. In 1983, after [the joint KDP-Iranian capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Karbala-7) of [Haj Omran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haj_Omran), the Iraqi government arrested 8,000 [Barzani](4198200.md) men and executed them. During the battle for Haj Omran, the Iraqi government also used gas weapons for the first time against both Kurdish and Iranian forces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Context-14]

## Name

"Al Anfal", literally meaning *the spoils (of war)*,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontgomery200170-15] was used to describe the military campaign of extermination and looting against the Kurds. It is also the title of the [eighth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Anfal) [sura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sura), or chapter, of the Qur'an[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontgomery200170-15] which describes the victory of 313 followers of the new Muslim faith over almost 900 non-Muslims at the [Battle of Badr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badr) in 624 AD. According to Randal, *[Jash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jash_\(term\))* (Kurdish collaborators with the Baathists) were told that taking cattle, sheep, goats, money, weapons and even women was *[halal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halal)* (religiously permitted or legal).[^cite_note-knowledge-16]

## Summary

The Anfal campaign began in February 1988 and continued until August or September and included the use of ground [offensives](15077993.md), [aerial bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing), [chemical warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_warfare), systematic destruction of [settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town), [mass deportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_deportation) and firing squads. The campaign was headed by [Ali Hassan al-Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hassan_al-Majid) who was a cousin of Iraqi President [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) from Saddam's hometown of [Tikrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikrit).[^cite_note-Cruelty_and_Silence-2]

The Iraqi Army was supported by Kurdish collaborators whom the Iraqi government armed, the so-called *[Jash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jash_\(term\))* forces, who led Iraqi troops to Kurdish villages that often did not figure on maps as well as to their hideouts in the mountains. The *Jash* forces frequently made false promises of amnesty and safe passage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201117-3] Iraqi state media extensively covered the *Anfal* campaign using its official name.[^cite_note-Cruelty_and_Silence-2] Approximately 1,200 Kurdish villages were destroyed during the *Anfal* campaign.[^cite_note-17] To many Iraqis, *Anfal* was presented as an extension of the ongoing [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), although its victims were overwhelmingly Kurdish civilians.[^cite_note-Cruelty_and_Silence-2] Like the atrocities committed on the [Marsh Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh_Arabs), the motive was seemingly to intimidate the Kurdish population rather than actually suppress the armed resistance.[^cite_note-18]

## Campaign

In March 1987, [Ali Hassan al-Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hassan_al-Majid) was appointed [secretary-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-general) of the Ba'ath Party's Northern Bureau,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201116–17-19] which included [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md).



Footwear of a child found in an Anfal mass grave

### Military operations and chemical attacks

Anfal, officially conducted in 1988, had eight phases (Anfal 1–Anfal 8) altogether, seven of which targeted areas controlled by the [Patriotic Union of Kurdistan](70789.md). The [Kurdish Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_Democratic_Party)-controlled areas in the northwest [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md), were the target of the Final Anfal operation in late August and early September 1988.

#### Anfal 1



Monument at the mass grave of victims of the [Halabja chemical attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halabja_chemical_attack)

The first Anfal stage was conducted between 23 February and 18 March 1988. It started with [artillery](2508.md) and [air strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_strikes) in the early hours of 23 February 1988. Then, several hours later, there were attacks at the [Jafali Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jafali_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1) headquarters of the [Patriotic Union of Kurdistan](70789.md) near the Iranian border, and the command centers in Sargallu and Bargallu. There was heavy resistance by the [Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga). The battles were conducted in a theater around 1,154 square kilometres (445 sq. mi.).[^cite_note-google.com-20] The villages of Gwezeela, Chalawi, Haladin and Yakhsamar were attacked with poison gas. During mid March, the PUK, in an alliance with Iranian troops and other Kurdish factions, captured Halabja.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201119-21] This led to the [poison gas attack on Halabja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halabja_massacre) on 16 March 1988,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201119-21] during which several thousand Kurdish people were killed, most of them civilians.[^cite_note-22]

#### Anfal 2

During the second Anfal from 22 March and 2 April 1988, the Qara Dagh region, including Bazian and Darbandikhan, was targeted in the [Suleimanya governorate](752619.md). Again several villages were attacked with poison gas. Villages attacked with poisonous gas were Safaran, Sewsenan, Belekjar, Serko and Meyoo.[^cite_note-23] The attacks began on 22 March after [Newruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newroz_as_celebrated_by_Kurds), surprising the Peshmerga. Although of shorter duration, Peshmerga suffered more severe casualties in this attack than the first Anfal.[^cite_note-google.com-20] As a result of the attack, the majority of the population in the Qara Dagh region fled in the direction of Suleimanya. Many fugitives were detained by the Iraqi forces, and the men were separated from the women. The men were not seen again. The women were transported to camps. The population that managed to flee, fled to the Garmia region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201119–20-24]

#### Anfal 3

In the next Anfal campaign from 7 to 20 April 1988, the [Garmian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmian_Region) east of Suleimanya was targeted. In this campaign, many women and children disappeared. The only village attacked with chemical weapons was Tazashar. Many were lured to come towards the Iraqi forces due to an amnesty announced through a loudspeaker of a mosque in Qader Karam from 10 to 12 April. The announced amnesty was a trap, and many who surrendered were detained. Some civilians were able to bribe Kurdish collaborators of the Iraqi Army and fled to Laylan or Shorsh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201120-25] Before the Anfal campaign, the mainly rural Garmian region counted over 600 villages around the towns of [Kifri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kifri), [Kalar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalar,_Iraq) and [Darbandikhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darbandikhan).[^cite_note-\:2-26]

#### Anfal 4

Anfal 4 took place between 3–8 May 1988 in the valley of the [Little Zab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Zab), which forms the border of the provinces of [Erbil](463860.md) and [Kirkuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkuk_Governorate). The morale of the Iraqi army was on the rise due to the capture of the [Faw Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Faw_Peninsula) on the 17–18 April 1988 from Iran in the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlack1993171–172-27] Major poisonous gas attacks were perpetrated in Askar and Goptapa.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlack1993172–176-28] Again it was announced an amnesty was issued, which turned out to be false. Many of the ones who surrendered were arrested. Men were separated from the women.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201121-29]

#### Anfal 5, 6 and 7

In these three consecutive attacks between 15 May and 16 August 1988, the valleys of [Rawandiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawandiz) and Shaqlawa were targeted, and the attacks had different successes. The Anfal 5 failed completely; therefore, two more attacks were necessary to gain Iraqi government control over the valleys. The Peshmerga commander of the region, [Kosrat Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosrat_Abdullah?action=edit\&redlink=1), was well prepared for a long siege with stores of ammunition and food. He also reached an agreement with the Kurdish collaborators of the Iraqi Army so that the civilians could flee. Hiran, Balisan, Smaquli, Malakan, Shek Wasan, Ware, Seran and Kaniba were attacked with poisonous gas. After the Anfal 7 attack, the valleys were under the control of the Iraqi government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201121-29]

#### Anfal 8

The last Anfal was aimed at the region controlled by the [KDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) named Badinan and took place from 25 August to 6 September 1988. In this campaign, the villages of Wirmeli, Barkavreh, Bilejane, Glenaska, Zewa Shkan, Tuka and Ikmala were targeted with chemical attacks. After tens of thousands of Kurds fled to Turkey, the Iraqi Army blocked the route to Turkey on 26 August 1988. The population who did not manage to flee was arrested, and the men were separated from the women and children. The men were executed, and the women and children were brought to camps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201121–22-30]

### Detention camps

Detention camps were established to accommodate thousands of prisoners. Dibs was a detention camp for women and children and located near an army training facility for the Iraqi commando forces.[^cite_note-31] From Dibs, groups of detainees were transferred to [Nugra Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nugra_Salman)[^cite_note-\:1-32] in a [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(geology\)) in the desert about 120 km southwest of [Samawah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samawah),[^cite_note-\:3-33] in the [Muthanna Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muthanna_Governorate). Nugra Salman held an estimated 5,000 to 8,000 prisoners during the Anfal campaign.[^cite_note-\:1-32] Another detention camp was Topzawa near an army base near the highway leading out of Kirkuk.[^cite_note-34]

### Arabization

[Arabization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabization), another major element of al-Anfal, was a tactic used by Saddam Hussein's regime to drive pro-insurgent populations out of their homes in villages and cities like [Kirkuk](199292.md), which are in the valuable [oil field](2910801.md) areas, and relocate them in the southern parts of Iraq.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlack199336-35] The campaign used heavy [population redistribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_transfer), most notably in Kirkuk, the results of which now plague negotiations between Iraq's [Shi'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'a) [United Iraqi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Iraqi_Alliance) and the Kurdish [Kurdistani Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistani_Alliance). Saddam's Ba'athist regime built several public housing facilities in Kirkuk as part of his "Arabisation", shifting poor [Arabs](2185.md) from Iraq's southern regions to Kirkuk with the lure of inexpensive housing. Another part of the Arabisation campaign was the census of October 1987. Citizens who failed to turn up for the October 1987 census were no longer recognized as Iraqi citizens. Most of the Kurdish population who learned that a census was taking place did not take part in the census.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201116–17-19] The government was particularly concerned with seven Kurdish tribes, the Zargush, [Qaralus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaralusi_\(tribe\)), Gurkush, [Kaka'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaka'i_\(tribe\)), [Suramiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suramiri_\(tribe\)), [Arkawazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_\(Kurdish_tribe\)), and [Hamawand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamawand_\(tribe\)).[^cite_note-36]

## Death toll

Precise figures of Anfal victims do not exist due to lack of records.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7] In its 1993 report, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) wrote that the death toll "cannot conceivably be less than 50,000, and it may well be twice that number".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlack1993345-1][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7] This figure was based on an earlier survey by the [Sulaymaniyah](653537.md)–based Kurdish organization Committee for the Defence of Anfal Victims' Rights.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7] According to HRW, Kurdish leaders met with Iraqi government official [Ali Hassan al-Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hassan_al-Majid) in 1991 and mentioned a figure of 182,000 deaths; the latter reportedly replied that "it couldn't have been more than 100,000".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlack1993345-1][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7] The 182,000 figure provided by the PUK was based on extrapolation[^cite_note-Cruelty_and_Silence-2] and "has no empirical relation to actual disappearances or killings",[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7] though it "has assumed mythical status among Kurds".[^cite_note-37] In 1995, the Committee for the Defence of Anfal Victims' Rights released a report documenting 63,000 disappeared and stating that the entire death toll was lower than 70,000, with almost all these deaths occurring in the area of Anfal III.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7] According to Hiltermann, the figure of 100,000, although considered too low by many Kurds, is probably higher than the actual number of deaths.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7]

## Aftermath

In September 1988, the Iraqi government was satisfied with its achievements. The male population between 15 and 50 had either been killed or fled.  The Kurdish resistance fled to Iran and was no longer a threat to Iraq. An amnesty was issued, and the detained women, children and elderly were released[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHardi201122-38] but not permitted to return.[^cite_note-\:0-39] They were sent into camps known as *mujamm'at* where they lived under military rule until a regional autonomy for Iraqi Kurdistan was achieved in 1991.[^cite_note-\:0-39] Following, most survivors returned, and began to reconstruct the villages.[^cite_note-\:0-39] In Kurdish society, the Anfal survivors are known as Anfal mothers ([Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages): *Daykan-î Enfal)*, Anfal relatives ([Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages): *Kes-u-kar-î Enfal)* or Anfal widows ([Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages): *Bewajin-î Enfal)*.[^cite_note-\:0-39]

## Trials

Human Rights Watch unsuccessfully attempted to attract support for a lawsuit under the [Genocide Convention](143988.md) against Iraq at the [International Court of Justice](14918.md). It convinced the United States [Department of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_State)'s legal bureau that Anfal met the legal criteria for genocide.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Interpretation_of_facts-13]

### Frans van Anraat

In December 2005, a court in [The Hague](30269.md) convicted [Frans van Anraat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frans_van_Anraat) of complicity in war crimes for his role in selling chemical weapons to the Iraqi government. He was given a 15-year sentence.[^cite_note-BBCHague-40] The court also ruled that the killing of thousands of Kurds in Iraq in the 1980s was indeed an act of genocide.[^cite_note-BBCHague-40] In the 1948 [Genocide Convention](143988.md), the definition of genocide is "acts committed with the [intent to destroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocidal_intent), in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial or religious group". The Dutch court said that it was considered "legally and convincingly proven that the Kurdish population meets the requirement under the Genocide Conventions as an [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group). The court has no other conclusion than that these attacks were committed with the intent to destroy the Kurdish population of Iraq".[^cite_note-BBCHague-40]

During another trial involving the legal appeal of Van Anraat, [The Hague](30269.md) appellate court upheld the previous conviction of his complicity in war crimes; but ruled that actions of [Iraqi military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_military) during Anfal operations cannot be regarded as constituting a "[genocide](12441.md)".[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-auto-43] The verdict of the Hague Court of Appeal stated on 9 May 2007 that tons of Iraqi documents collected by US government, based on which the [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) produced its reports, were not enough to establish "a sufficient degree of certainty for a finding of fact in respect of [genocide](12441.md) can be derived".[^cite_note-auto-43]

### Saddam Hussein

Main article: [Trial of Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_of_Saddam_Hussein)



[Rizgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizgary), former Sumud relocation camp for Anfal survivors (photographed 2011)

In an interview broadcast on Iraqi television on 6 September 2005, Iraqi President [Jalal Talabani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_Talabani), a Kurdish politician of the [Patriotic Union of Kurdistan](70789.md), said that judges had directly extracted [confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confession_\(law\)) from Saddam Hussein that he had ordered [mass killings](135327.md) and other crimes during his regime and that he deserves to die. Two days later, Saddam's lawyer denied that he had confessed.[^cite_note-44]

### Anfal trial

In June 2006, the [Iraqi Special Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Special_Tribunal) announced that Saddam Hussein and six co-defendants would face trial on 21 August 2006 in relation to the Anfal campaign.[^cite_note-45] In December 2006, Saddam was put on trial for the genocide during Operation Anfal. The trial for the Anfal campaign was still underway on 30 December 2006, when [Saddam Hussein was executed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_of_Saddam_Hussein) for his role in the unrelated [Dujail massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujail_massacre).[^cite_note-46]

The Anfal trial recessed on 21 December 2006, and when it resumed on 8 January 2007, the remaining charges against Saddam Hussein were dropped. Six co-defendants continued to stand trial for their roles in the Anfal campaign. On 23 June 2007, [Ali Hassan al-Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hassan_al-Majid), and two co-defendants, [Sultan Hashem Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Hashim_Ahmad_al-Tai) and [Hussein Rashid Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Rashid_al-Tikriti), were convicted of genocide and related charges and sentenced to death by [hanging](155019.md).[^cite_note-hang-47] Another two co-defendants ([Farhan Jubouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farhan_Jubouri) and [Saber Abdel Aziz al-Douri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_Abdel_Aziz_al-Douri)) were sentenced to [life imprisonment](148681.md), and one ([Taher Tawfiq al-Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taher_Tawfiq_al-Ani)) was acquitted on the prosecution's demand.[^cite_note-48]

Al-Majid was charged with [war crimes](48626.md), [crimes against humanity](61099.md) and [genocide](12441.md). He was convicted in June 2007 and was [sentenced to death](5732607.md). His appeal for the death sentence was rejected on 4 September 2007. He was sentenced to death for the fourth time on 17 January 2010 and was [hanged](155019.md) eight days later, on 25 January 2010.[^cite_note-49] Sultan Hashem Ahmed was not hanged due to opposition of the Iraqi President Jalal Talabani, who opposed capital punishment.[^cite_note-50]

The Anfal trial was widely criticized for its methodical defects marked by various acts of political meddling from the [Iraqi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_government). These involved the sacking of its presiding judge in September 2006 by [Iraqi Prime Minister](208879.md) [Nouri al-Maliki](4723094.md); who perceived the judge as biased towards defendants. [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) stated that many of the charges were "vague" and concluded that the defendants were unable to bring their witnesses due to safety issues within [Iraq](7515928.md). Video interactions from witnesses of the [defendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defendant) were also denied by the court; thus hampering the defendants' ability to challenge the claims of the prosecutors. The trial was marked by absence of fundamental [judicial proceedings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_proceedings), such as the murder of three defense lawyers and ample utilization of anonymous witnesses by the [prosecution](440936.md); whose claims couldn't be cross-analyzed by the defendants.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] Both within and outside Iraq, the trials by the [Special Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_special_tribunal) have been widely dubbed as a "show parade" designed to [execute Saddam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_of_Saddam_Hussein) and deemed as illegitimate by numerous lawyers and human rights organizations.[^cite_note-54]

## Sources

There have been few publications about the Anfal campaign and as of 2008, the only comprehensive account of it is that which was published by Human Rights Watch.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Bibliography-55] HRW's 1993 report on Anfal was based on Iraqi documents, examination of grave sites, and interviews with Kurdish survivors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlack199328-56]

In 1993, the United States government collected 18 tons of Iraqi government documents which were captured by the Peshmerga during the 1991 uprising and airlifted them to the United States.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontgomery200178–79-57] In those files, HRW conducted research on the Anfal campaign in collaboration with United States federal government agencies such as the [National Archives and Records Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archives_and_Records_Administration) (NARA), the [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency), and the [Defense Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontgomery200178–79-57] The US government provided Arabic translators and [CD ROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD-ROM) scanners.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontgomery200178–79-57] HRW accepted the US government role under the condition that personnel involved worked under its direction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontgomery200178–79-57] The files include documents which were collected by the Kurdish parties PUK and KDP, both parties hold the ultimate ownership of the documents that were airlifted to the US.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontgomery200178–79-57]



Memorial to Anfal victims at the [Amna Suraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amna_Suraka) museum in [Sulaimaniyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaimaniyya)

In exchange for access to the National Archives documents, HRW agreed to help the United States government find information about Iraqi atrocities. Joost Hiltermann, HRW's lead researcher on Anfal, referred to these files as "the good stuff…material to smear the enemy with".[^cite_note-Weaponizing-58] [Kanan Makiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanan_Makiya), an Iraqi–American academic and pro-Iraq War advocate,[^cite_note-59] criticized HRW for promising that the records proved genocide. He warned that the records contained neither "smoking guns" nor do they contain records of the "explosive nature" as HRW claimed. Furthermore, he said that certain documents that seemed incriminating could have been planted by Kurdish rebels.[^cite_note-Weaponizing-58] After the invasion of Iraq, Makiya said in December 2003 that the Iraqi document archives contained no "smoking gun" to convict Saddam Hussein of war crimes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPelletiere201662-60]

After the United States invasion of Iraq in 2003, mass graves were discovered in parts of western Iraq that had been under Ba'athist control.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Victims-7]

## Commemoration

The event has become an important element in the constitution of [Kurdish national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_national_identity).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECockrell-Abdullah201870-61] The [Kurdistan Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Regional_Government) has set aside 14 April as a day of remembrance for the Al-Anfal campaign.[^cite_note-62] In [Sulaymaniyah](653537.md) a museum was established in the [former prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amna_Suraka) of the [Directorate of General Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate_of_General_Security).[^cite_note-63]

In 2014, a [German](11867.md) organization in Sulaymaniyah agreed with [Kurdish](17068.md) artist and sculptor [Shwan Kamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwan_Kamal) to build a [monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument) and sculpture in [Beth Garmai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Garmai) region for Anfal.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

## Later perspectives

The campaign is considered a genocide by the [International Association of Genocide Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Genocide_Scholars).[^cite_note-IAGS-66] Various official bodies in Iraq have declared it a genocide, including the [Iraqi Special Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Special_Tribunal), the [Council of Representatives of Iraq](1445450.md), [Presidency Council of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_Council_of_Iraq), and [Federal Supreme Court of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Supreme_Court_of_Iraq).[^cite_note-IAGS-66] It has also been declared a genocide by the legislatures of the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-67] Norway, Sweden, and South Korea.[^cite_note-IAGS-66]

Many [Iraqi Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Arabs) reject that any mass killings of Kurds occurred during the Anfal campaign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltermann2008Interpretation_of_facts-13]
