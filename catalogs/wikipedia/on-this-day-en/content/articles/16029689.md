**Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): اللبّن الشرقية) is a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) village in the northern [West Bank](33209.md), 20 kilometers south of [Nablus](233369.md), in the [Nablus Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Governorate) of the [State of Palestine](241405.md). The town has a total land area of 12,075 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of which 200 dunams is built-up area. The village is just north of the historic Khan al-Lubban caravansary.

In 2012, the nearby village of [Ammuriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammuriya,_Nablus) was joined with Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya into one Municipal Council, also called **Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya**. The union has since been desolved[^cite_note-ARIJ5-2]

## Location

Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya is bordered by [Qaryut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaryut) and [As Sawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Sawiya) to the east, [As Sawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Sawiya), [Iskaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskaka) and [Salfit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salfit) to the north, *Khirbet Qeis* to the west, and ['Abwein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Abwein) and [Sinjil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjil) to the south.[^cite_note-3]

## History

### Iron Age through Abbasid period

Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya has been identified with [ biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) [Lebonah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebonah).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Pottery and [sherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherd) from [Iron Age II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age#Near_East_timeline), [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Hellenistic_period), [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period)/[Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Roman_period)[^cite_note-Finkelstein616-6] and [ Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Byzantine_period) period have been found,[^cite_note-Finkelstein616-6][^cite_note-7] as have sherds from the [ Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Umayyad_period)/[ Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Abbasid_period) periods.[^cite_note-Finkelstein616-6]

### Crusaders, Ayyubids, and Mamluks

The village was known as "Lubanum" to the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem),[^cite_note-Pringle-8][^cite_note-9] and sherds from the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states)/[Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) period have also been found there.[^cite_note-Finkelstein616-6]

Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahid al-Makhzumi al-Lubanni (d. 1260), a [qadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadi) (Islamic judge) in [Baalbek](58207.md), was born in the village in [AH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Hegirae) 593/1196 CE.[^cite_note-10] In the 1320s, it was marked as *Casale Lepna* on the map of [ Marino Sanuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marino_Sanuto_the_Elder).[^cite_note-Finkelstein617-11]

Sherds from the [ Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Egypt) period have also been found there.[^cite_note-Finkelstein616-6]

### Ottoman period

In 1517, the village was incorporated into the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) with the rest of [ Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)). Under the name "Lubban as-Sawi", the village appeared in the 1596 Ottoman [tax registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) as being in the *[Nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* of Jabal Qubal of the *[liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* of [Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_Nablus). It had a population of 75 [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) households. They paid a fixed tax-rate of 33.3% on agricultural products, including wheat, barley, summer crops, olives, and goats or beehives; a total of 15,454 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e). Half of the revenue went to a [Waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf).[^cite_note-12]

In the 18th and 19th centuries, the village formed part of the highland region known as Jūrat ‘Amra or Bilād Jammā‘īn. Situated between [Dayr Ghassāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayr_Ghassana) in the south and the present [Route 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_5_\(Israel%E2%80%93Palestine\)) in the north, and between [Majdal Yābā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdal_Yaba) in the west and [Jammā‘īn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamma'in), [Mardā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marda,_Salfit) and [Kifl Ḥāris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kifl_Haris) in the east, this area served, according to historian [Roy Marom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Marom), "as a buffer zone between the political-economic-social units of the [Jerusalem](16043.md) and the [Nablus](233369.md) regions. On the political level, it suffered from instability due to the migration of the [Bedouin](61770.md) tribes and the constant competition among local clans for the right to collect taxes on behalf of the [Ottoman authorities](22278.md)."[^cite_note-13]

In 1838, it was noted as *el-Lubban*, a village in the *Jurat Merda* district, south of Nablus.[^cite_note-14] It was also noted that it was inhabited, having the appearance of an old place, with rock cut tombs above it.[^cite_note-Robinson90-15] French explorer [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited the village in 1863, and found it to be in a poor state, but with beautiful old elements as part of the houses. The population was estimated to be 300.[^cite_note-16]

In 1870/1871 (AH 1288), an Ottoman census listed the village in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiyah)* (sub-district) of Jamma'in al-Thani, subordinate to Nablus.[^cite_note-17] In the 1882 [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* (SWP), the village was described as being perched on a terrace on the hill, with ancient tombs close by.[^cite_note-18]

### British Mandate

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine), conducted by the [British Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) authorities, Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya (called Lubban Sharqi) had a population of 356, all [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim),[^cite_note-Census1922-19] increasing in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to a population of 474 Muslims and one Christian, in a total of 116 houses.[^cite_note-Census1931-20]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) Lubban Sharqiya had a population of 620, all Muslims,[^cite_note-1945p18-21] with 12,545 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land, according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-22] Of this, 2,424 dunams were [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation) and irrigable land, 5,605 used for cereals,[^cite_note-23] while 34 dunams were built-up land.[^cite_note-24]

### Jordanian period

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya came under Jordanian rule. It was [annexed by Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) in 1950.

In 1961 the population of *Lubban Sharqiye* was 984.[^cite_note-25]

### 1967 war to Oslo Accords

Since the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md). The population of *Lubben Sharqiya* in the 1967 census conducted by Israel was 823, of whom 37 originated from the Israeli territory.[^cite_note-67census-26]

### Since Oslo Accords

Under the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord) of 1995, 33% of village land was classified as [Area A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) (full Palestinian control), 25% as [Area B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) (partial Palestinian control), and the remaining 40% as [Area C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_C_\(West_Bank\)) (full Israeli control). Israel has confiscated 1,144 dunums of land from Al Lubban ash Sharqiya for the establishment of the [Israeli settlements](15123.md) of [Alie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli,_Mateh_Binyamin) and [Ma’ale Levona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%E2%80%99ale_Levona), and additional land for service roads.[^cite_note-27]

On 5 November 1990 at Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya, a local villager, Ali el Khatib, aged 65 was gunned down while riding his donkey to an olive grove, and a few second later, gunfire from the same Israeli Peugeot killed Miriam Salman Rashid while she was standing outside her home. The car then sped off towards [Eli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli,_Mateh_Binyamin). On the basis of evidence collected in an intensive investigation, police concluded that it was an operation undertaken by members of the [Kach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach_and_Kahane_Chai) militant organization in retaliation for [Meir Kahane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meir_Kahane)'s murder in New York earlier that day. Three [Kach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach_\(political_party\)) activists, among them [David Ha'ivri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ha'ivri), were arrested on suspicion, but the case never came to trial due to lack of evidence.[^cite_note-28]

According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS), al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya had a population of 2,465 in the 2007 census and 2,640 by 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1][^cite_note-PCBS-29] The population is primarily made up of two clans, the Daraghmeh and Awaysa.

### [**Israeli Settler Violence**](47246246.md)

In 2009 two members of [Yesh Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yesh_Din) wrote in Haaretz about settlers from Eli who had taken control over land in the area, which had seriously damaged the ability of the villagers from Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya to work their land. According to the writers, this was part of a systematic strategy to remove all Palestinians from Area C, and the authors concluded that "an infrastructure of Jewish terror is being created in the West Bank."[^cite_note-30]

On 4 May 2010 a fire broke out in the main mosque of al-Lubban al-Sharqiyya, destroying carpets and religious texts.[^cite_note-Haaretz06-31] Police forensics officers were called in to determine whether it was arson or an electrical failure, while the [PA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) said the fire was started by settlers in a [price tag attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_tag_policy).[^cite_note-32] Israel firefighters later said the fire seemed to was deliberately set, and that the likely cause was arson.[^cite_note-Haaretz06-31] Later the same year, the olive harvest became one of the most violent for years on the West Bank, and olive trees belonging to the village, and situated near the Israeli settlement of Eli, were torched, though Eli residents say it was a "pruning fire that got out of control."[^cite_note-33] The family of Rasmia Awase found 40 olive trees they had planted two decades earlier on their plot near the settlement of Eli chopped down when they came to harvest the fruits. They blamed the destruction on Eli settlers.[^cite_note-34]

In February 2012 an [IDF](39237.md) soldier from the [Golani Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golani_Brigade), together with two women were arrested for defacing a village home with graffiti "Mohammed is a Pig". Security cameras in the village showed one of them destroying construction material.[^cite_note-35]

In March 2012 the UN published a report about the take-over by Israeli settlers of water resources on the West Bank, including the spring *Ein El Mukheimer* near al-Lubban al-Sharqiyya, traditionally used by villagers for irrigation and domestic purposes.[^cite_note-36]

According to the village head of Al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya, the villagers were only allowed near another local spring, *Ain Arik*, for a few days of the year during harvest time. The rest of the year they would be stopped by the Israeli military.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

One family, the Daraghmehs, have repeatedly complained of harassment from Israeli settlers, saying that both animals and crops have been destroyed. In response, both local and international supporters have come to their aid.[^cite_note-39]

In October 2025, [Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_and_Wall_Resistance_Commission_\(Palestine\)) reported that Israeli authorities issued a military order to seize approximately 70 dunams of privately owned land from [Qaryut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaryut), [Al-Lubban Al-Sharqiya](16029689.md), and [Al-Sawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Sawiya). The land is designated for a "buffer zone" around the nearby settlement of Eli.[^cite_note-PalestineChronicle-2026-landseizure-70dunams-40][^cite_note-ABNA24-70dunams-nearNablus-41]

&#x20;\
&#x20;On 6 January 2026, Israeli forces requisitioned an inhabited two-storey house in Al Lubban and used it as a military position for three days. During this period, the Palestinian family living in the building was confined to a single room while soldiers operated from the premises.[^cite_note-42]

On 5 July 2026, over 100 [Israeli settlers](15123.md) Lubban al-Sharqiya and [Ammuriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammuriya), vandalizing vehicles and setting fire to a café, stealing money and causing over one million [shekels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_new_shekel).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

## Khan al-Lubban



Arches of Khan al-Lubban

Between al-Lubban ash-Sharqiya and [Sinjil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjil) is the Khan al-Lubban [caravansary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravansary). The exact date of its construction is not clear, although its architectural style indicates it was built during the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)) or early [Ottoman](22278.md) eras. Large parts of its western and northern sides were restored and reconstructed in the later Ottoman period as indicated by the size and style of the stones. Factors behind its construction include its important location as a crossroads between central Palestine's major towns and the close proximity of a freshwater well.[^cite_note-AbuKhalaf-45]

In the spring of 1697, [Henry Maundrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Maundrell) stayed at the Khan first on the way south to Jerusalem,[^cite_note-Maundrell1-46] and then on the way back.[^cite_note-47] Maundrell was also the first person to identify the place as "Lebonah" (Judges 21\:19).[^cite_note-Maundrell1-46]

In 1838 [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) found the Khan "in ruins", but noted near it a "fine fountain of running water",[^cite_note-Robinson90-15] the same was found by [de Saulcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_F%C3%A9licien_de_Saulcy) in 1850.[^cite_note-48] In 1882, the Khan was also described as "ruined", but with a fine spring beneath it.[^cite_note-49]

During the British Mandate period, the authorities took advantage of its strategic position and used Khan al-Lubban as a police station. The Jordanians continued to use the complex for the same purpose following the [1948 Arab-Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab-Israeli_War). Presently, the site is open to the public and recent work has been carried out to accommodate more visitors.[^cite_note-AbuKhalaf-45]

Because of its proximity to the larger caravansary towns of [Nablus](233369.md) and [al-Bireh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bireh), Khan al-Lubban only consists of a single story, unlike most caravansaries which have two or more. The layout of Khan al-Lubban is square-shaped, with each side measuring roughly 23 meters in length. Most of the original building remains intact, with the entrance way bordered by stables on both sides and leading into a courtyard. The eastern and western rooms served administrative functions while the northern rooms served as visitor lodging.[^cite_note-AbuKhalaf-45]
