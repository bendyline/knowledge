**Beit Awwa** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بيت عوّا) is a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories) town in the southern [West Bank](33209.md), in the [Hebron Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebron_Governorate) of the [State of Palestine](241405.md), located 22 kilometres (14 mi) west of [Hebron](38577.md) and 4 kilometers west of [Dura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dura_\(town\)). According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics), Beit Awwa had a population of 10,436 inhabitants in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1]

Beit Awwa saw ruins and artificial caves noted by various visitors throughout the 19th century. Following the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), it came under [Jordanian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank), then [Israeli occupation](2781576.md) after the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967. Today, it is home to prominent clans, the Masalmea and Al Swaty.

## Geography

From the east is [Fuqeiqis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuqeiqis), from the west is the [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)), from the south is [Sikka, Hebron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikka,_Hebron), and from the north is [As Simiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Simiya).

## History

In 1838, during the [Ottoman](22278.md) era, [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) noted *Beit 'Auwa* as a place "in ruins or deserted," part of the area between the mountains and [Gaza](12047.md), but subject to the government of [el-Khulil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Khulil).[^cite_note-Robinson117-3] He further remarked that the ruins "covering low hills on both sides of the path, exhibiting foundations of hewn stones, from which all that can be inferred is, that here was once an extensive town."[^cite_note-4]

In 1863, [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited Beit Awwa, which he called *Khirbet Beit el-Haoua*. He described finding many artificial caves, some of which were large and had shaped [domes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome), other smaller with square ceilings. Most entries were surrounded by piles of stones from old demolished buildings.[^cite_note-5]

&#x20;In 1875, the [Palestine Exploration Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s [Survey of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine) visited Beit Awwa. They described several ruins, each with a different name;&#x20;

> &#x20;"Khurbet es Sueity, Khurbet el Mehami, Khurbet el Kusah are all sites with foundations and caves. El Kusr is an ancient watch-tower, with drystone walls in ruins; el Keniseh seems to be a ruined church; foundations, [capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(architecture\)), shafts, and [lintels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lintel_\(architecture\)) with the [Maltese cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_cross) on them, remain showing a [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) building. There is also a fine [font](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptismal_font) fitted for immersion. In the centre a square basin, 2 feet 3 inches side, 7 inches deep; four steps lead down, 5 inches high, 9 inches broad; the whole surrounded by four segmental recesses, the external form of the font being that of a rounded cross, the longest measurement either way being 5 feet, and the total height outside 2 feet 4 inches."[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

### British Mandate era

At the time of the [1931 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) the population of Beit Awwa was counted under [Dura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dura,_Hebron).[^cite_note-Census1931-7]

### Jordanian era

In November 1948, during the [1948 Arab-Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab-Israeli_War) soldiers from the [Israeli](9282173.md) [5th Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Givati_Brigade) attacked Beit Awwa, [Idhna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idhna) and Kh. Sikka. At Beit Awwa, the attackers were driven off.[^cite_note-8] In March 1949, the [4th Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiryati_Brigade) attacked Beit Awwa and surrounding villages. Their attack orders were to "hit every \[adult male] Arab" they encountered. All in all, they drove out 7,000 people eastward. With [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) the villagers were eventually allowed back.[^cite_note-9]

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Beit Awwa came under [Jordanian](7515964.md) rule.

In 1961, the population of Beit Awwa was 1,368.[^cite_note-10]

### 1967, aftermath

After the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Beit Awwa has been under [Israeli](9282173.md) occupation.

The population in the 1967 census conducted by the Israeli authorities was 1,468.[^cite_note-67census-11] After the 1967 six day war Beit Awwa was completely destroyed.[^cite_note-12] [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan) claimed the destruction was carried out under the orders of an officer who wished to expel the residents, Brigadier General [Uzi Narkiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzi_Narkiss) claimed the credit for the action.[^cite_note-13]

Its total land area is 470 dunams (0.47 km<sup>2</sup>; 0.18 sq mi), 30% of it lands before 1948; due to the town's proximity [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)) hundreds of dunams are a part of modern-day [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-14]

### 21st-century

On 18 March 2026, three Palestinian women [were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Beit_Awwa_salon_strike) and eight others injured by the "direct impact of missile shrapnel" on a beauty salon during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), after an earlier report said that there had been four fatalities in the incident.[^cite_note-Anadolu-15][^cite_note-16] The origin of the projectile was initially undetermined, though the Israeli military attributed the incident to an Iranian [cluster munition](79745.md);[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] this claim was not confirmed by Palestinian officials and remained independently unverified.[^cite_note-Anadolu-15] Palestinian medics said the deaths were caused by "shrapnel falling".[^cite_note-20]

## Economy

&#x20;Beit Awwa is among the Hebron Governorate locations where [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond) are cultivated.[^cite_note-21]



## Population

The two prominent clans of Beit 'Awwa are Masalmea and Al Swaty.[^cite_note-22] The Al Swaty clan relocated to the region either from [ar-Ramtha](4187988.md), [Jordan](7515964.md),[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] or, according to another account, from what is today the northern [West Bank](33209.md), passing through the [Judean Lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shephelah).[^cite_note-\:0-25] The Masalmea family originated from [Beit Jala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Jala).[^cite_note-\:0-25]
