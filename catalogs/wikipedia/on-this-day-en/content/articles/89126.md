The **East Coast of the United States**, also known as the **Eastern Seaboard**, the **Atlantic Coast**, and the **Atlantic Seaboard**, is the region encompassing the [coastline](5236.md) where the [Eastern United States](509486.md) meets the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md); it has always played a major socioeconomic role in the development of the United States.

The region is generally understood to include the [U.S. states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._states) that border the Atlantic Ocean: [Connecticut](6466.md), [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware), [Florida](18933066.md), [Georgia](48830.md), [Maine](19977.md), [Maryland](18858.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), [New Hampshire](21134.md), [New Jersey](21648.md), [New York](8210131.md), [North Carolina](21650.md), [Rhode Island](25410.md), [South Carolina](27956.md), and [Virginia](32432.md), as well as some landlocked states ([Pennsylvania](23332.md), [Vermont](32578.md), [West Virginia](32905.md), and the district of [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)).[^cite_note-2]

## Toponymy and composition

The [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy) derives from the concept that the contiguous 48 states are defined by two major coastlines, one at the [western edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) and one on the eastern edge. Other terms for referring to this area include the Eastern seaboard, which is another term for coastline, Atlantic Coast, and Atlantic Seaboard because the coastline lies along the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-3]

The 14 states that have a shoreline on the Atlantic Ocean are (from north to south) [Maine](19977.md), [New Hampshire](21134.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), [Rhode Island](25410.md), [Connecticut](6466.md), [New York](8210131.md), [New Jersey](21648.md), [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware), [Maryland](18858.md), [Virginia](32432.md), [North Carolina](21650.md), [South Carolina](27956.md), [Georgia](48830.md), and [Florida](18933066.md). [Pennsylvania](23332.md) and [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) border the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) and the [Potomac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River), respectively, both of which are [tidal arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) of the Atlantic Ocean.[^cite_note-East_Coast_States-4]

## Colonial history

Further information: [Colonial history of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_history_of_the_United_States)

The original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) in [North America](21139.md) all lay on or near the East Coast.[^cite_note-5]

Two additional U.S. states on the East Coast were not among the original Thirteen Colonies: [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Maine) became part of [Massachusetts Bay Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony) in 1677[^cite_note-6] and [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Florida) was held by the British from the end of the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War) until 1781 and was part of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) until 1821.[^cite_note-7]

In present-day Florida, Spanish explorer [Juan Ponce de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Ponce_de_Le%C3%B3n) made the first textual records of the state during his 1513 voyage. The state was initially named for Ponce de León, who called the peninsula La Pascua Florida in recognition of the verdant landscape and because it was the [Easter](9325.md) season.[^cite_note-8]

[Delaware Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Colony) and the provinces of [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_Jersey), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York), and [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) had been colonized by [the Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) as [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) until they were ceded to the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England) in the mid- to late-17th century.[^cite_note-9] From 1777 to 1791, [Vermont](32578.md) was an independent nation as the [Vermont Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermont_Republic).[^cite_note-vandewater-10]

In simplest terms, three (3) basic climate regions occur on the East Coast; 1) A cold-winter continental climate, from the US-Canadian border south to southern Rhode Island and western Maryland, 2) a temperate climate, from coastal Rhode Island south to [western North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_North_Carolina#Climate), and 3) a subtropical climate from extreme southeast Virginia south to central Florida.

The [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) region (*Dfa/Dfb/Dc*) includes Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, most of Massachusetts, most of Rhode Island, most of Connecticut, most of New York State, most of [North Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jersey), most of Pennsylvania, and [western Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Maryland). This region features warm to occasionally hot summers and cold winters with frequent snow (especially in Maine, Vermont, and New Hampshire). All locations have a least one month with a mean temperature below 0 °C (32 °F) and four to seven months averaging above 10 °C (50 °F).

The area from southern Rhode Island southward (coastal Connecticut, [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), [New York City](645042.md), most of New Jersey, most of Delaware, most of Maryland, most of Virginia, and western NC) has a warm [temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate) (*Cfa/Do*) with long, hot, humid summers and cool to cold winters with occasional snow. The mean temparature in the coldest month is 0 °C (32 °F) or higher. Normally, at least one month has a mean temperature over 22 °C (71.6 °F) and six to seven months average above 10 °C (50 °F)..

The area from the southern [Delmarva Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delmarva_Peninsula), southeast Virginia, and [central North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_North_Carolina) south to central Florida is humid [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics) (*Cfa/Cf*), with hot summers that have almost daily (but brief) thundershowers and mild and drier winters. In this zone, at least eight months have a mean temperature above 10 °C (50 °F). The region of Florida from the south-central region of the state south to the [Florida Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Keys) has a [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate) (*Af/Aw/Ar*) that is usually frost-free and warm to hot all year, and all of the 12 months of the year average above 18 °C (64.4 °F). This region of Florida is the only tropical climate in the continental United States.

The least common climate on the East Coast is the [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (*Cfb/Do*), which is only found on [Block Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island), [Nantucket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantucket), and the Outer Cape and [Chatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham,_Massachusetts) on [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod), and in areas of the southern [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains). This zone has all monthly averages between 0 and 22 °C and six to seven months above 50 °F. Although winter precipitation is more likely to fall as rain than as snow, occasional heavy snow is possible.

Although landfalls are rare, the Eastern Seaboard is susceptible to [hurricanes](8282374.md) in the Atlantic hurricane season, officially running from June 1 to November 30, although hurricanes can occur before or after these dates.[^cite_note-11] Hurricanes [Hazel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hazel), [Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hugo), [Bob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Bob), [Isabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Isabel), [Irene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irene), and [Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy), and most recently [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Florence), [Isaias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Isaias), [Henri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Henri), and [Ida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ida) are some of the more significant storms to have affected the region.

The East Coast, with the exception of Eastern Maine, is a low relief, [passive margin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_margin) coast.[^cite_note-12] It has been shaped by the [Pleistocene glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_glaciation) in the far northern areas in New England, with offshore islands such as [Nantucket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantucket), [Martha's Vineyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha's_Vineyard), [Block Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island), and [Fishers Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishers_Island,_New_York). From [northern New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jersey) southward, the coastal plain broadens southwards, separated from the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(United_States\)) region by the [Atlantic Seaboard fall line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Seaboard_fall_line) of the East Coast rivers, often marking the head of navigation and prominent sites of cities.

The coastal areas from [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island) south to Florida are often made up of [barrier islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island) that front the coastal areas, with the long stretches of sandy beaches. Many of the larger capes along the lower East Coast are in fact barrier islands, like the [Outer Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Banks) of North Carolina and [Cape Canaveral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral), Florida. The Florida Keys are made up of limestone coral and provide the only [coral reefs](87410.md) on the U.S. mainland.

## Demographics

[New York City](645042.md) is both the largest city and the largest metropolitan area on the East Coast. The East Coast is the most populated coastal area in the United States.[^cite_note-13]

## Transportation



[Philadelphia International Airport](262559.md) in [Philadelphia](50585.md) in June 2007

The primary [Interstate Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway) along the East Coast is [Interstate 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95), completed in 2018,[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-npr-15] which replaced the historic [U.S. Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_1) (Atlantic Highway), the original federal highway that traversed all East Coast states except [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware).[^cite_note-16]

By water, the East Coast is connected from the Annisquam River in [Gloucester, Massachusetts](116744.md) to [Miami, Florida](53846.md), by the [Intracoastal Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracoastal_Waterway), also known as the East Coast Canal, which was completed in 1912.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Amtrak's *[Downeaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downeaster_\(train\))* and *[Northeast Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional)* offer the main [passenger rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_rail) service on the Seaboard. The *[Acela Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acela_Express)* offers the only [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) passenger service in the Americas. Between New York and Boston, the *Acela Express* has up to a 54% share of the combined train and air passenger market.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Some of the largest airports in the United States are located along the East Coast of the United States, such as [John F. Kennedy International Airport](97218.md) in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), [New York City](645042.md), [Logan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_International_Airport) in [Boston](24437894.md), [Newark Liberty Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Liberty_Airport) in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), [Philadelphia International Airport](262559.md) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Baltimore–Washington International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore%E2%80%93Washington_International_Airport) near [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), [Dulles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport) near [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), [Hartsfield–Jackson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsfield%E2%80%93Jackson_Atlanta_International_Airport) in [Atlanta](3138.md), [Miami International Airport](128968.md) in [Miami](53846.md), [Charlotte Douglas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport) in [Charlotte, North Carolina](57447.md), [Tampa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_International_Airport) in [Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida), and [Orlando International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_International_Airport) in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida).

As the first spot in the United States that immigrants arrived and the close proximity of [Europe](9239.md), the [Caribbean](18956035.md), and [Latin America](18524.md), the East Coast is home to a diverse population and home to multi-cultures when compared to the rest of the U.S. From the strong [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) culture in [southern Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Florida), to the 200-year-old [Gullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullah) culture of the low country coastal islands of [Georgia](48830.md) and [South Carolina](27956.md), to the many historic cities in the [Mid-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)), where strong English, German, Italian, Irish, and French culture are present, the East Coast is significantly more diverse than the rest of the United States. Numerous [Chinatowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Manhattan) in [New York City](645042.md), and [Little Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Havana) in [Miami](53846.md), are examples of such cultural centers in the bigger cities.

The East Coast is home to much of the political and financial power and a center for resort and travel destinations in the United States. [New York City](645042.md) is the most populous city in the country and a major world financial center. Seventy-one of the world's Fortune 500 companies have their corporate headquarters in New York City, while [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), with 400 million square feet of office space in 2018, is the largest [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) in the world. [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) is the federal capital and political nerve center of the United States. Many organizations such as defense contractors, civilian contractors, nonprofit organizations, lobbying firms, trade unions, industry trade groups and professional associations have their headquarters in or near Washington, D.C., in order to be close to the [federal government](195149.md).

[Miami](53846.md) is one of the top domestic and international travel destinations in the United States. It is the warmest major city in the continental United States in winter, which contributes to it being a major tourism hub for international visitors. Miami has one of the largest concentrations of international banks in the United States, and the third-largest skyline in the U.S. with over 439 high-rises, 68 of which exceed 490 ft (150 m). The [Port of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Miami) is the busiest cruise port in the world in both passenger traffic and cruise lines, with over 5.5 million cruise passengers passing through the port each year. The center for tropical plant culture and research in the United States is based in Miami at [Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Tropical_Botanic_Garden). The state of Florida is the second-largest producer of [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)) in the world behind [Brazil](3383.md).
