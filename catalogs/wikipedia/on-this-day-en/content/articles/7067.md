The **Cook Islands**[^cite_note-9] is an [island country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country) in [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia), part of [Oceania](22621.md), in the [South Pacific Ocean](23070.md). While self-governing, the country is in [free association](346414.md) with [New Zealand](4913064.md). It consists of 15 islands whose total land area is approximately 236.7 square kilometres (91 sq mi). The Cook Islands [Exclusive Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_Economic_Zone) (EEZ) covers 1,960,027 square kilometres (756,771 sq mi) of ocean.[^cite_note-10] The capital is [Avarua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avarua), located on the island of [Rarotonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarotonga).

Since the start of the 21st century, the country has conducted its own independent foreign and defence policy, and also its own customs regulations.[^cite_note-11] Like most members of the Pacific Islands Forum, the country has no armed forces, but the Cook Islands Police Service owns a Guardian Class Patrol Boat, [CIPPB *Te Kukupa II*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIPPB_Te_Kukupa_II), provided by Australia, for policing its waters. In recent decades, the Cook Islands has adopted an increasingly assertive and distinct foreign policy, and a Cook Islander, [Henry Puna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Puna), served as Secretary General of the [Pacific Islands Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum) from 2021 to 2024.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Most [Cook Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islanders) have [New Zealand citizenship](2316836.md), plus the status of Cook Islands nationals, which is not given to other New Zealand citizens. The Cook Islands have been an active member of the [Pacific Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Community), formerly the South Pacific Commission, since 1980.

The Cook Islands' main population centres are on Rarotonga (10,863 in 2021),[^cite_note-\:3-5] also the location of [Rarotonga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarotonga_International_Airport), the main international gateway to the country. The census of 2021 put the total population at 14,987. There is also a larger population of Cook Islanders in New Zealand and Australia: in the [2018 New Zealand census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_New_Zealand_census), 80,532 people said they were Cook Islanders, or of Cook Islands descent.[^cite_note-14] The last Australian census recorded 28,000 Cook Islanders living in Australia, many with Australian citizenship.[^cite_note-15] With over 168,000 visitors to the islands in 2018,[^cite_note-16] tourism is the country's main industry and leading element of its economy, ahead of [offshore banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_banking), pearls, and marine and fruit exports.

## Etymology

The Cook Islands comprise 15 islands that have had individual names in indigenous languages, including [Cook Islands Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_M%C4%81ori) and [Pukapukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapukan_language) throughout the time they have been inhabited. The first name given by Europeans was *Gente Hermosa* (beautiful people) by Spanish explorers to [Rakahanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakahanga) in 1606.[^cite_note-Hooker_1998_54–62-17]

The islands as a whole are named after the English captain and explorer [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook), who visited during the 1770s and named [Manuae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuae_\(Cook_Islands\)) "Hervey Island" after [Augustus Hervey, 3rd Earl of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_Hervey,_3rd_Earl_of_Bristol). The southern island group became known as the "Hervey Islands" after this. In the 1820s, Russian Admiral [Adam Johann von Krusenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Johann_von_Krusenstern) referred to the southern islands as the "Cook Islands" in his *Atlas de l'Ocean Pacifique*.[^cite_note-Hist1-18] The entire territory (including the northern island group) was not known as the "Cook Islands" until after its annexation by New Zealand in the early 20th century. In 1901, the New Zealand parliament passed the *Cook and other Islands Government Act*, demonstrating that the name "Cook Islands" only referred to some of the islands. This situation had changed by the passage of the *Cook Islands Act 1915*, which defined the Cooks' area and included all presently included islands.[^cite_note-19]

The islands' official name in Cook Islands Māori is *Kūki 'Āirani*, a transliteration of the English name.[^cite_note-telegraphname-20][^cite_note-radionzname-21]

## History

Main article: [History of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Cook_Islands)

### Precolonial period

The Cook Islands were first settled around AD 1000[^cite_note-22] by [Polynesian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_people) who are thought to have migrated from [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti),[^cite_note-23] an island 1,154 kilometres (717 mi; 623 nmi) to the northeast of the main island of [Rarotonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarotonga).

### Early European contact

The first European contact with the islands took place in 1595 when the Spanish navigator [Álvaro de Mendaña de Neira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_de_Menda%C3%B1a_de_Neira) sighted the island of [Pukapuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapuka), which he named *San Bernardo* (Saint Bernard). [Pedro Fernandes de Queirós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Fernandes_de_Queir%C3%B3s), a Portuguese captain at the service of the [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_monarchy), made the first European landing in the islands when he set foot on [Rakahanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakahanga) in 1606, calling the island *Gente Hermosa* (Beautiful People).[^cite_note-Hooker_1998_54–62-17]

British explorer and naval officer Captain [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook) arrived in 1773 and again in 1777,[^cite_note-24] giving the island of [Manuae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuae_\(Cook_Islands\)) the name *Hervey Island*. The *Hervey Islands* later came to be applied to the entire southern group. The name "Cook Islands", in honour of Cook, first appeared on a Russian naval chart published by [Adam Johann von Krusenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Johann_von_Krusenstern) in the 1820s.[^cite_note-Hist1-18]

In 1813 the crew on the colonial brig *Endeavour* (not the same ship as Cook's) made the first recorded European sighting of Rarotonga.[^cite_note-History-25] The first recorded landing on Rarotonga by Europeans was in 1814 by the *Cumberland*; trouble broke out between the sailors and the Islanders and many were killed on both sides.[^cite_note-History-25] The islands saw no more Europeans until English missionaries arrived in 1821. Christianity quickly took hold in the culture and many islanders are Christians today.[^cite_note-\:2-26]

The islands were a popular stop in the 19th century for [whaling](33557.md) ships from the United States, Britain and Australia. They visited, from at least 1826, to obtain water, food, and firewood.[^cite_note-27] Their favourite islands were Rarotonga, [Aitutaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitutaki), [Mangaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangaia) and [Penrhyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrhyn_atoll).

### British protectorate and annexation by New Zealand

The [Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Rarotonga) became a British protectorate in 1888, following a petition from Queen [Makea Takau Ariki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makea_Takau_Ariki), largely because of the fear of British residents that France might occupy the islands, as it already had Tahiti.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-PIM1933-5-29] On 6 September 1900, the islanders' leaders presented a petition asking that the islands (including [Niue](21228.md) "if possible") should be annexed as British territory.[^cite_note-Kenneth_Roberts-Wray_1966._P._891-30][^cite_note-31] On 8 and 9 October 1900, seven instruments of cession of Rarotonga and other islands were signed by their chiefs and people. A British Proclamation was issued, stating that the cessions were accepted and the islands declared parts of Her Britannic Majesty's dominions.[^cite_note-Kenneth_Roberts-Wray_1966._P._891-30] However, it did not include Aitutaki. Even though the inhabitants regarded themselves as British subjects, the Crown's title was unclear until the island was formally annexed by that Proclamation.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] In 1901 the islands were included within the boundaries of the [Colony of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_Zealand) by Order in Council[^cite_note-34] under the Colonial Boundaries Act, 1895 of the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-Kenneth_Roberts-Wray_1966._P._891-30][^cite_note-35] The boundary change became effective on 11 June 1901, and the Cook Islands have had a formal relationship with New Zealand since that time.[^cite_note-Kenneth_Roberts-Wray_1966._P._891-30]

The Cook Islands responded to the call for service when [World War I](4764461.md) began, immediately sending five contingents, close to 500 men, to the war. The island's young men volunteered at the outbreak of the war to reinforce the Māori Contingents and the Australian and New Zealand Mounted Rifles. A Patriotic Fund was set up very quickly, raising funds to support the war effort. The Cook Islanders were trained at Narrow Neck Camp in [Devonport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonport,_New_Zealand), and the first recruits departed on 13 October 1915 on the SS *Te Anau*. The ship arrived in Egypt just as the New Zealand units were about to be transferred to the Western Front. In September 1916, the Pioneer Battalion, a combination of Cook Islanders, Māori and Pakeha soldiers, saw heavy action in the Allied attack on Flers, the first battle of the Somme. Three Cook Islanders from this first contingent died from enemy action, and at least ten died of disease as they struggled to adapt to the conditions in Europe. The 2nd and 3rd Cook Island Contingents were part of the [Sinai-Palestine campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_and_Palestine_campaign), first in a logistical role for the Australian and New Zealand Mounted Rifles at their Moascar base and later in ammunition supply for the Royal Artillery. After the war, the men returned to the outbreak of the influenza epidemic in New Zealand, and this, along with European diseases meant that a large number did not survive and died in New Zealand or on their return home over the coming years.[^cite_note-36]

### Self government

When the [British Nationality and New Zealand Citizenship Act 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nationality_and_New_Zealand_Citizenship_Act_1948) came into effect on 1 January 1949, Cook Islanders who were [British subjects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_subject) automatically gained [New Zealand citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_citizenship).[^cite_note-37] The islands remained a New Zealand dependent territory until the New Zealand Government decided to grant them self-governing status. On 4 August 1965, a constitution was promulgated. The first Monday in August is celebrated each year as [Constitution Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Cook_Islands).[^cite_note-38] [Albert Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Henry_\(politician\)) of the [Cook Islands Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Party) was elected as the first Premier and was knighted by [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II).[^cite_note-\:2-26] Henry led the nation until 1978, when he was accused of vote-rigging and resigned. He was stripped of his knighthood in 1979.[^cite_note-\:2-26] He was succeeded by [Tom Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Davis_\(Cook_Islands\)) of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Cook_Islands\)), who held that position until March 1983.[^cite_note-39]

On 13 July 2017, the Cook Islands established [Marae Moana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marae_Moana), making it become the world's largest [protected area](100406.md) by size.[^cite_note-40]

In March 2019, it was reported that the Cook Islands had plans to change its name and remove the reference to Captain James Cook in favour of "a title that reflects its 'Polynesian nature'".[^cite_note-telegraphname-20][^cite_note-radionzname-21] It was later reported in May 2019 that the proposed name change had been poorly received by the Cook Islands diaspora. As a compromise, it was decided that the English name of the islands would not be altered, but that a new Cook Islands Māori name would be adopted to replace the current name, a transliteration from English.[^cite_note-41] Discussions over the name continued in 2020.[^cite_note-42]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Cook_Islands)



Map of the Cook Islands

The Cook Islands are in the South Pacific Ocean, north-east of New Zealand, between [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa) and [French Polynesia](10737.md). There are 15 major islands spread over 2,200,000 km<sup>2</sup> (850,000 sq mi) of ocean, divided into two distinct groups: the [Southern Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Cook_Islands#Southern_Cook_Islands) and the [Northern Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Cook_Islands) of [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral) [atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll).[^cite_note-43]

The islands were formed by [volcanic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism); the northern group is older and consists of six atolls, which are sunken volcanoes topped by coral growth. The climate is [moderate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) to [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate). The Cook Islands consist of 15 islands and two reefs. From March to December, the Cook Islands are in the path of tropical cyclones, the most notable of which were the cyclones [Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Martin_\(1997\)) and [Percy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Percy).[^cite_note-44] Two [terrestrial ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion#Terrestrial) lie within the islands' territory: the [Central Polynesian tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Polynesian_tropical_moist_forests) and the [Cook Islands tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_tropical_moist_forests).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-45]

### Biodiversity



*[Tiare māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardenia_taitensis)*, the [national flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_flower) of the Cook Islands

The national flower of the Cook Islands is the *[tiare māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardenia_taitensis)* or *tiale māoli* (Penrhyn, Nassau, Pukapuka).[^cite_note-47]

The Cook Islands have a large population of non-native [ship rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_rat)[^cite_note-48] and *kiore toka* ([Polynesian rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat)).[^cite_note-49] The rats have dramatically reduced the bird population on the islands.[^cite_note-50]

In April 2007, 27 [Kuhl's lorikeets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuhl's_lorikeet) were re-introduced to [Atiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiu) from [Rimatara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimatara). Fossil and oral traditions indicate that the species was formerly on at least five islands of the southern group. Excessive exploitation for its red feathers is the most likely reason for the species' extinction in the Cook Islands.[^cite_note-51]

The islands' surrounding waters are the home of the [peppermint angelfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppermint_angelfish). While they are common, due to the difficulty of harvesting them, they are one of the most expensive marine aquarium fish with a price of US\$30,000.[^cite_note-52]

## Politics and foreign relations

Main articles: [Politics of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Cook_Islands) and [Foreign relations of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_Cook_Islands)

Further information: [Political status of the Cook Islands and Niue](44167788.md)



The parliament building of the Cook Islands, formerly a hotel



Prime Minister [Henry Puna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Puna) with U.S. secretary of state [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) in Rarotonga, 31 August 2012

The Cook Islands are a [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) with a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system)[^cite_note-53] in an [associated state](346414.md) relationship with New Zealand. [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) is exercised by the government, with the Prime Minister as [head of government](37570.md). [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) is vested in both the government and the [Parliament of the Cook Islands](4504372.md). While the country is de jure unicameral, there are two legislative bodies with the [House of Ariki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Ariki) acting as a *de facto* upper house.

There is a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is independent of the executive and the legislature. The [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) is the [King of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_New_Zealand), who is [represented in the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_in_the_Cook_Islands) by the [King's Representative](2369963.md).[^cite_note-54]

The islands are self-governing in "free association" with New Zealand. Under the Cook Islands constitution, New Zealand cannot pass laws for the Cook Islands. Rarotonga has its own foreign service and diplomatic network. Cook Islands nationals have the right to become citizens of New Zealand and can receive New Zealand government services when in New Zealand, but the reverse is not true; New Zealand citizens are not Cook Islands nationals. Despite this, as of 2018, the Cook Islands had diplomatic relations in its own name with 52 other countries. The Cook Islands is not a United Nations member state, but, along with [Niue](21228.md), has had their "full treaty-making capacity" recognised by the [United Nations Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretariat),[^cite_note-art102-55][^cite_note-map-56] and is a full member of the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO), [UNESCO](21786641.md), the [International Civil Aviation Organization](14985.md), the [International Maritime Organization](14986.md) and the UN [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization), all [UN specialised agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_specialized_agencies_of_the_United_Nations), and is an associate member of the [United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_and_Social_Commission_for_Asia_and_the_Pacific) (UNESCAP) and a Member of the Assembly of States of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md).

On 11 June 1980, the United States signed [a treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands%E2%80%93United_States_Maritime_Boundary_Treaty) with the Cook Islands specifying the [maritime border](29613061.md) between the Cook Islands and [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa) and also relinquishing any American claims to [Penrhyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrhyn_atoll), [Pukapuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapuka), [Manihiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manihiki), and [Rakahanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakahanga).[^cite_note-57] In 1990 the Cook Islands and France signed [a treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands%E2%80%93France_Maritime_Delimitation_Agreement) that [delimited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_boundary_delimitation) the boundary between the Cook Islands and [French Polynesia](10737.md).[^cite_note-58] In late August 2012, [United States Secretary of State](32293.md) [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) visited the islands. In 2017, the Cook Islands signed the UN [Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-59] On 25 September 2023, the Cook Islands and the United States of America established [diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands%E2%80%93United_States_relations) under the leadership of Prime Minister [Mark Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Brown_\(Cook_Islands\)) at a ceremony in Washington, DC.[^cite_note-60]

In 2024, the Cook Islands' efforts to join the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) as a full member were "ongoing" but, despite this, the government was unable to secure an invitation to attend the [2024 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting) in Samoa.[^cite_note-61]

In 2025, prime minister Mark Brown said that the Cook Islands did not meet the requirements for UN membership and foreign minister [Tingika Elikana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingika_Elikana) said that any decision to join the UN would require a referendum and re-evaluation of the relationship with New Zealand. Brown also confirmed that at the Commonwealth of Nations the Cook Islands is considered to be represented by the [Realm of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realm_of_New_Zealand), meaning that they would not have their own separate representation unless they become fully sovereign.[^cite_note-62] Additionally, in response to a push to introduce Cook Island passports and agreements made with China, a spokesperson for New Zealand foreign minister [Winston Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Peters) stated, "Unlike [Samoa](27238.md), [Tonga](30158.md) and [Tuvalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvalu), the Cook Islands is not a fully independent and sovereign state", unless its status and relationship with New Zealand are changed by referendum.[^cite_note-63]

### Defence and police

The [Cook Islands Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Police_Service) polices the waters, and shares responsibility for defence with New Zealand, in consultation with the Cook Islands Government and at its request. The total offshore EEZ is about 2 million square kilometres.[^cite_note-64] Vessels of the [Royal New Zealand Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Navy) can be employed for this task including its [Protector-class offshore patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protector-class_offshore_patrol_vessel).[^cite_note-65] These naval forces may also be supported by [Royal New Zealand Air Force](190903.md) aircraft, including [P-8 Poseidons](731169.md).[^cite_note-66] However, these forces are limited in size and in 2023 were described by the Government as "not in a fit state" to respond to regional challenges.[^cite_note-67] New Zealand's subsequently announced "Defence Policy and Strategy Statement" noted that shaping the security environment, "focusing in particular on supporting security in and for the Pacific" would receive enhanced attention.[^cite_note-68]

The Cook Islands Police Service is the police force of the Cook Islands. The Maritime Wing of the Police Service exercises sovereignty over the nation's EEZ. Vessels have included a [Pacific-class patrol boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific-class_patrol_boat), [CIPPB *Te Kukupa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIPPB_Te_Kukupa) commissioned in May 1989 which received a re-fit in 2015[^cite_note-69] but was withdrawn from service and replaced by a larger and more capable [Guardian-class patrol boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian-class_patrol_boat), [CIPPB *Te Kukupa II*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIPPB_Te_Kukupa_II), which entered service in 2022.[^cite_note-70] Cook Islands has its own customs regulations.[^cite_note-71]

### Human rights

Further information: [Human rights in the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_Cook_Islands) and [LGBTQ rights in the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_the_Cook_Islands)

Formerly, male homosexuality was *de jure* illegal in the Cook Islands and was punishable by a maximum term of seven years imprisonment; however, the law was never enforced.[^cite_note-72] In 2023, legislation was passed which legalised homosexuality.[^cite_note-73]

There are island councils, each headed by a mayor, on all of the inhabited outer islands (Outer Islands Local Government Act 1987 with amendments up to 2004, and Palmerston Island Local Government Act 1993) except [Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_\(Cook_Islands\)), which is governed by [Pukapuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapuka) ([Suwarrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwarrow), with only one caretaker living on the island, also governed by Pukapuka, is not counted with the inhabited islands in this context).

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Cook_Islands)

See also: [Transport in the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_Cook_Islands)

The economy is strongly affected by geography. It is isolated from foreign markets, and has some inadequate infrastructure; it lacks major natural resources except for significant seabed critical minerals, has limited manufacturing and suffers moderately from natural disasters.[^cite_note-77] Tourism provides the economic base that makes up approximately 67.5% of GDP. Additionally, the economy is supported by [foreign aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_aid), largely from New Zealand. China has also contributed foreign aid, which has resulted in, among other projects, the Police Headquarters building.[^cite_note-78]

|  |
| --- |

[Rarotonga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarotonga_International_Airport) is the only airport that receives international flights. Eight airports on other islands provide local or charter services. Only Rarotonga and [Aitutaki Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitutaki_Airport) have paved runways; the others have coral runways.[^cite_note-80] The main seaport and the only one where container ships can berth is [Rarotonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarotonga)'s port of [Avatiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatiu). At the port of [Arutanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arutanga) at [Aitutaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitutaki), ships anchor outside the reef and cargo is carried to the dock by [lighter barge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighter_\(barge\)).[^cite_note-LCA-81]

Since about 1989, the Cook Islands has specialised in so-called [asset protection trusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset-protection_trusts#Cook_Islands) through its International Trusts Act. This allows investors to shelter assets from the reach of creditors and legal authorities.[^cite_note-flyspeck-82][^cite_note-83] According to *The New York Times*, the Cooks have "laws devised to protect foreigners' assets from legal claims in their home countries", which were apparently crafted specifically to thwart the reach of American justice; creditors must travel to the Cook Islands and argue their cases under Cooks law, often at prohibitive expense.[^cite_note-flyspeck-82] Unlike other foreign jurisdictions such as the [British Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Virgin_Islands), the [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands) and Switzerland, the Cooks "generally disregard foreign court orders" and do not require that bank accounts, real estate, or other assets protected from scrutiny (it is illegal to disclose names or any information about Cooks trusts) be physically located within the archipelago.[^cite_note-flyspeck-82] Taxes on trusts and trust employees account for some 8% of the Cook Islands economy, behind tourism but ahead of fishing.[^cite_note-flyspeck-82]

In 2019, the Cook Islands passed the Sea Bed Minerals (SBM) Act to manage the seabed minerals located in the [Exclusive Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_Economic_Zone) surrounding the islands.[^cite_note-sbma-84] In 2022, the SBMA granted three exploration licences for polymetallic nodules to three private companies, including one co-owned by the government.[^cite_note-Carstens-85] In 2025, the Cook Islands signed seabed mineral exploration agreements with the United States and China.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-Inayatullah-87]

### Religion



[CICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Christian_Church) church in Avarua, Rarotonga

Main article: [Religion in the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Cook_Islands)

In the Cook Islands, there is [separation between the church and the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state), and most of the population is Christian.[^cite_note-\:0-90]

Various [Protestant groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) account for 62.8% of the believers, the most followed denomination being the [Cook Islands Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Christian_Church) with 49.1%. Other Protestant Christian groups include Seventh-day Adventist 7.9%, Assemblies of God 3.7% and Apostolic Church 2.1%. The main non-Protestant group are [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Rarotonga), with 17% of the population. [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) makes up 4.4%. "None" or "unspecified" account for 15.6% of the population.[^cite_note-\:0-90]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Cook_Islands)

### Language



Float parade during the annual Maeva Nui celebrations

The languages of the Cook Islands include English, [Cook Islands Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_M%C4%81ori) (or "Rarotongan"), and [Pukapukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapukan_language). Dialects of Cook Islands Māori include [Penrhyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrhyn_language); [Rakahanga-Manihiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakahanga-Manihiki_language); the [Ngaputoru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nga-Pu-Toru) dialect of [Atiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiu), [Mitiaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitiaro), and [Mauke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauke); the [Aitutaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitutaki) dialect; and the [Mangaian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangaia) dialect. Cook Islands Māori and its dialectic variants are closely related to both [Tahitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_language) and to New Zealand [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language). [Pukapukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapukan) is considered closely related to the [Samoan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoan_language). English and Cook Islands Māori are [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of the Cook Islands, per the [Te Reo Maori Act](http://www.paclii.org/ck/legis/num_act/trma2003130/). The legal definition of Cook Islands Māori includes Pukapukan.[^cite_note-91]

### Art

Main article: [Cook Islands art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_art)

#### Traditional arts



Confiscation and destruction of idol gods by European missionaries in Rarotonga, 1837

[Woodcarving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodcarving) is a common art form in the Cook Islands. The proximity of islands in the southern group helped produce a homogeneous style of carving but that had special developments in each island. Rarotonga is known for its fisherman's gods and staff-gods, [Atiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiu) for its wooden seats, [Mitiaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitiaro), [Mauke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauke) and Atiu for mace and slab gods and [Mangaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangaia) for its ceremonial adzes. Most of the original wood carvings were either spirited away by early European collectors or were burned in large numbers by missionaries. Today, carving is no longer the major art form with the same spiritual and cultural emphasis given to it by the Maori in New Zealand. However, there are continual efforts to interest young people in their heritage and some good work is being turned out under the guidance of older carvers. Atiu, in particular, has a strong tradition of crafts both in carving and local fibre arts such as tapa. Mangaia is the source of many fine adzes carved in a distinctive, idiosyncratic style with the so-called double-k design. Mangaia also produces food pounders carved from the heavy calcite found in its extensive limestone caves.[^cite_note-92]

The outer islands produce traditional [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) of mats, basketware and hats. Particularly fine examples of rito hats are worn by women to church. They are made from the uncurled immature fibre of the coconut palm and are of very high quality. The Polynesian equivalent of Panama hats, they are highly valued and are keenly sought by Polynesian visitors from Tahiti. Often, they are decorated with hatbands made of minuscule pupu shells that are painted and stitched on by hand. Although pupu are found on other islands the collection and use of them in decorative work has become a speciality of Mangaia. The weaving of rito is a speciality of the northern islands, Manihiki, Rakahanga and Penrhyn.[^cite_note-93]

A major art form in the Cook Islands is [tivaevae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivaevae). This is, in essence, the art of handmade Island scenery patchwork quilts. Introduced by the wives of missionaries in the 19th century, the craft grew into a communal activity, which is probably one of the main reasons for its popularity.[^cite_note-94]

#### Contemporary art

The Cook Islands has produced internationally recognised contemporary artists, especially from the main island of Rarotonga. Artists include painter (and photographer) [Mahiriki Tangaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahiriki_Tangaroa), sculptors [Eruera (Ted) Nia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruera_\(Ted\)_Nia?action=edit\&redlink=1) (originally a film maker) and master carver [Mike Tavioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tavioni), painter (and Polynesian tattoo enthusiast) Upoko'ina Ian George, Aitutakian-born painter Tim Manavaroa Buchanan, Loretta Reynolds, Judith Kunzlé, [Joan Gragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Gragg), Kay George (who is also known for her fabric designs), Apii Rongo, Varu Samuel, and multi-media, installation and community-project artist [Ani O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani_O'Neill), all of whom currently live on Rarotonga. Atiuan-based [Andrea Eimke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Eimke?action=edit\&redlink=1) is an artist who works in the medium of tapa and other textiles, and also co-authored the book *Tivaivai – The Social Fabric of the Cook Islands* with British academic [Susanne Kuechler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Kuechler). Many of these artists have studied at university art schools in New Zealand and continue to enjoy close links with the New Zealand art scene.[^cite_note-95]

New Zealand-based Cook Islander artists include [Michel Tuffery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Tuffery), print-maker David Teata, Richard Shortland Cooper, [Nina Oberg Humphries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Oberg_Humphries), [Sylvia Marsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Marsters) and [Jim Vivieaere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Vivieaere).

[Bergman Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergman_Gallery) (formerly BCA Gallery) is the main commercial dealer gallery in the Cook Islands, situated in Rarotonga, and represents Cook Islands artists such as [Sylvia Marsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Marsters), [Mahiriki Tangaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahiriki_Tangaroa), [Nina Oberg Humphries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Oberg_Humphries), [Joan Gragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Gragg) and [Tungane Broadbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungane_Broadbent).[^cite_note-96] The Art Studio Gallery in Arorangi was run by [Ian George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_George_\(artist\)) and [Kay George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kay_George) and is now Beluga Cafe.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] There is also Gallery Tavioni and Vananga run by [Mike Tavioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tavioni),[^cite_note-99] and the [Cook Islands National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_National_Museum) exhibits art.[^cite_note-100]

### Music

Main article: [Music of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Cook_Islands)



A [CICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Christian_Church) congregationalist church

Music in the Cook Islands is varied, with Christian songs being quite popular, but traditional dancing and songs in Cook Islands Maori and Pukapukan remain popular.

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_the_Cook_Islands)

The Cook Islands have competed at the [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md) since [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics), without winning a medal.[^cite_note-ONOC_2024-101] [Rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) is the most popular sport and the national sport of the country.[^cite_note-102]

### Newspapers

Newspapers in the Cook Islands are usually published in English with some articles in [Cook Islands Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_M%C4%81ori).[^cite_note-\:1-103] The *[Cook Islands News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_News)* has been published since 1945, although it was owned by the government until 1989.[^cite_note-104] Former newspapers include [Te Akatauira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Akatauira?action=edit\&redlink=1), which was published from 1978 to 1980.[^cite_note-\:1-103]

### Television

Main article: [Television in the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_the_Cook_Islands)

Television was introduced on 25 December 1989. [Cook Islands Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Television) is a private (public until 1996) television station and is available in Rarotonga, where it competes with [Vaka Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaka_Television). The channel is owned by the Pitt Media Group which formerly included [TV Niue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Niue).[^cite_note-105] Other islands have their own television stations; subscription television is also available.
