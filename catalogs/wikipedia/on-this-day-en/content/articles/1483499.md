# Surface-to-surface missile

A **surface-to-surface missile** (**SSM**) is a [missile](19594.md) designed to be launched from the ground or the sea and strike targets on land or at sea. They may be fired from hand-held or vehicle mounted devices, from fixed installations, or from a ship. They are often powered by a [rocket engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine) or sometimes fired by an explosive charge, since the launching platform is typically stationary or moving slowly. They usually have fins and/or wings for [lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_\(force\)) and stability, although hyper-velocity or short-ranged missiles may use body lift or fly a ballistic trajectory.[^cite_note-1] The first operational surface-to-surface missile was the [V-1 flying bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb), which was powered by a [pulsejet engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsejet_engine).

Contemporary surface-to-surface missiles are usually [guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile). An unguided surface-to-surface missile is usually referred to as a [rocket](26301.md) (for example, an [RPG-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPG-7) or [M72 LAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M72_LAW) is an [anti-tank rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_rocket)), whereas a [BGM-71 TOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGM-71_TOW) or [AT-2 Swatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT-2_Swatter) is an [anti-tank guided missile](37517.md).

Examples of surface-to-surface missile include the [MGM-140 ATACMS](147015.md)[^cite_note-2] and the [Scud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile) family of missiles.[^cite_note-3]

## Types

There are a wide variety of surface-to-surface missiles. They can be categorized by their intended usage, intended target (such as [anti-ship](140725.md)), flight profile, and launch platform. These categorizations often overlap. These types of missiles may be launched from fixed [silos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_silo), [road-mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transporter_erector_launcher) vehicle, railcar, or naval launch platforms.

[Cruise missiles](6590.md) travel at lower speeds and trajectories (often a few meters above ground), always within the atmosphere, and their motor burns during the entire flight. [Ballistic missiles](38025.md) travel at higher speeds and trajectories with a short powered flight (boost phase) followed by a period of typically unpowered flight often exiting the atmosphere (midcourse phase), followed by a high speed unpowered terminal re-entry. They are typically classified by range band, from shortest to longest:

- [Short-range ballistic missile](1320069.md) (SRBM): Range less than 1,000 km.

  [^cite_note-inventories-6]

  [^cite_note-CRS2022-7]

- [Medium-range ballistic missile](478272.md) (MRBM): Range between 1,000 km and 3,000 km.

  [^cite_note-inventories-6]

- [Intermediate-range ballistic missile](337326.md) (IRBM): Range between 3,000 km and 5,500 km.

  [^cite_note-inventories-6]

- [Intercontinental ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBM): Range greater than 5,500 km.
