# Cryptocurrency exchange

A **cryptocurrency exchange**, or a **digital currency exchange** (**DCE**), is a business that allows customers to trade [cryptocurrencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrencies) or [digital currencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_currencies) for other assets, such as conventional [fiat money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_money) or other digital currencies. Exchanges may accept credit card payments, wire transfers or other forms of payment in exchange for digital currencies or cryptocurrencies. A cryptocurrency exchange can be a [market maker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_maker) that typically takes the [bid–ask spreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bid%E2%80%93ask_spread) as a transaction commission for its service or, as a matching platform, simply charges fees.

Some brokerages which also focus on other assets such as stocks, let users purchase but not withdraw cryptocurrencies to [cryptocurrency wallets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_wallet) while dedicated cryptocurrency exchanges do allow cryptocurrency withdrawals.

## Operation

A cryptocurrency exchange can typically send cryptocurrency to a user's personal [cryptocurrency wallet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_wallet). Some can convert digital currency balances into anonymous prepaid cards which can be used to withdraw funds from [ATMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_teller_machine) worldwide[^cite_note-ndic2008-1][^cite_note-sood2013-2] while other digital currencies are backed by real-world commodities such as gold.[^cite_note-ByrnesMunro2013-3]

The creators of digital currencies are typically independent of the digital currency exchange that facilitate trading in the currency.[^cite_note-sood2013-2] In one type of system, digital currency providers (DCP) are businesses that keep and administer accounts for their customers, but generally do not issue digital currency to those customers directly.[^cite_note-secp-4][^cite_note-Hesterman2013-5] Customers buy or sell digital currency from digital currency exchanges, who transfer the digital currency into or out of the customer's DCP account.[^cite_note-Hesterman2013-5] Some exchanges are subsidiaries of DCP, but many are legally independent businesses.[^cite_note-secp-4] The denomination of funds kept in DCP accounts may be of a real or fictitious currency.[^cite_note-Hesterman2013-5]

A digital currency exchange can be a brick-and-mortar business or a strictly online business. As a brick-and-mortar business, it exchanges traditional payment methods and digital currencies. As an online business, it exchanges electronically transferred money and digital currencies.[^cite_note-secp-4]

Often, the digital currency exchanges operate outside the Western countries to avoid regulation and prosecution. However, they do handle Western fiat currencies and maintain bank accounts in several countries to facilitate deposits in various national currencies.[^cite_note-ndic2008-1][^cite_note-sood2013-2]

[Decentralized exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_exchange) such as Etherdelta, IDEX and HADAX do not store users' funds on the exchange, but instead facilitate [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer) cryptocurrency trading. Decentralized exchanges are resistant to [security problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_and_security) that affect other exchanges, but as of mid 2018 suffer from low trading volumes.[^cite_note-6]

## History

### 2004–2008 Pre crypto regulatory issues

In 2004 three [Australian](4689264.md)-based digital currency exchange businesses voluntarily shut down following an investigation by the [Australian Securities and Investments Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Securities_and_Investments_Commission) (ASIC). The ASIC viewed the services offered as legally requiring an [Australian Financial Services License](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Financial_Services_Licence), which the companies lacked.[^cite_note-asic2004-7]

In 2006, U.S.-based digital currency exchange business [Gold Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Age) Inc., a New York state business, was shut down by the U.S. Secret Service after operating since 2002.[^cite_note-8] Business operators [Arthur Budovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Budovsky) and Vladimir Kats were indicted "on charges of operating an illegal digital currency exchange and money transmittal business" from their apartments, transmitting more than \$30 million to digital currency accounts.[^cite_note-Hesterman2013-5] Customers provided limited identity documentation, and could transfer funds to anyone worldwide, with fees sometimes exceeding \$100,000.[^cite_note-Hesterman2013-5] Budovsky and Kats were sentenced in 2007 to five years in prison "for engaging in the business of [transmitting money without a license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_transmitter), a felony violation of state banking law", ultimately receiving sentences of five years' probation.[^cite_note-boddiger2013-9]

In April 2007, the U.S. government ordered [E-Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-Gold) administration to lock/block approximately 58 E-Gold accounts owned and used by The Bullion Exchange, AnyGoldNow, [IceGold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IceGold?action=edit\&redlink=1), GitGold, The Denver Gold Exchange, GoldPouch Express, [1MDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1MDC) (a [Digital Gold Currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_gold_currency), based on [e-gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-gold)) and others, forcing G\&SR (owner of OmniPay) to liquidate the seized assets.

A few weeks later, E-Gold faced four indictments.[^cite_note-10]

### 2008–2014 advent of crypto currency

Following the launch of a decentralized [cryptocurrency](36662188.md) [bitcoin](28249265.md) in 2008 and the subsequent introduction of other cryptocurrencies, many virtual platforms were created specifically for the exchange of decentralized cryptocurrencies. Their regulation differs from country to country.

In July 2008, [WebMoney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebMoney) changed its rules, affecting many exchanges. Since that time it became prohibited to exchange WebMoney to the most popular e-currencies like E-gold, [Liberty Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Reserve) and others.

Also in July 2008 E-gold's three directors accepted a bargain with the prosecutors and pleaded guilty to one count of "conspiracy to engage in money laundering" and one count of the "operation of an unlicensed money transmitting business".[^cite_note-guilty-11] E-gold ceased operations in 2009.

In 2013, Jean-Loup Richet, a research fellow at [ESSEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESSEC) ISIS, surveyed new [money laundering](19390.md) techniques that [cybercriminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_criminals) were using in a report written for the [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime).[^cite_note-Jean-Loup_Richet-12] A common approach to cyber money laundering was to use a digital currency exchanger service which converted dollars into [Liberty Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Reserve) and could be sent and received anonymously. The receiver could convert the Liberty Reserve currency back into cash for a small fee. In May 2013, digital currency exchanger Liberty Reserve was shut down after the alleged founder, Arthur Budovsky Belanchuk, and four others were arrested in Costa Rica, Spain, and New York "under charges for conspiracy to commit money laundering and conspiracy and operation of an unlicensed money transmitting business."[^cite_note-dhs2013-13] Budovsky, a former U.S. citizen and naturalized Costa Rican, was convicted in connection with the 2006 Gold Age raid.[^cite_note-boddiger2013-9] More than \$40 million in assets were placed under restraint pending [forfeiture](26263777.md), and more than 30 Liberty Reserve exchanger [domain names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_name) were seized.[^cite_note-dhs2013-13][^cite_note-14] The company was estimated to have laundered \$6 billion in criminal proceeds.[^cite_note-dhs2013-13]

### 2014 to present

In February 2014, [Mt. Gox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Gox), the largest cryptocurrency exchange at the time, suspended trading, closed its website and exchange service, and filed for [bankruptcy protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy_protection) in Japan from creditors.[^cite_note-auto-15][^cite_note-auto1-16] In April 2014, the company began [liquidation](565034.md) proceedings.[^cite_note-TheVerge-17] This was the result of a large theft of bitcoins that were stolen straight out of the Mt. Gox [hot wallet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_wallet) over time, beginning in late 2011.[^cite_note-auto2-18][^cite_note-auto3-19]

In December 2021 the MyCryptoWallet exchange called in [liquidators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidator_\(law\)).[^cite_note-abc-MyCryptoWallet-21feb'21-20]

In June 2022, the US Securities and Exchange Commission launched an enquiry into [Binance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binance) as an entity and not into the crypto products it was dealing in.[^cite_note-21]

On 11 November 2022, [FTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTX_\(company\)), which was at that time the third largest cryptocurrency exchange by [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_\(finance\)) and valued at \$18 billion,[^cite_note-valued-22] entered [bankruptcy proceedings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy_of_FTX) in the US court system, following what the exchange termed as "a [liquidity crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_crisis)".[^cite_note-nyt_Embattled_Crypto_Exchange_FTX_Files_for_Bankruptcy-23][^cite_note-filed-24][^cite_note-\:4-25][^cite_note-26] The financial impact of the collapse extended beyond the immediate FTX customer base, as reported,[^cite_note-far-27] while, at a [Reuters](18998750.md) conference, financial industry executives said that "regulators must step in to protect crypto investors."[^cite_note-protect-28] Technology analyst Avivah Litan commented on the cryptocurrency ecosystem that "everything...needs to improve dramatically in terms of user experience, controls, safety, \[and] customer service."[^cite_note-ton-29] On 13 December 2022, FTX founder and CEO [Sam Bankman-Fried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Bankman-Fried), after being [extradited](239648.md) from the [Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas), was charged by the US attorney’s office for the southern district of New York with [fraud](58790.md), [conspiracy](7230.md) to commit [money laundering](19390.md), and conspiracy to defraud the US and violate [campaign finance](339302.md) laws.[^cite_note-fraud-30]

## Legislation

See also: [Legality of cryptocurrency by country or territory](41517281.md)

By 2016, several cryptocurrency exchanges operating in the European Union obtained licenses under the EU [Payment Services Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_Services_Directive) and the [EU Electronic Money Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-Money_Directive).[^cite_note-32] The adequacy of such licenses for the operation of a cryptocurrency exchange has not been judicially tested. The [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) and the [European Parliament](9581.md) announced that they will issue regulations to impose stricter rules targeting exchange platforms.

In 2023, the European Union introduced the [MiCA regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markets_in_Crypto-Assets). Under this regulation, all cryptocurrency exchanges operating in the EU must obtain a license in accordance with this regulation by 1 July 2026.[^cite_note-33]

In 2018, the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) maintained that "if a platform offers trading of digital assets that are securities and operates as an "exchange," as defined by the federal securities laws, then the platform must register with the SEC as a national securities exchange or be exempt from registration".[^cite_note-34] The [Commodity Futures Trading Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_Futures_Trading_Commission) now permits the trading of cryptocurrency derivatives publicly.[^cite_note-35]

Among the Asian countries, Japan is more forthcoming and regulations mandate the need for a special license from the [Financial Services Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Services_Authority) to operate a cryptocurrency exchange.[^cite_note-36] China and Korea remain hostile, with China banning bitcoin miners and freezing bank accounts.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] While Australia is yet to announce its conclusive regulations on cryptocurrency, it does require its citizens to disclose their digital assets for [capital gains tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_gains_tax).[^cite_note-39]
