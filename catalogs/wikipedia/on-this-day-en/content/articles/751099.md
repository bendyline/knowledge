**Dagestan**,[^cite_note-8] officially the **Republic of Dagestan**,[^cite_note-9] is a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) of [Russia](25391.md) situated in the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus) of [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), along the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). It is located north of the [Greater Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Caucasus), and is a part of the [North Caucasian Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasian_Federal_District). The republic is at the southernmost tip of Russia, sharing land borders with the countries of [Azerbaijan](746.md) (south) and [Georgia](48768.md) (southwest), and the Russian republics of [Chechnya](6095.md) (west), [Stavropol Krai](474074.md) (northwest), and [Kalmykia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmykia) (north). [Makhachkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhachkala) is the republic's [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia); other major cities are [Derbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbent), [Kizlyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kizlyar), [Izberbash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izberbash), [Kaspiysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspiysk), and [Buynaksk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buynaksk).

Dagestan covers an area of 50,300 square kilometres (19,400 square miles), with a population of over 3.1 million,[^cite_note-est20-10] consisting of over 30 ethnic groups and 81 nationalities.[^cite_note-Britannica-11] With 14 official languages, and 12 ethnic groups each constituting more than 1% of its total population, the republic is one of Russia's most linguistically and ethnically diverse, and one of the most [heterogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneity_and_heterogeneity) administrative divisions in the world.[^cite_note-12] Most of the residents speak one of the [Northeast Caucasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Caucasian_languages), or [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) languages;[^cite_note-Britannica-11] however, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) is the primary language and the [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) in the republic.[^cite_note-dictionary-13] Dagestan is known internationally for its strong culture of [combat sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)), with many of its fighters having later turned to [mixed martial arts](228344.md).

## Toponymy

&#x20;The word *Dagestan* is of [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) origin, directly translating to "land of the mountains". The Turkic word *[dağ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/da%C4%9F#Turkish)* means "mountain", and the Persian suffix *[-stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-stan)* means "land".[^cite_note-14]

Some areas of Dagestan were known as *[Lekia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lezgistan)*, *[Avaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avars_\(Caucasus\))* and *[Tarki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamkhalate_of_Tarki)* at various times.[^cite_note-15]

Between 1860 and 1920, *Dagestan* was referred to as [Dagestan Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagestan_Oblast), corresponding to the southeastern part of the present-day republic. The current borders were created with the establishment of the [Dagestan Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagestan_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) in 1921, with the incorporation of the eastern part of [Terek Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terek_Oblast), which is not mountainous but includes the [Terek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terek_River) [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral) at the southern end of the [Caspian Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Depression).

### Mountains

Most of Dagestan is mountainous, with the Greater [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains) covering the south of the republic. The highest point is the [Bazardüzü/Bazardyuzyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Bazard%C3%BCz%C3%BC) peak at 4,470 meters (14,670 ft), on the border with [Azerbaijan](746.md). The [southernmost point of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_points_of_Russia) is located about seven kilometers southwest of the peak. Other important mountains are [Diklosmta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diklosmta) (4,285 m (14,058 ft)), [Gora Addala Shukgelmezr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gora_Addala_Shukgelmezr) (4,152 m (13,622 ft)) and [Gora Dyultydag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gora_Dyultydag) (4,127 m (13,540 ft)). The town of [Kumukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumukh) is one of the settlements on the mountains.

### Natural resources

Dagestan is rich in [oil](23195.md), [natural gas](22131.md), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), and many other minerals and resources.[^cite_note-16]

### Climate

The climate is classified as a [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate), with a significant lack of precipitation. It is among the warmest places in Russia. In the mountainous regions, it is [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate).

- *Average January temperature*: +2 °C (36 °F)

- *Average July temperature*: +26 °C (79 °F)

- *Average annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\))*: 250 mm (10 in) (northern plains) to 800 mm (31 in) (in the mountains).

## History

Main article: [History of Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dagestan)



Inside the Persian [fortress of Derbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Derbent), a [World Heritage Site](44940.md)

In the first few centuries AD, [Caucasian Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_Albania) (corresponding to modern Azerbaijan and southern Dagestan) became a vassal and eventually subordinate to the [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire). With the advent of the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire), it became a [satrapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_\(satrapy\)) (province) within the vast domains of the empire. In later antiquity, a few wars were fought as the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) unsuccessfully attempted to contest Sasanid rule over the region. Over the centuries, to a relatively large extent, the peoples within the Dagestan territory converted to [Christianity](5211.md) alongside [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism).

In the 5th century, the Sassanids gained the upper hand, and by the 6th century had constructed a strong citadel at [Derbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbent), known from then on as the [Caspian Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Gates), while the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns) overran the northern part of Dagestan, followed by the [Caucasian Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_Avars). During the Sassanian era, southern Dagestan became a bastion of Persian culture and civilization, with its center at Derbent.[^cite_note-18] A policy of "[Persianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persianisation)" can be traced over many centuries.[^cite_note-19]

### Islamic influence

During the [Islamic conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests), the Dagestani people (region of [Derbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbent)) were the first people to become Muslims within current Russian territory, after the [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_people) conquest of the region in 643.[^cite_note-20] In the 8th century Arabs repeatedly [clashed with the Khazars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Khazar_wars). Although the local population rose against the Arabs of Derbent in 905 and 913, [Islam](6037917.md) was [still adopted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization) in urban centers, such as [Samandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samandar_\(city\)) and [Kubachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubachi_language) (Zerechgeran), from where it steadily diffused into the highlands. By the 15th century, [Christianity](5211.md) had died away, leaving a 10th-century [Church of Datuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datuna_Church) as the sole monument to its existence.

### Seljuk Turks



Mongol horserider with "[cloud collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_collar)", House of Ahmad and Ibrahim, [Kubachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubachi_\(urban-type_settlement\)) in the Caucasus, second half 14th century CE

In the second half of the 11th century, the [Seljuk Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks) took part of the region of Dagestan under their control.[^cite_note-TDV-21]

### Mongol rule

See also: [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde) and [Ilkhanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate)

The Mongols raided the lands in 1221–1222 then conquered Derbent and the surrounding area from 1236 to 1239 during the [invasions of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_of_Georgia) and [Durdzuketia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_of_Durdzuketia).

### Timurids

The [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurids) incorporated the region into their realm following the Mongols.[^cite_note-TDV-21]

### Alternating Persian and Russian rule

See also: [Shamkhalate of Tarki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamkhalate_of_Tarki)



Silver coin of [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah), minted in Dagestan, dated 1741–42 (left = obverse; right = reverse)

As Mongolian authority gradually eroded, new centers of power emerged in Kaitagi and [Tarki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarki). In the early 16th century, the Persians (under the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids)) reconsolidated their rule over the region, which would, intermittently, last till the early 19th century. In the 16th and 17th centuries, legal traditions were codified, and mountainous communities (*djamaats*) obtained considerable autonomy. In the 1720s, as a result of the disintegration of the Safavids and the [Russo-Persian War (1722–23)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1722%E2%80%9323\)), the Russians briefly annexed maritime Dagestan from the Safavids. The Russians could not hold on to the interior of Dagestan, and could only be stopped in front of Baku with the help of [Ottoman](22278.md) forces under the command of Mustafa Pasha. With a treaty signed between Russia and the Ottoman Empire [in 1724](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Constantinople_\(1724\)), aimed at dividing the territories of Safavid Iran between them, Derbend, Baku and some other places in the region were left to Russia. Dagestan briefly came under Ottoman rule between 1578 and 1606.[^cite_note-TDV-21]

The territories were however returned to [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) in 1735 per the [Treaty of Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ganja).

Between 1730 and the early course of the 1740s, following his brother's murder in Dagestan, the new Persian ruler and military genius [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) led a lengthy [campaign in swaths of Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader's_Dagestan_campaign) in order to fully conquer the region, which was met with considerable success, although eventually he was forced to withdraw due to the extremity of the weather, the outbreak of disease and heavy raids by the various ethnic groups of Dagestan, forcing him to retreat with his army. From 1747 onwards, the Persian-ruled part of Dagestan was administered through the [Derbent Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbent_Khanate), with its center at Derbent. The [Persian expedition of 1796](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_expedition_of_1796) resulted in the Russian capture of Derbent in 1796. However, the Russians were again forced to retreat from the entire Caucasus following internal governmental problems, allowing Persia to capture the territory again.

### Russian rule consolidated

It was not until the aftermath of the [Russo-Persian War (1804–1813)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%931813\)) that Russian power over Dagestan was confirmed, and that [Qajar Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Persia) officially ceded the territory to Russia. In 1813, following Russia's victory in the war, Persia was forced to cede southern Dagestan with its principal city of Derbent, alongside other vast territories in the Caucasus to Russia, conforming with the [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan).[^cite_note-22] The 1828 [Treaty of Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay) indefinitely consolidated Russian control over Dagestan and removed Persia from the military equation.[^cite_note-23]

### Uprisings against Imperial Russia



[Imam Shamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Shamil), national hero and freedom fighter



Dagestani man, photographed by [Sergey Prokudin-Gorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Prokudin-Gorsky), between 1907 and 1915

The Russian administration, however, disappointed and embittered the highlanders. The institution of heavy taxation, coupled with the expropriation of estates and the construction of fortresses (including Makhachkala), electrified highlanders into rising under the aegis of the Muslim [Imamate of Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamate_of_Dagestan), led by [Ghazi Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Mohammed) (1828–1832), [Hamzat Bek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamzat_Bek) (1832–1834) and [Shamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Shamil) (1834–1859). This [Caucasian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_War) raged until 1864.

Dagestan and Chechnya profited from the [Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)), rising together against the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). Chechnya rose again at various times throughout the late 19th and 20th centuries.

### Soviet era

On December 21, 1917, [Ingushetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingushetia), [Chechnya](6095.md), Dagestan and the rest of the North Caucasus declared independence from Russia and formed a single state called the "United Mountain Dwellers of the North Caucasus" (also known as the [Mountainous Republic of the Northern Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountainous_Republic_of_the_Northern_Caucasus)). The capital of the new state was moved to [Temir-Khan-Shura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buynaksk).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The first prime minister of the state was [Tapa Chermoyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapa_Chermoyev), a prominent Chechen statesman. The second prime minister was an Ingush statesman Vassan-Girey Dzhabagiev, who in 1917 also became the author of the constitution of the land, and in 1920 was re-elected for a third term.[^cite_note-26] After the [Bolshevik Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik_Revolution), [Ottoman](22278.md) armies occupied [Azerbaijan](746.md) and Dagestan and the region became part of the short-lived [Mountainous Republic of the Northern Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountainous_Republic_of_the_Northern_Caucasus). After more than three years of fighting the [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Army) and local nationalists, the Bolsheviks achieved victory and the [Dagestan Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagestan_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) was proclaimed on January 20, 1921. As the newly created Soviet Union was consolidating control in the region, Dagestan declared itself a republic within the Russian Soviet federation but did not follow the other ASSRs in declaring [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade_of_sovereignties).[^cite_note-27]

### Post-Soviet era

On August 7, 1999, the [Islamic International Peacekeeping Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_International_Peacekeeping_Brigade) (IIPB), an Islamist group from [Chechnya](6095.md) led by warlords [Shamil Basayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamil_Basayev), [Ibn Al-Khattab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Al-Khattab) and [Ramzan Akhmadov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramzan_Akhmadov), launched a military [invasion of Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Dagestan), in support of the Shura separatist rebels with the aim of creating an "independent Islamic State of Dagestan".

The invaders were supported by part of the local population but were driven back by the Russian military and local paramilitary groups.[^cite_note-theindependent-28] In response to the invasion, Russian forces subsequently [reinvaded Chechnya later that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chechen_War).[^cite_note-29]

Dagestan has [one of the highest unemployment rates in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federal_subjects_of_Russia_by_unemployment_rate).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Dagestani soldiers participated in the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), many of whom were killed in action.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] In September, Dagestan became a center of the [2022 North Caucasian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_North_Caucasian_protests) against [mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_mobilization).[^cite_note-35]

## Politics



The Government Building of the Republic of Dagestan



[Sergey Melikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Melikov), the Head of Dagestan, with Azerbaijan's President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) on 6 December 2022

The [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) of Dagestan is the [People's Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Assembly_of_Dagestan), consisting of 72 deputies elected for a four-year term. The People's Assembly is the highest executive and legislative body of the republic.

The Constitution of Dagestan was adopted on July 10, 2003. According to it, the highest executive authority lies with the State Council, comprising representatives of fourteen ethnicities. The Constitutional Assembly of Dagestan appoints the members of the State Council for a term of four years. The State Council appoints the members of the Government.

The ethnicities represented in the State Council are [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_Avars), [Dargins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dargins), [Kumyks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumyks), [Lezgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lezgins), [Laks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lak_people_\(Dagestan\)), [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_people), [Tabasarans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabasaran_people), [Russians](59510.md), [Chechens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechens), [Nogais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogais), [Aguls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguls), [Rutulians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutuls), [Tsakhurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsakhurs), and [Tats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_people_\(Caucasus\)).

Formerly, the Chairman of the State Council was the highest executive post in the republic, held by [Magomedali Magomedovich Magomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magomedali_Magomedov) until 2006. On February 20, 2006, the People's Assembly passed a resolution terminating this post and disbanding the State Council. [Russian president](239646.md), [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) offered the People's Assembly the candidature of [Mukhu Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhu_Aliyev) for the newly established post of the [president of the Republic of Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Republic_of_Dagestan). The People's Assembly accepted the nomination, and Mukhu Aliyev became the first president of the republic. On February 20, 2010, Aliyev was replaced by [Magomedsalam Magomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magomedsalam_Magomedov). [Ramazan Abdulatipov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramazan_Abdulatipov) then became the head (acting 2013–2017, following the resignation of Magomedov).[^cite_note-36] On October 3, 2017, [Vladimir Vasilyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vasilyev_\(politician\)) was appointed and served as head until October 5, 2020.[^cite_note-37]

In the [2024 Russian presidential election](56748201.md), which critics called rigged and fraudulent,[^cite_note-38] President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) won 92.93% of the vote in Dagestan.[^cite_note-39]

## Human rights

Dagestan, like much of the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus), is considered to be a part of [peripheral Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_and_peripheral_Russia) and affected by weak governance, corruption and conflict, resulting in widespread human rights abuses.[^cite_note-Fordham-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] These include abuses in counter‑terrorism operations, killings and intimidation of journalists[^cite_note-43] and activists, and [violence against women](3634424.md) under local customary practices (based on *[adat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adat)*),[^cite_note-Fordham-40] and systemic discrimination against women in family law (including the denial of a [right to family life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_family_life), where courts often side with patrilineal kin against mothers).[^cite_note-Kogan-44] Examples of violence against women include [honour killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honour_killings), [enforced disappearances](686148.md) and torture, [bride-kidnapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bride-kidnapping) and [female genital cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_genital_cutting) (the latter is more common in remote mountain regions).[^cite_note-Kogan-44]

[Human Rights Watch](46530.md) has documented [arbitrary detention](13435705.md), enforced disappearances, and torture by security forces, alongside harassment of lawyers and human-rights defenders, particularly between 2012 and 2015, in the context of the [insurgency in the North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_the_North_Caucasus).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

[Russian anti-LGBTQ legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_anti-LGBTQ_law) intersects with conservative Islamic social norms in the region. According to the human rights groups [North Caucasus SOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus_SOS) and [Marem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marem_\(organisation\)), [gay men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_men) in Dagestan are frequently abducted by police and subjected to torture by authorities; this was especially true during the broader [anti-gay purges in the North Caucasus, including Chechnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-gay_purges_in_Chechnya).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

In 2023, during the [Gaza war](74998519.md), there were a [wave of antisemitic attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_anti-Jewish_unrest_in_the_North_Caucasus) across the North Caucasus, including Dagestan.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

### Ethnic groups

The people of Dagestan include a large [variety of ethnicities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples_of_the_Caucasus). According to the [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2021\)),[^cite_note-census2021-62] Northeast Caucasians (including [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avars_\(Caucasus\)), [Dargins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dargins), [Lezgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lezgins), [Laks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lak_people_\(Dagestan\)), [Tabasarans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabasaran_people), [Rutulians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutul_people) and [Chechens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechens)) make up almost 75% of the population of Dagestan. Turkic peoples, [Kumyks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumyks), [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagestani_Azerbaijanis), and [Nogais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogai_people) make up 21%, and [Russians](59510.md) 3.3%. Other ethnicities (e.g. [Tats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_people_\(Caucasus\)), who are an [Iranian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples)) each account for less than 0.4% of the total population.

### Languages



Main language areas

More than 30 local languages are commonly spoken, most belonging to the [Nakh-Daghestanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Caucasian_languages) language family. Russian became the principal [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) in Dagestan during the 20th century;[^cite_note-65] Over 20 of Russia's 131 [endangered languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_languages) as identified by UNESCO can be found in Dagestan. Most of these endangered languages have speakers in the mountainous region on the Dagestan-Georgia border.[^cite_note-66]

Prior to Soviet rule, the literary lingua-franca status to some extent belonged to [Classical Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Arabic).[^cite_note-67] The northern [Avar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avar_language) dialect of [Khunzakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khunzakh) has also served as a lingua franca in mountainous Dagestan where Avar-related peoples lived.[^cite_note-68] And throughout centuries the [Kumyk language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumyk_language) had been [the lingua-franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) for the bigger part of the Northern Caucasus, from Dagestan to [Kabarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabarda), until the 1930s.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Kumyk also had been an official language for communication of the Russian Imperial administration with the local peoples.[^cite_note-72]

The first Russian grammar written about a language from present-day Dagestan was for Kumyk.[^cite_note-73] Author Timofey Makarov wrote:

> From the peoples speaking Tatar language I liked the most Kumyks, as for their language's distinction and precision, so for their closeness to the European civilization, but most importantly, I take in account that they live on the Left Flank of the Caucasian Front, where we're conducting military actions, and where all the peoples, apart from their own language, speak also Kumyk.

According to a 2012 survey which interviewed 56,900 people,[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-74] 83% of the population of Dagestan adheres to [Islam](6037917.md), 2.4% to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), 2% to Caucasian folk religion and other native faiths, 1% are [non-denominational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Christianity) [Christians](18337522.md). In addition, 9% of the population identify as "[spiritual but not religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_but_not_religious)", 2% as [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), and 0.6% as other and no answer.[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-74]

#### Islam

Main article: [Islam in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Russia)

Dagestanis adherents of Islam are largely [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) Muslims of the [Shafii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafii) school. On the Caspian coast, particularly in and around the port city of [Derbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbent), the population (primarily made up of [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis) and [Tats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_people_\(Caucasus\))) is [Shia](26961.md). A [Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi) minority is also present, which is sometimes a target of official repression.[^cite_note-75]

The appearance of [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) [mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism) in Dagestan dates back to the 14th century. The two [Sufi orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqa) that are widely spread in the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus) were the [Naqshbandiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naqshbandiya) and the [Qadiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadiriya). The mystic [tariqas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqa) preached tolerance and coexistence between the diverse people in the region. The Communist total intolerance for any religion after the [Communist Revolution of 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) also suppressed the Sufi movements. [Shaykh Said Afandi al-Chirkawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaykh_Said_Afandi_al-Chirkawi) was a prominent scholar, spiritual leader, and [murshid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshid) (guide) of Naqshbandi and [Shadhili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadhili) [tariqahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqah) in Dagestan until his death.[^cite_note-76]

Since the dissolution of the Soviet Union, there has been an [Islamic revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_revival) in the region. By 1996, Dagestan had 1,670 registered mosques, nine Islamic universities, 25 [madrassas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrassas), 670 [maktab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maktab_\(education\)), and it is estimated that "nearly one in five Dagestanis was involved in Islamic education", while of the 20,000 or so Russian pilgrims for the [Hajj](23487999.md) more than half were from Dagestan.[^cite_note-77]

#### Judaism

Main article: [Judaism in Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism_in_Dagestan)

A relatively large number of native [Tati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_language_\(Caucasus\))-speaking [Jews](25955086.md) – the "[Mountain Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Jews)" – were also present in these same coastal areas. However, since 1991 and the collapse of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), many have migrated to Israel and the United States. These were an extension of much larger [Azerbaijani Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Jew) community across the border in the Azerbaijani districts of [Quba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quba) and [Shamakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamakhi).[^cite_note-78]

#### Christianity

Further information: [Christianity in Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Dagestan)

The number of [Christians](18337522.md) among the non-Slavic indigenous population is very low, with estimates between 2,000 and 2,500. Most of these are [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal) Christians from the [Lak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laks_\(Caucasus\)) ethnicity.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] The largest congregation is Osanna Evangelical Christian Church (Pentecostal) in [Makhachkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhachkala), with more than 1,000 members.[^cite_note-81]

- [Cathedral of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Assumption_of_Makhachkala) is an [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_church_architecture) [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral) located in the city of [Makhachkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhachkala), the main cathedral of the [Diocese of Makhachkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Makhachkala).

- [Church of the Holy Equal-to-the-Apostles Prince Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Equal-to-the-Apostles_Prince_Vladimir_of_Makhachkala) is a Russian Orthodox [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral) of the [Diocese of Makhachkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Makhachkala), located in the city of [Makhachkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhachkala).

### Genetics

In 2006, a genetic study of the Dagestan populations, published in [Human Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Biology_\(journal\)), suggested that inhabitants of Dagestan are closely related to [Anatolian Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_Turks) and [Cypriot Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriot_Turks). Yunusbayev et al. pointed out that these findings support the theory that indigenous groups of Dagestan can trace their roots back to ancient [Anatolian farming tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution#Europe) who introduced early agricultural traditions.[^cite_note-82]

## Economy

The major industries in Dagestan include [oil production](8082027.md), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [chemicals](58721.md), [machine building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_building), [textile manufacturing](2162735.md), [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing) and [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber_industry). [Oil deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_deposits) are located in the narrow coastal region. Dagestan's natural gas production goes mostly to satisfy local needs. [Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) is varied and includes grain-farming, [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture) and [wine-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine-making), sheep-farming, and [dairying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairying). The engineering and [metalworking](266443.md) industries own 20% of the republic's industrial production assets and employ 25% of all industrial workers. Dagestan's hydroelectric power industry is developing rapidly. There are five power plants on the [Sulak River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulak_River) providing [hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power). It has been estimated that Dagestan's total potential hydroelectric power resources are 4.4 billion kW. Dagestan has a well-developed transportation system. [Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) connect the capital [Makhachkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhachkala) to Moscow, [Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan), and the Azerbaijani capital, [Baku](4566.md). The Moscow-Baku [highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_highway) also [passes through Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Highway), and there are air links with major cities.[^cite_note-msn-83][^cite_note-kommersant-84]

Conditions for economic development are favorable in Dagestan, but – as of 2006 – the republic's low starting level for a successful transition to market relations, in addition to rampant corruption, has made the region highly dependent on its [underground economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_economy) and the subsidies coming from the central Russian government.[^cite_note-kommersant-84][^cite_note-jamestown-85] Corruption in Dagestan is more severe than in other regions of the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and is coupled with a flourishing black market and clan-based economic system.[^cite_note-crisisgroup-86]

In 2011 [Rostelecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostelecom) started the implementation of [WDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength-division_multiplexing)-based equipment on the backbone network for data transmission in the Republic of Dagestan. Due to WDM introduction, the fiber-optic communication lines bandwidth increased to 2.5 Gbit/s. Rostelecom invested about 48 million rubles in the project.[^cite_note-87]

## Culture

### Avar literature

Epic-historical songs about the defeat of the armies of Afshar Turk Nadir Shah and various episodes of the nineteenth-century wars are popular among the Avars. Best-known are the ballads "Khochbar" and "Kamalil Bashir". In the second half of the nineteenth century and the beginning of the twentieth, Avar culture and literature grew significantly. Well-known Avar literary figures include the poets Aligaji of Inkho (who died 1875) and Chanka (1866–1909), the lyric poet Makhmud (1873–1919), the satirist Tsadasa Gamzat (1877–1951), and the poet [Rasul Gamzatov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasul_Gamzatov) (1923–2003). Among his poems was Zhuravli, which became a well-known Russian song.[^cite_note-88]

### Music

There is a Dagestani Philharmonic Orchestra and a State Academic Dance Ensemble. [Gotfrid Hasanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotfrid_Hasanov), who is said to be the first professional composer from Dagestan, wrote *Khochbar*, the first Dagestani opera, in 1945. Dagestani folk dances include a fast-paced dance called the *[lezginka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lezginka)*. It derives its names from the Lezgin people; nevertheless, Azerbaijanis, Circassians, Abkhazians, Mountain Jews, Caucasian Avars, the Russian Kuban, and Terek Cossacks and many other tribes have their own versions.[^cite_note-89]

### Cuisine

*Khingal-bat* is Dagestan's national dish of small dumplings boiled in ram's broth. Depending on the cook's ethnicity, the dumplings can be oval or round, filled with meat or cheese, and served with a garlic or sour cream sauce. Dairy products and meat constitute a large part of the diet in the mountainous regions, while in the valley zones, vegetables and grain flour are eaten in addition to fruits, edible gourds, edible herbs, and wild grasses.[^cite_note-90]

### Martial arts

Main article: [Wrestling in Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling_in_Dagestan)

Since the dissolution of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the region has been recognized for producing some of the world's best athletes in combat sports and produces the most MMA fighters of any region relative to population. Dagestani born [Khabib Nurmagomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabib_Nurmagomedov) was a UFC lightweight champion who retired undefeated.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] His training partner, [Islam Makhachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Makhachev), who is also Dagestani, is the former lightweight champion and current welterweight champion, and currently the #1 ranked UFC pound-for-pound fighter. Khabib's cousin, [Umar Nurmagomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar_Nurmagomedov), is ranked #2 in the UFC's bantamweight division. Umar's younger brother, [Usman Nurmagomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usman_Nurmagomedov), is the former [Bellator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellator_MMA) lightweight champion and currently fights in the [PFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Fighters_League). [Magomed Ankalaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magomed_Ankalaev), who also hails from Dagestan, is the former UFC light heavyweight champion. [Abubakar Nurmagomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abubakar_Nurmagomedov) is also a cousin of Khabib's who is Dagestani, he is also a professional MMA fighter with a professional record of 17–4–1.

Dagestan has also historically produced a disproportionate number of Olympic and world champions in freestyle wrestling. [Buvaisar Saitiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buvaisar_Saitiev) was a three-time Olympic champion, and [Abdulrashid Sadulaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulrashid_Sadulaev) won gold at the 2016 Rio Olympics and 2021 Tokyo Olympics; they are both from Dagestan. [Magomed Ramazanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magomed_Ramazanov), who also hails from Dagestan and is known for his resemblance to Khabib Nurmagomedov, won gold at the 2024 Paris Olympics.

In boxing, [Artur Beterbiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Beterbiev) is a one-time World Cup gold medalist, a two-time Olympian, and held the undisputed light-heavyweight championship between October 2024 and February 2025.

As well, many Dagestanis have collected accolades for other nations, [Akhmed Tazhudinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhmed_Tazhudinov) represents Bahrain and won gold in the 2024 Paris Olympics, [Nassourdine Imavov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassourdine_Imavov) represents France and is the #2 ranked middleweight contender in the UFC; both were born in Dagestan.
