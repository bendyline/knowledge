# Algeria–Morocco border



Map of Morocco, with Algeria to the east

The [Algeria](358.md)–[Morocco](19291.md) border is 1,559 km (887 mi) in length and runs from the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) in the north, to the tripoint with [Western Sahara](33220.md) in the south.[^cite_note-Central_Intelligence_Agency-1]

The border has been officially closed to all travel since 1994, although clandestine crossings are common, especially for [drug trafficking](218449.md).[^cite_note-2]

## Description

The boundary starts in the north on the Mediterranean Sea just west of [Marsa Ben M'Hidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsa_Ben_M'Hidi); it then proceeds overland toward the south via a series of irregular lines, veering slightly to the southeast.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3] Near the Moroccan town of [Figuig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figuig) it veers sharply to the west, proceeding then in a broadly south-westerly direction via a series of straight and irregular lines. Upon reaching the [Draa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draa_River) the border then follows this for some distance, before veering sharply to the south, whereupon a straight north–south line proceeds for 116 km (72 mi) down to the Western Sahara tripoint.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3]

## History

[France](5843419.md) occupied much of the northern coastal areas of Algeria in the period 1830–47, which had hitherto been subject to the nominal control of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3] Morocco initially managed to maintain its independence throughout the '[Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa)' in the late 19th century, whereby European colonial control was established in the rest of [Africa](5334607.md). The border between it and the Ottoman domains to the east had been vague, and Morocco maintained a traditional, though often weakly enforced, claim to large areas of north-west Africa.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3][^cite_note-countrystudies-4] France sought to rectify this, and as a result a border was delimited from the Mediterranean south to Teniet el-Sassi via the [Treaty of Lalla Marnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghnia) of 18 March 1845.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3] Protocols confirming this treaty were signed on 20 July 1901 and 20 April 1902, which also extended the frontier south to Figuig, though not in any great detail.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3] In the thinly and scarcely inhabited areas south of Figuig, the border remained undemarcated, subject to vague and ill-defined administrative practices based on uncertain legal standing.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3][^cite_note-5] In 1912, via the [Treaty of Fes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fes), France and [Spain](26667.md) partitioned Morocco between them, with the northern littoral becoming a [Spanish protectorate in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_protectorate_in_Morocco) (excluding [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier), which later became an [international zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier_International_Zone)) and the rest [French Protectorate in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_in_Morocco) (minus [Ifni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifni), which formed a Spanish exclave).[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3]

Morocco gained independence in 1956, followed by Algeria in 1962.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3]

The uncertainty over much of the border alignment, and Morocco's claims for a so-called '[Greater Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Morocco)' encompassing much of north-west Africa, led to the [Sand War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_War) of 1963.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3] Various agreements were signed in 1969-70 aiming to provide a peaceful resolution of the dispute, which resulted in the treaty of 15 June 1972 which demarcated for the first time the entire boundary.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3] While Algeria ratified the treaty in 1973, Morocco didn't ratify it until 1992, though that didn't end Moroccan irredentism.[^cite_note-border-8] Relations worsened when Spain announced its intention to pull out of [Spanish Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara) (modern Western Sahara) in 1975, with Morocco then annexing the northern two-thirds, and later the whole, of the territory.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3] Algeria opposed the annexation and provided shelter for the [Polisario Front](201574.md) Saharawi nationalist militia and Saharawi refugees on its territory.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-3][^cite_note-cs-9] As Algeria slid into [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War) in the 1990s, relations once again soured, with Algeria closing the border in 1994.[^cite_note-cn-10] Relations thawed slightly with the advent of peace in Algeria in the early 2000s, though at present the border remains closed. Travel and trade between the two countries is allowed, but must be done either by air or sea.
