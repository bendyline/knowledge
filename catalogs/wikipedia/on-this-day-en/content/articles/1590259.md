**San Juan** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[saŋ ˈxwan\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is the capital and largest city of the [Argentine](18951905.md) [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina) of [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Province_\(Argentina\)) in the [Cuyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyo_\(Argentina\)) region, located in the Tulúm Valley, west of the [San Juan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_River_\(Argentina\)), at 650 m (2,133 ft) [above mean sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level), with a population of around 112,000 as per the 2001 [census](6889.md) \[[INDEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Statistics_and_Census_of_Argentina)] (over 500,000 in the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area)).

In agriculture, [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine) is economically important.

The [1944 San Juan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_San_Juan_earthquake) on January 15 killed around 10,000 people and left half of the provincial population homeless.[^cite_note-1]&#x20;



Downtown San Juan c. 1910.

## Geography

The city of San Juan is located in a fertile valley within a rocky mountainous area. Winter temperatures are generally mild, averaging between 1 °C (33.8 °F) and 16 °C (60.8 °F), but can drop below −9 °C (15.8 °F). Summers are hot, with average temperatures between 19 °C (66.2 °F) and 35 °C (95.0 °F), and a record maximum of 46.7 °C (116.1 °F) on December 20, 1995.[^cite_note-ORA-2] Sunny weather is common in all months and San Juan averages about 3,361.3 hours of bright sunshine, or about 76% of possible sunshine,[^cite_note-UNLPannual-3] ranging from a low of 68% in June to a high of 81% possible sunshine in May.[^cite_note-UNLPmay-4][^cite_note-UNLPjune-5] Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), San Juan has a [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) (*BWh/BWk*).

Since very little rain falls in the region, the San Juan River has been dammed to provide a regular source of water to the city. The resulting [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_\(water\)) is located in [Ullum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ullum?action=edit\&redlink=1), and is known as the [Quebrada de Ullum Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebrada_de_Ullum_Dam). The dam also provides electrical power to the region.

Sixty-five percent of the surrounding area's agricultural production is related to [wine production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_wine).

## Notable sites

The [San Juan de Cuyo Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_de_Cuyo_Cathedral) was completed in 1979.

## Wine

Harvest in the San Juan Region 1890

Harvest in the San Juan Region 1890

San Juan is ranked second among the wine-producing provinces of Argentina. It has a cultivated area of 116,700 acres at altitudes of between 1,970 (601m) and 4,590 feet (1399m) above sea level. This region specializes in [Syrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrah), [Malbec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malbec), [Cabernet Sauvignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabernet_Sauvignon), [Bonarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douce_noir), [Chardonnay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chardonnay) and [Torrontés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torront%C3%A9s).[^cite_note-11] Because of this area's [semi-desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-desert), [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture) is dependent on irrigation from the [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_River_\(Argentina\)) and [Jáchal Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A1chal_River).[^cite_note-12]
