**Sheila Cherfilus-McCormick** ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Cherfilus**; [/ˈʃɜːrfɪləs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*SHUR-fill-əss*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born January 25, 1979) is an American politician and businesswoman who served as the [U.S. representative](19468510.md) for [Florida's 20th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida's_20th_congressional_district) from 2022 until her resignation in 2026. She is a member of the [Democratic Party](5043544.md).

After the death of Representative [Alcee Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcee_Hastings) in 2021, Cherfilus-McCormick was elected in a [January 2022 special election](67319349.md) to complete the remainder of his unexpired term. She was elected to a full term in November [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Florida#District_20) and was re-elected in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Florida#District_20).

In November 2025, Cherfilus-McCormick was indicted and charged with laundering \$5 million in COVID-19 relief funds and using those funds for a congressional campaign. If convicted, she faces up to 53 years in prison. An investigation conducted by the [House Ethics Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Ethics_Committee) found Cherfilus-McCormick guilty of 25 out of 27 charges. On April 21, 2026, Cherfilus-McCormick resigned from Congress minutes before a hearing on her potential expulsion.

## Early life, education, and early career

Cherfilus-McCormick was born on January 25, 1979,[^cite_note-2] in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), New York, to parents from [Haiti](13373.md).[^cite_note-3] She spent her childhood in Brooklyn and [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens)[^cite_note-4] before moving to [Hollywood, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Florida) at 13 to attend high school.[^cite_note-JI_Profile-5] She earned a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) and government from [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University) and a [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) from the [St. Thomas University School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Thomas_University_School_of_Law).[^cite_note-Palm_Beach_Post_2020-08-06-6]

After graduating from college, Cherfilus-McCormick was a project manager for the [New York City Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Transit_Authority). From 1999 to 2007, she worked as the vice president for operations of Trinity Health Care Services, a Florida-based family home health care company co-founded by her stepfather, Gabriel Smith. She later served as the company's CEO.[^cite_note-7]

## U.S. House of Representatives

### Elections

#### 2018

Cherfilus-McCormick ran for the U.S. House of Representatives in [Florida's 20th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida's_20th_congressional_district) in the Democratic primary against incumbent [Alcee Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcee_Hastings) in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Florida).[^cite_note-8] She lost, 73.6%–26.4%.[^cite_note-9]

#### 2020

Cherfilus-McCormick challenged Hastings again in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Florida). She stated that she was running due to Hastings' ethical issues and health problems.[^cite_note-Palm_Beach_Post_2020-08-06-6] She lost the August 18 primary, 69.3%–30.7%.[^cite_note-10]

#### 2022 special election

See also: [2022 Florida's 20th congressional district special election](67319349.md)

After Hastings died on April 6, 2021, Cherfilus-McCormick once again ran for the U.S. House of Representatives in Florida's 20th district. A [special election](67319349.md) was held.[^cite_note-11] During the campaign, she loaned \$3.7 million to her campaign organization.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] She campaigned on [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) policies such as a [Green New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_New_Deal), [Medicare for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_for_All), and a \$1,000-a-month [universal basic income](28310828.md).[^cite_note-JI_Profile-5] Her campaign was supported by [Brand New Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_New_Congress), a progressive organization that also backed candidates such as [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez) and [Rashida Tlaib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Tlaib).[^cite_note-14]

After a recount, Cherfilus-McCormick was declared the winner of the Democratic primary by five votes over [Broward County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broward_County) [Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Commissioner) [Dale Holness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Holness).[^cite_note-The_Hill_2021-11-16-15] Cherfilus-McCormick easily defeated [Republican](32070.md) [Jason Mariner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Mariner) in the January 11, 2022 special election, winning 79% of the popular vote.[^cite_note-Boston_Globe_2022-01-13-16][^cite_note-17] Cherfilus-McCormick is the second [Haitian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian-American) to be elected to Congress (after Republican [Mia Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Love) of [Utah](31716.md)) and the first Haitian-American Democrat to be elected to Congress.[^cite_note-WLRN_2022-01-20-18][^cite_note-19]

#### 2022 general election

Following her narrow margin of victory in the special election, Cherfilus-McCormick was again challenged by Holness in the regular election.[^cite_note-20] She handily defeated Holness in the August 2022 Democratic primary, 66%–27%; [Anika Omphroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anika_Omphroy) received 6% of the vote.[^cite_note-21] Cherfilus-McCormick defeated Republican nominee Drew Montez-Clark with 72% of the vote in the November election.[^cite_note-22]

#### 2024

McCormick was re-elected without opposition in 2024.[^cite_note-23]

#### 2026

Activist and substitute teacher Elijah Manley launched a 2026 Democratic primary challenge against Cherfilus-McCormick.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In September 2025, Cherfilus-McCormick filed a \$1 million defamation lawsuit against Manley claiming that he had spread "blatant lies" about her record and reputation.[^cite_note-26] In these ads, Manley called Cherfilus-McCormick a "crook" and referenced her ongoing House Ethics Committee investigation.[^cite_note-27] Manley called the lawsuit frivolous. In January 2026, the lawsuit was dismissed.[^cite_note-28]

At an October 2025 town hall meeting, Cherfilus-McCormick and Manley got into a heated exchange that ended with Cherfilus-McCormick telling Manley "[your mama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Your_mama)" multiple times.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

On April 21, 2026, Cherfilus-McCormick resigned from Congress.[^cite_note-resign-31][^cite_note-CNBC_2026-04-21-32]

Cherfilus-McCormick did not withdraw her 2026 congressional candidacy and subsequently received 9.2% of the vote, placing fourth in the Democratic primary for the [redrawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Florida_redistricting) Florida's 20th congressional district.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Tenure

Cherfilus-McCormick voted with President [Joe Biden](145422.md)'s stated position 100% of the time during the [117th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/117th_United_States_Congress), according to a *[FiveThirtyEight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FiveThirtyEight)* analysis.[^cite_note-35] Cherfilus-McCormick voted to provide [Israel](9282173.md) with support following [2023 Hamas attack on Israel](74998317.md).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]



Cherfilus-McCormick with [Dolphin Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin_Democrats) president Alfredo Olvera in May 2025

Cherfilus-McCormick was a member of the [Congressional Black Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Black_Caucus).[^cite_note-WLRN_2022-01-20-18] She served on the [Committee on Foreign Affairs](1267657.md) and the [Committee on Veterans' Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Veterans'_Affairs).[^cite_note-ohsheila-38]

On April 21, 2026, Cherfilus-McCormick resigned from Congress minutes before a hearing on whether she should be [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_from_the_United_States_Congress).[^cite_note-resign-31]

## Financial fraud charges

On December 27, 2023, the [House Ethics Committee](1673155.md) announced that it was investigating Cherfilus-McCormick over allegations that she had violated campaign finance laws, failed to submit required disclosures, and carried out improper hiring practices.[^cite_note-44]

In January 2025, the state of Florida filed a \$5 million lawsuit against Cherfilus-McCormick's South Florida-based Trinity Health Care Services business for knowingly accepting overpayments of invoices for work that was not actually performed during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-45]

In November 2025, Cherfilus-McCormick was indicted on charges that she "stole and laundered \$5 million in federal relief funds and used the money for her congressional campaign". The specific charges included theft, [money laundering](19390.md), making illegal campaign contributions, and conspiring to file a false federal tax return.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-AP_2025-11-20-47][^cite_note-NBC_2025-11-20-48] Claiming innocence,[^cite_note-49] she pleaded not guilty.[^cite_note-plea-50] She faces up to 53 years in prison and millions of dollars in fines.[^cite_note-AP_2025-11-20-47][^cite_note-plea-50]

In January 2026, the investigative subcommittee of the House Committee on Ethics found that Cherfilus-McCormick's actions were consistent with the allegations in the criminal indictment against her, as well as more extensive misconduct, and included violations of campaign finance laws and regulations, criminal laws, the [Ethics in Government Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_in_Government_Act), the Code of Ethics for Government Service, and House rules.[^cite_note-subcommittee-51] Cherfilus-McCormick denied the allegations.[^cite_note-rare-52] The House Ethics Committee held a hearing on Cherfilus-McCormick's conduct on March 26, 2026.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The following day, the bipartisan committee found her guilty on 25 of 27 charges.[^cite_note-Politico_2026-03-27-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-CBS_2026-03-27-57] The finding paved the way for an expulsion vote by the full House of Representatives.[^cite_note-Politico_2026-03-27-55] Representative [Greg Steube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Steube) stated he intended to force a vote to [expel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_from_the_United_States_Congress) Cherfilus-McCormick.[^cite_note-58] Democratic representatives [Vicente Gonzalez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Gonzalez_\(American_politician\)), [Marie Gluesenkamp Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Gluesenkamp_Perez), and [Jim Himes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Himes) suggested Cherfilus-McCormick should resign or be removed.[^cite_note-v809-59][^cite_note-CBS_2026-03-27-57][^cite_note-60]

On April 21, 2026, Cherfilus-McCormick resigned her congressional post. She submitted her resignation minutes before a House Ethics Committee hearing; at that hearing, the committee was scheduled to consider recommending that she be removed from Congress.[^cite_note-resign-31][^cite_note-CNBC_2026-04-21-32]

## Personal life

Cherfilus-McCormick married lawyer Corlie McCormick in 2017. They reside in [Miramar, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramar,_Florida), and have two children together.[^cite_note-Palm_Beach_Post_2020-08-06-6]

Cherfilus-McCormick is [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]
