The **Sri Lanka women's national cricket team** represents [Sri Lanka](26750.md) in international [women's cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_cricket). One of ten teams competing in the [ICC Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Women's_Championship) (the highest level of the sport), the team is organised by [Sri Lanka Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Cricket) (SLC), a full member of the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) (ICC).

The Sri Lanka women's team made their [One Day International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_One_Day_International) (ODI) debut in 1997, against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_women's_national_cricket_team), and later in the year participated in the [1997 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup) in India. The team has since participated in every edition of the World Cup, with a fifth-place finish at the [2013 event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup) being its best performance. At the [Women's Twenty20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Women's_World_Twenty20), Sri Lanka has likewise played at every tournament, although the team has never progressed past the first round. Sri Lanka played their first and only [Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Test_cricket) match to date in April 1998, defeating [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_women's_national_cricket_team).

## History

### 1990s

Sri Lanka's international debut came in 1997 with a [three-match ODI series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_women's_cricket_team_in_Sri_Lanka_in_1997%E2%80%9398) against [The Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_women's_cricket_team), which they lost 2–1. This was part of their preparation for the [World Cup](3977980.md) in [India](14533.md) the following month, in which they reached the quarter finals, losing to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_women's_cricket_team). The following year they hosted a tour by [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_women's_cricket_team), winning the three ODIs as well as what was the first Test match for both countries. It remains Sri Lanka's sole Test match. The Netherlands toured Sri Lanka again in 1999, and this time the Sri Lankans were much more successful, winning all five ODIs.

### 2000s

Sri Lanka finished sixth in the 2000 World Cup, and their next international engagement was against Pakistan at home in 2002, winning all six ODIs. They also won all six ODIs in an away series against the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_women's_cricket_team) the following year.

In 2004 they hosted the inaugural [Women's Asia Cup](11395320.md), which was reduced to a series against [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_women's_cricket_team) after the withdrawal of Pakistan. They lost the series. The following year they again finished sixth in the World Cup. In 2005/06, they visited [Pakistan](23235.md) to take part in the second Asia Cup, finishing as runners up to India.

### 2010s

In 2014, allegations of sexual abuse by Sri Lanka officials surfaced in the media. Some players were apparently compelled to perform sexual favours for the officials to earn or keep their place in the national team.[^cite_note-7]

On [2013 Women's Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup), Sri Lanka beat England in a pool match, which was a major upset in Women's ODI history. England are world's top ranked team and Sri Lanka is in underdog status, but this win gave a full of attention in World Cricket.[^cite_note-8] Sri Lanka women next beat India women and finally they finished as fifth in the rankings.

On 8 August 2018, after a five-year gap since his previous spell in charge, [Harsha de Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha_de_Silva_\(coach\)) was reappointed as the head coach of the team by the SLC.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### 2020s

In 2021, Sri Lanka qualified for the [2022-25 ICC Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022-25_ICC_Women's_Championship) after the abandonment of the [2021 Women's Cricket World Cup Qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup_Qualifier), due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), resulted in qualifications for the [2022 Women's Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup) and the next cycle of the Women's Championship being decided via WODI rankings.

In 2023, Sri Lanka secured their first-ever series victory against [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_cricket_team) in England. Sri Lanka’s historic 2-1 victory was the first time England lost a bilateral T20I series to a team other than Australia since a 2-1 defeat to New Zealand in 2010.[^cite_note-11]

Playing in front of their home supporters at [Rangiri Dambulla International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangiri_Dambulla_International_Stadium) in [Dambulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dambulla), Sri Lanka won the [2024 Women's Twenty20 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Women's_Twenty20_Asia_Cup), the first time they had taken the title, defeating [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_women's_cricket_team) in the final by eight wickets after chasing a target of 166 with eight balls to spare.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]
