**Six Flags Magic Mountain**, formerly known and colloquially referred to as simply **Magic Mountain**, is a 209-acre (85 ha) [amusement park](137327.md) located in unincorporated [Los Angeles County](79734.md), just outside [Santa Clarita, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clarita,_California), United States, 35 miles (56 km) northwest of [downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles). It opened on May 29, 1971, as a development of the [Newhall Land and Farming Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhall_Land_and_Farming_Company) and [Sea World Inc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaWorld).[^cite_note-3] In 1979, [Six Flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags) purchased the park and added "Six Flags" to the park's name.

With 16 operating [roller coasters](137471.md),[^cite_note-4] Six Flags Magic Mountain is tied for third for the most coasters at any park, behind [Energylandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energylandia) in [Zator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zator,_Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship), [Poland](22936.md)[^cite_note-5] and a tie between sister parks [Cedar Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Point)[^cite_note-6] and [Canada's Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada's_Wonderland)[^cite_note-7] and is now, as of September 2026, tied with [Six Flags Great America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_America).[^cite_note-8] It became the first amusement park to offer 20 roller coasters with the opening of [Wonder Woman: Flight of Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Woman_Flight_of_Courage) in 2022.[^cite_note-Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain_to_reclaim_roller_coaster_title-9] The 2025 removal of [Superman: Escape from Krypton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman:_Escape_from_Krypton) would put an end to this record, and it would stop being tied for first place after the closure of [Magic Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Flyer) in January 2026.[^cite_note-10] In 2024, the park had an estimated 3.31 million visitors, ranking it 16th in attendance in North America.[^cite_note-2024_attendance-2]

## History



Six Flags Magic Mountain from [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5)



The Grand Carousel is a family friendly ride located in the Six Flags Plaza area.



[Jet Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Stream_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)) is a family friendly flume ride located near the entrance to Gold Rusher. The ride opened in 1972.

In 1968, [Sea World Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_World_Inc.) founder [George Millay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Millay) and his executives began looking for a place in [Los Angeles](18110.md) county to build a theme park. Knowing that [Newhall Land and Farming Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhall_Land_and_Farming_Company) had enough undeveloped land in the new town of [Valencia](27991949.md), he asked CEO John F. Dickason if they could build on his land. They eventually formed a partnership to build a 200-acre theme park. Construction began in November 1969 and continued until May 1971. The park was named after the nearby [Magic Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Mountain_\(California\)), a peak located to the east of the [Santa Clarita Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clarita_Valley).[^cite_note-11]

When the park opened, there were 500 employees and 33 attractions, many of which were designed and built by [Arrow Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Development), which had previously worked on attractions at [Disneyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland). The admission price in 1971 was \$5 for adults, and \$3.50 for children between the ages of 3 and 12. Because the park was in a relatively remote part of Los Angeles County at the time, the [Greyhound bus line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) provided bus service to and from the park and Los Angeles, and from Northern California, and optionally allowed purchase of park admission at the time the bus ticket was purchased.[^cite_note-12]

At its 1971 opening, the rides and attractions included [Gold Rusher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Rusher), a steel coaster; the Log Jammer, a log flume; the [Sky Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Tower_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)), an [observation tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_tower); Grand Prix, similar to Disneyland's [Autopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopia) ride; El Bumpo, [bumper boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumper_boats); a [carousel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carousel); and other smaller rides. There were four transportation rides to the peak: Funicular, a [cable railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_railway) or [funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular), which was renamed Orient Express, then Helpful Honda Express, and is currently named Magic Mover.

Attractions included The Metro, which was three monorail stations around the park, including the Whitewater Lake, Country Fair, and Mountain stations; and "Eagles Flight", a gondola lift ride that had two stations at the peak, the long one north to Galaxy Station and the short one west to El Dorado Station. The Showcase Theater (later renamed the Golden Bear Theater) was part of the original park and featured [Barbra Streisand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand) as the first of many headline performers who would appear at Magic Mountain over the years.

In the 1971 season, Magic Mountain obtained permission from [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros.) to use *[Looney Tunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looney_Tunes)*/*[Merrie Melodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrie_Melodies)* characters. [Six Flags Great America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_America), has continuously used such characters since its opening in 1976, eight years before [Marriott Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriott_Corporation) sold the park to Six Flags.[^cite_note-Worden_2012-13]

In 1972, Magic Mountain began using trolls as the park mascots. The trolls King Blop, also known as King Troll, Bleep, Bloop, and the Wizard became recognizable symbols of Magic Mountain.[^cite_note-Worden_2012-13] All King Productions, a contractor, provided the entertainers wearing the costumes until December 31, 1972, when Magic Mountain took on that role. The characters were used until 1985. Also in 1972, a second flume ride named [Jet Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Stream_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)) was added.

In 1973, the park added its second roller coaster, the Mountain Express, a compact [Schwarzkopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Schwarzkopf) Wildcat model steel coaster. In 1974, the park also installed a new complex of spinning rides in what would later be known as Back Street. The new additions consisted of the Himalaya, Electric Rainbow, and Tumble Drum.

In 1975, the Grand Centennial Railway opened in the Back Street. It took riders on a train journey to Spillikin Corners and back. "...the Grand Centennial Excursion used a narrow gauge (3-ft.) steam locomotive and employed "train conductors," decked out in old-fashioned three-piece black Western suits and bow ties, to take passengers on a trip through time (and through the [bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison) corral), with stops at Spillikin Corners and at the northwestern tip of the park where [Six Flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_\(1961%E2%80%932024\)) would later build "[Batman: The Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman:_The_Ride)."... "[^cite_note-14] Local television advertisements at the time described this new attraction as, "The other side of the Mountain".

### The Coaster Revolution



Roaring Rapids is a river rafting water ride at Six Flags Magic Mountain.

With the opening of [Great American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Revolution_\(roller_coaster\)) in 1976, Magic Mountain became the first park in the world to have a modern, 360-degree looping steel coaster. Previous roller coasters with loops had been built and dismantled elsewhere due to safety issues. When it was built, there was very little in the way of surrounding vegetation. In the time since, the tracks have been surrounded by trees and bushes, which prevents the riders from knowing the track layout beforehand. [Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures) filmed part of the suspense film [*Rollercoaster*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollercoaster_\(1977_film\)) at Magic Mountain in late 1976, which featured the Revolution as its centerpiece during the film's climax.



Colossus was a wooden roller coaster which opened in 1978.

In 1978, [Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)) opened, and at the time was the fastest and largest dual-tracked wooden coaster in the world. Following its first season, it was closed and extensively redone. When it reopened, it was a much smoother ride.

In 1991, the camel hump before the last, or third, turn was replaced by a block brake. Though it decreased the speed of the ride after this particular brake, it allowed three trains to run per side at a time, greatly increasing capacity. One of the trains sometimes ran backwards for a few years in the mid-1980s. In the late 1990s this kind of ride was no longer possible due to the newer ride system in place, as well as different trains. During *Fright Fest*, the park runs one side backwards using a set of trains acquired from the now-demolished [Psyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyclone_\(roller_coaster\)), which was located on the other side of the park.

In 2015, the coaster was re-tracked with steel tracking and several inversions were added. It was rebranded "[Twisted Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_Colossus)". This renovation was completed by [Rocky Mountain Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_Construction).

### Six Flags era



The opening drop on [Goliath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)). Goliath featured the longest drop on a closed circuit roller coaster when it opened in February 2000.

In 1979, the park was sold to [Six Flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_\(1961%E2%80%932024\)) and became known as Six Flags Magic Mountain. In 1981, Six Flags Magic Mountain introduced [Roaring Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roaring_Rapids), a river rapids ride that was on the West Coast for the first time. It was developed by [Intamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intamin) in conjunction with the now-defunct [Six Flags Astroworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Astroworld), which had opened a similar ride in 1979. Along with Rapids came the completion of the midway near Spillikin Corners to link with Revolution's area. A complete circuit could be made around the park.

It was originally designed as a dual-sided station, but only one was fully developed, and all that exists of the possible second side is a few supports. It uses large pumps to circulate water, and each of the two pumps can circulate 88,500 U.S. gallons per minute (5.58 m<sup>3</sup>/s). The reservoir can hold 1.5 million U.S. gallons (5.7 Ml) of water, and one of the innovations used on it was the introduction of guide boards to help eliminate jam ups.

In 1982, the attraction [Freefall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freefall_\(ride\)) was added. Also built by [Intamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intamin), it was considered a cutting-edge drop tower ride if not strictly a "roller coaster." It simply ascends the tower and then drops down, with the track curving horizontally, leaving riders on their backs. Others were built for other parks (some of which are Six Flags). Today, most of these rides are obsolete and have been removed. Some flat rides were added, and others were removed the following year.

In 1984, [Sarajevo Bobsleds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo_Bobsleds) was erected. Yet another ride built by [Intamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intamin), the coaster was basically a bobsled without ice and snow. The coaster was built in honor of the [1984 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Winter_Olympics), for which [Sarajevo](26786.md) was the host city. [Six Flags Great Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_Adventure) added a similar ride that same year. In 1986, Sarajevo Bobsleds was moved to [Six Flags Over Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Over_Texas), where it operated as [La Vibora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Vibora) until permanently closing in late 2024.[^cite_note-15] The other bobsled was moved to [Six Flags Great America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_America) and later to [The Great Escape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Escape_and_Hurricane_Harbor) in [Queensbury, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensbury,_New_York), where it operated as [Alpine Bobsled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Bobsled).

In 1985, Children's World was rethemed as [Bugs Bunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugs_Bunny) World, as Magic Mountain had abandoned the Trolls in favor of *Looney Tunes*/*Merrie Melodies* characters, licensed by Warner Bros. That year, [Michael Jackson](14995351.md) visited the park, riding rides such as Colossus, Revolution, and Roaring Rapids. In 1986, the park added a steel stand-up looping roller coaster called [Shockwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_The_Escape), also designed by Intamin. This coaster was located in the back of the park, replacing Sarajevo Bobsleds.

At the end of 1988, the coaster was removed as part of a ride rotation program and went to Six Flags Great Adventure in 1990. It was removed from there in 1992 and was repainted white and rethemed upon its removal to [Six Flags Astroworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Astroworld). There, it was known as Batman The Escape. When Astroworld closed in 2005, the ride was put in storage at [Six Flags Darien Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Darien_Lake), where it remained until 2018, when it was finally sold for scrap.

In 1987, the park rethemed the Back Street. Spinning flat rides were renamed Turbo (Electric Rainbow), Subway (Himalaya), and Reactor (Enterprise). The dance club was rethemed as well and located near Reactor. After Hours, as it was now called (formerly Decibels), stayed open for one summer later than the rest of the park. It, along with Back Street, would remain open an additional two hours as a place for locals to hang out. This format lasted one season.

In 1988, [Ninja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninja_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)), "The Black Belt of Roller Coasters," opened. Built by [Arrow Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Dynamics), it was the first [suspended swing roller coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspended_swing_roller_coaster) on the west coast. Ninja has gone through very few changes since it was opened in 1988; evidently, only the wheels and paint have been changed.



Tidal Wave was a Shoot the Chute water ride, featuring a 50-foot (15 m) splashdown into a large body of water.

Tidal Wave opened in 1989. It is a short, wet ride featuring a large boat that travels up a low-angled incline to a level water trough. The trough, in the shape of a semicircle, ends in a steep drop into a large splashpool. The impact displaces large amounts of water on its riders. The ride's exit ramp crosses over the splash pool, allowing willing patrons leaving the ride to get soaked from the splash. The attraction was closed to make room for Wonder Woman: Flight of Courage in 2020.



Viper, seen in the foreground, was built in 1990.

In 1990, [Viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viper_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)), a multiple-looping coaster designed by [Arrow Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Dynamics), opened. It features a 188-foot (57 m) drop, speeds up to 70 mph (110 km/h), three [vertical loops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclined_loop), a batwing turn that inverts riders twice, and a double corkscrew.

In 1991, Magic Mountain added [Psyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyclone_\(roller_coaster\)), a wooden coaster modeled after the [Coney Island Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island_Cyclone). The Spillikin Corners area of the park was rethemed as Cyclone Bay to suit the new coaster, drawing guests into this area. The change was largely cosmetic, as the earlier theme relied on retail establishments that had been removed previously. The Shooting Gallery had replaced the Glass Blower, and the Candy Kitchen viewing area was redesigned. With Psyclone, the crowds returned. Psyclone's structure was damaged in the [1994 Northridge earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Northridge_earthquake), and the ride was eventually removed in 2007. After adding Ninja, Viper, and Psyclone within four years, the park was building a large repertoire of big roller coasters.

A new coaster from Intamin called [Flashback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashback_\(roller_coaster\)) was added to the park the following year in 1992. The prototype Space Diver model was a first of its kind that had already operated at [Six Flags Great America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_America) and [Six Flags Over Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Over_Georgia) prior to its arrival. Due to the excessive noise it generated near Hurricane Harbor, Flashback was closed in 2003 and eventually removed in 2007.[^cite_note-Flashback-Psyclone_closures-16][^cite_note-17]

### Time Warner era

In 1993, Six Flags Magic Mountain entered the Time Warner era.[^cite_note-18] The new ride for the year was Yosemite Sam Sierra Falls, a water ride with two twisting tubes that riders could slide down using a raft. Also that year, there was retheming and the High Sierra Territory was opened. The Showcase Theatre became the Golden Bear Theater, the Animal Star Theatre was created in Bugs Bunny World, and a large, fake wooden tree was built.[^cite_note-latimes.com-19]

In 1993, live concerts ended in the park due to a riot that broke out as a result of a [TLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TLC_\(group\)) concert that was oversold. Magic Mountain was quickly overwhelmed by large crowds that vandalized and destroyed property. Park shops had their windows broken and looting quickly followed. Police were called to the scene in full riot gear. The park was evacuated and closed down for the night.[^cite_note-latimes.com-19]

In 1994, Magic Mountain added what two other Six Flags parks already had, a [Bolliger & Mabillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolliger_&_Mabillard) inverted looping roller coaster called [Batman: The Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman:_The_Ride). More Six Flags parks added the ride in the coming years. Batman: The Ride is an inverted coaster, meaning the usual coaster protocol is reversed: the track is overhead and the cars are below it. The trains travel on the outside of the loops, and rider's legs hang freely, as on a ski lift.[^cite_note-20]



Signs to nearby rides and other attractions

A separately gated waterpark called [Six Flags Hurricane Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Hurricane_Harbor) opened on June 16, 1995.[^cite_note-21] The 22-acre (8.9 ha) park included body slides, tube slides, a kiddie water play area, a lazy river, and a wave pool.[^cite_note-22] The following year, a [SkyCoaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyCoaster) called Dive Devil opened at Magic Mountain.

A dual launch coaster called [Superman: The Escape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman:_Escape_From_Krypton) debuted at the park on March 15, 1997. Designed by [Intamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intamin), the 30-second ride launches riders from 0 to 100 mph (160 km/h) in seven seconds on a track that scales up a 41-story tower. It was the first roller coaster in the world to reach speeds of 100 miles per hour (160 km/h). Originally slated to open in June 1996, the ride's opening was delayed and pushed back to 1997 as problems with the LSM launch motors were found. The tower structure was painted a grayish white when the ride first opened and lasted until 2011.

### Premier Parks era



The [Riddler's Revenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Riddler's_Revenge) is a [stand-up roller coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_roller_coaster) which features six inversions.

In 1998, a new Bolliger & Mabillard [stand-up roller coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_roller_coaster) called [Riddler's Revenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riddler's_Revenge) opened as the tallest and fastest stand-up roller coaster in the world. Later that year, Six Flags was acquired by Premier Parks. In 2000, a steel [hypercoaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercoaster) called [Goliath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)), which was manufactured by [Giovanola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanola), opened at the park.

Three new roller coasters opened in 2001, although only one opened on schedule – a kiddie steel coaster called [Goliath Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath_Jr.) The other two, [Déjà Vu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Inverted_Boomerang) and [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X%C2%B2_\(roller_coaster\)) (now X²), suffered early mechanical issues. Déjà Vu opened late in 2001 and X opened early in 2002. Déjà Vu was designed by [Vekoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vekoma) and is a Giant Inverted Boomerang coaster (GIB), a variant of their popular [Boomerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomerang_\(roller_coaster\)) design. It is an inverted coaster with coaches suspended beneath an overhead track that traverses an open-circuit track forward and in reverse and features two completely vertical drops and three inversions.

After its late opening, the ride still suffered a lot of downtime. X was designed by Arrow Dynamics as the world's first "fourth-dimensional" roller coaster. It was the only one in North America where riders experience going 360 degrees in their seats. Each seat lies on a separate axis from the track. This coaster managed to open briefly on January 12, 2002, only to close due to more technical problems. It reopened late in August of that year. The ride closed for a major refurbishment and retheme in 2008 where X transformed into X².

In 2003, [Scream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scream_\(roller_coaster\)), designed by Bolliger & Mabillard, was added. At this point, Six Flags Magic Mountain tied with [Cedar Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Point) for the park with the most roller coasters in the United States. Scream is similar in concept to [Medusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medusa_\(Six_Flags_Discovery_Kingdom\)) at [Six Flags Discovery Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Discovery_Kingdom) and is a mirror image of [Medusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medusa_\(Six_Flags_Great_Adventure\)) at [Six Flags Great Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_Adventure). It is a [floorless roller coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floorless_roller_coaster) with trains riding above the rails traversing seven inversions on 3,985 feet (1,215 m) of track on floorless trains. In 2006, [Tatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsu), a Bolliger & Mabillard [flying roller coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_roller_coaster), was added, causing a temporary closure of Revolution and Roaring Rapids to allow construction to take place. It was much larger than the other three Bolliger & Mabillard Flying Coasters at other Six Flags parks, all named [Superman: Ultimate Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman:_Ultimate_Flight). Tatsu has a suspended-track orientation featuring vehicles that recline passengers with their backs against the track and facing the ground.

### 2006 attempted sale

On June 22, 2006, Six Flags, Inc. announced that it was exploring options for six of its parks, including Magic Mountain and its neighboring water park, [Hurricane Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Hurricane_Harbor). Though management said closing the park was unlikely, rumors still began that the park could be sold to real estate developers, with an intent to close the park and build housing developments in the area.[^cite_note-23] Park officials cited dwindling attendance due to rowdy behavior among some of the park-goers as reasons for wanting to sell the park while management was wanting to move Six Flags into more of a family park direction.[^cite_note-24] Throughout the Six Flags chain, attendance in the second quarter of 2006 was 14 percent lower than it was in the second quarter of 2005.[^cite_note-25]

By the fall of 2006, Six Flags confirmed that Magic Mountain had remained for sale. They also stated, however, that it would be sold to a company that would continue to operate it as a park, and that closing Magic Mountain was not a possibility. [Cedar Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Fair), [Anheuser-Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anheuser-Busch), and several others considered buying the park but none of the offers came close to the asking price.

By the time Six Flags announced which parks it was selling in January 2007, Magic Mountain was no longer one of them. The company decided not to sell Magic Mountain and its adjacent water park. Spokeswoman Wendy Goldberg said that upon further evaluation, the company decided that the Los Angeles parks remained too valuable to relinquish, as sales were increasing, and that the park would not be sold. Other parks were sold as a package and remained open.

&#x20;With the removal of Flashback and Psyclone in 2007, Six Flags Magic Mountain was no longer tied for the record of having the most roller coasters in a single park, relinquishing the record back to [Cedar Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Point). The park instead turned its focus to family-oriented improvements, including a new children's themed area called [Thomas Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Tank_Engine_and_Friends), which was added in 2008.[^cite_note-dailynews.com-26]

One of its premier thrill rides received a makeover. X was closed on in late 2007 for its transformation into X<sup>2</sup>, adding new fourth-generation trains and special effects and being repainted. It reopened on May 24, 2008. Later in 2008, the park renovated the "Magic of the Mountain" museum at the top of its Sky Tower attraction, which houses memorabilia from the park's history including old television commercials, park maps, models, and equipment salvaged from defunct rides.[^cite_note-dailynews.com-26]

[Terminator Salvation: The Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse:_The_Ride), a wooden roller coaster, opened on May 23, 2009. It was built in the former location of Psyclone and featured tunnels, spraying mist, and special effects. On January 9, 2011, the ride was renamed Apocalypse and given an appropriate theme that reflects an "end of the world" scenario.[^cite_note-27] Later that year, Six Flags President and CEO Mark Shapiro said in a [Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times) published interview that Magic Mountain had plans to install a new roller coaster for its 2010 season, and would add a new themed area for children in 2011 called [Wiggles World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidzopolis). Shapiro also stated that the adjacent Hurricane Harbor would receive an expansion.[^cite_note-28]

On May 29, 2010, [Mr. Six's Dance Coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_Runner_Express_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)) was scheduled to open but was delayed until 2011 when it would open under a new theme.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-2011_plans_as_at_August_2010-30] On the same day, Mr. Six's Splash Island opened at the adjacent Hurricane Harbor water park.[^cite_note-31]

In August 2010, it was announced that Superman: The Escape would undergo a major redevelopment before the 2011 season.[^cite_note-2011_plans_as_at_August_2010-30] On October 20, 2010, Six Flags Magic Mountain officially announced their full plans for 2011 after a video was leaked six days earlier.[^cite_note-Screamscape-32][^cite_note-Official_announcement-33] In addition to opening Mr. Six's Dance Coaster under a new name and theme, Six Flags announced two other attractions. In time for the 2011 season, Superman: The Escape was refurbished to [Superman: Escape from Krypton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman:_Escape_from_Krypton) and opened on March 19, 2011. The coaster featured new backwards launching cars and a new color scheme.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

The third and final announcement regards an entirely new thrill roller coaster. [Green Lantern: First Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern:_First_Flight_\(roller_coaster\)) opened on July 1, 2011, as Magic Mountain's eighteenth roller coaster, which was an [Intamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intamin) [ZacSpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_dimension_roller_coaster). With this roller coaster, Magic Mountain reclaimed for the park the world record for the highest number of roller coasters at a single theme park.[^cite_note-36] It was later announced, on November 4, 2010, that the children's roller coaster would be called [Road Runner Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_Runner_Express_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)) and located in *Bugs Bunny World*.[^cite_note-Six_Flags_Entertainment_Corporation_Investor_Meeting-37][^cite_note-38]

In late 2010, Six Flags began the process of removing non-[Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros.) licensed theming from attractions. They terminated several licenses including [Terminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_\(franchise\)) and [Thomas the Tank Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Tank_Engine). [Terminator Salvation: The Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse:_The_Ride) was renamed and rethemed into *Apocalypse*, which re-opened on January 8, 2011.[^cite_note-39] Thomas Town was renamed and rethemed to [Whistlestop Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistlestop_Park) in time for the 2011 season.[^cite_note-40]

On January 18, 2011, the [Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times) reported after considering a new theme based on [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics) superhero sidekicks, the park opted for simplicity and renamed the Little Flash coaster to Road Runner Express.[^cite_note-LATimes_update-41] Due to Green Lantern being placed in Gotham City Backlot, the area was re-themed into [DC Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Universe_\(themed_area\)). In addition, Grinder Gearworks became "Wonder Woman: Lasso Of Truth" and Atom Smasher was renamed "The Flash: Speed Force".

In August 2011, several [media sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media_\(United_States\)) reported that [Six Flags New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_New_England) would install Six Flags Magic Mountain's *Déjà Vu* for the park's [2012 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Inverted_Boomerang).[^cite_note-MassLive.com-42][^cite_note-New_Coaster-43][^cite_note-LAT_Review-44]

On September 1, 2011, Six Flags Magic Mountain announced that they would be opening a new attraction for the 2012 season named [Lex Luthor: Drop of Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Luthor:_Drop_of_Doom). The free-fall drop attraction was integrated into both sides of the park's 415 feet (126 m) tall [Superman: Escape from Krypton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman:_Escape_from_Krypton) tower structure and ranks as the world's tallest drop tower, featuring a plummet from 400 feet (120 m) above ground level. The following day, Six Flags Magic Mountain confirmed on Facebook that Déjà Vu would be removed from the park.[^cite_note-Deja_Vu_removal-45] In September 2011, the park announced that Déjà Vu would be removed after October 16, 2011, "Déjà Vu fans, we have created some exclusive after-hours ride time for you to ride it again before October 16."[^cite_note-Deja_Vu_date-46]

On October 31, 2011, Log Jammer operated for the last time and was removed to make way for [Full Throttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Throttle_\(roller_coaster\)), which opened in 2013.

In August 2012, Six Flags Magic Mountain confirmed rumors that a new roller coaster, [Full Throttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Throttle_\(roller_coaster\)), would open the following season. Full Throttle opened as the park's 18th roller coaster, allowing Six Flags Magic Mountain to market having the most roller coasters in the world once again. The ride was built to feature the world's tallest vertical loop on a roller coaster at 160 feet (49 m). Full Throttle set a record for being the first roller coaster to feature a track section with rails on both sides of the spine. This occurs at the top of the ride's massive inversion.[^cite_note-Full_Throttle_Review-47]

In August 2013, Six Flags Magic Mountain announced that they would run both Batman: The Ride and Colossus backwards for a limited time of the 2014 season. They will also expand Bugs Bunny World with the addition of a new roller coaster.[^cite_note-48] On April 8, 2014, Six Flags Magic Mountain announced that the park will host its first ever *[Holiday in the Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_in_the_Park)* Christmas event in late 2014 and for future years after.[^cite_note-SFMM-49]

In the summer of 2014, the park placed banners across the property advertising the Bonzai Pipelines in the adjacent property, [Hurricane Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Hurricane_Harbor), along with the closing of Colossus which took place on August 16, 2014.[^cite_note-50] On August 28, 2014, Six Flags announced the [Rocky Mountain Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_Construction) conversion of Colossus into [Twisted Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_Colossus). Twisted Colossus opened on May 23, 2015.[^cite_note-51]

In September 2015, Six Flags announced the renovation of [Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Revolution_\(roller_coaster\)) with a new paint scheme, upgraded lighting, and new train eliminating the controversial over-the-shoulder restraints that had been the source of the ride's spotty reputation for much of its life. Named "The New Revolution", the roller coaster reopened on April 21, 2016.

In September 2016, the park announced [Justice League: Battle for Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_League:_Battle_for_Metropolis) to open in 2017. The 4D shooting dark ride is nearly identical to the six other installations located at Six Flags parks around North America. The ride opened on July 12, 2017, and is located in the Metropolis section near The Riddler's Revenge.[^cite_note-JusticeLeagueBattle-52]

In August 2017, Six Flags announced the addition of a [Zamperla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamperla) Giga Discovery flat ride to be built in a newly renovated Boardwalk Beach area near [DC Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Universe_\(themed_area\)).[^cite_note-53] Marketed as "the world's tallest pendulum ride", CraZanity takes riders to a height of 172 feet (52 m) and speeds up to 75 miles per hour (121 km/h).[^cite_note-54]

In August 2018, the park announced the brand new racing launch coaster [West Coast Racers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Racers) from [Premier Rides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Rides) and a revamp of the old Cyclone Bay area into a high-energy, urban [Los Angeles](18110.md). The ride is dubbed the first launched racing coaster in the world and the first quadruple-launched coaster, even though the existing [Fiorano GT Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorano_GT_Challenge) holds these records. The Möbius loop coaster opened to the public in January 2020, and became the 19th coaster at the park.

In March 2019, the park announced that [Green Lantern: First Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern:_First_Flight_\(roller_coaster\)) would permanently close and be removed from the park, which no longer makes West Coast Racers the park's 20th coaster.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

On March 13, 2020, the park closed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The theme park reopened on April 1, 2021, with Members and Passholders being granted access on April 1 and 2, with the general public being admitted to the park on April 3.[^cite_note-57] As of August 2021, Six Flags Magic Mountain visitors are no longer required to wear masks outdoors.[^cite_note-58]

In October 2021, the park announced [Wonder Woman Flight of Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Woman_Flight_of_Courage), the world's tallest and fastest single rail coaster, to open in summer 2022. The ride is an I-Box Raptor coaster built by Rocky Mountain Construction.[^cite_note-59]

Starting November 1, 2022, the 365-day schedule, introduced in 2018 to "maximize travel industry opportunities", was limited to select weekends only during non-peak months. Park Marketing & Communications Publicist Alexandria French said in a statement that the changed operation schedule would deliver a more "exceptional guest experience".[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

In Spring 2023, Scream Break debuted at Magic Mountain, as well as [Six Flags Over Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Over_Texas), [Six Flags Fiesta Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Fiesta_Texas), [Six Flags Over Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Over_Georgia), [Six Flags Discovery Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Discovery_Kingdom), [Six Flags America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_America), and [Six Flags Great Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_Adventure). The event lasted from March 18 to April 16 on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays except for April 1–9. Hosted in the areas of DC Universe, Screampunk District, Full Throttle Plaza, and Six Flags Plaza, the event featured aspects of Fright Fest, such as themed props, shows, and mazes. Admission was available free for passholders on a first come, first served basis.[^cite_note-62]

In early 2024, demolition work began on the Golden Bear Theater, Formerly Showcase Theater. After an incident at a TLC concert in the 90s, the theater sat dormant with the exception of the occasional regional cheer championship or religious gathering. In a November 2024 press release, Six Flags Magic Mountain publicly announced that a new 'Thrill Glider' Vekoma roller coaster, (currently under construction), was set to open in 2026, and would be built in and around the remnants of the Golden Bear Theater.

On March 28, 2025, it was announced that Six Flags Magic Mountain would close [Superman: Escape from Krypton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman:_Escape_from_Krypton)[^cite_note-63] forever. According to the park president, the reason for the ride's closure was due to high maintenance costs and the difficulty of acquiring parts for the aging attraction.

On December 16, 2025, it was announced that Bugs Bunny World and Whistlestop Park will be permanently closed to make way for a new land, **Looney Tunes Land** at Six Flags Magic Mountain, and divided into **TAZ–Mania**, **Road Runner Ridge**, **Bugs Bunny Play Park** and **Camp Duck Amuck**, which debuted on June 6, 2026.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

In July 2026, within six days of one another two women who rode X2 suffered from similar brain hemorrhages. Surgeons wrote that the severe brain injuries "were the result of a traumatic rapid acceleration-deceleration event experienced while on the X2 ride." Subsequent reporting revealed that X2 has been linked to more than a dozen reports of serious injuries and hospitalizations over nearly two decades, including riders who suffered life-altering disabilities, fell into comas and two who died of their injuries. Cal/OSHA is investigating and the ride remained closed since the second woman was hospitalized.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] On September 29, Six Flags announced that the closure would be permanent, stating their "purpose is to create FUN, thrills and a lifetime of memories by creating unforgettable experiences that are underpinned by comprehensive safety policies" while also stating that X2 had passed safety tests.[^cite_note-guard-29sep2026-68][^cite_note-sf-29sep2026-69]

## Cinema, television, and computer games

Magic Mountain's proximity to downtown Los Angeles, the hub of the American film and television industry, has resulted in its appearance in several productions, usually representing a park other than itself. The debut of *Revolution* was the focal point of the 1977 release *[Rollercoaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollercoaster_\(1977_film\))*. [Bob Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Einstein), as his character [Super Dave Osborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Dave_Osborne), performed his first "stunt" on a roller coaster at Magic Mountain. In 1983, Magic Mountain became the fictional "Walley World" for *[National Lampoon's Vacation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lampoon's_Vacation)*, with scenes featuring *Revolution* and *Colossus*, each using fictional names.

On television, Magic Mountain doubled as the theme park in the opening credits of the television series *[Step by Step](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Step_by_Step_\(TV_series\))*. Other television productions featuring Magic Mountain have included: *[NCIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCIS_\(franchise\))*, *[Entourage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entourage_\(U.S._TV_series\))*, *[The Bionic Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bionic_Woman)*, *[The A-Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A-Team)*, *[CHiPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHiPs)*, *[Wonder Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Woman_\(TV_series\))*, *[Way Out Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Way_Out_Games)*, *[Knight Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Rider_\(1982_TV_series\))*, *[Beverly Hills, 90210](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_90210)*, *[Melrose Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melrose_Place)*, *[The King of Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King_of_Queens)*, *[Fresh Off the Boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_Off_the_Boat)*, and *[Buffy the Vampire Slayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffy_the_Vampire_Slayer_\(TV_series\))*. The band [Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_\(band\)) filmed their acting debut in 1978's [made-for-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made-for-TV) *[Kiss Meets the Phantom of the Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_Meets_the_Phantom_of_the_Park)* that featured the band members in the park and near Colossus. In the 2000 film *[Space Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Cowboys)*, Donald Sutherland is shown riding Viper and is portrayed as the designer when [Clint Eastwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Eastwood) recruits him.

Magic Mountain's Showcase Theatre was the filming location for the video game-themed game show *[The Video Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Video_Game_\(game_show\))* from September 1984 to September 1985.

Magic Mountain was also the filming location for the children's educational video series *[Real Wheels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Wheels)* episode "Here Comes A Roller Coaster", with host [Dave Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Hood), which was released in 1995.

Magic Mountain was used as a filming site for the 1990 *[Kidsongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidsongs)* video, "Ride the Roller Coaster".

Colossus was filmed as the Serpent in the 1993 film, *[My Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_\(film\))*.

Colossus was used for filming for the 1995 *[Muppets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muppets)* video, *Muppets on Wheels*.

In the [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon) show *[Drake & Josh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_&_Josh)*, Drake, Josh, and Megan take a trip to Mystic Mountain (parody of Magic Mountain) in the episode "The Demonator", and they ride the "Demonator" roller coaster. Mystic Mountain was also featured in the *[Zoey 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoey_101)* episode "Rollercoaster" (as both series were created by [Dan Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Schneider_\(TV_producer\))), where Zoey and Lisa take Michael to the park to help him overcome his roller coaster fear. He rides the "Spine Twister", which was actually the [Goliath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)) from Magic Mountain.

In 1990, Nickelodeon's *[Wild and Crazy Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_and_Crazy_Kids)*, the wooden roller coaster, Colossus, was featured as a game called "Wacky RollerCoaster Spill". In the 1984 film *[This Is Spinal Tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Spinal_Tap)*, the band performs as second billing to a puppet show at the fictional "Themeland Amusement Park" in [Stockton, California](99302.md), located 300 miles (480 km) north of Santa Clarita. The actual filming location is Magic Mountain's amphitheater.

The [Kidsongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidsongs) video *Ride the Roller Coaster* is set at Six Flags Magic Mountain. Nick Cannon group *[The School Gyrls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_School_Gyrls)* movie premiere was at Magic Mountain. Six Flags Magic Mountain and its coaster Goliath are featured in the 2011 film *[Judy Moody and the Not Bummer Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Moody_and_the_Not_Bummer_Summer)*. Goliath was also featured as the "Aquaman" roller coaster in the third season of the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) series *[Entourage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entourage_\(U.S._TV_series\))*.

In 2011, the park was chosen as the setting for the [Travel Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_Channel)'s version of the quiz show *[Scream! If You Know the Answer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scream!_If_You_Know_the_Answer)*. The *[Glee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glee_\(TV_series\))* cast visited the park in 2012 for their senior skip day in the "[Big Brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother_\(Glee\))" episode, where they ride [Viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viper_\(Six_Flags_Magic_Mountain\)).[^cite_note-Glee-71]

In 2013, a large section of the parking lot was blocked off for a Toyota Camry commercial. Both pictures and the background footage reveal Goliath and Colossus, indicating that it is Magic Mountain where the commercial was shot. The ride that was built for the commercial bears a resemblance to the park's new coaster at the time, Full Throttle: a big hill, a barrier-test loop, a backwards propulsion section, and a forwards propulsion section that runs through a tunnel placed next to the hill.[^cite_note-ToyotaCommercial-72]

In 2013, the [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon) TV show *[Sam & Cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_&_Cat)* featured an episode about the protagonists organizing a day at Mystic Mountain (the same park featured in *Drake & Josh* and *Zoey 101*), although the park was never filmed this time.

In 2016, Goliath was used for a [Carpool Karaoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpool_Karaoke) segment with [Selena Gomez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selena_Gomez).

In 2017, the park and [Full Throttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Throttle_\(roller_coaster\)) were used in the filming of the music video for [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry)'s "[Chained to the Rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_to_the_Rhythm)".

In 2017, areas of the park were used in the filming of the Netflix comedy film *[Sandy Wexler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Wexler)* starring [Adam Sandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sandler).

In 2021, some areas of the park were used in the filming of the Netflix comedy film *[Yes Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_Day)*.

Although the park is not featured, Magic Mountain is mentioned numerous times in the Netflix horror-comedy *[Santa Clarita Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clarita_Diet)*.

A recreation of Magic Mountain was featured built in the computer game *[RollerCoaster Tycoon 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RollerCoaster_Tycoon_2)*, including both a blank version of the park with no rides and attractions and a version of the park as it stood at the game's release in 2002, albeit missing both Batman: The Ride and the Riddler's Revenge.
