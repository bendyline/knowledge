**Skyroot Aerospace Private Limited** is an Indian private [aerospace manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer) and commercial [launch service provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_launch_service_providers#Corporate) headquartered in [Hyderabad](37534.md), Telangana. The company was founded by two former [ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO) scientists, Pawan Kumar Chandana, and Naga Bharath Daka.

On 18 November 2022, Skyroot became the first private company in India to launch a [suborbital rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounding_rocket), [Vikram-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram-S).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] On 18 July 2026, Skyroot successfully launched India's first private orbital class rocket [Vikram-1](70739748.md) into orbit, making India the third country with a private orbital launch capability after the U.S. and China.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-BBC_2026-07-18-6]

## History

### Founding and early development



Initial logo of Skyroot Aerospace

Skyroot Aerospace was founded in 2018 by former ISRO scientists Pawan Kumar Chandana and Naga Bharath Daka. The company sought to develop commercially competitive launch vehicles for the global [small satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite) market.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

The company emerged during a period of increasing government support for private participation in India's space industry. Reforms introduced in 2020 enabled private firms to access certain ISRO facilities and created a regulatory framework under the oversight of [Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Space_Promotion_and_Authorisation_Centre) (IN-SPACe).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

During its initial years, Skyroot focused on developing launch vehicle technologies, and manufacturing processes aimed at reducing launch costs and production times. The company adopted technologies including [carbon-composite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-fiber_reinforced_polymer) structures, [3D printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printing), and modular launch vehicle designs.[^cite_note-Ars_2026-05-11-12][^cite_note-TheHindu_2023-04-04-13]

### Mission Prarambh



Pawan Kumar Chandana (CEO & CTO) and Naga Bharath Daka (COO) (standing on left and right respectively of Union minister [Jitendra Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jitendra_Singh_\(politician,_born_1956\)) in middle) on the eve of successful [Vikram-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram-S) launch

[^cite_note-14]

On 18 November 2022, Skyroot launched [Vikram-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram-S), a suborbital rocket, from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota under a mission designated Prarambh (lit. 'the beginning'). Vikram-S used the existing launch pad and ground support system infrastructure provided by the government of India at [Satish Dhawan Space Centre](2496664.md) (SDSC). The mission reached an altitude of approximately 89.5 km (55.6 mi) and marked the first successful launch of a privately developed rocket from India. The event was widely regarded as a milestone in the development of India's private space industry.[^cite_note-15]

### Expansion and funding

Following the Vikram-S mission, Skyroot raised capital from domestic and international investors. On 30 October 2023, the company announced that it had raised US\$27.5 million in a pre-Series C funding round led by [Temasek Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temasek_Holdings) that brought its cumulative funding to approximately US\$95 million.[^cite_note-16]

On 21 January 2025, the [Government of Telangana](42119537.md) signed a MoU with Skyroot Aerospace at the [World Economic Forum](172185.md) to establish a private rocket manufacturing, integration, and testing facility in [Telangana](990267.md) with an estimated investment of [₹](490783.md)500 [crores](121420.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

On 7 May 2026, following its latest funding round of approximately US\$60 million, Skyroot Aerospace crossed US\$1.1 billion in valuation. With this, it became India's first space-tech unicorn.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Mission Aagaman

On 2 July 2026, the company announced a launch window from 12 July to 4 August 2026 for its first orbital mission, 'Mission Aagaman' (lit. 'the arrival'), using the [Vikram-1](70739748.md) launch vehicle from SDSC. The mission is intended to demonstrate the rocket's propulsion, guidance, and stage-separation systems while carrying domestic and international payloads.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

On 18 July 2026, the launch was successful, with the mission meeting all its objectives and deploying payload at an altitude of 450 km (280 mi). Skyroot became the first private Indian company to successfully reach space in its first attempt. This made India the third country in the world after the U.S. and China to have rocket launch by private company.[^cite_note-BBC_2026-07-18-6]

### Rocket engines

#### Solid rocket motors

##### Kalam-100

The *Kalam-100* (named after [A. P. J. Abdul Kalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._P._J._Abdul_Kalam)) is the third-stage solid rocket motor of *Vikram-1*. The stage produces a peak vacuum thrust of approximately 100 kN (or \~10 tons). In April 2025, Skyroot conducted successful static firing of *Kalam-100* featuring an advanced flex-nozzle system for thrust vector control. The test lasted over 102 seconds and formed part of the qualification programme for the maiden orbital flight of *Vikram-1*.[^cite_note-25]

##### Kalam-250

The *Kalam-250* is the second-stage solid rocket motor of *Vikram-1*. for the second stage of the *Vikram-1* launch vehicle. It was successfully static-tested on 27 March 2024 at ISRO's Satish Dhawan Space Centre, validating its 85-second burn and critical propulsion systems.[^cite_note-26]

##### Kalam-1200

The *Kalam-1200* is the first-stage solid rocket motor of *Vikram-1*. On 8 August 2025, Skyroot Aerospace successfully conducted the first static test of the *Kalam-1200* solid rocket motor at SDSC, [Sriharikota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriharikota). The 110 second test validated the booster of the *Vikram-1*.[^cite_note-27]

#### Earth-storable rocket engine

Main article: [Raman (rocket engine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raman_\(rocket_engine\))

##### Raman-I

*Raman-I* (named after [C. V. Raman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._V._Raman)) is a liquid-fuel upper stage engine of *Vikram-1*.[^cite_note-28]

#### Cryogenic rocket engines

##### Dhawan-I

On 25 September 2020, Skyroot Aerospace unveiled the *Dhawan-I* (named after [Satish Dhawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satish_Dhawan)) upper stage cryogenic engine that will power heavier-lift systems such as *Vikram-2.* This is India's first privately developed small cryogenic engine running on [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) and [liquid oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_oxygen) (LOX). *Dhawan-I* is [3D printed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printing) and designed with a [regenerative cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regenerative_cooling_\(rocketry\)) configuration. The engine is made by using [superalloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superalloy).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

##### Dhawan-II

On 5 April 2023, Skyroot announced the successful completion of a 200-second endurance static-fire test of its *Dhawan-II* engine, showcasing the viability of large-scale additive manufacturing for cryogenic propulsion.[^cite_note-31]

##### Dhawan-III

The *Dhawan-III* engine represents an evolution of the earlier *Dhawan-I* and *Dhawan-II* engines, incorporating improvements aimed at future [reusable launch vehicles](336014.md) and advanced upper-stage propulsion systems. In February 2026, Skyroot announced that *Dhawan-III* had successfully completed a 145-second endurance static-fire test. The firing was conducted on an indigenous mobile test stand designed and built in-house by the company. The test was intended to validate the engine's performance, combustion stability, and operational reliability over an extended duration.[^cite_note-32]

## Facilities

Skyroot Aerospace has modern facilities for designing, building, testing, and assembling rockets and their components. These facilities help the company develop launch vehicles, test rocket engines, prepare missions, and support commercial satellite launches with advanced engineering and technology.[^cite_note-33]

### MAX-Q

On 24 October 2023, the largest private integrated rocket development facility in India was unveiled by Skyroot Aerospace.[^cite_note-\:0-34][^cite_note-35] It is situated next to the [Rajiv Gandhi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_International_Airport) and spans 60,000 square feet. With space for about 300 personnel, this facility will host Skyroot's integrated design, manufacturing, and testing infrastructure for developing rockets.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### Infinity Campus

On 27 November 2025, Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) inaugurated the Infinity Campus of Skyroot Aerospace. Spread across 200,000 square feet in Hyderabad, this facility will be able to design, manufacture, integrate, and test a number of launch vehicles and produce one orbital rocket each month.[^cite_note-38]

## Industry collaboration

An agreement has been signed by Nibe Space, a division of Nibe Defence and Aerospace on 9 September 2024, with Skyroot Aerospace, [AgniKul Cosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgniKul_Cosmos), [Centum Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centum_Electronics), SpaceFields, Sisir Radar, CYRAN AI Solutions, and [Larsen & Toubro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larsen_&_Toubro) for the launch of India's first constellation of multi-sensor, all-weather, high-revisit Earth observation satellites.[^cite_note-39]

On 25 June 2025, Skyroot Aerospace and [Axiom Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_Space) signed an MoU to work together to increase access to LEO.[^cite_note-40] Skyroot Aerospace and Axiom Space will look into orbital and launch systems that are integrated for next trips to Axiom Station and beyond.[^cite_note-41] The two businesses intend to look into potential joint ventures for the construction of space infrastructure.[^cite_note-42] By connecting Axiom's under-construction commercial space station and other LEO projects with Skyroot's upcoming Vikram-1 launch capability, the partnership hopes to create a new logistics corridor for research payloads, orbital data-center nodes, and upcoming commercial missions.[^cite_note-43]
