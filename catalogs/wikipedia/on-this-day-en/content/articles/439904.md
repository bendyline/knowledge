The **prime minister of Mongolia**[^cite_note-5] is the [head of government](37570.md) of [Mongolia](19271.md). Under the [Constitution of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Mongolia), the [State Great Khural](2226080.md) appoints the Prime Minister upon the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Mongolia)’s submission of a candidate nominated by the parliamentary majority.[^cite_note-6] The Prime Minister shall be dismissed if no fewer than one-fourth of the members of the State Great Khural formally submit a proposal for the Prime Minister’s dismissal and a majority of all members approve it,[^cite_note-7] or if a resolution expressing [confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_of_no_confidence) in the Prime Minister is not adopted.[^cite_note-8] The incumbent prime minister is [Nyam-Osoryn Uchral](61624092.md), who succeeded [Gombojavyn Zandanshatar](31495583.md) on 30 March 2026[^cite_note-9], following his resignation three days earlier.[^cite_note-\:5-10]

## Roles and responsibilities

The prime minister holds the authority to hire and dismiss Cabinet ministers[^cite_note-11] and is responsible for appointing the governors of the 21 [aimags of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Mongolia), as well as the [governor of the capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Ulaanbaatar), [Ulaanbaatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulaanbaatar).[^cite_note-12] Additionally, the Prime Minister plays a crucial role in shaping domestic policy and directing the government’s legislative agenda.[^cite_note-13] The appointment of key figures in the government, such as the head of various state agencies and ministries, also falls within the Prime Minister’s scope of influence.[^cite_note-14]

In the case of political reforms, the prime minister's decisions can significantly impact governance structures at both the national and provincial levels, reflecting the centralization of executive power within [Mongolia's political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Mongolia).[^cite_note-15] This concentration of authority can, at times, lead to calls for decentralization, as various political actors advocate for more autonomy in decision-making at the regional level.[^cite_note-16]

## History

The office of the Prime Minister was established by the [Bogd Khanate of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogd_Khanate_of_Mongolia) in 1912, shortly after Outer Mongolia first [declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Mongolian_revolution_of_1911) from the Manchu [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). This was not recognized by many nations in the 1910s. By the time of Mongolia's second (and more generally recognized) declaration of independence from the [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Mongolia) by [Beiyang China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiyang_China) in 1921, the office was controlled by the [Mongolian People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_People's_Party).

In 1924, after the death of the [Bogd Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogd_Khan), the party established the [Mongolian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_People's_Republic), and the prime minister's post was superseded by the post of Chairman of the [Council of People's Commissars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_People's_Commissars), an institution similar to those in many other Soviet republics at the time. This was later changed to the Chairman of the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers) in 1939. The title of prime minister was only revived in 1990, when the sole ruling party gradually released its hold on power after the [1990 Democratic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Democratic_Revolution_in_Mongolia). Regardless of the name changes, however, the modern [Mongolian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Mongolia) recognizes the office as having existed continuously since 1912 and counts all holders of the office as prime ministers.[^cite_note-17]

There is some confusion as to the first holder of the office. [Da Lam Tserenchimed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Lam_Tserenchimed) held office as "prime minister" (actually the [Interior minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_and_Internal_Affairs_\(Mongolia\))) during a provisional government from 1911 to 1912 and is sometimes cited as the first holder of the modern office. However, the Mongolian government considers [Tögs-Ochiryn Namnansüren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B6gs-Ochiryn_Namnans%C3%BCren), the first formal officeholder, to be the first.[^cite_note-18] There is also some confusion over the status of [Tsengeltiin Jigjidjav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsengeltiin_Jigjidjav); some consider him to have been only acting Prime Minister, while others consider him to have been a full Prime Minister. The Mongolian government takes the latter view.[^cite_note-19]
