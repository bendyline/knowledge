The **McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle** is an American [twin-engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinjet), all-weather [fighter aircraft](10929.md) designed by [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas) (now part of [Boeing](18933266.md)). Following reviews of proposals, the [United States Air Force](32090.md) (USAF) selected McDonnell Douglas's design in 1969 to meet the service's need for a dedicated [air superiority fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority_fighter). The Eagle took its maiden flight in July 1972, and entered service in 1976. It is among the most successful modern fighters, with over 104 victories and no losses in air-to-air combat.

The Eagle has been exported to several countries, including [Israel](177619.md), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Air_Self-Defense_Force), and [Saudi Arabia](570367.md). Although the F-15 was originally envisioned as a pure air superiority fighter, its design included a secondary ground-attack capability that was largely unused. It proved flexible enough that an improved all-weather [strike derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_fighter), the [F-15E Strike Eagle](495724.md), was later developed. That fighter entered service in 1989 and has been exported to several nations. Several additional Eagle and Strike Eagle subvariants have been produced for foreign customers, with production of enhanced variants ongoing.

The F-15 was the principal air superiority fighter of the USAF and several U.S. allies during the late [Cold War](325329.md), replacing the [F-4 Phantom II](11759.md). The Eagle was first used in combat by the Israeli Air Force in 1979 and saw extensive action in the [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War). In USAF service, the aircraft saw combat action in the 1991 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) and the conflict over [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars). The USAF began replacing its air superiority F-15 fighters with the [F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) in the 2000s. However, reduced procurement of the F-22 pushed the retirement of the remaining F-15C/D, mostly in the [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard), to 2030 and forced the service to supplement the F-22 with an advanced Eagle variant, the [F-15EX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F-15EX_Eagle_II), to maintain enough air superiority fighters. The F-15 remains in service with several countries.

## Development

### Early studies

The F-15 can trace its origins to the early [Vietnam War](32611.md), when the [U.S. Air Force](32090.md) and [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) fought each other over future tactical aircraft. [Defense Secretary](43998.md) [Robert McNamara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McNamara) was pressing for both services to use as many common aircraft as possible, even if performance compromises were involved. As part of this policy, the USAF and Navy had embarked on the [TFX (F-111)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-111_Aardvark) program, aiming to deliver a medium-range [interdiction aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdiction_aircraft) for the Air Force that would also serve as a long-range [interceptor aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interceptor_aircraft) for the Navy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200742-3]

In January 1965, Secretary McNamara asked the Air Force to consider a new low-cost tactical fighter design for short-range roles and [close air support](600792.md) to replace several types like the [F-100 Super Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_F-100_Super_Sabre) and various [light bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_bomber) then in service. Several existing designs could fill this role; the Navy favored the [Douglas A-4 Skyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk) and [LTV A-7 Corsair II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTV_A-7_Corsair_II), which were pure [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft), while the Air Force was more interested in the [Northrop F-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5) fighter with a secondary attack capability. The A-4 and A-7 were more capable in the attack role, while the F-5 less so, but could defend itself. If the Air Force chose a pure attack design, maintaining air superiority would be a priority for a new [airframe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airframe). The next month, a report on light tactical aircraft suggested the Air Force purchase the F-5 or A-7, and consider a new higher-performance aircraft to ensure its air superiority. This point was reinforced after the loss of two [Republic F-105 Thunderchief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_F-105_Thunderchief) aircraft to obsolete [MiG-17s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-17) attacking the [Thanh Hóa Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanh_H%C3%B3a_Bridge) on 4 April 1965.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200742-3]

In April 1965, [Harold Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Brown_\(Secretary_of_Defense\)), at that time director of the [Department of Defense Research and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Research_and_Engineering), stated the favored position was to consider the F-5 and begin studies of an "F-X".[^cite_note-4] These early studies envisioned a production run of 800 to 1,000 aircraft and stressed maneuverability over speed; it also stated that the aircraft would not be considered without some level of ground-attack capability.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200743-5] On 1 August, General [Gabriel Disosway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_P._Disosway) took command of [Tactical Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_Air_Command) (TAC) and reiterated calls for the F-X, but lowered the required performance from Mach 3.0 to 2.5 to lower costs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200744-6]



A model of the F-15 Eagle is adjusted before undergoing aerodynamic testing in the mid-1970s in the 4-foot [transonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transonic) [wind tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_tunnel) at Arnold Air Force Base, Tennessee

An official requirements document for an air superiority fighter was finalized in October 1965, and sent out as a [request for proposals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Request_for_proposal) to 13 companies on 8 December. Meanwhile, the Air Force chose the A-7 over the F-5 for the support role on 5 November 1965,[^cite_note-7] giving further impetus for an air superiority design as the A-7 lacked any credible air-to-air capability.

Eight companies responded with proposals. Following a downselect, four companies were asked to provide further developments. In total, they developed some 500 design concepts. Typical designs featured [variable-sweep wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable-sweep_wing), weight over 60,000 pounds (27,000 kg), a top speed of [Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number) 2.7 and a [thrust-to-weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-to-weight_ratio) of 0.75.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins19985–7-8] When the proposals were studied in July 1966, the aircraft were roughly the size and weight of the TFX F-111, and like that aircraft, were designs that could not be considered an air-superiority fighter.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200746-9]

### Smaller, lighter

Jet aircraft with distinctive orange markings banking left over desert, with landing gears extended

McDonnell Douglas F-15A (S/N 71-0280) during the type's first flight

Through this period, studies of combat over Vietnam were producing worrying results. Theory had stressed long-range combat using missiles and optimized aircraft for this role. The result was highly loaded aircraft with large radar and excellent speed, but limited maneuverability and often lacking a gun. The canonical example was the [McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II](11759.md), used by the USAF, USN, and [U.S. Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marine_Corps) to provide air superiority over Vietnam, the only fighter with enough power, range, and maneuverability to be given the primary task of dealing with the threat of Soviet fighters while flying with visual engagement rules.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavies20029–11-10]

In practice, due to policy and practical reasons,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavies20029–11-10] aircraft were closing to visual range and maneuvering, placing the larger US aircraft at a disadvantage to the much less expensive [day fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_fighter) such as the [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21). Missiles proved to be much less reliable than predicted, especially at close range. Although improved training and the introduction of the [M61 Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M61_Vulcan) cannon on the F-4 did much to address the disparity, these early outcomes led to considerable re-evaluation of the 1963 Project Forecast doctrine.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] This led to [John Boyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boyd_\(military_strategist\))'s [energy–maneuverability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy%E2%80%93maneuverability_theory), which stressed that [extra power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_power) and maneuverability were key aspects of a successful fighter design and these were more important than outright speed. Through tireless championing of the concepts and good timing with the "failure" of the initial F-X project, the "[fighter mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_mafia)" pressed for a lightweight day fighter that could be built and operated in large numbers to ensure air superiority.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200746–47-13] In early 1967, they proposed that the ideal design had a [thrust-to-weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-to-weight_ratio) near 1\:1, a maximum speed further reduced to Mach 2.3, a weight of 40,000 pounds (18,000 kg), and a [wing loading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_loading) of 80 pounds per square foot (390 kg/m<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200747-14]

By this time, the Navy had decided the F-111 would not meet their requirements and began the development of a new dedicated fighter design, the VFAX program. In May 1966, McNamara again asked the forces to study the designs and see whether the VFAX would meet the Air Force's F-X needs. The resulting studies took 18 months and concluded that the desired features were too different; the Navy stressed loiter time and mission flexibility, while the Air Force was now looking primarily for maneuverability.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200749-15]

### Focus on air superiority

In 1967, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) revealed the [Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-25) at the [Domodedovo airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodedovo_International_Airport) near [Moscow](19004.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavies20029–11-10][^cite_note-16] The MiG-25 was designed as a high-speed, high-altitude interceptor aircraft, and made many performance tradeoffs to excel in this role.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowman1980193-17] Among these was the requirement for very high speed, over Mach 2.8, which demanded the use of [stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainless_steel) instead of [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum) for many parts of the aircraft. The added weight demanded a much larger wing to allow the aircraft to operate at the required high [altitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude). However, to observers, it appeared outwardly similar to the very large F-X studies, an aircraft with high speed and a large wing offering high maneuverability, leading to serious concerns throughout the Department of Defense and the various arms that the US was being outclassed. The [MiG-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-23) was likewise a subject of concern, and it was generally believed to be a better aircraft than the F-4. The F-X would outclass the MiG-23, but now the MiG-25 appeared to be superior in speed, ceiling, and endurance to all existing US fighters, even the F-X.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200748-18] Thus, an effort to improve the F-X followed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaviesDildy200712-19]

Both USAF headquarters and TAC continued to call for a multipurpose aircraft, while both Disosway and Air Chief of Staff [Bruce K. Holloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_K._Holloway) pressed for a pure air-superiority design that would be able to meet the expected performance of the MiG-25. During the same period, the Navy had ended its VFAX program and instead accepted a proposal from [Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman) for a smaller and more maneuverable design known as VFX, later becoming the [Grumman F-14 Tomcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F-14_Tomcat). VFX was considerably closer to the evolving F-X requirements. The Air Force in-fighting was eventually ended by the worry that the Navy's VFAX would be forced on them; in May 1968, it was stated that "We finally decided – and I hope there is no one who still disagrees – that this aircraft is going to be an air superiority fighter".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200749-15]

F-15A [cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockpit)

In September 1968, a request for proposals was released to major aerospace companies. These requirements called for single-seat fighter having a maximum take-off weight of 40,000 pounds (18,000 kg) for the air-to-air role with a maximum speed of Mach 2.5 and a thrust-to-weight ratio of nearly 1\:1 at mission weight.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins19988–10-20] It also called for a twin-engined arrangement, as this was believed to respond to throttle changes more rapidly and might offer commonality with the Navy's VFX program. However, details of the avionics were left largely undefined, as whether to build a larger aircraft with a powerful radar that could detect the enemy at longer ranges was not clear, or alternatively a smaller aircraft that would make detecting it more difficult for the enemy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeufeld200752-21]

Four companies submitted proposals, with the Air Force eliminating [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics) and awarding contracts to [Fairchild Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)), [North American Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation), and [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas) for the definition phase in December 1968. The companies submitted technical proposals by June 1969. The Air Force announced the selection of McDonnell Douglas on 23 December 1969; like the Navy's VFX, the F-X skipped much of the prototype phase and jumped straight into full-scale development to save time and avoid potential program cancellation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins19989–11-22] The winning design resembled the twin-tailed [F-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F-14_Tomcat), but with fixed [wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing); both designs were based on configurations studied in wind-tunnel testing by [NASA](18426568.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaviesDildy200714-23]



An early USAF F-15A

Formally named the "Eagle" upon its introduction, the aircraft's initial versions were the F-15 single-seat variant and TF-15 twin-seat variant; after the F-15C was first flown, the designations were changed to "F-15A" and "F-15B". These versions would be powered by new [Pratt & Whitney F100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_F100) engines to achieve a combat thrust-to-weight ratio in excess of 1\:1. A proposed 25-mm Ford-Philco [GAU-7 cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAU-7_cannon) with [caseless ammunition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caseless_ammunition) suffered development problems and was dropped in favor of the standard M61 Vulcan gun. The F-15 used conformal carriage of four [Sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-7_Sparrow) missiles like the Phantom. The fixed wing was put onto a flat, wide [fuselage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage) that also provided an effective [lifting body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifting_body) surface. The airframe was designed with a 4,000 hour service life, although this was later increased through testing and life extension modifications to 8,000 hours and some would fly beyond that.[^cite_note-lifespan-24] The first F-15A flight was made on 27 July 1972, with the first flight of the two-seat F-15B following in July 1973.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpick2000130–131-25]

The F-15 has a "[look-down/shoot-down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-down/shoot-down)" [radar](25676.md) that can distinguish low-flying moving targets from ground [clutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutter_\(radar\)). It would use computer technology with new controls and displays to lower pilot workload and require only one pilot to save weight. Unlike the F-14 or F-4, the F-15 has only a single [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_canopy) frame with clear vision forward. The USAF introduced the F-15 as "the first dedicated USAF air-superiority fighter since the [North American F-86 Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_F-86_Sabre)".[^cite_note-26]

The F-15 was favored by customers such as the Israel and Japan air arms. Criticism from the [fighter mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_mafia) that the F-15 was too large to be a dedicated [dogfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogfight) and too expensive to procure in large numbers, led to the [Lightweight Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightweight_Fighter) (LWF) program, which led to the USAF [General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon](11642.md) and the middle-weight Navy [McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet](45998.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins20001–8-27]

### Upgrades and further development

Main articles: [McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle](495724.md) and [Boeing F-15EX Eagle II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F-15EX_Eagle_II)



USAF F-15C during [Operation Noble Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Noble_Eagle) patrol, 2007

The single-seat F-15C and two-seat F-15D models entered production in 1978 and conducted their first flights in February and June of that year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins199833–34-28] These models were fitted with the Production Eagle Package (PEP 2000), which included 2,000 pounds (910 kg) of additional internal fuel, provisions for exterior [conformal fuel tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_fuel_tank) (CFT), and an increased maximum takeoff weight up to 68,000 pounds (31,000 kg).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreenSwanborough1998371-29] The increased takeoff weight allows internal fuel, a full weapons load, conformal fuel tanks, and three external fuel tanks to be carried. The [APG-63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APG-63) radar would later be upgraded with a programmable signal processor (PSP), enabling the radar to be reprogrammable for additional purposes such as the addition of new armament and equipment. The PSP was the first of its kind in the world, and the upgraded AN/APG-63 radar was the first radar to use it. Other improvements included strengthened landing gear, a new digital central computer,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaviesDildy2007115-30] and an aural overload warning system (OWS), which allows the pilot to fly up to 9 *g* at all weights.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins199833–34-28]



F-15A 75–057 in service with the Florida Air National Guard flying over Florida with AIM-9X and AIM-120 missiles loaded

The F-15 Multistage Improvement Program (MSIP) was initiated in February 1983 with the first production MSIP F-15C produced in 1985. Initially this was planned to be done to both the F-15A and F-15C, with the MSIP I program for the F-15A and MSIP II for F-15C. However, MSIP I would be dropped and only the MSIP II portion for the F-15C would move forward originally. Improvements included an upgraded central computer; the [AN/APG-63 PSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APG-63\(V\)2) radar, a Programmable Armament Control Set, allowing for advanced versions of the [AIM-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-7), [AIM-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9), and [AIM-120A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120A) missiles; and an expanded Tactical Electronic Warfare System that provides improvements to the ALR-56C radar warning receiver and [ALQ-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/ALQ-135) countermeasure set. The final 43 production F-15Cs included the Hughes [APG-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APG-70) radar developed for the F-15E (see below); these are sometimes referred as Enhanced Eagles. Earlier F-15Cs were [retrofitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrofitted) with these improvements. Also beginning in 1985, F-15C and D models were equipped with the improved Pratt & Whitney F100-PW-220 engine and digital engine controls, providing quicker throttle response, reduced wear, and lower fuel consumption.

Despite the F-15A originally being passed over for MSIP, the U.S. Air National Guard (ANG) would receive the jets in the 1990s and embark on their own MSIP program for the F-15As in FY94. These would receive the upgraded AN/APG-63 PSP, larger central computer, countermeasures, AIM-120 and other advanced missile capability just like the F-15C MSIP II.[^cite_note-31] In this form, they would go on to serve until finally retiring in 2011.[^cite_note-F-15A_Retirement-32] Starting in 1997, original F100-PW-100 engines were upgraded to a similar configuration with the designation F100-PW-220E starting.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaviesDildy2007168–169-33] In 2000, the [APG-63(V)2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APG-63\(V\)2) [active electronically scanned array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_electronically_scanned_array) (AESA) radar was retrofitted to 18 U.S. Air Force F-15C aircraft.[^cite_note-34] Due to costs, it would not be a fleetwide upgrade, with the rest of the MSIP F-15Cs with the AN/APG-63 PSP upgraded to the APG-63(V)1 to improve maintainability and to bring performance similar to the AN/APG-70 beginning the next year in 2001, with the 27th Fighter Squadron at Langley Air Force Base in Virginia being the first to receive them in March.[^cite_note-35] Most of the Fleet would receive this upgrade.



F-15E with [speed brake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_brake_\(aircraft\)) deployed and CFTs fitted

The Zone Acquisition Program (ZAP) missile launch envelope has been integrated into the operational flight program system of all U.S. F-15 aircraft, providing dynamic launch zone and launch acceptability region information for missiles to the pilot by display cues in real-time.[^cite_note-36]

Although the Air Force's F-X requirements were focused on air superiority, McDonnell Douglas had quietly included a basic secondary ground attack capability in the F-15's design since the beginning and also performed early internal studies for enhancing that capability.[^cite_note-\:3-37] In 1979, McDonnell Douglas and F-15 radar manufacturer, [Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Aircraft), teamed to privately develop a [strike fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_fighter) version of the F-15. This version competed in the Air Force's [Dual-Role Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_Tactical_Fighter) competition starting in 1982. The [F-15E](495724.md) strike variant was selected for production over General Dynamics' competing [F-16XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16XL) in 1984; it is a two-seat, dual-role, totally integrated fighter for all-weather, air-to-air, and deep [interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interdiction) missions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavies200315–16,_25,_31–32-38] The rear cockpit is upgraded to include four multipurpose [cathode-ray tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube) displays for aircraft systems and weapons management. The digital, triple-redundant [Lear Siegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EG\&G) [aircraft flight control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_flight_control_system) permits coupled [automatic terrain following](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain-following_radar), enhanced by a [ring-laser gyro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_laser_gyroscope) inertial navigation system.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELambert1993523-39] For low-altitude, high-speed penetration and precision attack on tactical targets at night or in adverse weather, the F-15E carries a high-resolution [APG-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/APG-63_radar_family) radar and [LANTIRN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LANTIRN) pods to provide [thermography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermography).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins199897–104-40] The F-15E would be developed into the [F-15 Advanced Eagle](495724.md#F-15_Advanced_Eagle_variants) family, which features [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire) controls; the Advanced Eagle is currently the basis of all current F-15 production.[^cite_note-janes.com-41]



AN/APG-63(V)3 radar

Beginning in 2006, with the threat of curtailed procurement of the [F-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) that was to replace all air superiority F-15s, USAF planned to modernize 179 F-15Cs in the best material condition in order to maintain fighter fleet size by retrofitting the [AN/APG-63(V)3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APG-63_and_APG-70_radars) AESA radar and updated cockpit displays; the first upgraded aircraft was delivered in October 2010.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaviesDildy2007161–165-42][^cite_note-Upgrade_radar-43] A significant number of F-15s were equipped with the [Joint Helmet Mounted Cueing System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Helmet_Mounted_Cueing_System).[^cite_note-F-15_upgrades_2006-44] Lockheed Martin developed an [infrared search and track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_search_and_track) (IRST) sensor system for tactical fighters such the F-15C, eventually resulting in the AN/ASG-34(V)1 IRST21 sensor mounted in the Legion Pod; the AN/AAQ-33 Sniper XR pod was also integrated as a makeshift interim IRST solution.[^cite_note-45] A follow-on upgrade called the Eagle Passive/Active Warning Survivability System (EPAWSS) was planned.[^cite_note-46] Boeing was selected in October 2015 to serve as prime contractor for the EPAWSS, with [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) selected as a subcontractor. The EPAWSS is an all-digital system with advanced electronic countermeasures, radar warning, and increased chaff and flare capabilities in a smaller footprint than the 1980s-era Tactical Electronic Warfare System. More than 400 F-15Cs and F-15Es were planned to have the system installed.[^cite_note-47] However, in FY20, the USAF decided against continuing the EPAWSS program for the F-15C as the decision had been made to purchase the F-15EX instead; these would replace the F-15C in service, as primarily an Air Superiority fighter.[^cite_note-DOTE_EPAWSS_FY20-48]

In September 2015, Boeing unveiled its 2040C Eagle upgrade (also called "Golden Eagle"), designed to keep the F-15 relevant through 2040. Seen as a necessity because of the low numbers of F-22s procured, the upgrade builds upon the company's [F-15SE Silent Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15SE_Silent_Eagle) concept with low-observable features. Most improvements focus on lethality including quad-pack munitions racks to double its missile load to 16, [conformal fuel tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_fuel_tank) for extended range, "[Talon HATE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talon_HATE?action=edit\&redlink=1)" communications pod to communicate with fifth-generation fighters, the APG-63(V)3 AESA radar, long-range Legion IRST pod, and EPAWSS electronic warfare suite.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] The 2040C upgrade for the F-15C/D was not pursued, owing to the airframes' age that made it not economically sustainable, but many of the components such as EPAWSS and AESA radar were continued for F-15E upgrades as well as new-build [F-15EX Eagle II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15EX_Eagle_II) ordered by USAF in 2020; the F-15EX took advantage of existing Advanced Eagle production line for export customers to minimize lead times and start-up costs to replace the remaining F-15C/Ds, whereas F-22 production restart was considered cost-prohibitive.[^cite_note-F15EX_selection-53]

## Design

### Overview



Variable geometry engine air intake ramps with internal [pitot tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitot_tube) and automatic control for constant optimal airflow to engines. Above: open intake, aligned ramp. Below: closed intake, inclined ramp

The F-15 has an all-metal semi-[monocoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocoque) fuselage with a large-[cantilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilever), shoulder-mounted wing. The wing planform of the F-15 suggests a modified cropped [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_wing) shape with a leading-edge sweepback angle of 45°. Ailerons and a simple high-lift flap are located on the trailing edge. No leading-edge maneuvering flaps are used. This complication was avoided by the combination of low wing loading and fixed leading-edge conical camber that varies with spanwise position along the wing. Airfoil thickness ratios vary from 5.9% at the root to 3% at the tip.[^cite_note-Selig-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

The [empennage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empennage) is of metal and composite construction, with twin aluminum alloy/[composite material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_material) [honeycomb structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeycomb_structure) [vertical stabilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_stabilizer) with [boron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boron)-composite [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_\(aeronautics\)), resulting in an exceptionally thin tailplane and rudders. Composite horizontal [all-moving tails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilator) outboard of the vertical stabilizers move independently to provide roll control in some flight maneuvers; the horizontal tails have a [dogtooth notch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading-edge_extension#Dogtooth_extension) to mitigate [flutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroelasticity). The F-15 has a spine-mounted [air brake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_brake_\(aircraft\)) and [retractable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retractable_landing_gear) [tricycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycle_gear) [landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear). It is powered by two [Pratt & Whitney F100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_F100) [axial flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_compressor) [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) engines with [afterburners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterburner), mounted side by side in the fuselage and fed by rectangular inlets with variable [intake ramps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intake_ramp). The [cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockpit) is mounted high in the forward fuselage with a one-piece windscreen and large canopy for increased visibility and a 360° field of view for the pilot. The airframe consists of 37.3% aluminum, 29.2% honeycomb, 25.8% titanium, 5.5% steel, and 2% composites and fiberglass; the structure began to incorporate advanced [superplastically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superplastic_forming) formed titanium components in the 1980s.[^cite_note-57]

Gray jet fighter taking off at steep angle of attack, with full afterburner, as evident by hot gas ejected from its engines

F-15C executing a maximum-performance takeoff

The F-15's maneuverability is derived from low [wing loading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_loading) (weight to wing area ratio) with a high [thrust-to-weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-to-weight_ratio), enabling the aircraft to turn tightly at up to 9 *g* without losing [airspeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airspeed).[^cite_note-58] The F-15 can climb to 30,000 feet (9,100 m) in around 60 seconds. At certain speeds, the dynamic [thrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust) output of the dual engines is greater than the aircraft's combat weight and drag, so it has the ability to accelerate vertically. The weapons and flight-control systems are designed so that one person can safely and effectively perform air-to-air combat.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdenMoeng2002944-59] The A and C models are single-seat variants; these were the main air-superiority versions produced. B and D models add a second seat behind the pilot for training, although they are also fully combat capable. E models use the second seat for a [weapon systems officer](4072511.md). Visibly, the F-15 has a unique feature *vis-à-vis* other modern fighter aircraft; it does not have the distinctive "turkey feather" aerodynamic exhaust petals covering its [engine nozzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Laval_nozzle). Following problems during development of its exhaust petal design, including dislodgment during flight, the decision was made to remove them, resulting in a 3% [aerodynamic drag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamic_drag) increase.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaviesDildy200746–47-60]

Video showing the F-15's maneuverability in simulated dogfighting

The F-15 was shown to be capable of controlled flight with only one wing after an [Israeli F-15D suffered a mid-air collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Negev_mid-air_collision) with an [A-4 Skyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk) that removed most of the right wing, in the 1983 Negev mid-air collision. While the A-4 was instantly disintegrated with the pilot being automatically ejected, the F-15 was sent into an uncontrollable [roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_principal_axes). Through the application of full [afterburner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterburner) as well as a landing at twice the normal speed, pilot Zivi Nedivi managed to land successfully at [Ramon Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Airbase). Subsequent wind-tunnel tests on a one-wing model confirmed that controllable flight was only possible within a very limited speed range of ±20 knots and angle of attack variation of ±20 degrees. The event resulted in research into damage-adaptive technology and a system called "Intelligent Flight Control System".[^cite_note-61]

### Avionics



[AN/APG-63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/APG-63) radar

A multimission [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics) system includes a [head-up display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-up_display) (HUD), advanced radar, [AN/ASN-109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/ASN-109?action=edit\&redlink=1) [inertial guidance system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_guidance_system), flight instruments, [ultra high frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_high_frequency) communications, and [tactical air navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_air_navigation_system) and [instrument landing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_landing_system) receivers. It also has an internally mounted, tactical [electronic warfare](82272.md) system, [Identification friend or foe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_friend_or_foe) system, an [electronic countermeasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_countermeasure) suite, and a central [digital computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_computer).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGunston1986194-62]

The HUD projects all essential flight information gathered by the integrated avionics system. This display, visible in any light condition, provides the pilot information necessary to track and destroy an enemy aircraft without having to look down at [cockpit instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockpit_instrument).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuenecke1987227–230-63]

The F-15's versatile [APG-63 and 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/APG-63_radar_family) [pulse-Doppler radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse-Doppler_radar) systems can look up at high-flying targets and [look-down/shoot-down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-down/shoot-down) at low-flying targets without being confused by ground [clutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutter_\(radar\)). These radars can detect and track aircraft and small high-speed targets at distances [beyond visual range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_visual_range) down to close range, and at altitudes down to treetop level. The APG-63 has a basic range of 100 miles (87 nmi; 160 km). The radar feeds target information into the central computer for effective weapons delivery. For close-in [dogfights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogfight), the radar automatically acquires enemy aircraft, and this information is projected on the head-up display. The F-15's [electronic warfare](82272.md) system provides both threat warning ([radar warning receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_warning_receiver)) and automatic countermeasures against selected threats.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins199897–104-40]

The improved [APG-63(V)2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APG-63\(V\)2) and (V)3 [active electronically scanned array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_electronically_scanned_array) (AESA) radar upgrade included most of the hardware from the APG-63(V)1, but added an AESA antenna to provide increased pilot [situation awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situation_awareness). The AESA radar has an exceptionally agile beam, providing nearly instantaneous track updates and enhanced multitarget tracking capability. The APG-63(V)2 and (V)3 are compatible with current F-15C weapon loads and enable pilots to take full advantage of AIM-120 AMRAAM capabilities, simultaneously guiding multiple missiles to several targets widely spaced in [azimuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azimuth), elevation, or range.[^cite_note-Upgrade_radar-43]

### Weaponry and external stores



[M61 Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M61_Vulcan) mounted on the side of the right engine intake

A variety of air-to-air weaponry can be carried by the F-15. An automated weapon system enables the pilot to release weapons effectively and safely, using the head-up display and the avionics and weapons controls located on the engine throttles or control stick. When the pilot changes from one weapon system to another, visual guidance for the selected weapon automatically appears on the head-up display.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuenecke1987232–236-64]

The Eagle can be armed with combinations of four different air-to-air weapons: [AIM-7F/M Sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-7_Sparrow) missiles or [AIM-120 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM) advanced medium-range air-to-air missiles on its lower fuselage corners, [AIM-9L/M Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) or AIM-120 AMRAAM missiles on two pylons under the wings, and an internal 20 mm (0.79 in) [M61 Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M61_Vulcan) [Gatling gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatling_gun) in the right [wing root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_root).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELambert1993521-65]



F-15C underside with external stores

Low-drag conformal fuel tanks (CFTs), initially called Fuel And Sensor Tactical (FAST) packs, were developed for the F-15C and D models. They can be attached to the sides of the engine air intakes under each wing and are designed to the same load factors and airspeed limits as the basic aircraft.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELambert1993521-65] These tanks slightly degrade performance by increasing aerodynamic drag and cannot be jettisoned in-flight. However, they cause less drag than conventional external tanks. Each conformal tank can hold 750 U.S. [gallons](12882.md) (2,840 L) of fuel.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins1998111-66] These CFTs increase range and reduce the need for [in-flight refueling](237949.md). All external stations for munitions remain available with the tanks in use. Moreover, Sparrow or AMRAAM missiles can be attached to the corners of the CFTs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreenSwanborough1998371-29] The USAF [57th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_Fighter-Interceptor_Squadron) based at [NAS Keflavik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Keflavik), Iceland, was the only C-model squadron to use CFTs on a regular basis due to its extended operations over the North Atlantic. With the closure of the 57 FIS, the F-15E is the only variant to carry them on a routine basis. CFTs have also been sold to Israel and Saudi Arabia.

## Operational history

### Introduction and early service

The largest operator of the F-15 is the [United States Air Force](32090.md). The first Eagle, an F-15B, was delivered on 13 November 1974.[^cite_note-scutts-67] In January 1976, the first Eagle destined for a combat squadron, the [555th TFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/555th_Fighter_Squadron), was delivered.[^cite_note-scutts-67] These initial aircraft carried the [Hughes Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Aircraft) (now [Raytheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon)) APG-63 radar. The F-15 in early service was plagued by reliability and durability problems of its F100-PW-100 engines, whose ambitious specifications were critical for the aircraft's high performance. Furthermore, the issues were exacerbated by pilots making many more abrupt throttle changes than in previous fighters and engines due to the thrust available. The issues were addressed by the improved F100-PW-220 engines first delivered in 1986.[^cite_note-dtic.mil-68]



Israeli Air Force F-15 Eagle fighters [overflying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Air_Force_flight_over_Auschwitz) [Auschwitz Concentration Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp), 2003

The first kill by an F-15 was scored by Israeli Air Force (IAF) ace [Moshe Melnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Melnik?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1979.[^cite_note-1st-69] During IAF raids against Palestinian factions in Lebanon in 1979–1981, F-15As reportedly downed 13 Syrian MiG-21s and two Syrian MiG-25s. Israeli F-15As and Bs participated as escorts in [Operation Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Opera), an air strike on an Iraqi [nuclear reactor](22151.md). In the [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War), Israeli F-15s were credited with 41 Syrian aircraft destroyed (23 MiG-21s and 17 MiG-23s, and one [Aérospatiale SA.342L Gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Gazelle) helicopter). During [Operation Mole Cricket 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mole_Cricket_19), Israeli F-15s and F-16s together shot down 82 Syrian fighters (MiG-21s, MiG-23s, and MiG-23Ms) without losses.[^cite_note-grant-70]

Israel was the only operator to use and develop the air-to-ground abilities of the air-superiority F-15 variants, doing so because the fighter's range was well beyond other combat aircraft in the Israeli inventory in the 1980s. The first known use of F-15s for a strike mission was during [Operation Wooden Leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Wooden_Leg) on 1 October 1985, with six F-15Ds attacking PLO Headquarters in Tunis with one [GBU-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-15) guided bomb per aircraft and two F-15Cs restriking the ruins with six Mk-82 unguided bombs each.[^cite_note-71] This was one of the few times air-superiority F-15s (A/B/C/D models) were used in tactical strike missions.[^cite_note-72] Israeli air-superiority F-15 variants have since been extensively upgraded to carry a wider range of air-to-ground armaments, including [JDAM GPS-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Direct_Attack_Munition) and [Popeye missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popeye_\(missile\)).[^cite_note-73]

The first American combat use of the F-15 was during [Operation Urgent Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Grenada). F-15s of the [33rd Tactical Fighter Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_Fighter_Wing) provided air cover alongside [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) [F-14 Tomcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F-14_Tomcat) for [Marines](17349325.md) and the [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division) for contingency operations in Grenada.[^cite_note-74]

[Royal Saudi Air Force](570367.md) F-15C pilots reportedly shot down two [Iranian Air Force](412792.md) [F-4E Phantom IIs](11759.md) in a [skirmish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_of_June_5,_1984) on 5 June 1984.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

#### Anti-satellite trials



[ASM-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM-135) ASAT test launch from F-15A *76-0084* in 1985

The [ASM-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM-135_ASAT) missile was designed to be a standoff [anti-satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_weapon) (ASAT) weapon, with the F-15 acting as a [first stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_stage_\(rocketry\)). The Soviet Union could correlate a U.S. rocket launch with a spy satellite loss, but an F-15 carrying an ASAT would blend in among hundreds of F-15 flights. From January 1984 to September 1986, two F-15As were used as launch platforms for the ASAT missile. The F-15As were modified to carry one ASM-135 on the centerline station with extra equipment within a special centerline pylon.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins199831-77][^cite_note-78] The launch aircraft executed a Mach 1.22, 3.8 g climb at 65° to release the ASAT missile at an altitude of 38,100 ft (11,600 m).[^cite_note-asat-79][^cite_note-AF_mag_can-80][^cite_note-81] The ASM-135 ASAT missile reached an altitude of 345 mi (555 km).[^cite_note-AF_mag_can-80] The flight computer was updated to control the zoom-climb and missile release.

The third test flight involved a functional [P78-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P78-1) solar observatory satellite in a 345-mile (555 km) orbit, which was destroyed by [kinetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy).[^cite_note-asat-79][^cite_note-82] The pilot, USAF Major [Wilbert D. "Doug" Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbert_Pearson), became the only pilot to destroy a satellite.[^cite_note-AF_mag_can-80] The ASAT program involved five test launches. The program was officially terminated in 1988.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins199831-77][^cite_note-AF_mag_can-80]

### Gulf War and aftermath

The USAF began deploying F-15C, D, and E model aircraft to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) region in August 1990 for Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm. During the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), the F-15 accounted for 36 of the 39 air-to-air victories by the U.S. Air Force against Iraqi forces. Iraq has confirmed the loss of 23 of its aircraft in air-to-air combat.[^cite_note-83] The F-15C and D fighters were used in the air-superiority role, while F-15E Strike Eagles were used in air-to-ground attacks mainly at night, hunting [modified Scud missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hussein_\(missile\)) launchers and artillery sites using the LANTIRN system.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavies200231–40-84] According to the USAF, its F-15Cs had 34 confirmed kills of Iraqi aircraft during the 1991 Gulf War, most of them by missile fire: five [Mikoyan MiG-29s](185653.md), two [MiG-25s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-25), eight [MiG-23s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-23), two [MiG-21s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21), two [Sukhoi Su-25s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-25), four [Sukhoi Su-22s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-22), one [Sukhoi Su-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-7), six [Dassault Mirage F1s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_F1), one [Ilyushin Il-76](229943.md) cargo aircraft, one [Pilatus PC-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-9) trainer, and two [Mil Mi-8](957233.md) helicopters. According to [NHHC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_History_and_Heritage_Command), F-15s may have also shot down a friendly F-14 Tomcat.[^cite_note-85] In addition, the F-15E achieved its first-ever air-to-air kill on 14 February 1991, destroying an Iraqi [Mi-24 "Hind"](384591.md) helicopter with a [GBU-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-10_Paveway_II) laser-guided bomb.[^cite_note-86] Air superiority was achieved in the first three days of the conflict; many of the later kills were reportedly of Iraqi aircraft fleeing to Iran, rather than engaging American aircraft. Two F-15Es were lost to ground fire, and another was damaged on the ground by a Scud strike on [King Abdulaziz Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_Air_Base).[^cite_note-87]

On 11 November 1990, a [Royal Saudi Air Force](570367.md) (RSAF) pilot defected to [Sudan](27421.md) with an F-15C fighter during Operation Desert Shield. Saudi Arabia paid US\$40 million (\~\$84.6 million in 2024) for return of the aircraft three months later.[^cite_note-88] RSAF F-15s shot down two Iraqi [Mirage F1s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_F1) during the Operation Desert Storm.[^cite_note-89] One Saudi Arabian F-15C was lost to a crash during the Persian Gulf War in 1991.[^cite_note-90] The IQAF claimed this fighter was part of two USAF F-15Cs that [engaged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurra_Air_Battle) two Iraqi MiG-25PDs, and was hit by an R-40 missile before crashing.



An RSAF F-15 approaches a KC-135 for refueling during [Operation Desert Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Shield).

They have since been deployed to support [Operation Southern Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Southern_Watch), the patrolling of the [Iraqi no-fly zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_no-fly_zones) in Iraq; [Operation Provide Comfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Provide_Comfort) in Turkey; in support of [NATO](21133.md) operations in Bosnia, and recent air expeditionary force deployments. In 1994, two U.S. Army [Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_UH-60_Black_Hawk) were mistakenly downed by USAF F-15Cs in northern Iraq in a [friendly-fire incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Black_Hawk_shootdown_incident).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins199847-91] USAF F-15Cs shot down four Yugoslav [MiG-29s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-29) using [AIM-120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120) and AIM-7 Radar guided missiles during NATO's 1999 intervention in Kosovo, [Operation Allied Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Allied_Force).[^cite_note-Maxwell-92]

### Structural defects

All F-15s were grounded by the USAF after a [Missouri Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Air_National_Guard) F-15C came apart in flight and crashed on 2 November 2007. The newer F-15E fleet was later cleared for continued operations. The USAF reported on 28 November 2007 that a critical location in the upper [longerons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longeron) on the F-15C was the failure's suspected cause, causing the fuselage forward of the air intakes, including the cockpit and radome, to separate from the airframe.[^cite_note-image-93]

F-15A through D-model aircraft were grounded until the location received detailed inspections and repairs as needed.[^cite_note-94] The grounding of F-15s received media attention as it began to place strains on the nation's air-defense efforts.[^cite_note-F-15_grounding_strains-95] The grounding forced some states to rely on their neighboring states' fighters for air-defense protection, and Alaska to depend on [Canadian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces)' fighter support.[^cite_note-F-15_grounding_strains-95]

On 8 January 2008, the USAF [Air Combat Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Combat_Command) (ACC) cleared a portion of its older F-15 fleet for return to flying status. It also recommended a limited return to flight for units worldwide using the affected models.[^cite_note-partial_return-96] The accident review board report, which was released on 10 January 2008, stated that analysis of the F-15C wreckage determined that the longeron did not meet drawing specifications, which led to fatigue cracks and finally a catastrophic failure of the remaining support structures and breakup of the aircraft in flight.[^cite_note-accident_report-97] In a report released on 10 January 2008, nine other F-15s were identified to have similar problems in the longeron. As a result, General [John D. W. Corley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._W._Corley) stated, "the long-term future of the F-15 is in question".[^cite_note-98] On 15 February 2008, ACC cleared all its grounded F-15A/B/C/D fighters for flight pending inspections, engineering reviews, and any needed repairs. ACC also recommended release of other U.S. F-15A/B/C/Ds.[^cite_note-ACC_feb15_release-99]

### Later service



A USAF F-15C flying over Fresno, California, 2013

The F-15 has a combined air-to-air combat record of 104 kills and no losses through 2008; the F-15's air superiority versions, the A/B/C/D models, have not suffered any losses to enemy action.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaviesDildy2007inside_cover-100][^cite_note-Reformers-101] Over half of F-15 kills have been achieved by Israeli Air Force pilots.

On 16 September 2009, the last F-15A, an [Oregon Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Air_National_Guard) aircraft, was retired, marking the end of service for the F-15A and F-15B models in the United States.[^cite_note-102] With the retirement of those early models, the F-15C and D models continued operational service to supplement the new [F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) in frontline US service. Because the DOD was primarily focused on asymmetric [counterinsurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterinsurgency) warfare in the Middle East in the 2000s, the F-22 procurement was curtailed to just 187 operational aircraft and the USAF had to extend F-15C/D operations well beyond its planned retirement date in order to maintain adequate numbers of air superiority fighters; in 2007, the USAF planned to keep 179 F-15C/Ds along with 224 [F-15Es](495724.md) in service beyond 2025.[^cite_note-fighter_force-103] During the 2010s, USAF F-15C/Ds were regularly based overseas with the [Pacific Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Air_Forces) at [Kadena AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadena_AB) in Japan[^cite_note-104] and with the [U.S. Air Forces in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Forces_in_Europe) at [RAF Lakenheath](856907.md) in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-105] Other regular USAF F-15s are operated by ACC as adversary/aggressor platforms at [Nellis AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellis_AFB), Nevada, and by [Air Force Materiel Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Materiel_Command) in test and evaluation roles at [Edwards AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards_AFB), California, and [Eglin AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglin_AFB), Florida. All remaining combat-coded F-15C/Ds are operated by the [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard).



A USAF F-15C of the 125th Fighter Wing

To keep the F-15C/D viable, the fleet saw a series of upgrades, with 179 aircraft receiving the AN/APG-63(V)3 AESA radar starting in 2010 along with eventual addition of IRST pods and cockpit enhancements.[^cite_note-Upgrade_radar-43][^cite_note-F-15_upgrades_2006-44] However, problems with the aging fleet meant the F-15C faced cuts or retirement in the USAF's FY 2015 budget in response to sequestration.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] By the mid-2010s, the aging F-15C/D fleet was no longer economically sustainable to the 2030s as hoped due to structural fatigue,[^cite_note-108] and the USAF chose to forgo the more comprehensive F-15 2040C upgrade proposed by Boeing; in April 2017, USAF officials announced plans to retire the F-15C/D in the mid-2020s and press other aircraft such as F-16s into roles occupied by the F-15 while exploring options to recapitalize its fighter fleet.[^cite_note-f-16_replaces_F-15-109]

In late 2018 and early 2019, following a series of DoD Cost Analysis and Program Evaluation (CAPE) Office studies on affordably recapitalizing the fighter fleet, the Pentagon in its FY 2020 budget requested new-build [F-15EXs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F-15EX_Eagle_II) — an advanced variant based on the export F-15QA then in production — to replace the F-15Cs and supplement the F-22s to maintain fighter fleet size, with planned total procurement of 144 aircraft.[^cite_note-110] This allowed USAF to use the existing export production line to quickly and affordably bring fighters into operational service, as restarting the F-22 line was considered cost-prohibitive.[^cite_note-F15EX_selection-53][^cite_note-111]

In 2022, it was announced the USAF plan to retire their fleet of F-15C/Ds by 2026, while the F-15Es would retire in the 2030s.[^cite_note-112] By April 2022, RAF Lakenheath had divested its entire fleet of F-15C/Ds, with its aircraft going on to serve in ANG squadrons.[^cite_note-113] Kadena AB divested its Eagle fleet between December 2022 and January 2025,[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] with the last USAF active duty F-15C flight being flown by *81-0029* on 24 January 2025.[^cite_note-116]

### Yemen Civil War and Iran war

During the [Yemeni Civil War (2015–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_\(2015%E2%80%93present\)), [Houthis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement) have used [R-27T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-27_\(air-to-air_missile\)) missiles modified to serve as surface-to-air missiles. A video released on 7 January 2018 also shows a modified R-27T hitting a Saudi F-15 on a [forward-looking infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward-looking_infrared) camera. Houthi sources claim to have downed the F-15, although this has been disputed, as the missile apparently [proximity detonated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proximity_fuze), though the F-15 continued to fly in its trajectory seemingly unaffected.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-Archived_copy-118] Rebels later released footage showing an aircraft wreck, but serial numbers on the wreckage suggested the aircraft was a [Panavia Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado), also operated by Saudi forces. On 8 January, the Saudi admitted the loss of an aircraft but due to technical reasons.[^cite_note-119]

On 21 March 2018, Houthi rebels released a video where they hit and possibly shot down a Saudi F-15 in [Saada province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate).[^cite_note-120] In the video a R-27T air-to-air missile adapted for surface-to-air use was launched and appeared to hit a jet. As in the video of the previous similar hit recorded on 8 January, the target, while clearly hit, did not appear to be downed. Saudi forces confirmed the hit, while saying the jet landed at a Saudi base.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] Saudi official sources confirmed the incident, reporting that it happened at 3\:48 pm local time after a surface-to-air defense missile was launched at the fighter jet from inside Saada airport.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

After the [Houthi attack on Saudi oil infrastructure on 14 September 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Abqaiq%E2%80%93Khurais_attack), Saudi Arabia tasked F-15 fighters armed with missiles to intercept low flying drones, difficult to intercept with ground-based high altitude missile systems like the [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md)[^cite_note-125] with several drones being downed since then.[^cite_note-126] On 2 July 2020, a Saudi F-15 shot down two Houthi [Shahed 129](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahed_129) drones above Yemen.[^cite_note-127] On 7 March 2021, during a Houthi attack at several Saudi oil installations, Saudi F-15s shot down several attacking drones using heatseeking [AIM-9 Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) missiles, with video evidence showing at least two [Samad-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samad_\(UAV\)) UAVs and one [Qasef-2K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HESA_Ababil) downed.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] On 30 March 2021, a video made by Saudi border guards showed a Saudi F-15 shooting down a Houthi Quasef-2K drone with an AIM-120 AMRAAM fired at short range.[^cite_note-130]

In April 2026, it was reported that F-15 and F-35 aircraft have been successfully hit by Iranian air defense technology during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).

On 16 September 2026, Yemeni [Houthis](17240332.md) claimed to have shot down a [Royal Saudi Air Force](570367.md) F-15 fighter over the [Marib Governorate](3604391.md).[^cite_note-131] [^cite_note-132]

F-15N Sea Eagle

The F-15N was a carrier-capable variant proposed in the early 1970s to the U.S. Navy as an alternative to the heavier and, at the time, considered to be "riskier" technology program, the [Grumman F-14 Tomcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F-14_Tomcat). It did not have a long-range radar or the long-range missiles used by the F-14. The F-15N-PHX was another proposed naval version capable of carrying the [AIM-54 Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-54_Phoenix) missile, but with an enhanced version of the AN/APG-63 radar on the F-15A, designated AN/APG-64. These featured folding wingtips, reinforced landing gear and a stronger tailhook for shipboard operation.

F-15 2040C

Proposed upgrade to the F-15C, allowing it to supplement the [F-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) in the air superiority role. The 2040C concept is an evolution of the Silent Eagle proposed to South Korea and Israel, with some low-observable improvements but mostly a focus on the latest air capabilities and lethality. Proposal includes [infra-red search and track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infra-red_search_and_track), doubling the number of weapon stations, with quad racks for a maximum of 16 air-to-air missiles, Eagle Passive/Active Warning Survivability System (EPAWSS), conformal fuel tanks, upgraded APG-63(V)3 AESA radar and a "Talon HATE" communications pod allowing data transfer with the F-22. This upgrade program was not pursued due to the age of the existing airframes, but some of the upgrades were applied to the new-build F-15EX.

F-15A 71–0280, the first prototype

Twelve prototypes were built and used for trials by the F-15 Joint Test Force at Edwards Air Force Base using McDonnell Douglas and United States Air Force personnel. Most prototypes were later used by NASA for trials and experiments.

F-15A-1, AF Serial No. 71-0280

Was the first F-15 to fly on 11 July 1972 from Edwards Air Force Base, it was used as a trial aircraft for exploring the flight envelope, general handling and testing the carriage of external stores.

F-15A-1, AF Ser. No. 71-0281

The second prototype first flew on 26 September 1972 and was used to test the F100 engine.

F-15A-2, AF Ser. No. 71-0282

First flew on 4 November 1972 and was used to test the APG-63 radar and avionics.

F-15A-2, AF Ser. No. 71-0283

First flew on 13 January 1973 and was used as a structural test aircraft, it was the first aircraft to have the smaller wingtips to clear a severe buffet problem found on earlier aircraft.

F-15A-2, AF Ser. No. 71-0284

First flew on 7 March 1973 it was used for armament development and was the first aircraft fitted with an internal cannon.

F-15A-3, AF Ser. No. 71-0285

First flew on 23 May 1973 and was used to test the missile fire control system and other avionics.

F-15A-3, AF Ser. No. 71-0286

First flew on 14 June 1973 and was used for armament trials and testing external fuel stores.

F-15A-4, AF Ser. No. 71-0287

First flew on 25 August 1973 and was used for spin recovery, angle of attack and fuel system testing, it was fitted with an anti-spin recovery parachute. The aircraft was loaned to NASA from 1976 for engine development trials.

F-15A-4, AF Ser. No. 71-0288

First flew on 20 October 1973 and was used to test integrated aircraft and engine performance, it was later used by McDonnell Douglas as a test aircraft in the 1990s.

F-15A-4, AF Ser. No. 71-0289

First flew on 30 January 1974 and was used for trials on the radar, avionics and electronic warfare systems.

F-15B-1, AF Ser. No. 71-0290

The first two-seat prototype originally designated the TF-15A, it first flew on 7 July 1973.

F-15B-2, AF Ser. No. 71-0291

First flew on 18 October 1973 as a TF-15A and used as a test and demonstration aircraft. In 1976 it made an overseas sales tour painted in markings to celebrate the bicentenary of the United States. Also used as the development aircraft for the F-15E as well as the first F-15 to use Conformal Fuel Tanks.

Main article: [Aircraft in fiction § F-15 Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_in_fiction#F-15_Eagle)

The F-15 was the subject of the [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX) movie *[Fighter Pilot: Operation Red Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_Pilot:_Operation_Red_Flag)*, about the [RED FLAG exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Flag_\(USAF\)). In [Tom Clancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy)'s nonfiction book, *[Fighter Wing: A Guided Tour of an Air Force Combat Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_Wing:_A_Guided_Tour_of_an_Air_Force_Combat_Wing)* (1995), a detailed analysis of the Air Force's premier fighter aircraft, the F-15 Eagle and its capabilities are showcased.[^cite_note-255]

The F-15 has also been a popular subject as a toy, and a [fictional likeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_in_fiction#F-15_Eagle) of an aircraft similar to the F-15 has been used in cartoons, books, video games, [animated television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_series), and animated films.
