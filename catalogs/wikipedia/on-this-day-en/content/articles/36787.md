**Budapest**[^cite_note-16] is the [capital](181337.md) and [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_of_Hungary) of [Hungary](13275.md). It is Hungary's [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city) with 1.7 million inhabitants and its greater [metro area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) has a population of about 3.3 million,[^cite_note-auto3-9][^cite_note-auto2-10] representing one-third of the country's population and producing [more than 40% of the country's economic output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hungarian_counties_by_GDP). Budapest is the [political](13427.md), [economic](13428.md), and [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Hungary) center of the country, [among the ten largest cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_European_Union_by_population_within_city_limits) in the [European Union](9317.md) and the [second largest urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Europe) in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe). Budapest stands on the [River Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_on_the_river_Danube) and is strategically located at the center of the [Pannonian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Basin), lying on ancient trade routes linking the hills of [Transdanubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdanubia) with the [Great Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hungarian_Plain).

Budapest is a [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city), consistently ranked among the 50 most important cities in the world,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] belongs to the narrow group of cities with a [GDP over US\$120 billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_by_GDP_over_US\$100_billion),[^cite_note-19] was named a global cultural capital as having high-quality human capital,[^cite_note-20] and is among the 35 [most liveable cities in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Liveability_Index).[^cite_note-21] The city is home to [over 30 universities with more than 150,000 students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Hungary), most of them attending large public [research universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university) that are highly ranked worldwide in their fields, such as [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E%C3%B6tv%C3%B6s_Lor%C3%A1nd_University) in [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science),[^cite_note-22] [Budapest University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_University_of_Technology_and_Economics) in [engineering and technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology),[^cite_note-23] [MATE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_University_of_Agriculture_and_Life_Sciences) in [life sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_life_sciences),[^cite_note-24] and [Semmelweis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semmelweis_University) in [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine).[^cite_note-25] Budapest also hosts various international organizations, including several [UN agencies](31769.md),[^cite_note-26] the [WHO Budapest Centre](33583.md),[^cite_note-27] [IOM regional centre](36893.md),[^cite_note-28] the [EU headquarters](9317.md) of [EIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Institute_of_Innovation_and_Technology) and [CEPOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Agency_for_Law_Enforcement_Training),[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] as well as the first foreign office of [China Investment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Investment_Promotion_Agency).[^cite_note-31] Budapest opened the first [underground transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) line on the European continent in 1896,[^cite_note-32] which is still in use as [M1 Millennium Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Underground_Railway), and today the fixed-track [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Metro) and [tram network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Budapest) forms the backbone of Budapest's public transport system and transports 2.2 million people daily,[^cite_note-33] making it a [significant urban transit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metro_systems).

The [history of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Budapest) began with an early [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) settlement transformed by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) into the town of [Aquincum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquincum),[^cite_note-Aqua-34][^cite_note-35] capital of [Lower Pannonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia_Inferior) in the 1st century.[^cite_note-Aqua-34] Following the foundation of [Hungary](13275.md) in the late 9th century,[^cite_note-Travel-36] the area was [pillaged by the Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe) in 1241.[^cite_note-Eleventh-37] It became the [royal seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hungarian_monarchs) in 1361,[^cite_note-Eleventh-37] with [Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda) becoming one of the European centers of [renaissance culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_humanism) by the 15th century under [Matthias Corvinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Corvinus).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] The [siege of Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Buda_\(1541\)) in 1541 was followed by nearly 150 years of [Ottoman](22278.md) rule,[^cite_note-41] and after the [reconquest of Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Buda_\(1686\)) in 1686, the region entered a new age of prosperity, with Pest-Buda becoming a global city after the unification of Buda, [Pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest,_Hungary) and [Óbuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93buda) in 1873.[^cite_note-Encarta-42][^cite_note-43] By this time, Budapest had become the co-capital of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary),[^cite_note-44] a [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power) that dissolved in 1918 following [World War I](4764461.md). The city was also the focal point of the [Hungarian Revolution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1848), [Battle of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Budapest) in 1945, and [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-wilson-46]

The historic center of Budapest along the [Danube](21209639.md) is classified as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) due to its numerous notable monuments of [classical architecture](52687.md), from the 13th-century [Matthias Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Church)[^cite_note-47] and [Buda Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda_Castle) to 19th-century landmarks such as the [Hungarian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament_Building), the [State Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_State_Opera_House), the [Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts_\(Budapest\)) and [St. Stephen's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_Basilica).[^cite_note-48] Budapest has been a popular spa destination since [Roman times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and is considered the [spa capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spas_in_Budapest) of Europe,[^cite_note-metro-49] with more than 100 medicinal [geothermal springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring) and the largest thermal water cave system.[^cite_note-thermal97-50][^cite_note-51] The city is home to the second-largest [synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doh%C3%A1ny_Street_Synagogue) and third-largest [parliament building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) in the world,[^cite_note-52] [over 40 museums and galleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Museums_in_Budapest),[^cite_note-53] nearly ten [Michelin-starred restaurants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Michelin-starred_restaurants_in_Hungary), and named among the 50 best food cities globally for its focus on distinctive [Hungarian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Budapest is also renowned for its nightlife, with [ruin bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruin_bar) playing a significant role in it;[^cite_note-Mackintosh_2017_n404-56] moreover, the city has become a [center for Hollywood film production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Budapest) in recent years.[^cite_note-57] Budapest regularly hosts [major global sporting events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:International_sports_competitions_in_Budapest), with the nearly 70,000-seat [Puskás Aréna](27530864.md) serving as one of the venues, which most recently hosted four matches at [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), the [2023 UEFA Europa League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Europa_League_final), the [2020 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UEFA_Super_Cup), and the [2026 UEFA Champions League final](76987191.md). The city also hosted the [2023 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships) and the [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Aquatics_Championships) and [2022 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Aquatics_Championships). Budapest [attracted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Hungary) 7 million international overnight visitors in 2025, making it one of the most popular destinations in Europe.[^cite_note-58]

## Etymology and pronunciation

The previously separate cities of Buda, Óbuda, and Pest were officially unified in 1873[^cite_note-59] and given the new name *Budapest*. Before this, the towns together had sometimes been referred to colloquially as "Pest-Buda".[^cite_note-Jones_2017-60][^cite_note-61] *Pest* is often used *[pars pro toto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pars_pro_toto)* for the entire city in contemporary colloquial Hungarian,[^cite_note-Jones_2017-60] although it is also used to refer to all parts of the city east of the Danube. Conversely, *Buda* colloquially means all districts to the Danube's west—including the former Óbuda. The Danube islands—including [Csepel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepel), the city's XXI. district—are part of neither Buda nor Pest.

All varieties of English pronounce the *-s-* as in the English word *pest*. The *-u* in *Buda-* is pronounced either /u/ like *food* (as in [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/ˈbuːdəpɛst/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)[^cite_note-62]) or /ju/ like *cue* (as in [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˌb(j)uːdəˈpɛst/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)). In Hungarian, the *-s-* is pronounced /ʃ/ as in *wash*; in IPA: Hungarian: [\[ˈbudɒpɛʃt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hungarian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d9/Hu-Budapest.ogg/Hu-Budapest.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d9/Hu-Budapest.ogg/Hu-Budapest.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>.

The origins of the names "Buda" and "Pest" are obscure. Buda was

- probably the name of the first constable of the fortress built on the Castle Hill in the 11th century

  [^cite_note-63]

- or a derivative of *Bod* or *Bud*, a personal name of [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) origin, meaning 'twig'.

  [^cite_note-64]

- or a [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) personal name, *Buda*, the short form of *Budimír*, *Budivoj*.

  [^cite_note-65]

Linguistically, however, a German origin through the Slavic derivative вода (*voda*, water) is not possible, and there is no certainty that a Turkic word really comes from the word *buta* \~ *buda* 'branch, twig'.[^cite_note-66]

According to a legend recorded in chronicles from the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), "Buda" comes from the name of its founder, [Bleda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleda), brother of Hunnic ruler [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila).

> [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila) went in the city of Sicambria in Pannonia, where he killed [Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleda), his brother, and he threw his corpse into the [Danube](21209639.md). For while [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila) was in the west, his brother crossed the boundaries in his reign, because he named Sicambria after his own name Buda's Castle. And though King [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila) forbade the Huns and the other peoples to call that city Buda's Castle, but he called it Attila's Capital, the Germans who were terrified by the prohibition named the city as Eccylburg, which means Attila Castle, however, the Hungarians did not care about the ban and call it Óbuda \[Old Buda] and call it to this day.

— [Mark of Kalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_of_Kalt): *[Chronicon Pictum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicon_Pictum)*[^cite_note-67]

> The [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) are certainly an ancient people and the strength of [Scythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythia) lies in the east, as we said above. And the first king of [Scythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythia) was Magog, son of Japhet, and his people were called Magyars \[Hungarians] after their King Magog, from whose royal line the most renowned and mighty King [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila) descended, who, in the 451st year of Our Lord's birth, coming down from [Scythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythia), entered Pannonia with a mighty force and, putting the Romans to flight, took the realm and made a royal residence for himself beside the [Danube](21209639.md) above the hot springs, and he ordered all the old buildings that he found there to be restored and he built them in a circular and very strong wall that in the Hungarian language is now called Budavár \[Buda Castle] and by the Germans Etzelburg \[Attila Castle]

— [Anonymus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymus_\(notary_of_B%C3%A9la_III\)): *[Gesta Hungarorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesta_Hungarorum)*[^cite_note-68]

There are several theories about Pest. One[^cite_note-69] states that the name derives from [Roman times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia_\(Roman_province\)), since there was a local fortress ([Contra-Aquincum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contra-Aquincum)) called by [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy) "Pession" ("Πέσσιον", iii.7.§ 2).[^cite_note-70] Another has it that Pest originates in the Slavic word for cave, *пещера*, or *peštera*. A third cites *пещ*, or *pešt*, referencing a cave where fires burned or a limekiln.[^cite_note-71]

### Early history



[Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda) during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), woodcut from the [Nuremberg Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle) (1493)

The first settlement on the territory of Budapest was built by [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) before 1 AD. It was later occupied by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome). The Roman settlement – [Aquincum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquincum) – became the main city of [Pannonia Inferior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia_\(Roman_province\)) in 106 AD.[^cite_note-Aqua-34] At first, it was a military settlement, and gradually the city rose around it, making it the focal point of the city's commercial life. Today, this area corresponds to the Óbuda district within Budapest.[^cite_note-72] The Romans constructed roads, [amphitheatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheater), [baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_bathing) and houses with heated floors in this fortified military camp.[^cite_note-Frank-73] The Roman city of Aquincum is the best-conserved of the Roman sites in [Hungary](13275.md). The archaeological site was turned into a museum with indoor and open-air sections.[^cite_note-74] Meanwhile, settlement in the area east of the Danube, which was not part of the Roman Empire, remained Germanic and Sarmatian in character.[^cite_note-75]

[The Magyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people) tribes led by [Árpád](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81rp%C3%A1d), forced out of their original homeland north of [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire) by [Tsar Simeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_I_of_Bulgaria) after the [Battle of Southern Buh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Southern_Buh), settled in the territory at the end of the 9th century displacing the founding Bulgarian settlers of the towns of Buda and Pest,[^cite_note-Travel-36][^cite_note-76] and a century later officially founded the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary).[^cite_note-Travel-36] Research places the probable residence of the [Árpáds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81rp%C3%A1ds) as an early place of central power near what became Budapest.[^cite_note-77] The [Mongol invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mongol_invasion_of_Hungary) in the 13th century quickly proved it was difficult to defend a plain.[^cite_note-Encarta-42][^cite_note-Travel-36] King [Béla IV of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9la_IV_of_Hungary), therefore, ordered the construction of reinforced stone walls around the towns[^cite_note-Travel-36] and set his own royal palace on the top of the protecting hills of Buda. German settlers were invited to rebuild and inhabit both Buda and Pest.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] In 1361 it became the capital of Hungary.[^cite_note-Eleventh-37][^cite_note-Encarta-42]

The cultural role of Buda was particularly significant during the reign of [King Matthias Corvinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Corvinus_of_Hungary). The [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance) had a great influence on the city. His library, the [Bibliotheca Corviniana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliotheca_Corviniana), was Europe's greatest collection of historical chronicles and philosophic and scientific works in the 15th century, and second in size only to the [Vatican Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Library).[^cite_note-Encarta-42] After the foundation of the first Hungarian university in [Pécs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9cs) in 1367 ([University of Pécs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_P%C3%A9cs)), the second one was established in Óbuda in 1395 ([University of Óbuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_%C3%93buda)).[^cite_note-Sugar-80] The first Hungarian book was printed in Buda in 1473.[^cite_note-81] Buda had about 5,000 inhabitants around the year 1500.[^cite_note-Peter_F._Sugar_page_88-82]



*Retaking of Buda from the Ottoman Empire* (1686) by [Frans Geffels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frans_Geffels)

The [Ottomans](22278.md) conquered Buda in 1526, as well as in 1529, and finally [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Buda_\(1541\)) it in 1541.[^cite_note-83] The [Ottoman Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Hungary) lasted for more than 150 years.[^cite_note-Encarta-42] The Ottoman [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) constructed many prominent bathing facilities within the city.[^cite_note-Travel-36] Some of the baths that the Turks erected during their rule are still in use 500 years later, including [Rudas Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudas_Baths) and [Király Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kir%C3%A1ly_Baths). By 1547 the number of Christians was down to about a thousand, and by 1647 it had fallen to only about seventy.[^cite_note-Peter_F._Sugar_page_88-82] The unoccupied western part of the country became part of the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) as [Royal Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Hungary).

In 1686, two years after the unsuccessful [siege of Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Buda_\(1684\)), a renewed campaign was started to enter Buda. This time, the [Holy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_League_\(1684\))'s army was twice as large, containing over 74,000 men, including [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), [Croat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croat), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), Hungarian, English, Spanish, [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs), Italian, French, [Burgundian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundians), [Danish](419786.md) and Swedish soldiers, along with other Europeans as volunteers, [artillerymen](2508.md), and officers. The Christian forces [seized Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Buda_\(1686\)), and in the next few years, all of the former Hungarian lands, except areas near Temesvár ([Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara)), were taken from the Turks. In the 1699 [Treaty of Karlowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Karlowitz), these territorial changes were officially recognized as the end of the rule of the Turks, and in 1718 the entire [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) was removed from [Ottoman rule](22278.md).

### Contemporary history after Unification

See also: [Hungary during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_during_World_War_II)



[Millennium Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Budapest_Metro\)) (1894–1896), the second oldest metro in the world (after the [London Underground](17839.md))

The 19th century was dominated by the Hungarian struggle for independence[^cite_note-Encarta-42] and modernisation. The national insurrection against the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburgs) began in the Hungarian capital [in 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1848) and was defeated one and a half years later, with the help of the Russian Empire. 1867 was the year of [Reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Compromise_of_1867) that brought about the birth of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary). This made Budapest the twin capital of a dual monarchy. It was this compromise which opened the second great phase of development in the [history of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Budapest), lasting until [World War I](4764461.md). In 1849, the [Chain Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Chain_Bridge) linking Buda with Pest was opened as the first permanent bridge across the Danube[^cite_note-84] and in 1873 Buda and Pest were officially merged with the third part, Óbuda (Old Buda), thus creating the new metropolis of Budapest. The dynamic Pest grew into the country's administrative, political, economic, trade and cultural hub.&#x20;

Ethnic [Hungarians](17106358.md) overtook [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians) in the second half of the 19th century due to mass migration from the overpopulated rural [Transdanubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdanubia) and [Great Hungarian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hungarian_Plain). Between 1851 and 1910 the proportion of Hungarians increased from 35.6% to 85.9%, Hungarian became the dominant language, and German was crowded out. The proportion of Jews peaked in 1900 with 23.6%.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-Budapest_1946_p._12-87] Due to the prosperity and the large Jewish community of the city at the start of the 20th century, Budapest was often called the "Jewish Mecca"[^cite_note-Eleventh-37] or "Judapest".[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] Budapest also became an important centre for the [Aromanian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanian_diaspora) during the 19th century.[^cite_note-90] In 1918, Austria-Hungary lost the war and collapsed; Hungary declared itself an independent republic ([Republic of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Hungary)). On the 4th of August, 1919, the [Romanian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Army) occupied Budapest, marking the end of the [Hungarian-Romanian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian-Romanian_War). In 1920 the [Treaty of Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon) partitioned the country, and as a result, Hungary lost over two-thirds of its territory, and about two-thirds of its inhabitants, including 3.3 million out of 15 million ethnic Hungarians.[^cite_note-Macartney37-91][^cite_note-92]



The [Chain Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Chain_Bridge) was destroyed during the [Siege of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Budapest).

In 1944, a year before the end of [World War II](32927.md), Budapest was partly destroyed by [British](25679.md) and [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) air raids (first attack 4 April 1944[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]). From 24 December 1944 to 13 February 1945, the city was besieged during the [Battle of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Budapest). Budapest sustained major damage caused by the attacking Soviet and Romanian troops and the defending [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) and Hungarian troops. More than 38,000 civilians died during the conflict. [All bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_of_Budapest) were destroyed by the Germans. The stone lions that have decorated the Chain Bridge since 1852 survived the devastation of the war.[^cite_note-chbridge-96] Between 20% and 40% of Greater Budapest's 250,000 Jewish inhabitants died through [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) and [Arrow Cross Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Cross_Party), during the [German occupation of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Panzerfaust), from 1944 to early 1945.[^cite_note-97]

Swiss diplomat [Carl Lutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Lutz) rescued tens of thousands of Jews by issuing Swiss protection papers and designating numerous buildings, including the now famous Glass House (Üvegház) at Vadász Street 29, to be Swiss protected territory. About 3,000 Hungarian Jews found refuge at the Glass House and in a neighbouring building. Swedish diplomat [Raoul Wallenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Wallenberg) saved the lives of tens of thousands of Jews in Budapest by giving them Swedish protection papers and taking them under his consular protection.[^cite_note-98] Wallenberg was abducted by the Russians on 17 January 1945 and never regained freedom. [Giorgio Perlasca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Perlasca), an Italian citizen, saved thousands of Hungarian Jews posing as a Spanish diplomat.[^cite_note-timeisrael-99][^cite_note-ushm-100] Some other diplomats also abandoned diplomatic protocol and rescued Jews. There are two monuments for Wallenberg, one for Carl Lutz and one for Giorgio Perlasca in Budapest.



Soviet tanks in Budapest (1956)

Following the capture of Hungary from [Nazi Germany](21212.md) by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army), [Soviet military occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Hungary) ensued, which ended only in 1991. The Soviets exerted significant influence on Hungarian political affairs. In 1949, Hungary was declared a [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) People's Republic ([People's Republic of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Hungary)). The new Communist government considered the buildings like the [Buda Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda_Castle) symbols of the former regime, and during the 1950s the palace was gutted and all the interiors were destroyed (also see [Stalin era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin_era)). On 23 October 1956, citizens held a large peaceful demonstration in Budapest demanding democratic reform. The demonstrators went to the Budapest radio station and demanded to publish their demands. The regime ordered troops to shoot into the crowd. Hungarian soldiers gave rifles to the demonstrators who were now able to capture the building. This initiated the [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956). The demonstrators demanded to appoint [Imre Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Nagy) to be [Prime Minister of Hungary](378359.md). To their surprise, the central committee of the "[Hungarian Working People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Working_People's_Party)" did so that same evening. This uprising was an anti-Soviet revolt that lasted from 23 October until 11 November. After Nagy had declared that Hungary was to leave the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) and become neutral, Soviet tanks and troops entered the country to crush the revolt. Fighting continued until mid November, leaving more than 3000 dead. A monument was erected at the fiftieth anniversary of the revolt in 2006, at the edge of the [City Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Park_\(Budapest\)). Its shape is a wedge with a 56 angle degree made in rusted iron that gradually becomes shiny, ending in an intersection to symbolize Hungarian forces that temporarily eradicated the Communist leadership.[^cite_note-101]

From the 1960s to the late 1980s, Hungary was often satirically referred to as "[the happiest barrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goulash_Communism)" within the [Eastern bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_bloc), and much of the wartime damage to the city was finally repaired. Work on [Erzsébet Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzs%C3%A9bet_Bridge), the last to be rebuilt, was finished in 1964. In the early 1970s, [Budapest Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Metro)'s east–west [M2 line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Budapest_Metro\)) was first opened, followed by the [M3 line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Budapest_Metro\)) in 1976. In 1987, Buda Castle and the banks of the Danube were included in the [UNESCO](21786641.md) list of [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites). [Andrássy Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A1ssy_Avenue) (including the [Millennium Underground Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Underground_Railway), [Hősök tere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C5%91s%C3%B6k_tere), and [Városliget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1rosliget)) was added to the UNESCO list in 2002. In the 1980s, the city's population reached 2.1 million. In recent times a significant decrease in population occurred mainly due to a massive movement to the neighbouring agglomeration in [Pest county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_county), i.e., suburbanisation.[^cite_note-102]

In the last decades of the 20th century the political changes of 1989–90 ([Fall of the Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Iron_Curtain)) concealed changes in civil society and along the streets of Budapest. The monuments of the dictatorship were removed from public places, into [Memento Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memento_Park). In the first 20 years of the new democracy, the development of the city was managed by its mayor, [Gábor Demszky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A1bor_Demszky).[^cite_note-103]

In October 2019, opposition candidate [Gergely Karácsony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergely_Kar%C3%A1csony) won the Budapest mayoral [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Budapest_mayoral_election), meaning the first electoral blow for Hungary's nationalist prime minister [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) since coming to power in 2010.[^cite_note-104]

## Geography

### Topography



[Satellite imagery](1184557.md) of Budapest

Budapest, strategically placed at the centre of the [Pannonian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Basin), lies on an ancient route linking the hills of Transdanubia with the Great Plain. By car, it is 216 kilometres (134 mi) southeast of [Vienna, Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna,_Austria); 545 kilometres (339 mi) south of [Warsaw, Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw,_Poland); 1,565 kilometres (972 mi) southwest of [Moscow, Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Russia); 1,122 kilometres (697 mi) north of [Athens, Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens,_Greece); 1,235 kilometres (767 mi) northeast of [Rome, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome,_Italy); 788 kilometres (490 mi) northeast of [Milan](36511.md), Italy; 443 kilometres (275 mi) southeast of [Prague, Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague,_Czech_Republic); 343 kilometres (213 mi) northeast of [Zagreb, Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb,_Croatia); 748 kilometres (465 mi) northeast of [Split, Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia); and 1,329 kilometres (826 mi) northwest of [Istanbul, Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul,_Turkey).[^cite_note-105]

The 525-square-kilometre (203 sq mi) area of Budapest lies in [Central Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Hungary), surrounded by settlements of the agglomeration in Pest county. The capital extends 25 and 29 km (16 and 18 mi) in the north–south, east–west direction respectively. The Danube enters the city from the north; later it encircles two islands, [Óbuda Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93buda_Island) and [Margaret Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Island).[^cite_note-Encarta-42] The third island [Csepel Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepel_Island) is the largest of the Budapest Danube islands, however only its northernmost tip is within city limits. The river that separates the two parts of the city is 230 m (755 ft) wide at its narrowest point in Budapest. Pest lies on the flat terrain of the Great Plain while Buda is rather hilly.[^cite_note-Encarta-42]

The wide Danube was always fordable at this point because of a small number of islands in the middle of the river. The city has marked topographical contrasts: Buda is built on the higher river terraces and hills of the western side, while the considerably larger Pest spreads out on a flat and featureless sand plain on the river's opposite bank.[^cite_note-106] Pest's terrain rises with a slight eastward gradient, so the easternmost parts of the city lie at the same elevation as Buda's smallest hills, notably [Gellért Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gell%C3%A9rt_Hill) and Castle Hill.[^cite_note-budapest.com-107]

The Buda hills consist mainly of limestone and dolomite, the water created [speleothems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speleothem), the most famous ones being the Pálvölgyi cave (total length 7,200 m or 23,600 ft) and the Szemlőhegyi cave (total length 2,200 m or 7,200 ft). The hills were formed in the Triassic Period. The highest point of the hills and of Budapest is [János Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A1nos_Hill), at 527 metres (1,729 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level). The lowest point is the line of the Danube which is 96 metres (315 feet) above sea level. Budapest is also rich in green areas. Of the 525 square kilometres (203 square miles) occupied by the city, 83 square kilometres (32 square miles) is green area, park and forest.[^cite_note-108] The forests of [Buda hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda_hills) are environmentally protected.[^cite_note-109]

The city's importance in terms of traffic is very central, because many major [European roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_roads) and [European railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Europe) lines lead to Budapest.[^cite_note-budapest.com-107] The Danube was and is still an important water-way and this region in the centre of the Carpathian Basin lies at the cross-roads of [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route).[^cite_note-budpocketguide.com-110] Budapest is one of only three capital cities in the world which has [thermal springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring) (the others being [Reykjavík](25798.md) in [Iceland](14531.md) and [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) in [Bulgaria](3415.md)). Some 125 springs produce 70 million litres (18 million US gallons) of thermal water a day, with temperatures ranging up to 58 °C. Some of these waters have been claimed to have medicinal effects due to their high mineral contents.[^cite_note-budapest.com-107]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Budapest)

Budapest has a transitional climate between a [humid temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_temperate_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *[Cfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate)*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *[Doak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate)*), and a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Köppen: *Dfa*, Trewartha: *Dcao*), with warm to hot summers and chilly winters.[^cite_note-111] Winter (November until early March) can be cold and the city receives little sunshine. Snowfall is fairly frequent in most years, and nighttime temperatures of −10 °C (14 °F) are not uncommon between mid-December and mid-February. The spring months (March and April) see variable conditions, with a rapid increase in the average temperature. The weather in late March and in April is often very agreeable during the day and fresh at night. Budapest's long summer – lasting from May until mid-September – is warm or very warm. Sudden heavy showers also occur, particularly in May and June. The autumn in Budapest (mid-September until late October) is characterised by little rain and long sunny days with moderate temperatures. Temperatures often turn abruptly colder in late October or early November.

Mean annual precipitation in Budapest is around 586 mm (23.1 in). On average, there are 84 days with precipitation and 1988 hours of sunshine (of a possible 4383) each year.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] The city lies on the boundary between Zone 6 and Zone 7 in terms of the [hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

## Architecture

See also: [Category\:Buildings and structures in Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Buildings_and_structures_in_Budapest) and [List of churches in Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_churches_in_Budapest)













**Clockwise, from upper left**: the ruins of the Celtic and Roman civil town of [Aquincum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquincum); Romanesque 12th century [Gercse Parish Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gercse_Parish_Church?action=edit\&redlink=1); the [Buda Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda_Castle); [Kós Károly Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B3s_K%C3%A1roly_Square?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the [Wekerletelep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wekerletelep); Ottoman [Tomb of Gül Baba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_G%C3%BCl_Baba); and Gothic Mary Magdalene Church

Budapest has architecturally noteworthy buildings in a wide range of styles and from distinct time periods, from the ancient times as Roman City of Aquincum in Óbuda (District III), which dates to around 89 AD, to the most modern [Palace of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Arts_\(Budapest\)), the contemporary arts museum and concert hall.[^cite_note-Budapest_Architecture-123][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125]

Most buildings in Budapest are relatively low: in the early 2010s there were around 100 buildings higher than 45 metres (148 ft). The number of high-rise buildings is kept low by building legislation, which is aimed at preserving the historic cityscape and to meet the requirements of the [World Heritage Site](44940.md). Strong rules apply to the planning, authorisation and construction of high-rise buildings and consequently much of the [inner city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_city) does not have any. Some planners would like to see an easing of the rules for the construction of skyscrapers, and the possibility of building skyscrapers outside the city's historic core has been raised.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

In the chronological order of architectural styles Budapest is represented on the entire timeline, starting with the Roman City of Aquincum representing [ancient architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_architecture).

The next determinative style is the [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) in Budapest. The few remaining Gothic buildings can be found in the Castle District. Buildings of note are no. 18, 20 and 22 on Országház Street, which date back to the 14th century and No. 31 Úri Street, which has a Gothic façade that dates back to the 15th century. Other buildings with Gothic features are the [Inner City Parish Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_Parish_Church_in_Pest), built in the 12th century,[^cite_note-128] and the Mary Magdalene Church, completed in the 15th century.[^cite_note-129] The most characteristic Gothic-style buildings are actually [Neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture), like the most well-known Budapest landmarks, the [Hungarian Parliament Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament_Building)[^cite_note-Steves-130] and the [Matthias Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Church), where much of the original material was used (originally built in [Romanesque style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture) in 1015).[^cite_note-budapestbylocals.com-131]

The next chapter in the history of human architecture is [Renaissance architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture). One of the earliest places to be influenced by the Renaissance style of architecture was Hungary, and Budapest in particular. The style appeared following the marriage of King [Matthias Corvinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Corvinus) and [Beatrice of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_of_Naples) in 1476. Many Italian artists, craftsmen and masons came to Buda with the new queen. Today, many of the original renaissance buildings disappeared during the varied history of Buda, but Budapest is still rich in renaissance and neo-renaissance buildings, like the famous [Hungarian State Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_State_Opera_House), [St. Stephen's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_Basilica) and the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^cite_note-132]

During the Turkish occupation (1541–1686), Islamic culture flourished in Budapest; multiple mosques and baths were built in the city. These were great examples of [Ottoman architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_architecture), which was influenced by Muslims from around the world including Turkish, Iranian, Arabian and to a larger extent, [Byzantine architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_architecture) as well as Islamic traditions. After the Holy League conquered Budapest, they replaced most of the mosques with churches and minarets were turned into bell towers and cathedral spires. At one point the distinct sloping central square in Budapest became a bustling Oriental bazaar, which was filled with "the chatter of camel caravans on their way to Yemen and India".[^cite_note-NYT1991-133] Budapest is in fact one of the few places in the world with functioning original [hammams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammam) (bath houses) dating back to the 16th century, like Rudas Baths or [Király Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kir%C3%A1ly_Baths). Budapest is home to the northernmost place where the [tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_G%C3%BCl_Baba) of influential Islamic Turkish Sufi Dervish, [Gül Baba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCl_Baba) is found. Various cultures converged in Hungary seemed to coalesce well with each other, as if all these different cultures and architecture styles are digested into Hungary's own way of cultural blend. A precedent to show the city's self-conscious is the top section of the city's main square, named as [Szechenyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_square_\(P%C3%A9cs\)). When Turks came to the city, they built mosques here which was aggressively replaced with Gothic church of St. Bertalan. The rationale of reusing the base of the former Islamic building mosque and reconstruction into Gothic Church but Islamic style architecture over it is typically Islamic are still visible. An official term for the rationale is [spolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolia). The mosque was called the djami of Pasha Gazi Kassim, and djami means congregational or Friday mosque in Arabic. After Turks and Muslims were expelled and massacred from Budapest, the site was reoccupied by Christians and reformed into a church, the [Inner City Parish Church (Budapest)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_Parish_Church_in_Pest). The [minaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minaret) and Turkish entranceway were removed. The shape of the architecture is its only hint of exotic past—"two surviving prayer niches facing Mecca and an ecumenical symbol atop its cupola: a cross rising above the Turkish crescent moon".[^cite_note-NYT1991-133]



The most famous [Budapest bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_of_Budapest), the [Chain Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Chain_Bridge), the icon of the city's 19th century development, built in 1849

After 1686, the [Baroque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) designated the dominant style of art in catholic countries from the 17th century to the 18th century.[^cite_note-auto-134] There are many Baroque-style buildings in Budapest and one of the finest examples of preserved Baroque-style architecture is the Church of St. Anna in [Batthyhány square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batthy%C3%A1ny_t%C3%A9r). An interesting part of Budapest is the less touristy Óbuda, the main square of which also has some beautiful preserved historic buildings with Baroque façades. The Castle District is another place to visit where the best-known landmark Buda Royal Palace and many other buildings were built in the Baroque style.[^cite_note-auto-134]

The [Classical architecture](52687.md) and [Neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) are the next in the timeline. Budapest had not one but two architects that were masters of the Classicist style. [Mihály Pollack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mih%C3%A1ly_Pollack) (1773–1855) and [József Hild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zsef_Hild) (1789–1867), built many beautiful Classicist-style buildings in the city. Some of the best examples are the [Hungarian National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_National_Museum), the [Lutheran Church of Budavár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church_of_Budav%C3%A1r) (both designed by Pollack) and the seat of the [Hungarian president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_Hungary), the [Sándor Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1ndor_Palace,_Budapest). The most iconic and widely known Classicist-style attraction in Budapest is the [Széchenyi Chain Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Chain_Bridge).[^cite_note-structurae-135] Budapest's two most beautiful [Romantic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture#Romanticism_and_nationalism) buildings are the [Great Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doh%C3%A1ny_Street_Synagogue) in Dohány Street and the [Vigadó Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigad%C3%B3_Concert_Hall) on the [Danube Promenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Promenade), both designed by architect [Frigyes Feszl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigyes_Feszl) (1821–1884). Another noteworthy structure is the [Budapest Western Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest-Nyugati_Railway_Terminal), which was designed by August de Serres and built by the [Eiffel Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_\(company\)) of Paris in 1877.[^cite_note-136]



The [Hungarian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament_Building), completed in 1904

[Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) came into fashion in Budapest by the exhibitions which were held in and around 1896 and organised in connection with the Hungarian [Millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium) celebrations.[^cite_note-137] Art Nouveau in Hungary (*Szecesszió* in Hungarian) is a blend of several architectural styles, with a focus on Hungary's specialities. One of the leading Art Nouveau architects, [Ödön Lechner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96d%C3%B6n_Lechner) (1845–1914), was inspired by Indian and Syrian architecture as well as traditional Hungarian decorative designs. One of his most beautiful buildings in Budapest is the [Museum of Applied Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Applied_Arts_\(Budapest\)). Another examples for Art Nouveau in Budapest is the [Gresham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_Palace) in front of the Chain Bridge, the [Hotel Gellért](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Gell%C3%A9rt), the [Franz Liszt Academy of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Liszt_Academy_of_Music) or [Budapest Zoo and Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Zoo_and_Botanical_Garden).[^cite_note-Budapest_Architecture-123]

> It is one of the world's outstanding urban landscapes and illustrates the great periods in the history of the Hungarian capital.

— *[UNESCO](21786641.md)*

[^cite_note-138]

The second half of the 20th century also saw, under the communist regime, the construction of [blocks of flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blocks_of_flats) ([panelház](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panelh%C3%A1z)), as in other Eastern European countries. In the 21st century, Budapest faces new challenges in its architecture. The pressure towards the high-rise buildings is unequivocal among today's world cities, but preserving Budapest's unique cityscape and its very diverse architecture, along with green areas, forces Budapest to balance between them. The [Contemporary architecture](3970948.md) has wide margin in the city. [Public spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_space) attract heavy investment by business and government also, so that the city has gained entirely new (or renovated and redesigned) squares, parks and monuments, for example the city central [Kossuth Lajos square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kossuth_Lajos_t%C3%A9r,_Budapest), [Deák Ferenc square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De%C3%A1k_Ferenc_t%C3%A9r) and [Liberty Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Square_\(Budapest\)). Numerous landmarks have been created in the last decade in Budapest, like the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_\(Budapest\)), Palace of Arts, [Rákóczi Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A1k%C3%B3czi_Bridge), [Megyeri Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megyeri_Bridge), [Budapest Airport Sky Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Ferenc_Liszt_International_Airport#Sky_Court_between_Terminal_2A_and_2B) among others, and millions of square meters of new [office buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_buildings) and [apartments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residential_area). But there are still large opportunities in [real estate development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_development) in the city.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

Contemporary Budapest is divided into 23 districts (Hungarian: *kerületek*, sing.: *kerület*), each with a mayor and municipal government elected separately from the general municipal government. The districts and the general municipal government have [constitutionally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Hungary) and legally defined, non-overlapping areas of competence. Each district has a municipally recognized name, some of which correspond to how locals call that area or neighborhood (e.g., [Belváros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_\(Budapest\)), V. district; [Terézváros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ter%C3%A9zv%C3%A1ros), VI. district), others which (e.g., [Újbuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ajbuda), XI. district) are neologisms. Street signs display the district and that neighborhood's colloquial name. The latter are often the names of villages that were gradually annexed to the city (e.g., [Sashalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sashalom), [Budafok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budafok)) or of superseded administrative units of former boroughs.[^cite_note-144]

After the unification of Buda, Pest, and Óbuda in 1873, Budapest initially had 10 districts. It was during the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_\(1920%E2%80%931946\)) that [Károly Szendy](https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szendy%20K%C3%A1roly)'s 1934-1944 mayoral administration first seriously considered annexing peripheral towns and villages. This only came about, however, after the rise of [state communism in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_People's_Republic). In 1950, for reasons of social and industrial policy—including the [Hungarian Working People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Working_People's_Party)'s desire to [proletarianize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarianization) the traditionally right-wing suburbs—7 [cities with county rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_with_county_rights) and 16 towns were annexed to the capital to form contemporary [Greater Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Budapest) (Hungarian: *Nagy-Budapest*).[^cite_note-145] This reorganized the city into 22 districts, a number that grew to 23 after [Soroksár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroks%C3%A1r) seceded from [Pesterzsébet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesterzs%C3%A9bet) in 1994. The contemporary city thus consists of 6 districts in Buda, 16 in Pest, and [Csepel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepel). Today, districts I., II., XI., and XII. in Buda and V., VI., VII., VIII., and IX. in Pest make up the city centre in its broadest sense, corresponding roughly to the 1873 municipal boundaries.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

Budapest's districts are numbered according to three concentric semicircles. The I. district is a small area in central Buda, including the [Castle Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Quarter_\(Budapest\)). District II. is in Buda to the castle's northwest while district III. stretches along the northernmost part of Buda and includes the former Óbuda. District IV. continues this semicircle in northernmost Pest, but the V. district is in the very centre of Pest and inaugurates a new circle that then loops back through Pest to Buda as the VI., VII., VIII., IX., XI., and XII. districts. Districts XIII., XIV., XV., XVI., XVII., XVIII., XIX., XX., XXI., and XXII. form yet another semicircle in outermost Pest. Districts X. and XXIII. form irregularities within the overall pattern.[^cite_note-148]

Budapest is the most [populous city in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Hungary) and [one of the largest cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_cities_of_the_European_Union_by_population_within_city_limits) in the [European Union](9317.md), with a growing number of inhabitants, estimated at 1,763,913 in 2019,[^cite_note-152] whereby inward migration exceeds outward migration.[^cite_note-TIME2-153] These trends are also seen throughout the [Budapest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_metropolitan_area), which is home to 3.3 million people.[^cite_note-Budapest_City_Review2-154][^cite_note-155] This amounts to about 34% of Hungary's population. In 2014, the city had a population density of 3,314 people/km<sup>2</sup> (8,580 people/mi<sup>2</sup>), rendering it the most densely populated of all municipalities in Hungary. The population density of [Elisabethtown-District VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzs%C3%A9betv%C3%A1ros) is 30,989/km<sup>2</sup> (80,260/mi<sup>2</sup>), which has the highest population density figure in Hungary and [one of the highest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_population_density). For comparison, the density in [Manhattan](45470.md) is 25,846/km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-156]

Budapest is the fourth most "dynamically growing city" by [population in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Europe),[^cite_note-157] and the Euromonitor predicts a population increase of almost 10% between 2005 and 2030.[^cite_note-158] The [European Observation Network for Territorial Development and Cohesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Observation_Network_for_Territorial_Development_and_Cohesion) says Budapest's population will increase by 10% to 30% only due to migration by 2050.[^cite_note-159] A constant inflow of migrants in recent years has fuelled population growth in Budapest. Productivity gains and the relatively large economically active share of the population explain why [household incomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_income) have increased in Budapest to a greater extent than in other parts of Hungary. Higher incomes in Budapest are reflected in the lower share of expenditure the city's inhabitants allocate to necessary spending such as on food and non-alcoholic drinks.[^cite_note-Budapest_City_Review2-154]

According to the 2016 microcensus, there were 1,764,263 people living in Budapest in 907,944 dwellings.[^cite_note-160] Some 1.6 million persons from the metropolitan area may be within Budapest's boundaries during working hours, and during special events. This fluctuation in the population is caused by hundreds of thousands of suburban residents who travel to the city for work, education, health care, and special events.[^cite_note-suburbanisation-161]

By ethnicity there were 1,697,039 (96.2%) [Hungarians](17106358.md), 34,909 (2%) [Germans](152735.md), 16,592 (0.9%) [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people), 9,117 (0.5%) [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) and 5,488 (0.3%) [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks).[^cite_note-162] In Hungary people can declare multiple ethnic identities, hence the sum may exceed 100%.[^cite_note-micro_2016-163] The share of ethnic Hungarians in Budapest (96.2%) is slightly lower than the national average (98.3%) due to the international migration.[^cite_note-micro_2016-163]

According to the 2011 census, 1,712,153 people (99.0%) speak [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language), of whom 1,692,815 people (97.9%) speak it as a [first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language), while 19,338 people (1.1%) speak it as a [second language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_language). Other spoken (foreign) languages were: English (536,855 speakers, 31.0%), German (266,249 speakers, 15.4%), French (56,208 speakers, 3.3%) and Russian (54,613 speakers, 3.2%).[^cite_note-Census2011-164]

According to the same census, 1,600,585 people (92.6%) were born in Hungary, 126,036 people (7.3%) outside Hungary while the birthplace of 2,419 people (0.1%) was unknown.[^cite_note-Census2011-164] Although only 1.7% of the population of Hungary in 2009 were foreigners, 43% of them lived in Budapest, making them 4.4% of the city's population (up from 2% in 2001).[^cite_note-Novekszik-165] Nearly two-thirds of foreigners living in Hungary were under 40 years old. The primary motivation for this age group living in Hungary was employment.[^cite_note-Novekszik-165]

Budapest is home to one of the most populous [Christian communities](5211.md) in Central Europe, numbering 698,521 people (40.4%) in 2011.[^cite_note-Census2011-164] According to the 2011 census, there were 501,117 (29.0%) [Roman Catholics](606848.md), 146,756 (8.5%) [Calvinists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism), 30,293 (1.8%) [Lutherans](23371382.md), 16,192 (0.9%) [Greek Catholics](214903.md), 7,925 (0.5%) Jews and 3,710 (0.2%) [Orthodox](10186.md) in Budapest. 395,964 people (22.9%) were [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) while 585,475 people (33.9%) did not declare their religion.[^cite_note-Census2011-164] The city is home to the fifth largest [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_population_by_city) in Europe.

## Economy

Further information: [List of companies based in Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_based_in_Budapest) and [Economy of Hungary](13428.md)

[Research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) centre of [Richter Gedeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedeon_Richter_Plc.) in Budapest

Budapest is a significant economic hub, classified as a Beta + world city in the study by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) and it is the second fastest-developing [urban economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_economy) in Europe as GDP per capita in the city increased by 2.4 per cent and employment by 4.7 per cent compared to the previous year in 2014.[^cite_note-Brookings_Institution-166][^cite_note-auto1-167] On a national level, Budapest is the [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city) of Hungary regarding business and the economy, accounting for 39% of the national income. The city had a [gross metropolitan product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_metropolitan_product) of more than \$100 billion in 2015, making it one of the largest regional economies in the European Union.[^cite_note-168] According to [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat) GDP, per capita in [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) is 147% of the EU average in Budapest, which means €37,632 (\$42,770) per capita.[^cite_note-Iz.sk2-169] Budapest is also among the Top 100 GDP performing cities in the world, measured by [PricewaterhouseCoopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PricewaterhouseCoopers). The city was named as the 52nd most important business centre in the world in the [Worldwide Centres of Commerce Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldwide_Centres_of_Commerce_Index), ahead of Beijing, São Paulo and [Shenzhen](83368.md) and ranking 3rd (out of 65 cities) on the [MasterCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterCard) [Emerging Markets Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_Markets_Index).[^cite_note-MasterCard-170][^cite_note-171] The city is 48th on the [UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS) *The most expensive and richest cities in the world* list, standing before cities such as Prague, Shanghai, Kuala Lumpur and [Buenos Aires](255919.md).[^cite_note-172] In a global city competitiveness ranking by the [EIU](15303116.md), Budapest stands before [Tel Aviv](31453.md), Lisbon, Moscow and [Johannesburg](58893.md) among others.[^cite_note-173]

The city is a major centre for banking and finance, real estate, retailing, trade, transportation, tourism, [new media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_media) as well as [traditional media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_media), advertising, [legal services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_services), [accountancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountancy), insurance, fashion and the arts in Hungary and regionally. Budapest is home not only to almost all national institutions and government agencies, but also to many domestic and international companies. In 2014 there were 395.804 companies registered in the city.[^cite_note-174] Most of these entities are headquartered in Budapest's Central Business District, in [District V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belv%C3%A1ros-Lip%C3%B3tv%C3%A1ros) and [District XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ajlip%C3%B3tv%C3%A1ros). The retail market of the city (and the country) is also concentrated in the downtown area, among others, in the two largest shopping centres in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe), the 186,000 sqm [WestEnd City Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WestEnd_City_Center) and the 180,000 sqm [Arena Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Plaza).[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

Budapest has notable innovation capabilities as a technology and start-up hub. Many [start-ups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) are headquartered and begin their business in the city. Some of the best known examples are [Prezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prezi), [LogMeIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LogMeIn) and [NNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NNG_\(company\)). Budapest is the highest ranked [Central and Eastern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe) city in the Innovation Cities' Top 100 index.[^cite_note-Innovation-cities.com-177] A good indicator of the city's potential for innovation and research, is that the [European Institute of Innovation and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Institute_of_Innovation_and_Technology) chose Budapest for its headquarters, along with the UN, whose Regional Representation for Central Europe office is in the city, responsible for UN operations in seven countries.[^cite_note-178] Moreover, the global aspect of the city's research activity is shown through the establishment of the European Chinese Research Institute in the city.[^cite_note-179] Other important sectors also include, [natural science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science) research, information technology and medical research, non-profit institutions, and universities. The leading business schools and universities in Budapest, the [Budapest Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Business_School), the [CEU Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEU_Business_School) and [Corvinus University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvinus_University_of_Budapest) offer a whole range of courses in economics, finance and management in English, French, German and Hungarian.[^cite_note-180] The [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) in Budapest is by far the lowest within Hungary. It was 2.7%, with many thousands of employed foreign citizens.[^cite_note-181]

Budapest is among the 25 most visited cities in the world, welcoming more than 4.4 million international visitors each year,[^cite_note-182] therefore the traditional and the congress tourism industry also deserve a mention, as they contribute greatly to the city's economy. The capital is home to many [convention centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_center) and there are thousands of restaurants, bars, coffee houses and party places, besides a full range of hotels. As regards restaurants, examples can be found of the highest quality [Michelin-starred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Michelin_starred_restaurants#By_country) restaurants, such as Onyx, Costes, Tanti and Borkonyha. The city ranked as the most liveable city in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe) on EIU's [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life) index in 2010.

## Politics and government

Main article: [Politics of Hungary](13427.md)



[Model United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_United_Nations) conference in the assembly hall of [House of Magnates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament_Building#Features)



Old building (from 1890) of the [Hungarian Royal Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Curia_of_the_Kingdom_of_Hungary), that operated as the [highest court](4913027.md) in the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) between 1723 and 1949. Now it houses a [museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnographic_Museum_\(Budapest\)).



[Sándor Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1ndor_Palace), the official residence and workspace of the [president of Hungary](380393.md)

As the capital of Hungary, Budapest is the seat of the country's [national government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_government). The President of Hungary resides at the Sándor Palace in the District I (Buda Castle District),[^cite_note-185] while the office of the Hungarian Prime Minister is in the Carmelite Monastery in the Castle District.[^cite_note-186] [Government ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)) are all located in various parts of the city, most of them are in the District V, [Leopoldtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_\(Budapest\)). The [National Assembly](585416.md) is seated in the Hungarian Parliament, which also located in the District V.[^cite_note-187] The [President of the National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Speakers_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Hungary), the third-highest public official in Hungary, is also seated in the largest building in the country, in the Hungarian Parliament.

Hungary's highest courts are located in Budapest. The Curia ([supreme court](4913027.md) of Hungary), the highest court in the judicial order, which reviews criminal and civil cases, is located in the District V, Leopoldtown. Under the authority of its president it has three departments: criminal, civil and administrative-labour law departments. Each department has various chambers. The Curia guarantees the uniform application of law. The decisions of the Curia on uniform [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) are binding for other courts.[^cite_note-188] The second most important judicial authority, the National Judicial Council, is also housed in the District V, with the tasks of controlling the financial management of the judicial administration and the courts and giving an opinion on the practice of the president of the National Office for the Judiciary and the Curia deciding about the applications of judges and court leaders, among others.[^cite_note-189] The [Constitutional Court of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Hungary) is one of the highest level actors independent of the politics in the country. The Constitutional Court serves as the main body for the protection of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Hungary), its tasks being the review of the constitutionality of statutes. The Constitutional Court performs its tasks independently. With its own budget and its judges being elected by Parliament it does not constitute a part of the ordinary judicial system. The constitutional court passes on the [constitutionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality) of laws, and there is no right of appeal on these decisions.[^cite_note-190]

Budapest hosts the main and regional headquarters of many international organizations as well, including [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](31964.md), [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization_of_the_United_Nations), European Institute of Innovation and Technology, [European Police Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Police_College), [International Centre for Democratic Transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Democratic_Transition), [Institute of International Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_International_Education), [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization), [International Organization for Migration](36893.md), [International Red Cross](9720597.md), Regional Environmental Center for Central and Eastern Europe, [Danube Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Commission_\(1948\)) and even others.[^cite_note-191] The city is also home to more than 100 [embassies](8970.md) and representative bodies as an international political actor.

Environmental issues have a high priority among Budapest's politics. Institutions such as the Regional Environmental Center for Central and Eastern Europe, located in Budapest, are very important assets.[^cite_note-192] To decrease the use of cars and [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md), the city has worked to improve public transportation, and nowadays the city has achieved a sufficient level of[mass transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transit) usage. Budapest's public transportation system works as a network of buses, [trolleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus), trams and [subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit). Budapest has an above-average proportion of people commuting on public transport or walking and cycling for European cities.[^cite_note-A_good_place_to_live_–_Budapest-193] Riding on [bike paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_paths) is one of the best ways to see Budapest – there are about 180 kilometres (110 miles) of bicycle paths in the city, fitting into the [EuroVelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroVelo) system.[^cite_note-194]

Crime in Budapest is investigated by different bodies. [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime) notes in their 2011 Global Study on Homicide that, according to criminal justice sources, the homicide rate in Hungary, calculated based on UN population estimates, was 1.4 in 2009, compared to Canada's rate of 1.8 that same year.[^cite_note-195] The homicide rate in Budapest is below the EU capital cities' average according to [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) also.[^cite_note-196] However, [organised crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organised_crime) is associated with the city, the Institute of Defence in a UN study named Budapest as one of the "global epicentres" of illegal pornography, money laundering and contraband tobacco, and also a negotiation centre for international crime group leaders.[^cite_note-197]

Budapest has been a [metropolitan municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_municipality) with a mayor-council form of government since its consolidation in 1873, but Budapest also holds a special status as a county-level government, and also special within that, as holds a capital-city territory status.[^cite_note-198] In Budapest, the central government is responsible for the [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning), [statutory planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_planning), public transport, housing, [waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management), municipal taxes, correctional institutions, libraries, public safety, recreational facilities, among others. The Mayor is responsible for all city services, police and fire protection, enforcement of all city and state laws within the city, and administration of public property and most public agencies. Besides, each of Budapest' twenty-three districts has its own town hall and a directly elected council and the directly elected mayor of district.[^cite_note-municipality-199]

The Mayor of Budapest is [Gergely Karácsony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergely_Kar%C3%A1csony) who was elected on 13 October 2019. The mayor and members of General Assembly are elected to five-year terms.[^cite_note-municipality-199] The Budapest General Assembly is a [unicameral](204304.md) body consisting of 33 members, which consist of the 23 mayors of the districts, 9 from the electoral lists of political parties, plus Mayor of Budapest (the Mayor is elected directly). Each term for the mayor and assembly members lasts five years.[^cite_note-200] Submitting the budget of Budapest is the responsibility of the Mayor and the deputy-mayor in charge of finance. The latest, 2014 budget was approved with 18 supporting votes from ruling Fidesz and 14 votes against by the opposition lawmakers.[^cite_note-201]

## Main sights and tourism

See also: [List of sights and historic places in Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sights_and_historic_places_in_Budapest) and [List of tourist attractions in Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tourist_attractions_in_Budapest)



[St. Stephen's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_Basilica)

Budapest is widely known for its well-kept pre-war cityscape, with a great variety of streets and landmarks in [classical architecture](52687.md).

The most well-known sight of the capital is the [neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Gothic) [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament_Building), the biggest building in Hungary with its 268 metres (879 ft) length, also holding (since 2001) the [Hungarian Crown Jewels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Crown_of_Hungary).

[Saint Stephen's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Stephen's_Basilica) is the most important religious building of the city, where the [Holy Right Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Right_Hand?action=edit\&redlink=1) of Hungary's first king, [Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary) is on display as well.

[Hungarian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine) and café culture can be seen and tasted in many places, like [Gerbeaud Café](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_Gerbeaud), the *Százéves*, *Biarritz*, *Fortuna*, *Alabárdos*, *Arany Szarvas*, *[Gundel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundel)* and the world-famous Mátyás-pince restaurants and beer bars.

There are Roman remains at the [Aquincum Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquincum_Museum), and historic furniture at the [Nagytétény Castle Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagyt%C3%A9t%C3%A9ny_Castle_Museum), just 2 of 223 museums in Budapest. Another historical museum is the [House of Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Terror), hosted in the building that was the venue of the [Nazi Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Headquarters). The Castle Hill, the River Danube embankments and the whole of Andrássy út have been officially recognized as [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage_Sites).

Castle Hill and the [Castle District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_District,_Buda); there are three churches here, six museums, and a host of interesting buildings, streets and squares. The former Royal Palace is one of the symbols of Hungary – and has been the scene of battles and wars ever since the 13th century. Nowadays it houses two museums and the [National Széchenyi Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Library). The nearby Sándor Palace contains the offices and official residence of the [President of Hungary](380393.md). The seven-hundred-year-old Matthias Church is one of the jewels of Budapest, it is in neo-Gothic style, decorated with coloured shingles and elegant pinnacles. Next to it is an equestrian statue of the first king of Hungary, King Saint Stephen, and behind that is the [Fisherman's Bastion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisherman's_Bastion), built in 1905 by the architect [Frigyes Schulek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigyes_Schulek), the Fishermen's Bastions owes its name to the namesake corporation that during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) was responsible of the defence of this part of ramparts, from where opens out a panoramic view of the whole city. Statues of the [Turul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turul), the mythical guardian bird of Hungary, can be found in both the Castle District and the [Twelfth District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegyvid%C3%A9k).

A wide-angle view of Heroes Square in Budapest showing the Millennium Monument, colonnades, and visitors in the open plaza under a clear sky.

Heroes Square Budapest panoramic view

In Pest, arguably the most important sight is Andrássy út. This Avenue is an elegant 2.5 kilometres (2 miles) long tree-lined street that covers the distance from Deák Ferenc tér to the Heroes Square. This Avenue overlooks many important sites. It is a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md). As far as [Kodály körönd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kod%C3%A1ly_k%C3%B6r%C3%B6nd) and [Oktogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktogon_\(intersection\)) both sides are lined with large shops and flats built close together. Between there and Heroes' Square the houses are detached and altogether grander. Under the whole runs continental Europe's oldest Underground railway, most of whose stations retain their original appearance. Heroes' Square is dominated by the [Millenary Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C5%91s%C3%B6k_tere#Millenary_Monument), with the [Tomb of the Unknown Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier) in front. To the sides are the [Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts_\(Budapest\)) and the [Kunsthalle Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthalle_Budapest), and behind City Park opens out, with [Vajdahunyad Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajdahunyad_Castle). One of the jewels of Andrássy út is the Hungarian State Opera House. [Statue Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_Park), a theme park with striking statues of the [Communist era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_era), is located just outside the main city and is accessible by public transport.



[Dohány Street Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doh%C3%A1ny_Street_Synagogue), the largest synagogue in Europe

The [Dohány Street Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doh%C3%A1ny_Street_Synagogue) is the largest synagogue in Europe, and the second largest active synagogue in the world.[^cite_note-202] The synagogue is located in the Jewish district taking up several blocks in central Budapest bordered by Király utca, Wesselényi utca, [Grand Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Boulevard_\(Budapest\)) and Bajcsy Zsilinszky road. It was built in moorish revival style in 1859 and has a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of 3,000. Adjacent to it is a sculpture reproducing a weeping willow tree in steel to commemorate the Hungarian victims of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Hungary).

The city is also home to the largest medicinal [bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_baths) in Europe ([Széchenyi Medicinal Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Medicinal_Bath)) and the third largest Parliament building in the world, once the largest in the world. Other attractions are the [bridges of the capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_of_Budapest). Seven bridges provide crossings over the Danube, and from north to south are: the [Árpád Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81rp%C3%A1d_Bridge) (built in 1950 at the north of Margaret Island); the [Margaret Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Bridge) (built in 1901, destroyed during the war by an explosion and then rebuilt in 1948); the Chain Bridge (built in 1849, destroyed during [World War II](32927.md) and then rebuilt in 1949); the [Elisabeth Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Bridge) (completed in 1903 and dedicated to the murdered [Queen Elisabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Elisabeth_of_Austria), it was destroyed by the Germans during the war and replaced with a new bridge in 1964); the [Liberty Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Bridge_\(Budapest\)) (opened in 1896 and rebuilt in 1989 in Art Nouveau style); the [Petőfi Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C5%91fi_Bridge) (completed in 1937, destroyed during the war and rebuilt in 1952); the Rákóczi Bridge (completed in 1995). Most remarkable for their beauty are the Margaret Bridge, the Chain Bridge and the Liberty Bridge. The world's largest panorama photograph was created in (and of) Budapest in 2010.[^cite_note-203]

Tourists visiting Budapest can receive free maps and information from the nonprofit Budapest Festival and Tourism Center at its info-points.[^cite_note-204] The info centres also offer the Budapest Card which allows free public transit and discounts for several museums, restaurants and other places of interest. Cards are available for 24-, 48- or 72-hour durations.[^cite_note-205] The city is also well known for its ruin bars both day and night.

### Parks and gardens

The [City Park Ice Rink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Park_Ice_Rink), located in the [City Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Park_\(Budapest\)); the [Vajdahunyad Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajdahunyad_Castle) is in the background.

Budapest has many [municipal parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_parks) and most have playgrounds for children and seasonal activities like skating in the winter and boating in the summer. Budapest has a complex park system, with various lands operated by the Budapest City Gardening Ltd.[^cite_note-parks-206] The wealth of greenspace afforded by Budapest's parks is further augmented by a network of open spaces containing forest, streams, and lakes that are set aside as natural areas which lie not far from the inner city, including the Budapest Zoo and Botanical Garden (established in 1866) in the City Park.[^cite_note-207] The most notable and popular parks in Budapest are the [City Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Park_\(Budapest\)) which was established in 1751 (302 acres) along with [Andrássy Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A1ssy_Avenue),[^cite_note-208] the [Margaret Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Island) in the Danube (238 acres or 96 hectares),[^cite_note-209] the [People's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Park_\(Budapest\)), the [Római Part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B3mai_Part_\(Roman_Beach\)), and the Kopaszi Dam.[^cite_note-210]

The [Buda Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda_Hills) also offer a variety of outdoor activities and views. A place frequented by locals is [Normafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normafa), offering activities for all seasons. With a modest ski run, it is also used by skiers and snowboarders – if there is enough snowfall in winter.

### Spas



[Széchenyi Thermal Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Medicinal_Bath) in the [City Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Park_\(Budapest\))



Gate of the Gellért Spa

One of the reasons the Romans first colonised the area immediately to the west of the River Danube and established their regional capital at Aquincum (now part of Óbuda, in northern Budapest) is so that they could use and enjoy the thermal springs. There are still ruins visible today of the enormous baths that were built during that period. The new baths that were constructed during the Turkish period (1541–1686) served both bathing and medicinal purposes, and some of these are still in use to this day.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]

Budapest gained its reputation as a city of [spas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spas_in_Budapest) in the 1920s, following the first realisation of the economic potential of the thermal waters in drawing in visitors. Indeed, in 1934 Budapest was officially ranked as a "City of Spas". Today, the baths are mostly frequented by the older generation, as, with the exception of the "Magic Bath" and "Cinetrip" water discos, young people tend to prefer the lidos which are open in the summer.

Construction of the Király Baths started in 1565, and most of the present-day building dates from the Turkish period, including most notably the fine cupola-topped pool.

The [Rudas Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudas_Baths) are centrally placed – in the narrow strip of land between Gellért Hill and the River Danube – and also an outstanding example of architecture dating from the Turkish period. The central feature is an octagonal pool over which light shines from a 10 metres (33 ft) diameter cupola, supported by eight pillars.

The [Saint Gellért Medicinal Baths and Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gell%C3%A9rt_Baths) were built in 1918, although there had previously been medicinal baths on the site and, earlier, in the Middle Ages a hospital. In 1927, the Baths were extended to include the wave pool, and the effervescent bath was added in 1934. The interior of the hotel was largely destroyed during the January 1945 bombing raids but was later restored, and its Art Nouveau interior includes colourful mosaics, marble columns, stained glass windows and statues.[^cite_note-213] The complex closed in 2025 for a major renovation, with a scheduled reopening in 2028.[^cite_note-214]

The [Lukács Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luk%C3%A1cs_Baths) are also in Buda and are also Turkish in origin, although they were only revived at the end of the 19th century. This was also when the spa and treatment centre were founded. There is still something of an atmosphere of fin-de-siècle about the place, and all around the inner courtyard there are marble tablets recalling the thanks of patrons who were cured there. Since the 1950s it has been regarded as a centre for intellectuals and artists.

The [Széchenyi Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Baths) are one of the largest bathing complexes in all Europe, and the only "old" medicinal baths to be found in the Pest side of the city. The indoor medicinal baths date from 1913 and the outdoor pools from 1927. There is an atmosphere of grandeur about the whole place with the bright, largest pools resembling aspects associated with Roman baths, the smaller bath tubs reminding one of the bathing culture of the Greeks, and the saunas and diving pools borrowed from traditions emanating in northern Europe. The three outdoor pools (one of which is a fun pool) are open all year, including winter. Indoors there are over ten separate pools, and a whole host of medical treatments is also available. The Szécheny Baths are built in modern Renaissance style.

## Infrastructure and transportation

### Airport

Main article: [Budapest Ferenc Liszt International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Ferenc_Liszt_International_Airport)



[Budapest International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Ferenc_Liszt_International_Airport) arrivals and departures lounge between [terminal](423849.md) 2A and 2B, named SkyCourt

Budapest is served by [Budapest Ferenc Liszt International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Ferenc_Liszt_International_Airport) (BUD) (named after [Franz Liszt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Liszt), the notable Hungarian composer), one of the busiest airports in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe), located 16 kilometres (9.9 mi) east-southeast of the centre of Budapest, in the [District XVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pestszentl%C5%91rinc-Pestszentimre). The airport offers international connections among all major European cities, and also to North America, Africa, Asia and the Middle East. As Hungary's busiest airport, it handles nearly all of the country's air passenger traffic. Budapest Liszt Ferenc handled around 250 [scheduled flights](1942.md) daily in 2013, and an ever-rising number of [charters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_charter). London, [Brussels](3708.md), Frankfurt, [Munich](19058.md), Paris, and [Amsterdam](844.md) are the busiest international connections respectively, while [Toronto](64646.md), Montreal, [Dubai](211583.md), [Doha](26214389.md) and [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante) are the most unusual in the region.[^cite_note-215] Today the airport serves as a base for [Ryanair](262878.md), [Wizz Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air), [Budapest Aircraft Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Aircraft_Service), [LOT Polish Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOT_Polish_Airlines) and [Smartwings Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_Service_Hungary) among others. The airport is accessible via public transportation from the city centre by the Metro line 3 and then the airport bus [200E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_bus_route_200E) and [100E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_bus_route_100E).[^cite_note-216]

As part of a strategic development plan, €561 million have been spent on expanding and modernising the [airport infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_infrastructure) until December 2012. Most of these improvements are already completed,[^cite_note-217] the postponed ones are the new cargo area and new piers for terminal 2A and 2B, but these development are on standby also, and will start immediately, when the airport traffic will reach the appropriate level. SkyCourt, the newest, state-of-the-art building between the 2A and 2B terminals with 5 levels. Passenger safety checks were moved here along with new baggage classifiers and the new Malév and SkyTeam [business lounges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_lounge), as well as the first MasterCard lounge in Europe.[^cite_note-218]

### Public transportation



Budapest [metro and rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) network within the city and to suburbs



CAF Tram on Line 17 at Széll Kálmán Square



A Mercedes-Benz Citaro C2G articulated bus in Budapest on Line 8E operated by BKK-BKV

Public transit in Budapest is provided by the [Centre for Budapest Transport (BKK, *Budapesti Közlekedési Központ*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapesti_K%C3%B6zleked%C3%A9si_K%C3%B6zpont), one of the largest [transportation authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_authority) in Europe.[^cite_note-219] BKK operates 4 [metro lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Metro) (including the historic [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Budapest_Metro\)), the oldest underground railway in continental Europe), 5 [suburban railway lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%89V), 33 [tram lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Budapest), 15 trolleybus lines, 264 bus lines (including 40 [night routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_bus)), 4 boat services, and *[BuBi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuBi)*, a smart [bicycle sharing network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_sharing_system). On an average weekday, BKK lines transports 3.9 million riders; in 2011, it handled a total of 1.4 billion passengers.[^cite_note-220] In 2014, the 65% of the passenger traffic in Budapest was by public transport and 35% by car. The aim is 80%–20% by 2030 in accordance with the strategy of BKK.[^cite_note-221]

People aged 65 and over and under 14 travel free.[^cite_note-222]

The development of an [intelligent transportation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_transportation_system) in the city is progressing, including the application of [smart traffic lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_traffic_light) that are GPS and computer controlled. Computers give priority to the GPS connected public transport vehicles automatically, and the traffic is measured and analyzed on the roads. Drivers are also informed about the expected travel time and traffic by intelligent displays (EasyWay project).[^cite_note-223] Public transport users are immediately notified of any changes in public transport online, on [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphones) and on [PIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_information_system) displays. Users can keep track of changes in traffic and road management in real-time online and via [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone) through the *BKK Info,*[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] and with the *Futár* PIDS system,[^cite_note-226] while the continuous introducing of [integrated e-ticket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_ticketing) system is anticipated to help the measurement of passenger numbers on each line and the intelligent control of service frequency.

The development of *Futár*, the citywide [real-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_business_intelligence) [passenger information system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_information_system) and real-time [route planner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_route_planner) is finished already and now all of the public transport vehicle is connected via satellite system. The real-time information of trams, buses and trolleybuses are available for both the operators in the control room and for all the passengers in all stops on smartphone and on city street displays.[^cite_note-227] The implementation of latest generation [automated fare collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_fare_collection_system) and [e-ticket system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_ticket) with [NFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_field_communication) compatibility and reusable [contactless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contactless_payment) [smart cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_card) for making [electronic payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_money) in online and offline systems in Budapest was started in 2014, the project is implemented and operated by the operator of Hong Kong [Octopus card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopus_card) jointly with one of the leading European companies of e-ticket and automated fare collection, [Scheidt & Bachmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheidt_&_Bachmann_Ticket_XPress).[^cite_note-228] The deployment of 300 new digital contactless [ticket vending machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticket_vending_machine) will be finished by the end of 2014 in harmonization with the e-ticket system.[^cite_note-229] In 2022, *Futár* was rebranded as *BudapestGo.*[^cite_note-230]

Tram lines no. 4 and 6 are the busiest city tram lines in the world,[^cite_note-231] with one of the world's longest trams (54-metre long Siemens [Combino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combino)) running at 2–3-minute intervals at peak time and 4–5 minutes off-peak. Day services are usually from 4 am until between 11 pm and 0\:30 am.[^cite_note-A_good_place_to_live_–_Budapest-193] [Hungarian State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_State_Railways) operates an extensive network of [commuter rail](6286.md) services, their importance in the suburban commuter passenger traffic is significant, but in travel within the city is limited. The organiser of public transport in Budapest is the [municipal corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation) *Centre for Budapest Transport* (Budapesti Közlekedési Központ – BKK), that is responsible for planning and organising network and services, planning and developing tariff concepts, attending to [public service](1291782.md) procurer duties, managing public service contracts, operating controlling and monitoring systems, setting and monitoring service level agreements related to public transport, attending to customer service duties, selling and monitoring tickets and passes, attending to integrated passenger information duties, unified Budapest-centric traffic control within public transport, attending to duties related to river navigation, plus the management of Budapest roads, operating [taxi stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_station), unified control of [bicycle traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_traffic) development in the capital, preparing [parking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking) strategy and developing an operational concept, preparation of road traffic management, developing an optimal [traffic management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_management) system, organising and co-ordinating road reconstruction and more, in short, everything which is related to transport in the city.[^cite_note-232]

### Roads and railways

Main articles: [Hungarian State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_State_Railways) and [Motorways in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorways_in_Hungary)



[Keleti Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Keleti_railway_station) (Budapest East Central)

Budapest is the most important Hungarian road terminus, all of the major highways and railways end within the city limits. The road system in the city is designed in a similar manner to that of Paris, with several ring roads, and avenues radiating out from the centre. [Ring road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road) [M0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M0_motorway_\(Hungary\)) around Budapest is nearly completed, with only one section missing on the west side due to local disputes. The ring road is 80 kilometres (50 miles) in length, and once finished it will be 107 kilometres (66 mi) of highway in length.

The city is a vital traffic hub because all major European roads and European railway lines lead to Budapest.[^cite_note-budapest.com-107] The Danube was and is still today an important water-way and this region in the centre of the Carpathian Basin lies at the cross-roads of trade routes.[^cite_note-budpocketguide.com-110] Hungarian main line railways are operated by Hungarian State Railways. There are three main railway stations in Budapest, [Keleti (*Eastern*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Keleti_p%C3%A1lyaudvar), [Nyugati (*Western*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Nyugati_p%C3%A1lyaudvar) and [Déli (*Southern*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_D%C3%A9li_p%C3%A1lyaudvar), operating both domestic and international [rail services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_service). Budapest is one of the main stops of the Orient Express on its Central and Eastern European route.[^cite_note-233] There is also a [suburban rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban_trains_in_Budapest) service in and around Budapest, three lines of which are operated under the name HÉV.

### Ports, shipping and others

The river Danube flows through Budapest on its way from (Germany) to the [Black Sea](3386.md). The river is easily navigable and so Budapest historically has a major [commercial port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_port) at [Csepel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepel) District and at [New Pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ajpest) District also. The Pest side is also a famous port place with [international shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_shipping) ports for cargo[^cite_note-234] and for passenger ships.[^cite_note-235] In the summer months, a scheduled [hydrofoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofoil) service operates on the Danube connecting the city to Vienna.

BKK (through the operator [BKV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapesti_K%C3%B6zleked%C3%A9si_Zrt.)) also provides public transport with boat service within the borders of the city. Two routes, marked D11 and D12, connect the two banks with Margaret Island and Óbuda Island, from Rómaifürdő (Buda side, north to Óbuda Island) or Árpád Bridge (Pest side) to Rákóczi Bridge, with a total of 18 stops, while route D2 circulates in the downtown.[^cite_note-bkk-boats-236] Line D14 is a ferry service, connecting Királyerdő on the [Csepel Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepel_Island) with Molnár Island on the Pest side, south to the city centre.[^cite_note-bkk-boats-236] In addition, several companies provides sightseeing boat trips and also an amphibious vehicle (bus and boat) operates constantly.

Water quality in Budapest harbours improved dramatically in the recent years, treatment facilities processed 100% of generated sewage in 2010. Budapesters regularly [kayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayak), [canoe](99960.md), [jet-ski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet-ski) and [sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail) on the Danube, which has continuously become a major recreational site for the city.

Special vehicles in Budapest, besides metros, include suburban rails, trams and boats. There are a couple of less common vehicles in Budapest, like the trolleybus on several lines in [Pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_\(city\)), the [Castle Hill Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Castle_Hill_Funicular) between the [Chain Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Chain_Bridge) and Buda Castle, the [cyclecar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclecar) for rent in Margaret Island, the [chairlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairlift), the [Budapest Cog-wheel Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Cog-wheel_Railway) and [children's railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_railway). The latter three vehicles run among Buda hills.

## Culture and contemporary life

Main pages: [Category\:Culture in Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Culture_in_Budapest) and [Culture of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Hungary)

The culture of Budapest is reflected by Budapest's size and variety. Most Hungarian cultural movements first emerged in the city. Budapest is an important centre for music, film, theatre, dance and visual art. ### Performing arts and festivals

Further information: [Music of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Budapest)



[Sziget Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sziget_Festival) Budapest. One of the largest music festivals in Europe provides a multicultural, diverse meeting point for locals and foreigners every year.

Several annual festivals take place in Budapest. The [Sziget Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sziget_Festival) is one of the largest outdoor music festivals in Europe. The [Budapest Spring Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Spring_Festival) includes concerts at several venues across the city. The [Café Budapest Contemporary Arts Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_Budapest_Contemporary_Arts_Festival) (formerly the Budapest Autumn Festival) brings free music, dance, art, and other cultural events to the streets of the city. The Budapest Wine Festival and Budapest [Pálinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1linka) Festival, occurring each May, are [gastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomy) festivals focusing on culinary pleasures. The [Budapest Pride](18367052.md) (or Budapest Pride Film and Cultural Festival) occurs annually across the city, and usually involves a parade on the Andrássy Avenue. Other festivals include the [Budapest Fringe Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Fringe_Festival), which brings more than 500 artists in about 50 shows to produce a wide range of works in [alternative theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_theatre), dance, music and comedy outside the mainstream. The [LOW Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOW_Festival) is a contemporary cultural festival held in Hungary in the cities Budapest and Pécs from February until March; the name of the festival alludes to the [Low Countries](51402.md), the region encompassing the Netherlands and Flanders. The Budapest Jewish Summer Festival, in late August, is one of the largest in Europe.



Festive fireworks of the [foundation of the Hungarian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Hungary) on 20 August

There are many symphony orchestras in Budapest, with the [Budapest Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Philharmonic_Orchestra) being the preeminent one. It was founded in 1853 by [Ferenc Erkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Erkel) and still presents regular concerts in the Hungarian State Opera House and [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_\(Budapest\)). Budapest also has one of the more active jazz scenes in Central Europe.[^cite_note-242]

The dance tradition of the Carpathian Basin is a unique area of the European dance culture, which is also a special transition between the Balkans and Western Europe regions. The city is home to several authentic [Hungarian folk dance ensembles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Budapest) which range from small ensembles to professional troupes. Budapest is one of the few cities in the world with a high school for learning folk dance.

### Cuisine

Further information: [Hungarian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine)

In the modern age, Budapest developed its own peculiar cuisine, based on products of the nearby region, such as lamb, pork and vegetables special to the region. Modern Hungarian cuisine is a synthesis of ancient Asiatic components mixed with French, Germanic, Italian, and Slavic elements. The food of Hungary can be considered a melting pot of the continent, with a culinary base formed from its own, original [Magyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyar_tribes) cuisine. Considerable numbers of [Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxons), Armenians, Italians, Jews and Serbs settled in the Hungarian basin and in Transylvania, also contributing with different new dishes. Elements of ancient Turkish cuisine were adopted during the Ottoman era, in the form of sweets (for example different [nougats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nougat), like white nougat called *törökméz*), [quince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quince) (*birsalma*), [Turkish delight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_delight), [Turkish coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_coffee) or rice dishes like [pilaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilaf), meat and vegetable dishes like the [eggplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eggplant), used in [eggplant salads and appetizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eggplant_salads_and_appetizers), stuffed peppers and stuffed cabbage called *[töltött káposzta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B6lt%C3%B6tt_k%C3%A1poszta)*. Hungarian cuisine was influenced by [Austrian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_cuisine) under the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire), dishes and methods of food preparation have often been borrowed from Austrian cuisine, and vice versa.[^cite_note-247]

Budapest restaurants reflect diversity, with menus carrying traditional regional cuisine, fusions of various culinary influences, or innovating in the leading edge of new techniques. Budapest's food shops also have a solid reputation for supplying quality specialised culinary products and supplies, reputations that are often built up over generations. These include many shop and served in several [Michelin-starred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Michelin_starred_restaurants#By_country) restaurants.

Budapest hosted many global [sporting events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport) in the past, among others the [1994 IAAF World Cross Country Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_IAAF_World_Cross_Country_Championships), [1997 World Amateur Boxing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_World_Amateur_Boxing_Championships), [2000 World Fencing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_World_Fencing_Championships), [2001 World Allround Speed Skating Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_World_Allround_Speed_Skating_Championships), [Bandy World Championship 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy_World_Championship_2004), [2008 World Interuniversity Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_World_Interuniversity_Games), [2008 World Modern Pentathlon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_World_Modern_Pentathlon_Championships), [2010 ITU World Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_ITU_World_Championship_Series), 2011 [IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_Championship), [2012 European Speed Skating Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_European_Speed_Skating_Championships), [2013 World Fencing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Fencing_Championships), [2013 World Wrestling Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Wrestling_Championships), 2014 [World Masters Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Masters_Athletics_Championships), [2017 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Aquatics_Championships), and [2017 World Judo Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Judo_Championships), only in the last two-decade. Besides these, Budapest was the home of many European-level tournaments, like [2006 European Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_European_Aquatics_Championships), [2010 European Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_European_Aquatics_Championships), [2010 UEFA Futsal Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_UEFA_Futsal_Championship), [2013 European Judo Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_European_Judo_Championships), [2013 European Karate Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_European_Karate_Championships) and was the host of [2023 World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Championships_in_Athletics) and 4 matches in the [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), which was held in the 67,215-seat new [multi-purpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_stadium) [Puskás Ferenc Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Pusk%C3%A1s_Ferenc_Stadium), to mention a few.

In 2015, the Assembly of the [Hungarian Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Olympic_Committee) and the [Assembly of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_of_Budapest) decided to bid for the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics). Budapest has lost several bids to host the games, in 1916, 1920, 1936, 1944, and 1960 to [Berlin](3354.md), Antwerp, London, and Rome, respectively.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249] The [Hungarian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament) also voted to support the bid on 28 January 2016, later Budapest City Council approved list of venues and [Budapest became an official candidate for the 2024 Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_bid_for_the_2024_Summer_Olympics). However, they withdrew their bid later on.

Numerous Olympic, World, and European Championship winners and medalists reside in the city, which follows from Hungary's 8th place among all the nations of the world in the [All-time Olympic Games medal table](3729318.md).



The [Danube Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Arena)



The [National Athletics Centre](59274354.md)

Hungarians have always been avid sports people: during the history of the [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md), Hungarians have brought home 476 medals, of which 167 are gold. The top events in which Hungarians have excelled are fencing, swimming, water polo, canoeing, wrestling and track & field sports. Beside classic sports, recreational modern sports such as bowling, pool billiard, darts, go-carting, wakeboarding and squash are very popular in Budapest, and extreme sports are also gaining ground. Furthermore, the [Budapest Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Marathon) and [Budapest Half Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Half_Marathon) also attract many people every year. The city's largest football stadium is named after [Ferenc Puskás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Pusk%C3%A1s), recognised as the top scorer of the 20th century and for whom [FIFA Puskás Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Pusk%C3%A1s_Award) was named.[^cite_note-250]

One of Budapest's most popular sport is football and it has many [Hungarian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_football_league_system) football club, including in the top level [Nemzeti Bajnokság I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemzeti_Bajnoks%C3%A1g_I) league, like [Ferencvárosi TC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferencv%C3%A1rosi_TC) (32 Hungarian League titles), [MTK Budapest FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTK_Budapest_FC) (23 titles), [Újpest FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ajpest_FC) (20 titles), [Budapest Honvéd FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Honv%C3%A9d_FC) (14 titles), [Vasas SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasas_SC) (6 titles), [Csepel SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepel_SC) (4 titles), [Budapesti TC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapesti_TC) (2 titles).

The [Hungarian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Grand_Prix) in [Formula One](10854.md) has been held at the [Hungaroring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungaroring) just outside the city, a circuit which has [FIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA) Grade 1 license.[^cite_note-251] Since 1986, the race has been a round of the FIA Formula One World Championship. At the [2013 Hungarian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Hungarian_Grand_Prix), it was confirmed that Hungary will continue to host a Formula 1 race until 2021.[^cite_note-252] The track was completely resurfaced for the first time in early 2016, and it was announced the Grand Prix's deal was extended for a further five years, until 2026.[^cite_note-253]

Budapest is home to two four-star UEFA stadiums: [Puskás Aréna](27530864.md), [Groupama Aréna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupama_Ar%C3%A9na), and two three-star UEFA stadiums: [Hidegkuti Nándor Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidegkuti_N%C3%A1ndor_Stadion) and [Bozsik Aréna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozsik_Ar%C3%A9na) (currently under renovations to upgrade to UEFA's four-star category).[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257]

Budapest will be the home of the headquarters of the [World Aquatics](317029.md) after moving from [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne) by the [2027 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_World_Aquatics_Championships).[^cite_note-258]

## Education

See also: [Education in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Hungary)

Budapest is home to over 35 higher education institutions, many of which are universities. Under the [Bologna Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Process), many offered qualifications are recognised in countries across Europe. Medicine, dentistry, pharmaceuticals, veterinary programs, and engineering are among the most popular fields for foreigners to undertake in Budapest. Most universities in Budapest offer courses in English, as well as in other languages like German, French, and Dutch, aimed specifically at foreigners. Many students from other European countries spend one or two semesters in Budapest through the [Erasmus Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Programme).[^cite_note-259]

## International relations

Budapest has quite a few [sister cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_towns_and_sister_cities) and many partner cities around the world.[^cite_note-Budapest_twinnings-260] Like Budapest, many of them are the most influential and largest cities of their country and region, most of them are the primate city and political, economical, cultural capital of their country. The Mayor of Budapest says the aim of improving sister city relationships is to allow and encourage a mutual exchange of information and experiences, as well as co-operation, in the areas of city management, education, culture, tourism, media and communication, trade and business development.[^cite_note-261]&#x20;
