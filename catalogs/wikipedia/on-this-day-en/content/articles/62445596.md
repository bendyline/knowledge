**Bayraktar TB2** ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *[Standard-bearer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-bearer) TB2*)[^cite_note-tb2ifri2023-3] is a family of [medium-altitude long-endurance](9309406.md) (MALE) [unmanned combat aerial vehicles](337755.md) (UCAV) capable of [remotely controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remotely_controlled) or [autonomous flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_flight) operations. It is manufactured by the Turkish company [Baykar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar) for the [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md) and export markets.[^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB2_official-4][^cite_note-tb2ifri2023-3][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] The aircraft are monitored and controlled by an aircrew in a ground control station, including weapons employment. The development of the UAV has been largely credited to [Selçuk Bayraktar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sel%C3%A7uk_Bayraktar), a former [MIT](18879.md) graduate student along with his brother [Haluk Bayraktar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haluk_Bayraktar).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-Haluk_Bayraktar:_We_Concentrate_on_Being_the_Best_in_Technology_Over_Profits-8] It is the first UCAV in the world to successfully perform a [barrel roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_roll) and an autonomous spin recovery maneuver.[^cite_note-BAYRAKTAR_TB2_BREAKS_NEW_GROUND_ONCE_AGAIN-9][^cite_note-Türkiye's_Bayraktar_TB2_makes_aviation_history_with_spin_exit-10] It is the most produced and export-successful UCAV in the world.[^cite_note-Turkey_presents_version_of_Bayraktar_TB2_drone_with_artificial_intelligence-1][^cite_note-Haluk_Bayraktar:_We_Concentrate_on_Being_the_Best_in_Technology_Over_Profits-8] Baykar has a production capacity of 250 Bayraktar TB2s per year and is aiming to increase the production capacity to 500 per year.[^cite_note-Haluk_Bayraktar:_We_Concentrate_on_Being_the_Best_in_Technology_Over_Profits-8][^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB2_MILITARY_UAS_-_FIXED-WING-11] Baykar will start a production line for the Bayraktar TB2 along with the [Bayraktar Akıncı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar_Bayraktar_Ak%C4%B1nc%C4%B1) at its Italy-based subsidiary, [Piaggio Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Aerospace). New production lines for the Bayraktar TB2 are going to start by the equal [joint venture](538782.md) company established by Baykar and [Leonardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_\(company\)) known as LBA Systems, along with the [Bayraktar TB3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar_Bayraktar_TB3), [Bayraktar Akıncı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar_Bayraktar_Ak%C4%B1nc%C4%B1) and [Bayraktar Kızılelma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_K%C4%B1z%C4%B1lelma).[^cite_note-Leonardo_CEO_Details_Joint_Production_for_Baykar’s_UAVs-12]

By December 2024, the TB2 drone had completed 1 million flight hours globally.[^cite_note-Türkiye's_First_Indigenous_UCAV_Bayraktar_TB2_Exceeds_1_Million_Flight_Hours-13][^cite_note-Baykar_400k_hours-14][^cite_note-tb2-400bin-15] The largest operator of TB2 drones is the Turkish military, and the drone was exported to the militaries of at least 34 other countries.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Turkey has used the drone extensively in strikes on [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK) and [People's Protection Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Protection_Units) (YPG) targets in Iraq and Syria.[^cite_note-Battle_for_Idlib:_Turkey's_drones_and_a_new_way_of_war-18][^cite_note-TURKEY’S_DRONE_WAR_IN_SYRIA_–_A_RED_TEAM_VIEW-19][^cite_note-GuardianRevealed-20] Bayraktar drones were later deployed by a number of other nations around the world in various wars, such as by [Azerbaijan](746.md) in the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War), by the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md) during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine),[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-\:0-22] as well as by the [Ethiopian National Defense Force](9405.md) during the [Tigray war](65760352.md).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

Bayraktar TB2 played a fundamental role in Turkey's development of a new military tactic and initiated a wave of change in modern military doctrines. It was accepted as the initiator of a new method of war by many military analysts and strategists,[^cite_note-WarOnTheRock2024-26][^cite_note-turkeysdroneblitz-27][^cite_note-The_Institute_for_the_Study_of_War2020-28][^cite_note-middleaestins5-29][^cite_note-The_Turkey_Analyst2020-30] including the political scientist [Francis Fukuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Fukuyama).[^cite_note-Fukuyama_2021-31]

## Development



Bayraktar TB2 technical specifications in Turkish

The development of the Bayraktar TB2 was spurred by a U.S. ban on exports of armed unmanned aircraft to Turkey due to concerns that they would be used against [PKK](69680.md) groups inside and outside Turkey.[^cite_note-GuardianRevealed-20]

[Baykar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar) started developing a new combat tactical aerial vehicle system at the request of the [Presidency of Defense Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Defense_Industries), after the experiences of its first tactical UAV, the Bayraktar Çaldıran or [Bayraktar TB1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_TB1), delivered to the Turkish army in 2011.[^cite_note-32] The Bayraktar TB2 made its maiden flight in August 2014.[^cite_note-33] On 18 December 2015, a video was published of a test firing of a missile from the Bayraktar TB2, a result of a collaboration with [Roketsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roketsan). Roketsan's [MAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAM_\(Smart_Micro_Munition\)) and [TUBITAK-SAGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUBITAK-SAGE)'s BOZOK [laser-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser-guided_bomb) were tested for the first time.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-The_Institute_for_the_Study_of_War2020-28]

### Components and technologies

The aircraft previously relied on imported and regulated components and technologies such as [Rotax 912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotax_912) engines[^cite_note-OVD_2020-36] (manufactured in Austria) and optoelectronics ([FLIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward-looking_infrared) sensors imported from [Wescam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wescam) in Canada or [Hensoldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hensoldt) from Germany). [Bombardier Recreational Products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Recreational_Products), owner of [Rotax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotax), suspended delivery of their engines to certain countries in October 2020, after becoming aware of their military use despite being certified for civil use only.[^cite_note-OVD_2020-36][^cite_note-37]

According to the British newspaper *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, the arming of the Bayraktar TB2 would not have been possible without the help of the UK Hornet micro-munitions [bomb rack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_rack) by [EDO MBM Technology Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDO_MBM_Technology_Ltd). The bomb rack was provided to Turkey in 2015, and a variant of it was integrated into the aircraft by EDO MBM and Roketsan.[^cite_note-38] In response to *The Guardian* newspaper article, Baykar Chief Technical Officer Selçuk Bayraktar denied that the bomb rack came from the UK. "We are not buying it from you, we never did. It not only does not work under any circumstances but is also very expensive", Bayraktar said on Twitter. "We have designed and manufactured a more advanced and cost-effective one ourselves."[^cite_note-39]

On 19 August 2020 the UK [Department for International Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_International_Trade) (DIT) disclosed details of a six-year history of exports of the Hornet bomb rack to Turkey between 2014 and 2020, suggesting that supply of the critical technology to Turkey had continued well beyond the development stage of the Bayraktar TB2 and right up to the publication of the *Guardian* story in November 2019. 18 Standard Individual Export Licence (SIEL) applications were submitted by EDO MBM Technology between 2014 and 2020 for exports of goods 'related to Hornet Bomb Racks / Hornet Missile Launchers' for end-users in Turkey. Of these, 16 of the licences were granted.[^cite_note-40]

In October 2020, the drone's use of the Canadian [Wescam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wescam) MX-15D system was disclosed after Armenian officials claimed that remains of a MX-15D system had been recovered from a downed TB2 drone during the nation's conflict with Azerbaijan. That triggered the stopping of MX-15D exports to Turkey while an investigation by [Global Affairs Canada](256258.md) evaluates the use of Canadian technology in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-\:1-42][^cite_note-43] Turkish industry responded to foreign sales boycotts by announcing provision of domestically manufactured alternatives, including the **TM100** (manufactured by Bayker)[^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB2_Flies_with_the_Indigenous_TM100_Engine-44] and [TEI-PD170](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TEI-PD170) (manufactured by [TEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusa%C5%9F_Engine_Industries)) engines,[^cite_note-defenceturk-1-45][^cite_note-46] fuel valves, and the [Aselsan CATS](https://www.aselsan.com/en/defence/product/2633/cats) FLIR system (manufactured by [Aselsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aselsan)).[^cite_note-47] Turkish defense industry researcher Kadir Doğan tweeted that cancellation of sales of components to Baykar by foreign companies did not pose a major problem, and that, as of January 2021, all of those components have been replaced by locally manufactured alternatives.[^cite_note-48]

While the initial alternative Aselsan CATS electro-optical turret faced problems in export market regarding its weight (61 kg) compared to the Wescam MX-15D (45 kg),[^cite_note-\:3-49][^cite_note-GreyDynamics_TB2-50] Aselsan developed a successor system, the [Aselsan ASELFLIR-500](https://www.aselsan.com/en/defence/product/2768/aselflir500), to address these performance parameters. The ASELFLIR-500 offers improved performance with a reduced weight of approximately 52 kg.[^cite_note-Aselsan_Brochure-51] The system has since been integrated into the Bayraktar TB2 and other platforms.[^cite_note-TurDef_Export-52][^cite_note-Baykar_TB3-53] The system features a 4096x2880 high-definition camera, alongside high-definition MWIR and SWIR sensors for long-range detection.[^cite_note-TurDef_Specs-54][^cite_note-DefenseMirror_Akinci-55] Following its development, ASELSAN exported ASELFLIR 500 system to more than 20 countries including NATO members.[^cite_note-TurDef_Export-52][^cite_note-Aselsan_ADEX-56]

### Design



Bayraktar TB2 first trial flights

The Bayraktar TB2 platform has a [blended wing body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blended_wing_body) design with an [inverted V-tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-tail) structure. Thrust is generated by a [variable pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable-pitch_propeller_\(aeronautics\)) two-blade propeller in [pusher configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pusher_configuration). The propeller is mounted between the tail booms and driven by an internal combustion engine located in the body. The [monocoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocoque) platform is modular with detachable main items such as wing, tail boom, and V-tails. Fuselage pieces are made mostly of [carbon fiber composite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-fiber-reinforced_polymers) with machined aluminum parts at joints. Fuel is stored within [bladder tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder_tank) and fuel consumption is balanced with [solenoid valves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenoid_valve).

The ground control station (GCS) is based on a [NATO](21133.md)-spec shelter unit, which is equipped with cross-redundant command and control systems. The mobile unit supports three personnel: pilot, payload operator, and mission commander. The GCS is equipped with redundant air conditioners and nuclear, biological, and chemical filtration (NBC) filtering units. All hardware inside the shelter is placed inside racked cabinets. Each operator has dual screens in front, along with the operator interface software used for real-time command, control, and monitoring.[^cite_note-57]

While the Turkish Armed Forces describe Bayraktar TB2 as "Tactical UAV Class" to prevent it from being a competitor to the [TAI Anka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka) UAV, international standards would classify it as a [medium-altitude long-endurance UAV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-altitude_long-endurance_unmanned_aerial_vehicle).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-hurriyet-gear-59]

### Configuration



Bayraktar TB2 Ground Control Station System



Bayraktar ground control station on a mobile platform

Each TB2 is configured with six aerial vehicle platforms, two ground control stations, three ground data terminals (GDT), two remote video terminals (RVT), and ground support equipment. Each aerial platform is equipped with a triply-redundant avionics system. Its ground control system's cross-redundant architecture allows for pilot, payload operator, and mission commander to command, control, and monitor the platform.[^cite_note-hurriyetdailynews-gear-60]

### Digital flight control system

The TB2 has a [triple-redundant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_modular_redundancy) flight control system with autonomous taxi, takeoff, cruise, landing, and parking capability. The computerized [flight control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_flight_control_system) is the primary component, programmed with [sensor fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_fusion) algorithms that act on real-time sensor data. Mission-specific controls are handled through the mission-control computer system. The aerial platform is guided using various redundant rotary and linear [servo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servo_\(radio_control\)) actuators. All of the main airborne avionics equipment, software, and hardware are under constant development.[^cite_note-taktik-iha-61]

The electronic [power supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_supply) for the onboard systems is supported with triple [alternators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternator) and balanced, smart [lithium-ion battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) units. A ruggedized heated camera unit is placed in the tail section of the platform to monitor flight, and all payload and [telemetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemetry) data are saved to the [airborne data recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_recorder). The redundant architecture of the avionics supports [autonomous emergency landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoland) on different airfields if necessary. [Sensor fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_fusion) algorithms, including an [inertial navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system), allow navigation and auto landing even with loss of [global positioning signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_navigation_device).[^cite_note-taktik-iha-61]

### Price

Baykar has not stated a price, but has republished on its website several news reports about crowdfunding campaigns launched in 2022 in Europe to buy Bayraktar UAVs for Ukraine, all of them fixing a goal of around US\$5–5.5 million for the unit.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

That is around one-sixth of the price for the US-built [Reaper](4769988.md) UAV, albeit with the TB2 capable of carrying only one-tenth the payload of the Reaper.[^cite_note-64]

## Operational history

### Turkey



Bayraktar TB2 loaded with [MAM-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAM_\(Smart_Micro_Munition\))

The Turkish military's use of the TB2 gained prominence in counterinsurgency operations against sites of the [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK) and [People's Protection Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Protection_Units) (YPG) that were in Iraq and Syria.[^cite_note-Battle_for_Idlib:_Turkey's_drones_and_a_new_way_of_war-18][^cite_note-TURKEY’S_DRONE_WAR_IN_SYRIA_–_A_RED_TEAM_VIEW-19]

On 30 June 2018, one Turkish Air Force Bayraktar TB2 crashed due to technical problems in [Hatay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatay) province, Turkey.[^cite_note-65]

On 15 August 2018, [Turkish Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Land_Forces) successfully used a Bayraktar TB2 in a [joint cross-border operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_%C4%B0smail_%C3%96zden), of the [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md) and the [National Intelligence Organization of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Organization_\(Turkey\)), to kill the senior (PKK) leader and board member of the [Kurdistan Communities Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Communities_Union) [İsmail Özden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_%C4%B0smail_%C3%96zden#%C4%B0smail_%C3%96zden) in [Sinjar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjar_District), northwestern [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-66]

On 16 May 2021, one Bayraktar TB2 crashed in Zebari, northern Iraq; Kurdish militants claimed to have shot down the drone.[^cite_note-67]

A total of 37 Turkish Bayraktar TB2s successfully flew over 1551 hours in the earthquake zone of the [2023 Turkey-Syria earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Turkey-Syria_earthquake). The drones have been instrumental in providing continuous updates and data to the crisis-response team for damage detection and search-and-rescue support, along with coordination activities.[^cite_note-68]

On 20 June 2023, a Turkish Bayraktar TB2 drone crashed in Qandil, northern Iraq. The wreck fell in an area under Kurdish militia control.[^cite_note-69]

#### Turkish use in Syria

See also: [Turkish involvement in the Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_involvement_in_the_Syrian_Civil_War) and [List of aviation shootdowns and accidents during the Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aviation_shootdowns_and_accidents_during_the_Syrian_Civil_War)

In March 2020 Bayraktar TB2s, [Anka-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka) UAVs, and an array of [Koral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KORAL_Electronic_Warfare_System) [electronic jammers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_jamming_and_deception) were deployed and extensively used in coordinated action to strike Syrian army targets on the ground during [Operation Spring Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Syria_offensive_\(December_2019%E2%80%93present\)), launched by Turkey following [losses the Turkish forces incurred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Balyun_airstrikes) at the hands of the [Russian forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_military_intervention_in_the_Syrian_Civil_War) in northwestern Syria at the end of February 2020.[^cite_note-bloomberswarm-70][^cite_note-Karnozov_2020-71] In this operation, in which Turkish forces used TB-2s in large numbers, they virtually "blinded" enemy radars and neutralized Syrian air defense systems such as [Pantsir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantsir) and [Buk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buk_missile_system).[^cite_note-The_Turkey_Analyst2020-30][^cite_note-turkeysdroneblitz-27][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] As a result, the attacks of the Assad regime forces, supported by Russia and Iran, on Idlib were forced to stop.[^cite_note-The_New_Arab2023-74][^cite_note-The_Institute_for_the_Study_of_War2020-28] The deployment was assessed by experts to be a success and a tactical game-changer.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-Gatopoulos_2020-76][^cite_note-Petkova_2020-77]

### Libya

In June 2019, international news media reported that Libya's UN-recognized[^cite_note-87] [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) (GNA) used Bayraktar TB2s to strike an airbase held by General Haftar's [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) (LNA). Despite the UN embargo on [Libya's ongoing civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War), it is suspected that at least 3 Bayraktar TB2s were being used over Tripoli by the government forces. Video evidence, as early as 11 June 2019, showed at least one TB2 flying over Tripoli[^cite_note-88] about to land at [Mitiga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitiga_International_Airport)'s military section, under control of GNA-allied forces. Turkish supplied TB-2s were successful against [Pantsir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantsir) air defense systems in Libya and destroyed 9 units.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-randcor2020-90]

### Azerbaijan



Bayraktar TB2 on display at the 2020 [Victory Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Victory_Parade_of_2020) in [Baku](4566.md), Azerbaijan

In June 2020, the Defence Minister of Azerbaijan, [Zakir Hasanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakir_Hasanov), announced that Azerbaijan had decided to purchase Bayraktar drones from Turkey.[^cite_note-124] During the [2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_war), Bayraktar TB2s were used against the [Armed Forces of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Armenia) with great success.[^cite_note-125] Azerbaijan used TB2s to destroy Armenian artillery, infantry positions, and military vehicles, including [BM-30 Smerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-30_Smerch) [MLRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_rocket_launcher), [T-72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72) [tanks](26595097.md), and [BMP-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-1) and [BMP-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-2) [IFVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle).[^cite_note-Everything_We_Know_About_The_Fighting_That_Has_Erupted_Between_Armenia_And_Azerbaijan-126][^cite_note-127] Nine [Osa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K33_Osa) and [Strela-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K35_Strela-10) air defense systems were also destroyed by Azerbaijani drones, likely TB2s.[^cite_note-Everything_We_Know_About_The_Fighting_That_Has_Erupted_Between_Armenia_And_Azerbaijan-126]

### Ukraine



A Bayraktar TB2 of the [Ukrainian Air Force](1135769.md) armed with [MAM-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAM_\(Smart_Micro_Munition\)); two ground control stations are in the background

As part of its military modernization program, the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md) purchased 12 Bayraktar TB2s in 2019.[^cite_note-131] In January 2019, Baykar signed an agreement with Ukrspetsproject, part of [Ukroboronprom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukroboronprom), for the purchase of six TB2s and 3 ground control stations worth US\$69 million for the Ukrainian army.[^cite_note-132] Ukraine received the first batch of the UAVs in March 2019.[^cite_note-auto-133] After successful testing of the aircraft, the [Ukrainian Navy](1590554.md) placed a separate order for six TB2s, to be delivered in 2021, according to navy officials.[^cite_note-134] Meanwhile, Turkish and Ukrainian officials announced the establishment of a joint venture to produce 48 additional Bayraktar TB2s in [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-135] The first batch of the Bayraktar TB2 complex was delivered to the navy in July 2021.[^cite_note-Bay1-136]

During a [Russian military buildup](42085878.md#2021%E2%80%932022_Russian_military_buildup) in Crimea and near Ukraine's borders, a TB2 conducted a reconnaissance flight over the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) region on 9 April 2021. This was the first operational use of the aircraft by Ukrainian forces within an [active conflict zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] In October 2021, a TB2 drone was used for the first time in combat during the war, targeting a [Russian separatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas) artillery position, destroying a [D-30 howitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/122_mm_howitzer_2A18_\(D-30\)), and halting the bombardment of Ukrainian troops near [Hranitne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hranitne,_Volnovakha_Raion).[^cite_note-139]

#### 2022 Russian invasion

During the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), TB2 drones were used by Ukraine's armed forces against Russian forces and equipment.[^cite_note-Ukraine_posts_videos_Business_Insider_1-140] In January, prior to the invasion, the spokesperson for the air force command, Lt. Col. Yuri Ihnat, stated that "Ukraine has approximately 20 Bayraktar drones, but we will not stop there".[^cite_note-AlM-141] On 2 March, Ukrainian defense minister [Oleksii Reznikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksii_Reznikov) announced the arrival of additional TB2 drones.[^cite_note-142]

According to video footage released by the armed forces, TB2 drones have successfully destroyed, on different occasions, a Russian command post, military vehicles—including [tanks](26595097.md), [IFVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle), and different types of trucks—[surface-to-air missile](182664.md) systems (including [Buk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buk_missile_system) and [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_missile_system) systems), [self-propelled artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-propelled_artillery), [multiple rocket launcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_rocket_launcher) (MLRS), [howitzers](14392.md), and an [electronic warfare system](82272.md).[^cite_note-Ukraine_posts_videos_Business_Insider_1-140][^cite_note-Ukraine_credits_Turkish_drones_with_eviscerating_Russian_tanks_and_armor_in_their_first_use_in_a_major_conflict-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-In_three_days,_two_Bayraktar_drones_destroy_nearly_\$27_million_of_Russian_hardware-145][^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB2_Videos_Show_New_Wave_Of_Ukrainian_Attacks_Against_Russian_Forces_On_Snake_Island-146] The drone also reportedly destroyed two Russian fuel trains, [patrol boats](536773.md), and a [helicopter](8286923.md).[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-drive2may22-148][^cite_note-Ukrainian_TB2_struck_Russian_Mi-8_just_as_troops_are_disembarking-149]

On 24 February, the day of invasion, four Bayraktar TB2 drones stationed at [Chuhuiv Air base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuhuiv_air_base_attack) were abandoned and later destroyed on the ground by Ukrainian Forces, amid reported Russian rocket attacks.[^cite_note-150] The [People's Militia of the Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas) claimed it shot down two TB2 drones near the city of Luhansk.[^cite_note-151] On 27 February, Ukraine's air force confirmed two strikes by TB2s on Russian convoys in the [Kherson](1025861.md) and [Zhytomyr](1025103.md) regions.[^cite_note-Ukraine_credits_Turkish_drones_with_eviscerating_Russian_tanks_and_armor_in_their_first_use_in_a_major_conflict-143]

The chief of Ukraine's air force, Lieutenant General [Mykola Oleschuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykola_Oleschuk), called the UAV system "life-giving".[^cite_note-152] The popularity of the drone in Ukraine led to a song, "[Bayraktar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_\(song\))", which praised the drone while also insulting the Russian army and its invasion.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

Ukrainian drones appear to be equipped with anti-jamming antennas. They appear to use [MAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Ak%C4%B1ll%C4%B1_M%C3%BChimmat)-C and MAM-L laser-guided bombs. Traditional search radars appear to struggle against the TB2 because of its slow speed and size, which give it a low radar cross section. One radar that was destroyed on 7 May appeared to be actively scanning just prior to supposedly being hit, although Russian units reportedly turn off their own radars to reduce the radar being detected and targeted. The drone can also be deployed within minutes and launched from a normal road.[^cite_note-156]

On 17 March 2022, a Bayraktar TB2 was shot down over Kyiv; Russia published images of the drone wreckage.[^cite_note-157] A second TB2 drone was shot down on 29 March 2022, in eastern Ukraine.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] On 2 April a third TB-2 drone was shot down.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-forbesorion-161]

On 12 April 2022, one Bayraktar TB2 was claimed to have been shot down by [Russian frigate *Admiral Essen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_frigate_Admiral_Essen) off the coast of [Crimea](163045.md).[^cite_note-162]

On 13 April 2022, Ukrainian sources claimed at least two [R-360 Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-360_Neptune) missiles were responsible for the sinking of the [Russian cruiser *Moskva*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Moskva) – causing an explosion on one of the ship's exposed deckside missile tubes. Drones, likely TB2s, were alleged to have helped decoy the cruiser's defences.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164]

On 26 and 27 April, three additional TB2 drones were destroyed, two in Kursk Oblast[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] and the third in [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md), Russia, by a [Pantsir-S1](8645085.md).[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

On 1 May, a TB2 drone with registration *S51T* was shot down in Kursk region, Russia.[^cite_note-169] TB2 losses amounted to seven units confirmed visually.[^cite_note-drive2may22-148]

On 2 May, Bayraktar drones operated by Ukraine attacked and destroyed two Russian [Raptor-class patrol boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raptor-class_patrol_boat) near [Snake Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_Island_\(Ukraine\)).[^cite_note-drive2may22-148]

On 7 May, a TB2 drone attacked and destroyed a Russian [Mil Mi-8](957233.md) transport helicopter as it was unloading passengers on Snake Island.[^cite_note-Ukrainian_TB2_struck_Russian_Mi-8_just_as_troops_are_disembarking-149] In the same deployment, a TB2 drone destroyed a [Tor missile launcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_missile_system) on the island, while a second launcher was destroyed as it was being unloaded from a landing craft. This cleared the way for a bombing run by a Ukrainian Air Force [Sukhoi Su-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-27) 'Flanker' aircraft against buildings on the island.[^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB2_Videos_Show_New_Wave_Of_Ukrainian_Attacks_Against_Russian_Forces_On_Snake_Island-146]

On 23 May 2022, the remains of a Ukrainian Bayraktar TB2, tail number *75*, were found in Romanian territorial waters by Romanian authorities. The drone was likely shot down during the Snake Island attacks of 7 May.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

On 28 May 2022 Lithuanian citizens fundraised \$3.2 million, out of the \$5.37 million unit cost, to buy a single Bayraktar TB2 drone for Ukraine.[^cite_note-172] On 2 June, Baykar said: "The people of Lithuania have honorably raised funds to buy a Bayraktar TB2 for Ukraine. Upon learning this, Baykar will give a Bayraktar TB2 to Lithuania free of charge and asks those funds go to Ukraine for humanitarian aid."[^cite_note-173]

From the beginning of the invasion to late June, Ukraine received over 50 TB2s. On 28 June, Defence Minister Reznikov announced that Baykar would be devoting all of its capacities to meet the needs of the Ukrainian armed forces, fulfilling its requirement for "dozens more" of the aircraft.[^cite_note-174]

In July 2022, Haluk Bayraktar, [CEO](52234.md) of Baykar, stated in an interview that his company would never supply Russia with its drones as he supports Ukraine's sovereignty and independence.[^cite_note-175]

#### Further use and vulnerability

Although at the start of the invasion Ukrainian drones, including the TB2, were able to be used to harass Russian forces, by summer 2022 they had become less effective in this role. The disorganized Russians were initially slow to set up proper air defenses; but once they did, Ukrainian UAVs were downed with increasing frequency. In addition to being shot down, electronic warfare is used to jam and disrupt communication links. This may have caused the Ukrainians to scale back TB2 use.[^cite_note-176] On 25 July, a military expert told the BBC that the slow speed and medium altitudes of Bayraktar drones made them easy targets for Russian air defenses, and that many were shot down.[^cite_note-177]

In late July 2022, a TB2 with tail number *U139* was reported shot down in Belgorod Oblast, Russia.[^cite_note-178] On 2 August another drone, with call sign *409*, was shot down in Ukraine.[^cite_note-179] On 2 September, the remains of a Ukrainian TB2 drone were discovered in Kherson.[^cite_note-180]

By July 2022, Bayraktar TB2 losses reached 14 units confirmed visually by that time.[^cite_note-181]

On 15 January 2023 a Bayraktar TB2 was downed by Russian Electronic Warfare systems Downed using EW systems.[^cite_note-182]

On 22 January 2023, the remains of a destroyed Bayraktar TB2 drone were found in Odesa.[^cite_note-183]

The commander of the Russian Air Defense Force, Lieutenant General Andrey Demin, claimed in April 2023 that Russia had shot down "more than 100" Bayraktar drones since the war began.[^cite_note-184]

On 23 February 2023, a Bayraktar TB2 crashed in Kharkiv oblast due to friendly fire.[^cite_note-185]

On 5 May 2023, the Ukrainian air force said it downed one of its own TB2 drones over Kyiv, after they lost control of it due to a "likely" technical malfunction. No casualties were reported from the incident.[^cite_note-186]

On 12 May, another Bayraktar TB2 drone was shot down by Russian forces near Marinka.[^cite_note-187]

By June 2023, the role of the remaining TB2 drones had changed from attack to reconnaissance, staying out of the range of Russian air defenses and using their more advanced optics and sensors to guide other drones.[^cite_note-188]

On 17 July 2023, another TB2 drone, T 263 was shot down by Russian forces in Kherson Oblast.[^cite_note-189]

On 3 September 2023, Ukraine released video of a KS-701 patrol boat being destroyed by a TB2 drone. This was the first time the drone had been confirmed as being used offensively in several months. Recently, it had been used for reconnaissance, such as correcting artillery fire, due to Russian air defences.[^cite_note-190] It is believed that the degrading of Russian air defenses due to Ukrainian attacks have allowed TB2 drones to be used in air strikes again.[^cite_note-191] However, Colonel Volodymyr Valiukh, a commander in Ukrainian Main Intelligence Directorate, reported in late October 2023 that TB2 drones are now rarely used and only for short-time reconnaissance missions because of more sophisticated Russian air defense and electronic warfare systems. He specifically said: "For the TB2, I don't want to use the word useless, but it is hard to find situations where to use them".[^cite_note-192]

On 3 September 2025, a Russian high speed boat is destroyed by Ukrainian TB2, killing 7 and injuring 4 on board.[^cite_note-193] Ukraine systematically destroying Russian air defences in Crimea allowed TB2 drones fly again over the Black Sea and Ukraine released footage of a TB2 destroying Russian USVs in June 2026. [^cite_note-194]

According to the [Oryx Blog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oryx_\(website\)), as of 25 February 2025, 26 Bayraktar TB2s were destroyed, either on the ground or having been shot down by Russian forces.[^cite_note-195]

### Ethiopia

Ethiopian forces have reportedly been using the TB2 against the [TPLF](1276759.md) in the [Tigray War](65760352.md). Satellite images have shown TB2 drones in [Harar Meda airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar_Meda_Airport), and debris of [MAM-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAM_\(Smart_Micro_Munition\)) guided munition have been found in [Tigray](357350.md).[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] On 7 January 2022, a drone strike killed nearly 60 civilians and injured dozens more in a camp of internally displaced people in [Dedebit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedebit_\(town\)) in Tigray; the missile used was a MAM-L exclusively used with the TB2 drone.[^cite_note-199]

### African insurgencies

Some parts of Africa face attacks by highly mobile bands of Islamist militants, who move through scrubby terrain by motorbike to attack isolated military and civilian targets. These attacks had largely been in the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel), but by 2019 they had been spreading. Drones provide countries with the aerial surveillance capacity to seek and potentially attack insurgents. Buying from Turkey reduces the dependence of Niger and Togo on former-colonial-power France.[^cite_note-africa-200]

### 2025 India-Pakistan conflict

The [Indian armed forces](7171338.md) claimed, on the nights of 8 and 9 May during [Operation Sindoor](79896622.md), the Indian [Akashteer system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akashteer) directed Indian missile batteries to shoot down several Pakistani drones, including the Bayraktar TB2s. India claimed that the TB-2s failed to strike their targets. According to reports made by the Indian media, most drones were shot down as soon as they crossed into Indian airspace while several Bayraktar TB2s were downed while still over Pakistan.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202]. There was no independent corroboration of these claims, or of Pakistan using the Bayraktar TB2 during the conflict.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204]

## Variants

### Bayraktar TB2S

In October 2020, Baykar [CTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_technology_officer) [Selçuk Bayraktar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sel%C3%A7uk_Bayraktar) showed the new, improved version of TB2, named TB2S. In Bayraktar's [Twitter](9988187.md) post, the TB2S has a protrusion on its body and an antenna on its nose for satellite communication ([SATCOM](45207.md)).[^cite_note-TB2S-savunmasanayist.com-205][^cite_note-206] In the basic model, TB2, communication between the aircraft and the control station was via ground-based antennas. Communication via the [TÜRKSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrksat_\(company\)) [satellite](27683.md) provides a much greater control range than the 150–300 kilometres (93–186 mi) range of the basic model. The satellite communication will also make the TB2S more resistant to the jamming of communications by the enemy.[^cite_note-207] The SATCOM-integrated TB2S made its maiden flight on 4 December 2020.[^cite_note-208]

### Bayraktar TB2T-AI

In February 2025, the CTO of Baykar, [Selçuk Bayraktar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sel%C3%A7uk_Bayraktar), unveiled the new version on his official X page, followed by a video of the first flight of the updated drone. According to the post, the new variant has the following specifications:

- Turbo engine

- New aerodynamic design (New added winglets and horizontal stabilisers to the inverted V-tail of TB2)

- Three advanced AI computers

- Speed: 160 kts (\~300 km/h)

- Increased MTOW and payload capacity

- Max. altitude: 30,000 feet

- 30,000 feet climb: 25 min

### General characteristics

- **Crew:** 0 on board, 3 per [ground control station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAV_ground_control_station)

- **Length:** 6.5 m (21 ft 4 in)

- **Wing span:** 12 m (39 ft 4 in)

- **Max. take-off mass:** 700 kg (1,540 lb)

- **Payload:** 150 kg (331 lb)

- **Powerplant:** 1 × 100 hp (75 kW) internal combustion engine with injection&#x20;

  - Baykar TM100 (option 1)

    [^cite_note-Haluk_Bayraktar:_We_Concentrate_on_Being_the_Best_in_Technology_Over_Profits-8]

  - [Rotax 912-iS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotax_912) (option 2)

- **Propeller:** 2-bladed [variable-pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable-pitch_propeller_\(aeronautics\))

- **Fuel capacity:** 300 litres (79 US gal)

- **Fuel type:** gasoline (petrol)

### Performance

- **Maximum speed:&#x20;**&#x31;20 knots (222 km/h)

- **Cruise speed:** 70 knots (130 km/h)

- **[Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_\(aeronautics\)):**

- **Communication range:** [line-of-sight propagation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-of-sight_propagation), < 300 kilometres (186 mi)

- **Service ceiling:** 25,000 ft (7,620 m)

- **Operational altitude:** 18,000 ft (5,486 m)

- **Endurance:** >20 hours
