[Parliamentary elections](1442279.md) were held in [Kosovo](17391.md) on 28 December 2025 to elect the 120 members of the [Assembly](1068656.md). In the [previous election in February 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2025_Kosovan_parliamentary_election), no party had won a majority of seats, resulting in a [hung parliament](652214.md) with [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md), party of Prime Minister [Albin Kurti](4774301.md), remaining the largest party.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

The result was a decisive victory for Vetëvendosje, which gained nine seats, winning 57 in total, just four short of an outright majority.[^cite_note-4]

## Background

See also: [February 2025 Kosovan parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2025_Kosovan_parliamentary_election) and [2025–2026 Kosovo political deadlock](80359264.md)

The February elections saw [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) (LVV) winning 48 seats, short of the 61 needed for a majority. Following the election, both [PDK](1260606.md) and [AAK](1260747.md) indicated that they would not form a government with LVV.[^cite_note-ABCKosovo-5][^cite_note-RL_Feb-6] The [LDK](1256321.md) later announced that it would not support joining a coalition with LVV, instead proposing a transitional government composing all ethnic Albanian parties,[^cite_note-RL_May-7] though LVV rejected this.[^cite_note-8] As a result, the party was required to seek the support of other minority parties if it wished to form a coalition.[^cite_note-ABCKosovo-5]

The political crisis in Kosovo began on 15 April, when the country's political parties failed to elect the [Speaker of the Assembly of Kosovo](1260708.md), a deadlock that lasted until [Dimal Basha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimal_Basha) was elected speaker on 26 August.[^cite_note-9] From 26 August to 10 October, Kosovo entered another institutional crisis, this time related to the election of the Serbian Deputy Speaker of the Assembly.[^cite_note-10] This crisis was resolved when [Nenad Rašić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenad_Ra%C5%A1i%C4%87) was elected in the position on 10 October, and the Assembly was declared constituted.[^cite_note-11] A first attempt to form a Vetëvendosje government under Albin Kurti was rejected on 26 October.[^cite_note-12] [Glauk Konjufca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glauk_Konjufca) was nominated for prime minister in a second attempt to form a Vetëvendosje government but a parliamentary majority was not secured in a vote on 19 November, resulting in the dissolution of the Assembly the next day.[^cite_note-13]

Following the dissolution of the Assembly on 20 November, President [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md) set 28 December 2025 as the date for the election.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Electoral system

The 120 members of the Assembly are elected by [open list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) [proportional representation](23298.md) for a four-year term, with 20 reserved for national minorities.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Seats were allocated using the [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method) with an [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 5%.[^cite_note-18] For overseas voters there were 66 polling stations located in 36 countries around the world.[^cite_note-19]

## Campaign

Election campaigns in Kosovo are regulated to start 30 days before the scheduled election date and must conclude on the day before the election.[^cite_note-22]

The early days of the campaign saw protests in late November from public and private transport companies in [Pristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pristina) over financial strains and unpaid salaries that were caused by government failures to approve budgets for both 2025 and 2026.[^cite_note-23] On 30 November, Vetëvendosje formally established the Vetëvendosje Movement Coalition with [Guxo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guxo), [Alternativa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternativa_\(Kosovo\)), and [PSHDK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Christian_Democratic_Party_of_Kosovo).[^cite_note-24] On 3 December, the Central Election Commission (CEC) rejected the [Serb List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serb_List_\(Kosovo\)) in running in the December elections.[^cite_note-25] However, the Election Complaints and Appeals Panel (ECAP) overturned the CEC decision on 8 December, allowing the Serb List to run.[^cite_note-26] Amid campaigning in early December, Serb mayors were sworn into office on 5 December in four Serb-majority municipalities in Kosovo, replacing the Albanian mayors that had served in office since 2023.[^cite_note-27] The inauguration of the Serb mayors resulted in [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), the [President of the European Commission](24468.md), announcing on 18 December the lifting of sanctions from Kosovo that first began in June 2023 and the releasing of financial assistance in early 2026.[^cite_note-28] Debates over the issues of justice reform, security, energy policy, and governance were held by [Kallxo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallxo) between 17 December and 22 December.[^cite_note-29] On 24 December, four days before the election, Kurti announced that 100 euros will be allocated to children and pensioners at the end of the year, sparking accusations of vote buying.[^cite_note-30]

## Results

[Albin Kurti](4774301.md)'s [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) party received 51% of the vote, translating to 57 seats. While short of the 61 seats needed to form a government, Vetëvendosje gathered support from non-Serb minority representatives as it did after the 2021 elections. The [Democratic Party of Kosovo](1260606.md) came second with 20%, maintaining a similar level of support from the last election. The [Democratic League of Kosovo](1256321.md) came in third with 13%, dropping in support from 18% earlier the same year.[^cite_note-Zeqiri-35][^cite_note-36] Turnout was estimated at 47%.[^cite_note-37] With the fourth successive victory for Vetëvendosje in parliamentary elections, Kurti was sworn in for a third term as Prime Minister on 11 February 2026.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Aftermath

Albin Kurti, leader of Vetëvendosje, promised to constitute the [Assembly](1068656.md) and immediately form a new government once the results were certified. He called the opposition to support motions for international loan deals, which require a two-thirds majority to pass.[^cite_note-41]

President [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md) said that elected lawmakers should move swiftly to form a government and agree upon international agreements in the afternoon once results were certified. She warned that not forming institutions immediately would result in the blocking of nearly 1 billion euros in international funding, including growth funds from the EU and World Bank-backed agreements.[^cite_note-42]

[Bedri Hamza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedri_Hamza), the prime ministerial candidate for the [PDK](1260606.md), expressed hope for a quick formation of institutions and support of initiatives that support the interests of Kosovo. [Lumir Abdixhiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumir_Abdixhiku), a candidate from the [LDK](1256321.md), also similarly stressed the country's need for functional democratic institutions.[^cite_note-Zeqiri-35]

On 29 December 2025, High Representative/Vice-president [Kaja Kallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Kallas) and Commissioner [Marta Kos](77809600.md) published a statement on the election calling on the incoming Assembly to ratify the Growth Plan-related agreements and for the normalisation of relations between Kosovo and Serbia. It also said it would release additional grants for Kosovo in 2026.[^cite_note-43]

On 14 January 2026, the Central Election Commission (CEC) adopted a decision to recount 10% of polling stations in 28 municipalities and to conduct a full recount in 10 municipalities, following suspicions of manipulation of votes cast for candidates from various political parties.[^cite_note-44] On 19 January, following the identification of significant discrepancies between the votes counted at the Municipal Counting Centers and those recounted at the Counting and Results Center, a decision was made to recount all ballot boxes in all municipalities, as the observed differences were substantial.[^cite_note-45]

On 23 January, authorities announced the detention of 109 people on suspicion of falsifying election results and exerting pressure, threats and bribery, of which 68,017 ballots in [Prizren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prizren) alone were believed to have been affected.[^cite_note-46]

The recount finished on 30 January and the results were certified by the Central Election Commission (KQZ) by 1 February.[^cite_note-47]
