The **Academy Award for Best Cinematography** is an [Academy Award](324.md) awarded each year to a [cinematographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematographer) for work on one particular [motion picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film).

## History



[Charles Rosher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rosher), the first recipient in 1928

In its first film season, 1927–28, this award (like others such as the acting awards) was not tied to a specific film; all of the work by the nominated cinematographers during the qualifying period was listed after their names. The problem with this system became obvious the first year, since [Karl Struss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Struss) and [Charles Rosher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rosher) were nominated for their work together on *[Sunrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise:_A_Song_of_Two_Humans).* Still, three other films shot individually by either Rosher or Struss were also listed as part of the nomination. In the second year, 1929, there were no nominations at all, although the Academy has a list of unofficial titles that were under consideration by the Board of Judges. In the third year, 1930, films, not cinematographers, were nominated, and the final award did not show the cinematographer's name.

Finally, for the 1931 awards, the modern system in which individuals are nominated for a single film was adopted in all profession-related categories. From 1939 to 1966 with the exception of 1957, there were also separate awards for color and black-and-white cinematography. After *[Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who's_Afraid_of_Virginia_Woolf%3F_\(film\))* (1966), the most recent black-and-white films to win since then are *[Schindler's List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schindler's_List)* (1993), *[Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_\(2018_film\))* (2018) and *[Mank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mank)* (2020).

[Floyd Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_Crosby) won the award for *[Tabu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabu:_A_Story_of_the_South_Seas)* in 1931, which was the last silent film to win in this category. [Hal Mohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Mohr) won the only write-in Academy Award ever, in 1935 for *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night's_Dream_\(1935_film\))*. Mohr was also the first person to win for both black-and-white and color cinematography.

No winners are [lost films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_film), although some of the earliest nominees (and of the unofficial nominees of 1928–29) are lost, including *[The Devil Dancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Dancer)* (1927), *[The Magic Flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_Flame)* (1927), and *[4 Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Devils)* (1928). *[The Right to Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Right_to_Love_\(1930_American_film\))* (1930) is incomplete, and *[Sadie Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadie_Thompson_\(film\))* (1927) is incomplete and partially reconstructed with stills.

[David Lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lean) holds the record for the director with the most films that won the Academy Award for Best Cinematography at the Oscars with five wins out of six nominations for *[Great Expectations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Expectations_\(1946_film\))*, *[The Bridge on the River Kwai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bridge_on_the_River_Kwai)*, *[Lawrence of Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_of_Arabia_\(film\))*, *[Doctor Zhivago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Zhivago_\(film\))*, and *[Ryan's Daughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan's_Daughter)*.

The first nominees shot primarily on [digital video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video) were *[The Curious Case of Benjamin Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curious_Case_of_Benjamin_Button_\(film\))* and *[Slumdog Millionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slumdog_Millionaire)* in 2009, with *Slumdog Millionaire* being the first winner.[^cite_note-1] The following year, *[Avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_\(2009_film\))* was the first nominee and winner to be shot entirely on digital video.[^cite_note-2]

In January 2017, [Bradford Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Young) became the first African-American cinematographer to be nominated for an Academy Award, for his work on *Arrival*.

In 2018, [Rachel Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Morrison) became the first woman to receive a nomination.[^cite_note-3] Prior to that, it had been the last non-acting Academy Award category to never nominate a woman.[^cite_note-4]

In 2019, [Alfonso Cuarón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_Cuar%C3%B3n) became the first winner of this category to have also served as director on the film, for *[Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_\(2018_film\))*.[^cite_note-5] This followed a public dispute between Cuarón and the Academy over the Academy's plan to shorten the Oscars broadcast by relegating four awards, including cinematography, to the commercial breaks in the show. Cuarón objected by saying, "In the history of cinema, masterpieces have existed without sound, without color, without a story, without actors and without music. No single film has ever existed without cinematography ..."[^cite_note-6]

In 2026, [Autumn Durald Arkapaw](42818757.md) became the first woman, and first [woman of color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person_of_color), to win this category.[^cite_note-7]

## Winners and nominees

Winners are listed first in colored row, followed by the other nominees.



[Charles Rosher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rosher) was the inaugural co-winner of this category, winning the award twice, first for *[Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise:_A_Song_of_Two_Humans)* (1927) alongside [Karl Struss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Struss) and his second for *[The Yearling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Yearling_\(1946_film\))* (1946) alongside [Leonard Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Smith_\(cinematographer\)) and [Arthur Arling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Arling).



[Karl Struss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Struss) was the inaugural co-winner of this category, winning alongside [Charles Rosher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rosher) for *[Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise:_A_Song_of_Two_Humans)* (1927).



[Clyde De Vinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_De_Vinna) won for *[White Shadows in the South Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Shadows_in_the_South_Seas)* (1928).



[Lee Garmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Garmes) won for *[Shanghai Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Express_\(film\))* (1932).
