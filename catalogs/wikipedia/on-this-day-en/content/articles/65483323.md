# Rafael López Aliaga

This [Spanish surname is barreled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-barrelled_name#Traditions_on_Iberian_Peninsula), being made up of multiple names. It should be written as *López Aliaga*, not *López.*

**Rafael Bernardo López-Aliaga Cazorla** (born 11 February 1961) is a Peruvian businessman, [industrial engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_engineering) and politician who served as [mayor of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Lima) from 2023 to 2025. He is currently the leader of the [far-right](221220.md)[^cite_note-5] [Popular Renewal](66197743.md) party, the successor to [National Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_\(Peru\)) in 2020, and the party's candidate in the [2026 Peruvian presidential election](69679018.md).

Entering politics late in his career, López Aliaga was elected to [Lima](85423.md)'s Metropolitan Council, serving under mayor [Luis Castañeda Lossio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Casta%C3%B1eda_Lossio)'s second term. Following a failed run for the [Peruvian Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Congress) in 2011, he remained a member of National Solidarity but stayed in the private sector. It was not until 2019 when he assumed the leadership of his party as General Secretary following Castañeda's pretrial detention for a corruption trial.

At the [2020 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Peruvian_parliamentary_election), López Aliaga gained political attention as he started shifting his party further to the [right](56522.md). Eventually, [National Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_\(Peru\)) failed to attain congressional representation in the election. Announcing a presidential bid, he was elected president of his party to lead a reconstruction process, effectively refounding the party as [Popular Renewal](66197743.md). López Aliaga ran for [President of Peru](239109.md) at the [2021 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Peruvian_general_election). Fighting for the right-wing vote alongside [Keiko Fujimori](4643445.md) and [Hernando de Soto](285909.md), he eventually placed third with 11.75% of the popular vote.[^cite_note-6]

Following the election of [Pedro Castillo](49637220.md) as president, López Aliaga has been considered as one of the most visible leaders of the Peruvian right.[^cite_note-7] He ran successfully for the [mayorship of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Lima) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Lima_municipal_election), narrowly winning with 26.32% of the vote.[^cite_note-8]

As a businessman, López Aliaga is the co-founder (along with Lorenzo Sousa) and a shareholder of Peruval Corp SA, parent company of [Ferrocarril Transandino SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarril_Transandino_SA) and [Peru Rail SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_Rail_SA), both train companies with operations in [Cusco](72735.md), [Arequipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arequipa) and [Puno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puno).

## Early life and education

López Aliaga was born in [Lima](85423.md) on 11 February 1961, but was raised in [Chiclayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiclayo), [Lambayeque Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Lambayeque). His father, Fernando López Aliaga, worked in the city's Hacienda [Pomalca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomalca_District) while his mother, Paula Cazorla, was from the [Cajamarca Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Cajamarca). Both of his parents were [chemical engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineer), graduates of the [National University of San Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_San_Marcos) in Lima.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

Upon graduating from [San Agustín School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colegio_San_Agustin) of Chiclayo located in [Pimentel district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimentel_District), López Aliaga was admitted to the [Pedro Ruiz Gallo National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Ruiz_Gallo_National_University?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Lambayeque city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambayeque,_Peru) to study [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering), ranking first in the entrance examination. He subsequently dropped out due to the political unrest of the 1980s. Relocating in [Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piura), he was admitted to the [University of Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Piura) to study [industrial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_engineering), completing the degree in four years and graduating first in his class. He obtained a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in 1983. Later, he obtained a master's degree in Business Administration (MBA) at the [University of the Pacífic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_del_Pac%C3%ADfico_\(Peru\)), and completed the Senior Management Program (PAD) at the University of Piura.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

López Aliaga pursued a career in banking, serving at first in [Banco Wiese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Wiese), and as Corporate and Development Banking Manager at [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank). Subsequently, he founded Peruvian Corp, a securities enterprise, and Grupo Acres, a hotel and railway finance company. Since 1999, he has been a co shareholder along with Lorenzo Sousa of [PeruRail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeruRail) as well as [Belmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmond_Limited)'s hotels and Trasandino Railway in Peru.[^cite_note-\:6-13]

## Political career

Entering politics through the [Lima City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Municipality_of_Lima) in 2007, López Aliaga maintained a low profile until 2019, when he was elected to the general secretaryship of [National Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_\(Peru\)) in September 2019. Following the dissolution of the [Peruvian Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Congress), his party was set to run at the [2020 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Peruvian_parliamentary_election). Initially a moderate conservative, López Aliaga adopted a [conservative](6675.md) platform.[^cite_note-14] He also ran for a seat in Congress in 2011 under the [National Solidarity Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_Alliance), but he was unsuccessful.[^cite_note-15]

### 2021 Peruvian presidential election

Further information: [2021 Peruvian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Peruvian_presidential_election)

During the 2020 campaign, López Aliaga announced his intention to run for [President of Peru](239109.md) at the [2021 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Peruvian_general_election), stating in a televised interview that he considered himself to be "the Peruvian [Bolsonaro](46603496.md)".[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-16]

Following his party's poor results at the [2020 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Peruvian_parliamentary_election), he announced an organizational re-foundation.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] In October 2020, López Aliaga officially founded [Popular Renewal](66197743.md), effectively dissolving [National Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_\(Peru\)) by changing the registered organization's name and platform. Assuming the position of party president, he stated that the party is "re-founded with principles of solidarity and anchored in God, Christ."[^cite_note-19] He campaigns on a [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) platform on economic issues and a conservative one on societal issues. A member of [Opus Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei), he is sometimes described as a [religious fundamentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_fundamentalist). He calls for a ban on [sex education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_education) in colleges, which he sees as a form of "[cultural Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Marxism)" promoting homosexuality, a ban on [abortion](765.md), including in cases of rape or danger to the life of the mother, and a ban on gay marriage.[^cite_note-\:pagina-20]

As part of his campaign, he stated the voting in 2021 would be "the last free election in Peru" and "if we don't go well, this will be Venezuela or Cuba, remember me".[^cite_note-\:2-21] He also blamed presidents [Martín Vizcarra](51682201.md) and [Francisco Sagasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Sagasti) for Peru's economic issues, describing the Vizcarra administration as a "communist style of the government" and called Sagasti a "clown".[^cite_note-\:2-21] He proposed further deregulation of Peru and the elimination of half of its ministries.[^cite_note-\:2-21] According to *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, human rights groups were "alarmed by his use of conspiracy theories, disinformation and hate speech against opponents and journalists".[^cite_note-22]

In an interview conducted on January 19, 2021 on the [ATV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATV_\(Peruvian_TV_channel\)) channel, he generated controversy with the metaphorical opinion he provided regarding his stance against abortion in rape cases. The words he mentioned were: "I am a hotelier. I have five-star hotels, for me to give a 5-star hotel as a home for a girl is to give her the most affection I can."[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

On February 19, 2021, the Special Electoral Jury (JEE) initiated a sanctioning process against the candidacy of Rafael López Aliaga for violating electoral propaganda regulations. This measure was approved because the applicant said that upon reaching the presidency "he will donate his salary to charitable organizations".[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] He was temporarily disqualified from the presidential election on 25 February 2021 for alleged vote-buying based on his statement that if he was elected President, he will donate his salary to charity.[^cite_note-\:0-27] [Hernando de Soto](285909.md), his political rival in the 2021 elections, appeared at the demonstration outside the JNE, where López Aliaga was, whom he praised.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

On March 12, 2021, he presented his technical team, which included former ministers Alfonso Miranda Eyzaguirre, Carlos Herrera Descalzi, Alfonso Velásquez Tuesta, former Vice Minister Eduardo Ponce Vivanco, former Vice President of the Republic, [Máximo San Román](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1ximo_San_Rom%C3%A1n), the former parliamentarian [Fabiola Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabiola_Morales), the former president of the Judiciary Javier Villa Stein, the former director general of the Police José Tisoc Lindley, among others.[^cite_note-30]

### 2022 Lima mayoral election

In the aftermath of the first round of the [2021 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Peruvian_general_election), López Aliaga confirmed his bid for [mayor of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Lima) in the upcoming [2022 mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Lima_mayoral_election).[^cite_note-31]

Leading the polls early into the election, López Aliaga trailed behind former congressman and Interior minister, [Daniel Urresti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Urresti), who ran for a second time for the mayorship following his defeat to [Jorge Muñoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Mu%C3%B1oz_\(Peruvian_politician\)) at the [2018 Lima municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Lima_municipal_election).

As part of his campaign, López Aliaga promised to fight crime with support of the reservists group of the [Peruvian Armed Forces](23438.md), in addition to calling for a radical reform of the capital's transportation system.[^cite_note-32] His campaign was managed by former right-wing congressmen [Julio Gagó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Gag%C3%B3) and [Renzo Reggiardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Reggiardo), with the latter running for lieutenant mayor.[^cite_note-33]

At the election, [Popular Renewal](66197743.md) placed first by receiving 26.35% of the popular vote, claiming victory for López Aliaga, in addition winning twelve districts of the capital.[^cite_note-34] In contrast to previous municipal elections, no winning candidate for [mayor of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Lima) since 1963 had been elected by less than 30% of the vote, making him the least voted winner in the history of the capital.[^cite_note-35]

In April 2023, an [Ipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipsos) poll showed that 49% of respondents disapproved of López Aliaga, compared to a 40% approval rate.[^cite_note-\:8-36]

### 2026 Peruvian presidential election

Further information: [2026 Peruvian general election](69679018.md)

In October 2025, López Aliaga resigned as mayor of Lima[^cite_note-37] to campaign in the [Peruvian general election](69679018.md) scheduled for 12 April 2026. He was considered an early frontrunner in the presidential race.[^cite_note-38]

The first round presidential election resulted in [Keiko Fujimori](4643445.md) and [Roberto Sánchez Palomino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_S%C3%A1nchez_Palomino) will be going to the second round. Despite losing the presidential election, López-Aliaga was elected to the Senate for the nationwide constituency.

Following the first round, official results confirmed that neither López Aliaga nor other major candidates advanced to the runoff, which was contested between Keiko Fujimori and Roberto Sánchez Palomino. López Aliaga finished in third place nationally with 11.91% of the vote, as reflected in the final tally published by Peru’s electoral authorities.[^cite_note-39]

With his elimination from the presidential race, López Aliaga did not proceed to the second round campaign and instead transitioned toward his elected position in the Senate for the nationwide constituency, scheduled to assume office in July 2026, as part of the broader congressional transition following the general election cycle.

Observers have placed him as [right-wing](56522.md) to [far-right](221220.md), and ideologically [conservative](6675.md).[^cite_note-\:3-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-aldia-42][^cite_note-\:4-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] Rafael López Aliaga, a [member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_membership_of_Opus_Dei#Numeraries) of [Opus Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei), regards himself as a "social Christian", saying that he maintains [celibacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celibacy) through daily [self-flagellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-flagellation) with a [chain cilice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilice) and thinking of the [Virgin Mary](73513.md).[^cite_note-\:2-21][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] He opposes [same-sex marriage](92656.md), the use of [contraceptives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraceptives), the [legalization of marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalization_of_marijuana) and [abortion](765.md).[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-\:5-49][^cite_note-\:4-43] In Peru, he is commonly known under the nickname "Porky", referring to the [*Porky Pig Show*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Porky_Pig_Show). López Aliaga adopted his nickname for his campaign, so he occasionally costumed himself as a pig to protest against the omnipresent [corruption in the country](41862074.md).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-\:pagina-20][^cite_note-aldia-42]

López Aliaga signed the *[Madrid Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Charter)*, a document drafted by the Spanish party [Vox](41928488.md) that describes left-wing groups as enemies of [Ibero-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibero-America) involved in a "criminal project" that are "under the umbrella of the Cuban regime".[^cite_note-\:42-51][^cite_note-\:102-52] He signed the document along with other right-wing politicians such as [Javier Milei](56599840.md) of Argentina, [José Antonio Kast](55052190.md) of [Chile](5489.md) and [Eduardo Bolsonaro](58054973.md) of Brazil, the son of President [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md).[^cite_note-\:102-52]

Regarding crime, López Aliaga applauded [Nayib Bukele](46452145.md) during the [Salvadoran gang crackdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_gang_crackdown_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)), calling on the [Ministry of Defense](5629239.md) to allow the [Peruvian Armed Forces](23438.md) to patrol the streets of Lima, including with [tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanks).[^cite_note-\:8-36]

On the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Peru), he opposed travel restrictions and the use of masks. He generally did not use masks in his public appearances, although he explains that he sometimes did wear them "so that the press does not criticise \[him]." He advocated that the vaccination campaign should be left to the private sector and that individuals should pay for access to vaccination.[^cite_note-\:pagina-20] According to [*Vice*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_\(magazine\)), López Aliaga disseminated [misinformation](2203174.md) about [COVID-19 vaccines in Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccines_in_Peru), accusing President [Sagasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Sagasti) of committing "[genocide](12441.md)" for purchasing what he called ineffective vaccines.[^cite_note-VICE-53] A month before elections, López Aliaga called for the removal of President Sagasti from office, raising concerns among presidential candidates that he was promoting a [coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup) against the president.[^cite_note-VICE-53][^cite_note-\:3-40]

On economic issues, he wants to favour private investment and limit the presence of the state. He proposed to abolish social programmes such as food distribution in poor schools, believing that social assistance should be handled by private associations and not by the public authorities.[^cite_note-\:pagina-20]

## Investigations

According to Peruvian media, López Aliaga has been allegedly involved in controversies surrounding his finances.[^cite_note-\:6-13][^cite_note-\:7-54] He was investigated by the Congress of Peru in 2001 regarding allegations of [tax evasion](2256795.md).[^cite_note-55] The [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers) scandal in 2017 mentioned López Aliaga as allegedly being involved with [money laundering](19390.md).[^cite_note-\:6-13][^cite_note-\:7-54] During the campaign his businesses were accused of having a large debt with the [SUNAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendencia_Nacional_de_Administraci%C3%B3n_Tributaria) and of not paying personal debts to the state.[^cite_note-56]

On 18 July 2021, the Public Ministry of Peru opened an investigation into [Willax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willax_Televisi%C3%B3n) journalists Phillip Butters, Humberto Ortiz and Enrique Luna Victoria, alleging that they were responsible for acts of [sedition](324091.md) and inciting [civil unrest](329906.md).[^cite_note-\:32-57][^cite_note-\:43-58] Prosecutor Juana Meza wrote that Willax disseminated "news with a conspiratorial connotation, inciting the electoral results to be unknown, trying to weaken the electoral institutions and even calling for a coup d'état" and was used as a platform "to send messages of hatred and incitement to kill".[^cite_note-\:32-57] Rafael López Aliaga was also named in the investigation.[^cite_note-\:32-57][^cite_note-\:43-58] López Aliaga reportedly called for death in two separate incidents; in May 2021 he chanted "Death to communism! Death to [Cerrón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Cerr%C3%B3n)! Death to [Castillo](49637220.md)!" to supporters and at the Respect My Vote rally that was organized by Willax TV owner [Erasmo Wong Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmo_Wong_Lu) on 26 June 2021, where the politician stated "Death to communism, get out of here, filthy communists, you have awakened the lion, to the streets!"[^cite_note-\:32-57][^cite_note-\:43-58][^cite_note-59]
