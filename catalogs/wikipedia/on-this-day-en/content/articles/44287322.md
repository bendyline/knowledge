The **mayor of Greater Manchester** is the directly elected [strategic authority mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_authority_mayor) of [Greater Manchester](79819.md), responsible for strategic governance in the region that includes health, transport, housing, strategic planning, waste management, policing, the [Greater Manchester Fire and Rescue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Fire_and_Rescue_Service) and skills. The creation of the mayor of Greater Manchester was agreed between the then [Chancellor of the Exchequer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer), [George Osborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Osborne), and Greater Manchester's 10 district council leaders. As well as having specific powers, the mayor chairs the [Greater Manchester Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Combined_Authority), also assuming the powers of the [Greater Manchester Police and Crime Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Police_and_Crime_Commissioner).

[Tony Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Lloyd) was appointed as interim mayor for Greater Manchester in May 2015. The [first Greater Manchester mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_election) took place on 4 May 2017 and was won by [Andy Burnham](414818.md), who was subsequently [re-elected for a second term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_election) in May 2021 and for a third in May 2024.

[Paul Dennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dennett) was [acting](691917.md) in the absence of a mayor, as he is legally required to do as deputy mayor,[^cite_note-5] following Burnham's election to the House of Commons in the [2026 Makerfield by-election](83181171.md) which disqualified him from holding the post.[^cite_note-Dennett-6][^cite_note-7] The [2026 Greater Manchester mayoral by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_by-election) was held on 30 July 2026, and was won by [Bev Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bev_Craig).

## Background

The ten local authorities which make up Greater Manchester work together as the [Greater Manchester Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Combined_Authority), which carries out work through bodies including [Transport for Greater Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_Greater_Manchester) and the [Greater Manchester Waste Disposal Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Waste_Disposal_Authority). There is a directly elected [Mayor of Salford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Salford) for the [City of Salford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Salford). In 2008, [Bury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Bury) rejected a proposal for an elected mayor for the borough only.[^cite_note-8] In 2012, Manchester [rejected a similar proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_English_mayoral_referendums#Manchester) for the [City of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manchester) only. There is also a [Lord Mayor of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lord_mayors_of_Manchester#Lord_mayors_of_Manchester) which is a ceremonial post.

The proposal for an elected mayor was announced in November 2014 by [George Osborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Osborne).[^cite_note-9] The creation of an elected mayor for Greater Manchester required new primary legislation[^cite_note-10] and the first election was announced to take place on 4 May 2017.[^cite_note-Mayoral_election_date-2] On 29 May 2015, [Tony Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Lloyd) was appointed as interim mayor by the combined authority leaders.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] The [Labour Party](19279158.md) candidate was confirmed as being [Andy Burnham](414818.md) on 9 August 2016, fending off [Ivan Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Lewis) and Tony Lloyd to the position.[^cite_note-13] The [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) candidate was confirmed in September 2016 as [Jane Brophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Brophy), who is a Trafford Borough councillor.[^cite_note-14] Later in September, the [Green Party](79787.md) announced that their candidate would be Deyika Nzeribe;[^cite_note-15] however Nzeribe later died as a result of a heart attack on New Year's Day 2017[^cite_note-16] and Will Patterson was chosen to replace him.[^cite_note-17] In October 2016, the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) announced that Sean Anstee, Leader of [Trafford Metropolitan Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafford_Metropolitan_Borough_Council), would run as their candidate for mayor.[^cite_note-18]

## Governance arrangements

Unlike the directly elected [London Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Assembly) scrutiny structure that operates in [Greater London](45367389.md), the mayor of Greater Manchester sits on the [Greater Manchester Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Combined_Authority) alongside the ten council leaders as the eleventh member. The council leaders form part of the mayor's cabinet, each with a clear portfolio of responsibilities.[^cite_note-19] The mayor can be vetoed if a majority vote against any proposals put forward, and the spatial planning strategy requires a unanimous vote of the mayor's cabinet.[^cite_note-20]

## Powers and functions

Powers of the mayor initially announced include spatial planning, housing, transport, policing, waste management and skills.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] In addition to setting the policy direction of the GMCA the mayor serves as an ambassador and public figurehead for the region.

### Planning

The mayor is responsible for the creation of a county-wide spatial development strategy with adoption subject to unanimous approval of the Members of the GMCA. The mayor is able to make compulsory purchase orders and establish a Mayoral Development Corporation for an area subject to the agreement of the members whose district(s) the order/corporation covers. The mayor has not been granted the ability to call in local planning application decisions judged to be of strategic importance, unlike some other combined authority mayors.

### Housing

The mayor oversees the administration of the £300m Greater Manchester Housing Investment Fund with the intention of delivering an additional 15,000 homes over a 10-year period.[^cite_note-25] The mayor jointly controls the Greater Manchester Land Commission with the [housing minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Communities_and_Local_Government) and other appropriate government ministers, which includes a database of all public sector land and oversee its efficient use including disposal with the aim of contributing toward a target of 10,000 homes being built annually in the region.

### Policing and fire

See also: [Greater Manchester Police and Crime Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Police_and_Crime_Commissioner)

With the creation of the office of mayor, the role of [Police and Crime Commissioner for Greater Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Police_and_Crime_Commissioner) was subsumed into the new post. The mayor also took over the role formerly exercised by the Greater Manchester Fire & Rescue Authority in setting budgets and taking strategic decisions.

### Waste management

The mayor is responsible for the administration of the [Greater Manchester Waste Disposal Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Waste_Disposal_Authority), which is the largest waste disposal authority in the United Kingdom responsible for the waste of 2.4 million people and covering all districts except Wigan which has its own waste authority.

### Skills

The GMCA will have full control of the apprenticeship and adult skills budget for the region from the 2018/19 academic year as well as a commitment to explore devolution of 16–19 education spending. The combined authority also has the power to co-commission alongside the [Department for Work and Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Work_and_Pensions), the region's unemployment and back to work programmes.

### Justice

As part of the [2016 UK Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_UK_Budget), it was announced that powers relating to criminal justice would be devolved to the mayor as part of a drive to offer seamless interventions for offenders transitioning between prisons and the community and also to join up public services that prevent crime. As part of this, there will also be a new 'Life Chances Investment Fund' which combines several streams of funding for troubled families and back to work for programmes for those with health issues.[^cite_note-26] GMCA will take on the commissioning of National Offender Management Services, liaise in the commissioning of rehabilitation programmes, youth justice and secure schools and female and child sentences under 2 years. The GMCA will also liaise in the running of the court and prison estates and there is an eventual government aim to fully devolve the prisons estate.

### Health

The £6 billion health and social care budget for the region is devolved to the GMCA and the mayor works alongside the other members and the 22 local clinical commissioning groups to set budgets and direct spending priorities. The GMCA has worked with the commissioning groups in the creation of a strategic sustainability plan.

### Transport

See also: [Transport in Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Manchester) and [Bee Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_Network)



The cross-borough [Manchester Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Metrolink) which has grown from 20 stops in 2009 to 92 in 2014

[^cite_note-27]



[Manchester Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport) is the largest publicly operated airport in the UK, owned in part by the ten councils of Greater Manchester.

[^cite_note-28]

The mayor possesses powers over transport in [Greater Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Manchester).

The successful delivery of large infrastructure such as the second runway at the publicly owned [Manchester Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport) in 1998, the amalgamation of the [M60 orbital motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M60_motorway_\(Great_Britain\)) in 2000 and a rapidly expanding and self-sustainable [Metrolink tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Metrolink) - from 20 stations in 2009 to 92 stations in 2014 - emboldened local authorities and instilled confidence at Whitehall.[^cite_note-29] Responsibilities include overseeing road management (transferred to TfGM in 2009) which include road safety, bus lanes and congestion as well as influence over bus services, the Metrolink tram system and cycling schemes. The regional rail network within Greater Manchester, run mostly by [Northern Trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Trains) and in parts by [TransPennine Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransPennine_Express), is franchised by the UK government with no direct control by the mayor (though some potential for input exists via [Transport for the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_the_North)).

The mayor is responsible for the creation of the Local Transport Plan for the region outlining policy and spending decision subject to a 2/3rd majority ratification. The mayor is also responsible for allocating £300m of infrastructure funding over 30 years from the government as well as a yet to be finalised transport funding settlement to follow the existing 2014-2019 settlement. The mayor also has powers to reform the local bus market following the passage of the [Bus Services Act 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_Services_Act_2017).

In October 2018, the mayor of Greater Manchester, Andy Burnham, stated his powers to influence transport improvements in the region were insufficient. He described the performance of [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva_Rail_North) since the May 2018 timetable as "shocking", the struggle to introduce an holistic [bus franchising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_franchising) system as a continuing "challenge" and congestion on the roads as an impediment to the region's future growth - without government action this would result in lost opportunities for Greater Manchester.[^cite_note-30]

The creation of the [Bee Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_Network) launched in September 2023 is a integrated transport system linking bus, tram, bicycle and train travel which has been a emerging tenet of transport policy in the region and has been endorsed under Burnham's tenure.[^cite_note-31]

### Intergovernmental relations

The mayor is a member of the [Mayoral Council for England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoral_Council_for_England) and the [Council of the Nations and Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Nations_and_Regions).

## Elections

Main article: [Greater Manchester mayoral elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_mayoral_elections)

The [first election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_election) by [supplementary vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplementary_vote) took place in 2017.[^cite_note-33] The normal cycle of elections is one every four years, with the first term lasting three years.[^cite_note-34] The [second election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_election) was delayed for one year because of the COVID-19 pandemic and took place in 2021.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The [third election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_election) in 2024 was the first on the regular four-year cycle and was the only election to use [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md). Andy Burnham was the winner of the first three elections.

The position of mayor became [vacant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_vacancy) in June 2026 following Burnham's election to the House of Commons in the [2026 Makerfield by-election](83181171.md) which disqualified him from holding the post.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-39] The [2026 Greater Manchester mayoral by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_by-election) was announced on 19 June 2026 and was held on 30 July 2026.[^cite_note-40] Elections will return to the four-year cycle from the [2028 Greater Manchester mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Greater_Manchester_mayoral_election).
