**Yoane Wissa** (born 3 September 1996) is a professional [footballer](10568.md) who plays as a [striker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striker_\(association_football\)) for [Premier League](11250.md) club [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.). Born in France, he represents the [DR Congo national team](1030243.md).

A product of the [Châteauroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LB_Ch%C3%A2teauroux) academy, Wissa began his senior career with the club in 2015. Following a spell with [Angers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angers_SCO), he moved to [Lorient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient) in 2018. He was a part of their team that finished the [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FC_Lorient_season) [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) season as champions. After [one season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Ligue_1) in [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1), he joined [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C.) in 2021. After four seasons at Brentford, Wissa joined Newcastle United in 2025.

## Club career

### Early years

Wissa began his youth career as a [goalkeeper](5348091.md) at the age of seven.[^cite_note-Goodwin-3] He played for his local youth club Épinay-sous-Sénart, before moving to [midfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midfield) and then the [forward](428681.md) line.[^cite_note-4] As his youth career progressed, he also became adept as a [winger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winger_\(association_football\)) and [number 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_striker).[^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5] Wissa began his senior career as a forward with [Châteauroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LB_Ch%C3%A2teauroux) and progressed through the [reserve team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_team) to break into the first team during the [2015–16 Championnat National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Championnat_National) season,[^cite_note-Goodwin-3][^cite_note-6] which he finished with 24 appearances and seven goals.[^cite_note-Soccerway-7]

A transfer to [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Angers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angers_SCO) followed in 2016, but Wissa managed just two substitute appearances during the first half of the [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Angers_SCO_season) season and he played much of 2017 away on [loan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_\(sports\)) at [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) clubs [Laval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Lavallois) and [Ajaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Ajaccio).[^cite_note-Soccerway-7][^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5] Wissa departed the [Stade Raymond Kopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Raymond_Kopa) in January 2018.[^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5]

### Lorient



Wissa warming up for [Lorient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient) in 2019.

In January 2018, Wissa transferred to Ligue 2 club [Lorient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient) and immediately established himself at the promotion-chasing club.[^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5] Promotion to Ligue 1 was attained at the end of the [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FC_Lorient_season) season, when Wissa's 15 goals in 28 appearances helped the club to the Ligue 2 championship.[^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5]

He made 38 appearances and scored 10 goals during the [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FC_Lorient_season) season,[^cite_note-Soccerway-7] in which Lorient narrowly avoided finishing in the relegation playoff places.[^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5] Wissa departed the club in August 2021 and finished his 3+1⁄2-year stay at the [Stade du Moustoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_du_Moustoir) with 128 appearances and 37 goals.[^cite_note-Soccerway-7][^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5]

### Brentford

#### 2021–22

On 10 August 2021, Wissa moved to England to join newly-promoted [Premier League](11250.md) club [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C.) on a four-year contract, with the option of a further year, for an undisclosed fee, reported to be £8.5 million.[^cite_note-\:0-8] The transfer had been in the works for two years and he had turned down the opportunity to move to the [Community Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_Community_Stadium) during the previous [transfer window](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_window).[^cite_note-Dean-9] Despite being unable to link up with the club during [pre-season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-season) after undergoing eye surgery,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:1-11] Wissa scored five goals in his first six appearances for the club.[^cite_note-NewContract-12]

His [brace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brace_\(sports\)) in a 7–0 [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) third round win over [Oldham Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldham_Athletic_A.F.C.) on 21 September 2021 was recognised with a place in the EFL Cup Team of the Round and his second goal,[^cite_note-13] a [bicycle kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_kick), was voted Goal of the Round and Goal of the Tournament.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Wissa's goalscoring run was ended by an ankle knock suffered in mid-October 2021.[^cite_note-16] He returned to match play two months later and was deployed in a mixture of [starting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_lineup) and substitute roles through to the end of the season.[^cite_note-Soccerway-7] Wissa ended the 2021–22 season with 34 appearances and 10 goals.[^cite_note-Soccerbase2122-17]

#### 2022–2024

Wissa was deployed in a mix of starting and substitute roles and scored seven goals in 40 appearances during the [2022–23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Brentford_F.C._season) season,[^cite_note-Soccerway-7] in which Brentford were in contention for a [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_football) place on the final day.[^cite_note-18] In May 2023, he signed a new three-year contract, with the option of a further year.[^cite_note-NewContract-12]

Following [Ivan Toney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Toney)'s eight-month ban from all football-related activity, Wissa began the [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Brentford_F.C._season) season as Brentford's starting centre forward.[^cite_note-19] Following the transfer deadline day loan signing of forward [Neal Maupay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Maupay),[^cite_note-20] Wissa alternated between the forward and left wing positions.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Wissa top-scored for the club during the 2023–24 season, with 12 goals from 36 appearances,[^cite_note-23] predominantly as a starter.[^cite_note-Soccerway-7] He ended the season with a run of eight goals in 13 league matches.[^cite_note-Soccerbase2324-24]

#### 2024–2025

A long-term injury suffered by new centre forward signing [Igor Thiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Thiago) during the [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Brentford_F.C._season) pre-season allowed Wissa to begin the regular season in the position.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] He began the season with three goals from four Premier League starting appearances and three goals across four appearances in November 2024 saw him nominated for the Premier League [Player of the Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League_Player_of_the_Month) award.[^cite_note-Soccerbase2425-27][^cite_note-28] Wissa's 37th Premier League goal, scored in a 2–2 draw with [Manchester City](165813.md) on 14 January 2025, made him the club's record Premier League goalscorer.[^cite_note-29] He ended the 2024–25 season with 39 appearances and a season-high 20 goals.[^cite_note-Soccerbase2425-27]

### Newcastle United

On 1 September 2025, Wissa completed a transfer to [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) for a fee of £50 million plus add ons, ending his four-year spell with Brentford.[^cite_note-30] Later that year, on 6 December, he made his debut in a 2–1 victory over [Burnley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_F.C.), becoming the 1000th player to represent the club in the league.[^cite_note-31] A few days later, on 10 December, he made his [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) debut, coming on in the stoppage time of a 2–2 away draw against [Bayer Leverkusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_04_Leverkusen).[^cite_note-32]

A week later, on 17 December, he scored his first goal for the club in the [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_EFL_Cup) quarter-final, which ended in a 2–1 victory over [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.).[^cite_note-33] On 30 December, he netted his first league goal for the club in a 3–1 away victory over Burnley.[^cite_note-34] On 21 January 2026, he scored his first [Champions League](74127997.md) goal in a 3–0 victory over [PSV Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSV_Eindhoven).[^cite_note-35]

## International career

Wissa won his maiden call into the [DR Congo](1030243.md) squad for a pair of [friendly matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendly_matches) in October 2020.[^cite_note-36] He scored his first two international goals on his second and third caps, in friendly and [2022 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_third_round) matches versus [Morocco](1024865.md) respectively.[^cite_note-NFT-37]

Wissa was a part of the DR Congo squad which qualified for the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and he was named in the squad for the tournament finals.[^cite_note-NFT-37][^cite_note-38] He appeared in each match and scored two goals during the team's run to the third place play-off,[^cite_note-NFT-37] which was lost on [penalties](511302.md) to [South Africa](1018598.md).[^cite_note-39] Wissa's performances were recognised with a place in the Team of the Tournament.[^cite_note-TOTT-40]

On 19 May 2026, he was included in the 26-man squad selected by head coach [Sébastien Desabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_Desabre) to represent the DR Congo at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-41] He scored DR Congo's first-ever FIFA World Cup goal in their 1–1 draw against [Portugal](576328.md) in the opening [group stage](81660204.md) match on 17 June, earning the country its first point in the tournament.[^cite_note-42] On 27 June, he scored a brace and earned Man of the Match award in a 3–1 victory over [Uzbekistan](1032413.md), securing his nation's first-ever World Cup win and qualification for the knockout stage.[^cite_note-43] In addition, he became the fourth African player to score three goals in a World Cup group stage, following [Papa Bouba Diop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_Bouba_Diop) in 2002, and both [Ismael Saibari](65163271.md) and [Ismaïla Sarr](51591292.md) earlier in the tournament.[^cite_note-44] Wissa nearly scored another goal during DR Congo's [round of 32](81667041.md) match against [England](9904.md) on 1 July, but the ball hit the right-side goalpost. DR Congo ultimately lost to England.[^cite_note-45]

## Style of play

Wissa has been described as a player who "fits many different positions", including winger, number 10 and forward.[^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5] He has "pace and power", is "a threat in behind", "has good ability to take on players and create overloads" and "good [pressing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressing_\(association_football_tactic\)) abilities".[^cite_note-SignsBrentford-5] At Brentford, along with [Bryan Mbeumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Mbeumo), he formed a lethal duo with 39 Premier League goals between each other in the 2024/2025 season.[^cite_note-46]

## Personal life

Born in France of [Congolese](76762.md) descent,[^cite_note-47] Wissa can speak the [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala) language.[^cite_note-48] He acquired [French nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nationality_law) in December 2000 through the collective effect of his parents' [naturalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalisation).[^cite_note-49]

Prior to making the decision to concentrate on football at age 15, he also played [rugby union](25405.md).[^cite_note-Dean-9] He was the subject of an [acid attack](30862640.md) in July 2021 and made a full recovery from emergency eye surgery.[^cite_note-\:0-8][^cite_note-\:1-11]

In September 2025 he graduated from the PFA Business School with a Postgraduate Diploma in Global Football Business Management,[^cite_note-50] an award from the [University of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Portsmouth).[^cite_note-51]

Wissa is Practicing Muslim.[^cite_note-52]
