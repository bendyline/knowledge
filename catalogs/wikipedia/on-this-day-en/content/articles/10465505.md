The **National Development and Reform Commission** (**NDRC**) is the third-ranked ministerial-level [cabinet department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_departments_of_the_State_Council) of the [State Council of China](44498.md) and is tasked for [macroeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics) management. Established as the **State Planning Commission**, the NDRC has broad administrative and planning control over the [economy](19284336.md) of [mainland China](55079.md), and has a reputation of being the "mini-state council".[^cite_note-1]

## History

The body was first established in November 1952 as the State Planning Commission of the [Central People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_People's_Government_\(1949%E2%80%931954\)). It was modeled after [Gosplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosplan).[^cite_note-\:0223-2]<sup>: 109 </sup> [Gao Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao_Gang) was its first director.[^cite_note-\:0223-2]<sup>: 109 </sup> In 1954, it was transformed to the State Planning Commission of the People's Republic of China. Three of the NDRC's functions are to study and formulate policies for economic and social development, maintain the balance of economic development, and to guide restructuring of the economic system of mainland China.[^cite_note-NDRC-3]

In March 1998, the commission was renamed into the State Development Planning Commission. It was renamed again in March 2003 to its current name, the National Development and Reform Commission.

In 2005, the NDRC issued a [circular economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_economy)-focused policy document requiring maximization of recycling and reuse of wastewater, exhaust gas, and water residue generated during [mining](58230808.md) and smelting.[^cite_note-\:72-4]<sup>: 12 </sup>

In 2005, the NDRC increased the approved scope of government [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital) at national and local levels.[^cite_note-\:632-5]<sup>: 140 </sup> In 2008,the NDRC supported the creation of state-backed venture funds.[^cite_note-\:632-5]<sup>: 140 </sup>

In 2008, the NDRC issued a set of policies designed to further development the economies of central regions of China, consistent with the [Hu-Wen administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu%E2%80%93Wen_Administration)'s efforts to balance regional development.

In 2016, the NDRC and [Alibaba Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alibaba_Group) signed an agreement to promote rural [e-commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-commerce_in_China) development.[^cite_note-\:Liu-6]<sup>: 129 </sup>

In 2017, the NDRC announced the creation of [China's national carbon emissions trading system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_national_carbon_trading_scheme).[^cite_note-\:922-7]<sup>: 76 </sup>

Prior to 2018, it was also responsible for enforcing China's [antitrust law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antitrust_law), but this function has been transferred to the [State Administration for Market Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Administration_for_Market_Regulation) as part of the [deepening the reform of the Party and state institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepening_the_reform_of_the_Party_and_state_institutions). In February 2015, the NDRC completed an investigation into [Qualcomm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualcomm), finding that violated the Anti-Monopoly Law by imposing unreasonable requirements for patent licensing.[^cite_note-\:4-8] Qualcomm was fined the equivalent of US\$975 million.[^cite_note-\:4-8] Also in 2018, the NDRC's climate policymaking functions were transferred to the newly created [Ministry of Ecology and Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Ecology_and_Environment).[^cite_note-\:0-9]<sup>: 95 </sup>

On 19 December 2020, the NDRC published rules for reviewing foreign investment on national security grounds.[^cite_note-ndrcns-10][^cite_note-bbn19-11] The rules allow government agencies "to preview, deny and punish foreign investment activities in areas that are deemed as important to national security."[^cite_note-bbn19-11] In October 2021, the NDRC published rules restricting private capital in "news-gathering, editing, broadcasting, and distribution."[^cite_note-12]

On 4 September 2023, the NDRC announced it established the Private Economy Development Bureau in order monitor the country's private economy, as well as establish regular communication with private businesses.[^cite_note-13]

## Functions

The NDRC is China's main macroeconomic control institution,[^cite_note-\:02222-14]<sup>: 102 </sup> as well as the top organization in the State Council in matters related to economic policymaking. It oversees the planning system in China, including producing the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md)'s [five-year plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_plans_of_China).[^cite_note-\:022-15] The NDRC has responsibilities over economic targets, price policies, market policies, supply-side structural reform, overseas investment, domestic investment policy, regional development strategies, industrial development strategies, major infrastructure projects, consumption policy, innovation-driven development, scientific and technological infrastructure, high-tech industries, social development, basic public services and social development.[^cite_note-\:022-15] NDRC's responsibility for large infrastructure is intended to prevent the economy from becoming too hot or cold, as well as to address China's overcapacity in production for sectors like aluminum, iron, steel, and energy.[^cite_note-\:02222-14]<sup>: 106 </sup>

The NDRC works with other departments to formulate policies, including drafting laws and regulations.[^cite_note-\:Zhang-16]<sup>: 39 </sup> It monitors Chinese businesses' outbound [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) to ensure they do not invest in blacklisted projects.[^cite_note-\:Chen-17]<sup>: 80 </sup> The NDRC must approve sensitive projects, including projects in countries that do not recognize the People's Republic of China, projects in countries experiencing civil war or other major domestic difficulties, or projects involving sensitive subject matter like cross-border water issues or weapons production.[^cite_note-\:Chen-17]<sup>: 80 </sup> The NDRC maintains the Negative List for Market Access, which identifies the economic sectors which cannot receive private investment.[^cite_note-18]<sup>: 194 </sup>

The NDRC works with the [National Health Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Commission) to research demographic trends and formulate policies on population.[^cite_note-\:022-15] It promotes [sustainable development strategies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy_in_China).[^cite_note-\:Zhang-16]<sup>: 39 </sup> The NDRC is involved in the [foreign aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_foreign_aid) process through coordinating aid to other countries for climate cooperation.[^cite_note-\:Chen-17]<sup>: 73 </sup> The NDRC is also one of the main government agencies responsible for data collection for the [Chinese Social Credit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Credit_System).[^cite_note-19] The NDRC's Social Development Division has a planning role in cultural industries including sports, tourism, and mass media.[^cite_note-\:04-20]<sup>: 100 </sup>

The NDRC manages the General Offices several leading groups, including the [National Defense Mobilization Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Mobilization_Commission), the State Council Leading Group for Western Development, and the State Council Leading Group for the Revitalization of Old Industrial Bases in Northeast China; all of these are led by the premier.[^cite_note-\:022-15] It also hosts the General Offices of the State Council Leading Group for Promoting the Belt and Road Initiative, the Leading Group for Coordinated Development of the [Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing-Jin-Ji) Region, the Leading Group for Promoting the Development of the Yangtze River Economic Belt, the Leading Group for Promoting the Development of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, and the Leading Group for Promoting Comprehensive Deepening of Reform and Opening in Hainan; these are led by the [first-ranking vice premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Premier_of_China), with the NDRC chairman usually being the Office director.[^cite_note-\:022-15]
