***Wireless* Festival** is an annual rap and [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop) music festival, owned and managed by [Live Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Nation_Entertainment), that takes place in [London](17867.md), England. Debuting in 2005, the festival's earlier years primarily featured rock and pop artists; since the 2010s, the focus has shifted largely towards hip-hop and other genres, such as [urban contemporary music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_contemporary_music).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

From its 2005 inception until 2008, the festival was sponsored by telecommunications company [O2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O2_\(brand\)), and was called the *O2 Wireless Festival*. From 2009 to 2012, the main sponsor was [Barclaycard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclaycard), and the festival was renamed to *Barclaycard Wireless Festival*. In 2013, the sponsor changed to [Yahoo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!), thus renaming it to *Yahoo! Wireless*. From 2015 to 2020, the festival's official partners were the carbonated soft drinks [Pepsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi) and [Pepsi Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi_Max). Delivery company [Gopuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopuff) were the official sponsor in 2021 and remained as the sponsor in the 2023 edition of the festival.[^cite_note-4] Sponsorship again changed in 2024, to be partnered with [PepsiCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PepsiCo)'s [Rockstar Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_\(drink\));[^cite_note-5] in 2026, PepsiCo pulled its sponsorship after the controversial booking of the American rapper [Kanye West](523032.md).[^cite_note-6] The 2026 event was later cancelled following the denial of West's [Electronic Travel Authorisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_\(United_Kingdom\)) by the [Home Office](225695.md).[^cite_note-7]

The capacity of the 2023 event was just shy of 50,000 people.[^cite_note-8] Adjacent festivals were held in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds) in 2006 and 2007, as well as [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) in 2014. In 2017, Live Nation also established Wireless Germany in [Frankfurt](10992.md), as well as Wireless Middle East in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md).[^cite_note-9]

The organisers also offered those attending on Friday in Birmingham £20 of "Wireless Credit" which was redeemable against any concessions, merchandise stands and funfair attractions at the event.

### 2015

On 31 January 2015, it was confirmed that the festival would be held in [Finsbury Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finsbury_Park) between 3 and 5 July. The lineup was confirmed via the festival's [Twitter](9988187.md) page, with confirmation that [Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_\(musician\)) would return to the festival following his cancellation in 2014. It was also revealed that [David Guetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Guetta) and [Nicki Minaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicki_Minaj) would perform as co-headliners on the Sunday, and that [Avicii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicii) and [Kendrick Lamar](29909823.md) would be co-headlining on Saturday. As it was the festival's tenth birthday, there was a special 'birthday' event on Sunday, 28 June. Following the announcement of the lineup, several artists cancelled their appearance. [Jess Glynne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jess_Glynne) tweeted a day later to say she would also be cancelling a number of summer dates, including Wireless, due to having to undergo vocal chord surgery. The Friday sold out by the end of April, whilst the other three dates remained on sale until the festival.

### 2025

It was announced that Wireless would take place at Finsbury Park from 11–13 July 2025. On 16 February 2025, [Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_\(musician\)) was announced as the headlining act for all three nights of the event, which commemorated the 20th anniversary of the festival.[^cite_note-30] The rest of the lineup was announced on 8 July, less than a week before the start of the festival, leading to some discontent among fans.[^cite_note-31] Drake's three headlining shows included many guest appearances, including those from [Lauryn Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauryn_Hill), [21 Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_Savage), [Vanessa Carlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Carlton), [Central Cee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Cee), [Dave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_\(rapper\)), [Burna Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burna_Boy) and [Vybz Kartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vybz_Kartel).[^cite_note-32] Drake's Sunday appearance was originally billed as two different performances at 18\:25 and 20\:55, but the first performance was removed from the schedule during the day.[^cite_note-\:1-33] Drake eventually started his performance after Burna Boy finished, but was only able to perform a rushed medley of songs before curfew restrictions forced him to end after 40 minutes.[^cite_note-\:1-33][^cite_note-34]

### 2026

On 30 March 2026, Wireless announced that [Kanye West](523032.md) would be the headlining artist for all three nights of the 2026 festival,[^cite_note-35] marking West's first UK performance since headlining [Glastonbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival_2015).[^cite_note-36] The selection drew criticism from the [Jewish Leadership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Leadership_Council) and London mayor [Sadiq Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_Khan) due to West's [history of antisemitic remarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Views_of_Kanye_West#Antisemitism).[^cite_note-37] On 5 April, [PepsiCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PepsiCo) announced that they were withdrawing sponsorship for the event, citing West's antisemitic comments.[^cite_note-38] The same day, UK prime minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) stated it was "deeply concerning" that West was booked to perform at the festival.[^cite_note-39] On 6 April, other sponsors withdrew support for Wireless, including [PayPal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal),[^cite_note-40] while the [UK government](25318118.md) confirmed a review of West's right to enter the UK, with several [members of parliament](3776360.md) in opposition to West's appearance.[^cite_note-41]

Melvin Benn, managing director of Wireless' organisers [Festival Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_Republic), defended West's planned appearance. Benn argued West's antisemitic remarks were comparable to behaviour he encountered in other individuals with mental illness, attributed it to West's [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder), and stated West considers his previous behaviour "abhorrent".[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-\:0-43] Benn, who lived on a [kibbutz](106200.md) in the 1970s, urged others to "reflect on their instant comments of disgust at the likelihood of \[West] performing (as was mine) and offer some forgiveness and hope to him as I have decided to do".[^cite_note-\:0-43] On 7 April, the [Home Office](225695.md) denied West's [Electronic Travel Authorisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation) application (which was initially approved) to travel to the UK, stating, "\[West's] presence would not be conducive to the public good".[^cite_note-44]

With West barred from entering, and the loss of sponsors, the organisers subsequently cancelled the festival and announced ticket refunds, also stating, "As with every Wireless Festival, multiple stakeholders were consulted in advance of booking \[West] and no concerns were highlighted at the time",[^cite_note-45] with Benn later claiming PepsiCo "signed off and approved" West's appearance.[^cite_note-46]

Later, Festival Republic shared a statement by West, where he wrote, "My only goal is to come to London and present a show of change, bringing unity, peace, and love through my music. I would be grateful for the opportunity to meet with members of the Jewish community in the UK in person, to listen. I know words aren't enough – I'll have to show change through my actions. If you're open, I'm here".[^cite_note-47] The organisers have declined to comment if the Festival will return in 2027.
