The **Australia men's national soccer team** represents [Australia](4689264.md) in international men's [soccer](10568.md). Officially nicknamed the **Socceroos**,[^cite_note-4] the team is controlled by the governing body for [soccer in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_in_Australia), [Football Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Australia), which is affiliated with the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC) and the regional [ASEAN Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Football_Federation) (AFF).

The team played its [first match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_New_Zealand_v_Australia_soccer_match) in 1922 and originally participated in the [Oceania Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) (OFC) from 1966 to 1972, before moving to the AFC from 1973 to 1978 and back to the OFC from 1979 to 2005. In this confederation, Australia won the [OFC Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OFC_Nations_Cup) four times between 1980 and 2004, and dominated many [FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) campaigns during earlier rounds. The team won [31–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_31%E2%80%930_American_Samoa) against the [American Samoa national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa_national_football_team) in a FIFA World Cup qualification match, achieving the world record for the largest victory in a senior international match. Despite this, the team only managed to qualify for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) twice in 11 attempts while in this confederation, because Oceania's single qualification berth always led to an intercontinental playoff, which did not guarantee straight qualification.

In a bid to improve its competitiveness, the team returned to the AFC in 2006, finishing the [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) as runners-up in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup) and winning in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup) as hosts. This victory made Australia the only team in the world to win two different confederation cups.[^cite_note-5] Since switching confederations, the team has maintained a perfect qualification record for the World Cup, earning five consecutive spots for a total of seven appearances. Australia has reached the World Cup knockout stages three times, advancing to this round in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2022](17742072.md) and [2026](36581929.md). Despite its AFF membership, Australia currently does not contest the [ASEAN Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Championship) due to a mutual agreement, though it does compete in youth iterations.

The team additionally represented Australia at the now-defunct [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) four times, winning a silver and bronze medal in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) and [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) respectively. [Mark Schwarzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Schwarzer) has the most caps for Australia, with 109 to his name, while [Tim Cahill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Cahill) has the [most goals scored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_goals_scored_by_Tim_Cahill), with 50. Australia initially developed a [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93New_Zealand_soccer_rivalry) with [New Zealand](1023035.md) and an intercontinental [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry) with [Uruguay](679782.md), and, after returning to the AFC, has developed a [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Japan_football_rivalry) with [Japan](993546.md).

## History

Main article: [History of the Australia men's national soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Australia_men's_national_soccer_team)

### Early years



The first Australia soccer team (wearing light blue shirts) of 1922

The first Australia national team was constituted in 1922 for a tour of New Zealand,[^cite_note-1922_tour-6] which included two defeats and a draw. For the next 36 years, Australia, [New Zealand](1023035.md) and [South Africa](1018598.md) became regular opponents in tour ([exhibition](977090.md)) matches.[^cite_note-Early_history-7] During that period, Australia also competed against [Canada](723688.md) and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_national_football_team) during their tours of Australia in 1924 and 1938 respectively.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Australia recorded their worst ever defeat on 30 June 1951 as they lost 17–0 in a match to a touring [England](9904.md) side.[^cite_note-10] The country had a rare opportunity to compete on the world's stage during the team's first major international tournament as hosts of the [1956 Melbourne Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Summer_Olympics). However, an inexperienced squad proved to be reason for the team's disappointing performance, including a 4–2 loss to India in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-11] With the advent of cheap air travel, Australia began to diversify its range of opponents,[^cite_note-Early_history-7] but its geographical isolation continued to play a role in its destiny for the next 30 years.[^cite_note-Early_history-7] Australia won the [1967 South Vietnam Independence Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_South_Vietnam_Independence_Cup) against seven other nations, but this gained little recognition domestically.[^cite_note-conversation-12]

After failing to qualify for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [1966](61629.md) and [1970](157227.md), losing in play-offs to [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team) and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team) respectively, Australia finally appeared at their first World Cup in [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-Timeline-13] After managing only a draw with [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) and losses to [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) and eventual champions [West Germany](250204.md), the team, which was made up of mostly amateur players, was eliminated at the end of the [first round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup#First_round), finishing last in their group without scoring a goal. It would prove to be the only appearance for the Australian team until the World Cup returned to Germany more than three decades later in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-Timeline-13] Over a 40-year period, the Australian team was known for its near misses in its attempts to qualify for the World Cup; they lost play-offs in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(Africa,_Asia_and_Oceania\)) to [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team), [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC_and_OFC\)) to [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team), [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)) to [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team), [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(OFC%E2%80%93CONMEBOL_play-off\)) to [Argentina](454699.md), [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)) to [Iran](272865.md) and [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(OFC%E2%80%93CONMEBOL_play-off\)) to [Uruguay](679782.md).

### First successes and "golden generation"

While World Cup qualification eluded the team between 1974 and 2006, Australia produced some notable performances against strong European and South American sides. In 1988, Australia defeated reigning world champions Argentina 4–1 in the [Australian Bicentennial Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Bicentenary_Gold_Cup).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Internationals_for_1988-15] In 1997, Australia drew with reigning world champions [Brazil](149286.md) 0–0 in the group stage and then defeated Uruguay 1–0 in the semi-finals to reach the [1997 FIFA Confederations Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_final).[^cite_note-16]

In 2001, after a victory against reigning world champions [France](250197.md) in the group stage, Australia finished the [2001 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in third place after defeating Brazil 1–0 in the third-place match.[^cite_note-17] Australia defeated England 3–1 at [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.)'s [Boleyn Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boleyn_Ground) in 2003 as [Wayne Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Rooney) made his international debut.[^cite_note-18]

In early 2005, it was reported that [Football Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Australia) had entered into discussions to join the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC) and end an almost 40-year association with the [Oceania Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) (OFC).[^cite_note-19] Many commentators and fans, including soccer broadcaster and former Australian captain [Johnny Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Warren) (deceased in 2004), felt that the only way for Australia to progress was to abandon Oceania.[^cite_note-20] On 13 March, the AFC executive committee made a unanimous decision to invite Australia to join the AFC.[^cite_note-AFC_move-21] After the OFC executive committee unanimously endorsed Australia's proposed move, FIFA approved the move on 30 June 2005.[^cite_note-AFC_move-21] Australia joined Asia, with the move taking effect on 1 January 2006, though until then, Australia had to compete for a [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) position as an OFC member country.[^cite_note-AFC_move-21]

After a successful [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(OFC\)), the team took the first steps towards qualification for the 2006 World Cup.[^cite_note-Hiddink_as_coach-22] After coach [Frank Farina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Farina) stood down from the position after Australia's dismal performance at the [2005 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), [Guus Hiddink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guus_Hiddink) was announced as the new national coach.[^cite_note-Hiddink_as_coach-22] Australia, ranked 49th, would then have to play the 18th ranked [Uruguay](679782.md) in a rematch of the [2001 qualification play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(OFC%E2%80%93CONMEBOL_play-off\)) for a spot in the 2006 FIFA World Cup. After a 5–0 [friendly](977090.md) win against [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_football_team),[^cite_note-23] the first leg of the [play-off tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)) was lost (1–0), with the return leg still to be played in Australia four days later in Sydney on 16 November 2005.[^cite_note-Australia_VS_Uruguay_2006-24]



Australia playing [Uruguay](679782.md) at [Stadium Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_Australia) to determine the last qualifying spot for the 2006 FIFA World Cup.

The second leg of the qualifying play-off was played in front of a crowd of 82,698 at [Stadium Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_Australia).[^cite_note-25] Australia led Uruguay 1–0 after 90 minutes following a goal by [Mark Bresciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Bresciano) in the first half. The aggregate was tied, and extra time was played. Neither team scored after two periods of extra time, bringing the game to a penalty shootout. Australia won the penalty shootout 4–2, making them the first-ever team to qualify for a World Cup via a penalty shootout.[^cite_note-26] Australian goalkeeper [Mark Schwarzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Schwarzer) made two saves, with [John Aloisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Aloisi) scoring the winning penalty for a place in the World Cup, Australia's first qualification in 32 years.[^cite_note-Australia_VS_Uruguay_2006-24]

Australia went into the 2006 World Cup as the second lowest-ranked side. Although their ranking vastly improved in subsequent months after a series of exhibition matches against high-profile teams, including a 1–1 draw against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), and a 1–0 win at the sold-out 100,000 capacity [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground) against the then [European champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_national_football_team)[^cite_note-27] for the 2006 World Cup, Australia was placed into [Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F), along with [Japan](993546.md), [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) and defending champions Brazil. In their opening group game, Australia defeated Japan 3–1, with [Tim Cahill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Cahill) scoring two goals (84', 89') and [John Aloisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Aloisi) scoring one (90+2') in the last eight minutes. The goals were the first ever scored by Australia's men's soccer team in a World Cup and it was also the first victory of an Oceanian team in the tournament's history, as well as all three goals being scored in the last seven minutes of the game, which was never before done in a World Cup match.[^cite_note-28] Australia met Brazil in their second group game, which Australia lost to Brazil 2–0. The Socceroos faced Croatia in their third match. The final score (2–2) was enough to see Australia proceed to the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage), where they were eliminated from the competition after a 1–0 defeat by the eventual champions [Italy](362466.md) after conceding a controversial penalty in the 93rd minute.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] The loss marked the official end of Hiddink's tenure as Australia's national coach.[^cite_note-31] The success achieved at the 2006 World Cup later saw [the team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#Australia) named [AFC National Team of the Year](715249.md#Team_of_the_Year),[^cite_note-32] as well as being dubbed the "[golden generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_generation)" in the history of the Socceroos.[^cite_note-33]

### Later success

Led by coach [Graham Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Arnold), Australia went to their first [AFC Asian Cup in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup), sending a strong squad which included 15 players from the [previous year's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) team. In [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup#Group_A) they played against [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) (a 1–1 draw), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_national_football_team) (a 4–0 win) and eventual champions [Iraq](1039652.md) (a 3–1 loss), assuring Australia's progression to the quarter-final stage of the tournament. After drawing 1–1 with Japan with 120 minutes played, Australia exited the tournament on penalties at the quarter-final stage. An international friendly on 11 September 2007 against Argentina (a 1–0 loss) was Graham Arnold's last game as head coach, with the position eventually being filled by [Pim Verbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pim_Verbeek) on 6 December 2007.[^cite_note-34]

Australia began their [2010 FIFA World Cup campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) in the [third round of qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round), drawn into a group consisting of [Qatar](1111350.md), Iraq and [China PR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team), in which Australia finished first. Australia eventually saw progression through to the [tournament in South Africa](168079.md) after comfortably winning the [fourth round of qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round) in a group consisting of Japan, [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team), Qatar and [Uzbekistan](1032413.md).[^cite_note-35] Australia's qualification was already assured before the final two games, finally topping [its group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round#Group_A) ahead of Japan by five points.



Australia against [Germany](250204.md) in [Moses Mabhida Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Mabhida_Stadium), at the 2010 FIFA World Cup

Australia was drawn into [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_D) in the World Cup, which featured three-time world champion Germany, [Ghana](1029620.md) and [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team). On 14 June, Australia faced Germany. Pim Verbeek's surprising decision to play without a recognised striker saw Australia comprehensively defeated 4–0. Verbeek received heavy criticism for his tactics,[^cite_note-36] with [SBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Broadcasting_Service) (Australia's World Cup broadcaster) chief soccer analyst [Craig Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Foster) calling for his immediate sacking.[^cite_note-37] Australia's second group match against Ghana resulted in a 1–1 draw, and their third and final group match against Serbia resulted in a 2–1 win. Ultimately, Australia were eliminated on goal difference behind Ghana. Pim Verbeek completed his term as Australian coach at the end of the 2010 World Cup and was soon replaced by [Holger Osieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Osieck).[^cite_note-38]

In 2010, Australia qualified for their second [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup), topping their [qualification group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification#Group_B). A successful campaign at the [2011 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup) saw Australia become runners-up to Japan, after losing in the [Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_final) 1–0 in extra time.[^cite_note-39]

In 2012, Australia agreed to compete in the [East Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Football_Championship).[^cite_note-40] Australia travelled to Hong Kong to compete in a series of qualification matches with the hopes of qualifying for the [2013 East Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_EAFF_East_Asian_Cup). Despite handing several debuts and fielding an in-experienced squad, Australia was successful, finishing ahead of [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_national_football_team), North Korea, [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam_national_football_team) and [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_football_team) to progress to the 2013 East Asian Cup, where Australia eventually finished last behind Japan, [South Korea](1018627.md) and China PR.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] On 26 August 2013, Australia became full members of the [ASEAN Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Football_Federation)[^cite_note-43] but as part of their entrance agreement with the sub-confederation, their national team is barred from participating in the [AFF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFF_Championship) due to their perceived wide gap in playing standards between Australia and the rest of the region.[^cite_note-entrance2020-44]



Australia against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) in [Arena Pantanal, Cuiabá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Pantanal), at the 2014 FIFA World Cup.

&#x20;Australia's [2014 FIFA World Cup qualification campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) began with a series of friendlies against the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) (0–0), Germany (a 2–1 win), New Zealand (a 3–0 win), Serbia (0–0) and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) (a 2–1 win).[^cite_note-45] Australia's World Cup campaign started in the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round) of qualification, with Australia topping [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round#Group_D) to progress to the [fourth round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round). After winning their last fourth round-game, Australia finished as runners-up in [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round#Group_B), qualifying for the [finals tournament in Brazil](656933.md) on 18 June 2013.[^cite_note-46]

Shortly after achieving qualification to the World Cup, Australia played a series of friendly matches against Brazil and France, suffering consecutive 6–0 defeats. This along with previous poor performances during the 2014 World Cup qualification campaign resulted in manager Holger Osieck's sacking, bringing his three-year tenure as Australia's manager to an end.[^cite_note-Osieck_sacking-47]

### New generation: the 2015 AFC Asian Cup triumph



Australia match against [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team) in [2015 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup) opening match

After a two-week search for a new manager, [Ange Postecoglou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ange_Postecoglou) was eventually appointed in the position.[^cite_note-48] Postecoglou was tasked with regenerating the Australian national team, which was deemed to have been too reliant on members of their Golden Generation of 2006, subsequently leading to a stagnation of results, culminating in successive 6–0 defeats to Brazil and France.[^cite_note-49] In his first game as Australia's manager, a home friendly match against [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team), Australia won 1–0 courtesy of a goal from Tim Cahill.[^cite_note-50]

At the World Cup, Australia were drawn in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B) alongside [defending world champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_final) [Spain](609825.md), 2010 runners-up [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) and [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team).[^cite_note-51] Their first match was off to a lacklustre start, having conceded two goals in the opening 15 minutes from [Alexis Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_S%C3%A1nchez) and [Jorge Valdivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Valdivia). Despite a goal from Tim Cahill that inspired a late resurgence from Postecoglou's team, they ultimately lost to Chile 3–1. Their second match against the Netherlands was a close one, with the Socceroos giving up a 2–1 lead, eventually resulting in a 3–2 loss, thus earning their early exit along with the Spanish. In the end, Australia finished Group B with a third consecutive defeat to Spain, 3–0. Australia's competitive World Cup performances in a difficult group led to believe that a new Golden Generation was about to begin.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

In their first international match after the World Cup, Australia played 2014 quarter-finalists [Belgium](679716.md) in [Liège](28341707.md), with Australia going down 2–0. Four days later, Australia achieved their first international win in ten months, and just their second win under Ange Postecoglou, with a 3–2 victory over [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) in [London](17867.md). After drawing against the United Arab Emirates, and suffering successive losses against Qatar and Japan, combined with previous poor results earlier in the year, Australia slipped to 94 and 102 in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings), their lowest-ever ranking.[^cite_note-54]



Australia playing in the [2015 AFC Asian Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_final) where they would go on to lift the cup after defeating [South Korea](1018627.md) 2–1 in extra time

The new year saw Australia host the [2015 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup), with the team making their third consecutive appearance in the tournament. Australia won their first two [group matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_A) against [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team) and Oman comfortably, with scorelines of 4–1 and 4–0 respectively. This guaranteed their qualification for the knockout stage, despite losing their final group match against South Korea in Brisbane 1–0.[^cite_note-55] They faced China PR in the quarter-finals and won 2–0, courtesy of a second-half brace from Tim Cahill. In the semi-finals, Australia won 2–0 over the United Arab Emirates and advanced to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_Final) for the second time in a row. They faced South Korea in the final on 31 January at [Stadium Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_Australia), winning 2–1 after extra time to claim their first Asian title and qualify for the [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

After Australia qualified for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in November 2017, [Ange Postecoglou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ange_Postecoglou) resigned from his position as coach;[^cite_note-58] and former manager of the Netherlands national team, [Bert van Marwijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_van_Marwijk), was subsequently appointed as his replacement.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] On 8 March 2018, after van Marwijk's first squad announcement, the FFA announced that [Graham Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Arnold) will take the coaching role from after the 2018 World Cup until the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md).[^cite_note-62]

With van Marwijk, Australia was [grouped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C) with [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team), [France](250197.md) and [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team). The Socceroos' first group match against eventual [world champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_final) France was praised by a valiant effort, in which Australia only lost 1–2 by a virtual own goal from [Aziz Behich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aziz_Behich).[^cite_note-63] After the defeat to France, Australia produced another outstanding performance, drawing Denmark 1–1.[^cite_note-64] However, in the crucial match against already eliminated Peru, Australia lost 0–2 and crashed out from the World Cup with only a point, becoming the only team from the AFC to be winless in the 2018 World Cup.[^cite_note-65] Subsequently, van Marwijk left his post and Arnold replaced him as the new coach of the Socceroos.

Under [Graham Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Arnold), Australia started their [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) in hope of defending the title, being [grouped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_B) with [Jordan](1090065.md), Syria and Palestine, but their hope was shattered by a shocking 0–1 defeat to Jordan.[^cite_note-66] Australia soon returned to the race by beating Palestine 3–0[^cite_note-67] before winning an important encounter with a hard-fought 3–2 win over Syria,[^cite_note-68] eliminating both Palestine and Syria in the process. The win gave Australia to qualify for the round of sixteen, where they overcame [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) after winning on penalties 4–2, having drawn 0–0 for 120 minutes.[^cite_note-69] In the quarter-finals, however, in the [Hazza bin Zayed Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazza_bin_Zayed_Stadium), where Australia had lost their opening match against Jordan, Australia once again failed to register a win in the same ground, losing to the host United Arab Emirates 0–1 due to a mistake from [Miloš Degenek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Degenek), eventually failing to defend the title.[^cite_note-70]

### 2022 FIFA World Cup resurgence

Australia took part in the [2022 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)), which they entered in the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round), in which they faced [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team), Jordan, [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_national_football_team) and [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_football_team).[^cite_note-71] Australia dominated the group with eight wins out of eight to reach the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round), where it faced [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), [Japan](993546.md), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team), [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) and [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team).[^cite_note-72] After a start with three straight wins over China, Vietnam and Oman, Australia then won only one game, against Vietnam, in their final seven matches, being held thrice and losing three more, finishing third in the group. It then had to rely on [fourth round playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round).[^cite_note-73] Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), all of Australia's playoffs were centralised in [Doha](26214389.md). The Socceroos began their quest with a 2–1 win over the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), to face fifth place [CONMEBOL qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)) finisher [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team), a rematch of the [2018 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C).[^cite_note-74] This time around, Australia held Peru goalless, before winning on penalties to qualify for the FIFA World Cup in Qatar.

Australia was drawn in [group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_D) along with [France](250197.md), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and [Tunisia](1029953.md).[^cite_note-75] Australia's qualification also meant that the [Asian confederation](715249.md) had the largest number of teams in their FIFA World Cup history, with six countries qualifying.[^cite_note-76]



Australia before their match against [France](250197.md) in the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) opening match

As preparation for the 2022 FIFA World Cup, Australia played two friendlies against neighbouring [New Zealand](1023035.md), winning both games.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] It began its World Cup quest on 23 November against France, losing 4–1 after initially taking the lead with goal from [Craig Goodwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Goodwin).[^cite_note-79] Three days later Australia registered its first World Cup win since [2010](168079.md), overcoming Tunisia with a header from [Mitchell Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Duke) to seal a 1–0 win, sending Australia from bottom to second place.[^cite_note-80] Four days later, against [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) semi-finalists Denmark, Australia won 1–0, thanks to a [Mathew Leckie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathew_Leckie) goal. Australia finished the group stage in second place behind France on goal difference, making Australia the first Asian representative to reach the [knockout stage in Qatar 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage).[^cite_note-81] Australia's resurgence in the group stage was widely watched and followed by Australian supporters. Mass celebrations occurred after the upsets over Tunisia and Denmark, and Prime Minister [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) called the achievement "magnificent".[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] In the round of 16, Australia lost 2–1 to [Argentina](454699.md), with [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) opening the scoring and [Julián Álvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_%C3%81lvarez_\(footballer\)) getting the second after dispossessing [Mathew Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathew_Ryan). Australia pulled one back when Goodwin's shot deflected into goal off [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez_\(footballer,_born_2001\)) and [Garang Kuol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garang_Kuol) almost scored the equaliser in injury time, but his shot was smothered by Argentinian goalkeeper [Emiliano Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiliano_Mart%C3%ADnez).[^cite_note-85]

### 2026 World Cup qualification

On 20 September 2024, Graham Arnold announced he was stepping down as head coach, two games into the third round of [2026 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round).[^cite_note-86] The Socceroos had only picked up one point from two matches. On 23 September 2024, Football Australia's [James Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Johnson_\(sports_administrator\)) announced that [Tony Popovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Popovic) had been appointed as head coach.[^cite_note-87] Popovic led the Socceroos to wins over Japan and Saudi Arabia, and Australia eventually ensured direct qualification to the FIFA World Cup for the first time since 2014, and their sixth consecutive World Cup appearance.[^cite_note-88]

Australia played their first match of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) against [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) on 13 June 2026. The match was played at [BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place), [Vancouver](32706.md), [British Columbia](3392.md).[^cite_note-89] Goals from [Nestory Irankunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestory_Irankunda) and [Connor Metcalfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Metcalfe), as well as a clean sheet and eight saves from surprise starting goalkeeper, [Patrick Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Beach), sealed an upset 2-0 victory for Australia.[^cite_note-90]

### Media coverage

Australian matches are broadcast by [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+#Sports_programming) and on [free-to-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-to-air) by [Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10) until 2028, with the exception during the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) finals tournament matches that still aired live and free on [SBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Broadcasting_Service).[^cite_note-91]

Previous coverage has been provided by [Fox Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_\(Australia\)) until 2021.

The national team has set multiple ratings records for both subscription and free-to-air television. Australia's final [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) qualifying match against [Uruguay](679782.md) was the highest rating program in SBS history with an audience of 3.4 million viewers,[^cite_note-92] while a [2010 World Cup](168079.md) qualifying match against [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) set a record for the highest subscription television audience, with an average of 431,000 viewers.[^cite_note-93] The [2015 AFC Asian Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_final) against [South Korea](1018627.md) had a total reach of 5.3 million Australians overall.[^cite_note-94]

### Kit

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Australia national soccer team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Australia%20national%20soccer%20team%20kits).

For the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), the home shirt is mostly gold, with green accents including around the neck and underarm/sides. The logos and player numbers are also in green. The home shorts are a light to dark green gradient, with logos and numbers in gold. The home socks are white. The away kit is mostly blue-green with a gradient into coral at the shoulder level, and the logos and player numbers are white. The away shorts and socks are the same blue-green colour. There is also white ribbing on both home and away kits.[^cite_note-\:0-95][^cite_note-96]

The previous Socceroos kit from 2025 was designed by Reko Rennie, a [First Nations](12598742.md) artist.[^cite_note-97] This kit is still used across Australia's other national football teams, including the [Matildas](30864460.md).[^cite_note-\:0-95] The home shirt is predominantly yellow and light green chevron patterning. The shorts are a solid dark green, along with the collar, side, and cuffs of the shirt. They are usually worn with white socks. The away kit is predominantly black, with a light green collar and cuffs on the shirt. It is also adorned with pink and green chevron patterning on its sides.[^cite_note-98]

&#x20;Australia's jersey traditionally features the [coat of arms of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Australia) over the left breast, however the fan replica versions show a slightly modified version with the Football Australia logo rather than the shield.[^cite_note-99]















Australia's first national kit, 1922

Australia's first kit was sky blue with a maroon hoop on the socks, the colours representing the states of [New South Wales](21654.md) and [Queensland](59051.md), a look that was reminiscent of the [Australian national rugby league team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_rugby_league_team)'s strips of the period.[^cite_note-100] They wore the predominantly light blue kit until 1924 when they changed to [green and gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_colours_of_Australia), Australia's national colours.[^cite_note-101]

Australia has worn a yellow jersey, usually accompanied by green shorts, and yellow socks since the 1960s. The colour of the socks altered throughout the 1970s, 1980s, and 1990s from white to the same green as the shorts to the same yellow colour as the jersey. The team first wore the traditional green and yellow colours in 1924.[^cite_note-evening_news-soccer-102]

Australia's kits have been produced by manufacturers including [Umbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbro), [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas), KingRoo, and since 2004 by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.).[^cite_note-103] Australia's 1974 World Cup kits were produced by Adidas as were all other national team kits in the tournament, with Adidas sponsoring the event. The kits, however, contained Umbro branding, due to the manufacturer's Australian partnership at the time.[^cite_note-104] Nike renewed the kit manufacturer deal with FFA for another 11 years in 2012, handing them the rights to make national team kits until 2022.[^cite_note-105] In the lead-up to the 2014 World Cup, the new kits to be worn by the team were revealed. The design of the new kits included a plain yellow shirt with a green collar, plain dark green shorts and white socks, a tribute to the 1974 Socceroos. Inside the back of the neck also had woven the quote, "We Socceroos can do the impossible", from [Peter Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Wilson_\(footballer,_born_1947\)), the captain of the 1974 Australian team.[^cite_note-106] This kit was well received. In March 2016, FFA revealed the new Socceroos kit, which featured a yellow jersey, yellow shorts and green socks. This was reportedly in accordance with a FIFA directive, instructing all national teams to have matching shirts and shorts.[^cite_note-People_weren’t_impressed_with_the_new_Socceroos_kit-107][^cite_note-Why_everybody_lost_their_minds_over_a_pair_of_yellow_shorts-108] This kit was met with wide public contention, primarily due to the colour change of the shorts from the traditional green to yellow.[^cite_note-People_weren’t_impressed_with_the_new_Socceroos_kit-107][^cite_note-Why_everybody_lost_their_minds_over_a_pair_of_yellow_shorts-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

### Nickname



Socceroos livery on a [Qantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas) [Boeing 747–400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747%E2%80%93400)

Australia's nickname, "Socceroos", was [coined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) in 1967 by Sydney journalist Tony Horstead in his coverage of the team on a [goodwill tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Quoc_Khanh_Cup) to [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam) during the [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-113] It is commonly used by both the Australian people and the governing body, the FFA.[^cite_note-114] The nickname represents a cultural propensity for the use of colloquialisms [in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Australia). It also represents the [Australian English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_English_vocabulary) use of [the sport's name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_for_association_football).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

The name itself is similar to most other [Australian national representative sporting team nicknames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_national_sports_team_nicknames); used informally when referring to the team, in the media or in conversation. Similarly, the name is derived from a well-known symbol of Australia, in this case, the [kangaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangaroo). The words *soccer* and *kangaroo* are combined into a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) word as *soccer-roo*; such as *Olyroos* for the [Australia Olympic soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_men's_national_under-23_soccer_team)[^cite_note-117] or *Hockeyroos* for the [Australian national women's hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_field_hockey_team).

### Underdog mentality

As is the case with many national sports teams from [Australia](4689264.md), the Socceroos and their supporters tend to embrace the [underdog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underdog) status when facing opposition that is perceived by onlookers to be a higher quality opponent that should defeat the Australians.[^cite_note-118] The underdog mentality is considered a defining characteristic of the Australian national soccer team and there have been numerous famous examples of the Socceroos pulling off upset wins that were inspired by being underestimated by opposition players, coaches, fans or the media.[^cite_note-119]

In February 2003, [England](9904.md) coach [Sven Goran-Eriksson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_Goran-Eriksson) had announced in the lead up to a friendly match against the Socceroos that he would rest his star players including [David Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Beckham), [Michael Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Owen), [Frank Lampard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lampard), [Steven Gerrard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Gerrard) and [Paul Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Scholes) at half-time, which the Australians considered disrespectful.[^cite_note-120] The Aussies would shock England by scoring twice in the first half while England remained scoreless with their full strength team on the park and the game ended in a 3–1 win for the Socceroos in what would be considered one of Australia's greatest ever international victories.[^cite_note-121]

In November 2005, [Uruguyan](679782.md) star striker [Álvaro Recoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Recoba) stated that Uruguay had a "divine right" to play in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) ahead of the Socceroos when asked to comment on the upcoming second leg of the [2006 FIFA World Cup qualification inter-confederation play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)) with Australia.[^cite_note-122] Despite leading the play-off after a 1–0 win in the first leg, Uruguay would lose the second leg match 1–0 and would ultimately lose the subsequent penalty shootout 4–2 that sent Australia through to its first World Cup appearance in 32 years.[^cite_note-123]

In June 2026, [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) captain [Hakan Çalhanoğlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakan_%C3%87alhano%C4%9Flu) stated that he believed Turkey would "dominate" Australia in their Group D match at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) because they "have more qualities and a more talented team".[^cite_note-124] Turkish star [Arda Güler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arda_G%C3%BCler) was then overheard telling his teammates "we are much better" than the Socceroos in the tunnel as the two teams were about to run out for the match.[^cite_note-125] Australia would defeat the highly fancied Turkey team 2–0 in the match in one of its best ever World Cup performances[^cite_note-126] and goalscorer [Nestory Irankunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestory_Irankunda) revealed post-match that they used the pre-match comments from the Turkish players as motivation and stated that "they did their talking, but they couldn’t back it up on the field."[^cite_note-127]

### Naming rights and sponsorship

The team has been branded the "CommBank Socceroos" since 1 September 2025, after [Commonwealth Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Bank) (who also have the naming rights for the [women's national team](30864460.md)) took over the naming rights from restaurant chain [Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subway_\(restaurant\)), whose previous deal started in 2022.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

Previously, they were known as the "Caltex Socceroos" under the sponsorship of [Caltex Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampol) from 2017 to 2019.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] During the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md), some of Caltex's service stations were re-branded as "Cahilltex" as a nod to Socceroos player [Tim Cahill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Cahill).[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

Football Australia has been sponsored by [Qantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas) as its official airline since 2004.[^cite_note-134]

### Rivalries



One of the matches of the 2006 World Cup was a group stage match between Australia and Japan at [Fritz-Walter-Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz-Walter-Stadion) in [Kaiserslautern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiserslautern), Germany.

#### New Zealand

Main article: [Australia–New Zealand soccer rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93New_Zealand_soccer_rivalry)

Australia's longtime rivals are [trans-Tasman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Tasman) neighbours [New Zealand](1023035.md).[^cite_note-135] The two teams' history dates back to 1922, where they first met in both their international debuts. The rivalry between the Socceroos and the *All Whites* (New Zealand) is part of a [wider friendly rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93New_Zealand_relations) between the neighbours Australia and New Zealand, which applies not only to sport but to the culture of the two countries. The rivalry was intensified when Australia and New Zealand were both members of the OFC, regularly competing in [OFC Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OFC_Nations_Cup) finals and in [FIFA World Cup qualifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), where only one team from the OFC progressed to the World Cup. Since Australia joined the AFC in 2006, competition between the two teams has been infrequent, with the occasional match still receiving much media and public attention.[^cite_note-136]

#### Japan

Main article: [Australia–Japan football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Japan_football_rivalry)

After joining the AFC, Australia began to develop a fierce rivalry with fellow Asian powerhouse [Japan](993546.md).[^cite_note-137] The rivalry began at the 2006 FIFA World Cup, where the two countries were grouped together. The rivalry continued with the two countries meeting regularly in various AFC competitions, including the [2011 Asian Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_Final) and qualification for the 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022, and 2026 World Cups.[^cite_note-138]

#### South Korea

Main article: [Australia–South Korea football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93South_Korea_football_rivalry)

Another major rival within Asia is [South Korea](1018627.md), who Australia came up against in three World Cup qualification campaigns in the 1970s and, since joining the AFC, have met regularly, including the [2015 Asian Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-caltexsocceroos-139]

#### Uruguay

Main article: [Australia–Uruguay football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry)

A rivalry exists with [Uruguay](679782.md) since their first meeting on the eve of the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup). Both nations have faced each other in consecutive FIFA World Cup play-offs in 2001 and 2005 with each nation winning a playoff final each to progress through to the World Cup. Australia and Uruguay also faced off in the [1997 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in Saudi Arabia, with Australia progressing through to the final against [Brazil](149286.md) via a [golden goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal) winner from [Harry Kewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Kewell).[^cite_note-140]

### Supporters

The main [supporter group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporters'_groups) of the Australian national team is **Australian Active Support** (AAS).[^cite_note-141] AAS, then known as **Socceroos Active Support** (SAS), was founded in January 2015 as an independent group,[^cite_note-SASINFO-142] who uses social media to organise and keep in touch. This replaced the former active support group **Terrace Australis**,[^cite_note-143] who were founded by Football Federation Australia and fans in 2013, during Australia's 2014 World Cup qualification campaign.[^cite_note-144] Its establishment came in the wake of poor off-field action and minimal community engagement.[^cite_note-145] Previously, the emergence of Terrace Australis saw the **Green and Gold Army** relinquish its role as a hub for active support, which it had claimed since its establishment in 2001.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] Since the 2015 AFC Asian Cup triumph, the supporters had encouraged people in Australia to focus more on the national team, and the nation's soccer pride.

### Home stadium

Australia does not have a dedicated [national stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_stadium); instead, the team plays at different venues throughout the country for exhibition or tournament purposes. In recent years, major international matches have usually been rotated around various large grounds, including [Stadium Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_Australia) in Sydney, [Hunter Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Stadium) in Newcastle and [Docklands Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docklands_Stadium) in Melbourne. International matches have also been played at the [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground) and [Melbourne Rectangular Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Rectangular_Stadium) in Melbourne and [Canberra Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra_Stadium) in Canberra.

Australia has played at [the Gabba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gabba) in Brisbane, which hosted Australia's first international match on home soil in 1923, a 2–1 win over New Zealand. It was the fourth Australian team match overall, with the first three internationals played in New Zealand.[^cite_note-148] Other venues which regularly hosted international home matches included [Olympic Park Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Park_Stadium_\(Melbourne\)) in Melbourne, the [Sydney Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Cricket_Ground), [Sydney Sports Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Sports_Ground), [Sydney Showground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Showground_\(Moore_Park\)), and [Sydney Football Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Football_Stadium_\(1988\)) in Sydney, and [Subiaco Oval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subiaco_Oval) in Perth.

#### Games in England

Since the 2003 friendly against England, the *Socceroos* have also played a significant number of games in England, especially London, since a high proportion of the senior team play in European leagues. This includes games at [Madejski Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madejski_Stadium) in Reading ([Reading Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_F.C.)'s home ground) in 2003 against Jamaica, [Loftus Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loftus_Road) in [Shepherd's Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd's_Bush) ([Queens Park Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Park_Rangers_F.C.)' home ground) in 2004 and 2008 against South Africa, 2006 against Ghana, and 2007 against Denmark.

[Craven Cottage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craven_Cottage) in [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham) ([Fulham Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.)'s home ground) hosted Australia's matches against Norway in 2004, New Zealand and Jamaica in 2005. Craven Cottage also hosted Australia's matches against Jamaica in 2007, 2013 against Canada, 2014 against Saudi Arabia, and 2018 against Colombia, while Australia played at [The Den](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Den) in [Bermondsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermondsey) ([Millwall Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.)'s home ground) in 2014 against Ecuador, and [Brentford Community Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_Community_Stadium) in [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford) ([Brentford Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C.)'s home ground) in 2023 against New Zealand.

Australia currently hold the world record for the [largest win and the most goals scored by a player in an international match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_31%E2%80%930_American_Samoa).[^cite_note-Telegraph-151] Both records were achieved during the [2002 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) match against [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa_national_football_team) on 11 April 2001. Australia won [31–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_31%E2%80%930_American_Samoa) with [Archie Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archie_Thompson) scoring 13 goals and [David Zdrilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Zdrilic) scoring eight.[^cite_note-Telegraph-151][^cite_note-152] Two days before the 31–0 win, Australia broke the record for largest win with a [22–0 win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga_0%E2%80%9322_Australia) over [Tonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga_national_football_team).[^cite_note-Tonga_routed-153] With 13 and 8 goals respectively, both Thompson and Zdrilic broke the previous record jointly held by another Australian, [Gary Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Cole_\(footballer\)), who scored seven goals against [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji_national_football_team) in 1981,[^cite_note-154] and Iranian [Karim Bagheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Bagheri), who also scored seven goals against [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_national_football_team) in 1997.[^cite_note-155]
