The **Armenian diaspora** are the communities of [Armenians](387816.md) outside the [Armenian highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands). Since antiquity, Armenians have established communities in many regions throughout the world. The Armenian diaspora is one of the oldest and largest diasporas in the world,[^cite_note-Aditya_Anshu-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Marcello_Mollica,_Arsen_Hakobyan-3] with the oldest community being the [Armenian Quarter of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Quarter).

The modern Armenian diaspora was largely formed as a result of the [genocide](140376.md) committed by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), which forced [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire) in [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia) to flee.[^cite_note-RHansen-4][^cite_note-5] From that point forward, Armenians became fundamentally a diaspora nation, with a majority of their people living outside their [ancestral homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands).[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-7]

Another wave of emigration from [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) occurred in the 1990s amid the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union),[^cite_note-\:8-8][^cite_note-Astghik_Chaloyan-9][^cite_note-Jerry_L._Johnson-10] the [Turkish-Azeri blockade of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish%E2%80%93Azeri_blockade_of_Armenia), and an [energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_Armenian_energy_crisis). The [High Commissioner for Diaspora Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioner_for_Diaspora_Affairs_\(Armenia\)) established in 2019 is in charge of coordinating and developing Armenia's relations with the diaspora. The vast majority of ethnic Armenians worldwide do not live in the [Republic of Armenia](10918072.md),[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] and this has led to [repatriation campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repatriation_of_Armenians).

## Terminology

In [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language), the diaspora is referred to as **spyurk** (pronounced [\[spʰʏrkʰ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Armenian)), spelled սփիւռք in [classical orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Armenian_orthography) and սփյուռք in [reformed orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_orthography_reform).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In the past, the word **gaghut** ([գաղութ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D5%A3%D5%A1%D5%B2%D5%B8%D6%82%D5%A9) pronounced [\[ɡɑˈʁutʰ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Armenian)) was used mostly to refer to the Armenian communities outside the [Armenian homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highlands). It is borrowed from the Aramaic (Classical Syriac) cognate[^cite_note-15] of Hebrew *[galut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galut)* (גלות).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

[Armenians in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Turkey), such as [Hrant Dink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrant_Dink), do not consider themselves a part of the Armenian Diaspora, since they have been living in their historical homeland for more than four thousand years.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] They are not considered part of the diaspora either by the [Minister of Diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Diaspora) [Hranush Hakobyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hranush_Hakobyan): "Diaspora represents all the Armenians who live beyond the [Armenian Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands). In this context, we have singled out the Armenians of Istanbul and those living on the territory of [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia). Those people have inhabited the lands for thousands of years, and they are not considered Diaspora."[^cite_note-20]

## History

The Armenian diaspora has been present for over 1,700 years.[^cite_note-21] The Armenian diaspora is divided into two communities – the communities from [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) (or [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia)) and the communities from the Caucasus or [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) (Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Iran, and other communities from the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)). Some Armenians in [Crimea](163045.md) and in the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) switched to [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) [Armeno-Kipchak language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armeno-Kipchak_language).

The modern Armenian diaspora was largely formed during [World War I](4764461.md) as a result of the [Armenian genocide](140376.md).[^cite_note-\:1-6] According to [Randall Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randall_Hansen), "Both in the past and today, the Armenian communities around the world have developed in significantly different ways within the constraints and opportunities found in varied host cultures and countries."[^cite_note-RHansen-4]

In the fourth century, Armenian communities already existed outside [Greater Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)). [Diasporic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) Armenian communities emerged in the [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire) empires, and they also defended the eastern and northern borders of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_of_Diasporas-22] In order to populate the less populated areas of Byzantium, Armenians were relocated to those regions. Some Armenians converted to Greek Orthodoxy while retaining [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) as their primary language, whereas others remained in the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) despite pressure from official authorities. A growing number of Armenians migrated to [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia) during the course of the eleventh and twelfth centuries as a result of the Seljuk Turk invasions. After the fall of the kingdom to the [Mamelukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamelukes) and loss of Armenian statehood in 1375, up to 150,000 went to [Cyprus](5593.md), [the Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Balkans), and [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_of_Diasporas-22] The Armenian diaspora is also notable for its historical [mercantile communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_merchantry) throughout Asia in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) and in the Early Modern Period, in countries such as China, India, and Iran, many of whom rose to high positions within the various Asian royal courts.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Although an Armenian diaspora existed during [Antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Antiquity) and the Middle Ages, it grew in size due to emigration from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Russia](25391.md), and the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus).

Before 1870, 60 Armenian immigrants settled in New England.[^cite_note-\:0-25] Armenian immigration rose to 1,500 by the end of the 1880s, and rose to 2,500 in the mid-1890s due to massacres caused by the Ottoman Empire. Armenians who immigrated to the United States before WWI were primarily from Asia Minor and settled on the East Coast.[^cite_note-\:0-25]



Routes of Armenian refugees during the 1920s and 1930s, including the exodus from the Hatay province.

The Armenian diaspora grew considerably both during and after the [First World War](4764461.md) due to the [dissolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md)[^cite_note-26] In the year 1910, over 5,500 Armenians immigrated to the United States, and by 1913, 9,355 more Armenians entered the North American borders.[^cite_note-\:0-25] As World War I approached, the rate of Armenian immigration rose to about 60,000. In 1920 and until the [Immigration Act of 1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Act_of_1924), 30,771 Armenians came to the United States; the immigrants were predominantly widowed women, children, and orphans.[^cite_note-\:0-25] Although many Armenians perished during the [Armenian genocide](140376.md), some of the Armenians who managed to escape established themselves in various parts of the world.&#x20;

By 1966, around 40 years after the start of the Armenian genocide, 2 million Armenians still lived in Eastern Armenia, while 330,000 Armenians lived in Russia, and 450,000 Armenians lived in the [United States](3434750.md) and [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-Cohen_2010_48–63-27]

In the United States, the rate of immigration increased after the [Immigration Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_and_Nationality_Act_of_1965) was passed in 1965.[^cite_note-\:0-25] The outbreak of the [civil War in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) in 1975 and the outbreak of the [Islamic Revolution in Iran](347268.md) during 1978 were factors which pushed Armenians to immigrate. The 1980 U.S. Census reported that 90 percent of the immigration to the United States was undertaken by Iranian-Armenians during the years from 1975 and 1980.[^cite_note-\:0-25]

The [energy crisis in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_Armenian_energy_crisis) in the early 1990s also resulted in the emigration of 676,000-800,000 Armenians from the Caucasus.[^cite_note-\:8-8][^cite_note-Jerry_L._Johnson2-28][^cite_note-\:5-29]

## Distribution

Main article: [Armenian population by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_population_by_country)

See also: [Historical Armenian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Armenian_population)

Less than one third of the world's Armenian population lives in Armenia. Their pre-[World War I](4764461.md) population area was six times larger than that of present-day Armenia, including the [eastern regions of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia), northern part of [Iran](14653.md), and the [southern part of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samtskhe-Javakheti).[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_of_diasporas-30]

By 2000, there were 7,580,000 Armenians living abroad in total.[^cite_note-Cohen_2010_48–63-27]
