**Jharkhand** ([Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_language): *Jhārkhaṇḍ*, pronounced [\[d͡ʒʱaːɾkʰəɳɖ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/86/LL-Q1568\_%28hin%29-Flashthomsom-Jharkhand.wav/LL-Q1568\_%28hin%29-Flashthomsom-Jharkhand.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/86/LL-Q1568_%28hin%29-Flashthomsom-Jharkhand.wav/LL-Q1568_%28hin%29-Flashthomsom-Jharkhand.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; lit. 'the land of forests') is a landlocked [state](375986.md) in [eastern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India).[^cite_note-15] The state shares borders with the states of [West Bengal](34040.md) to the east, [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) to the west, [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) to the northwest, [Bihar](442482.md) to the north and [Odisha](250724.md) to the south. It is the [15th largest state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_India_by_area), and the [14th largest by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population). [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) is the official language of the state.[^cite_note-NCLM52nd-5] The city of [Ranchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchi) is its capital, and [Dumka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumka) its sub-capital. The state is known for its waterfalls, hills and holy places; [Baidyanath Dham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidyanath_Temple), [Parasnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasnath), [Dewri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maa_Dewri_Temple) and [Rajrappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajrappa) are major religious sites.[^cite_note-nyoooz.com-16][^cite_note-17] As per the 2011 census, Jharkhand is primarily rural, with about 24% of its population living in cities.[^cite_note-18]

Jharkhand suffers from what is sometimes termed a [resource curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_curse): it accounts for more than 40% of [India's mineral production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_India) but 39.1% of its population is below the poverty line and 19.6% of children under five years of age are malnourished.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Etymology

The word "*Jhar*" means 'forest' and "*Khand*" means 'land' in various [Indo-Aryan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages). Thus, "Jharkhand" means 'the land of forests'.[^cite_note-22]

In the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) epic [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata), the region was referred as *Kark Khand* due to its location near *Kark Rekha*, that is, [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer).[^cite_note-23] It was also known as *Ark Khand*.[^cite_note-24] During the Medieval period, the region was known as *Jharkhand*. According to [Bhavishya Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavishya_Purana) (1200 CE), Jharkhand was one of the seven [Pundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pundra) desa. The name is first found on a 13th-century copper plate in [Kendrapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendrapara_district), Odisha, from the reign of Narasimha Deva II of [Eastern Ganga dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ganga_dynasty). Forest land from [Baidhnath dham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidyanath_Temple) to [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_\(city\)) was known as Jharkhand. In [Akbarnama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbarnama), from [Panchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchet) in the east to [Ratanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratanpur) to west, [Rohtasgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtasgarh) to the north and the frontier of Odisha to the south was known as Jharkhand.[^cite_note-Gautam_Kumar_Bera-2008-25][^cite_note-26]

## History

Main article: [History of Jharkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jharkhand)

### Ancient period

The region has been inhabited since the Mesolithic-Chalcolithic period, as shown by several ancient cave paintings.[^cite_note-Publications_Division,_Ministry_of_Information_&_Broadcasting-2016-27][^cite_note-Cave_paintings_lie_in_neglect-28][^cite_note-Pearson_Education_India-29] Stone tools have been discovered from Chota Nagpur Plateau region which are from the [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) and [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) periods.[^cite_note-Publications_Division,_Ministry_of_Information_&_Broadcasting-2016-27] There are ancient cave paintings in Isko, [Hazaribagh district](2240581.md) which are from Meso-chalcolithic period (9000–5000 BCE).[^cite_note-Cave_paintings_lie_in_neglect-28] During 2nd millennium BCE the use of Copper tools spread in [Chota Nagpur Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau) and these find complexes are known as the [Copper Hoard Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Hoard_Culture).[^cite_note-30] In the Kabra-Kala mound at the confluence of the [Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_River) and [North Koel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Koel_River) rivers in [Palamu district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palamu_district) various antiquities and art objects have been found which are from the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) to the medieval period and [pot-sherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherd) of [Redware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redware), [black and red ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_and_red_ware_culture), black ware, [black slipped ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipware) and [NBP ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Black_Polished_Ware) are from [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) to the late medieval period.[^cite_note-31] Several [iron slags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slag), [microliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microlith), and [potsherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsherds) have been discovered from [Singhbhum district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhbhum_district) that are from 1400 BCE according to carbon dating age.[^cite_note-Pearson_Education_India-29] The region was ruled by many empires and dynasties including [Nanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Empire), [Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) and [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) during ancient period.

In the Mahabharata, the region was referred as Kark Khand due to its location near Tropic of Cancer.[^cite_note-Nagpuri_Shist_Sahitya-32] During the age of Mahajanpadas around 500 BCE, Jharkhand state was a part of [Magadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_\(Mahajanapada\)) and [Anga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anga).[^cite_note-33] In the Mauryan period, this region was ruled by a number of states, which were collectively known as the Atavika (forest) states. These states were subdued and were forced to accept the [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony) of the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) during [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka)'s reign (c. 232 BCE). In the ancient site of [Saridkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saridkel), burnt brick houses, red ware pottery, copper tools, coins and iron tools have been found which belong to the early centuries CE.[^cite_note-34] Brahmi inscriptions have been found in Khunti district which are from the 3rd century BCE.[^cite_note-35] [Samudragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta), while marching through the present-day Chotanagpur region ([North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Chotanagpur_division) and [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Chotanagpur_division)), directed the first attack against the kingdom of [Dakshina Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kosala) in the [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi) valley.[^cite_note-36]

### Medieval period

In the 7th century, the Chinese traveller [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) passed through the region. He described the kingdom as [Karnasuvarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnasuvarna), with [Shashanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashanka) as its ruler. To the north of Karn-Suberna was Magadha, Champa was in the east, Mahendra in the west, and Orissa in the south.[^cite_note-37]

During the medieval period, the region was governed by [Nagvanshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagvanshi_dynasty), [Pala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire), [Khayaravala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khayaravala_dynasty), [Ramgarh Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramgarh_Raj) and [Chero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chero_dynasty) rulers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoma_Niyogi1959119-38][^cite_note-https-39] A Buddhist monastery has been found in [Hazaribagh](2240581.md) which was built during the Pala period in the 10th century.[^cite_note-40] [Bhim Karn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhim_Karn) was a Nagvanshi king during medieval period. He defeated the [Raksel dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raksel_dynasty) of Surguja when they invaded the region with cavalry.[^cite_note-41]



Khakparta Temple, a 9th-century Shiva temple in Lohardaga

### Modern period

[Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) influence reached [Palamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palamu_district) during the reign of Emperor [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) when it was conquered by [Rajput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajput) [Raja Mansingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Mansingh) in 1574. Several invasions took place during Mughal rule.[^cite_note-latehar.nic.in-42] During the reign of the Nagvanshi King [Madhu Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhu_Singh_\(king\)), Akbar's general invaded [Khukhra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khukhragarh). Also there was an invasion during the reign of [Durjan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durjan_Shah).[^cite_note-43]



Administrative jurisdictions of present-day Jharkhand region in 1770s

King [Ram Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Shah_\(king\)) ruled Navratangarh from 1640 to 1663. He built the [Kapilnath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapilnath_Temple) in 1643. He was succeeded by his son [Raghunath Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghunath_Shah). Thakur [Ani Nath Shahdeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani_Nath_Shahdeo) built the Jagannath temple of Ranchi in 1691.[^cite_note-44] The King [Medini Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medini_Ray) ruled from 1658 to 1674 in Palamu.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELahiry201424-45] His rule extended to areas in South [Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaya_district) and [Hazaribagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaribagh). He attacked Navratangarh and defeated the [Nagvanshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagvanshi_Kingdom) Maharaja of [Chhotanagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau).[^cite_note-46] Chero rule in the [Palamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palamu_division) region lasted until the 19th century until internal conflict between various factions weakened the Cheros and they were defeated by the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company). Later Palamu estates were sold by the British.[^cite_note-47]

During the 18th century, regions under the Chero dynasty, Nagvanshi dynasty, [Ramgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramgarh_Raj) and [Kharagdiha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharagdiha) became territories of the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company). Ramgarh Raj along with estates of other chiefs in the regions were permanently settled as Zamindari estates. The [Kharagdiha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharagdiha) [Rajas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajas) were settled as Rajas of Raj Dhanwar in 1809, and the Kharagdiha gadis were separately settled as zamindari estates. Some of the notable Kharagdiha Zamindari estates were [Koderma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koderma), [Gadi Palganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasnath) and [Ledo Gadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghochia).[^cite_note-48] The princely states in the [Chota Nagpur Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau) came within the [sphere of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_of_influence) of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy), but they became tributary states of East India Company as a result of the [Anglo-Maratha Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Maratha_Wars_\(disambiguation\)) and became known as [Chota Nagpur Tributary States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Tributary_States).[^cite_note-49]

Subjugation, colonisation and imposition of taxes by the British East India Company resulted in spontaneous resistance from the local people. [Chuar Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuar_Rebellion), the first revolt against the British East India Company was led by Jagannath Singh Patar in 1767 with the [Bhumij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_people) tribals. The Bhumijes again revolted in 1769–71, led by their Sardar Ghatwals in Dhalbhum. In 1769, [Raghunath Mahato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghunath_Mahato) also revolted against the East India Company.[^cite_note-50]

In 1771, a revolt against the landlords and the British government was led by [Tilka Majhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilka_Manjhi), a Paharia leader in Rajmahal Hills. Soon after in 1779, the [Bhumij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_people) tribes again rose in arms against the British rule in Manbhum, called the Chuar Rebellion. In 1807, the Oraons in Barway murdered their landlord from [Srinagar](294148.md). The [Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munda_people) tribe rose in revolt in 1811 and 1813. [Bakhtar Say](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhtar_Say) and [Mundal Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundal_Singh), two landowners, fought against the British East India Company in 1812.[^cite_note-51]

The Hos in Singhbhum revolted in 1820 and a [Kol revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kol_uprising) occurred in 1832. Also in 1832 the [Bhumijs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_people) revolted again against the British, this time under the leadership of [Ganga Narayan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Narayan_Singh), known as the [Bhumij rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_rebellion). During the 19th century, large numbers of santals from [Manbhum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbhum), [Hazaribagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaribagh), [Midnapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnapore) were settled by British in [Damin-i-koh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damin-i-koh) to cultivate the land and generate revenue. But the Santal revolted against the tax imposition. The [Santhal rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_rebellion) broke out in 1855 under the leadership of two brothers [Sidhu and Kanhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidhu_and_Kanhu_Murmu). Later the British renamed it Santal Pargana.[^cite_note-52]



[Santhal rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_rebellion) against tax imposition during British [Company Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India) in 1855

[Thakur Vishwanath Shahdeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thakur_Vishwanath_Shahdeo) and [Pandey Ganpat Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandey_Ganpat_Rai) rebelled against the British East India Company in the [1857 rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857). In the [Battle of Chatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chatra), conflict took place between the rebels and the East India company.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] [Tikait Umrao Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikait_Umrao_Singh), [Sheikh Bhikhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Bhikhari), Nadir Ali and Jai Mangal Singh played pivotal role in the Indian Rebellion of 1857.[^cite_note-55] The brothers [Nilambar and Pitambar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilambar_and_Pitambar) were chiefs of Bhogta clan of the [Kharwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharwar) tribe who held ancestral jagirs with many [Chero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chero) Jagirdars and led revolt against the British East India company.[^cite_note-latehar.nic.in-42]

After the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857), the [rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India) of the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) was transferred to the Crown in the person of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria),[^cite_note-56] who, in 1876, was proclaimed [Empress of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_of_India). The [Cheros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chero) and [Kharwars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharwar) again rebelled against the British in 1882 but the attack was repulsed.[^cite_note-57] Then [Birsa Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birsa_Munda) revolt,[^cite_note-58] broke out in 1895 and lasted until 1900. The revolt though mainly concentrated in the [Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munda_people) belt of Khunti, Tamar, Sarwada and Bandgaon.

In October 1905, the exercise of British influence over the predominantly [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi)-speaking states of [Chang Bhakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang_Bhakar), [Jashpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jashpur_State), [Koriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_State), [Surguja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surguja_State), and [Udaipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udaipur_State,_Chhattisgarh) was transferred from the Bengal government to that of the [Central Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces), while the two [Oriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriya_language)-speaking states of [Gangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangpur_State) and [Bonai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonai) were attached to the [Orissa Tributary States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa_Tributary_States), leaving only [Kharsawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharsawan_State) and [Saraikela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraikela_State) answerable to the Bengal governor.[^cite_note-59]

In 1936, all nine states were transferred to the [Eastern States Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_States_Agency), the officials of which came under the direct authority of the Governor-General of India, rather than under that of any provinces.

In March 1940, the Indian National Congress (INC) 53rd Session[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] occurred under the presidency of [Maulana Azad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Azad) at Jhanda Chowk, Ramgarh, which is now [Ramgarh Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramgarh_Cantonment). [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi),[^cite_note-62] [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), [Sardar Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Patel), [Rajendra Prasad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Prasad), [Sarojini Naidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarojini_Naidu), [Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Abdul_Ghaffar_Khan), [Acharya J.B. Kripalani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acharya_J.B._Kripalani), Industrialist [Jamnalal Bajaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnalal_Bajaj) and other great leaders[^cite_note-63] of the Indian freedom movement attended the Ramgarh Session.[^cite_note-64] Mahatma Gandhi also opened the Khadi and Village Industries Exhibition at Ramgarh.[^cite_note-65]

At that time, under the leadership of Netaji [Subhash Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhas_Chandra_Bose) a conference against Samjhauta was also completed. In Ramgarh, Subhas Chandra Bose was seen as president of the All India Forward Block and M. N. Roy was seen as leader of the Radical democratic party.

### Post Independence

After Indian independence in 1947, the rulers of many states chose to accede to the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India). [Changbhakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changbhakar), [Jashpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jashpur), [Koriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koriya), [Surguja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surguja) and [Udaipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udaipur) after that became part of the state of [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), but [Gangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangpur) and [Bonai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonai) became part of the state of [Orissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa,_India) and [Kharsawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharsawan) and [Saraikela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraikela) became part of the state of [Bihar](442482.md).[^cite_note-66] In 1928, a separate state was demanded by *Unnati Samaj*, the political wing of the Christian Tribals Association, which submitted a memorandum to the [Simon Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Commission) to constitute a tribal state in eastern India.[^cite_note-Gautam_Kumar_Bera-2008-25] Prominent leaders like [Jaipal Singh Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipal_Singh_Munda) and [Ram Narayan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Narayan_Singh) also demanded a separate state. In 1955, the [Jharkhand Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharkhand_Party), led by Jaipal Singh Munda, submitted a memorandum to the [States Reorganization Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_Reorganization_Commission) for a separate Jharkhand state comprising the tribal area of South Bihar, but it was rejected because there were many languages, no link language in the region, tribals were in the minority, Hindustani was the majority language, and it risked adverse effect on the economy of Bihar.[^cite_note-Concept_Publishing_Company-67][^cite_note-68]

Later the [Sadan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadan_people), the native various caste/non-tribal groups, also joined the movement for a separate state.[^cite_note-Concept_Publishing_Company-67] In 1972, [Binod Bihari Mahato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binod_Bihari_Mahato), [Shibu Soren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibu_Soren) and [A. K. Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._K._Roy) founded [Jharkhand Mukti Morcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharkhand_Mukti_Morcha). [Nirmal Mahto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirmal_Mahto) founded the [All Jharkhand Students Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Jharkhand_Students_Union). They led the movement for a separate state of Jharkhand. The Jharkhand coordination committee (JCC), consisting of [Ram Dayal Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Dayal_Munda), [B. P. Keshri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisheshwar_Prasad_Keshari), [Binod Bihari Mahato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binod_Bihari_Mahato), Santosh Rana and Suraj Singh Besra started a new initiative and tried to coordinate between different parties. Keshri sent a memorandum to form Jharkhand state in 1988.[^cite_note-69] The Jharkhand co-ordination committee was then led by Congress General Secretary [Ram Ratan Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Ratan_Ram), who urged [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi) to pay attention to the issue at hand.

In July 1988, the [Bharatiya Janata party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_party) led by [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee), [Lal Krishna Advani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Krishna_Advani), and [Murli Manohar Joshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murli_Manohar_Joshi) decided to demand a separate state, Vanachal, comprising the forest region of South Bihar in [Jamshedpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamshedpur). [Inder Singh Namdhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inder_Singh_Namdhari), [Samresh Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samresh_Singh), and [Rudra Pratap Sarangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudra_Pratap_Sarangi) were the leaders of the Vanachal movement. They organised several rallies to form a separate state.[^cite_note-jagran2018-70]

The central government formed a committee on the Jharkhand matter in 1989. It stressed the need for greater allocation of development funds for the area. There was a provision for limited internal autonomy in the hill area of [Assam](186162.md). Other [tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal) areas were covered by the fifth schedule of the constitution. Chotanagpur and [Santal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santal) Pargana development boards were constituted under the chairmanship of the then chief minister of Bihar under the provision of the fifth schedule in 1972. This failed to achieve the desired result. Jharkhand Mukti Morcha wanted more representation and the All Jharkhand Students Union was against it. Due to differences these parties broke away from each other. The All Jharkhand Students Union introduced elements of violence in the movement and called for a boycott of election while Jharkhand Mukti Morcha opposed this. The Jharkhand Area Autonomous Council bill passed in Bihar's legislative assembly in December 1994. The Jharkhand Area Autonomous Council were given responsibility for forty areas including agriculture, rural health, public work, public health and minerals. The council has power to recommend legislation to the Assembly through the state government and to frame bylaws and regulations.[^cite_note-https-39][^cite_note-Concept_Publishing_Company-67]

In 1998, when the separate state movement was falling apart, Justice [Lal Pingley Nath Shahdeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Pingley_Nath_Shahdeo) was leading the movement. In 1998, the [Union government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_government) decided to send the bill concerning the formation of the state of Jharkhand to the [Bihar Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar_Legislative_Assembly) to which [Lalu Prasad Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalu_Prasad_Yadav) had said that the state would be divided over his dead body. A total of 16 political parties including the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md), Jharkhand Mukti Morcha, the [All Jharkhand Students Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Jharkhand_Students_Union), and the [Congress](149333.md) came in one platform and formed the 'All Party Separate State Formation Committee' to start the movement. Shahdeo was elected as the convener of the committee. Voting on the Jharkhand Act was to be done on 21 September 1998 in the Bihar legislature. On that day the committee, under the leadership of Shahdeo called for Jharkhand Bandh and organised a protest march. Thousands of supporters of a separate state took to the streets led by Shahdeo. He was arrested and detained in a police station for hours along with many supporters.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

In 1999 the Bharatiya Janata party promised to form a separate Vanachal state if they won the state election with a majority of votes.[^cite_note-jagran2018-70] After the last Assembly election in the state resulted in a hung assembly, [RJD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtriya_Janata_Dal)'s dependence on the Congress extended support on the precondition that RJD would not pose a hurdle to the passage of the [Bihar reorganisation Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar_Reorganisation_Act,_2000). Finally, with the support from both RJD and [Congress](149333.md), the ruling coalition at the Centre led by the Bharatiya Janata Party which had made statehood its main poll plank in the region in successive polls earlier, cleared the Bihar reorganisation Bill in the monsoon session of the Parliament on 2 and 11 August in Loksabha and Rajyasabha. This paved the way for the creation of a separate Vanachal state comprising the [Chota Nagpur Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Division) and [Santhal Pargana Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_Pargana_Division) of South Bihar.[^cite_note-73] [NDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance_\(India\)) formed the government with [Babulal Marandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babulal_Marandi) as chief minister. Later the name of the state was changed from Vanachal to Jharkhand.[^cite_note-jagran2018-70][^cite_note-74] [Babulal Marandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babulal_Marandi) took the oath of chief minister on 15 November 2000 on the anniversary of the birth of tribal leader [Birsa Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birsa_Munda).[^cite_note-https-39]

### Jharkhand statehood

The dynamics of resources and the politics of development still influence the socio-economic structures in Jharkhand, which was carved out of the relatively underdeveloped southern part of [Bihar](442482.md). According to the 1991 census, the state has a population of over 20 million out of which 28% is [tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal) while 12% of the people belong to [scheduled castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_castes_and_scheduled_tribes). Jharkhand has 24 districts, 260 blocks, and 32,620 villages out of which only 45% have access to electricity while only 8,484 are connected by roads. Jharkhand is the leading producer of mineral wealth in the country after [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) state, endowed as it is with a vast variety of minerals like iron ore, coal, copper ore, mica, bauxite, graphite, limestone, and uranium. Jharkhand is also known for its vast forest resources.[^cite_note-75]

### Naxal insurgency

See also: [Tritiya Prastuti Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritiya_Prastuti_Committee) and [People's Liberation Front of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Front_of_India)

Jharkhand has been at the centre of the [Naxalite–Maoist insurgency](21636599.md). Since the uprising of the [Naxalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxalites) in 1967, 6,000 people have been killed in fighting between the Naxalites and counter-insurgency operations by the [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharkhand_Police), and [paramilitary](146730.md) groups such as the [Salwa Judum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salwa_Judum).[^cite_note-76]

Jharkhand is part of the "[Red Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Corridor)", which comprises 92,000 square kilometres, where the highest concentration of the group's estimated 20,000 combatants fight.[^cite_note-globalpolitician.com-77][^cite_note-78] Part of this is due to the fact that the state harbours an abundance of natural resources, while its people live in abject poverty and destitution.[^cite_note-atimes.com-79] The impoverished state provides ample recruits for [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) insurgents, who argue that they are fighting on behalf of the landless poor and tribals that see few benefits from the resource extractions.[^cite_note-atimes.com-79] As the federal government holds a monopoly on sub-surface resources in the state, the [tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal) population is prevented from staking any claim on the resources extracted from their land.[^cite_note-atimes.com-79] In response, the insurgents have recently begun a campaign of targeting infrastructure related to the extraction of resources vital for Indian energy needs, such as coal.[^cite_note-globalpolitician.com-77]

On 5 March 2007, [Sunil Mahato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Kumar_Mahato), a member of the national parliament, was killed by [Naxalite](4760079.md) rebels near [Kishanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishanpur) while watching a football match on the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) festival of [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi). His widow, [Suman Mahato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suman_Mahato), the [Jharkhand Mukti Morcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharkhand_Mukti_Morcha) candidate, won the [Jamshedpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamshedpur) Lok Sabha by-election in September 2007 and served in parliament until 2009.[^cite_note-80]

## Geography

Jharkhand is located in the eastern part of India and is enclosed by [West Bengal](34040.md) to the east, [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) and [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) to the west, [Bihar](442482.md) to the north and [Odisha](250724.md) to the south.

Jharkhand envelops a geographical area of 79,716 square kilometres (30,779 sq mi). Much of Jharkhand lies on the [Chota Nagpur Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau). Many rivers pass through the Chota Nagpur plateau. They are: [Damodar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damodar_River), [North Koel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Koel_River), [Barakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barakar), [South Koel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Koel_River), [Sankh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankh_River) and [Subarnarekha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarnarekha_River) rivers. The higher watersheds of these rivers stretch out within the Jharkhand state. Much of the Jharkhand state is still enclosed by forest. Forests sustain the population of elephants and tigers.

### Climate

Climate of Jharkhand varies from [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) in the north to [tropical wet and dry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) in the south-east.[^cite_note-81] The main seasons are summer, rainy, autumn, winter and spring. The summer lasts from mid-April to mid-June. May, the hottest month, characterised by daily high temperatures around 37 °C (99 °F) and low temperatures around 25 °C (77 °F). The [southwest monsoon](31096097.md), from mid-June to October, brings nearly all the state's annual rainfall, which ranges from about 1,000 mm (40 in) in the west-central part of the state to more than 1,500 mm (60 in) in the southwest. Nearly half of the annual precipitation falls in July and August. The winter season lasts from November to February. The temperatures in [Ranchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchi) in December usually vary from 10–24 °C (50–75 °F). Spring season lasts from mid-February to mid-April.[^cite_note-82]

### Hills and mountain ranges

- [Parasnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasnath): Parasnath Hill is also called as [Sri Sammed Sikharji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Sammed_Sikharji) by Jains and [Marang Buru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marang_Buru) by [Santal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santal) tribes. The Parasnath Hill is situated in Giridih district of Jharkhand. It is a chief [Jain pilgrimage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_pilgrimage_site) and the holy place for [Jains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain). Additionally, for [Santal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santal) tribes, their supreme deity [Marang Buru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marang_Buru) is prayed and *Jug Jaher* is the holy enshrine in its valley where *Jaher Ayo* being worshiped. It is believed in the Jain culture that 20 of the 24 Tirthankaras attained Moksha from this place. The height of the hill is 1,365 meters.

- [Netarhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netarhat): Netarhat is a town in Latehar district. Referred to as the "Queen of Chotanagpur", it is a hill station. [Netarhat Residential School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netarhat_Residential_School) is located here. Netarhat Dam is also located in this area.

- [Rajmahal Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajmahal_Hills): These hills are located in [Sahibganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahibganj) and [Godda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godda) districts of Eastern part of Jharkhand. The [Rajmahal hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajmahal_hills) belong to the Jurassic era. These hills like others also have many waterfalls, lakes and greenery.

- [Trikut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trikut): Trikut Hill is located ten kilometres away from Deoghar and lies on the way to Dumka in Jharkhand. Trikut hill is also called Trikutchal because there are 3 major peaks on the hill. The height of Trikut hill is 2470 feet.

- [Tagore Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagore_Hill): The [Tagore Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagore_Hill) is also recognised as the Morabadi Hill. The Tagore hill is located in Morabadi, Ranchi. The brother of Rabindranath Tagore, Jyotirindranath Tagore had made a tour at Ranchi in the year 1908.

### Flora and Fauna

Jharkhand has a rich variety of [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_\(plants\)) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_\(animals\)). The wildlife sanctuaries, national parks and zoological gardens in the state present a panorama of this variety. ## Government and administration

Main article: [Government of Jharkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Jharkhand)

The constitutional head of the government of Jharkhand is the [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(India\)), who is appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India). The real executive power rests with the [chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_minister_\(India\)) and the cabinet. The political party or the coalition of political parties having a majority in the Legislative Assembly forms the government.

The head of the bureaucracy of the state is the [chief secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_secretary_\(India\)). Under this position, is a hierarchy of officials drawn from the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service), [Indian Police Service](451884.md), [Indian Forest Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Forest_Service) and different wings of the state civil services.

The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is headed by the [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_justice). Jharkhand has a [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharkhand_High_Court) which has been functioning since the state's formation in 2000. All branches of the government are located in the state capital, Ranchi.

### Administrative districts

Main articles: [Administrative divisions of Jharkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Jharkhand) and [Districts of Jharkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Jharkhand)

The state was formed in 2000 with 18 districts that were formerly part of south Bihar. Some of these districts were reorganised to form 6 new districts, namely, Latehar, Saraikela Kharsawan, Jamtara, Pakur, Khunti and Ramgarh. At present, the state has 5 Divisions and 24 Districts. Interestingly, all of the state's districts, except Lohardaga and Khunti, share a border with a neighbouring state.[^cite_note-98] Each district is administered by a [Deputy Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_magistrate) (who also functions as *District Collector/District Magistrate*), an [IAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) officer appointed by the government.

For land revenue administration, each district is divided into subdivisions, headed by a [Sub Divisional Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-divisional_magistrate) (SDO), and each subdivision again is divided into circles/anchals, headed by Circle Officers (CO).[^cite_note-Administrative_divisions_of_Jharkhand-99]

For development administration, each district is divided into blocks (also known as C.D Blocks), headed by [Block Development Officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_\(district_subdivision\)) (BDO). A block consists of several rural subdivisions (panchayats).[^cite_note-Administrative_divisions_of_Jharkhand-99]

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) of Jharkhand is estimated at ₹6.25 lakh crore (US\$65 billion) in 2026–27. The per capita GDP of Jharkhand in 2026-27 was ₹189,422 (US\$2,000).[^cite_note-101]



Open-cast coal mining in [Dhanbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanbad)

Jharkhand has several towns and innumerable villages with civic amenities. Urbanization ratio is 24.1%.[^cite_note-102] Jharkhand also has immense [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) resources: minerals ranging from (ranking in the country within bracket) from [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) [ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore) (4th), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) (3rd), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) ore (1st), [mica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mica) (1st), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) (3rd), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [china clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_clay), [fire clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_clay), [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite) (8th), [kainite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kainite) (1st), [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite) (2nd), [asbestos](21492663.md) (1st), [thorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorium) (3rd), [sillimanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sillimanite), [uranium](31743.md) (Jaduguda mines, Narwa Pahar) (1st) and even [gold](12240.md) ([Rakha Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakha_Mines)) (6th) and [silver](27119.md) and several other minerals. Large deposits of coal and iron ore support concentration of [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector), in centres like [Jamshedpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamshedpur), [Dhanbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanbad), [Bokaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokaro_Steel_City) and Ranchi. [Tata Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Steel), a *[NSE NIFTY 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSE_NIFTY_500)* conglomerate has its corporate office and main plant in Tatanagar, Jharkhand.[^cite_note-103] It reported a gross income of ₹. 204,910 million for 2005. NTPC will start coal production from its captive mine in state in 2011–12, for which the company will be investing about Rs 18 billion.[^cite_note-104]

In February 2006, the government of Jharkhand established the Jharkhand Silk Textile and Handicraft Development Corporation (Jharcraft), which promotes local sericulture and weaving and the wider marketing of these products.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

Agriculture is another major economic sector. Farmers in Jharkhand produce several crops such as rice, wheat, maize, pulses, potatoes, and vegetables such as tomato, carrots, cabbage, brinjal, pumpkin, and papaya. Other important industries include the cottage industry and IT.[^cite_note-107]

### Cuisine

See also: [Cuisine of Jharkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Jharkhand)



A Jharkhand Rice Plate

Staple foods of Jharkhand are [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal), [roti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable), and [tubers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuber). Spices are sparingly used in cuisine. Famous dishes include [Chhilka Roti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhilka_Roti), [Malpua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malpua), [Pitha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitha), [Dhooska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhooska), Arsa roti, Dudhauri, and [Panipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panipuri) (Gupchup).[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] Rugra and Putoo is a type of edible mushroom that is grown extensively in Jharkhand and harvested during the rainy months. It has a hardened, white, edible shell and a softer dark coloured centre. [Bamboo shoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_shoot) are a versatile ingredient used in many culinary traditions, particularly in Jharkhand cuisine. They can be boiled, steamed, stir-fried, or pickled, making them a popular addition to dishes such as soups, curries, and salads. Bamboo shoots are known for their ability to absorb the flavours of the ingredients they are cooked with, enhancing the overall taste of the dishes.[^cite_note-110] The leaves of Munga ([Moringa oleifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moringa_oleifera)) and Koinar ([Bauhinia variegata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhinia_variegata)) trees are used as a leafy vegetable or [Saag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saag).[^cite_note-111]

Local alcoholic drinks include rice beer, originally known as Handi or [Handia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handia_\(drink\)), named after the vessel, handi (earthen pot), used to make it. Handia is culturally associated with natives, i.e., Sadans and Tribals; this drink is consumed by both men and women on social occasions like marriage and festivals.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] Another common liquor is called *Mahua [daru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desi_daru)*, made from flowers of the Mahua tree ([Madhuca longifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhuca_longifolia)).[^cite_note-114]

## Public health

Because of its mild climate, Jharkhand, particularly its capital Ranchi, has been a health resort. As far back as 1918, facilities were set up for treatment of [mentally challenged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentally_challenged).[^cite_note-136]

European Mental Hospital was established along with Indian Mental Hospital. Today they are called Central Institute of Psychiatry and Ranchi Institute of Neuro-psychiatry and Allied Sciences respectively. In certain areas of Jharkhand, poverty and consequent malnutrition have given rise to diseases like [tuberculosis](30653.md) (TB). In fact, TB has assumed [epidemic](66981.md) proportions in certain areas of the state. For management and treatment of such TB, Itki TB Sanatorium, Ranchi, established in 1928 has been doing work as a premier institute for clinical and programmatic management of TB. The Itki TB Sanatorium is well equipped and accredited by the Indian government for quality assurance and Culture and Drug Sensitivity Testing for M.TB. It provides free of cost treatment for TB as well as drug-resistant TB. Likewise, in the field of treatment of cancer, Tata Main Hospital, Jamshedpur,[^cite_note-137] is rendering pioneering work. In the same way, Bokaro General Hospital equipped with modern facilities for the treatment of cancer and heart-related problems with the capacity of 1100 beds one of the largest in eastern India.

Although several public and private health facilities are available in the state, overall infrastructure for dispensing health related services require improvements. An exception is the Tata Motors Hospital which is an example of an ISO 14001 and 18001 certified hospital with DNB teaching facilities.

Ranchi, the capital, has witnessed a sharp growth in the number of hospitals.

[Fluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluoride) in groundwater presents a public health problem in Jharkhand. A recent survey led by the [Birla Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birla_Institute_of_Technology), Mesra, Ranchi in collaboration with [UNICEF](19698110.md) in the northwest districts of [Palamau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palamau) and [Garhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garhwa) found fluoride levels above the drinking [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) drinking water guidelines.[^cite_note-138] Excessive amounts of fluoride in drinking water can lead to [dental fluorosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_fluorosis), prevalent bone fractures, and [skeletal fluorosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_fluorosis), an irreversible disabling condition.[^cite_note-139] Some work has focused on combating fluorosis through increased calcium intake by consuming local plants.[^cite_note-140] Researchers at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and the Birla Institute of Technology, Mesra, Ranchi are currently investigating defluoridation options, while performing an epidemiological survey to assess the extent of fluoride linked health problems and the impact of future interventions.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

Almost 80% of Jharkhand's people are farmers, although it contains 40% of India's mineral reserves it has some of India's poorest people, in Summer 2009 the state was threatened by drought, with people criticising the government for not providing food aid or assistance.[^cite_note-143]

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Jharkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Jharkhand)

Jharkhand is known for its waterfalls, hills and holy places.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-nyoooz.com-16] [Parasnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasnath), [Baidyanath Dham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidyanath_Dham), [Maa Dewri Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maa_Dewri_Temple) and [Chhinnamasta Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhinnamasta_Temple) are major religious places.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

Tattapani Hot Water Spring is located 8 km from [Latehar](1673467.md). The hot spring water come out from different places on the Sukari River bed. Rich in sulfur, the hot spring is believed to have medicinal properties and good for skin.[^cite_note-151]

[Itkhori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itkhori_Block) is a holy place for Hindus, Buddhists and Jains. It is believed to be the place from where [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) started his journey for [Bodh Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodh_Gaya). Many sculptures of Hindu, Jain and Buddhist art styles were found in 2018.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] [Rankini Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rankini_Temple,_Jadugora) of [Jadugora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadugora) is famous in Jharkhand, as well as in Odisha, West Bengal and Bihar.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] There are several waterfalls in the state including [Jonha Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonha_Falls), [Hundru Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundru_Falls), [Dassam Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassam_Falls), [Perwaghagh Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perwaghagh_Falls) and [Panchghagh Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchghagh_Falls).[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] [Netarhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netarhat) is a hill station in the state.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

There are several wildlife sanctuaries and national park in Jharkhand including [Betla National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betla_National_Park) and [Dalma Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalma_Wildlife_Sanctuary), which are major attraction for tourists.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

[State Museum Hotwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Museum_Hotwar) and [Tribal Research Institute and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_Research_Institute_and_Museum) showcase various cultural heritage and tribal culture of Jharkhand.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]
