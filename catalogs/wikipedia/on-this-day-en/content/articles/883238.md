The **Scottish Football Association Challenge Cup**,[^cite_note-Rules-1] commonly known as the **Scottish Cup**[^cite_note-SFA_ScotCup-2][^cite_note-BBC_ScotCup-3], is an annual [association football](10568.md) [knock-out cup competition](939680.md) for men's football clubs in Scotland.[^cite_note-Rules-1] The competition was first held in [1873–74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1873%E2%80%9374_Scottish_Cup). Entry is open to all 134 clubs with full membership of the [Scottish Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Football_Association) (SFA), along with up to eight other clubs who are associate members.[^cite_note-SFA_TheCup-4] The competition is called **[Scottish Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Gas) Men's Scottish Cup** for sponsorship reasons.[^cite_note-Scottish_FA-5]

Although it is the [second-oldest competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_football_competitions) in association football history, after the English [FA Cup](11237.md), the Scottish Cup trophy is the oldest in association football and is also the oldest national trophy in the world. It was first presented to [Queen's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Park_F.C.), who won the [final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1874_Scottish_Cup_final) of the inaugural tournament in March 1874.[^cite_note-SFA_TheCup-4] The current holders are [Celtic](69708.md), who won the tournament by defeating [Dunfermline Athletic](451181.md) in the [2026 final](82990377.md).

## Format

The tournament starts at the beginning of the Scottish [football season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_association_football_season), in August.[^cite_note-\:0-6] The Scottish Cup Final is usually the last game of the season, taking place at the end of May.[^cite_note-\:0-6] Participating teams enter the tournament at different stages depending on their [league ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_football_league_system).[^cite_note-Scot_Cup_Composition-7] The lowest ranked clubs enter the tournament at the preliminary round whilst the highest ranked, those that compete in the [Scottish Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Premiership), enter at the fourth round stage in January.

A football player scores a goal against the opposing goalkeeper from a penalty-kick. Stewards and camera-operators are visible behind the goal net.

The [2006 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Scottish_Cup_Final) between [Heart of Midlothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_Midlothian_F.C.) and [Gretna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretna_F.C.) was decided by a penalty shoot-out.

The competition is a [knock-out tournament](939680.md).[^cite_note-Rules-1] In each round of games, the teams are paired at random, with the first team drawn listed as the [home team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_\(sports\)). Every game lasts 90 minutes plus any additional [stoppage time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoppage_time).[^cite_note-Rules-1] The winner of each game advances to the next round, whilst the loser is eliminated from the tournament. In prior seasons, if a game ended in a [draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tie_\(draw\)) before the fourth round, the fixture was replayed at the home ground of the other team at a later date, before the fourth round.[^cite_note-\:0-6] If the [replay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replay_\(sports\)) also ended in a draw, a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) took place to decide the winner. From the fourth round onwards, if the game ended in a draw there was no replay; 30 minutes of [extra time](633242.md) would be played, followed by a penalty shoot-out if there is still no winner.[^cite_note-Rules-1] Following a vote by the Scottish FA's member clubs in August 2022, it was decided that, for the 2022—23 competition, there would be no replays following the preliminary round, extra time and penalties will be used to decide the winner of drawn games from Round One onwards.[^cite_note-8]

The competition has a staggered entry system. The preliminary rounds are contested by qualifiers and clubs who are compete in a league below tier 5 in the [Scottish pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_football_league_system). Eighteen [Highland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Football_League) and thirty-two [Lowland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland_Football_League) clubs begin in the first round. Ten [Scottish League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_League_Two) clubs enter the second round. [Scottish League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_League_One) and [Scottish Championship](40057914.md) clubs start in the third round, then finally twelve [Scottish Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Premiership) clubs enter in the fourth round.[^cite_note-format-9]

### Eligible clubs and players

Any club that is a full or associate member of the [Scottish Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Football_Association) (SFA) is entitled to compete in the tournament.[^cite_note-Rules-1] Full members qualify automatically, which includes every team that plays in the [Scottish Professional Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Professional_Football_League) (SPFL), [Highland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Football_League), and [Lowland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland_Football_League). Between 1895 and 2007, clubs that were SFA members but not competitors in the country's professional football leagues could only qualify for the tournament through the [Scottish Qualifying Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Qualifying_Cup).[^cite_note-Junior_BBC-10]



Former junior club [Bonnyrigg Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnyrigg_Rose_Athletic_F.C.) (in red) qualified to compete by winning the SJFA [East Superleague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Junior_Football_East_Region_Super_League) in 2012.

Clubs which are not full members of the SFA may still qualify for the tournament by winning one of the six leagues at tier 6 in the [Scottish football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_football_league_system) ([East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Scotland_Football_League), [Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands_Football_League), [North Caledonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caledonian_Football_Association), [North Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region_Junior_Football_League), [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_of_Scotland_Football_League), [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_of_Scotland_Football_League)) or the [East, South and West of Scotland Cup-Winners Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Scotland_Football_League#Cup_competitions). Clubs that are members of the [Scottish Junior Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Junior_Football_Association) (SJFA) have been able to qualify since [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Scottish_Cup) by winning the [Scottish Junior Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Junior_Cup).[^cite_note-Junior_BBC-10] And, since [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Scottish_Cup), the winners of the [Scottish Amateur Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Amateur_Cup) are also eligible to qualify.[^cite_note-11]

Players that are registered with a competing club are eligible to play, however, cannot represent more than one club during the same tournament.[^cite_note-Rules-1] Each club names [eleven players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_team#Variation_of_player_numbers_among_football_codes) and up to seven [substitutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_\(sport\)) before every match.[^cite_note-Rules-1] In order to play in the final match, a player must have also been registered to compete in the semi-final round for the same club.[^cite_note-Rules-1] If a club fields a player that is not registered, the club may be [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejection_\(sports\)) from the tournament.[^cite_note-Rules-1][^cite_note-Shire_expelled_STV-12]

### Venues

Before the semi-final and final rounds, the venue of each match is determined when the fixtures are drawn; the first club drawn in a fixture is named the home team and chooses the venue for the match, usually its own [home ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_\(sports\)).[^cite_note-Rules-1] In the event of a game ending in a draw, the venue for the replay is the home ground of the second club drawn.[^cite_note-Rules-1] The semi-final ties are played at a [neutral venue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_venue);[^cite_note-Rules-1] usually [Hampden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden_Park) in Glasgow.[^cite_note-Semis_CelticPark_Ibrox-13] On occasions when Hampden has been unavailable, such as when it was being renovated in the late 1990s and when it was being transformed into an athletics stadium for the [2014 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Commonwealth_Games), the semi-finals have been hosted at [Celtic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Park) and [Ibrox Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrox_Stadium), also in Glasgow.[^cite_note-Semis_CelticPark_Ibrox-13][^cite_note-Scotsman_venues-14]

The interior of a football stadium.

The semi-final and final games are hosted at [Hampden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden_Park).

Hampden Park also usually hosts the final match of the tournament.[^cite_note-Rules-1] The venue – across three sites in close proximity bearing the name – has hosted the majority of finals including the first in [1874](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1874_Scottish_Cup_Final). Other venues that have hosted the final in the tournament's early years are [Hamilton Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Crescent), [Kinning Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinning_Park_\(sports_ground\)) and [the first Cathkin Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathkin_Park_\(1872%E2%80%931903\)); all in Glasgow (although just outside the city boundaries at the time). The last game of the [1896 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896_Scottish_Cup_Final) is the only final that has been hosted outside Glasgow when rivals Heart of Midlothian and [Hibernian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibernian_F.C.) played at [New Logie Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Logie_Green) in Edinburgh.[^cite_note-Logie_Green_final-15] Hampden Park has held world and European records for the highest attendance, some of which were recorded at Scottish Cup games. The [1937 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Scottish_Cup_Final) played between [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_F.C.) and [Celtic](69708.md) attracted a crowd of 147,365 spectators[^cite_note-SFA_TheCup-4][^cite_note-SFM_1937_Final-16] which was a world record for a national cup final and remains a European record.[^cite_note-SFA_TheCup-4]

### European qualification

As [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Football_Association) is a member of the [Union of European Football Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_European_Football_Associations) (UEFA), the winner of the Scottish Cup qualifies to compete in European-wide competitions organised by UEFA. Between 1960 and 1998, the Scottish Cup winners qualified for the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) along with winners of other domestic cup competitions across Europe before it was abolished.[^cite_note-UCWC_Format-17] The Scottish Cup winners now qualify to compete in the following season's [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) (formerly known as the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup)).[^cite_note-UEL_regulations-18] It is possible for the Scottish Cup winners to have already qualified for a UEFA competition through their league ranking in the [Scottish Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Premiership). In this scenario, the qualification spot passes to the highest ranked team in that competition not yet qualified, rather than to the Scottish Cup runners-up.[^cite_note-UEL_Qualification-19][^cite_note-new_approach-20] Until 2014, the Scottish Cup runners-up qualified for European competition if the cup winners had also qualified for the [Champions League](44220.md).[^cite_note-new_approach-20]

## History



[Celtic F.C.](69708.md) are the side with the most Scottish Cup victories. The trophy is pictured second from left, alongside the [1907–08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1907%E2%80%9308_Scottish_Cup) team

The Scottish Football Association was founded in 1873 and the Scottish Cup was created as an annual competition for its members.[^cite_note-SFA_brief_history-21] The first Scottish Cup match took place on 18 October 1873 when [Renton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renton_F.C.) defeated [Kilmarnock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilmarnock_F.C.) 2–0 in the first round.[^cite_note-SFA_then\&now-22] In its early years, the competition was dominated by [Queen's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Park_F.C.) who won the final 10 times in the first twenty years.[^cite_note-SFA_previous_winners-23] [Vale of Leven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_Leven_F.C.), [Dumbarton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumbarton_F.C.) and Renton were also successful during this period.[^cite_note-SFA_TheCup-4] In 1885, the record margin of victory in the tournament was recorded when [Arbroath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbroath_F.C.) defeated [Bon Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Accord_F.C.) [36–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbroath_36%E2%80%930_Bon_Accord) in a first round match.[^cite_note-SFA_TheCup-4][^cite_note-SFA_previous_winners-23] It was also the highest scoring professional football game recorded in history.

### Trophy



Balloon in the shape of the Scottish Cup trophy (2013)

The Scottish Cup trophy is the oldest surviving national trophy and also the oldest surviving association football trophy in the world.[^cite_note-Guinness_Record-24] (The original FA Cup trophy predated it, but was stolen in 1895 and never recovered.)[^cite_note-Scotsman_trophy-25] It was made by [silversmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silversmith) Martin Hall & Co in London in 1873 and has been presented to the winners of the tournament since 1874.[^cite_note-Scotsman_trophy-25] The solid [silver](27119.md) trophy is 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) in height and weighs 2.25 kg (72 ozt).[^cite_note-SFA_previous_winners-23] The original trophy is displayed at the [Scottish Football Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Football_Museum) at [Hampden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden_Park).[^cite_note-damage-26] It is removed once each year to be cleaned and presented to the tournament winners.[^cite_note-Evening_Times_trophy-27] After the presentation ceremony, the trophy is returned to the museum.[^cite_note-Scottish_Football_Museum-28] A replica of the original trophy is given to the tournament winners after the ceremony and is also used for promotional purposes.[^cite_note-damage-26]

### Domestic double and treble

See also: [List of football clubs in Scotland by major honours won](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_clubs_in_Scotland_by_major_honours_won)

Clubs that win the Scottish Cup can complete a domestic "[double](88184.md#Scotland)" by becoming [Scottish league champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Scottish_football_champions) in the same season. Only three clubs have won both competitions in the same season.[^cite_note-Double_RSSSF-33] Celtic have completed the domestic league and Scottish Cup double on 22 occasions, followed by Rangers on 18.[^cite_note-Double_RSSSF-33] The only other Scottish club to achieve this feat was Aberdeen, in [1983–84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_in_Scottish_football).[^cite_note-Double_RSSSF-33] Since the creation of the [Scottish League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_League_Cup) in 1947, clubs can complete a domestic [treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)#Domestic_trebles) by also winning this tournament in the same season. Celtic have achieved this feat on eight occasions, a world record achieved in 2023.[^cite_note-Double_RSSSF-33] Celtic won four consecutive domestic trebles ("quadruple treble") in 2016–17, 2017–18, 2018–19 and 2019–20. No team had previously won consecutive trebles.

### Cup "shocks"

A football match.

[Second-tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_football_league_system) club [Airdrieonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airdrieonians_F.C._\(1878\)) played in the [1995 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Scottish_Cup_Final) against top-tier [Celtic](69708.md).

Some clubs have become renowned for eliminating higher ranked clubs from the tournament despite being underdogs. [Division Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Football_League_Division_Two) club [East Fife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Fife_F.C.) won the tournament in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Scottish_Cup_Final) by defeating [Division One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Football_League_Division_One) club [Kilmarnock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilmarnock_F.C.), the first team from outside the top-tier of league football to win the trophy. East Fife had previously reached the final in [1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1927_Scottish_Cup_Final) after eliminating three higher ranked clubs in the preceding rounds.[^cite_note-LondonHearts_Shocks-34] [Hibernian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibernian_F.C.) became the second tier-two side to win the cup as they defeated [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangers_F.C.), who were also then in the second tier, in the [2016 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Scottish_Cup_Final). Only one other club from outside the top-tier of league football has won the competition; [non-league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-league_football#Other_countries) [Queen's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Park_F.C.) defeated [Celtic](69708.md) in the [1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_Scottish_Cup_Final) final.[^cite_note-35] Several other clubs have reached the final whilst competing outside the top tier of league football, but were defeated.[^cite_note-36]

In the rounds before the final, some notable shocks have occurred. In 1959, [Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee_F.C.) were eliminated by Highland League club [Fraserburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraserburgh_F.C.) despite having [Scotland internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) in their squad.[^cite_note-Scotsman_shocks-37][^cite_note-Dundee_shock-38] A season later, [Eyemouth United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyemouth_United_F.C.) reached the quarter-finals after defeating two higher league clubs.[^cite_note-Eyemouth_QF-39] In 1967, [Berwick Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Rangers_F.C.) [eliminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Rangers_F.C._1%E2%80%930_Rangers_F.C.) defending champions Rangers in the first round.[^cite_note-Shocks_BBC-40]

Celtic's shock defeat by First Division club Inverness Caledonian Thistle in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999-2000_Scottish_Cup) led to the famous[^cite_note-Super_Caley_BBC-41] newspaper headline "[Super Caley go ballistic, Celtic are atrocious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Caley_go_ballistic,_Celtic_are_atrocious)".[^cite_note-Super_Caley_BBC-41] In the [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Scottish_Cup) competition, [Highland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Football_League) club [Brora Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brora_Rangers_F.C.) knocked out [Championship](40057914.md) leaders Heart of Midlothian, who had been runners-up in each of the two previous seasons.[^cite_note-42] Cup holders [St Johnstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Johnstone_F.C.) were knocked out by [League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_League_Two) (fourth tier) club [Kelty Hearts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelty_Hearts_F.C.) in [2021–22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Scottish_Cup).[^cite_note-43]

[Drumchapel United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drumchapel_United_F.C.) of the [West of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_of_Scotland_Football_League) First Division, a seventh tier league in the [Scottish pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_football_league_system), defeated [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_League_One) side (third tier) [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_City_F.C.) in the [2022–23 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Scottish_Cup).[^cite_note-drumchapel-44] This was the biggest statistical cup shock in the history of the competition, with 62 places separating the teams in the leagues at the time.[^cite_note-drumchapel-44] Later in that season, West of Scotland Premier Division (sixth tier) side [Darvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darvel_F.C.) knocked out Premiership club [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_F.C.), with 56 places separating the two teams.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

In [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Scottish_Cup), Rangers were eliminated in the Fifth round by mid-table second tier Queen's Park after a 1–0 defeat at [Ibrox Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrox_Stadium). It was the first time in Rangers' history that they lost a home tie to a lower division team in the competition, as well as the first time since 1967 that they had been eliminated by a lower division rival. Queen's Park had not defeated their [historic Glasgow rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_Glasgow_derby) in any competition since 1948, and had not won a Scottish Cup meeting since 1882.[^cite_note-Sky2025-47][^cite_note-Guardian2025-48][^cite_note-Independent2025-49]

Other results regarded as shocks include [Stenhousemuir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenhousemuir_F.C.)'s win against Aberdeen in 1995,[^cite_note-Scotsman_shocks-37] and [Albion Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion_Rovers_F.C.)' defeat of [Motherwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motherwell_F.C.) in 2013.[^cite_note-Albion_2013_BBC-50]

## Sponsorship

The Scottish Cup has been [sponsored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponsor_\(commercial\)) several times since the first organisation backed the tournament in 1983. The sponsor has been able to determine the name of the competition.[^cite_note-Scotsman_end-51] There have been five sponsors since 1983 as well as several name changes within the duration of each sponsorship. The competition relies on revenue earned from these agreements although it ran without a title sponsor for over 100 years until the late 1980s.[^cite_note-Health_row_Herald-52]

| Period | Sponsor | Name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1873–1982 | No sponsor | Scottish Cup |
| 1983–88 | Scottish Health Education Group | Scottish Cup[^cite_note-Scotsman_end-51][^cite_note-Health_row_Herald-52] |
| 1988–89 | No sponsor | Scottish Cup |
| 1989–2007 | [Tennent Caledonian Breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellpark_Brewery) | Tennent's Scottish Cup[^cite_note-53] |
| 2007–08 | No sponsor | Scottish Cup |
| 2008–10 | [Scottish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government) | [Homecoming Scottish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homecoming_Scotland_2009) in 2008–09[^cite_note-Homecoming_BBC-54] and the Active Nation Scottish Cup in 2009–10[^cite_note-activenation-55] |
| 2010–11 | No sponsor | Scottish Cup |
| 2011–20 | [William Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hill_\(bookmaker\)) | William Hill Scottish Cup[^cite_note-williamhill-56][^cite_note-57] |
| 2020–23 | No sponsor | Scottish Cup |
| 2023–present | [Scottish Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gas) | Scottish Gas Men's Scottish Cup[^cite_note-Scottish_FA-5] |

The Scottish Health Education Group was the first organisation to sponsor the Scottish Cup in 1983 with the largest sponsorship package in Scottish football at the time, worth around £200,000.[^cite_note-Herald_SHEG-58] The partnership was praised for the promotion of a healthy lifestyle linked with football.[^cite_note-Health_row_Herald-52] The deal ended in 1989 when [Tennent Caledonian Breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennent_Caledonian_Breweries) won the sponsorship rights. Tennent's association with the tournament raised the debate about alcohol sponsorship within sports following the riots at the [1980 Scottish Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Scottish_Cup_Final) which resulted in the sale of alcohol being banned at Scottish sporting events.[^cite_note-Health_row_Herald-52] Despite this controversy, the partnership was largely successful and lasted 18 years until 2007. The SFA received around £25 million over the duration of the sponsorship deal.[^cite_note-Scotsman_end-51] The [Scottish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government) in association with businessman [Willie Haughey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Haughey) sponsored the Scottish Cup between 2008 and 2010. The [2008–09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Scottish_Cup) competition was known as the Homecoming Scottish Cup to promote Scotland's year of homecoming and tourism.[^cite_note-Homecoming_BBC-54] The [2009–10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Scottish_Cup) competition was known the Active Nation Scottish Cup to promote a healthy living through football.[^cite_note-activenation-55] [Carling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carling_brewery) was an additional sponsor between 2010 and 2014 as the competition's official beer.[^cite_note-carling-59]

## Media coverage

### UK and Ireland

Scottish Cup matches are currently broadcast live by both [BBC Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Scotland) in Scotland and [Premier Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Sports) across the rest of the United Kingdom and Ireland.[^cite_note-60]

[BBC Radio Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Scotland) provide radio coverage including several full live commentaries with additional commentaries broadcast on Radio Scotland's local frequencies. Radio broadcasting rights are also held by [BBC Radio nan Gàidheal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_nan_G%C3%A0idheal) and [BBC Radio 5 Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_5_Live) also carry some games.

The Scottish Cup Final is one of several events reserved for live broadcast in Scotland [terrestrial television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_television) under the [Ofcom Code on Sports and Other Listed and Designated Events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofcom_Code_on_Sports_and_Other_Listed_and_Designated_Events).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]
