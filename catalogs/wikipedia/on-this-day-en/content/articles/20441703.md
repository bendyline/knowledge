**Peavine Peak**, the highest point on Peavine mountain, is located in [Washoe County, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_County,_Nevada),[^cite_note-gnis-3] at the northwest corner of the [Truckee Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_Meadows) and about 3.5 miles (5.6 km) due east of the California state line. It forms one of the most dominant geographical features in the [Reno/Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_metropolitan_area) area. Early prospectors to the mountain discovered wild pea vines growing in the vicinity of Peavine Springs on the northeast flank of the mountain, near [Poeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poeville,_Nevada), hence the name.[^cite_note-Nevada_Place_Names-4]

## Geology

Much of the northern Truckee Meadows is built atop Peavine [alluvium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium), most significantly from just west of Lake Park in Northwest [Reno](26388.md), across the campus of the [University of Nevada, Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Reno), and toward [Proctor Hug High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hug_High_School) in Northeast Reno. Above these alluvial deposits, the sediments become coarser and are frequently interspersed with [diatomaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatomaceous_earth) material.[^cite_note-Map-5]

Where the gentler lower slopes of the foothills join the significantly steeper slopes of the mountain proper, the sediments give way to their igneous parents. [U.S. Route 395](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_395) traverses the so-called *Alta Formation*, which is composed largely of [pyroxene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroxene) [andesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite) flows, flow [breccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breccia), and [laharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) breccia. On the flanks of Peavine Mountain, the Alta Formation is frequently interrupted by [rhyolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyolite) plugs and [hornblende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornblende) [andesites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite) of the Kate Peak Formation.[^cite_note-Map-5]

The higher reaches of the mountain display large outcroppings of [granodiorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granodiorite), many of which predominate the features on the upper reaches of the south slope. In addition, one of the most common features of Peavine's geology is the so-called Peavine Sequence, [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic)-aged[^cite_note-6] altered and unaltered [metavolcanic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metavolcanic_rock) composed of a variety of material, including rhyolite flows and [pyroclastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_rock), [dacite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacite), andesite, and laharic breccias.[^cite_note-Map-5]

## History

### Native American presence

With the discovery of dozens of archaeological sites on Peavine and in the surrounding areas, it is apparent that aboriginal Great Basin cultures were active in this area. The predominant cultures were those of the [Washo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_people) and [Northern Paiute peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Paiute_people), their artifacts having been found at locations ranging from Bull Ranch, at the southern terminus of Bull Ranch Creek on the southern flank of the mountain, to the shores of White Lake north of the Peavine (Many of these sites have been found above the 6,000 foot level). Extant artifacts have been traced to both the [Kings Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Beach,_California) (ca. 500-1500 A.D.) and [Martis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martis_people) (ca. 2000 B.C. to 500A.D.) cultures at sites primarily located within the Truckee River drainage.[^cite_note-Mentzelia_pp._50-57-7]

### Modern history and development

Mineral values were extracted from placer deposits as early as 1856 by prospectors returning to California. These small-scale operations lasted only as long as the water in the stream beds permitted, at which times the miners would clear off and move on. Such intermittent placer mining continued through the 1860s.[^cite_note-Mentzelia_pp._50-57-7]

A prospector named John Poe, allegedly a cousin of [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe),[^cite_note-Nevada_Place_Names-4] came to the area ca. 1862, and soon established the Poe Mining Company. He began work at the Paymaster Mine, and developed and platted the townsite of [Poeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poeville,_Nevada) on the northeast side of the mountain.[^cite_note-8] Poeville maintained a post office from September 1, 1874 to March 22, 1878.[^cite_note-Nevada_Place_Names-4]

Aside from Poeville, at least two townsites developed, however briefly, on or immediately near Peavine Mountain:

- Webster, named for [Daniel Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Webster), was platted in August, 1873

- Brooklyn, named for the [New York](645042.md) [Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_New_York_City), was settled in March, 1875, on the southeast face of the mountain about midway between Reno and [Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdi,_Nevada). ## Recreation

Due to its proximity to the Reno-Sparks area, Peavine is attractive to and popular with all varieties of outdoor enthusiasts. Under the stewardship of the [Humboldt–Toiyabe National Forest](224701.md) (HTNF), many miles of hiking and mountain bike trails have been added, and the varied geography provides a range of challenges. Peavine is also a favorite for off-road vehicles, although access has become increasingly restricted in the past fifteen years from Northwest Reno, due largely to residential building in that area. The HTNF maintains assorted access points across this area on the southwest flank of the mountain, some allowing for motor vehicles, with others designed for pedestrian traffic; access from the east and the north is substantially less regulated.

To service and install the radio towers and transmitters located on the peak and on several promontories, an access road was constructed, winding up the north and east faces of the mountain. Weather permitting, this road is passable by most two-wheel-drive vehicles with modest undercarriage clearance and extends to the uppermost summit.
