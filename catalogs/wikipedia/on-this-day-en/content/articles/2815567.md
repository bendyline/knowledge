Map of Chhindwara District

**Chhindwara district** (Hindi pronunciation: [\[t͡ʃʰɪn̪d̪ʋaːɽaː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu)) is a [district of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Madhya_Pradesh) state in India, and [Chhindwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhindwara) town is the district headquarters. Chhindwara was the largest district in [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) with an area of 10,293 square km before the bifurcation of [Pandhurna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandhurna_District) district. The district is part of [Jabalpur division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_division). chindwara district is located in south part of Madhya Pradesh and Chhindwara District, located in the Satpura Range of Madhya Pradesh, India

The name Chhindwara has been derived from the word [*Chhind*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_sylvestris), the local name of a tree found commonly in the district. Chhindwara district was formed on 1 November 1956.[^cite_note-MSME-1]

It is on the southwest region of the Satpura Range.[^cite_note-MSME-1] It is spread from 21.28 to 22.49 deg. North (latitude) and 78.40 to 79.24 deg. East (longitude) and spread over an area of 11,815 km<sup>2</sup>. This district is bound by the plains of [Pandhurna district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandhurna_district) and [Nagpur district](3356007.md) (in [Maharashtra](20629.md) State) on the south, [Narmadapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmadapuram_district) and [Narsinghpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narsinghpur_district) districts on the north, [Betul district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betul_district) on the west and [Seoni district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoni_district) on the east.[^cite_note-MSME-1]

## History

The early history of Chhindwara is not well-known. Based on copper-plate inscriptions found in Chhindwara and neighbouring Seoni districts, it was speculated that the Vakatakas were in power here until the 3rd century CE. By the late 7th century their power faded and the dynasty was ruled by the Gaulis. The southern part of the district, like the Nagpur plain to the south, was ruled by the Rashtrakutas for several centuries. A copper plate inscription from the 9th century CE gives a village named Mohagrama to a Kannada Brahmin, which some scholars have identified as [Mohgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohgaon) in southern Chhindwara.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

Until the advent of the Gond dynasties in the 14th century, Chhindwara's history is scant. The western part of the district could have been part of the Kherla kingdom in nearby Betul. [Deogarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deogarh,_Madhya_Pradesh) on the Chhindwara plateau is believed to be the last seat of Gauli power. According to legend, founder of the Gond dynasty, Jatha slew and supplanted the Gauli chiefs Ransur and Ghamsur during a temple festival.[^cite_note-gondhitoryd-3] Briefly the Deogarh kingdom was the most powerful of the four Gond states, but was quickly made into a tribute-paying sarkar under the [Berar subah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berar_Subah) of the Mughals. The Deogarh sarkar included parts of Chhindwara and Nagpur districts. King Bakht Bulund was most powerful in the dynasty and he has adopted [Islam](6037917.md) as his religion during the rule of Emperor [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb). By 1720, under Bakht Buland's leadership, Deogarh was largely independent from Mughal control. Bakht Buland mainly resided in Deogarh and conquered large territories from the kingdoms of Mandla and Chanda. When Aurangzeb's army was faltering, Bakht Buland took the opportunity to attack Mughal territory on both sides of the Wardha river. He and his sons encouraged the migration of many non-tribal cultivators to the Berar plains and the plateau region. After his son Chand Sultan's death, one of his illegitimate sons claimed the throne and his widow called the Marathas for aid. The Marathas conquered Deogarh and it became the territory of the Bhonsles of Nagpur.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

On 17 September 1803, the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) had taken over this kingdom by defeating [Raghoji II Bhonsle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghoji_II_Bhonsle), starting the British rule. Nagpur state continued to administer the territory until in 1853 it was annexed as part of the Doctrine of Lapse. After the 1857 rebellion, Chhindwara district became part of the [Nerbudda Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerbudda_Division) of the [Central Provinces and Berar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces_and_Berar), which became the state of Madhya Bharat (later [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md)) after India's independence in 1947.[^cite_note-4] After Independence, [Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur) was made the capital of Chhindwara District, and on 1 November 1956, when Maharashtra was formed and took Nagpur, this district was re-constituted with Chhindwara as the capital.

The altitude of the district varies from 1,550 ft (470 m) to 3,820 ft (1,160 m) above sea level with an average elevation of 2215 feet (675 m).[^cite_note-\:1-5]

There are five major rivers which flow through the district — the [Kanhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanhan_River), the [Pench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pench_River), the [Jam River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jam_River), the [Kulbehra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulbehra_River), the [Shakkar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakkar_River) and the [Dudhi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudhi_River).[^cite_note-\:1-5]

According to the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India) Chhindwara District has a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 2,090,922,[^cite_note-districtcensus-9] This gives it a ranking of 218th in India (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-districtcensus-9] The district has a population density of 177 inhabitants per square kilometre (460/sq mi).[^cite_note-districtcensus-9] Its [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) over the decade 2001-2011 was 13.03%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-9] Chhindwara has a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 966 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) for every 1000 males,[^cite_note-districtcensus-9] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 72.21%. 24.16% of the population lives in urban areas. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes made up 11.11% and 36.82% of the population respectively.[^cite_note-districtcensus-9]

After the bifurcation of Pandhurna district, Chhindwara district had a population of 1,716,612. Chhindwara has a sex ratio of 968 females per 1000 males and a literacy rate of 69.43%. 23.09% of the population lives in urban areas. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes made up 194,788 (11.35%) and 673,263 (39.22%) of the population respectively.[^cite_note-districtcensus-9]

There are 1,984 villages in the district, out of which 1,903 villages are inhabited. It is divided into 19 Revenue Circles, 319 Patwari Halkas. There are 808 [Panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayats) in the district. Chhindwara is the Parliamentary Constituency in the district and there are eight assembly segments (Junnardeo, Chhindwara, Parasia, Damua, [Amarwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarwara_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)), Chourai, Sausar and Pandhurna). As per Census 2001 the population of Chhindwara town was 1,22,309 and of the district is 18,48,882 with a population density of 156 people per km<sup>2</sup>. There are 953 females for every 1000 males.

The sex ratio of rural Chhindwara is more (962) than that of urban Chhindwara (926). As per Census 2001, the average literacy rate of the district was 66.03%, which is above the average of the MP state's 64.08%. The literacy rate in the rural area of the district is 60.76% and that of urban area is 81.46%.

Hindus are 92.01%, Muslims are 4.82% and Buddhists are 1.17%. Other religions (mainly tribal traditions) are 1.23%.[^cite_note-Religion-8]

At the time of the [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), 92.97% of the population in the residual district spoke [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), 2.47% [Gondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondi_language), 1.88% [Korku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korku_language) and 1.73% [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language) as their first language.[^cite_note-language-10]

Hindi is the primary language throughout the district. The local dialect in Chhindwara is a variant of western Hindi related to Bundeli. [Pawari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhoyari) is a name for a dialect of spoken by the Kshatriya Pawar/Bhoyar community in this region. Gondi was formerly spoken by over 30% of the population in 1931, and over 75% of Gonds. This percentage has decreased rapidly as most Gonds have shifted to Hindi. Today, it is only spoken by shrinking minorities in Junnardeo, Mokhed and Bichhua tehsils and has vanished everywhere else. Korku, primarily the Mawasi dialect, is spoken in the same regions as Gondi and is experiencing a similar steep decline. In Patalkot, Tamia tehsil, is spoken the poorly-known [Bhariati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhariati) language, an unclassified Indo-Aryan language. All three tribal languages are highly endangered.

Chhindwara has a [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate) bordering [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate). It has a hot, dry summer (April–June) followed by [monsoon](57630.md) rains (July–September) and a cool and relatively dry winter. Average annual rainfall is 1,183 mm. Minimum temperature during winter is 4 to 6 degrees Celsius while maximum temperature during summer is 38 to 42 degrees Celsius.
