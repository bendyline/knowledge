The **Sistan and Baluchestan province** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان سيستان و بلوچستان),[^cite_note-4] simply referred to as **Sistan-Baluchestan**, is the second largest of the 31 [provinces of Iran](448160.md), after [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_Province) province, with an area of 180,726 km<sup>2</sup>. Its capital is the city of [Zahedan](1044089.md).[^cite_note-Sistan_and_Baluchestan_Province_Structure-5] The province is in the southeast of the country, bordering [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

The province is mainly populated by the [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochs) ethnicity with a small minority of [Sistani Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistani_Persians). Unlike the rest of Iran, the majority of the population is [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim), and the local culture is heavily influenced by neighboring [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-8]

## Etymology

The name of the region was simply *[Balochistan](8431743.md)* at first (to refer to Balochistan as a whole), but later became *Baluchistan and Sistan* before becoming *Sistan and Baluchestan*.[^cite_note-The_Diplomat-9][^cite_note-ADVENTURE_IRAN-10][^cite_note-11] Balochistan simply means "land of the Baloch".

## History

Further information: [History of Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Balochistan)

### Pre-Islamic era

In the inscriptions at [Behistun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun) and [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis), [Sistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistan) is mentioned as one of the eastern territories of [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great). The name Sistan is derived from [Saka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka) (also sometimes *Saga*, or *[Sagastan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakastan)*), a [Central Asian](6742.md) tribe that had taken control over this area in the year 128 BC. During the [Arsacid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_Empire) (248 BC to 224 AD), the province became the seat of [Suren-Pahlav clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suren-Pahlav_Clan). From the [Sassanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid) period until the early Islamic period, Sistan flourished considerably.

### Post-Islamic era

During the reign of [Ardashir I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_I_of_Persia), Sistan came under the jurisdiction of the Sassanians, and in 644 AD, the [Arab Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims) gained control as the Persian empire. During the reign of the [*sahaba*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companions_of_the_Prophet) and second [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [*caliph*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate), [Umar ibn al-Khattab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_ibn_Al-Khattab), this territory was conquered by the Arabs and an [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) commander was assigned as governor. The famous [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) ruler [Ya'qub ibn al-Layth al-Saffar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya'qub_ibn_al-Layth_al-Saffar), whose descendants dominated this area for many centuries, later became governor of this province. In 916 AD, Balochistan was ruled by the [Daylamids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buwayhid) and thereafter the [Seljuqids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks), when it became a part of [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_Province). Dynasties such as the [Saffarids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffarid_dynasty), [Samanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanid), [Ghaznavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids), and Seljuqids, also ruled over this territory.

In 1508 AD, Shah [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I) of the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids) dynasty conquered Sistan. After the assassination of [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) in 1747, Sistan-Baluchestan became part of the [Brahui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahui_people) [Khanate of Kalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Kalat), which ruled it until 1896. Afterwards, it became part of [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran).[^cite_note-s-12]

## Demographics



[Jameh Mosque of Makki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameh_Mosque_of_Makki)

### Ethnic demographics

The [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) form a majority 70–76% of the population and the [Persian Sistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistani_Persians) a minority. Smaller communities of [Kurds](17068.md) (in the eastern highlands and near [Iranshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranshahr_\(city\))); the expatriate [Brahuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahui_people) (along the border with Pakistan); and other resident and itinerant ethnic groups, such as the [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people), are also found within the province.

Most of the population are Baloch and speak the [Balochi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_language), although there also exists among them a small community of speakers of the [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages) language [Jadgali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadgali_language).[^cite_note-13]<sup>: 25 </sup>; Sistani Persians are the second largest ethnic group in this province who speak the [Sistani dialect of Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistani_dialect).

### Religion

The majority of the [Baloch people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) of the Balochistan area in the province are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) Muslims, belonging to [Hanafi school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi_school) of thought.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Geography



The southern coasts of the province along the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md)

The whole of the province had previously been called [Balochistan](8431743.md), but the government added Sistan to the end of Balochistan and became *Baluchestan and Sistan*. After the [1979 Iranian revolution](347268.md), the name of the province was changed to *Sistan and Baluchestan*.[^cite_note-The_Diplomat-9][^cite_note-ADVENTURE_IRAN-10]

Today, Sistan refers to the area comrising [Zabol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabol_County), [Hamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamun_County), [Hirmand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirmand_County), [Zehak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zehak_County), and [Nimruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimruz_County) Counties.[^cite_note-Sistan_Dust-47] The province borders [South Khorasan province](1289358.md) in the north, [Kerman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province) and [Hormozgan province](448064.md) in the west, the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md) in the south, and Afghanistan and Pakistan in the east.

### Climate

Sistan-Baluchestan province is one of the driest regions of Iran, with a slight increase in rainfall from east to west, and a rise in humidity in the coastal regions. The province is subject to seasonal winds from different directions, the most important of which are the "[120-day wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/120-day_wind)" of Sistan, known in Balochi as *Levar*; the seventh wind (*Gav-kosh*); the south wind (*Nambi*); the *Hooshak* wind; the humid and seasonal winds of the Indian Ocean; the north wind (*Gurich*); and the western wind (*Gard*).

In 2023, the Sistan region was affected by several dust events, occurring in April,[^cite_note-48] June,[^cite_note-Sistan_Dust-47] and August. The latter sent 1120 people to hospitals from 10 to 14 August. Winds reached a speed of 108 km/h (67 mph) in Zabol station and reduced visibility to 600 m (2,000 ft).[^cite_note-49]

## Economy



Mountains in [Chabahar County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabahar_County)

Sistan-Baluchestan is the poorest of Iran's 31 [provinces](448160.md), with a [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI) score of 0.688.[^cite_note-GlobalDataLab-3] The government of Iran has been implementing new plans such as creating the [Chabahar Free Trade-Industrial Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabahar_Free_Trade-Industrial_Zone) (CFZ).



View of the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) from Chabahar

Industry is new to the province. Efforts have been done and tax, customs and financial motivations have caused more industrial investment, new projects, new producing jobs, and improvement of industry. The most important factories are the Khash cement factory with production of 2600 tons cement daily.

The province has important geological and metal mineral potentials such as: chrome, copper, granite, antimony, talc, manganese, iron, lead, zinc, tin, nickel, platinum, [gold](12240.md), and silver.

One of the main mines in the province is the Chel Kooreh copper mine 120 km north of Zahedan.

[Sistani embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistan_embroidery) has been an ancient handicraft of the region that has been traced as far back as [5th-century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_century_BC), originating from the [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians).[^cite_note-\:0-50]

## Water

Further information: [Water scarcity in Iran § Water shortage and the IRGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_scarcity_in_Iran#Water_shortage_and_the_IRGC)

### Shortage of water

[Iran](14653.md) ranks among the most [water stressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_scarcity_in_Iran) countries in the world. Sistan-Baluchestan province suffers from major water problems that were aggravated by corruption in Iran's [water supply](684928.md) sector, lack of transparency, neglect of marginalized communities, and [political favoritism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-group_favoritism). Water diversion projects in the [Isfahan](448061.md) and [Yazd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazd_province) provinces receive priority despite critical shortages in [Khuzestan](448079.md) and Sistan-Baluchestan.

Iran's central government prioritizes water allocation for industrial and urban centers, often at the expense of rural and minority populations. These groups face severe water shortages, ecological degradation, and a loss of livelihoods. This pattern of unequal development not only exacerbates regional disparities but also fuels social unrest and environmental crises. Iran's water policy is also characterized by an overreliance on dam construction and large-scale diversion projects, primarily benefiting politically connected enterprises and urban elites.

#### IRGC involvement

The [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) and other politically connected entities control water resources, prioritizing projects for political and economic gain rather than public need. They divert supplies to favored regions, causing shortages in vulnerable provinces like [Khuzestan](448079.md) and Sistan-Baluchestan.

Reports also indicate that certain agricultural and industrial enterprises with ties to the IRGC have received significant amounts of water, while small farmers and rural communities struggle with severe shortages.[^cite_note-55]

### Outcome

This has led to the drying of rivers, wetlands, and other vital ecosystems, intensifying dust storms and land subsidence in regions like Khuzestan and Sistan-Baluchestan. Such environmental degradation, combined with insufficient governmental oversight and transparency, worsens living conditions for marginalized communities, reinforcing cycles of poverty and socio-political marginalization.[^cite_note-56]
