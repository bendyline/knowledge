**Kfar Kila** or **Kfarkila** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): كفركلا, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Kfarkilā*, also *Kfarkela*, *Kafarkela*) is a municipality in [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md). The majority of its population are [Shia Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims).[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Etymology

The name Kafarkila means "the village of the pasturage".[^cite_note-2]

## History

[Al-Maqdisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Maqdisi) (c. 945/946 – 991) noted that Kafr Kila (called *Kafar Kila*) was "A place lying a day's march from [Tabariyyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabariyyah)".[^cite_note-3]

### Ottoman era

In the 1596 [tax records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter), it was named as a village, *Kafr Kuk*, in the [Ottoman](22278.md) *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of [Tibnin](6787186.md), part of [Safad Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safad_Sanjak), with a population of 31 households and 2 bachelors, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). The villagers paid taxes on agricultural products, such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), olive trees, goats, beehives and winter pastures; a total of 4,700 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-Hutteroth-4][^cite_note-5]

In 1838, [Eli Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Smith) noted Kafr Kila's population as [Metawileh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metawileh).[^cite_note-6]

In 1875 [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited and noted that the village had about 1,000 [Metawileh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metawileh) inhabitants.[^cite_note-7] He further noted: "The mosque and several of the houses are built of old materials. The spring is partly ancient."[^cite_note-8]

In 1881, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s [*Survey of Western Palestine* (SWP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine) described it as "a village, built of stone and mud, containing about 150 Moslems, situated on sloping ground, with figs, olives, and arable land around. A good spring near."[^cite_note-9]

### Modern era

The total population of the village is about 14,500. The occupants' number increases dramatically during summer. Its altitude is around 700 m from sea level.[^cite_note-10]

Many developments are currently taking place due to the efforts and the determination of the municipality side by side with the people in order to mitigate the problems that accumulated over the years from [central government](17775.md) indifference, [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War), [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_occupation_of_Lebanon) and [border wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria-Lebanon_relations).

Following the 1982 Israeli invasion Kafr Kila became part of the [Israeli security zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation_of_Southern_Lebanon_\(1982%E2%80%932000\)). On 11 September 1989 three [IDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Defence_Force) soldiers were wounded in an ambush in Kafr Kila. The [Lebanese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Communist_Party) claimed responsibility. Two of the attackers were killed.[^cite_note-11]

During the [Israel–Hezbollah conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Hezbollah_conflict_\(2023%E2%80%932024\)), much of the village's population was displaced. Israeli artillery barrages also destroyed many of the buildings.[^cite_note-\:0-1] During the [2024 Israeli invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon), the IDF claimed it has destroyed 158 Hezbollah targets in the preceding months in Kfar Kila, including 28 lookout posts and an array of weapons.[^cite_note-12] Back on October 25th almost the entire southern part of the village had been destroyed during the [2024 Kfar Kila clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kafr_Kila_clashes).

During the [2026 Lebanon War](82549840.md) Israeli officials stated that 90% of the town had been completely destroyed. Despite the destruction, reports suggest some civilians plan to come back following the end of the war,[^cite_note-\:1-13] even though such return is not guaranteed following Israeli reports stating that those displaced may never come back.[^cite_note-14]

## Demographics

As of 2010, the village had 7,000 year round residents and its population rose to 9,000 during the summer months. At least 2,500 villagers had been displaced to other parts of Lebanon by the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) and another 500 had migrated abroad.[^cite_note-15]

In 2014 [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon) made up 99.56% of registered voters in Kfar Kila. 99.41% of the voters were [Shiite Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims).[^cite_note-16]

In April 2026 the town was reported to be roughly fully depopulated after almost the entirety of it was destroyed.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]
