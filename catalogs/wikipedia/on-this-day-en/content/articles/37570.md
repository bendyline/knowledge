The **head of government** serves as the highest official in the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch) of a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)).[^cite_note-1] The head of government usually handles the day to day affairs of the state, and leads the ministers who control the government.[^cite_note-Oxford-2] In many democratic systems the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) is also the head of government.[^cite_note-Oxford-2][^cite_note-3] In non democratic systems the head of government is usually established via military intervention.[^cite_note-Oxford-2]

In most [parliamentary systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system), including [constitutional monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchies), the head of government is the *de facto* political leader of the government, and is answerable to at least [one chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambers_of_parliament) of the legislature. Although there is often a formal reporting relationship to a head of state, the latter usually acts as a [figurehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurehead) who may take the role of chief executive on limited occasions, either when receiving [constitutional advice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_advice) from the head of government or under specific provisions in a constitution.[^cite_note-4]

In [semi-presidential systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system), the head of government may answer to both the head of state and the legislature with the specifics provided by each country's constitution.[^cite_note-5]

In [communist states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state), the [general secretary of the Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party) is the supreme leader, serving as *de facto* head of state and government. In [China](5405.md), the *de jure* head of government is the [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_China). The [Chinese president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_China) is legally a [ceremonial office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurehead), but the [general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) ([top leader](262766.md) in a [one-party](102459.md) system) has always held this office since 1993 except for the months of [transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_of_power_in_the_People's_Republic_of_China).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Types

In some governments, those who are the [heads of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heads_of_state) also perform the duties of the head of government.[^cite_note-Oxford-2] The [President of the United States](24113.md) is one example.[^cite_note-8] Certain governments those roles are separate, with the head of state merely being a figurehead.[^cite_note-9] In the [United Kingdom](31717.md) the Prime Minister handles the affairs of the state, while the King/Queen of England (who is the head of state) has a largely ceremonial role with no political power.[^cite_note-10] Russia also employs a similar structure, though the role of president has full political power.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-Oxford-2] In Japan, there exists an emperor with little political power, with all government issues being handled by the prime minister.[^cite_note-12] In [Israel](9282173.md), while the [Government](5006115.md) is nominally a [collegiate body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_body) with a *primus inter pares* role for the [Prime Minister](24898.md), the Israeli Prime Minister is the dominant figure in the executive branch in practice.[^cite_note-13]

## Assumption of power

### Via election/appointment

Elections have been the usual mechanism by which representative government has operated since the 17th century.[^cite_note-rg-14] However, in most parliamentary systems the prime minister is not directly elected by voters, but is the leader of the most dominant party in parliament.[^cite_note-15] In presidential systems, the head of government is appointed by the president.[^cite_note-Britannica-16] According to Wolfgang Müller and Wilfried Philipp, heads of government have lasted in office on average 3.8 years in post-1945 Western Europe.[^cite_note-17]

### Seizure of power

In some instances, the head of government comes to political authority via force, such as in a [coup](25914154.md). In authoritarian systems, the selection of the head of government is typically not open to the general public.[^cite_note-Britannica-16]

Under [military dictatorships](146728.md), the military’s leader usually fills the role of head of state, head of government, or both.[^cite_note-Britannica-16] A military dictator may install a figurehead as head of government, but generally keeps some or all executive governing powers for themselves.[^cite_note-Britannica-16]
