**Patras** ([/pəˈtræs, ˈpætrəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-3] [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Πάτρα, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek): *Pátra* pronounced [\[ˈpatra\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/50/Patra\_-\_%CE%A0%CE%AC%CF%84%CF%81%CE%B1.ogg/Patra\_-\_%CE%A0%CE%AC%CF%84%CF%81%CE%B1.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/50/Patra_-_%CE%A0%CE%AC%CF%84%CF%81%CE%B1.ogg/Patra_-_%CE%A0%CE%AC%CF%84%CF%81%CE%B1.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Katharevousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharevousa) and [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Πάτραι;[^cite_note-4] [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Patrae*[^cite_note-5]) is the capital and largest city of [Western Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Greece), in the northern [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese), 215 km (134 mi) west of [Athens](1216.md). It is [Greece](12108.md)'s [third-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Greece). The city is built at the foot of Mount [Panachaikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panachaikon), overlooking the [Gulf of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Patras).

As of the 2021 census, the municipality of Patras has a population of 215,922, while the urban population is 173,600. The core settlement has a history spanning four millennia. In the Roman period, it had become a cosmopolitan centre of the eastern Mediterranean whilst, according to the Christian tradition, it was also the place of [Saint Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Andrew)'s [martyrdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_martyr).

Dubbed Greece's "Gate to the West", Patras is a commercial hub, while its busy port is a nodal point for trade and communication with Italy and the rest of [Western Europe](33800.md). The city has three public universities, hosting a large student population and rendering Patras an important scientific centre[^cite_note-6] with a field of excellence in technological education. The [Rio-Antirrio Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio-Antirrio_Bridge) connects Patras's easternmost suburb of [Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio,_Greece) to the town of [Antirrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antirrio), connecting the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) peninsula with mainland Greece.

Every year, in February, the city hosts one of Europe's largest carnivals. Notable features of the [Patras Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Carnival) include its mammoth satirical floats and balls and parades, enjoyed by hundreds of thousands of visitors in a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate). Patras is also famous for supporting an indigenous cultural scene active mainly in the performing arts and modern urban literature. It was [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2006.[^cite_note-7]

### Middle Ages and early modern

See also: [Byzantine Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greece), [Frankokratia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankokratia), [Principality of Achaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Achaea), [Ottoman Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Greece), and [Kingdom of the Morea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Morea)



The Principality of Achaea in southern Greece, 1278

In 1205, the city was captured by [William of Champlitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_of_Champlitte) and [Geoffrey I of Villehardouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_I_of_Villehardouin), and became a part of the [principality of Achaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Achaea). It became the seat of the [Barony of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barony_of_Patras), and its [Latin archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Archbishop_of_Patras) primate of the principality. In 1408, Patras became [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice), until it was recaptured in 1430 by the [Despotate of Morea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despotate_of_Morea) and its despot [Constantine Palaiologos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_XI), who thus succeeded in recovering for the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) the whole of the [Morea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morea), apart from Venetian possessions. The administration of Patras was given to [George Sphrantzes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sphrantzes), while Constantine was immediately contested by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and later, in 1449, became emperor of the Byzantine Empire.[^cite_note-8]

Patras remained a part of the [Despotate of Morea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despotate_of_Morea) until 1458, when it was conquered by the Sultan of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), [Mehmet II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmet_II). Under the Ottomans, it was known as "Baliabadra", from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Παλαιά Πάτρα ('Old Patras'), as opposed to Νέα Πάτρα ('new Patra'), the town of [Ypati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ypati) in [Central Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Greece_\(geographic_region\)). Though Mehmet granted the city special privileges and tax reductions, it never became a major centre of commerce. [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) attacked and captured it several times in the 15th and 16th centuries, but never re-established their rule effectively, except for a period of [Venetian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Morea) in 1687–1715 after the [Morean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morean_War).[^cite_note-9]

It has been noted that during and in the aftermath of the suppressed 1770 [Orlov revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlov_revolt) "when the Greeks got the upper hand they settled old scores; when the Turks and Albanians reasserted themselves they were merciless: recapturing Patras, they left scarcely anyone alive."[^cite_note-10]

In 1772, a [naval battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Patras_\(1772\)) took place off the city between the Russians and Ottomans.

### Modern era

Patras was one of the first cities in which the [Greek Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Revolution) began in 1821;[^cite_note-11] the Ottoman garrison, confined to the citadel, [held out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Patras_\(1821\)) until 1828. After the war, most of the city and its buildings were completely destroyed.

Patras developed quickly into the second-largest urban centre in late-19th-century Greece.[^cite_note-12] The city benefited from its role as the main export port for the agricultural produce of the Peloponnese.[^cite_note-13]

In the early 20th century, Patras developed fast and became the first Greek city to introduce public streetlights and electrified tramways.[^cite_note-14] In the Second World War, the city was a major target of Italian [air raids](323167.md). In the Axis occupation period, a German military command was established and German and Italian troops stationed in the city. After the liberation in 1944, the city recovered, but in later years was increasingly overshadowed by the urban pole of Athens. Since 2014, the city's mayor is [Kostas Peletidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostas_Peletidis).

## Urban landscape

&#x20;The city is divided into the upper and the lower section, connected with roads and broad stairs. The upper section (*Ano Poli*) is the older and the more picturesque; however, the lower section (*Kato Poli*) is laid out according to the 1858 city plan, featuring a variety of squares. The most notable of these are the *Psila Alonia* and the [Georgiou I Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgiou_I_Square). A number of notable [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) buildings are to be found, including the [Apollon Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollon_Theatre_\(Patras\)) in Georgiou I Square, the City Hall, the headquarters of the Local Trade Association and the Court of Justice. A replica of [Patras Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Lighthouse), the city's emblematic old lighthouse – which was at the dock of *[Ayios Nikolaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas)* – rises at the end of Trion Navarchon street, near the temple of Saint Andreas.

Patras and its region is home to various Ancient Greek, Roman and Byzantine Monuments, including the Roman Odeon of Patras, the Fortress of Rio and the Fortress (castle) of Patras.[^cite_note-15] More specifically, the main sights of the city are:

- The [Patras Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Patras) focuses on the exhibition of various archaeological finds, from the Mycenaean to the Late Roman era, discovered in Patras and the wider Achaea region. The museum is housed in a modern and special architectural building designed by the architect Theophanis Bobotis.

  [^cite_note-16]

- The [Mycenaean cemetery of Voudeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_cemetery_of_Voudeni) (Skioessa), 8 km (5.0 mi) from the center of Patras, is one of the most important sites of the Mycenaean world, showing active use for nearly five hundred years (1500–1000 BC). The site itself appears to have been inhabited from the Bronze Age until middle Roman times (1800 BC–AD 400).

- The Roman [Odeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_\(building\)), the most significant ancient monument, is in the upper town and was built around 160 AD, in the reign of either [Antoninus Pius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoninus_Pius) or [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius). It has been restored and partially reconstructed, and is used as an open-air theatre for performances and concerts in the summer.

- The Roman [Amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheatre), near the Roman Odeon, in Ifestou street, dating from the 1st century AD, at a period of the biggest development of Roman Patras. Its area has been only partially excavated.

  [^cite_note-17]

- The [Roman aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Aqueduct_of_Patras)

  [^cite_note-18]

  &#x20;that led from the springs of Romanos to the acropolis. The aqueduct measured 6.5 km (4.04 mi) from the water cistern to the castle. For the greater part of this distance, the water passed through an underground channel, passing over valleys and gorges on carefully constructed archways, parts of which still stand, in the area of [Aroi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aroi,_Patras).

  [^cite_note-19]

Other Roman monuments include the ruins of the Roman stadium, remains of the Roman wall and a preserved bridge over the river Kallinaos.

- The medieval [Patras Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Castle), in the ancient acropolis overlooking the city, was initially built in the 6th century AD by the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) emperor [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian), having many additions from the period of the Frankish and Venetian rule of the city, up to as far as the time of the [Despotate of Morea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despotate_of_Morea) and later the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). Its current outline dates back to the second Venetian rule of the town (1687–1715). Today, is visiting and its interior is used as a public garden.

- The orthodox church of [Saint Andrew of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Andrew_of_Patras) was founded in 1908 by [King George I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_I_of_Greece) and was inaugurated in 1974. It is dedicated to [Saint Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Andrew), the patron of the city, and contains relics of the saint at the location of his crucifixion.

  [^cite_note-standrew-20]

  &#x20;It is the second-largest temple of [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) style in the Balkans (after the [Cathedral of Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Sava) in Belgrade). The central cupola is 46 m (151 ft) tall and is the base for a 5 m (16 ft) gold-plated cross and twelve smaller ones, symbolising Christ and the twelve apostles. A congregation of at least 5,000 can attend a sermon within the church.

  [^cite_note-21]

- The municipal [Theatre Apollon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollon_Theatre_\(Patras\)), built in 1872 designed by architect [Ernst Ziller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Ziller). The building is characteristic of the 19th-century neoclassical style and is in the central square of the city.

- The [Achaia Clauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaia_Clauss) wine industry and tasting center, which is on the outskirts in [Petroto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroto,_Achaea) village. It was founded in 1861 by the [Bavarian](3764.md) Gustav Clauss and is most famous for its [Mavrodaphne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavrodafni).This place also houses the oldest wine of Greece, the old mavrodaphne of 1873.

- Residence of [Kostis Palamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostis_Palamas), a preserved neoclassical building on 241 Corinthou Street in the city center, where [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet) Kostis Palamas and the Italian painter [Matilde Serao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilde_Serao) were born.

- Old Patras Hospital, a neoclassical building on Ano Poli, near Castle, designed by architect [Christian Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Hansen_\(architect\)).

- The Ottoman baths (16th century), still retain their initial use, and are one of the oldest Ottoman baths surviving in Europe.

  [^cite_note-22]

- The [Patras Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Lighthouse), a reconstructed "Faros", a landmark of the city.

- The Agiou Nikolaou Stairs, Gerokostopoulou Stairs, Patreos Stairs and Trion Navarchon Stairs, outdoor grand staircases all over the centre of the city dividing the upper town from downtown.

- [Rio-Antirrio Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio%E2%80%93Antirrio_Bridge), is a modern landmark of place and one of the world's longest multi-span [cable-stayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable-stayed_bridge) [bridges](3397.md) and longest of the fully suspended type.

### Parks and squares

- [Georgiou I Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgiou_I_Square), the central square and the heart of the city. It was named after King [George I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_I_of_Greece). The square's fountains were installed in 1875 at a cost of 70,000 drachmas each, a huge amount for the finances of Greece and Patra at the time. It was and continues to be the center of political and cultural life in the city, hosting all significant activities, political gatherings, rallies, cultural events and, most importantly for some, its carnival.

- [Ethnikis Antistaseos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnikis_Antistaseos_Square_\(Patras\)) ("National Resistance Square")



Inside the home of Palamas

- [Kapodistria Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapodistria_Square) in the district of [Markato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markato).

- [Trion Symmachon Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trion_Symmachon_Square?action=edit\&redlink=1) bears the name of the three Allied Powers who fought in the [Battle of Navarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Navarino); Britain, France and Russia. The square features a flower clock and links the Agiou Nikolaou pedestrian way with the seaside front and the dock of Agios Nikolaos.

- [Psilalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psilalonia) Square (Ψηλαλώνια or formally Πλατεία Υψηλών Αλωνίων) is one of Patras's most popular squares. It is 1.5 km (0.93 mi) from downtown Patras, next to the city's main north–south street, Gounari Street. It features a fountain, many sidewalks, [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_tree) trees and playgrounds. It is surrounded by several shops, restaurants and cafes and a number of [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture) buildings. It was completed in the mid-to-late 19th century, when trees were added, along with neoclassical buildings. After World War II and the [Greek Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Civil_War), however, and through the 1960s and 1970s, most neoclassical buildings were replaced by eight-storey residential buildings.  In the west end, a 15-metre-tall (49 ft) cliff overlooks the pedestrian [Trion Navarchon Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trion_Navarchon_Street?action=edit\&redlink=1), and offers a wide vista across the western [Gulf of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Patras), including the mountains of [Aitoloakarnania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aetolia-Acarnania).

  

  The declaration of the revolutionaries of Patras (1821), engraved on a stele, Saint George Square

- Saint George Square (Πλατεία Αγίου Γεωργίου). There is the monument to the fighters of 1821 on which is engraved the "declaration of the revolutionaries of Patras to the states of Europe" (22 March 1821).

- The Spinney of Patras (Δασύλλιο), is in a pine-tree-covered hill, which is dubbed "the [Gulf of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Patras)'s veranda" because of the panoramic view it offers. The spinney is ideal for recreational walks and jogging, with its specially formed paths and the shade offered by the tall trees.

### Architecture



External view of the Roman Οdeon

Patra is a relatively newly built city, as its medieval buildings were completely destroyed in the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence). The oldest surviving buildings (apart from ancient monuments and the castle) are the church of [Pantocrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantocrator_Church,_Patras) in Ano Poli and a residential building (Tzini's house) at the corner of Agiou Nikolaou and Maisonos street, built in 1832. The area on the south of the castle, around the Roman [Odeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_\(building\)), the church of Pantokrator, in the Upper Town (Ano Poli), is the most appealing of the city, because of its status as the only area where construction height is limited to two-storey buildings.[^cite_note-23] Ιn Ano Poli is interesting the old school complex "Georgios Glarakis" work of the architect Georgios Petrιtsopoulos in 1931 which is built with stone and recently became a nice bioclimatic school. At the beginning of the 20th century, outside the school complex "Georgios Glarakis", line 2 of the tram ended, starting from Agios Dionysios, going up Dimitriou Gounari Street, passing behind the church of Pantanassa, entering Roman Odeon and finished outside the Glarakis school complex. Historical buildings and mansions of the city, apart Tzini's house, include also the Prapopoulos building, Golfinopoulos mansion (*Alhambra*), Perivolaropoulos mansion, Palamas house, while among the demolished after WWII were Tsiklitiras mansion, Kanellopoulos house, Chaidopoulos building, Frangopoulos house, Green mansion and Mineyko mansion.[^cite_note-24]

## Geography

Patras is 215 km (134 mi) west of Athens by road, 94 km (58 mi) northeast of [Pyrgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrgos,_Elis), 7 km (4.3 mi) south of [Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio,_Greece), 134 km (83 miles) west of [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinth_\(modern_city\)), 77 km (48 miles) northwest of [Kalavryta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalavryta) and 144 km (89 mi) northwest of [Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli,_Greece).

A central feature of the [urban geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_geography) of Patras is its division into upper and lower sections. This is the result of an interplay between natural geography and human settlement patterns; the lower section of the city (Kato Poli), which includes the 19th-century urban core and the port, is adjacent to the sea and stretches between the estuaries of the rivers of Glafkos and Haradros. It is built on what was originally a bed of river soils and dried-up swamps. The older upper section (Ano Poli) covers the area of the pre-modern settlement, around the Fortress, on what is the last elevation of Mount Panachaikon (1,926 m (6,319 ft))[^cite_note-ABC-25] before the Gulf of Patras.

### Hydrology

The largest river in the area is the [Glafkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glafkos_\(river\)), flowing to the south of Patras. Glafkos springs in Mount [Panachaikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panachaikon) and its water is, since 1925, collected in a small mountainous reservoir-dam near the village of Souli and subsequently pumped in order to provide energy for the country's first [hydroelectric plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_plant).[^cite_note-26] Other smaller streams are *[Charadros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charadros)*, *[Meilichos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meilichos)*, *Kallinaos*, *[Panagitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagitsa_\(stream\))* and the mountain torrent *Diakoniaris*.

### Climate

Patras, like much of [Western Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Greece), has a hot-summer [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*). It features the typical mild, wet winters and hot, dry summers, with spring and autumn being pleasant transitional seasons. Autumn in Patras, however, is wetter than spring. Humidity is high during the winter months and lower during the summer. Snowfall is extremely rare in coastal areas but is more common and abundant in the surrounding mountains. Patras falls in 10b [hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone).[^cite_note-meteo.gr-27]

### Ecology

Of great importance for the biological diversity of the area and the preservation of its climate is the swamp of [Agyia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agyia), a small and coastal [aquatic ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_ecosystem) of only 30 ha (74 acres), north of the city centre. The main features of this wetland are its apparent survival difficulty, being at the heart of a densely populated urban centre that features a relatively arid climate and its admittedly high level of biodiversity, with over 90 species of birds being observed until the early 1990s, according to a study by the Patras Bureau of the *[Hellenic Ornithological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Ornithological_Society)*.[^cite_note-29]

## Infrastructure

Heavy infrastructure works performed in the 2000s include the [Peiros-Parapeiros dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peiros-Parapeiros_dam?action=edit\&redlink=1) (to provide water supply for Patras and surrounding towns)[^cite_note-35] and a "small industries" park that will be constructed next to the Glaykos river and provide an easy connection with the new port.

The city is one of the main Greek internet and [GRNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRNET) hubs and is connected with high speed lines to Athens as part of the backbone. A metropolitan optical network will be deployed in the city, with a total length of 48 km (30 mi).[^cite_note-opnet-36]

Two major state hospitals operate in the city: the [Agios Andreas Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agios_Andreas_Hospital) is the oldest of the two; and [General University Hospital of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_University_Hospital_of_Patras). There also exists two smaller state hospitals, Karamandanio - a children's hospital, and the Center of Chest Diseases of Southwestern Greece. A large range of private hospitals and clinics operate in parallel.

Numerous art venues[^cite_note-infracult-37] and an ultra-modern [archaeological museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Patras)[^cite_note-archaeological-38] were constructed for the needs of European Culture Capital designation. The cultural and educational facilities include the Municipal Library, the university libraries, many theatres, the municipal art gallery,[^cite_note-39] the [University of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Patras)'s facilities, the Hellenic Open University and the Technical Institute of Patras. A number of research facilities are also established in the university campus area.

The economy of the city largely depends on its service sector.

The area still retains some of its traditional winemaking and foodstuff industries as well as a small agricultural sector.  Major businesses in Patras include:

### Services sector

Most Greek banks have their regional headquarters for Western Greece in Patras.

In 2010, the new Infocenter of Patras was established, inside the neoclassical building of the former market "Agora Argyri", in Ayiou Andreou street. The building includes a conference hall, along with multi-purpose and exhibitional spaces.[^cite_note-40] The regional unit of [Achaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaea) has about 4,800 hotels rooms and in 2006, 286,000 tourists, mainly from Greece, stayed in the area for a total of 634,000 days.[^cite_note-tourismstat1-41][^cite_note-tourismstat2-42]

### Manufacturing sector

Patras still has a large manufacturing base for a variety of industries.

The [Titan Cement Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_Cement) operates a large cement factory, with a private port, in [Psathopyrgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psathopyrgos), a suburb of Patras.

Patras hosts several timber manufacturing companies, and a wood distribution center of [Shelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelman?action=edit\&redlink=1). The largest local company is *Abex*.[^cite_note-abex-43] The paper sector is also active including a paper factory belonging to [Georgia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia-Pacific) (*Delica*) and two important Greek companies, *Elite* and *El-pack*, headquartered in the city.

Patras has several packing and industrial equipment companies. The most important of them are the local *Antzoulatos* and the [multinational](214491.md) [Frigoglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigoglass?action=edit\&redlink=1), a subsidiary of [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola), headquartered in the suburbs of Patras. [Ideal Bikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_Bikes) is the leading bike producer in Greece, with large export activities.

The once omnipresent [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry) of the city is now almost defunct after the shut-down of the huge factory of *[Peiraiki-Patraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peiraiki-Patraiki)* (Πειραϊκή-Πατραϊκή), followed by numerous smaller textile industries. The remains of the facilities still cover hundreds of acres in the south side of the city.

### Food



The [Achaia Clauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaia_Clauss) wine factory, founded in 1861 by Gustav Clauss, famous for its [Mavrodaphne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavrodaphne).

Some of the largest industries in the city belong to the soft drinks and drinks sector. There are factories from [Coca-Cola HBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola_Hellenic_Bottling_Company_\(CCHBC\)) and [Athenian Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_brands#Athenian_Brewery) established in area, along with the facilities of the largest local company in soft-drinks production, *[Loux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loux_\(company\))* (ΛΟΥΞ). The city is also home to many leading Greek wineries and distilleries, among them the venerable [Achaia Clauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaia_Clauss) and Parparoussi located in Rio. In the food sector, [Friesland Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesland_Foods), through the local subsidiary *NoyNoy*, operates a new yogurt factory in the city's industrial area. Patras is also home to important fish-farming companies (*Andromeda*, *Nireus*).[^cite_note-andromeda-44][^cite_note-nireus-45] ECOFEED operates in the industrial zone of Patras, the largest fish-feeds factory in the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-ecofeed_factory-46] The city hosts the second-largest [flour-mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gristmill) in Greece, *Kepenou-Mills*.[^cite_note-kepenou-47]

### Energy sector

### IT sector

[Intracom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracom) (Greece's largest multinational provider of telecommunications products) facilities in Patras house the offices of Telecommunications Software Development, Terminal Equipment Design, Development Programmes, and Support Services divisions. Expansion plans have recently been completed.[^cite_note-intracom-50] INTRASOFT, another core company of INTRACOM holdings group, has recently (2018) began operations in Patras and it is expected to expand its activities in 2019.[^cite_note-51] The Corallia Innovation Hub, Innohub hosts many companies focusing on Microelectronics.[^cite_note-Innohub-52] Among them one of the largest is the multinational software company [Citrix Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrix_Systems) which operates a R\&D centre with more than 100 computer scientists and engineers. Another large Greek IT company, Unisystems announced recently (October 2018) the signing of a cooperation agreement with the Patras-based IT company Knowledge SA, that lays the foundation for the establishment of a Remote Development Center in Patras.[^cite_note-53]

The cultural activity of Patras includes the Patras International Festival (with various artistic activities, mainly in the fields of theatre and music), the [Patras Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Carnival) and the Poetry Symposium.[^cite_note-Poetry_Symposium-60]

The city hosts many museums, including the [Patras Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Patras) the History and Ethnology Museum, the Folk Art Museum, the Press Museum and the Technology Museum, the latter in the campus of [Patras University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_University).

Other cultural institutes are: the Visual Arts Workshop, the [icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon) painting school, the [Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival) Float Workshop, the Municipal [Library](17727.md), the Municipal [Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_museum), along with many private art galleries. The architectural heritage of the city is dominated by neo-classicism, but also includes structures from other periods. Patras is also a pilot city of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and EU [Intercultural cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercultural_cities?action=edit\&redlink=1) programme.

### Theatrical tradition and music

The [Patras Municipal and Regional Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Municipal_and_Regional_Theatre) was founded in June 1988, having as its main stage the city's landmark, the [Apollon Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollon_Theatre_\(Patras\)). Throughout its existence it has mounted critically acclaimed performances ranging from ancient [dramaturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramaturgy) and modern Greek, to international repertoire. The theatre cooperates with other theatrical groups, such as the *Viomichaniki* (Industrial) group and the *Michani Technis* (Art Machine).

The Roman [Odeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_\(building\)) hosts ancient dramas in the summer, while the Pantheon theater, the Art Factory, the Lithographeion and the Agora theatres provide additional venues. The International Festival of Patras takes place every summer, with a program consisting mostly of plays—both ancient drama and modern theatre—as well as various musical events.

Patras has also an [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock) scene [^cite_note-61] with bands such as [Raining Pleasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raining_Pleasure), Abbie Gale, Serpentine, Doch an Doris and others.

### Carnival

The [Patras Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Carnival) (*Patrino Karnavali*) is the largest event of its kind in Greece and one of the biggest in Europe, with a heritage reaching back 160 years. The events begin on 17 January each year (St. Anthony's nameday), and last until [Clean Monday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Monday). Hundreds of thousands of visitors from all over the world gather each year for its festivities, which include large events such as the mammoth sized parades of the last two weeks (up to 50.000 participants each), the Hidden Treasure Hunt (Krymmenos Thisavros), concerts, expositions, theatrical, musical, comedy and other artistic contests and events. Patras Carnival was originally introduced as ball-masquee' events in 1835 by the Italian origin merchant family of Moretti.

### European Capital of Culture 2006



[Patras 2006 commemorative coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_gold_and_silver_commemorative_coins_\(Greece\)#2006_coinage).

Patras was chosen by the [European Commission](9974.md) to be the [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) for the year 2006. The concept of the event revolved around the main theme of "Bridges" and "Dialogues", drawing benefit from the city's rich history and its position as a "Gate to the West", to underline the essence of the productive interaction of culture and civilisations in Europe. The EU Commission found Patras's plans very ambitious and also commented that a successful hosting of the title by a medium-sized city would make it possible to redefine the meaning of the term Cultural Capital.

The Selection Panel for 2006 noted in its final report:

> The current cultural activity of the city includes the Patras International Festival (various artistic activities, mainly in the field of music), the Patras Carnival and the Poetry Symposium (organised each year for the 25 years by an ad hoc committee at the University of Patras).[^cite_note-Poetry_Symposium-60] The Patras 2006 proposal focuses on two central ideas: "bridges" and "dialogues". Cultural managers from Patras and the general public will be involved in developing these ideas. Further, four poles/programmes of cultural attraction will be developed. The first, "A city for Europe", will relate to the architectural heritage, the industrial revolution and similar subjects. "The counterpart cities" programme will be developed in the fields of human and social sciences and in diverse artistic fields. "The three sea battles" will present a cultural programme focusing on peace and understanding. The last theme, "The many homelands", is directly linked to the etymology of the name of the city. This programme will among other things concentrate on art workshops, the transfer of know-how, way of life and entertainment.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]&#x20;

In 2006 various cultural events were held in the context of the European Capital of Culture. Among the artists presenting their work in Patras were: [Gary Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Burton), [Maxim Shostakovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Shostakovich), [Ian Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Anderson) - with the Patras Municipal Orchestra, [Jean Louis Trintignant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Louis_Trintignant), [Roberto Benigni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Benigni), [Eros Ramazzotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eros_Ramazzotti) and [José Carreras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Carreras).[^cite_note-64] With the completion of the Capital of Culture programme, a part of the old Ladopoulos factory was renovated to host exhibitions, a small theatre (named the Art Factory), was built and a number of neoclassical buildings around the city were renovated as part of a plan to preserve the city's architectural heritage and link it to its cultural life. The new [Archaeological museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Patras) was completed in 2009. Its globe-like roof and modern architectural design enhances the town's northern entrance, taking its place among the other city landmarks.

### Religion



The [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) Church of Saint Andrew.

The city is the seat of the [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Greece) [Metropolis of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_of_Patras). As in the rest of the country, the largest denomination is the Orthodox Church, which represents the majority of the population. There is also a sizeable community of [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) and an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion) church, part of the [Church of England](5955.md)'s [Diocese in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_in_Europe).[^cite_note-anglican-66]



The old Orthodox church of Saint Andrew, next to the Cathedral

The most significant church in the city is the Orthodox [Cathedral Church of Saint Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Andrew,_Patras), in the south west of the city center. The construction of the church began in 1908 under the supervision of the architect [Anastasios Metaxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasios_Metaxas), followed by Georgios Nomikos. It was inaugurated in 1974. It is the largest church in Greece and the third-largest Byzantine-style church in the [Balkans](4829.md), after the [Cathedral of Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Sava) in Belgrade and [Alexander Nevsky Cathedral in Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Nevsky_Cathedral_in_Sofia). It holds relics of [Andrew the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_the_Apostle), which were returned to the city of Patras from [St. Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Basilica), Rome in September, 1964, on the orders of [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI). Other historical churches of the city are:

- The church of *Pantokrator* (1832), the old cathedral, in the upper town district

- The *Metropolitan Church of Patras* (1846) dedicated to [Panayia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panayia) Evangelistria, on Maisonos Street

- The church of *Ayios Nikolaos* (1885), next to the steps of Ayiou Nikolaou street

- The church of *Pantanassa* (1859), Ipsilanti street

- The church of *Ayios Dimitrios*, in the upper town district

- The Catholic Church of *Saint Andrew* (1937), on Maisonos Street

- The [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) church of *Saint Andrew* (1878), on Odos Agiou Andreou&#x20;

  [^cite_note-anglican-66]

- The old church of *Ayios Andreas* (1836–1843), next to the new temple. Situated in the site of [Andrew the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_the_Apostle)'s martyrdom, it was built in [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica) style by the architect [Lysandros Kaftanzoglou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysandros_Kaftanzoglou).

- *Girokomiou Monastery (Holy Monastery of Panagia Girokomitissa)*: This historic monastery was founded in the 10th century AD in the eastern part of Patras. It was built on the ruins of an ancient temple of the goddess Artemis and for this reason the monastery's cathedral is dedicated to Saint Artemiοs. It is obvious that the monastery maintained a nursing home during the Byzantine period.

- *Monastery of Agios Nikolaos Bala (Paleomonastiro):* Ιt is built at the foot of Panachaikos, at an altitude of 500 meters, near the village of Bala, 8 km (5.0 mi) northeast of Patras. This historic and picturesque monastery was founded at the end of the 17th century. A marble slab on the north outer side of the Cathedral tells of the restoration of the monastery in 1693. The monastery has also recently been renovated, numbering nineteen nuns and celebrating 6 December and 10 May.

The patron saint of Patras is [Saint Andrew the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Andrew_the_Apostle). One of the modern saints is Saint [Gervasios Paraskevopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gervasios_Paraskevopoulos) of Patras (2023)

### Seaport

The city has always been a sea-trade hub because of its strategic position. The port manages more than half of the foreign sea-passenger transportation in Greece,[^cite_note-olpstats-70] and has excellent car-ferry links with the [Ionian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Islands) and the major [Adriatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic) ports of Italy. Additionally, a new port was built in the southern section of the city to accommodate the increased traffic and relieve the city centre from port operations.[^cite_note-olp-71] In 2011, this port went into operation. Ferries to Italy now dock there.[^cite_note-72]

The port is connected by a number of daily routes to the Ionian islands [Kerkyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerkyra), [Kefallonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefallonia), to the port of [Igoumenitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igoumenitsa) and to the Italian cities [Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancona), [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari), [Brindisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brindisi), [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice).[^cite_note-e-patras.gr-73]

### Roads

A newly constructed, 20-kilometre (12 mi) [ring road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_National_Road_9) (the Bypass of Patras) was first opened in 2002 in order to alleviate heavy traffic throughout the city.[^cite_note-perimetrikh-74] A mini ring road (known as the "Mini bypass" of Patras) is now complete (2019), alleviating heavy traffic-related problems in the city centre.[^cite_note-minibelt-75] The mini-bypass is a two lanes mototway bridging the northern city entrance at the Zavlani neighborhood to the eastern entrance at the Aroi, Synora and Upper town (Ano poli) neighborhoods reducing the city centre crossing time to less than 4 minutes drive.

Two large highways were also constructed, connecting the seacoast and the new port with the Bypass of Patras. The first is over the small Diakoniaris river (from Eleftheriou Venizelou street until the Bypass'es exit in [Eglykada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglykada)), while the second consists of two roads, 4 km (2 mi) each, that run in parallel with the [Glafkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glafkos_\(river\)) river entering at the city' s New Port.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] Another project was completed recently, leading to an additional entrance to the downtown area after expanding and widening Kanakari street. This work led to a fast, direct connection of the city's mini bypass road with the city centre.

The highway connection with Athens was recently upgraded to a 220 km closed motorway ([Olympia Odos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Odos)), with a speed limit of 130 km/hour, reducing the transit time to 1 hour and 45 minutes. The motorway was connected to the Large bypass highway and was extended all the way to [Pyrgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrgos,_Elis) in July 2025.[^cite_note-pathe-78] Patras will eventually be a central hub of the [A5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_motorway_\(Greece\)) and [A8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A8_motorway_\(Greece\)) motorways, intended to bridge western Greece from [Kalamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamata) to [Ioannina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioannina) and the Kakavia border station. The [Rio-Antirio Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio-Antirio_Bridge) is north of the city and links the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) to mainland Greece. It was completed in August 2004.

Additional work was recently announced to begin in 2023 in order to connect via highway the Rion Antirrion bridge with Nafpaktos, Itea, Amfissa and Lamia. This project is expected to reduce the trip to Lamia to 90 minutes and its completion is expexted in 2025.

Patras is bypassed by the [Olympia Odos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Odos) (A8) motorway, which is also part of the [E55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E55) route that crosses the [Rio-Antirio Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio-Antirio_Bridge), dominating the sealine across the [Gulf of Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Corinth).

### Rail

A rudimentary [single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_track_\(rail\)), [narrow-gauge railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railway) track crosses the city and connects it to [Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio,_Greece). In the past regional rail links were provided by the [Hellenic Railways Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Railways_Organisation), connecting Patras to Athens and Piraeus as well as to [Pyrgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrgos,_Elis) and [Kalamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamata).[^cite_note-e-patras.gr-73] OSE announced the suspension of all the rail service in the Peloponnese in January 2011[^cite_note-79] so today the railway track is in use only by suburban trains that connect Patras with the adjacent villages of Rio and Agios Vasileios.

The [central passenger train station of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_railway_station) which is a small building constructed in 1954, lies to the west of the downtown area, between [Othonos-Amalias Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Othonos-Amalias_Avenue?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the north port. The main freight station of Aghios Andreas lies further to the south, next to the homonymous church and it is not in use any more. Finally, the old depot of Aghios Dionysios, consisting of about ten tracks, offers basic turntable and roundhouse facilities; it is about 400 m (1,312.34 ft) long.

A new double [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) railway line, the [Athens Airport–Patras railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens_Airport%E2%80%93Patras_railway), is under construction and will link Patras to [Korinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinth_\(modern_city\)) and further to [Athens](1216.md). The construction works are currently in progress close to the suburbs of Patras, but the remaining few kilometres till the city centre and the new port are still under study because of various financial and technical problems.[^cite_note-80] As of 2020, the new railway reaches [Aigio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aigio), 40 kilometres (25 mi) east of Patras.

### Public transport

Patras is served by buses. There are two transport lines to and from the [University of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Patras) and some nearby lines to city suburbs such as [Saravali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saravali), [Glafkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarouchleika), and [Paralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralia,_Achaea). All the urban bus lines are about 40, with three numbers.

Commuter rail services have recently been established by [Proastiakos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proastiakos), with one line currently connecting Patras, [Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio,_Greece), and Agios Vasileios.

Regional bus links are provided by the [KTEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTEL_\(Greece\)) bus company and connect the city to most of Greece.

### Air

Seasonal civilian air transport is provided by the military [Patras Araxos Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras_Araxos_Airport), about 40 km (25 mi) from the city's centre.
