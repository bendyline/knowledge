# Maritime boundary



Maritime Zones under International Law

A **maritime boundary** is a conceptual division of [Earth](9228.md)'s water surface areas using [physiographical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_geography) or [geopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_geography) criteria. As such, it usually bounds areas of exclusive [national rights](28791.md) over mineral and biological resources,[^cite_note-vliz_info-1] encompassing maritime features, limits and zones.[^cite_note-geoscience-2] Generally, a maritime boundary is delineated at a particular distance from a jurisdiction's coastline. Although in some countries the term *maritime boundary* represents [borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border) of a [maritime nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_nation)[^cite_note-3] that are recognized by the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md), maritime borders usually serve to identify the edge of [international waters](394357.md).

Maritime boundaries exist in the context of [territorial waters](496983.md), [contiguous zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_zone), and [exclusive economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone); however, the terminology does not encompass [lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake) or [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River) boundaries, which are considered within the context of land boundaries.

Some maritime boundaries have remained indeterminate despite efforts to clarify them. This is explained by an array of factors, some of which involve regional problems.[^cite_note-4]

The delineation or delimitation of maritime boundaries has strategic, economic and environmental implications (see *[maritime delimitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_delimitation)*).[^cite_note-5]

## Terminology

The terms [boundary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/boundary), [frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier) and [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border) are often used as if they were interchangeable, but they are also terms with precise meanings.[^cite_note-6]

A boundary is a line. The terms "frontier", "borderland" and "border" are zones of indeterminate width. Such areas form the outermost part of a country. Borders are bounded on one side by a national boundary.[^cite_note-7] There are variations in the specific terminology of maritime boundary agreements which have been concluded since the 1970s. Such differences are less important than what is being delimited.[^cite_note-8]

### Features

Features that affect maritime boundaries include [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island) and the submerged seabed of the [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf).[^cite_note-geoscience-2]

The process of [boundary delimitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_boundary_delimitation) in the [ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean) encompasses the [natural prolongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_prolongation) of [geological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology) features and [outlying territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlying_territory). The process of establishing "positional" borders encompasses the distinction between previously resolved and never-resolved controversies.[^cite_note-9]

### Limits

The limits of maritime boundaries are expressed in [polylines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyline) and in [polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon) layers of sovereignty and control,[^cite_note-10] calculated from the declaration of a [baseline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseline_\(sea\)). The conditions under which a state may establish such baseline are described in the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). A baseline of a country can be the low water line, a straight baseline (a line that encloses bays, estuaries, inland waters,...) or a combination of the two.[^cite_note-vliz_info-1]

### Classification

Maritime spaces can be divided into the following groups based on their legal status:&#x20;

- Maritime spaces under the sovereignty and authority (exercising power) of a [coastal State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_State): internal waters, territorial sea, and [archipelagic waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_waters),

- Maritime spaces with mixed legal regime, which fall under both the jurisdiction of the coastal State and under the international law: contiguous zone, the continental shelf and the exclusive economic zone, and

- Maritime spaces that can be used by all States (including land-locked ones) on an equal basis: high seas.&#x20;

While many maritime spaces can be classified as belonging to the same group, this does not imply that they all have the same legal regime. International straits and canals have their own legal status as well.[^cite_note-11]

### Zones

The zones of maritime boundaries are expressed in concentric limits surrounding coastal and feature baselines.[^cite_note-vliz_info-1]

- Inland waters—the zone inside the baseline.

- Territorial sea—the zone extending 12 [nautical miles](21289.md) (nm) from the baseline.

  [^cite_note-geoscience-2]

- Contiguous zone—the area extending 24 nm from the baseline.

  [^cite_note-geoscience-2]

- Exclusive Economic Zone—the area extending 200 nm from the baseline except when the space between two countries is less than 400 nm.

  [^cite_note-geoscience-2]

In the case of overlapping zones, the boundary is presumed to conform to the [equidistance principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equidistance_principle) or it is explicitly described in a multilateral treaty.[^cite_note-vliz_info-1]

Contemporary negotiations have produced [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) and [quadripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadripoint) determinations. For example, in the 1982 [Australia–France Marine Delimitation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93France_Marine_Delimitation_Agreement), for the purposes of drawing the treaty's equidistant lines it was assumed that France has sovereignty over [Matthew and Hunter Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_and_Hunter_Islands), a territory that is also claimed by [Vanuatu](32443.md). The northernmost point in the boundary is a tripoint with the [Solomon Islands](265083.md). The boundary runs in a roughly north–south direction and then turns and runs west–east until it almost reaches the [170th meridian east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/170th_meridian_east).[^cite_note-anderson-12]

## History

Main article: [List of countries and territories by maritime boundaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_by_maritime_boundaries)

The concept of maritime boundaries is a relatively new concept.[^cite_note-vliz_info-1] The historical record is a backdrop for evaluating border issues.[^cite_note-13] The evaluation of historic rights are governed by distinct legal regimes in customary international law, including research and analysis based on

- acquisition and occupation

  [^cite_note-jagota183-14]

- the existence of rights *[ipso facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipso_facto)* and *[ab initio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab_initio)*.

  [^cite_note-jagota183-14]

The study of treaties on maritime boundaries is important as (a) as a source of general or particular international law; (b) as evidence of existing customary law; and (c) as evidence of the emerging development of custom.[^cite_note-15] The development of "customary law" affects all nations.

The attention accorded this subject has evolved beyond formerly-conventional norms like the [three-mile limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-mile_limit).

### Treaties

Main article: [List of maritime boundary treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_maritime_boundary_treaties)

Multilateral treaties and documents describing the baselines of countries can be found on the website of the United Nations.[^cite_note-vliz_info-1]

For example, the Australia–France Marine Delimitation Agreement establishes ocean boundaries between Australia and New Caledonia in the Coral Sea (including the boundary between Australia's Norfolk Island and New Caledonia). It consists of 21 straight-line maritime segments defined by 22 individual coordinate points forming a modified equidistant line between the two territories.[^cite_note-anderson-12]

## Disputes

Main article: [List of territorial disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_territorial_disputes)

Controversies about territorial waters tend to encompass two dimensions: (a) territorial sovereignty, which are a legacy of history; and (b) relevant jurisdictional rights and interests in maritime boundaries, which are mainly due to differing interpretations of the law of the sea.[^cite_note-16] An example of this may be reviewed in the context of the ongoing Kuwait-Iraq maritime dispute over the [Khawr Abd Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khawr_Abd_Allah) waterway.

Many disputes have been resolved through negotiations,[^cite_note-17] but not all of them.

- On one hand, the boundary line created by the United Nations ("A") reflects the geographic features of the coastal baseline.
