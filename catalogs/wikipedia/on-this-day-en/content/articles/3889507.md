**Bakhmut**[^cite_note-4] is a city in eastern [Ukraine](31750.md). Officially the administrative center of [Bakhmut urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhmut_urban_hromada) and [Bakhmut Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhmut_Raion) in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md), it is on the [Bakhmutka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhmutka) River, about 90 kilometres (55 miles) north of [Donetsk](562951.md), the administrative center of the oblast. Bakhmut was designated a [city of regional significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)) until 2020, when the designation was abolished. In January 2022, it had an estimated population of 71,094.[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-1]

Bakhmut was originally founded in the 16th century as a minor border post on the southern border of the Russian state. Its population grew in the early 18th century, and it served as the capital of [Slavo-Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavo-Serbia) (1753–1764), a colony in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) established by settlers from the [Balkans](4829.md).

It received city status in 1783, and underwent major industrialization over the following few centuries. In 1920–1924, the city was an administrative center of the newly created [Donets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Governorate) of the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) in the Soviet Union. The city was known as **Artemivsk** or **Artemovsk** between 1924 and 2016. During [World War II](32927.md), it was the site of the [Artemivsk massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemivsk_massacre) of Soviet Jews by Nazi Germany.

During the beginning of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas) between the independent Ukrainian government and pro-Russian separatists, the city was the site of the [battle of Artemivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Artemivsk) in 2014. During the full-scale [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) which commenced in February 2022, Bakhmut was the site of a [major battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bakhmut) between Russian and Ukrainian forces. The city was largely destroyed, with most of its population having fled, and what remained being placed under Russian occupation.[^cite_note-bbc-mar23-5][^cite_note-auto-6]

According to a theory by [Kharkiv](494901.md) historian Igor Rassokhaa, the word may derive from a Turkic word meaning 'salt water' or 'beach'.[^cite_note-bahmut.in.ua-10][^cite_note-24tv-11] It may also be derived from *bakhmát* ([бахмат](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D0%B1%D0%B0%D1%85%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%82#Ukrainian)), ultimately from Turkic *paχn at*, meaning "steppe horse". Another theory states that the name Bakhmut is based on Turkish or Tartar *Mahmut*, a variant of Muhammad.[^cite_note-12]

In 1924, the city was renamed *Artemivsk* in honour of [Fyodor Sergeyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Sergeyev), who was pseudonymously known as "Comrade Artyom". On 23 September 2015, the city council decided to restore the name *Bakhmut* under the [Decommunisation Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_decommunization_laws), in which cities with communist-related names were required to be renamed.[^cite_note-13] The [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) approved a bill of renaming to *Bakhmut* on 4 February 2016.[^cite_note-14]

## History

### Pre-founding

Near the city, archaeologists have discovered a [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic)-era flint processing workshop, excavated ancient burial grounds from the [Copper Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Age) and [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), and discovered [stone baba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_baba) sculptures associated with [nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomads) from the ninth-to-twelfth century.[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

### Early history



Historical map of Bakhmut and its fortifications

Although there is evidence of prior settlement in 1556, the first official mention of Bakhmut dates from 1571, when [Ivan the Terrible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_the_Terrible), in order to protect the southern border of the Russian state from [Crimean–Nogai slave raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean%E2%80%93Nogai_slave_raids_in_Eastern_Europe), ordered the creation of border fortifications along the [Aidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidar_\(river\)) and [Siverskyi Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siverskyi_Donets) rivers.[^cite_note-16] The settlement was described then as a guard-fort (*storozha*) named after the nearby [Bakhmutka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhmutka) River, a tributary of the Siverskyi Donets, and located at the mouth of a stream called the Chornyi Zherebets.[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

The ultimate origin of the name *Bakhmut* is uncertain. According to a theory by [Kharkiv](494901.md) historian Igor Rassokhaa, the word may derive from a Turkic/Tatar word meaning 'salt water' or 'beach'.[^cite_note-bahmut.in.ua-10][^cite_note-24tv-11] The name dates back to 1571.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-24tv-11]

Bakhmut was initially a border post, and later became a fortified town. In 1701, [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) ordered the fort at Bakhmut to be upgraded and the adjacent [sloboda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_\(settlement\)) (free village) of Bakhmut be designated a city. The new fort was completed in 1703 and housed 170 people. In 1704, Peter commanded some [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) to settle at the Bakhmutka River and mine salt. The population of Bakhmut doubled, and the town was assigned to the [Izium Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izium_Regiment?action=edit\&redlink=1), a province of [Sloboda Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine).[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

In the autumn of 1705, Bakhmut became one of the centers of the [Bulavin Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulavin_Rebellion). A detachment of [Don Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cossacks) headed by Ataman [Kondraty Bulavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondraty_Bulavin) captured the Bakhmut salt mines[^cite_note-18] and occupied the city until they were defeated and the city retaken by government troops. According to official Soviet sources, the government forces "brutally" suppressed the revolution and Bakhmut was completely destroyed.[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

From 1708 to 22 April 1725, Bakhmut was assigned to the first [Azov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Governorate_\(1708-1779\)). On 29 May 1719, it became the administrative center of [Bakhmut Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhmut_Province) within Azov Governorate.[^cite_note-19] From 1753 until the colony's abolition, it was the administrative center of [Slavo-Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavo-Serbia),[^cite_note-EOU-20][^cite_note-BakhmutHistory24TV-21] a short-lived territory that was settled by thousands of colonists from the [Balkans](4829.md), predominantly [Serbs](59512.md).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-BakhmutHistory24TV-21] Bakhmut was fortified, to serve the colony's purpose of frontier protection.[^cite_note-23] In 1764, the [Bakhmut hussar regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhmut_hussar_regiment?action=edit\&redlink=1) was formed by merging two Serbian settler regiments, with its headquarters in Bakhmut.[^cite_note-k141-24] After the abolition of Slavo-Serbia, in 1765 Bakhmut was assigned to [Novorossiya Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiya_Governorate). In 1775, it became part of the second [Azov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Governorate).[^cite_note-historyUA-25]

In 1783, Bakhmut received city status, and was assigned to [Yekaterinoslav Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Province) of the re-established [Novorossiysk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Governorate).[^cite_note-historyUA-25][^cite_note-hd-26] On 2 August 1811, a coat of arms of Bakhmut was approved, featuring symbolism evoking the salt reserves of the city.[^cite_note-BakhmutHistory24TV-21] In 1863, a large [synagogue](48559.md) was built in the city, as a place of worship for Bakhmut's Jewish community of 1,560 people.[^cite_note-haaretz-27] In 1875, a municipal water system was installed. In 1876, due to the work of Russian geologist [Alexander Karpinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Karpinsky), large deposits of rock salt were discovered near Bakhmut. Bakhmut soon produced 12.3% of the total output of salt in the Russian Empire.[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

### Industrialization



An early 20th-century postcard of Bakhmut

Bakhmut saw [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization) during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In the early 1870s, German industrialist Edmund Farke built [alabaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabaster), brick and tile factories in the city. In 1878, the Kharkiv-Bakhmut-Popasna railroad was constructed.[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

Streets were paved in Bakhmut in 1900. At the beginning of the twentieth century, the city began to develop metal-working industry. By 1909, the city had 64 industrial enterprises, which employed 1,075 workers.[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

In 1905, after the release of the [October Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Manifesto), an antisemitic [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom) took place in Bakhmut, killing and wounding several [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) residents of the city.[^cite_note-28] In April 1918, after the collapse of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), troops loyal to the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic) took control of Bakhmut.[^cite_note-29] Later, it was captured by [White movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) soldiers led by [Pyotr Krasnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Krasnov), who were eventually defeated by Soviet forces.[^cite_note-BakhmutHistory24TV-21]

From 1920 to 1925, Bakhmut was the administrative center of the newly created [Donets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Governorate) of the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-EOU-20] In 1923, there were many enterprises in Bakhmut, including the "Victory of Labor" factory that made nails and spikes, the "Blyskavka" ("Lightning") factory that produced agriculture tools, as well as brick, tile, and alabaster factories, and a shoe factory.[^cite_note-ukssr2-31] In 1922, to help rebuild the salt industry, a state salt mining company was created, which is now [Artemsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemsil). In 1925, the salt mining areas were split off from Bakhmut into their own urban-type settlement named [Karlo-Libknekhtivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlo-Libknekhtivsk) (now Soledar).[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

In 1924, the city's name was changed from Bakhmut to **Artemivsk**, in honour of the [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) leader [Fyodor Sergeyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Sergeyev), who was known as *Comrade Artem* (or Artyom).[^cite_note-haaretz-27][^cite_note-24tv-11] The city's synagogue was shuttered in 1928.[^cite_note-haaretz-27] 3,255 residents of Artemivsk died as a result of the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor).[^cite_note-EOU-20] During Stalin's [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge) in the late 1930s, more than 500 residents of Artemivsk were victims of the repressions.[^cite_note-haaretz-27]

### World War II and later 20th century

Further information: [Artemivsk massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemivsk_massacre)



Civilians in Artemivsk reporting to the Nazi occupation offices for [forced labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II), May 1942

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), at the beginning of the [Nazi invasion of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) in 1941, Artemivsk's population included 5,300 Jews, making up almost 10% of the total population.[^cite_note-haaretz-27] The majority of these were either drafted into the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) or evacuated into the interior areas of the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-haaretz-27][^cite_note-YV-32][^cite_note-Bakhmut-33]

On 31 October 1941,[^cite_note-34] Nazi German troops began their occupation of Artemivsk.[^cite_note-YV-32] On 19 November, the occupation authorities issued a decree forcing the remaining local Jews to register at the local commandant's office and wear [armbands marking them as Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_badge).[^cite_note-YV-32] On 9 January 1942, under the pretext of needing to gather in one place for relocation, Artemivsk's Jewish population was gathered in the city park,[^cite_note-haaretz-27][^cite_note-Bakhmut-33] where they were forced to hand over all their valuable possessions, then were locked in the [cellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basement) of a former [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD) building.[^cite_note-Bakhmut-33] They were locked in the "freezing" cellar for three days without food or water. During this period, according to *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)*, local residents threw lumps of [snow](28191.md) through the windows in an attempt to provide some sort of drinkable water to the imprisoned Jews. A few residents risked their lives to rescue some Jewish children, a feat for which they would later receive the title of [Righteous Among the Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Righteous_Among_the_Nations) from [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-haaretz-27]



The "Wailing Wall" memorial to the murdered Jews of the city

The [Artemivsk massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemivsk_massacre) took place on 11–12 January 1942,[^cite_note-haaretz-27] when *Sonderkommando 4b* of *[Einsatzgruppe C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzgruppen)* led thousands of Jews into a mineshaft in an [alabaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabaster) mine,[^cite_note-Bakhmut-33][^cite_note-JS-35] where they shot into the crowd, killing several people.[^cite_note-haaretz-27][^cite_note-Bakhmut-33] The soldiers then bricked up the entrance to the tunnel, suffocating the remaining people trapped inside.[^cite_note-Bakhmut-33] The exact number of dead is unclear,[^cite_note-36] and records of the Jewish death toll differ: Soviet documents reported a number of about 3,000, while the German occupation authorities recorded 1,200 victims.[^cite_note-YV2-37][^cite_note-Bakhmut-33] The city was eventually liberated by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) on 5 September 1943.[^cite_note-YV-32][^cite_note-haaretz-27]

In 1990, [Oleksiy Reva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksiy_Reva) became mayor of Bakhmut during the last years of the Soviet period.[^cite_note-reva-38] In the [1991 Ukrainian independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), the overwhelming majority of Bakhmut residents voted for independence from the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-voa-39] In January 1999, a charitable Jewish foundation in the city, as well as the Artemivsk city council and a winery that had opened on the site in 1952, inaugurated a memorial to commemorate the victims of the 1942 mass murder. The memorial was built into a rock face in the old mine where water collects and was named the "Wailing Wall" for the murdered Jews of the city.[^cite_note-Bakhmut-33]

### Russo-Ukrainian War

#### War in Donbas

Further information: [Battle of Artemivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Artemivsk)



A Russian [T-64BV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-64BV), captured after the rebel retreat from Artemivsk

In April 2014, at the beginning of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)), pro-Russian rebels led by [Igor Bezler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Bezler)[^cite_note-BakhmutHistory24TV-21] and belonging to the [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md) claimed the city of Artemivsk as part of their territory.[^cite_note-40] As the rebels entered the city, mayor Oleksiy Reva temporarily fled the city.[^cite_note-reva-38] Local military units defended the city for months, repelling separatist assaults over the course of the [Battle of Artemivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Artemivsk).[^cite_note-BakhmutHistory24TV-21][^cite_note-41] Ukrainian government forces fully recaptured the city on 7 July 2014, ending the battle.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

On 15 May 2015, Ukrainian president [Petro Poroshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko) signed a bill into law that started a six-month period for the [removal of communist monuments and the mandatory renaming of settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine) with names related to [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism).[^cite_note-decommupbbcU-44] On 23 September 2015, the city council voted to restore the city's former name of Bakhmut.[^cite_note-45] The final decision was made by the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) on 4 February 2016, and the city returned to its original name.[^cite_note-rename1-46][^cite_note-rename-47] The Russian government, as well as Russian state media, have continued to refer to the town as *Artyomovsk*, especially in military contexts.[^cite_note-Reuters_Artyomovsk-48][^cite_note-49]

#### Russian invasion

Main article: [Battle of Bakhmut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bakhmut)

During the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Bakhmut became a frontline city in May, and was regularly shelled by Russian forces.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] In May 2022, according to local authorities, an estimated 20,000 people remained in the city.[^cite_note-53] It became a major battle of the war, attracting worldwide attention due to the level of destruction in the city and the numbers of casualties on both sides.[^cite_note-\:22-54]

According to the [Associated Press](18935732.md) in October 2022, "taking Bakhmut would rupture Ukraine's supply lines and open a route for Russian forces to press on toward [Kramatorsk](2864798.md) and [Sloviansk](2533448.md), key Ukrainian strongholds in Donetsk province".[^cite_note-55] In a December analysis of the offensive, however, the UK [Ministry of Defence](203793.md) said "the capture of the town would have limited operational value although it would potentially allow Russia to threaten the larger urban areas of Kramatorsk and Sloviansk".[^cite_note-56] On 11 December 2022, Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) said that Russian forces had turned the city into "burned ruins".[^cite_note-aj-57]

By early March 2023, Russian forces had not taken Bakhmut, but were continuing to press the attack, and hoped to complete their encirclement of the city.[^cite_note-58] On 4 March, the deputy mayor of the city said that 4,000 civilians remained in Bakhmut and were living in shelters with no access to water, gas or electricity.[^cite_note-bbc-mar23-5] On 20 May, Russia claimed to have fully taken Bakhmut;[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] however, Ukraine has denied this.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] At the [G7 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_G7_summit), Zelenskyy stated that the images of the [ruined Hiroshima after atomic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) reminded him of the level of destruction in Bakhmut.[^cite_note-63] The battle was still ongoing with Ukraine claiming to still control a strip of territory within city limits along the T0504 highway, as well as performing attacks on the flanks of the city.[^cite_note-ISW_June_2-64] By May 24, reports that corroborated the claimed seizure of the city by Russian and [Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group) forces had surfaced.[^cite_note-auto-6][^cite_note-65] By 25 May, [Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group) had begun withdrawing from the city to be replaced by regular Russian troops.[^cite_note-66] On June 4, [Yevgeny Prigozhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Prigozhin) conceded that Ukrainian forces still controlled parts of the city along the T0504 highway.[^cite_note-ISW_June_4-67]

On 4 June 2023, the [Ukrainian summer counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Ukrainian_counteroffensive) began and the Bakhmut sector was one of its three main axes.[^cite_note-ISW_June_8-68][^cite_note-BI_2023-12-22-69] However, only small amounts of territory were recaptured,[^cite_note-BI_2023-12-22-69] most notably [Andriivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriivka,_Bakhmut_Raion,_Donetsk_Oblast) on 15 September and [Klishchiivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klishchiivka) around 17 September.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] By 29 November 2023, after the counteroffensive had stalled, Russian forces had regained the initiative and captured [Khromove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khromove) on the western outskirts of Bakhmut. They had also recaptured considerable portions of territory south of the [Berkhivka Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkhivka_Reservoir) that they previously lost in May.[^cite_note-CT_2023-11-29-72][^cite_note-ISW_2023-12-30-73] Bakhmut was the largest Ukrainian city to switch hands in 2023 and the battle greatly contributed to Russia's net gain of territory during the year.[^cite_note-74]

Bakhmut's population has continuously declined in recent years, with the death rate (535) significantly higher than the birth rate (187) in 2017.[^cite_note-2017pop-77] In January 2022 (the last estimate of the city's population before the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)), the estimated population of Bakhmut was 75,900.[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-1]

The majority of Bakhmut's residents are ethnic [Ukrainians](145144.md) (69.4%), with a large minority of ethnic [Russians](59510.md) (27.5%).[^cite_note-census1-78] Many of the latter group are descendants of migrants who arrived in Bakhmut during industrialization efforts in the Soviet era, between the later 1920s and the 1940s.[^cite_note-adventure-79] There are also small minorities of ethnic [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians) (0.6%), [Armenians](387816.md) (0.3%), [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (0.2%), and [Jews](25955086.md) (0.2%).[^cite_note-census1-78]

The most spoken native language is [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) (62%), with a large minority speaking [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) (35%), and very small minorities speaking [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) (0.19%), [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_language) (0.15%), and [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language) (0.10%).[^cite_note-census1-78][^cite_note-census2-80] The Russian speech of many residents has characteristics of *[surzhyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surzhyk)*, a kind of mixed Ukrainian and Russian speech common in [eastern Ukraine](13065940.md).[^cite_note-adventure-79]

Historically, Bakhmut was a more Ukrainian-speaking city. In 1897, most residents of Bakhmut spoke Ukrainian (61.8%), while minorities spoke Russian (18.9%) and [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish) (16.7%).[^cite_note-EOU-20]

## Government and politics

Bakhmut's political leaning and sense of identity has historically been mixed.[^cite_note-adventure-79] In the [1991 Ukrainian independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), the overwhelming majority of Bakhmut residents voted for independence from the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-voa-39] In the [2010 Ukrainian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Ukrainian_presidential_election), most voters in Bakhmut and surrounding areas voted for Russia-leaning [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) and his party the [Party of Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Regions). However, Christopher Miller, visiting the city in 2010, reported that "few seemed enthusiastic about having \[voted for Yanukovych] and openly acknowledged that they believed he was corrupt. His party was seen by many as the least bad option." He said that most residents of the city considered themselves "people of the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas)" first and foremost, and that while they valued autonomy, separatist sentiments were extremely rare.[^cite_note-adventure-79]

Oleksiy Reva, who became mayor of Bakhmut in 1990 during the last years of the Soviet period, has become the longest-running mayor of any city in Ukraine.[^cite_note-reva-38][^cite_note-adventure-79] Ukrainian media, describing Reva, said "He survived the collapse of the empire, two revolutions and six presidents." He has "only once" been accused of [corruption](66220.md). He has been criticized for his conduct during the 2014 [battle of Artemivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Artemivsk) during which he fled the city, but in September 2019, when he held a city council session in the [Ukrainian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) for the first time, it was considered a "historic event" for Bakhmut.[^cite_note-reva-38]

Bakhmut is the administrative center of [Bakhmut urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhmut_urban_hromada), one of the [hromadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hromada) of Ukraine. In addition to Bakhmut, the hromada also contains the nearby town [Krasna Hora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasna_Hora) and numerous small surrounding villages.[^cite_note-81]

## Economy



Artwinery in 2008, its sign showing its old name of "Artemovsk Winery"

Since 1950, the winery [Artwinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artwinery) (or Artvaineri, formerly Artemovsk Winery) has operated in the city. Its production was disrupted by the [Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), as it used to procure 70% of its grapes from [Crimea](163045.md).[^cite_note-82]

The [Artemsil salt mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemsil_salt_mine) is located in the suburb of [Soledar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledar).[^cite_note-giantmine-83] The chambers in the mine are large enough that a [hot air balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon) has been floated inside,[^cite_note-BBCua12Jan-84] and classical music concerts have been played.[^cite_note-BI-85]

### Transport

The highway between [Kharkiv](2788558.md) and [Rostov-on-Don](317562.md) passes near the city.[^cite_note-ukssr1-15]

## Education



Symphony Orchestra and Choir of the Ivan Karabyts Bakhmut College of Arts

The city contains an institute of the salt industry, several [technical colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_college), medical schools, music schools, and [teacher-training colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher-training_college). Among others, there is the Ivan Karabyts Bakhmut College of Arts (named after composer [Ivan Karabyts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Karabyts)) and the Bakhmut Pedagogical College.[^cite_note-86]

There are twelve [libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libraries), including one for [blind people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_people).[^cite_note-EOU-20]

After the outbreak of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas) in 2014 the [Horlivka Institute for Foreign Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horlivka_Institute_for_Foreign_Languages) was evacuated and is now operating in Bakhmut.[^cite_note-Bakhmut248239tyzhden.ua-87]

## Media

The [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) in Bakhmut includes [Russian-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-language) newspapers such as *Sobytiia* and *Vpered*, as well as local television and radio channels. There is also an internet publication, *bahmut.in.ua*.[^cite_note-EOU-20]

## Sports

The Metalurh Stadium, a football stadium constructed in 1949, is located within the city and has a seating capacity of 4,800.[^cite_note-Europlan-Online-88] The stadium was damaged during the Russian invasion.[^cite_note-Europlan-Online-88]
