[British Prime Minister](24150.md) [Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak) with [Belgian Prime Minister](204622.md) [Alexander De Croo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_De_Croo) in [10 Downing Street](226294.md), January 2024.

[Belgium](3343.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) maintain [diplomatic and foreign relations](28165911.md). In addition, both countries' royal families are descended from the [House of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Saxe-Coburg_and_Gotha), with the British branch being known as the [House of Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Windsor) and the Belgian branch as the [House of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Belgium).

Both countries share common membership of the Atlantic Co-operation Pact,[^cite_note-1] the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights), the [International Criminal Court](14880.md), [NATO](21133.md), the [OECD](33853117.md), the [OSCE](22796.md), the [United Nations](31769.md), and the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization). Bilaterally the two countries have a Classified Information Protection Agreement,[^cite_note-2] a Double Taxation Convention,[^cite_note-3] and a Maritime Cooperation Agreement.[^cite_note-4]

## History



British forces at [Passchendaele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Passchendaele) in 1917

In the early years of the [Hundred Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Years'_War), [Edward III of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III_of_England) allied with the nobles of the [Low Countries](51402.md) and the burghers of [Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Flanders) against France.&#x20;



The [Rio Nuñez incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Nu%C3%B1ez_incident) of 1849: French and Belgian warships attack British traders



HM [King George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_VI) With the [British Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Liberation_Army) in Belgium, October 1944

Belgium established its independence in the [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Revolution) of 1830. Like the other European Great Powers, Britain was slow to recognise the new state. Even the election of [Leopold of Saxe-Coburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_I_of_Belgium), former son-in-law of Britain's [King George IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_IV_of_the_United_Kingdom) and uncle to the future [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria), as King of the Belgians failed to win diplomatic recognition from [London](17867.md). Belgium's emergence had caused the break-up of the [United Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands), one of several [buffer states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state) established after the end of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) as a check against future French expansion, and London feared this newly formed nation would be unable to survive hostile expansion by its neighbours. A British-organised European Congress produced the [Treaty of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London_\(1839\)) of 1839, whereby the Great Powers (and The Netherlands) all formally recognised the independence of Belgium, and (at Britain's insistence) guaranteed its neutrality.[^cite_note-5]

At the [Berlin Conference (1884)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference_\(1884\)), Britain had recognised the [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State) as the personal domain of the King of the Belgians. Britain was subsequently to become a centre for opposition to [Leopold II's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium) personal rule in the territory through organisations such as the [Congo Reform Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Reform_Association). At one point, Britain even demanded that the 14 signatories to the Berlin Conference meet again to discuss the situation. In 1894, the [British East Africa Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_British_East_Africa_Company) led by [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) and the British Prime Minister [Lord Rosebery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Philip_Primrose,_5th_Earl_of_Rosebery) had established and declared [Uganda](31816.md) a [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Uganda) of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) next to the border of Belgium's African colony in the Congo. In 1908, Belgium's parliament took control of the Congo, which became a conventional European colony. In the years prior to [World War I](4764461.md), many Belgians bore considerable resentment over Britain's campaign against Leopold II's activities in the Congo. In 1916, the Belgians advanced the German territory of [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa) such as the [Battle of Tabora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tabora) during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_campaign_\(World_War_I\)) and forced [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) to cede [Ruanda-Urundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruanda-Urundi) to Belgium as a [Class-B Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi%E2%80%93Rwanda_relations).[^cite_note-6]

The guarantees of neutrality of 1839 failed to prevent the invasion of Belgium by [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in 1914. It was the final straw for an element of the Liberal Party that needed a moralistic reason to enter the war, beyond the need to prevent the defeat of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_French_Republic). Historian [Zara Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zara_Steiner) says of German's invasion:&#x20;

The public mood did change. Belgium proved to be a catalyst which unleashed the many emotions, rationalizations, and glorifications of war which had long been part of the British climate of opinion. Having a moral cause, all the latent anti-German feelings, that by years of naval rivalry and assumed enmity, rose to the surface. The 'scrap of paper' proved decisive both in maintaining the unity of the government and then in providing a focal point for public feeling.

[^cite_note-7]

&#x20;Much of the British fighting took place on Belgian soil, around [Ypres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ypres). ([Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\))). During [World War II](32927.md), the [Belgian government in exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_government_in_exile) based itself in London, as did the governments of many other countries; including [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_government-in-exile) and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_government-in-exile).

[^cite_note-FCO_profile-8]

Around 250,000 Belgian refugees came to the UK during World War I; about 90% returned to Belgium soon after the war ended.[^cite_note-9] [Agatha Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_Christie)'s fictional detective [Hercule Poirot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercule_Poirot) was depicted as one of them.

[South Africa](17416221.md) was ruled by the [Dutch](21148.md) and then the [British](31717.md) colonization as the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa), while the [Belgians](3343.md) ruled as the [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo) until its independence from Belgium on 30 June 1960, while South Africa became a republic during the [Apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) era from 1948 until 1994, when [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela) was elected and became the first [President of South Africa](321700.md) following the landslide victory of [1994 South African general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_South_African_general_election). With [Mobutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko) ruled as the dictator of [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire) which has its name from 1971 to his overthrow in 1997 by the rebel leader [Laurent Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Kabila) during the [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War) and has renamed originally as the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) which then forced Mobutu to leave [Kinshasa](45773.md) and heading for exile in [Morocco](19291.md), as he later died in the same year.

## Economic relations



The [Eurostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostar) connects [London](17867.md) and [Brussels](3708.md) by train

Historically, the south eastern parts of Great Britain and the area that is now Belgium has evidence of trade since the 1st century[^cite_note-10] and wool exports from the UK to cloth imports in the 10th-century [County of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Flanders). Flemish bricks were used on work to the Tower of London in 1278.[^cite_note-11] Today as much as 7.8% of Belgium’s exports are to the UK.[^cite_note-12] with just over 5% of Belgium's imports, over €12,000,000 coming from the UK.[^cite_note-13] Belgium is the UK's sixth-largest export market, worth £10,000,000 a year. The UK is Belgium's fourth-largest export market with two-way trade worth in the region of £22,000,000,000 of which £2,000,000,000 is in services.[^cite_note-14] The Golden Bridge Awards were established in 2012 for UK export success in Belgium and recognising the importance of a close by market.[^cite_note-15]

Following [Brexit](41688778.md), Trade between the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreements_of_the_United_Kingdom) and Belgium is governed by the [EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93UK_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement) since 1 January 2021.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Modern relations

Today, there are roughly 30,000 British people living in Belgium, and 30,000 Belgians living in the UK.[^cite_note-FCO_profile-8] In 2014, the UK government announced £5,000,000 for the restoration of First World War graves in Flanders.[^cite_note-18]

[Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) made four [state visits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_visits_made_by_Elizabeth_II) to Belgium during her reign; in 1966 (being received by [King Baudouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Baudouin)), and in 1993, 1998 and 2007, where she was received by [King Albert II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_II_of_Belgium).
