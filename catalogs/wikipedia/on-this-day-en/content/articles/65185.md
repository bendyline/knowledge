**Sichuan**[^cite_note-8] is an inland [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [Southwestern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_China). It is located in the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin) and [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau)—between the [Jinsha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_River) to the west, the [Daba Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daba_Mountains) to the north, and the [Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Guizhou_Plateau) to the south. Its capital city is [Chengdu](170375.md), and the province's population stands at 83 million. Sichuan neighbors [Qinghai](173818.md) and [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) to the north, [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) and [Chongqing](171752.md) to the east, [Guizhou](173815.md) and [Yunnan](166410.md) to the south, and [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) to the west.

During antiquity, Sichuan was home to the kingdoms of [Ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_\(state\)) and [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\)) until their incorporation by the [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)) round 316 BC. During the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) era (220–280), [Liu Bei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Bei)'s state of [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) was based in Sichuan. The area was devastated in the 17th century by [Zhang Xianzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Xianzhong)'s rebellion and the area's subsequent [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) conquest. The area recovered to become one of China's most productive by the 19th century. During [World War II](32927.md), Chongqing served as the temporary capital of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), and was [heavily bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Chongqing). It was one of the last [mainland](55079.md) areas captured by the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) during the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), and was divided into four parts from 1949 to 1952, with Chongqing restored two years later. It suffered gravely during the [Great Chinese Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine) (1959–1961) but remained China's [most-populous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provinces) until Chongqing was again separated from it in 1997.

The [Sichuanese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_people) speak distinctive [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_dialects) of [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese). The [Sichuan pepper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_pepper), with its distinctive flavor and numbing effect, is prominent in modern [Sichuan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine), featuring dishes, including [Kung Pao chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kung_Pao_chicken) and [mapo tofu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapo_tofu), that have become staples of Chinese cuisine around the world. There are many [panda](12713.md) stations in the province and large reserves for these creatures, such as the [Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Research_Base_of_Giant_Panda_Breeding).

Sichuan is the [5th-largest provincial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) of China, the largest in [Western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China), and the largest among inland provinces. As of 2021, its nominal GDP was CN¥5,385 billion (US\$847.68 billion), ahead of that of Turkey (\$815 billion).[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-\:2-10] If it were its own country, Sichuan would be the [18th-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) and [19th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population) as of 2021.[^cite_note-\:1-11]

## &#x20;Names

It is commonly assumed that the name *Sichuan* means 'four [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivers_of_China)'; in [folk etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology), this is usually taken to mean any four of the province's major rivers: [Jialing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jialing_River), [Jinsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_River) (or the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze)), [Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_River_\(Yangtze_tributary\)), [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Sichuan\)), and [Tuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuo_River).[^cite_note-ruf-12][^cite_note-rowan-13][^cite_note-14] According to historical geographer [Tan Qixiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Qixiang), 'four rivers' is an erroneous interpretation of the name.[^cite_note-谭其骧-15] The name of the province is a contraction of the phrases 'Four Plain [Circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(administrative_subdivision\))' (四川路; *Sìchuān lù*) and 'Four Circuits of Chuanxia' (川峽四路; *chuānxiásìlù*),[^cite_note-16] referring to the division of the existing [imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_China) administrative circuit in the area into four during the [Northern Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty), which were [Yizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Province), [Lizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizhou_District), [Zizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zi_Prefecture_\(Sichuan\)), and [Kuizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuizhou).[^cite_note-17] The word *chuan* (川) here means 'plain', not its typical meaning of 'river' as popularly assumed.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In addition to its [postal map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_Map_Romanization) and [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade%E2%80%93Giles) forms of Szechwan or Szechuan, the name has also been irregularly romanized as *Szű-chuan* and *Szechuen*.[^cite_note-romanization-6][^cite_note-romanisations_2-7]

In antiquity, the area of modern Sichuan including the now-separated Chongqing Municipality was known to the Chinese as *Ba–Shu*, in reference to the [ancient states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_ancient_China) of [Ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_\(state\)) and the [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\)) that once occupied the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin). *Shu* continued to be used to refer to the region to the present day; several states formed in the area used the same name, for example, the [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) of the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) period (220–280), and [Former Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Shu) and [Later Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Shu) of the [Ten Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Kingdoms) period (907–979).[^cite_note-20] Currently, both characters for *Shu* and *Chuan* are common abbreviations for Sichuan.[^cite_note-21]

The region was formerly referred to as "West China" or "Western China" by [Protestant missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Sichuan).

## History

### Prehistory

The [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin) and adjacent areas of the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) watershed were a cradle of indigenous civilizations dating back to at least the 15th century BC, coinciding with the [Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty) in northern China. The region had its own distinct religious beliefs and worldview. The earliest excavated culture found therein is the [Baodun culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baodun_culture) (c.2700–1750 BC) excavated in the [Chengdu Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Plain).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### Ba and Shu Kingdoms



Bronze figure of a [high priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_priest) from [Sanxingdui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxingdui), dating from the [Shu kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\))



[Golden Sun Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Sun_Bird) from [Jinsha site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_site)

The most important native states were those of Ba and Shu.

[Ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_\(state\)) stretched into Sichuan from the [Han Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Shaanxi\)) in [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) and [Hubei](83319.md) down the [Jialing River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jialing_River) as far as its confluence with the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) at [Chongqing](171752.md).[^cite_note-kongzi-24]

[Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\)) occupied the valley of the [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Sichuan\)), including [Chengdu](170375.md) and other areas of western Sichuan.[^cite_note-kongzi-24] The existence of the early state of Shu was poorly recorded in the main historical records of China. It was, however, referred to in the *[Book of Documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Documents)* as an ally of the Zhou.[^cite_note-25] Accounts of Shu exist mainly as a mixture of mythological stories and historical legends recorded in local annals such as the *[Chronicles of Huayang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicles_of_Huayang)* compiled in the [Jin dynasty (266–420)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(266%E2%80%93420\)),[^cite_note-sanxingdui-26][^cite_note-27] and the Han-dynasty compilation *[Chronicle of the Kings of Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicle_of_the_Kings_of_Shu?action=edit\&redlink=1)*.[^cite_note-28] These contained folk stories such as that of [Emperor Duyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duyu) who taught the people agriculture and transformed himself into a cuckoo after his death.[^cite_note-perf-29]

The existence of a highly developed civilization with an independent bronze industry in Sichuan was excavated in 1986 at a small village named [Sanxingdui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxingdui) in [Guanghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanghan), Sichuan.[^cite_note-perf-29] This site, believed to be an ancient city of Shu, was initially discovered by a local farmer in 1929 who found jade and stone artifacts. Excavations by archeologists yielded few significant finds until 1986 when two major sacrificial pits were found with spectacular bronze items as well as artifacts in jade, gold, earthenware, and stone.[^cite_note-30] This and other discoveries in Sichuan contest the conventional historiography that the local culture and technology of Sichuan were undeveloped in comparison to the technologically and culturally "advanced" [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) valley of north-central China.

### Qin dynasty

The rulers of the expansionist state of [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)), based in present-day [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) and [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi), were the first strategists to realize that the area's military importance matched its commercial and agricultural significance. The Sichuan basin is surrounded by the [Hengduan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengduan_Mountains) to the west, the [Qin Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Mountains) to the north, and [Yungui Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yungui_Plateau) to the south. Since the Yangtze flows through the basin and then through the perilous Three Gorges to eastern and southern China, Sichuan was a staging area for amphibious military forces and a haven for political refugees.[^cite_note-31]

Qin armies finished their conquest of the kingdoms of Shu and Ba by 316 BC. Any written records and civil achievements of earlier kingdoms were destroyed. Qin administrators introduced improved agricultural technology. [Li Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Bing_\(Qin\)), engineered the [Dujiangyan irrigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujiangyan_irrigation_system) to control the [Min River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Sichuan\)), a major [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the Yangtze. This innovative hydraulic system was composed of movable weirs which could be adjusted for high or low water flow according to the season, to either provide irrigation or prevent floods. The increased agricultural output and taxes made the area a source of provisions and men for Qin's unification of China.[^cite_note-32]

### Han dynasty



A stone-carved gate pillar, or *[que](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Que_\(tower\))*, 6 m (20 ft) in total height, located at the tomb of Gao Yi in Ya'an, Sichuan, built during the [Eastern Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Han) (25–220 CE)

Sichuan was subjected to the autonomous control of kings named by the imperial family of the Han dynasty. During the 11 years hiatus between 25 and 36 AD, Sichuan was controlled by the [Chengjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengjia) Kingdom. Following the declining central government of the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) in the second century, the Sichuan basin, surrounded by mountains and easily defensible, became a popular place for upstart generals to found kingdoms that challenged the authority of Yangtze Valley emperors over China.[^cite_note-Haw-33]

### Three Kingdoms

In 221, during the partition following the fall of the [Eastern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Han) – the era of the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) – [Liu Bei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Bei) founded the southwest kingdom of [Shu Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) ([蜀](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%9C%80)[漢](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%BC%A2); 221–263) in parts of Sichuan, Guizhou, and Yunnan, with [Chengdu](170375.md) as its capital. Shu-Han claimed to be the successor to the Han dynasty.[^cite_note-Haw-33]

In 263, the [Cao Wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cao_Wei) of North China [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Shu_by_Wei) the Kingdom of Shu-Han as a step on the path to reuniting China. [Salt production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_in_Chinese_history) became a major business in [Ziliujing District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziliujing_District). During the [Six Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Dynasties) period of Chinese disunity, Sichuan began to be populated by non-[Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) ethnic minority peoples, owing to the migration of [Gelao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelao_people) from the [Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Guizhou_Plateau) to the Sichuan basin.[^cite_note-34]

### Tang dynasty



The [Leshan Giant Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leshan_Giant_Buddha), built during the latter half of the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) (618–907).

Sichuan came under the firm control of a Chinese central government during the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty), but it was during the subsequent [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) that Sichuan regained its previous political and cultural prominence for which it was known during the Han. Chengdu became nationally known as a supplier of armies and the home of [Du Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Fu), who is sometimes called China's greatest poet. During the [An Lushan Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Lushan_Rebellion) (755–763), [Emperor Xuanzong of Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Xuanzong_of_Tang) fled from [Chang'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'an) to Sichuan which became his refuge. The region was torn by constant warfare and economic distress as it was besieged by the [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire).[^cite_note-Tara-35]

### Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms

In the [Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms_period), Sichuan became the heart of the [Shu kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Shu) with its capital in [Chengdu](170375.md). In 925, the kingdom was absorbed into [Later Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Tang) but would regain independence under [Meng Zhixiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Zhixiang) who founded [Later Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Shu) in 934. Later Shu would continue until 965 when it was absorbed by the [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty).

### Song and Yuan dynasties

During the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) (960–1279), [Sichuanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_people) were able to protect themselves from [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) attacks with the help of the central government. There were rebellions against the Song by Li Shun in 994 and Wang Jun in 1000. Sichuan also saw cultural revivals like the great poets [Su Xun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su_Xun) (蘇洵), [Su Shi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su_Shi), and [Su Zhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su_Zhe).[^cite_note-Tara-35] Although paper currency was known in the Tang dynasty, in 1023 AD, the first true [paper money](208286.md) in human history ([交子](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BA%A4%E5%AD%90); *jiāozǐ*) was issued in [Chengdu](170375.md).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

It was also during the Song dynasty that the bulk of the native [Ba people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_\(state\)) of eastern Sichuan assimilated into the Han Chinese ethnicity.[^cite_note-39]

In the 12th and 13th centuries, the Southern Song dynasty established coordinated defenses against the [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), in Sichuan and [Xiangyang](2926292.md). The Southern Song state monopolized the Sichuan [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) industry to pay for warhorses, but this state intervention eventually brought devastation to the local economy.[^cite_note-40]

The line of defense was broken through after the first use of [firearms](11966.md) in history during the six-year [Battle of Xiangyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Xiangyang), which ended in 1273. Allegedly there were a million pieces of unspecified types of skeleton bones belonging to war animals and both Song and Yuan soldiers who perished in the fighting over the city, although the figure may have been grossly exaggerated.[^cite_note-41] The recorded number of families in Sichuan dropped from 2,640,000 families,[^cite_note-42] as recorded from the census taken in 1162 AD, to 120,000 families[^cite_note-43] in 1282 AD.[^cite_note-44]

Possible causes include forced population transfer to nearby areas, evacuation to nearby provinces, census under-reporting or inaccuracy, and war-related deaths. One instance of the deportation of Sichuanese civilians to Mongolia occurred in the aftermath of a battle in 1259 when more than 80,000 people were taken captive from one city in Sichuan and moved to Mongolia.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### Ming dynasty



Map of *Suchuen* (Sichuan) from [Willem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Blaeu) and [Joan Blaeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Blaeu)'s 1659 *[Geographia Blaviana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Maior)*.

The Ming dynasty defeated [Ming Yuzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Yuzhen)'s Xia polity which ruled Sichuan.[^cite_note-MoteTwitchett1988-47]

During the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), major architectural works were created in Sichuan. Buddhism remained influential in the region. [Bao'en Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'en_Temple_\(Pingwu\)) is a well-preserved 15th-century monastery complex built between 1440 and 1446 during the [Zhengtong Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Yingzong_of_Ming)'s reign (1427–64). Dabei Hall enshrines a thousand-armed wooden image of [Guanyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanyin) and Huayan Hall is a repository with a revolving [sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutra) cabinet. The wall paintings, sculptures, and other ornamental details are masterpieces of the Ming period.[^cite_note-pan-48]

In the middle of the 17th century, the peasant rebel leader [Zhang Xianzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Xianzhong) (1606–1646) from [Yan'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan'an), [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) Province, nicknamed *Yellow Tiger*, led his peasant troop from north China to the south and conquered Sichuan. Upon capturing it, he declared himself emperor of the Daxi dynasty (大西王朝). In response to the resistance from local elites, he massacred a large number of people in Sichuan, killing around one in three people.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] As a result of the massacre as well as years of turmoil during the [Ming-Qing transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_conquest_of_the_Ming), the population of Sichuan fell sharply, requiring massive resettlement of people from the neighboring [Huguang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguang_Province) (modern Hubei and Hunan) and other provinces during the Qing dynasty.[^cite_note-parsons-51][^cite_note-Dai-52][^cite_note-Dai2009-53]

### Qing dynasty

See also: [Hakka people § Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people#Sichuan)

Sichuan was originally the origin of the Deng lineage until one of them was hired as an official in Guangdong during the Ming dynasty but during the Qing plan to increase the population in 1671 they came to Sichuan again. In 1904 [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) was born in Sichuan.[^cite_note-Dai2009_2-54]

During the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), Sichuan was merged with [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) and [Shanxi](81262.md) to create "Shenzhuan" during 1680–1731 and 1735–1748.[^cite_note-Tara-35] The current borders of Sichuan (which then included [Chongqing](171752.md)) were established in the early 18th century. In the aftermath of the [Sino-Nepalese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Nepalese_War) on China's southwestern border, the Qing gave Sichuan's provincial government direct control over the minority-inhabited areas of Sichuan west of [Kangding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangding), which had previously been handled by an [amban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amban).[^cite_note-Dai-52]

A [landslide dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_dam) on the [Dadu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadu_River_\(Sichuan\)) caused by an earthquake gave way on 10 June 1786. The resulting flood killed 100,000 people.[^cite_note-55]

### Republic of China



Japanese bombers bombing a road in [Chongqing](171752.md), then part of Sichuan province, during WW2

In the early 20th century, the newly founded [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) established the Chuanbian Special Administrative District (川邊特別行政區) on the province's territories to the west of the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin). The Special District later became the province of [Xikang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xikang), incorporating the areas inhabited by [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people), [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people), and [Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_people) ethnic minorities to its west, and eastern part of today's [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region).[^cite_note-56]

In the 20th century, as [Beijing](18603746.md), [Shanghai](27643.md), [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), and [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan) had all been occupied by the Japanese during the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), the capital of the Republic of China had been temporarily relocated to [Chongqing](171752.md), then a major city in Sichuan. An enduring legacy of this move is those nearby inland provinces, such as [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi), [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu), and [Guizhou](173815.md), which previously never had [modern Western-style universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_education_in_China), began to be developed in this regard.[^cite_note-57] The difficulty of accessing the region overland from the eastern part of China and the foggy climate hindering the accuracy of the [Japanese bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_invasion) of the Sichuan Basin made the region the stronghold of [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek)'s [Kuomintang](16903.md) government during 1938–45 and led to the [Bombing of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Chongqing).[^cite_note-58]

The [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) was soon followed by the resumed [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), and the cities of East China were obtained by the Communists one after another, the Kuomintang government again tried to make Sichuan its stronghold on the mainland, although it already saw some Communist activity since it was one area on the road of the [Long March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March). Chiang Kai-shek himself flew to Chongqing from [Taiwan](25734.md) in November 1949 to lead the defense. But the same month Chongqing switched to the Communists, followed by Chengdu on 10 December. The Kuomintang general [Wang Sheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Sheng_\(soldier\)) wanted to stay behind with his troops to continue the anticommunist guerilla war in Sichuan, but was recalled to Taiwan. Many of his soldiers made their way there as well, via [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma).[^cite_note-59]

### People's Republic of China

The People's Republic of China was founded in 1949, and it split Sichuan into four areas and separated Chongqing municipality. Sichuan was reconstituted in 1952, with Chongqing added in 1954, while the former [Xikang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xikang) province was split between [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) in the west and Sichuan in the east.[^cite_note-Tara-35]

The province was deeply affected by the [Great Chinese Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine) of 1959–1961, during which period some 9.4 million people (13.07% of the population at the time) died.[^cite_note-cao-60]

In 1978, when [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) took power, Sichuan was one of the first provinces to experiment with the market economic enterprise.

From 1955 until 1997, Sichuan had been China's most populous province; the population hit the 100 million mark shortly after the 1982 census figure of 99,730,000.[^cite_note-61] This changed in 1997 when the [Sub-provincial city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-provincial_city) of Chongqing as well as the three surrounding prefectures of [Fuling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuling), [Wanxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanxian), and [Qianjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianjiang_District) were split off into the new [Chongqing Municipality](171752.md). The new municipality was formed to spearhead China's effort to economically develop its western provinces, as well as to coordinate the resettlement of residents from the reservoir areas of the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) project.

In 1997, when Sichuan split, the sum of the two parts was recorded to be 114,720,000 people.[^cite_note-62] As of 2010, Sichuan ranks as both the [3rd largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_area) (the largest among Chinese [provinces with a population greater than 50 million) and 4th most populous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_People's_Republic_of_China_administrative_divisions_by_population) in China.[^cite_note-bare_url-63]

On 12 May 2008, [an earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Sichuan_earthquake) with a magnitude of 7.9/8.0 hit just 79 km (49 mi) northwest of the provincial capital of Chengdu. Official figures recorded a death toll of over 87,000 people, and millions of people were left homeless.[^cite_note-data-64]

## Geography and biodiversity

Sichuan consists of two very distinct geographic parts. The eastern part of the province is mostly within the fertile [Sichuan basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_basin) (which is shared by Sichuan with Chongqing Municipality). The western Sichuan consists of numerous mountain ranges forming the easternmost part of the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau), which are known generically as the [Hengduan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengduan_Mountains). One of these ranges, the [Daxue Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daxue_Mountains), contains the highest point of the province [Gongga Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongga_Shan), at 7,556 m (24,790 ft) above sea level. The mountains are formed by the collision of the Tibetan Plateau with the [Yangtze Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_Plate). Faults here include the [Longmenshan Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longmenshan_Fault) which ruptured during the 2008 Sichuan earthquake. Other mountain ranges surround the Sichuan Basin from north, east, and south. Among them are the [Daba Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daba_Mountains), in the province's northeast.

The [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) and its tributaries flow through the mountains of western Sichuan and the Sichuan Basin; thus, the province is upstream of the great cities that stand along the Yangtze River further to the east, such as [Chongqing](171752.md), [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), and [Shanghai](27643.md). One of the major tributaries of the Yangtze within the province is the [Min River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Sichuan\)) of central Sichuan, which joins the Yangtze at [Yibin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yibin). There are also a number of other rivers, such as the [Jialing River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jialing_River), [Tuo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuo_River), [Yalong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalong_River), [Wu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_River_\(Yangtze_tributary\)), and [Jinsha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_River), and any four of the various rivers are often grouped as the "four rivers" that the name of Sichuan is commonly and mistakenly believed to mean.[^cite_note-ruf-12][^cite_note-rowan-13]

Sichuan borders [Qinghai](173818.md) to the northwest, [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) to the north, [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) to the northeast, [Chongqing](171752.md) to the east, [Guizhou](173815.md) to the southeast, [Yunnan](166410.md) to the south, and the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) to the west.

### Giant panda



[Giant pandas](12713.md) eating bamboo in [Chengdu](170375.md), Sichuan

[Giant pandas](12713.md) live in [bamboo forests](39029.md) and low mountainous areas such as the [Minshan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Mountains) in Sichuan.[^cite_note-84] The majority of the panda population lives in Sichuan, with their range spreading into [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) and [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu). As it is abundant where they live, pandas' diet consists of 99% bamboo, with small other plants, or small animals consisting of the other 1%. As the panda is native to China, they have become a national [symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol) of China.[^cite_note-85]

### Climate

Köppen–Geiger climate classification map at 1-km resolution for Sichuan (China) for 1991–2020

A Köppen climate classification map of Sichuan

Due to great differences in terrain, the climate of the province is highly variable. In general, it has strong [monsoonal](57630.md) influences, with rainfall heavily concentrated in the summer. Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin) (including [Chengdu](170375.md)) in the eastern half of the province experiences a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cwa* or *Cfa*), with long, hot, wet summers and short, mild to cool, dry, and cloudy winters. Consequently, it has China's lowest sunshine totals.

The western region has mountainous areas producing a cooler but sunnier climate. Having cool to very cold winters and mild summers, temperatures generally decrease with greater elevation. Due to high altitude and inland location, the far northwestern areas like [Garzê County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garz%C3%AA_County) and [Zoigê County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoig%C3%AA_County) exhibit a [subalpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subalpine_climate) (Köppen *Dwc*) or even an [alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) (*ETH*), featuring frigid winters down to −30 °C or −22 °F and even cold summer nights. The region is geologically active with landslides and earthquakes. Average elevation ranges from 2,000 to 3,500 metres or 6,600 to 11,500 feet; average temperatures range from 0 to 15 °C or 32 to 59 °F.[^cite_note-TibetansSichuan-86]

The southern part of the province, including Panzhihua and Xichang, has a sunny climate with short, very mild winters and very warm to hot summers.

## Politics

Main articles: [Politics of Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Sichuan) and [List of current Chinese provincial leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_Chinese_provincial_leaders)

The politics of Sichuan is structured in a dual party-government system like all other governing institutions in [mainland China](55079.md).

The [governor of Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Sichuan) is the highest-ranking official in the People's Government of Sichuan. However, in the province's dual party-government governing system, the Governor has less power than the [Party Secretary of Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Sichuan), colloquially termed the "Sichuan CPC Party Chief".

### Governance

The [Sichuan Provincial Prison Administrative Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Provincial_Prison_Administrative_Bureau) is the main corrections agency in Sichuan.[^cite_note-87]

The [Sichuan Provincial Public Security Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Provincial_Public_Security_Department?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the primary law enforcement agency in Sichuan. It has a SWAT unit, a forestry unit, an anti-drug unit, an economic crime unit, a food safety unit and an investigation unit.[^cite_note-\:3-88] In 2021, the agency had a budget of 45.8 million [Renminbi](66958.md).[^cite_note-\:3-88]

On July 10, 2017, the Sichuan Provincial Public Security Department established the [Sichuan Provincial Expressways Public Security Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Provincial_Expressways_Public_Security_Bureau?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 四川高速公路公安局), a provincial [Highway patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_patrol) agency.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

## Economy



Chengdu IFS



[Tianfu New Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianfu_New_Area)

Sichuan is the [5th-largest provincial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) of China, the largest in [Western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China) and the largest among inland provinces. As of 2021, its nominal GDP was 5,385 billion yuan (US\$847.68 billion), ahead of the GDP of Turkey of 815 billion.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-\:2-10] Compared to a country, it would be the [18th-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) as well as the [19th most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population) as of 2021.[^cite_note-\:1-11] As of 2021, its nominal GDP per capita was 64,357 RMB (US\$10,120).[^cite_note-\:0-9] In 2021, the per capita net income of rural residents was 17,575 yuan (US\$2760). The per capita [disposable income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_income) of the urbanites averaged 41,444 yuan (US\$6510).

Sichuan has been historically known as the "Province of Abundance". It is one of the major agricultural production bases of China. Grain, including rice and wheat, is the major product with output that ranked first in China in 1999. Commercial crops include citrus fruits, sugar cane, sweet potatoes, peaches, and grapes. Sichuan also had the largest output of pork among all the provinces and the second largest output of silkworm cocoons in 1999. Sichuan is rich in mineral resources. It has more than 132 kinds of proven underground mineral resources including vanadium, titanium, and lithium is the largest in China. The Panxi region alone possesses 13.3% of the reserves of iron, 93% of titanium, 69% of vanadium, and 83% of cobalt in the whole country.[^cite_note-94] Sichuan also possesses China's largest proven natural gas reserves (such as the [Dazhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazhou_gas_field) and [Yuanba gas fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuanba_gas_field)), the majority of which are transported to more developed eastern regions.[^cite_note-bare_url-63]

Sichuan is one of the major industrial centers of China. It was a major recipient of China's investment in industrial capacity during the [Third Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Front_\(China\)) campaign.[^cite_note-\:6-95]<sup>: 298 </sup> In addition to heavy industries such as coal, energy, iron, and steel, the province has also established a light industrial sector comprising building materials, wood processing, food, and silk processing. [Chengdu](170375.md) and [Mianyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mianyang) are the production centers for textiles and electronics products. [Deyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deyang), [Panzhihua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzhihua), and [Yibin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yibin) are the production centers for machinery, metallurgical industries, and wine, respectively. Sichuan's wine production accounted for 21.9% of the country's total production in 2000.

Great strides have been made in developing Sichuan into a modern hi-tech industrial base, by encouraging both domestic and foreign investments in electronics and information technology (such as software), machinery and metallurgy (including automobiles), hydropower, pharmaceutical, food and beverage industries.

The auto industry is an important and key sector of the machinery industry in Sichuan. Most of the auto manufacturing companies are located in Chengdu, Mianyang, [Nanchong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchong), and [Luzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzhou).[^cite_note-96]

Other important industries in Sichuan include [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) and defense (military) industries. A number of China's rockets ([Long March rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_rocket)) and [satellites](27683.md) were launched from the [Xichang Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xichang_Satellite_Launch_Center), located in the city of [Xichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xichang).

Sichuan's landscapes and rich historical relics have also made the province a center for tourism.

The [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam), the largest [dam](51518.md) ever constructed, was built on the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) in nearby [Hubei](83319.md) province to control flooding in the Sichuan Basin, neighboring [Yunnan](166410.md) province, and downstream. The plan is hailed by some as China's efforts to shift towards alternative energy sources and to further develop its industrial and commercial bases, but has been denounced for mass resettlement, loss of archeological sites, and ecological damage.

### Economic development zones

#### Chengdu Hi-tech Comprehensive Free Trade Zone

Chengdu Hi-tech Comprehensive Free Trade Zone was established with the approval of the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) on October 18, 2010, and passed the national acceptance on February 25, 2011. It was officially operated in May 2011. Chengdu High-tech Comprehensive Free Trade Zone is integrated and expanded from the former Chengdu Export Processing Zone and Chengdu Bonded Logistics Center. it is located in the Chengdu West High-tech Industrial Development Zone, with an area of 4.68 square kilometers and divided into three areas A, B, and C. The industries focus on notebook computer manufacturing, tablet computer manufacturing, wafer manufacturing, chip packaging testing, electronic components, precision machining, and the biopharmaceutical industry. Chengdu Hi-Tech Comprehensive Free Trade Zone has attracted the top 500 multinational enterprises such as Intel, Foxconn, Texas Instruments, Dell, Morse, and so on.

In 2020, the Chengdu Hi-Tech Comprehensive Free Trade Zone achieved a total import and export volume of 549.1 billion yuan (including the Shuangliu Sub-zone), accounting for 68% of the province's total foreign trade import and export volume, ranking first in the national comprehensive insurance zone import and export volume for three consecutive years.

#### Chengdu Economic and Technological Development Zone

Chengdu Economic and Technological Development Zone (Chinese: 成都经济技术开发区; pinyin: *Chéngdū jīngjì jìshù kāifā qū*) was approved as state-level development zone in February 2000. The zone now has a developed area of 10.25 km<sup>2</sup> (3.96 sq mi) and a planned area of 26 km<sup>2</sup> (10 sq mi). Chengdu Economic and Technological Development Zone (CETDZ) lies 13.6 km (8.5 mi) east of Chengdu, the capital city of Sichuan Province and the hub of transportation and communication in southwest China. The zone has attracted investors and developers from more than 20 countries to carry out their projects there. Industries encouraged in the zone include mechanical, electronic, new building materials, medicine, and food processing.[^cite_note-97]

#### Chengdu Export Processing Zone

Chengdu [Export Processing Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export_Processing_Zone) (Chinese: 成都出口加工区; pinyin: *Chéngdū chūkǒu jiāgōng qū*)) was ratified by the State Council as one of the first 15 export processing zones in the country in April 2000. In 2002, the state ratified the establishment of the Sichuan Chengdu Export Processing West Zone with a planned area of 1.5 km<sup>2</sup> (0.58 sq mi), located inside the west region of the Chengdu Hi-tech Zone.[^cite_note-98]

#### Chengdu Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone



South Renmin Road, Chengdu

Established in 1988, Chengdu Hi-tech Industrial Development Zone (Chinese: 成都高新技术产业开发区; pinyin: *Chéngdū Gāoxīn Jìshù Chǎnyè Kāifā Qū*) was approved as one of the first national hi-tech development zones in 1991. In 2000, it was open to [APEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation) and has been recognized as a national advanced hi-tech development zone in successive assessment activities held by China's Ministry of Science and Technology. It ranks 5th among the 53 national hi-tech development zones in China in terms of comprehensive strength.

Chengdu Hi-tech Development Zone covers an area of 82.5 km<sup>2</sup> (31.9 sq mi), consisting of South Park and West Park. By relying on the city sub-center, which is under construction, South Park is focusing on creating a modernized industrial park of science and technology with scientific and technological innovation, incubation R\&D, modern service industry, and Headquarters economy playing leading roles. Priority has been given to the development of the software industry. Located on both sides of the "Chengdu-Dujiangyan-Jiuzhaigou" golden tourism channel, the West Park aims at building a comprehensive industrial park targeting industrial clustering with complete supportive functions. West Park gives priority to three major industries i.e. electronic information, biomedicine, and precision machinery.[^cite_note-99]

#### Mianyang Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone

Mianyang Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone was established in 1992, with a planned area of 43 km<sup>2</sup> (17 sq mi). The zone is situated 96 kilometers away from Chengdu and is 8 km (5.0 mi) away from Mianyang Airport. Since its establishment, the zone accumulated 177.4 billion yuan of industrial output, 46.2 billion yuan of gross domestic product, and fiscal revenue of 6.768 billion yuan. There are more than 136 high-tech enterprises in the zone and they accounted for more than 90% of the total industrial output.

The zone is a leader in the electronic information industry, biological medicine, new materials, and the production of motor vehicles and parts.[^cite_note-100]

## Transportation



[Sichuan–Tibet Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_318) passes by Lake Kasa in [Luhuo County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhuo_County).

For millennia, Sichuan's rugged and riverine landscape presented enormous challenges to the development of transportation infrastructure, and the lack of roads out of the Sichuan Basin contributed to the region's isolation. Since the 1950s, numerous highways and railways have been built through the Qinling in the north and the Bashan in the east. Dozens of bridges [across the Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_bridges_and_tunnels) and its tributaries to the south and west have brought greater connectivity with Yunnan and Tibet.

### Airports

[Chengdu Shuangliu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Shuangliu_International_Airport) is the 4th-busiest airport in mainland China. It was among the world's top 30 busiest airports in 2015, and the busiest in western and central China. It was also the fifth-busiest airport in terms of cargo traffic in China in 2013. Chengdu airport is the hub of [Sichuan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Airlines), [Chengdu Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Airlines), [Shenzhen Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Airlines), [Tibet Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Airlines), [China Southern Airlines](382884.md), [China Eastern Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Eastern_Airlines), [Lucky Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_Air), and [Air China](297958.md). Alongside Shuangliu Airport, [Chengdu Tianfu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Tianfu_International_Airport) has opened in 2021.

Chengdu airports are also 144-hour transit visa-free airports for foreigners from 53 countries.

### Expressways

On 3 November 2007, the Sichuan Transportation Bureau announced that the [Suining-Chongqing Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G93_Chengyu_Ring_Expressway) was completed after three years of construction. After the completion of the Chongqing section of the road, the 36.64 km (22.77 mi) expressway connected [Chengdu-Nanchong Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G42_Shanghai%E2%80%93Chengdu_Expressway) and formed the shortest expressway from [Chengdu](170375.md) to [Chongqing](171752.md). The new expressway is 50 km (31 mi) shorter than the pre-existing road between Chengdu and Chongqing; thus journey time between the two cities was reduced by an hour, now taking two and a half hours. The Sui-Yu Expressway is a four-lane overpass with a speed limit of 80 km/h (50 mph). The total investment was 1.045 billion [yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_yuan).

### Rail

[China Railway Chengdu Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Railway_Chengdu_Group) is headquartered in Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan Province, managing railway systems in Sichuan, Chongqing, and Guizhou. Sichuan's major railways in Sichuan include the [Baoji–Chengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baoji%E2%80%93Chengdu_railway), [Chengdu–Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Chongqing_railway), [Chengdu–Kunming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway), [Neijiang–Kunming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neijiang%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway), Suining–Chongqing, and Chengdu–Dazhou railways. [High-speed railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) in Sichuan include the [Chengdu–Chongqing high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Chongqing_high-speed_railway), [Xi'an-Chengdu high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an%E2%80%93Chengdu_high-speed_railway), [Chengdu-Guiyang high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Guiyang_high-speed_railway), and Chengdu–Kunming high-speed railway. A suburban railway [connects Chengdu and Dujiangyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Dujiangyan_intercity_railway).

The majority of the province's population is [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) (95% of the provincial population), who are found scattered throughout the region except for the far western areas. Thus, significant minorities of [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people), [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people), [Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_people), and [Nakhi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhi_people) reside in the western portion that is impacted by inclement weather and natural disasters, environmentally fragile, and impoverished. Sichuan's capital of [Chengdu](170375.md) is home to a large community of Tibetans, with 30,000 permanent Tibetan residents and up to 200,000 Tibetan [floating population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_population).[^cite_note-112] The [Eastern Lipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lipo), included with either the Yi or the [Lisu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people), as well as the [A-Hmao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-Hmao), also are among the ethnic groups of the provinces.

Sichuan was China's [most populous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population) before [Chongqing](171752.md) became a directly controlled municipality; it is currently the fourth most populous, after Guangdong, Shandong, and Henan. As of 1832, Sichuan was the most populous of the 18 provinces in China, with an estimated population at that time of 21 million.[^cite_note-Roberts1-113] It was the third [most populous sub-national entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-level_administrative_divisions_by_population) in the world, after [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), [India](14533.md), and the [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) until 1991, when the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was dissolved. It is also one of the only eight subnational divisions to ever reach 100 million people (Uttar Pradesh, Russian RSFSR, [Maharashtra](20629.md), Sichuan, [Bihar](442482.md), [Shandong](105032.md), [Guangdong](65109.md), and [Punjab](24751.md)). It is currently ranked 10th.

The predominant religions in Sichuan are [Chinese folk religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion), [Taoist traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism), and [Chinese Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Buddhism). According to surveys conducted in 2007 and 2009, 10.6% of the population believes and is involved in [cults of ancestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_ancestral_religion), while 0.68% of the population identifies as [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Sichuan).[^cite_note-Wang2015-115] According to the Japanese publication *Tokyo Sentaku* in 1999, there were 2 million members of [Yiguandao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiguandao) (Tiandao) in Sichuan, equal to 2.4% of the province's population.[^cite_note-YiguandaoSichuan-119]

The reports did not give figures for other types of religion; the vast majority may be either irreligious or involved in Chinese folk religion, Buddhism, etc. [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) is widespread, especially in areas inhabited by ethnic Tibetans. Sichuan is one of the cradles of the early [Heavenly Masters' Taoist religious movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengyi_Taoism). Ethnic minorities often practice their own [ethnic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_religions), often syncretically, such as [Daba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daba_\(religion\)), [Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon) and [Dongba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongba).

## Culture

Main article: [Ba–Shu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%E2%80%93Shu_culture)

Further information: [Sichuanese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_people)

The *Sichuanese people* ([Sichuanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_Mandarin): 巴蜀人 Ba<sup>1</sup>su<sup>2</sup>ren<sup>2</sup>; [IPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet): \[pa˥su˨˩zən˨˩]; alternatively 川人, 川渝人, 四川人 or 巴蜀民系) are a subgroup of Han Chinese living in mostly Sichuan province and the neighboring [Chongqing](171752.md) municipality. Beginning from the 9th century BC, [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\)) (on the [Chengdu Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Plain)) and [Ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_\(state\)) (which had its first capital at [Enshi City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshi_City) in [Hubei](83319.md) and controlled part of the [Han Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Shaanxi_and_Hubei\))) emerged as cultural and administrative centers where two rival kingdoms were established. Although eventually, the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty) destroyed the kingdoms of Shu and Ba, the Qin government accelerated the technological and agricultural advancements of Sichuan making it comparable to that of the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) Valley. The now-extinct [Ba–Shu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%E2%80%93Shu_Chinese) was derived from Qin-era settlers and represents the earliest documented division from what is now called [Middle Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Chinese).

During the [Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) and [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) dynasties, the population of the area was reduced through wars and the [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague), and settlers arrived from the area of modern Hubei, replacing the earlier common Chinese with a new standard.

The [Li Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Bai) Memorial, located in [Jiangyou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangyou), is a museum in memory of Li Bai, a Chinese poet of [Tang China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) (618–907) built at the place where he grew up. The building was begun in 1962 on the occasion of the 1200th anniversary of his death, completed in 1981, and opened to the public in October 1982. The memorial is built in the style of the classic Tang garden.

In 2003, Sichuan had "88 art performing troupes, 185 culture centers, 133 libraries, and 52 museums". Companies based in Sichuan also produced 23 television series and one film.[^cite_note-126]

### Languages

Main articles: [Sichuanese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_dialects) and [Ba–Shu Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%E2%80%93Shu_Chinese)



Extent of present-day Sichuanese language

The Sichuanese once spoke their variety of Spoken Chinese called Ba-Shu Chinese, or Old Sichuanese before it became extinct during the Ming dynasty. Now most of them speak Sichuanese Mandarin. The Minjiang dialects are thought by some linguists to be a bona fide descendant of Old Sichuanese, but there is no conclusive evidence whether Minjiang dialects are derived from Old Sichuanese or Southwestern Mandarin.

The languages of Sichuan are primarily members of three subfamilies of the [Sino-Tibetan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_languages).

The most widely used variety of Chinese spoken in Sichuan is [Sichuanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_dialects), which is the [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) in Sichuan, [Chongqing](171752.md), and parts of the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region). Although Sichuanese is generally classified as a dialect of [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), it is highly divergent in phonology, vocabulary, and even grammar from [Standard Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese).[^cite_note-《四川方言与巴蜀文化》-127] The [Minjiang dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minjiang_dialect) is especially difficult for speakers of other Mandarin dialects to understand.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

[Garzê Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garz%C3%AA_Tibetan_Autonomous_Prefecture) and [Ngawa Tibetan and Qiang Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngawa_Tibetan_and_Qiang_Autonomous_Prefecture) in western Sichuan are populated by [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) and [Qiang people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_people). Tibetans speak the [Khams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khams_Tibetan) and [Amdo Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo_Tibetan), which are [Tibetic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetic_languages), as well as various [Qiangic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiangic_languages). The Qiang speak Qiangic languages and often Tibetic languages as well. The [Yi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people) of [Liangshan Yi Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangshan_Yi_Autonomous_Prefecture) in southern Sichuan speak the [Nuosu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuosu_language), which is one of the [Lolo-Burmese languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolo-Burmese_languages); Yi is written using the [Yi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_script), a [syllabary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syllabary) standardized in 1974. The [Southwest University for Nationalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_University_for_Nationalities) has one of China's most prominent [Tibetology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetology) departments and the Southwest Minorities Publishing House prints literature in minority languages.[^cite_note-132] In the minority-inhabited regions of Sichuan, there is bilingual signage and public school instruction in non-Mandarin minority languages.

### Sichuan brocade

Main article: [Sichuan embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_embroidery)



Sichuan brocade manufactured between the 7th and early 10th century, featuring double bird in floral roundel pattern; collection of [Chengdu Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Museum).

Commonly known as "Shu brocade" (蜀錦) in Chinese, Sichuan brocade is referred to as the "mother of brocade in China" given its age.[^cite_note-133] This technique of embroidery originates in the capital city of [Chengdu](170375.md) during the time of the [Ancient Kingdom of Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\)). It enjoyed high popularity throughout the regions along the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road), which stimulated an "[exoticization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoticism)" of the embroidery designs during the 1st millennium, with most of the patterns imported from [Sogdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogdia) and other parts of [Central Asia](6742.md).[^cite_note-134]

According to the *[Book of Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Sui)*, in the year 605 AD, the head of the Sichuan ateliers producing silks in the "western style" was a certain He Chou, a name which betrays his Sogdian origins.[^cite_note-135] Most of the silk products unearthed in [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) ([Chinese Turkestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Xinjiang)) and [Qinghai](173818.md) ([Tuyuhun Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuyuhun)) confirmed to be manufactured in Sichuan.[^cite_note-136]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Sichuan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine)

Sichuan is known for its spicy cuisine and use of [Sichuan peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_pepper) due to its humid climate.



**Hotpot**

Another famous Sichuan delicacy is [hot pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_pot). Hot pot is a Chinese soup containing a variety of East Asian foodstuffs and ingredients, prepared with a simmering pot of soup stock at the dining table. While the hot pot is kept simmering, ingredients are placed into the pot and cooked at the table. Typical hot pot dishes include thinly sliced meat, leaf vegetables, mushrooms, wontons, egg dumplings, tofu, and seafood. The cooked food is usually eaten with a dipping sauce.
