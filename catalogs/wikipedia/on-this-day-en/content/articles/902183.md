# Double-decker bus



A [New Routemaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Routemaster) double-decker bus in [London](17867.md)

A **double-decker bus** is a [bus](4146.md) that has two [storeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey) or decks. Double-deckers are used primarily for commuter public transport, but there are [coach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(bus\)) variants for long-distance travel and [open top buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_top_bus) that are most commonly used as sightseeing buses for tourists. They appear in many places around the world, but are presently most commonly used as [mass transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transport) in major cities.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

The earliest double-decker was the [horse-drawn omnibus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse-drawn_omnibus), which appeared in [Paris](22989.md) in 1853 and became [motorised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle) in the 1900s.[^cite_note-3] Double-decker buses were popularised in Great Britain at the start of the 20th century,[^cite_note-4] with the best-known example being the [red London bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_London), namely the [AEC Routemaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Routemaster).[^cite_note-5] Double-deckers in urban transport were also in common use in other places, such as major cities of [India](14533.md),[^cite_note-6] but were mostly phased out by the end of the 20th century. However, they remain common in Britain as well as Ireland and Hong Kong, while in [Singapore](27318.md) and [Dhaka](56656.md) they have been introduced and greatly expanded in the aftermath of British [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) rule.

Early double-deckers put the driver in a separate cab. Passenger access was via an open platform at the rear and a [bus conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_conductor) collected fares. Modern double-deckers have a main entrance door at the front and the driver takes fares, thus halving the number of workers aboard, but slowing the boarding process. The rear open platform, popular with passengers, was abandoned for safety reasons, as there was a risk of passengers falling when running and jumping onto the bus.

### Europe

In the [European Union](9317.md), the maximum height for any vehicle is 4 metres, for motor vehicles in categories M2 and M3 and their trailers in category 0 and motor vehicles in categories N2 and N3 and their trailers in categories 03 and 04, in national and international traffic according to Council directive 96/53/EC of 25 July 1996 and in continuity of council directive 85/3/CEE.

The [United Kingdom](31717.md) has a triple standard for the double-decker bus: highbridge bus (urban Britain), lowbridge bus (countryside Britain) and 4 metres height coach such as the [Neoplan Skyliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplan_Skyliner) that can traverse Europe.[^cite_note-7]

Outside the British Isles in Europe double-decker buses are most prominent in Skopje and Berlin.

#### United Kingdom



An historical Brixton to Clapham horse-drawn omnibus on display at the [London Bus Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Bus_Museum)



First appearing in London in 1947, the [AEC Regent III RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Regent_III_RT) (pictured) was the predecessor to the [AEC Routemaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Routemaster).



[Arriva Southern Counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva_Southern_Counties) [Volvo B7TL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B7TL) with [TransBus ALX400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransBus_ALX400) bodywork in England



[Stagecoach Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_Yorkshire) [Enviro400EV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enviro400EV) electric double-decker in [Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterfield,_Derbyshire) in May 2025

The first commercial horse-drawn double-decker omnibuses were introduced in England in 1847 by [Adams & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bridges_Adams) of Fairfield, [Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow,_London); it was then improved by John Greenwood, who introduced a new double-decker in 1852.[^cite_note-8] [William Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone), speaking of [London](17867.md)'s double-deck [horse-drawn omnibus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse-drawn_omnibus), once observed that "the best way to see London is from the top of a bus".[^cite_note-9]

Double-decker buses are in common use throughout the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and have been favoured over [articulated buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulated_bus) by many operators because of the shorter length of double-deckers and larger [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity); they also may be safer to operate through narrow streets and round tight corners. The majority of double-decker buses in the UK are between 9.5 metres (31 ft 2 in) and 11.1 metres (36 ft 5 in) long, the latter being more common since the mid-1990s, though there are three-[axle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axle) 12-metre (39-foot-4-inch) models in service with some operators. Double-decker coaches in the UK have traditionally been 12 metres (39 feet 4 inches) in length, though many newer models are about 13.75 metres (45 ft 1 in).

The red double-decker [buses in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_London) have become a [national symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbol) of England. Most buses in London are double-deckers. A particular example was the [AEC Routemaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Routemaster) bus, which was a staple of the public transport network in London for nearly half a century following its introduction in 1956. The remaining Routemasters were finally retired from general service in 2005 because of the difficulty of accommodating disabled passengers. [Transport for London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_London) kept these vintage buses in operation on heritage route [15H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses_route_15_\(Heritage\)) until 2020, when it was discontinued due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The contract expired in November 2020 and was not renewed; in 2021 it was announced that the service would no longer continue.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] There was formerly a second heritage route ([9H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses_route_9_\(Heritage\))) but this ceased operation in 2014 due to low patronage and increased operation costs.[^cite_note-12]

In 2007, a [hybrid-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_bus) double-decker entered service on [London Buses route 141](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses_route_141). By late 2008, more hybrid double-deckers from three manufacturers had entered service in London. A [New Routemaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Routemaster) was developed that year and entered service on 20 February 2012. In October 2015, London added five all-electric double-decker buses - the world's first - made by Chinese firm [BYD](1461760.md).[^cite_note-13]

The maximum permissible length of a rigid double-decker bus and coach in the UK is 15 metres (49 ft 3 in) with 3 axles and 13.5 metres (44 ft 3 in) metres with two.[^cite_note-14] However, the total maximum dimensions, including trailer or articulated section, in normal circumstances are:

| Dimension | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 18.75 metres (61 ft 6 in) |
| Width | 2.55 metres (8 ft 4 in) |
| Height | 4.95 metres (16 ft 3 in) |
| Mass | 12,000 kilograms (26,455 lb) |

Coaches are normally built to 4.38 metres (14 ft 4 in) high, while 'highbridge' buses are normally about 20 centimetres (8 in) taller. [Articulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulated_bus) double-deckers are also allowed at a maximum length of 18.65 metres (61 ft 2 in).

#### France



Left, double-decker bus Schneider Brillié P2; Centre, double decker horse-drawn omnibus in Paris, France

The first French double-decker bus was brought into service in [Paris](22989.md) in 1853; it was a horse-drawn omnibus. The upper floor was cheaper and often uncovered.

The first double-decker motor bus in Paris, the Schneider Brillié P2, appeared in 1906. It was designed to carry more passengers and to replace the horse-drawn double-decker omnibus. Like trams and omnibuses, double-decker motor buses included two classes of travel: first class inside the car and second class outdoors on top. But this type of vehicle was withdrawn in 1911 because one of them overturned at [place de l'Étoile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_Charles_de_Gaulle); following this incident the P2s lost their upper deck and were renamed P3s.

It was not until 1966 that the [RATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RATP_Group) re-tried double-deckers on two lines in Paris. A prototype built by [Berliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berliet) (type E-PCMR), was put into service in 1966, with an order being placed for 25 vehicles. The first production car was commissioned on 19 June 1968 for line 94, Gare Montparnasse - Levallois. On 17 February 1969, line 53, Opera - Porte d'Asnieres was in turn equipped with this model. But traffic problems caused RATP to definitively abandon this vehicle in 1977, because this type of bus was found to be poorly suited to the structure of the Paris network, the stops being too close to each other, preventing people from going upstairs. Consequently, there are no Parisian bus routes using double-deckers.

SITAC operates a service 5 between [Calais](85468.md) and [Sangatte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangatte) using a double decker bus.

#### Germany

Main article: [Bus transport in Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_transport_in_Berlin)



MAN A39 in [Berlin](3354.md), Germany

In [Germany](11867.md), double-decker buses in [Berlin](3354.md) are operated by [Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berliner_Verkehrsbetriebe) (BVG). Berlin has had the largest double-decker fleet on continental Europe with 197 vehicles operating as of 2023 (compared to 484 single-deckers and 928 articulated buses).[^cite_note-28] However it used to be higher: 1,000 in 1992, reduced to 450 in 2002.[^cite_note-deutsche_welle-2002-29] The city originally had double-decker buses at least since the 1920s.[^cite_note-30]

The models in operation in 2004 were 13.5 metres (44 ft 3 in) long and held around 95 passengers. The replacements, which are supplied by [Neoman Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoman_Bus), are 1.8 metres (5 ft 11 in) longer. The new buses are able to hold 128 passengers.[^cite_note-deutsche_welle-2002-29]

Double Decker tour coach in the Netherlands

#### Portugal



MAN A39 double-deck bus on route 500 in Porto, Portugal

Double-decker buses were introduced in Portugal during the 1950s when buses in general started to be used in the main cities such as Lisbon, Porto, Coimbra and Setúbal. The types used were the AEC Regent and later the Daimler Fleetline and the Leyland Atlantean, with Portuguese-built bodies. There was also one Leyland Olympian as a demonstration vehicle in Lisbon. In Porto, there were double-decker trolleybuses, produced by Lancia and with Dalfa bodywork, in use from the mid-1960s until the mid-1990s. Double-decker buses were not in widespread use for normal service but were mainly used for sightseeing purposes. They were most commonly Portuguese-produced vehicles, including rebodies of regular service buses (for example, the Volvo B10R from Carristur), as well as some from former companies, such as the MAN SD202 from BVG Berlin, many of them still in circulation.

The absence of double-decker buses on regular service lasted until 2011, when STCP acquired 15 double-decker buses, of the type MAN A39 (as used in Berlin). They were introduced at an event by the company, named "Duplex Tour", on 26 February 2011 and put into normal service on the 28th of that month. These buses can be seen usually on route 500.

#### Sweden



[VDL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VDL_Bus_&_Coach) Synergy double-decker bus in [Norrtälje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrt%C3%A4lje), Sweden

### Africa

#### Egypt



A double-decker bus in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), Egypt

#### Ethiopia

In 2017, as part of a larger order of 850 new buses, the city of [Addis Ababa](52593.md) purchased a fleet of 50 double-decker buses to operate routes on its public transportation system. Of these, 25 are operated by the [Anbessa City Bus Service Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anbessa_City_Bus_Service_Enterprise) and 25 are part of the Sheger bus company's fleet; both are government-owned.[^cite_note-49]

A double-decker bus on its first day of operation in [Mombasa](200152.md), Kenya

#### South Africa



[Johannesburg](58893.md) Metrobus

Double-decker buses are a feature of a number of transportation systems in [South Africa](17416221.md). [Johannesburg](58893.md)'s public bus system, known as Metrobus and operated by the city, has 550 buses that run on 84 routes throughout the city. Of these, 150 are modern double-decker buses manufactured by [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Buses), [Scania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania_AB), and [Marcopolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcopolo_S.A.).[^cite_note-54] The [City of Tshwane Metropolitan Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Tshwane_Metropolitan_Municipality) in [Gauteng Province](309661.md) boasts 113 double-decker buses among its public transportation fleet.[^cite_note-55] [Golden Arrow Bus Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Arrow_Bus_Services), the main operator of bus services in [Cape Town](6653.md), is the owner of recently acquired double-decker [MAN Lion's City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Lion's_City) buses.[^cite_note-56]

Like in other countries across [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa), double-decker buses are often utilized by private companies for [intercity transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus_service) connecting major population centers, as well as linking South African cities with hubs in [Botswana](3464.md), [Malawi](19088.md), [Mozambique](19301.md), [Namibia](21292.md), and [Zimbabwe](34399.md).[^cite_note-intercape-53] A number of tourism companies offer [open top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_top_bus), double-decker bus sightseeing tours of major cities in South Africa.

The [Volvo Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Olympian) was previously used.

[New Lantao Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Lantao_Bus), uses a fleet of 59 double decker buses, mainly from the [MAN A95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_A95) and the [Alexander Dennis Enviro400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro400).

#### Japan



A [Mitsubishi Fuso Aero King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Fuso_Aero_King) at Tokyo station as "Dream-go" in Japan



A [Neoplan Megaliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplan_Megaliner) at Osaka station as "Seishun Mega Dream" in Japan

By [Japanese](15573.md) law, vehicles are confined to maximum 3.8 metres (12 ft 6 in) height and 12.0 metres (39 ft 4 in) length. Japanese double-decker buses are mainly used for inter-city [highway buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(vehicle\)) (i.e., motor coaches), city tours, and charter buses. In 1960, [Kinki Sharyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinki_Sharyo) and [Hino Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hino_Motors) manufactured the first original double-decker bus "Vista Coach" for [Kinki Nippon Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinki_Nippon_Railway) (Kintetsu).

In 1979, Chuo Kotsu, a chartered bus operator in Osaka, imported the [Neoplan Skyliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplan_Skyliner). Skyliner, and the other imported buses: [Van Hool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Hool) Astromega TD824, [Drögmöller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr%C3%B6gm%C3%B6ller) E440 Meteor, and a few [MAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Truck_&_Bus) coaches inspired Japanese bus manufactures, who developed three domestic models in the mid-1980s: "[Nissan Diesel Space Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Diesel_Space_Dream)", "Hino Grand View" and "[Mitsubishi Fuso Aero King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Fuso_Aero_King)". They did not, however, sell very well as the ceiling was only 1.7 metres (5 ft 7 in) high. Nevertheless, Aero King was sold for 22 years, but, being unable to meet exhaust gas emission and safety levels, production stopped in 2005.

In 1982, Toei Bus operated Neoplan Skyliners in Tokyo, between Asakusa and Ueno to 2001. Joban Kotsu operated Skyliners in a trans-Fukushima route: between Iwaki and Aizu-Wakamatsu via Koriyama from 1983 to 1996.

Since the 1990s, [JR Buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Bus) started to use Aero King for an overnight inter-city highway bus service named "Dream-go". The first Aero King in Dream-go, operated to "Fuku Fuku Tokyo" between Tokyo and Shimonoseki, Yamaguchi with Sanden Kotsu, which was replaced with a "super high-decker" coach in middle of the 1990s, "Fuku Fuku Tokyo," and finally stopped in 2006.

Japanese overnight highway buses are mainly equipped with a three-line, two-aisle (1+1+1) seat configuration with reclining seats. When this configuration is used on an ordinary coach, it has 28, 29 or 31 seats. When this configuration is used on a double-decker bus, it has 36 or 40 seats: the vehicle's price and capacity increase while operating cost decreases.

JR Bus group mainly uses Aero King, Skyliner, and a few [Jonckheere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonckheere) Monaco (equipped with Nissan Diesel engine) for inter-city highway bus operations between Kanto (near Tokyo) and Kansai (near Osaka), which is named "Dream-go" (overnight express) and "Hiru-tokkyu" (Daytime Express). The other bus operators, inspired by "Dream-go", increased use of the Aero King for overnight inter-city bus service.

JR Bus Kanto imported four [Neoplan Megaliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplan_Megaliner) N128/4, leasing two to an operating partner (from 2003 to 2006, [Kanto Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanto_Railway), since 2006 Nishinihon JR Bus). The Megaliner is 15.0 metres (49 ft 3 in) long, and has 84 seats (with 2+2 configuration), and is operated on an inter-city highway route between Tokyo and Tsukuba, Ibaraki from 2002 to 2005. The Megaliner has also been converted for a low-price overnight highway bus service between Tokyo and Osaka called "Seishun Mega Dream-go," with special authorisation.

### Southeast Asia

#### Indonesia



[Jakarta](16275.md) double-decker city tour bus passing through landmarks and points of interest in Jakarta, Indonesia

[Indonesia](14579.md) first operated its double-decker bus fleet in 1968, with [Leyland Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Titan_\(front-engined_double-decker\)) double-decker bus first operated in [Jakarta](16275.md). The double-decker bus service linked Salemba in Central Jakarta with [Blok M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_M) area in South Jakarta from 1968 to 1982.[^cite_note-92] Between 1984 and 1996, the Jakarta municipal bus service, Perusahaan Umum Pengangkutan Penumpang Djakarta (Perum PPD) operated a fleet of 180 [Volvo B55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Ailsa_B55) double-decker buses, connecting various corners in the city.

The double-decker bus service ceased to operate in 1996 due to aging fleet, lack of spare parts, and there are no plan to renew the double-decker fleet in Jakarta. By that time, the remnant of double-decker bus body were sold and repurposed as bus-themed clothing store in Blok M and restaurant in Senayan (now [SCBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudirman_Central_Business_District)) area, but now the establishment has been demolished.[^cite_note-93]

By the early 2000s, the PPD has shifted their bus fleet from European built double-decker buses to cheaper second-hand Japanese buses and imported articulated buses from China. By that time, the double-decker seems to be lost in favour of [articulated bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulated_bus), which provides more exit and entry points to accommodate faster embarkment. By 2004 the [TransJakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransJakarta) bus rapid transit began its service in Jakarta, but uses no double-decker bus and chosen conventional and articulated buses instead.

Since February 2014, the Jakarta Government provides free double-decker bus tours that offers sightseeing in Central Jakarta. The buses' route covers tourist attractions, such as [Monas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monas), [Istiqlal Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istiqlal_Mosque,_Jakarta), the [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Cathedral), [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_\(Indonesia\)), [Sarinah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarinah), and [Plaza Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Indonesia), as well as [Grand Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Indonesia) shopping centres.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] As 2016 there are 18 double-decker buses in Jakarta, and the service is expanded to include [Kota Tua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Tua) and [Gelora Bung Karno Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelora_Bung_Karno_Stadium) in [Senayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senayan,_Kebayoran_Baru) area via [Sudirman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalan_Jenderal_Sudirman) avenue.[^cite_note-96]

Other than the capital Jakarta, there are some cities in Indonesia that have operated double-decker buses, mostly as city sightseeing tour service. They are [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung), [Semarang](1134311.md)[^cite_note-97] and [Surakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surakarta).[^cite_note-98] The [Bandros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandros) is a double-decker tourist bus operating in Bandung since 2014.[^cite_note-a-99]

After the completion of [Trans-Java highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Java_Toll_Road) section connecting Jakarta and Surabaya in 2018, some [intercity bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus) services began operating fleet of double decker buses.[^cite_note-100] This choice was due to a larger capacity and the available seat space for a more comfortable journey across Java. Previously, the problem with operating intercity double-decker buses was the steep and narrow roads of mountainous interior on Java island. The Trans-Java highway enables a rather straight and even road terrain for smooth travel between major cities in Java.

#### Malaysia



A [Rapid KL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_KL) [Alexander Dennis Enviro500 MMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500_MMC) during a test run in Cheras, Malaysia

[Malaysia](3607937.md) has historically seen the use of double-decker buses in mass transit to varying degrees, but were significantly limited in use due operational costs and constrained space. Early double-decker municipal buses primarily existed in [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya) within the [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) area of [Selangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selangor) and [George Town, Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Town,_Penang), between the late 1940s and the early 1960s, when double-deckers were eventually withdrawn in favour of cheaper and more agile single-deck buses.

The earliest recorded use of double-deckers by Malayan bus companies was in Selangor in 1948 when the Toong Fong Omnibus Company acquired two [Park Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Royal_Vehicles)-built [Guy Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Arab) IIIs at a cost of [M\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_dollar)40,000 each; the [General Transport Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Jaya) (GTC) followed by acquiring Park Royal-built [AEC Regent IIIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Regent_III). While the buses saw service for over a decade, all of them were taken out of service for a variety of reasons and were never replaced with new double-deckers; the buses were often obstructed by narrow streets, trees, low bridges, and increasing overhead wires, while passengers eventually favoured staying on the lower deck of the bus; the cost of operating the buses was also higher due to a local vehicle tax calculated based on the number of seats of a taxed vehicle. One Toong Fong double-decker was burned in the late-1950s by [communist insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency), while the remaining double-deckers were ultimately disused by the mid-1960s due to age. The successor of the GTC, [Sri Jaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Jaya), experimented with a reintroduction of double-deckers in 1989 by leasing a [Singapore](27318.md)-assembled, 102-seat [Leyland Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Olympian) for use within Kuala Lumpur for 6 months,[^cite_note-101] but found that street conditions were problematic as before and discontinued the use of the bus after the trial.

In George Town, Penang, five retired [AEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Equipment_Company) [C1-class double-decker trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_London) were procured in 1956 by the [George Town Municipal Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_George_Town,_Penang) from [London Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Transport_Executive) as an experiment for the possible use of double-decker buses in George Town. Poor performance results and the advancing ages of the buses, coupled with efforts to replace the entire trolleybus fleet with single-deck diesel-powered buses in the 1960s, led to the withdrawal of the only double-deck buses in early Penangite public transport.

Following increasing public bus ridership, more open roadways and the feasibility of operating double-deck Hop-On Hop-Off tourist buses within Kuala Lumpur, [Prasarana Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prasarana_Malaysia) purchased 40 (revised from an earlier 111) [Alexander Dennis Enviro500 MMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500_MMC) double-decker buses in 2014 to serve high volume [Rapid KL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_KL) [Rapid Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Bus) routes; with a capacity of 108 passengers each, it is double that of a contemporary single-deck bus in the fleet. The first five buses of the batch entered service in September 2015; with the rest of the fleet gradually added into service in the following months. In November 2019, Prasarana ordered a further 90 [Gemilang Coachworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemilang_Coachworks)–bodied [Volvo B8Ls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B8L) as part of Rapid KL's bus fleet replacement programme,[^cite_note-102] with the first batch of buses entering service in June 2020. Feasibility studies were also conducted by Prasarana in 2015 on the reintroduction of double-deckers in Penang through [Rapid Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Penang)'s bus service.[^cite_note-103] By August 2016, a fleet of three Rapid Penang Enviro500 MMCs were officially launched into service, with a total of 33 buses planned.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Although plans were reaffirmed in 2017 to expand the double-decker fleet by 30,[^cite_note-106] delays in procurement led to the initial Enviro500 MMC fleet's quiet withdrawal from service by 2018 and transfer to the Rapid KL fleet due to operational issues; new Rapid Penang Enviro500 MMCs would reenter service in 2022.[^cite_note-107]

Beyond mass transit, long-distance [coach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(bus\)) operators have widely adopted double-decker coaches since the late-2000s in response to growing demand for intercity travel, as expressways and outlying bus stops lack physical obstacles that plagued urban bus services.

#### Singapore

There are currently over 2,000 double-deckers in operation in [Singapore](27318.md).[^cite_note-108]



An ex-[SBS Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SBS_Transit) [Leyland Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Olympian) 3-axle bus as preserved in the UK after its retirement from Singapore in 2013

In October 1953, a single [AEC Regent III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Regent_III) double-decker from the fleet of the [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md)–based General Transport Company was sent to Singapore for demonstration. It was used on service by the [Singapore Traction Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Traction_Company) for two weeks. After that, it was inspected by two Chinese-owned bus companies, and then sent back to Kuala Lumpur. No orders for double deckers were followed all the way until the 1970s.[^cite_note-109]

[Sentosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentosa) was already operating several ex-London double decker buses such as the [AEC Regent III RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Regent_III_RT), [Leyland Titan RTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Titan_\(front-engined_double-decker\)) from 1975 to the early 1980s and subsequently [Leyland Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Olympian) from Bexleybus from 1991 to 2007. They were followed by Volvo B7R Open Top double deckers from 2006 to 2015 until they were modified and converted back to single-deck. The first bus route to Sentosa, service 123 also had used double decker buses from 30 July 2017.[^cite_note-110]

Normal double-decker buses were re-introduced into the [Singapore's public bus system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_transport_in_Singapore) on 13 June 1977 when Singapore Bus Service (SBS, present-day [SBS Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SBS_Transit)), introduced 20 [Leyland Atlantean AN68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Atlantean#AN68/AN69) buses on route 86 which was launched by then-Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Communications [Ong Teng Cheong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Teng_Cheong) that day, with the success, SBS had purchased 500 Leyland Atlantean AN68, 200 [Mercedes-Benz O305](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_O305) and 200 [Leyland Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Olympian) double decker buses until 1986 with the Alexander R-type body, while also trialing several demonstrators such as the [Volvo B55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B55), [Dennis Dominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Dominator), [Scania BR112DH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania_N112#BR112DH) and [Volvo B10MD Citybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B10M#Citybus_\(B10MD/D10M\)) concurrently.[^cite_note-111] The next batch of double-deck buses was introduced from 1984 to 1986 and consists of the [Mercedes-Benz O305](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_O305) and the Leyland Olympian, both of which were bodied by [Walter Alexander Coachbuilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Alexander_Coachbuilders) with an R-type body.

In 1993, the first air-conditioned 12-metre or 39-foot (as opposed to the average 10.5-metre or 34-foot length then) double-decker bus, the [Leyland Olympian 3-axle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Olympian) was launched as the "Superbus". The original 200 "Superbuses" were followed by an additional 471 [Volvo Olympian 3-axle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Olympian) "Superbuses" and 100 non air-conditioned [Volvo Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Olympian) buses from 1994 to 2000. The [Volvo B10TL Super Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Super_Olympian), the first stepless, ultra-low-floor "Superbus", was launched in 1999 while 20 low-floor [Dennis Trident 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Trident_3) buses followed in 2001. The first wheelchair-accessible double-decker buses, the [Volvo B9TL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B9TL), were introduced in 2006; SBS Transit's monopoly on double-decker buses in Singapore ended in 2014 when rival [SMRT Buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMRT_Buses) ordered 201 [Alexander Dennis Enviro500 Mark III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500_MMC) buses and were succeeded by 15 more buses. These Enviro500 buses where similar to those delivered to Hong Kong. It was followed up with 16 [MAN ND323F A95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Lion's_City#Chassis_variants_\(for_external_bodywork\)#MAN_ND323F_/_ND363F_\(A95\)) buses in 2015, and as of 2023, 542 double deckers are in service. Those are being ordered by LTA, and newer units introduced from 2018 onwards are also [Euro 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_emission_standards)–compliant and are equipped with USB charging ports and [Passenger Information Display System (PIDS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_information_system).



The [MAN A95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Lion's_City#Chassis_variants_\(for_external_bodywork\)#MAN_ND323F_/_ND363F_\(A95\)) is one of the newest double-decker buses purchased for the [Bus Contracting Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_Contracting_Model).

Some of the existing double-deckers owned by SMRT Buses and SBS Transit were transferred to new operators [Tower Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_Transit_Singapore) and [Go-Ahead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-Ahead_Singapore) under the [Bus Contracting Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_Contracting_Model). Subsequent orders of Volvo B9TL and MAN A95 buses are being made by the [Land Transport Authority](434512.md), are painted in a lush green livery (rather than the operator-specific liveries of SMRT and SBS Transit), and are used by all operators. Double-decker units are prohibited on certain services due to height restrictions, narrow roads and width restrictions.

In March 2017, the first three-door, two-staircase 12.8 metre-long double-decker bus in Singapore was introduced by Tower Transit with the registration plate SG5999Z for a six-month trial period.[^cite_note-112]

#### Israel



[Egged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egged_\(company\)) Neoplan N122 Skyliner

In Israel, [Egged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egged_\(company\)) operated double-decker buses from 1984 to 2013.[^cite_note-116]

First double-decker (built by [Neoplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplan)) arrived to Israel on 26 August 1984, and during the trial period it performed 434 test drives on four different lines, passed 195583 kilometers and gathered public attention and interest. Double-deckers were found commercially attractive, and in 1988 an agreement between Egged and Neoplan was signed, ordering first 20 buses, with additional 30 buses ordered in the following year. The double-deckers started to arrive in 1989, and they worked on lines [Tel-Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel-Aviv)—[Jerusalem](16043.md), Tel-Aviv—[Haifa](55606.md), Tel-Aviv—[Be'er Sheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'er_Sheba) and on [Eilat](66162.md) lines.

However, both technical issues (in connection between the engine and the [transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_\(mechanics\))) and religious issues ([Haredi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_Jews) had issues with women sitting above and in front of them) led to gradual decline in use of these double-deckers. They were removed from the last major line operating them, the Tel-Aviv—Jerusalem line (480), in the end of the 1990s, worked for some time on local lines in the [Shfela region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shfela), and in the beginning of 2000s were finally removed from service. Some of the buses were sold to an auto dealer from [Jordan](7515964.md), who in turn sold them to [Iraq](7515928.md) and other countries of the Persian gulf.

In August 2005, three double-deckers returned to service on [line 99 "Scenic route"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egged_Bus_99) in Jerusalem. These 3 buses were assembled from parts of 7 double-deckers that were still in storage. The operational cost for double-deckers gradually grew beyond the limit when repairs were still attractive, which led to their final removal from service in 2013.

In June 2021, Egged began operating a double-decker bus on route 190 between [Rishon Lezion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishon_Lezion) and [Tel Aviv](31453.md). The bus is based on a Mann chassis and a chassis from the Spanish UNVI company, can transport about 90 passengers (73 seated) and is also accessible with a ramp for the disabled. Egged purchased 4 buses of this model.[^cite_note-117]

### North America

#### Canada



An [Alexander Dennis Enviro500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500) equipped with bike rack, servicing Victoria, British Columbia, Canada

In 2000, the cities of [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia) and [Kelowna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelowna), British Columbia, placed an order for 10 [Dennis Trident 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Trident_3) buses imported from the United Kingdom, becoming the first cities in North America to use modern double-decker buses in their public transit systems. Several more orders have been placed since then,[^cite_note-118] and as of 2017 [BC Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Transit) operates 69 double-decker buses, including Trident 3s and the newer [Alexander Dennis Enviro500s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500), of which 62 operate on the [Victoria Regional Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Regional_Transit_System) and the remaining 7 with the [Kelowna Regional Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelowna_Regional_Transit_System).[^cite_note-119] In Victoria, the buses are mainly used on routes that go from downtown to the suburbs, and to the [Swartz Bay Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swartz_Bay_Ferry_Terminal) near [Sidney, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney,_British_Columbia). They can also be found on routes that head to the [University of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Victoria) and the Western Communities, and have proven to be very popular amongst both locals and tourists.

[TransLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransLink_\(British_Columbia\)), the transit authority of [Metro Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver_Regional_District), British Columbia, tested 2 Enviro500 buses on lines 301, 311, 351, 354, 555, 601 and 620 between November 2017 and March 2018.[^cite_note-120] It was announced soon after that 32 double-deckers will be purchased, arriving in 2019. On 30 October 2019, TransLink's first double-decker bus made its first ever run along route 620 from the [Bridgeport station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport_station_\(SkyTrain\)) in [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_British_Columbia) to the [Tsawwassen Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsawwassen_Ferry_Terminal).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

From March 2009 to June 2012, three imported [Alexander Dennis Enviro500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500) double-decker buses similar to those in Victoria were used on [OC Transpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OC_Transpo) express routes on the [Transitway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Rapid_Transit) in [Ottawa](22219.md), Ontario. Delivered in November 2008, these buses proved to be efficient in reducing costs, but their height prohibited their use on many routes. Consequently, these three buses were withdrawn and sold to Victoria in late 2012 after a new series of 75 Enviro500 buses with a lower height that met [MTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transportation_of_Ontario) regulations entered service earlier that year. As of 2018 OC Transpo has 133 of these buses.



One of GO Transit's 3.9-metre height (12 ft 9+1⁄2 in) *Super-Lo* double-decker buses

[GO Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit), a regional transit system serving the [Greater Toronto Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Toronto_Area) in [Ontario](22218.md) is the largest user of double-decker buses in Canada, with over 150 such vehicles in service as of 2017.[^cite_note-123] Its fleet comprises Alexander Dennis Enviro500s in a single-door, commuter-type configuration similar to its fleet of [highway coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(bus\)). The first 22 entered service between 2008 and 2009, with its 4.3-metre (14 ft 1+1⁄4 in) roof height limiting usage to the [Highway 407 corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Highway_407). 105 additional buses were delivered between 2012 and 2015 and feature a lower roof height of 4.1 metres (13 ft 5+3⁄8 in). The latest series of 253 buses, which is currently built in a local factory in [Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughan), has a roof height of 3.9 metres (12 ft 9+1⁄2 in) and is expected to replace most of the single-decker coach fleet by 2020, at which point 75% of the active fleet is expected to be composed of double-deckers.[^cite_note-124]

[Strathcona County Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strathcona_County_Transit) of [Strathcona County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strathcona_County), Alberta, started a pilot project in September 2010[^cite_note-125] which explored using different high-capacity bus types to carry more passengers on high-demand commuter routes between Strathcona County and [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton). This involved a one-year lease of an Alexander Dennis Enviro500 from the manufacturer.[^cite_note-126] After completing a year of testing between September 2010 and October 2011,[^cite_note-127] a firm order of 14 Enviro500s was placed in 2013 for their service between [Sherwood Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_Park) and downtown Edmonton, with the first arriving in late August.[^cite_note-128] Five more buses were ordered in 2016, bringing the fleet of double-deckers to 19 as of 2017. Strathcona County Transit currently has 24 Enviro500s in their fleet.[^cite_note-129]

#### United States



A [Van Hool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Hool) US-specification double-decker bus in New York City, US



A [Community Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Transit) double-decker bus in Downtown Seattle, Washington

With the exception of [coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(bus\)), double-decker buses are uncommon in the United States. Several private operators, such as [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(United_States\)), run by [Coach USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_USA), employ double-decker buses on busier intercity routes.

For publicly run transport, [articulated buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulated_bus) are generally preferred. Nonetheless, a handful of municipal operators use double-decker buses, primarily on the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States).

In [Davis, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis,_California), [Unitrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitrans), the student-run bus company of [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis), operates six double-decker buses imported from London. One of these buses has been converted to run on [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas) (CNG). There was also the prototype GX-1 [Scenicruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenicruiser) of [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), which enters from the first floor: the second floor contains the driver's compartment and more seats.

[Citizens Area Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Area_Transit), the transit authority in the [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_metropolitan_area) area, introduced a fleet of double-deckers to serve the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip) route in October 2005. The route is branded as "[The Deuce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Deuce_\(transit_bus_service\))". As of 2009 it serviced eight lines.

In [Snohomish County, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snohomish_County,_Washington), [Community Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Transit) operates 45 [Alexander Dennis Enviro500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500) double-decker buses, which are used on commuter routes between [Snohomish County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snohomish_County,_Washington) and [Seattle](11388236.md). An initial order of 23 buses went into service in 2011,[^cite_note-131] and a second order of 17 went into service in 2015.[^cite_note-132] [Sound Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_Transit), another operator in the Seattle area, bought five double-decker buses through a Community Transit order and began operating their own fleet in 2015.[^cite_note-133] In 2016, a joint procurement between three transit agencies in [Washington state](13015878.md) ordered additional double-decker buses from Alexander Dennis. Community Transit would order 17 buses, with an option for 40, Sound Transit would receive 32 with an option for 43, and [Kitsap Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsap_Transit) would buy 11 of their own.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] As of 1 January 2020, Community Transit owns 52 and Sound Transit owns 37. Community transit purchased 23 in 2010 (10800-10822), 22 in 2015 (15800-15821), and 8 in 2019 (19850-19857). Sound transit purchased 5 in 2015 (91501-91505) and another 32 in 2017 (91701-91732). One of the 10800s has been retired, bringing the total for Community Transit from 53 to 52.



[Spokane Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Transit_Authority) [Alexander Dennis Enviro500 MMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500_MMC) double-decker bus at [Eastern Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington_University)

In [San Luis Obispo, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo,_California), SLO Transit tested a double-decker bus in late 2008 to see if it would alleviate the over-crowdedness of [Route 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_4). The borrowed bus has been returned, and SLO Transit has purchased one double-decker bus of its own using a combination of Federal, State and local funding. The bus went into operation on 8 September 2010.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

In [Los Angeles County, California](79734.md), [Foothill Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothill_Transit) uses double-decker [battery electric buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_electric_bus) as part of its commuter service to the Los Angeles area.[^cite_note-138]

Also in Los Angeles, SCRTD used Neoplan AN 122/3 Skyliners double-decker buses from the late 1970s until 1993.

[New York City](645042.md) phased out double-decker buses in 1960. They briefly returned from 1976 to 1978, although they only ran in [Manhattan](45470.md).[^cite_note-139] In 2008 the [Metropolitan Transportation Authority](384273.md) (MTA) briefly ran a Van Hool double-decker bus on several express routes.[^cite_note-140] However, that year's financial crisis meant the end of the trial period. In 2018, the MTA tested another double decker bus, an [Alexander Dennis Enviro500 SuperLo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500), on the X17J express bus route between Manhattan and [Staten Island](127062.md). However, the MTA has no current plans to purchase double decker buses.

In [San Francisco, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco,_California), the [San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Municipal_Transportation_Agency) operated one [Alexander Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis) double-decker bus as a demonstrator between 12 December 2007 and 8 January 2008.[^cite_note-141] The bus was running on some high capacity routes as trial.[^cite_note-142]

In California, [AC Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Transit) began experimental use of a double-decker bus on the commuter route between [Fremont, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont,_California), and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 2015.[^cite_note-143] On 3 December 2018 the company introduced double-deckers on its FS and J routes from Berkeley to San Francisco, and later added routes L and LA serving Richmond, El Sobrante, San Pablo and Albany.[^cite_note-144]

In [Spokane County, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_County,_Washington), [Spokane Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Transit_Authority) took delivery and has plans to use 7 [Alexander Dennis Enviro500 MMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500_MMC) in 2025. The double-decker buses are planned to be used on routes connecting downtown [Spokane, Washington](138268.md) and [Cheney, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheney,_Washington) which serves [Eastern Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington_University).[^cite_note-145]

[AppalCART](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AppalCART), operated by [Watauga County, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watauga_County,_North_Carolina), serving the [town of Boone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boone,_North_Carolina) and [Appalachian State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_State_University), debuted a double-decker bus on 8 July 2025 to alleviate passenger congestion on its network. It is [North Carolina](21650.md)'s first double-decker bus in transit service.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

### Oceania

#### Australia



[Leyland Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyland_Titan_\(front-engined_double-decker\)) double decker bus in [Sydney](27862.md), Australia



[Bustech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bustech) bodied double decker in [Sydney](27862.md), Australia

Double-decker buses plied route services in [Sydney](27862.md) from the 1920s until 1986. Popular makers included AEC, Albion and Leyland. Disputes over one-man operation of double-deckers[^cite_note-148] led to the phasing-out of this configuration. Double-deckers were thereafter limited to charter and tourist services.

Double-decker buses were reintroduced to the Sydney area in 2012, with [Busways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busways) operating regular services from [Blacktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacktown) to [Rouse Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouse_Hill), in Sydney's north-west. These were expanded in 2013, to traverse routes from [Castle Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Hill,_New_South_Wales) and the [Northern Beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Beaches) to Sydney's CBD.[^cite_note-149] [Forest Coach Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Coach_Lines) and [Hillsbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsbus) also purchased some. The [B-Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-Line_\(Sydney\)) service uses an exclusive fleet of 42 MAN Lions City A95s.Forrest Coachlines also ordered 3 Gemaling-Bodied MAN A95s, and the Bustech CDI. Hillsbus operates 26 Bustech CDIs, and Transport Systems NSW took delivery of 3 Gemaling bodied [Volvo B8l](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B8l?action=edit\&redlink=1), in 2022[^cite_note-TfNSW091115-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]

CDC Melbourne bought 1 [Volgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgren) bodied [Volvo B9TL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B9TL) [Melbourne](17306237.md) by [CDC Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDC_Melbourne) to operate between [Werribee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werribee_railway_station), [Wyndham Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyndham_Vale_railway_station) and [Tarneit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarneit_railway_station) railway stations in 2015. this bus was briefly transferred to Sydney, and painted in Hillsbus colours, but was never driven due to axel weight restrictions, and was returned to Melbourne, and is often used on the [route 190](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_190)[^cite_note-153] Built from 2015 to 2019, [SkyBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyBus_\(airport_bus\)) operates 17 Bustech CDi's between [Melbourne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Airport) and Melbourne's [Southern Cross Coach Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cross_railway_station).[^cite_note-154]

Double deck coaches were built by [Denning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denning_\(bus_manufacturer\)) between 1988 and 1992 with [AAT Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAT_Kings), [Australian Pacific Touring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Pacific_Touring) and [Deluxe Coachlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deluxe_Coachlines) the main customers.[^cite_note-155] The concept was revived in 2011 by [Denning Manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denning_Manufacturing).[^cite_note-156] [Volgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgren) fitted double deck coach bodies to [Volvo B10Ms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_B10M) in the 1980s for [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Australia).[^cite_note-157]

[Transperth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transperth) used to operate an unknown quantity of Denning double decker bus coaches, on a route between [Elizabeth Quay Busport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Quay_Busport) and [Mandurah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandurah), but were phased out with the introduction of the [Mandurah line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandurah_line).

#### New Zealand



An AT Metro double decker [Northern Busway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Busway,_Auckland) bus at [Albany busway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_busway_station)

Until 2013–14, double decker buses were used only by tour operators and for long-distance coach services operated by [Intercity Coachlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_\(New_Zealand\)) and [Manabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manabus). They were not used for public transport on urban routes.

In the 1970s a number of former London double decker buses were imported for museums, such as the [Museum of Transport and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Transport_and_Technology) who used [AEC Regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Regent_III) Low Height (RLH) buses to connect museum sites and for charters. Sydney double decker and more London buses of various models ([AEC Regent III RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Regent_III_RT), [AEC Routemasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Routemaster) etc.) were imported by charter and tourist operators and slowly became more commonplace. Bridge heights and shop verandas restricted the use of double deckers around New Zealand until congestion and high public transport use required some innovative solutions.

A single double-decker bus arrived in [Auckland](18660332.md) in early March 2013 for a trial, with more planned to arrive in 2014 if the trial proved successful. The [Scania K320UD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania_K_UD) bus, operated by [Ritchies Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchies_Transport), began revenue service on 11 March 2013 on the well-patronised Northern Express services on the [Northern Busway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Busway,_Auckland) between [Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_New_Zealand) and [Britomart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britomart_Transport_Centre) in downtown Auckland.[^cite_note-158] In addition, [NZ Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZ_Bus) and Howick & Eastern investigated the use of double-decker buses on the [Dominion Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Road), Mount Eden Road, and [Botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany_Downs,_New_Zealand) to downtown routes.[^cite_note-159] By May 2016, double deckers were running on the busway and on many other Auckland urban routes, operated by several companies, with more to be introduced.[^cite_note-160]

Four 87-seat [DesignLine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Performance_Vehicles) double deckers started on [Waikato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_in_Hamilton_and_Waikato) services in 2018.[^cite_note-161] Since 15 July 2018, double deckers, including some fully electric models, have been operating in [Wellington](33804.md). There are currently 51 diesel double deckers running and 10 electric ones. 34 of the diesel double deckers are euro 6, while the remaining 17 are euro 5.[^cite_note-162]

### South America

#### Argentina

In [Argentina](18951905.md), double-decker buses are the second most widely used means of transport for long-distance trips, surpassed only by aeroplanes. Double-decker buses are also popularly used by foreign tourists in [Buenos Aires](255919.md) where they're used in city-tours.[^cite_note-163]

#### Bolivia

In [Bolivia](3462.md), double-deck buses are a common means of transportation for long-distance trips between large cities such as department capitals. These buses also connect Bolivia with different countries. The double deck buses travel to Argentina, Brazil, Peru and Chile. The buses are equipped with toilets, and several companies offer buses with large seats called Leito (Bus Cama) that can be pulled back and be shaped into a bed.

#### Brazil

Double-decker buses, built mainly by Comil or Marcopolo, are common in long-distance services interstate and international connecting whole South America. It is possible to see VIP double-decker buses also connecting cities in the same state, such as [São Paulo City](390875.md), [São José do Rio Preto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_do_Rio_Preto) and [Ribeirão Preto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribeir%C3%A3o_Preto). Inside these buses there are one TV for each person and boarding service for example. Open double-decker are used for city tours (such as [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) and [Bahia](199070.md)). In São Paulo, there was an experience of use for urban services in the 1980s, built by Thamco, but without success due to issues with the height of the vehicle. These buses are fabricated in Brazil and exported to many countries.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

#### Chile



BYD B12C01 double-decker bus operating in Santiago, Chile

The first double-decker intercity buses arrived from Germany at the end of the 1970s to serve national long-distance routes and international services, mainly to Argentina, for the Varmontt, Flota L, Chile Bus and Tas Choapa lines. Since the end of the 1990s, they have become the standard for interurban transport, due to the advantageous cost-benefit ratio for transport companies.

During the decades of 2000 and 2010, the heyday of this means of transport was experienced, which has meant that practically all Chilean intercity bus companies have these buses, this time not of European origin but with bodies in Brazil, Peru, Argentina and some of Chinese Origin. However, on several occasions, the safety and low comfort provided by these vehicles have been a strong subject of discussion; however, the increase in passenger capacity makes them quite profitable for interurban companies.

On 9 March 2017, the British Embassy in Chile reached an agreement with the Ministry of Public Transport in Santiago to test double-decker buses for public transport. That day the first bus, an [Alexander Dennis Enviro500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dennis_Enviro500) was tested on the streets of Santiago for six weeks.[^cite_note-166]

On 26 February 2019, it was announced that another double-decker bus, a [Wrightbus Streetdeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_StreetDeck) will start to be used in a particular route in Central Santiago. On that day the first bus was tested on Santiago roads.[^cite_note-167]

On 17 August 2023, the first fleet of 10 [BYD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_Company) electric double-decker buses disembarked for public transport that will travel the streets of Santiago de Chile, they are the first double-decker electric buses that currently operate in America and in October 2023 they will be operational to integrate into the [public transport network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Metropolitana_de_Movilidad) of the Chilean capital.[^cite_note-168] [^cite_note-169]

#### Ecuador



A double-decker bus in front of the Presidential Palace in the [Historic Center of Quito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Quito) - World Heritage Site by [UNESCO](21786641.md)

Double-decker buses are used in city-tours in Quito, Cuenca and parts of the coast. They are very popular in the touristic district of the Historic District in Quito. Double-decker buses are common on long distance interurban trips.

#### Peru

Double-decker buses are common on long distance interurban trips to main cities of the country. Open top double-deckers are used in city tours in downtown Lima and in the tourist district of Miraflores. Pio Delgado Arguedas bought 300 Greyhound buses and was the distributor of the buses in South America and Mexico. He also created TEPSA, and was the owner for years until he sold his company.

#### Uruguay

Since the 1990s, Uruguay long-distance bus service operators have operated Brazilian double-deckers. Two AEC Routemasters were imported in the 1970s for urban tourism services – one is now held by the transport heritage group ERHITRAN. In the 2010s [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo) had an urban tourism circuit using open roof Argentinian double-deckers (suspended with the COVID-19 lockdowns).

## Triple-decker buses



[Knight Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bus)

There have been attempts to build a triple-decker bus. However such vehicles are problematic in that the high centre of gravity leads to instability and there is the risk of hitting trees or bridges. In almost all models the third level was a small compartment in the rear part of the bus, such as a triple-decker capable of carrying eighty-eight people from Rome to Tivoli in 1932[^cite_note-170] or the General American Aerocoach 3 Decker Bus of 1952.[^cite_note-171] The only three-decker with a full-length third level ever built is the [Knight Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bus) that [John Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Richardson_\(special_effects_designer\)) created for *[Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Prisoner_of_Azkaban_\(film\))* by combining two [AEC Regent III RTs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEC_Regent_III_RT). Although the AEC was merely a film prop, a British built ECW bodied Bristol VR double-decker bus imitation of the Knight bus was a functioning bus, which even went on tour.[^cite_note-172] Some coachbuses in Pakistan have a partial bottom level that uses a separate cabin from the upper two levels.[^cite_note-173]

## Comparison with articulated buses

Operators worldwide must often decide between articulated and double-decker buses on popular routes. [Articulated buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulated_bus), entirely on one level, offer more room for disabled passengers, luggage and pushchairs; they may also be needed on routes going under low bridges or weak bridges that cannot take high axle loads. Double-decker buses, however, have a smaller road footprint and as such disrupt traffic, or block turning lanes, less than articulated buses.

Double-decker buses may be more popular with passengers because of the better view, and with cyclists, who may be at less risk than they are with the unpredictable swing of an articulated bus's tail. Articulated buses normally offer more standing room while double-decker buses may sometimes (not always) offer more seats. Articulated buses have less [dwell time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_dwell_time) because of the extra doors, and double-decker buses offer fewer chances for [fare dodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fare_evasion) since there are fewer or no unmanned doors.

## Collisions with bridges

There have been a significant number of incidents in which a double-decker bus has collided with a low bridge, often a railway bridge. This is often caused by the driver making a wrong turn, driving a route they are unfamiliar with, or being used to driving single-decker buses and forgetting to allow for their vehicle's extra height when driving a double-decker.

A collision with a railroad bridge by a [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(North_America\)) in September 2010 at [Syracuse, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York), killed four passengers and injured 17.[^cite_note-174]

In recent years in the United Kingdom, six people had minor injuries after their bus hit a railway bridge at [Stockport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockport) in July 2013.[^cite_note-175] An empty bus had its roof removed after hitting a railway bridge in [Birkenhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkenhead) in December 2014.[^cite_note-176]

In March 2015, a bus carrying 76 children hit a bridge at [Staines-upon-Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staines-upon-Thames). Eleven passengers were taken to hospital but none were seriously injured.[^cite_note-177] In the same month, an empty bus had its roof removed after hitting a railway bridge in [Isleworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isleworth) West London.[^cite_note-178] A [Stagecoach Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_Highlands) bus collided with a railway bridge at [Balloch, Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloch,_Highland), Scotland in April 2015. There were no casualties, one top-deck passenger narrowly escaped injury by throwing himself to the floor.[^cite_note-179]

A bus operated by [Bluestar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluestar_\(bus_company\)) had its roof removed after colliding with a railway bridge at [Romsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romsey) in May 2015.[^cite_note-180] An incident in July 2015 in Norwood, London also resulted in the removal of the bus' roof; seven people were injured.[^cite_note-181] Similar incidents occurred in September 2015 in [Rochdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochdale), Greater Manchester (seventeen were injured)[^cite_note-182] and in [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth) in April 2016, with all thirty passengers escaping without injury.[^cite_note-183]

On 11 September 2020, a bus carrying 72 children hit a bridge, taking the complete roof off, in [Winchester, Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester,_Hampshire), on the way to school. Three children were seriously injured and required surgery whilst a further 12 suffered minor injuries.[^cite_note-184] The bus was operated by [Stagecoach South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_South).

In May 2023, ten people were taken to hospital after a bus crashed into a railway bridge on Cook Street, [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow).[^cite_note-185] The same bridge was involved in another crash in December 2024. Again, the roof of a bus was torn off, this time after the driver reportedly took a wrong turn into the one-way street. Numerous injuries were caused and eight people went to hospital.[^cite_note-186] Days later, in a separate incident, another double decker bus hit another railway bridge in [Scotland](26994.md), in [Kilmarnock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilmarnock).[^cite_note-187]

In July 2025, twenty people were injured after a bus crashed struck the [Bridgewater Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgewater_Canal) aqueduct bridge on Barton Lane in [Eccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccles,_Greater_Manchester) and having the roof torn off.[^cite_note-188] Another collision occurred at the railway bridge on Ten Acres Lane, [Newton Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_Heath), being the second crash within 24 hours in [Greater Manchester](79819.md).[^cite_note-189]
