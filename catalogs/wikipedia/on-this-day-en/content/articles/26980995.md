# List of power stations in Iran

Iran power stations

In 2012, [Iran](14653.md) had roughly 400 power plant units. By the end of 2013, it had a total installed electricity generation capacity of 70,000 MW, up from 90 MW in 1948, and 7024 MW in 1978.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] There are plans to add more than 5,000 MW of generation capacity annually to the power grid, which would almost double the total power generation capacity to 122,000 MW by 2022.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The [government of Iran](50913538.md) planned to [privatize 20 power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatization_in_Iran#Utilities) by September 2010.[^cite_note-6] Iran's [peak demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_demand) for electricity was 45,693 MW during the summer of 2013.[^cite_note-7]

It was predicted in 2014 that Iran would account for 17.08% of [MENA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MENA) power generation. Natural gas was the major fuel used to generate electricity in Iran in 2009, accounting for an estimated 56.8% of [primary energy demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_resources_and_consumption) (PED), followed by oil at 40.8% and [hydroelectricity](381399.md) at 1.4%. As of 2010, the average efficiency of power plants in Iran was 38%. The figure should reach 45% within five years and 50% under [Vision 2025](64704.md#National_planning).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Electricity generation in 2008 accounted for 203.8 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour), or roughly 1% of world's total production, an increase of 5.9% from the year before. In 2008, the total [electricity generated](9540.md) was 190.2 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour), of which 93.3% was generated by [power plants](212141.md) affiliated with the [Ministry of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Energy_\(Iran\)) and 13.6 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour) (6.7%) by other institutions, which were mostly from the private sector.[^cite_note-en.tpo.ir-10][^cite_note-11] The largest share of electricity (91.1 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour)) was generated by [steam power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_power_plant), while [diesel power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_generator) accounted for the smallest share of generation (0.2 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour)). In 2008, the highest growth in generation of electricity belonged to [gas](22131.md) and [combined-cycle power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_cycle_power_plant), with a 9.3% growth rate while, the amount of electricity generated by [hydroelectric power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power_plant) declined by 1.7%. As of 2010, the consumer price of electricity in Iran was 1.6 US cents per kilowatt-hour, while the real production cost was about 8.0 US cents.[^cite_note-en.tpo.ir-10][^cite_note-payvand.com-12] (See also: [Cost of electricity by source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_electricity_by_source))

In 2010, 900,000 jobs were directly or indirectly related to the Iranian power industry. Currently, Iran's spares power capacity stands at 3%, but this amount is much lower than the ideal 25% of peak power used.[^cite_note-payvand.com-12] It has been estimated that 23.5% of the electricity generation is wasted in the transmission network.[^cite_note-payvand.com-12] Iran's [power grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_grid) has been connected to seven neighboring countries, [Afghanistan](737.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_Turkey), [Armenia](10918072.md), [Azerbaijan](746.md), and [Turkmenistan](198149.md), and annually exports 5.5 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour) of electricity.

[Shahid Salimi Combined Cycle Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Salimi_Combined_Cycle_Power_Plant) in [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran)

The electric power industry in Iran has become self-sufficient in producing the required equipment to build power plants.[^cite_note-13] While most of the electricity generators are run by the government, the equipment producers and contractors are generally from the private sector.[^cite_note-payvand.com-12] Iran is among the top ten manufacturers of [gas turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine), with a capacity of up to 160 megawatts.[^cite_note-14]

Iranian engineers at JEMCO (a subsidiary of [IDRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Development_and_Renovation_Organization_of_Iran)) have developed and produced one- and four-megawatt [generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_generator). Iran can manufacture materials for over 80% of hydraulic turbines and over 90% of gas turbines. In the near future, Iran could become a major player in building [power plants](212141.md) with advanced technology (2009).[^cite_note-15] Iran planned to build its first indigenous gas turbines by 2015.

Iran has achieved the technical expertise to set up hydroelectric, gas, and combined-cycle power plants.[^cite_note-16] Iran is one of the four countries in the world which can manufacture advanced [V94.2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V94.2?action=edit\&redlink=1) [gas turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine).[^cite_note-17] The [Industrial Development and Renovation Organization of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Development_and_Renovation_Organization_of_Iran) (IDRO) is currently building the country's first 4-megawatt (MW) combined heat and power (CHP) turbo-generator in cooperation with the private sector.

### International projects

Iran is self-sufficient in power plant construction and has built power plants in neighboring states .[^cite_note-iran-daily.com-21][^cite_note-nitc.co.ir-22] As of 2010, Iranian energy- and resource-development firms are involved in 50 projects worth over US\$2.2 billion in more than 20 countries across the world.[^cite_note-23] As at 2011, [MAPNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAPNA) was building power plants in Syria, Oman, and Iraq, and negotiations were underway to build two power plants in Lebanon.



Pushkin Pass, [Armenia](10918072.md)

One of Iran's most important international projects will see the construction of a \$200-million hydroelectric dam in [Nicaragua](21362.md), starting in 2011. Iran is currently engaged in dam construction in [Tajikistan](30108.md), [Armenia](10918072.md), and [Azerbaijan](746.md), and consultations are underway with a number of other countries. [Kenya](188171.md), [Sri Lanka](26750.md), [Bolivia](3462.md), and [Mali](19127.md) are the potential target markets being considered for exporting the country's technical and engineering services.[^cite_note-24] In 2010, Iran won a contract to build a dam in [Afghanistan](737.md) and the third contract to build a power plant station in [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-25]

In December 2005, a [wind farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm) was put in operation at [Pushkin Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushkin_Pass?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Armenia](10918072.md). The total installed capacity of the farm is 2.64 MW, from four 660 kW wind turbines. The wind farm was built with the support of a US\$3.1M grant from the Iranian government.[^cite_note-26] The Armenian and Iranian energy sectors are currently jointly constructing the [Iran-Armenia Wind Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-Armenia_Wind_Farm), which is set to become the country's largest [wind farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm), having an installed electric capacity of 90 [MW](21347693.md).[^cite_note-27] As at 2012, Iran is building seven large power plants in Syria, Oman, Iraq, and Tajikistan.[^cite_note-28]

### Foreign direct investment

See also: [Foreign Direct Investment in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment_in_Iran)

According to the [Ministry of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Energy_\(Iran\)), [Germany](11867.md) has invested \$445 million in construction of the Pareh-Sar combined-cycle power plant in northern Iran, while the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) has invested \$720 million in construction of a gas power plant and a combined-cycle power plant in [Isfahan](223149.md) and [Shiraz](39622.md). In 2015, Iran and Russia signed an agreement regarding the construction of eight [thermal power plants](2549543.md) in Iran, with a total installed capacity of 2,800 MW. The investment per MW will be \$3.57 million (\$10 billion in total).[^cite_note-29]

## Privatization

Main article: [Privatization in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatization_in_Iran)



It is estimated that 18.5% of electricity generated in Iran is wasted before it reaches consumers due to technical problems. [Electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) wastage hit \$1.1 billion in 2006.

[^cite_note-30]

&#x20;Solutions include production and use of more efficient light bulbs.

[Mapna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapna) Company, Sahand, Bistoun, Shazand, Shahid Montazeri, Tous, Shahid Rajaei, and Neishabour power stations are among the profit-making plants. Work on privatizing them was scheduled to be finalized by late March 2007.[^cite_note-31] Jahrom, Khalij-e Fars (Persian Gulf), and Sahand power plants were to be ceded to the private sector in 2009.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-32][^cite_note-33]

All domestic power plants will be privatized gradually, except those the government feels it should run to ensure security of the national electricity grid. Power plants of Damavand, Mashhad, Shirvan, Kerman, Khalij-e Fars, Abadan, Bisotoon, Sanandaj, Manjil and Binalood, which have been turned into public limited firms, are ready for privatization.[^cite_note-34] As of 2010, 20 power plants were ready for [privatization in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatization_in_Iran).[^cite_note-35]

Upon ceding the 20 power plants to [IPO](63879.md), some 40% of the capacity of power plants nationwide will be assigned to the private and cooperative sectors.[^cite_note-36] As of 2011, about 45 power plants across the country were to be handed over to the private sector.[^cite_note-37] In 2012, it was announced that Iran's government, which had already turned over 17 of its 45 power plants to the private sector since 2008, would transfer 28 more plants with an estimated value of \$11.4 billion (USD), by March 2013.[^cite_note-38]

## Energy/electricity bourse

See also: [Iranian targeted subsidy plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_targeted_subsidy_plan), [Tehran Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Stock_Exchange), and [Energy in Iran](10580575.md)

The new energy/electricity bourse will be inaugurated in 2012.[^cite_note-39] This will bring about more competition and transparency in Iran's electricity market.[^cite_note-40] Experts believe that, following the launch of the [subsidies reform plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_targeted_subsidy_plan), the electricity industry will undergo significant changes and will become more appealing to private investors.[^cite_note-41]

As at 2012, Iran had over 400 power-plant units and 38 electricity-distribution companies, which buy the electricity from producers. Iran has over 100 companies that consume more than 20 MW of electricity. The average price of each kilowatt-hour of electricity is 450 [rials](708656.md) (around 5 cents) during the first phase of the [Subsidy Reform Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_targeted_subsidy_plan). The average final price of each kilowatt-hour of electricity will be 1000 rials (around 10 cents) in 2015.[^cite_note-iran-daily1-42] According to the [government of Iran](50913538.md), power stations have to pay the export price of gas if they want to export electricity and must improve efficiency.[^cite_note-43]

Iran's electricity export and related technical and engineering services exports was valued at \$4 billion in 2011.[^cite_note-44] In 2010, the total of Iran's electricity exports to [Afghanistan](737.md), [Iraq](7515928.md) (650 MW), [Azerbaijan](746.md), [Armenia](10918072.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), and [Turkey](11125639.md) reached 878 megawatts, and the total of imports from Armenia (237 MW) and [Turkmenistan](198149.md) was recorded at 463 megawatts.[^cite_note-iran-daily1-42] Jordan, India, Oman, Qatar, Russia, Syria, and the UAE are the new countries that have expressed interest in buying electricity from Iran.[^cite_note-iran-daily1-42]

## Decentralized power generation

In addition to the above power plants, there was 1800 MW cumulative installed capacity in 2011, which belonged to small -scale [decentralized power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_generation), some of which were not connected to the national grid, and many being privately built and run. This capacity is planned for increase to more than 10,000 MW with emphasis on [renewable energy](25784.md) and [trigeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigeneration). Similarly, there was 418 MW of capacity belonging to [diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_generator)-based plants supplying hard-to-reach areas.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]
