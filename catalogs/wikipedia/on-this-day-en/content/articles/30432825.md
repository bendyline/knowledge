A **ford** is a shallow section of a [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River) or [stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream) where it may be crossed by wading, on horseback, or inside a vehicle.[^cite_note-1] A ford may occur naturally or be constructed. Fords may be impassable during high water. A [low-water crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-water_crossing) is a low bridge that allows crossing over a river or stream when water is low but may be treated as a ford when the river is high and water covers the crossing.

The word *ford* is both a [noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun) (*describing the water crossing itself*) and a [verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verb) (*describing the act of crossing a ford*).

## Description

A ford is a much cheaper form of river crossing than a [bridge](3397.md), and it can transport much more weight than a bridge, but it may become impassable after heavy rain or during flood conditions. A ford is therefore normally only suitable for very minor roads (and for paths intended for walkers and horse riders etc.). Most modern fords are usually shallow enough to be crossed by [cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) and other wheeled or tracked vehicles (a process known as "fording"). Fords may be accompanied by [stepping stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepping_stones) for pedestrians.

The [United Kingdom](31717.md) has more than 2,000 fords, and most of them do not have any way of stopping vehicles from crossing when the water makes them impassable.[^cite_note-TheAA-2] According to [The AA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AA_plc), many flood-related vehicle breakdowns within the UK are at fords.[^cite_note-TheAA-2]

In [New Zealand](4913064.md), fords are a normal part of roads, including, until 2010, along [State Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_State_Highway_1) on the [South Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Island)'s east coast.[^cite_note-3] In dry weather, drivers become aware of a ford by crunching across outwash detritus on the roadway. A [Bailey bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_bridge) may be built off the mainline of the road to carry emergency traffic during high water.

At places where the water is shallow enough, but the material on the riverbed will not support heavy vehicles, fords are sometimes improved by building a submerged [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete) floor. In such cases, a [curb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curb) (kerb) is often placed on the downstream side to prevent vehicles from slipping off, as the growth of [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae) will often make the slab very slippery. Fords may be also equipped with a post indicating the water depth, so that users may know if the water is too deep to attempt to cross. Some have an adjacent footbridge so that pedestrians may cross dryshod.

Fords were sometimes the only way to cross bodies of water, such as at the Milkhouse at [Rock Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Creek_\(Potomac_River\)) in Washington DC. However, the regular use of this ford has been superseded by bridges.[^cite_note-4] The crossing remained open for "adventurous" drivers until 1996, when the [National Park Service](161535.md) closed the ford to cars.[^cite_note-5]

### Watersplash



One of the two watersplashes in [Clatterford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clatterford,_Isle_of_Wight), [Isle of Wight](15102.md). The one shown is over [Lukely Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukely_Brook?action=edit\&redlink=1), a tributary of the [River Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Medina).

A road running below the water level of a stream or river is often known as a "watersplash". It is a common name for a ford or stretch of wet road in some areas, and is sometimes also used to describe tidal crossings. They have become a common feature in [rallying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallying) courses. Some enthusiasts seek out and drive through these water features, recording details (such as wave created, position, and access) on dedicated websites.[^cite_note-6]

There are many old fords known as watersplashes in the [United Kingdom](31717.md). Examples are at [Brockenhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brockenhurst) in [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire), [Wookey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wookey) in [Somerset](51763.md), and [Swinbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swinbrook) in [Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfordshire). Some of these roads are being replaced by bridges, as they are a more reliable form of crossing in adverse weather conditions.

Some very spectacular watersplashes can be found in diverse locations. [Australia](4689264.md) has the [Gulf Savannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Savannah), and others may be found in [Canada](5042916.md), [Italy](14532.md), [South Africa](17416221.md), and [Finland](10577.md). They are also found on some [Tennessee](30395.md) backroads, where they are referred to as "underwater bridges".

In [Israel](9282173.md) and part of the British areas under the [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine) a low water crossing or watersplash had been known as the "Irish bridge"[^cite_note-7] in reference to the [Anglo–Irish war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_War_of_Independence).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Placenames

The names of many towns and villages are derived from the word 'ford'. Because fords were often on major [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route), towns near them could grow quickly. Examples include [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford) (a ford where [oxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ox) crossed the river: see the [Coat of arms of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Oxford)); [Hertford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford), the county town of [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire) (the ford where [harts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_\(deer\)) cross or "deer crossing"); [Brantford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brantford), (the ford where [Joseph Brant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Brant) Forded The [Grand River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_River_\(Ontario\))); [Ammanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammanford) (a ford on the [River Amman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Amman)); [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford), the county town of [Staffordshire](58733.md) ('ford by a [staithe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staithe)', or 'ford by the landing place'), [Staffort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffort) crossing the river [Pfinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfinz); and [Stratford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford,_London) (a ford on a [Roman street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_road)). Similarly, the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) word *Furt* (as in [Frankfurt](10992.md), the ford of the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks); [Ochsenfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochsenfurt), synonymous to Oxford; [Schweinfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schweinfurt), a ford where [swine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swine) crossed the river; and [Klagenfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klagenfurt), literally "ford of complaints") and the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) *voorde* (as in [Vilvoorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilvoorde), [Coevorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coevorden), [Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zandvoort), or [Amersfoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amersfoort)) are [cognates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate) with the same meaning, all deriving from [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language) *\*pértus* 'crossing'. This is the source of [Brythonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Brittonic) and [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaulish_language) *ritus* (modern [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) *rhyd*; the Welsh name of Oxford is *Rhydychen* "ford of oxen"), which underlies such names as [Chambord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambord,_Loir-et-Cher) (from Gaulish *\*Camboritum* "ford at the bend") and [Niort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niort) (*Novioritum* "new ford").

Towns such as [Maastricht](20125.md), [Dordrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dordrecht), and [Utrecht](50333.md) also formed at fords. The endings *tricht*, *drecht*, and *trecht* are derived from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word *traiectum*, meaning "crossing". Thus the name Utrecht, originally the Roman fort of [Traiectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traiectum_\(Utrecht\)), is derived from "Uut Trecht", meaning "downstream crossing". The [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans) form was taken into [South African English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English) as *drift* and led to place names like [Rorke's Drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorke's_Drift) and [Velddrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velddrif). Similarly, in [Slavic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages), the word *[brod](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/brod)* comes from the linguistic root that means "river-crossing" or "place where a river can be crossed". Although today *brod* in the [Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian) means 'ship', [Slavonski Brod](149285.md) in Croatia, as well as [Makedonski Brod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makedonski_Brod) in [North Macedonia](23564616.md) and other place names containing *Brod* in Slavic countries, where *brod* is still the word for 'ford', are named after fords.

In areas with Old Norse influences in placenames, *vað* refers to a ford. Examples from northern England include [Wath-in-Nidderdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wath-in-Nidderdale) and [Yanwath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanwath).[^cite_note-10] Elsewhere, "wade" – from *waed*, the Saxon word for ford – appears in names such as [Biggleswade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biggleswade)[^cite_note-11] and [Wadebridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadebridge).[^cite_note-12] The word appears as "worth" in [Rainworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainworth).[^cite_note-13]

## Famous battles



The Battle of Rorke's Drift in the Anglo-Zulu war

In historic times, positioning an army in large units close to a river was thought best for direct defense as well as to attack the enemy at any crossing point.[^cite_note-14] Therefore, a ford was often a strategic military point with many famous battles fought at or near fords:

- [Battle of Xiaoyao Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Xiaoyao_Ford), 215–217, during the wars at the [end of the Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_Han_dynasty) in [China](5405.md)

- [Battle of Fulford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fulford), 1066, on the [Ouse River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ouse,_Yorkshire) during [Harald Hardrada's invasion of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Hardrada#Invasion_of_England)

- [Battle of Jacob's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jacob's_Ford), 1179, on the [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River) during the period between the [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Crusade) and [Third Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade)

- [Battle of Imjin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Imjin_River_\(1592\)), 1592, on the [Imjin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imjin_River) during the [Japanese invasion of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasions_of_Korea_\(1592%E2%80%931598\))

- [Battle of the Yellow Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Yellow_Ford), 1598, on the [Blackwater River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Blackwater_\(Northern_Ireland\)) during the [Nine Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Years'_War_\(Ireland\)) in [Ireland](147575.md)

- [Battle of Newburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Newburn) Ford, 1640, on the [Tyne River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tyne) during the [Second Bishops' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Bishops%E2%80%99_War) in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland)

- [Battle of the Boyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Boyne), 1690, on the [Boyne River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Boyne) during the [Williamite-Jacobite War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamite_War_in_Ireland) in Ireland

- [Battle of Matson's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Matson's_Ford), 1777, on the [Schuylkill River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuylkill_River) during the [Philadelphia campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_campaign) of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in [Pennsylvania](23332.md)

- [Battle of Brandywine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Brandywine), 1777, on [Brandywine Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandywine_Creek_\(Christina_River_tributary\)) during the Philadelphia campaign of the American Revolutionary War in Pennsylvania

- [Battle of Minisink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Minisink), 1779, on the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) during the [Northern theater of the American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_theater_of_the_American_Revolutionary_War_after_Saratoga) in [New York](8210131.md)

- [Battle of Cowan's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cowan's_Ford), 1781, on the [Catawba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_River) during the [Southern theater of the American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_theater_of_the_American_Revolutionary_War) in [North Carolina](21650.md)

- [Battle of Assaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Assaye), 1803, on the Kalina River during the [Second Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Maratha_War) in [India](14533.md)

- [Battle of Blackburn's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blackburn's_Ford), 1861, on [Bull Run Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Run_\(Occoquan_River_tributary\)) during the [Manassas campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manassas_campaign) of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) in Virginia

- [Battle of Kelly's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kelly's_Ford), 1863, on the [Rappahannock River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappahannock_River) during the [Eastern Theater of the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) in Virginia

- [Battle of Buffington Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buffington_Island), 1863, on the Ohio River during [Morgan's Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan's_Raid) in the American Civil War in Ohio and West Virginia

- [Battle of Chancellorsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chancellorsville), 1863, on the [Rappahannock River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappahannock_River) during the Eastern Theater of the American Civil War in Virginia

- [Battle of Byram's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Byram's_Ford), 1864, on the [Blue River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_River_\(Missouri_River_tributary\)) during [Price's Missouri Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price's_Missouri_Expedition) in the American Civil War in [Missouri](19571.md)

- [Battle of Morton's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Morton's_Ford), 1864, on the [Rapidan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapidan_River) during the American Civil War in Virginia

- [Battle of Rorke's Drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rorke's_Drift), 1879, on the [Buffalo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_River_\(Eastern_Cape\)) during the [Anglo-Zulu War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Zulu_War) in [South Africa](17416221.md)

- [Battle of Cut Knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cut_Knife), 1885, on the [North Saskatchewan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Saskatchewan_River) during the [North-West Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Rebellion) in [Canada](5042916.md)

- [Battle of Davydiv Brid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Davydiv_Brid), 2022, on the [Inhulets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhulets) river during the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md)
