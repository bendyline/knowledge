The **CAC/PAC JF-17 Thunder**,[^cite_note-2] also known as **FC-1 *Xiaolong***,[^cite_note-3] is a Sino-Pakistani single-engine [lightweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_combat_aircraft) [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_aircraft) [multirole combat aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft) developed jointly by the [Pakistan Aeronautical Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Aeronautical_Complex) (PAC) and the [Chengdu Aircraft Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Aircraft_Corporation) (CAC) of [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-auto1-4] It is a [fourth-generation fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-generation_fighter), designed and developed as a replacement for the [third-generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_fighter_generations#Third) [A-5C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang_Q-5#Export_variants), [F-7P/PG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7), [Mirage III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_III), and [Mirage 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_5) combat aircraft in the [Pakistan Air Force (PAF)](377433.md).[^cite_note-\:9-5][^cite_note-6] The JF-17 can be used for multiple roles, including [interception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interceptor_aircraft), [ground attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-attack_aircraft), anti-ship, and [aerial reconnaissance](20338498.md). The Pakistani designation "JF-17" stands for "Joint Fighter-17", with the "Joint Fighter" denoting the joint Pakistani-Chinese development of the aircraft and the "-17" denoting that, in the PAF's vision, it is the successor to the [F-16](11642.md). The Chinese designation "FC-1" stands for "Fighter China-1".

The JF-17 can deploy diverse [ordnance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_ordnance), including [air-to-air](320568.md), [air-to-surface](1544116.md), and [anti-ship missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ship_missiles); guided and unguided bombs; and a 23-mm [GSh-23-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gryazev-Shipunov_GSh-23) twin-barrel [autocannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocannon). Powered by the Russian [Klimov RD-93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_RD-93) (JF-17 Blocks 1 and 2) or [Klimov RD-93MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_RD-33#RD-93) (JF-17 Block 3) afterburning [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) engine, it has a top speed of [Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number) 1.6.[^cite_note-diplomat_2019-03-7] The JF-17 is the backbone and workhorse of the PAF, complementing the [F-16 Fighting Falcon](11642.md) at approximately half the cost,[^cite_note-8] with the Block II variant costing \$25 million.[^cite_note-diplomat_2019-03-7] The JF-17 was inducted in the PAF in 2007.



JF-17 Block III at [RIAT 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_International_Air_Tattoo)

Fifty-eight per cent of the JF-17 airframe, including its front fuselage, wings, and vertical stabiliser, is produced in Pakistan, whereas forty-two per cent is produced in China, with the final assembly and serial production taking place in Pakistan.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-auto-10][^cite_note-ReferenceA-11] In 2015, Pakistan produced 16 JF-17s.[^cite_note-The_Express_Tribune-12] As of 2016, PAC has the capacity to produce 20 JF-17s annually. By April 2017, PAC had manufactured 70 Block 1 aircraft[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-quwa.org-14] and 33 Block 2 aircraft for the PAF.[^cite_note-15] By 2016, PAF JF-17s had accumulated over 19,000 hours of operational flight.[^cite_note-16] In 2017, PAC/CAC began developing a dual-seat variant known as the JF-17B for enhanced operational capability, conversion training, and lead-in fighter training.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] The JF-17B Block 2 variant went into serial production at PAC in 2018 and 26 aircraft were delivered to the PAF by December 2020.[^cite_note-19] In December 2020, PAC began serial production of a more advanced Block 3 version of the aircraft with an [active electronically scanned array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_electronically_scanned_array) (AESA) radar, a more powerful Russian Klimov RD-93MA engine along with compatibility with the Chinese [Guizhou WS-13 Taishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou_WS-13) engine, a larger and more advanced wide-angle [Head-Up Display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-up_display) (HUD), [electronic countermeasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_countermeasure), an additional hardpoint, and enhanced weapons capability.[^cite_note-auto5-20]



[Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) JF-17 armed with [PL-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-5) infrared homing air-to-air missile

The PAF JF-17s have seen military action, both air-to-air and air-to-ground, including bombing terrorist positions in [North Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Waziristan) near the Pakistan-[Afghanistan](737.md) border during anti-terror operations in 2014 and 2017, using both guided and unguided munitions,[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-ReferenceA-11] shooting down an intruding Iranian military drone near the Pakistan-[Iran](14653.md) border in [Balochistan](8431743.md) in 2017,[^cite_note-22] in [Operation Swift Retort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Jammu_and_Kashmir_airstrikes) during the [2019 Jammu and Kashmir airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Jammu_and_Kashmir_airstrikes) and aerial skirmish between [India](14533.md) and Pakistan,[^cite_note-auto8-23] and during [Operation Marg Bar Sarmachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Marg_Bar_Sarmachar) in 2024 in which Pakistan launched a series of air and artillery strikes inside Iran's [Sistan and Baluchestan province](448105.md) targeting [Baloch separatist](4591548.md) groups. In [March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan-Pakistan_skirmishes_\(2024-present\)) and [December 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pakistani_airstrikes_in_Afghanistan), PAF JF-17s were used in cross-border airstrikes against [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md) hideouts inside Afghanistan.[^cite_note-24] [Nigerian Air Force](538869.md) (NAF) JF-17s have seen military action in anti-terrorism and anti-insurgency operations in Nigeria.[^cite_note-auto2-25] [Myanmar Air Force](9484818.md) has also frequently deployed its JF-17 fleet against various insurgent groups.[^cite_note-26] During the [May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_standoff), the PAF deployed JF-17s in combat in both the air-to-air and air-to-ground roles.[^cite_note-auto14-27][^cite_note-auto16-28][^cite_note-auto12-29]

## Development

### Background

The JF-17 was designed and developed primarily to meet the PAF requirement for an affordable,[^cite_note-30] unsanctionable, fourth-generation, lightweight, multi-role combat aircraft as a replacement for its large fleet of third-generation [Nanchang A-5C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang_Q-5) [bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber), [Chengdu F-7P/PG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) [interceptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interceptor_aircraft), [Mirage III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_III) multi-role combat aircraft, and [Mirage 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_5) [strike aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft), with a cost of [US\$](18717338.md)500 million, divided equally between Pakistan and China.[^cite_note-fighter-planes.com-31] The aircraft was also intended to have export potential as a cost-effective and competitive alternative to more expensive Western fighters.[^cite_note-FC-1/JF-17,_sinodefence.com-32][^cite_note-Thunder_Storm,_Usman_Ansari-33][^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] The development of this aircraft was headed by [Yang Wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Wei_\(aircraft_designer\)), considered to be China's "ace designer",[^cite_note-35] who also designed the [Chengdu J-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-20).

By 1989, because of economic sanctions by the US, Pakistan had abandoned [Project Sabre II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Sabre_II), a design study involving US aircraft manufacturer [Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman) and China, and had decided to redesign and upgrade the Chengdu F-7.[^cite_note-Pakistan_considers_new_fighter_plan-36] In 1988, China and Grumman carried out a nine-month preliminary design study of the Super 7, an upgrade of the Chengdu F-7.[^cite_note-Grumman_to_upgrade_Chinese_F-7Ms-37] Grumman left the project when sanctions were placed on China following the political fallout from the 1989 [Tiananmen Square protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989). After Grumman left the Chengdu Super 7 project, the project was abandoned and, in its place, the Fighter China-1 (FC-1) project was launched in 1991.[^cite_note-Janes-38] In 1995, Pakistan and China signed a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) (MoU) for joint design and development of a new fighter, and over the next few years worked out the project details.[^cite_note-Page_33,_AFM_magazine,_issue_July_2004-39] In June 1995, [Mikoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan) had joined the project to provide "design support", this also involved the secondment of several engineers by CAC.[^cite_note-21_June_1995_flightglobal.com-40]

A PAF JF-17 Block 1 in [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir), Turkey for the 2011 Izmir Air Show

In October 1995, Pakistan was reportedly to select a Western company by the end of the year to provide and integrate the FC-1's avionics, which was expected to go into production by 1999. The avionics were said to include radar, [Inertial navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system), [Head-up display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-up_display), and [Multi-function displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-function_display). Competing bids came from [Thomson-CSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson-CSF) with a variant of the [Radar Doppler Multitarget (RDY)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_Doppler_Multitarget), [SAGEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAGEM) with a similar avionics package to those used in the [ROSE upgrade project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_ROSE), and [Marconi Electronic Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marconi_Electronic_Systems) with its Blue Hawk radar. FIAR's (now [SELEX Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SELEX_Galileo)) Grifo S7 radar was expected to be selected due to the company's ties with the PAF.[^cite_note-18_October_1995_flightglobal.com-41] In February 1998, Pakistan and China signed a [letter of intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_intent) covering airframe development. Russia's [Klimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov) offered a variant of the RD-33 turbofan engine to power the fighter.[^cite_note-04/03/98_flightglobal.com-42] In April 1999, South Africa's [Denel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denel) offered to arm the Super 7 with the T-darter [beyond-visual-range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond-visual-range_missile) (BVR) air-to-air missile (AAM), rather than the previously reported [R-Darter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-Darter_\(missile\)).[^cite_note-43] Previously in 1987, [Pratt & Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney) offered the Super-7 project three engine options; [PW1212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_J52), [F404](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_F404), and [PW1216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_J52), with local manufacturing in either China or Pakistan. Rolls-Royce offered its RB199-127/128 turbofan engine; this plan was scrapped in 1989.[^cite_note-44]

In June 1999, the contract to jointly develop and produce the Chengdu FC-1/Super 7 was signed. After GEC-Marconi had abandoned the bidding to supply an integrated avionics suite, FIAR and Thomson-CSF proposed a number of avionics suites based on the Grifo S7 and RC400 radars respectively, despite previously hoping to use the PAF's Super 7 to launch its new Blue Hawk radar.[^cite_note-14_July_1999_flightglobal.com-45][^cite_note-23_June_1999_flightglobal.com-46] Because of sanctions placed on Pakistan after the country's 1998 nuclear weapons tests, design work progressed very slowly over the next 18 months, preventing delivery of the Western avionics to the PAF. In early 2001, the PAF decided to decouple the [airframe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airframe) from the avionics, enabling design work on the aircraft to continue. As the airframe was developed, any new avionics requirements by the PAF could be more easily integrated into the airframe.[^cite_note-Page_33,_AFM_magazine,_issue_July_2004-39]

Prototype production began in September 2002; a full-size mock-up of the FC-1/Super 7 was displayed at Airshow China in November 2002.[^cite_note-12/11/02_flightglobal.com-47] The first batch of [Klimov RD-93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_RD-93) turbofan engines that would power the prototypes was also delivered in 2002.[^cite_note-Janes-38] According to a [China National Aero-Technology Import & Export Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Aero-Technology_Import_&_Export_Corporation) (CATIC) official, the JF-17's low cost is due to some of the on-board systems having been adapted from those of the [Chengdu J-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-10). The official said, "This transfer of technology—transposing the aircraft systems from the J-10 to the JF-17—is what makes the JF-17 so cost-effective".[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-JF-17_and_J-10:_21st_Century_Jets_at_Yesterday's_Price-49] The use of [computer-aided design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_design) software shortened the design phase of the JF-17.[^cite_note-50]

### Flight testing and redesigning

The first prototype, PT-01, was rolled out on 31 May 2003[^cite_note-FC-1/JF-17,_sinodefence.com-32][^cite_note-29_July_2003_flightglobal.com-51] and transferred to the Chengdu Flight Test Centre to be prepared for its maiden flight.[^cite_note-Page_33,_AFM_magazine,_issue_July_2004-39] This was initially planned to take place in June, but was delayed due to concerns about the [SARS outbreak](196741.md#Outbreak_in_south_China).[^cite_note-Page_33,_AFM_magazine,_issue_July_2004-39][^cite_note-29_July_2003_flightglobal.com-51] The designation Super-7 was replaced by "JF-17" (Joint Fighter-17) around this point.[^cite_note-9_September_2003_flightglobal.com-52] Low speed taxiing trials began at Wenjiang Airport, [Chengdu](170375.md), on 27 June 2003.[^cite_note-Janes-38] The maiden flight was made in late August 2003;[^cite_note-FC-1/JF-17,_sinodefence.com-32][^cite_note-9_September_2003_flightglobal.com-52] an official maiden flight of the prototype took place in early September. The prototype was marked with the new PAF designation JF-17.[^cite_note-Page_33,_AFM_magazine,_issue_July_2004-39] By March 2004, CAC had made around 20 test flights of the first prototype.[^cite_note-23_March_2004_flightglobal.com-53] On 7 April 2004, PAF test pilots Rashid Habib and Mohammad Ehsan-ul-Haq flew PT-01 for the first time. The maiden flight of the third prototype, PT-03, took place on 9 April 2004.[^cite_note-Page_33,_AFM_magazine,_issue_July_2004-39] In March 2004, Pakistan was planning to induct around 200 aircraft.[^cite_note-54]



JF-17 Thunder model

Following the third prototype, several design improvements were developed and incorporated into further aircraft. Because of excessive smoke emissions by the RD-93 engine, the air intakes were widened. Reported control problems found in testing resulted in alterations to the wing [leading edge root extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading-edge_extension) (LERX). The vertical tail fin was enlarged to house an expanded [electronic warfare](82272.md) equipment bay in the tip.[^cite_note-FC-1/JF-17,_sinodefence.com-32][^cite_note-08/05/06_flightglobal.com-55][^cite_note-Jane's_Sino-Pakistani_fighter_improved-56] The redesigned aircraft had a slightly increased maximum take-off weight and incorporated an increased quantity of Chinese-sourced avionics; however PAF had selected Western avionics for their aircraft, postponing PAF deliveries from late 2005 until 2007. Pakistan evaluated British, French, and Italian avionics suites, the winner of which was expected to be finalised in 2006.[^cite_note-27_September_2005_flightglobal.com-57] PT-04, the fourth prototype and the first to incorporate the design changes, was rolled out in April 2006 and made its first flight on 28 April 2006.[^cite_note-08/05/06_flightglobal.com-55][^cite_note-Foreign_Ministry_Spokesperson_Liu_Jianchao's_press_conference_on_16_May_2006-58]



JF-17 and their DSI air intakes.

The modified air intakes replaced conventional intake ramps—whose function is to divert turbulent [boundary layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_layer) airflow away from the inlet and prevent it entering the engine—with a [diverterless supersonic inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diverterless_supersonic_inlet) (DSI) design.[^cite_note-08/05/06_flightglobal.com-55] The DSI uses a combination of forward-swept inlet cowls and a three-dimensional compression surface to divert the boundary layer airflow at high sub-sonic and supersonic speeds. According to [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin), the DSI design prevents most of the boundary layer air from entering the engine at speeds up to two times the [speed of sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_sound), reduces weight by removing the need for complex mechanical intake mechanisms,[^cite_note-Code_One_Magazine_article_on_DSI-59] and is [stealthier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_technology) than a conventional intake.[^cite_note-08/05/06_flightglobal.com-55] In 1999, developmental work on the DSI with the aim of improving aircraft performance commenced. The JF-17 design was finalised in 2001.[^cite_note-APP-60] Multiple models underwent wind tunnel tests; it was found that the DSI reduced weight, cost, and complexity while improving performance.[^cite_note-Code_One_Magazine_article_on_DSI-59]

For the avionics and weapons qualification phase of the flight testing, PT-04 was fitted with a fourth-generation avionics suite that incorporates [sensor fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_fusion), an electronic warfare suite, enhanced man-machine interface, [Digital Electronic Engine Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FADEC) (DEEC) for the RD-93 turbofan engine, FBW flight controls, day/night precision surface attack capability, and multi-mode, pulse-Doppler radar for BVR air-to-air attack capability.[^cite_note-PakTribune1-61] The sixth prototype, PT-06, made its maiden flight on 10 September 2006.[^cite_note-cnair-62] Following a competition in 2008, [Martin-Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin-Baker) was selected over a Chinese firm for the supply of fifty PK16LE ejection seats.[^cite_note-63]

### Production



A PAF JF-17 Block 1 at the [Le Bourget Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris%E2%80%93Le_Bourget_Airport), Paris, France for the 2015 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show)&#x20;

On 2 March 2007, the first consignment of two small-batch-production (SBP) aircraft arrived in a dismantled state in Pakistan. They flew for the first time on 10 March 2007 and took part in a public aerial demonstration during a [Pakistan Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Day) parade on 23 March 2007. The PAF intended to induct 200 JF-17 by 2015 to replace all its Chengdu F-7, Nanchang A-5, and Dassault Mirage III/5 aircraft. In preparation for the [in-flight refuelling](237949.md) of JF-17s, the PAF has upgraded several Mirage IIIs with IFR probes for training purposes.[^cite_note-The_News,_JF-17_engine_row_resolved:_Air_chief-64] A dual-seat, combat-capable trainer was originally scheduled to begin flight testing in 2006;[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] in 2009 Pakistan reportedly decided to develop the training model into a specialised attack variant.[^cite_note-IASC_Chinese_Dimensions_of_the_2007_Dubai_Airshow-65][^cite_note-66]

In November 2007, the PAF and PAC conducted flight evaluations of aircraft fitted with a variant of the NRIET KLJ-10 radar developed by China's [Nanjing Research Institute for Electronics Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Research_Institute_for_Electronics_Technology?action=edit\&redlink=1) (NRIET), and the LETRI [SD-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD-10_\(missile\)) active radar homing AAM.[^cite_note-Jane's_—_Chinese,_French_weapons_for_JF-17-67] In 2005, PAC began manufacturing JF-17 components; production of sub-assemblies commenced on 22 January 2008.[^cite_note-paf.gov.pk,_Manufacturing_of_JF-17_Thunder_Sub-Assemblies_Commence_at_PAC,_Kamra-68][^cite_note-Dawn.com,_Sub-assembly_of_Thunder_aircraft_begins_at_Kamra-69] The PAF was to receive a further six pre-production aircraft in 2005, for a total of 8 out of an initial production run of 16 aircraft. [Initial operating capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_operating_capability) was to be achieved by the end of 2008.[^cite_note-Jane's,_January_2008,_JF-17_production_commences-70] Final assembly of the JF-17 in Pakistan began on 30 June 2009; PAC expected to complete production of four to six aircraft that year. They planned to produce twelve aircraft in 2010 and fifteen to sixteen aircraft per year from 2011; this could increase to twenty-five aircraft per year.[^cite_note-01/07/09_flightglobal.com-71] On 29 December 2015, PAC announced the rollout of the 16th JF-17 Thunder fighter manufactured in the calendar year 2015, taking total number of manufactured aircraft to more than 66. Later, a PAF spokesperson said that in light of the interest shown by various countries, it has been decided that production capacity of JF-17 Thunder at PAC Kamra will be expanded.[^cite_note-The_Express_Tribune-12]

Russia signed an agreement in August 2007 for re-export of 150 RD-93 engines from China to Pakistan for the JF-17.[^cite_note-72] In 2008, the PAF reported it was not fully satisfied with the RD-93 engine and that it would only power the first 50 aircraft; it was alleged that arrangements for a new engine, reportedly the [Snecma M53-P2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snecma_M53#Variants), may have been made.[^cite_note-New_engine,_Snecma_M53_-_defensenews.com-73] [Mikhail Pogosyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Pogosyan), head of the MiG and Sukhoi design bureaus, recommended the Russian defence export agency [Rosoboronexport](3094695.md) block RD-93 engine sales to China to prevent export competition from the JF-17 against the MiG-29.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] At the 2010 [Farnborough Airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Airshow), the JF-17 was displayed internationally for the first time; aerial displays at the show were intended but were cancelled due to a late attendance decision as well as licence and insurance costs.[^cite_note-76] According to a Rosoboronexport official at the Airshow China 2010, held on 16–21 November 2005 in [Zhuhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai), China, Russia and China had signed a contract worth \$238 million for 100 RD-93 engines with options for another 400 engines developed for the FC-1.[^cite_note-77]

According to media reports, Pakistan planned to increase production of JF-17s by 25% in 2016.[^cite_note-78]

### Further development



JF 17 Thunder in [Pakistan Flag Livery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Pakistan) at [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show)&#x20;

Pakistan negotiated with British and Italian defence firms regarding avionics and radars for the JF-17 development. Radar options include the Italian Galileo Avionica's Grifo S7,[^cite_note-Grifo-79] the French Thomson-CSF's RC400 (a variant of the [RDY-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDY_\(Radar_Doppler_Multitarget\))),[^cite_note-defensenews.com_Pakistan_Surmounts_Sanctions_To_Revive_Airpower-80] and the British company SELEX Galileo's Vixen 500E AESA radar.[^cite_note-Thunder_Storm,_Usman_Ansari-33] In 2010, the PAF had reportedly selected ATE Aerospace Group to integrate French-built avionics and weapons systems over rival bids from Astrac, Finmeccanica and a [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group)-[Sagem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagem) joint venture. Fifty JF-17s were to be upgraded and an optional fifty from 2013 onwards, at a cost of up to US\$1.36 billion. The RC-400 radar, [MICA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MICA_\(missile\)) AAMs, and several air-to-surface weapons are believed to be in the contract. The PAF also held talks with South Africa for the supply of Denel A-darter AAMs.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

In April 2010, after eighteen months of negotiations, the deal was reportedly suspended; reports cited French concerns about Pakistan's financial situation, the protection of sensitive French technology, and by Indian [lobbying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying), which operates many French-built aircraft.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] France wanted the PAF to purchase several [Mirage 2000-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) fighters from the [United Arab Emirates Air Force](10815770.md), which would overlap with the upgraded JF-17.[^cite_note-85] In July 2010, the PAF's Chief of Air Staff, [Air Chief Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Chief_Marshal) [Rao Qamar Suleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rao_Qamar_Suleman), said such reports were false, stating: "I have had discussions with French Government officials who have assured me that this is not the position of their government...someone was trying to cause mischief—to put pressure on France not to supply the avionics we want".[^cite_note-86]

On 18 December 2013, production of Block 2 JF-17s began at PAC's Kamra facility.[^cite_note-87] These have an air-to-air refuelling capability, improved avionics, enhanced load carrying capacity, data link, and electronic warfare capabilities.[^cite_note-dipl_01-88] Block 2 construction was planned to run until 2016, after which the manufacturing of further developed Block III aircraft was planned.[^cite_note-dawn-89] In December 2015, it was announced that the 16th Block II aircraft had been handed over resulting in standing up of the 4th squadron.[^cite_note-Fourth-90]

On 17 June 2015, Jane's Defence Weekly confirmed[^cite_note-91] that JF-17 Block III will have an AESA radar, a helmet-mounted display (HMD) and possibly an internal infrared search and tracking (IRST) system.[^cite_note-92] A two-seat version was also reportedly to be produced in Block III.[^cite_note-93] Unconfirmed reports claim that Block III will also have a better flight management system.[^cite_note-94] Selex ES has promoted its next-generation cockpit as a possible upgrade of JF-17 Block III; this cockpit includes a new mission computer, an enhanced head-up display and contemporary multi-function displays, plus the capability for the pilot to use a single, large-area display instead.[^cite_note-95]

In July 2020, despite Indian protests, Russian state-owned United Engine Corporation developed a new engine designated *RD-93MA* for the JF-17 fighter being built by Pakistan.[^cite_note-Russian_engine-96]

## Design



Front View

### Airframe

The airframe is of [semi-monocoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage#Semi-monocoque) structure constructed primarily of aluminium alloys. High-strength steel and titanium alloys are partially adopted in some critical areas. The airframe is designed for a service life of 4,000 flight hours or 25 years, the first overhaul being due at 1,200 flight hours.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] Block 2 JF-17s incorporate greater use of composite materials in the airframe to reduce weight. The retractable undercarriage has a [tricycle arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear#Gear_arrangements) with a single steerable nose-wheel and two main undercarriages. The hydraulic brakes have an automatic anti-skid system. The position and shape of the inlets is designed to give the required airflow to the jet engine during manoeuvrees involving high angles of attack.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34]

The mid-mounted wings are of [cropped-delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cropped-delta) configuration. Near the [wing root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_root) are the LERX, which generate a vortex that provides extra lift to the wing at high angles of attack encountered during combat manoeuvrees. A conventional tri-plane [empennage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empennage) arrangement is incorporated, with all-moving [stabilators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilator), single [vertical stabiliser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_stabiliser), [rudder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudder), and twin ventral fins. The flight control system (FCS) comprises conventional controls with stability augmentation in the [yaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_principal_axes#Vertical_axis_\(yaw\)) and [roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_principal_axes#Longitudinal_axis_\(roll\)) axis and a digital [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire#Fly_by_wire_control_systems) (FBW) system in the [pitch axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_principal_axes#Transverse_axis_\(pitch\)). The [leading-edge slats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading-edge_slat)/[flaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flap_\(aeronautics\)) and [Trailing edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailing_edge) [flaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flap_\(aeronautics\)) are automatically adjusted during manoeuvring to increase turning performance.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] The FCS of serial production aircraft reportedly have a digital quadruplex (quad-redundant) FBW system in the pitch axis and a duplex (dual-redundant) FBW system in the roll and yaw axis.[^cite_note-cnair-62]

Up to 3,400 lb (1,500 kg) of ordnance, equipment, and fuel can be mounted under the [hardpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint), two of which are on the wing-tips, four are under the wings and one is under the fuselage.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34]

### Cockpit



JF-17 Block 1 HUD

The aircraft has three large Multifunction Colour Displays (MFD) and smart Heads-Up Display (HUD) with built-in symbol generation capability. A [center stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_stick) is used for [pitch and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_dynamics) control while [rudder pedals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_rudder) control yaw. A throttle is located to the left of the pilot. The cockpit incorporates [hands-on-throttle-and-stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOTAS) (HOTAS) controls. The pilot sits on a Martin-Baker Mk-16LE [zero-zero ejection seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-zero_ejection_seat). The cockpit incorporates an [electronic flight instrument system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_flight_instrument_system) (EFIS) and a wide-angle, holographic head-up display (HUD), which has a minimum total field of view of 25 degrees. The EFIS comprises three color multi-function displays, providing basic flight information, tactical information, and information on the engine, fuel, electrical, hydraulics, flight control, and environment control systems. The HUD and MFD can be configured to show any available information. Each MFD is 20.3 cm (8.0 in) wide and 30.5 cm (12.0 in) tall and is arranged side by side in [portrait orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_orientation). The central MFD is placed lowest to accommodate a control panel between it and the HUD.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34]

### Avionics



JF-17 Block 1 cockpit avionics display

The avionics software incorporates the concept of [open architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_architecture). Instead of the military-optimised [Ada programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_\(programming_language\)), the software is written using the popular [C++ programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++), enabling the use of the numerous civilian programmers available.[^cite_note-defenseindustrydaily.com_article-97] The aircraft also includes a health and usage monitoring system, and automatic test equipment.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34][^cite_note-98]

The JF-17 has a [defensive aids system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_aids_system) (DAS) composed of various integrated sub-systems. A [radar warning receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_warning_receiver) (RWR) provides data such as direction and proximity of enemy radars, and an [electronic warfare](82272.md) (EW) suite housed in a fairing at the tip of the tail fin interferes with enemy radars. The EW suite is also linked to a [Missile Approach Warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Approach_Warning) (MAW) system to defend against radar-guided missiles. The MAW system uses several optical sensors across the airframe to detect the rocket motors of missiles across a 360-degree coverage.[^cite_note-Thunder_Storm,_Usman_Ansari-33] Data from the MAW system, such as direction of inbound missiles and the time to impact, is shown on cockpit displays and the HUD. A countermeasures dispensing system releases [decoy flares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flare_\(countermeasure\)) and [chaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaff_\(countermeasure\)) to help evade hostile radar and missiles. The DAS systems will also be enhanced by integration of a self-protection radar-jamming pod that will be carried externally on a [hardpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint).[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34]

The first forty-two PAF production aircraft are equipped with the [NRIET KLJ-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLJ-7) radar,[^cite_note-Milavia.net_2007_news_report-99][^cite_note-Jane's_Avionics_KLJ-7-100] a variant of the KLJ-10 radar developed by China's Nanjing Research Institute of Electronic Technology (NRIET) and also used on the Chengdu J-10. Multiple modes can manage the surveillance and engagement of up to forty air, ground, and sea targets; the [track-while-scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_while_scan) mode can track up to ten targets at BVR and can engage two simultaneously with [radar-homing AAMs](320568.md#Radar_guidance). The operation range for targets with a [radar cross-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross-section) (RCS) of 5 m<sup>2</sup> (54 sq ft) is stated to be ≥ 105 km (65 miles) in look-up mode and ≥ 85 km (53 miles) in look-down mode.[^cite_note-Jane's_Avionics_KLJ-7-100][^cite_note-Jane's,_NRIET_outlines_fighter_radar_improvements-101] A [forward looking infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_looking_infrared) (FLIR) pod for low-level navigation and [infra-red search and track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infra-red_search_and_track) (IRST) system for passive targeting can also be integrated;[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] the JF-17 Block 2 is believed to incorporate an IRST. In April 2016, Air Marshal Muhammad Ashfaque Arain said that, "JF-17 needs a targeting pod, as the jets' usefulness in current operations was limited due to lack of precision targeting. To fulfill this gap the Air Force was interested in buying the Thales-made Damocles, a third-generation targeting pod; which was a priority."[^cite_note-102] In 2017, [Aselsans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASELSAN) ASELPOD was tested and successfully integrated with the JF-17 and Pakistan has subsequently purchased at least eight [targeting pods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeting_pod) from Aselsan.[^cite_note-103] This integration has significantly enhanced the JF-17 platform's ability to launch precision strikes.

A helmet-mounted sight (HMS) developed by [Luoyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoyang) Electro-Optics Technology Development Centre of [AVIC](20138455.md) was developed in parallel with the JF-17; it was first tested on Prototype 04 in 2006.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] It was dubbed as EO HMS, (Electro-Optical Helmet Mounted Sight) and was first revealed to the public in 2008 at the 7th [Zhuhai Airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai_Airshow), where a partial mock-up was on display. The HMS tracks the pilot's head and eye movements to guide missiles towards the pilot's visual target.[^cite_note-Thunder_Storm,_Usman_Ansari-33] An externally carried day/night [laser designator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_designator) [targeting pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeting_pod) may be integrated with the avionics to guide [laser-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser-guided_bomb) (LGBs).[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] An extra [hardpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint) may be added under the starboard air intake, opposite the cannon, for such pods. To reduce the numbers of targeting pods required, the aircraft's [tactical data link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_data_link) can transmit target data to other aircraft not equipped with targeting pods.[^cite_note-Thunder_Storm,_Usman_Ansari-33] The communication systems comprise two [VHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHF)/[UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UHF) radios; the VHF radio has the capacity for [data linking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHF_Data_Link) for communication with ground control centers, [airborne early warning and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control) aircraft and combat aircraft with compatible data links for [network-centric warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network-centric_warfare), and improved [situation awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situation_awareness).[^cite_note-106] The aircraft uses [RLGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_laser_gyroscope) along with [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) for navigation. The aircraft is equipped with an [IFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_friend_or_foe) [Transponder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponder) which allows it to differentiate between friendly aircraft and enemy aircraft. The [ACMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_combat_maneuvering_instrumentation) aids in aerial combat for manoeuvring.

### Engine



&#x20;The JF-17 Block 1 & 2 used the RD-93 which is a newer variant of the above shown RD-33.

The first two blocks of JF-17 is powered by a single Russian RD-93 [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) engine, which is a variant of the [Klimov RD-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_RD-33) engine used on the [MiG-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-29) fighter. The engine gives more [thrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust) and significantly [lower specific fuel consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_specific_fuel_consumption) than [turbojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbojet) engines fitted to older combat aircraft being replaced by the JF-17. The advantages of using a single engine are a reduction in maintenance time and cost when compared to twin-engined fighters. A [thrust-to-weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-to-weight_ratio) of 0.99 can be achieved with full internal fuel tanks and no external payload. The engine's air supply is provided by two bifurcated air inlets (see [airframe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JF-17_Thunder#Airframe_and_cockpit) section).[^cite_note-stuck-in-sichuan-107]

The RD-93 is known to produce smoke trails.[^cite_note-stuck-in-sichuan-107] The [Guizhou Aero Engine Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou_Aircraft_Industry_Corporation) has been developing a new turbofan engine, the [WS-13 *Taishan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou_WS-13), since 2000 to replace the RD-93. It is based on the RD-33 and incorporates new technologies to boost performance and reliability. A thrust output of 80 to 86.36 kN (17,980 to 19,410 lb<sub>f</sub>), a lifespan of 2,200 hours, and a thrust-to-weight ratio of 8.7 are expected. An improved version of the WS-13, developing a thrust of around 100 kN (22,000 lb<sub>f</sub>) (22,450 lb), is also reportedly under development.[^cite_note-October_Surprises_in_Chinese_Aerospace-108] During the 2015 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show), it was announced that flight testing of a JF-17 equipped with the [WS-13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou_WS-13) engine had begun.[^cite_note-janes.com-109] In 2015, a representative of PAC said that Pakistan would continue to use the RD-93 engine on their fighters.[^cite_note-110] Local media reports in January 2016 said that Russia was planning to sell engines for JF-17 directly to Pakistan.[^cite_note-111] According to a PAC representative, Pakistan is looking to collaborate with Russia in developing and repairing engines.

### Fuel system

The fuel system comprises internal fuel tanks located in the wings and fuselage with a capacity of 2,330 kg (5,140 lb); they are refuelled through a single point pressure refuelling system (see [turbine fuel systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_fuel_system#Turbine_fuel_system)). Internal fuel storage can be supplemented by external fuel tanks. One 800-litre (180 imp gal) [drop tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_tank) can be mounted on the aircraft's centerline hard point under the fuselage and two 800-litre or 1,110-litre (240 imp gal) drop tanks can be mounted on the two inboard under-wing hardpoints.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] The fuel system is compatible with [in-flight refuelling (IFR)](237949.md), allowing tanker aircraft to refuel inflight, and increasing its range and loitering time significantly. All production aircraft for the PAF are to be fitted with IFR probes.[^cite_note-Thunder_Storm,_Usman_Ansari-33] In June 2013, PAF Air Chief Marshal [Tahir Rafique Butt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahir_Rafique_Butt) said ground tests on the JF-17's refuelling probes had been successfully completed and the first mid-air refuelling operations would commence that summer.[^cite_note-112]

### Armaments



JF-17 Thunder with its weapons



Thunder with weapons

The JF-17 can be armed with up to 3,400 lb (1,500 kg) of air-to-air and [air-to-ground weaponry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-ground_weaponry), and other equipment mounted externally on the aircraft's seven hardpoints.[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] One hardpoint is located under the fuselage between the main landing gear, two are underneath each wing, and one is at each wing-tip. All seven hardpoints communicate via a [MIL-STD-1760](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIL-STD-1760) data-bus architecture with the Stores Management System,[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] which is stated to be capable of integration with weaponry of any origin.[^cite_note-defensenews.com_Pakistan_Surmounts_Sanctions_To_Revive_Airpower-80] Internal armament comprises one 23 mm (0.91 in) GSh-23-2 twin-barrel cannon mounted under the [port side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_\(nautical\)) air intake, which can be replaced with a 30 mm (1.2 in) [GSh-30-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSh-30-2) twin-barrel cannon.[^cite_note-FC-1/JF-17,_sinodefence.com-32][^cite_note-113]

The wing-tip hard-points are typically occupied by short range infra-red homing AAMs. Many combinations of ordnance and equipment such as [targeting pods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeting_pods) can be carried on the under-wing and under-fuselage hard-points. Underwing hard-points can be fitted with [multiple ejector racks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_point#Ejector_racks), allowing each hard-point to carry two 500 lb (230 kg) unguided bombs or LGBs—[Mk.82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mk.82) or [GBU-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-12).[^cite_note-Thunder_Storm,_Usman_Ansari-33] Active radar homing BVR AAMs can be integrated with the radar and data-link for mid-course updates. The aircraft can carry the [PL-12/SD-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-12) along with the [PL-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-5)E and [PL-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-9)C Short range, infra-red homing missiles. The more advanced [PL-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-10)E High-Off Bore Sight missiles were integrated into the aircraft in April 2021, operated Within Visual Range using the HMD/S. With the Block 3 variant of the JF-17, the ability to fit and operate the [PL-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-15)E, the most advanced BVR missile developed by China for export with a claimed operating range of 145 km, is also integrated.[^cite_note-114]

Unguided air-to-ground weaponry includes rocket pods, [gravity bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unguided_bomb) and [Matra Durandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_Durandal) anti-runway munitions. [Precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition) such as LGBs and [satellite-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition#Satellite-guided_weapons) are also compatible with the JF-17, as are other guided weapons such as [anti-ship missiles](140725.md) and [anti-radiation missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-radiation_missile).[^cite_note-JF-17_Thunder_Detailed_Specifications,_PAC_Kamra-34] Pakistan planned to bring the Brazilian [MAR-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAR-1) anti-radiation missile into service on its JF-17 fleet in 2014.[^cite_note-janes_mar1-115]

## Operational history

### Pakistan



Pakistan Air Force JF-17 flies in front of the 26,660 ft high [Nanga Parbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanga_Parbat)

Small batch production of the single-seat, single-engine JF-17s began in China in June 2006. The first two small-batch-produced aircraft were delivered on 2 March 2007 and first flew in Pakistan on 10 March.[^cite_note-116] They took part in an aerial display on 23 March 2007 as part of the [Pakistan Day Joint Services Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Day_Parade) in Islamabad.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-PakTribune-118] Another six small-batch-produced aircraft were delivered by March 2008.[^cite_note-119] These were extensively flight-tested and evaluated by the PAF.[^cite_note-120] Two serial production aircraft were delivered from China in 2009 and the first Pakistani-manufactured aircraft was delivered to the PAF in a ceremony on 23 November 2009.[^cite_note-121]

On 18 February 2010, the first JF-17 squadron, [No. 26 *Black Spiders*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._26_Squadron_\(Pakistan_Air_Force\)), was officially inducted into the PAF with an initial strength of 14 fighter planes.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] These aircraft first saw service in the [anti-terrorist operation in South Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rah-e-Nijat), during which various types of weapons were evaluated.[^cite_note-124] They took part in the PAF's *High Mark 2010* exercise from 29 April, where they were used by the *Blue Force* to attack *Red Land* surface targets with precision air-to-surface weapons.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

On 11 April 2011, a re-equipment ceremony for No. 26 *Black Spiders* Squadron took place, during which it was stated that the JF-17 had "revolutionized the PAF's operational concepts". Then Air Chief Marshal [Rao Qamar Suleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rao_Qamar_Suleman) reported the re-equipping of No. 26 squadron and the addition of the JF-17 Thunder to the [No. 16 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._16_Squadron_\(Pakistan_Air_Force\)). He also thanked the contribution and support of the Chinese in helping to acquire a technological breakthrough in the shape of the aircraft.[^cite_note-Daily_Times_Pakistan-127]

During [Operation Zarb-e-Azb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zarb-e-Azb) 2014–2016, JF-17 were deployed frequently to carry out airstrikes against [TTP](18953699.md) hideouts, killing hundreds of terrorists.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

In September 2015, the No. 2 Squadron tasked with sea strikes was re-equipped with JF-17s replacing the F7s.[^cite_note-130] The No. 16 Squadron "Black Panthers" has also been equipped with the JF-17.[^cite_note-131]

On 19 June 2017, it was reported that a JF-17 shot down an [Iranian](412792.md) [UAV](58900.md) operating in [Pakistan](23235.md)'s [Pangjur District](2489393.md).[^cite_note-132]

In February 2019, PAF JF-17s took part in Pakistan's [retaliatory airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Jammu_and_Kashmir_airstrikes) against India during which two Thunders of the [No. 16 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._16_Squadron_PAF) struck Indian ground targets with [Mk. 83 REKs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_83).[^cite_note-kaiser-133][^cite_note-tufail-134][^cite_note-auto8-23] According to reports,[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] as of March 2021, JF-17s are operational in seven fighter squadrons based at five airbases.[^cite_note-137] Pakistan also Claimed to Shot down an Indian [MIG 21 Bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21) using JF-17 but India maintained that the MiG-21 was actually shot down by a Pakistani [F-16](11642.md).[^cite_note-138]

In March 2023, the first batch of JF-17 Block 3 aircraft were inducted into the PAF.

During the [May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_standoff), the PAF publicly showcased, for the first time, a JF-17 Block 3 fighter aircraft equipped with [PL-15E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-15#Variants) long-range [beyond-visual-range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond-visual-range_missile) [air-to-air missiles](320568.md) (BVRAAMs).[^cite_note-139] During the conflict, the PAF deployed JF-17s and [J-10CEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-10CE) in combat in both the air-to-air and air-to-ground roles.[^cite_note-auto14-27][^cite_note-auto16-28][^cite_note-auto12-29] Pakistan claimed that its JF-17 successfully targeted and destroyed an Indian [S-400 missile defence system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-400_missile_system) at [Adampur Air Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adampur_Airport) in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) on 10 May 2025 using two [CM-400AKG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CM-400AKG) long-range supersonic [air-to-surface missiles](1544116.md).[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-auto17-141][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] On 11 May, the PAF, in a press briefing said that it had targeted the 96L6E Cheese Board radar of the S-400 system, one of the units of the combined air defence system.[^cite_note-144] India's [Ministry of External Affairs](10302410.md) dismissed the claim, stating that all Indian S-400 squadrons are still functional.[^cite_note-145] Indian Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) visited the Adampur Air Force Station on 13 May 2025, and posed in front of an S-400 Launcher, which the [Indian media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_media) claimed to be fully operational. According to Indian media, his visit served as a rebuttal to Pakistan's claims, reinforcing that the missile system was intact and operational, though Pakistan never claimed to have targeted the launcher itself.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

Following the [2026 Iran war ceasefire](82888027.md) in April 2026, Indian media sources reported that the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) had mobilised its JF-17 and [F-16](11642.md) fighters, as well as [Il-78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-78) tankers and [C-130](7697.md) cargo planes. The fighters are expected to fly an escort mission for the Iranian representatives and the PAF set up a protective shield over Iran and the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) for the Iranian delegation to travel to [Islamabad](51153.md) for further negotiations with the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] [Reuters](18998750.md) also confirmed the escort mission, and reported it involved around 20 aircraft including the J-10 and unspecified [AEWACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control) but made no mention of the JF-17.[^cite_note-151]

During [2026 Iran](82533582.md) war between US, Israel and Iran and heightened tensions in [Middle East](19323.md), Pakistan deployed squadron of JF-17 consisted of 16 jets at [King Abdul Aziz Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_Air_Base) to ensure defence of Saudi Arabia under [Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement](81115024.md) under which both states [Pakistan](23235.md) and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) are mutually obliged to defend each other.[^cite_note-152]

**Exercises**

The PAF has deployed the JF-17 Thunder in a number of bilateral and multinational air exercises since the 2010s. PAF JF-17s have participated in joint air exercises with the People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF) under the Shaheen exercise series since at least 2014, focusing on air combat training and interoperability. In 2019 and again in 2021, PAF JF-17s took part in the Turkish Air Force–hosted multinational exercise Anatolian Eagle, operating alongside aircraft from several participating air forces.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] In 2022, PAF JF-17s participated in the Saudi-led multinational air exercise Spears of Victory at King Abdulaziz Air Base, flying alongside Royal Saudi Air Force Tornados, Typhoons and F-15s, as well as allied aircraft.[^cite_note-155] JF-17s continued participation in subsequent editions of Spears of Victory, including 2023 and 2025, with later deployments featuring the Block-III variant.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] In 2025, PAF JF-17 Block-III fighters were also deployed to Azerbaijan for the bilateral air exercise Indus Shield Alpha, marking one of the type's longest overseas deployments.[^cite_note-158]

**Airshows**

The JF-17 Thunder has participated in international airshows since 2010 as part of export promotion and military diplomacy efforts by the PAF.[^cite_note-ISPR-2010-159] Early public appearances took place at airshows in Pakistan and China, followed by displays at major international aerospace exhibitions, including the Paris Air Show in 2011 and 2015,[^cite_note-160] the Dubai Airshow in 2013, 2017, 2021, and 2025[^cite_note-161] and the Farnborough International Airshow in 2014 and 2016, primarily as static displays.[^cite_note-162] The aircraft has also featured at the China International Aviation & Aerospace Exhibition, including static and flying displays, with later editions showcasing the Block III variant.[^cite_note-163] In 2019, 2022, and 2025, PAF JF-17s participated in the Royal International Air Tattoo (RIAT), in static and air displays.[^cite_note-164] The aircraft was also displayed at the [World Defense Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Defense_Show) in 2024.[^cite_note-165]

### Myanmar

In July 2015, Myanmar ordered 16 Block 2 JF-17s from [Pakistan](23235.md) and [China](5405.md) for approximately \$560 million.[^cite_note-166] In late 2015, [Myanmar](19457.md) ordered 16 [RD-93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_RD-33) spare engines from [Russia](25391.md), which were received in 2018 and 2019.[^cite_note-SIPRI-167] On 17 December 2018, [Jane's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Information_Group) disclosed that the [Myanmar Air Force](9484818.md) had received the first batch of JF-17s.[^cite_note-168] An official Myanmar Air Force video released on Air Force day showcased a number of JF-17s, both on static display and in the air.[^cite_note-169] As per reports, the [Myanmar Air Force](9484818.md) is in possession 11 JF-17 Block 2s.

In May 2022, a PAF cargo plane supplied spare parts for the JF-17s of the Myanmar Air Force. In June 2022, it was reported that a team of 15 PAF personnel were scheduled to visit [Meiktila Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiktila_Air_Base) in Myanmar to provide technical support for the Myanmar Air Force JF-17s, including setting up of a JF-17 simulator at Meiktila Air Base to train pilots of the Myanmar Air Force and to address technical issues relating to JF-17s that Myanmar Air Force was facing.

In November 2022, the Myanmar anti-junta news website [The Irrawaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irrawaddy) claimed that most of the aircraft had been grounded due to technical malfunctions, citing unnamed analysts and Myanmar Air Force pilots. The article specified that the sources reported that the aircraft have structural cracks, and poor perceived performance of the KLJ-7 radar. The grounding was exacerbated by post-2021 military coup sanctions, which restricted access to spare parts for Western-sourced components in the avionics and electronics. Myanmar lacked the local expertise to maintain or repair the complex systems, and initial attempts by Pakistani technicians in September 2022 to set up a simulator at Pathein air base and resolve issues were only partially successful.[^cite_note-\:5-170]

In 2023, Pakistan had reportedly sent a team of engineers and technicians to Myanmar to help repair and maintain its JF-17s.[^cite_note-\:6-171]

In July 2026, Azerbaijan MOD officially released multiple footages of Azerbaijani JF-17 block 3s and JF-17B being operational under the 408th squadron "Jaguars", Participating in exercises alongside Su25s. Confirming the induction of JF-17s into Azerbaijani service.[^cite_note-197]

### Former interests

#### Argentina

At the 2013 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show), officials from Argentine aerospace conglomerate [Fábrica Argentina de Aviones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A1brica_Argentina_de_Aviones) (FAdeA) revealed that the firm had held multiple discussions with Chinese officials over a potential co-production of the FC-1/JF-17, for the [Argentine Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Air_Force) (FAA); this was regarded as the first formal effort by Argentina to possibly procure, or co-produce the aircraft.[^cite_note-226] FAdeA officials said that the co-produced FC-1 could be classified as the "Pulqui-III", with regard to FAdeA's [Pulqui-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulqui_II) fighter.[^cite_note-ja23j13-227]

In 2015, following a three-day visit by [Argentine president](229473.md) [Cristina Fernández de Kirchner](1644780.md) to [China](5405.md), Argentina announced that it would consider purchasing around 20 JF-17s from [CAIG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Aircraft_Industry_Group); however the deal did not materialise.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229]

The primary reason for Argentine interest was reportedly the aircraft's lesser requirement for parts of British origin, as the United Kingdom had barred any sale of military equipment consisting of British-manufactured parts to Argentina since the [1982 Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War).[^cite_note-defensenews.com-230][^cite_note-231] Likewise, Argentina's earlier efforts to procure other aircraft, namely, the [Mirage F1M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_F1), the [IAI Kfir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Kfir), the [JAS 39 Gripen](87577.md) and the [KAI FA-50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle) were scuttled due to diplomatic pressure from the United Kingdom, given the aforementioned aircraft were found to contain British-origin parts.[^cite_note-232][^cite_note-en.mercopress.com-233][^cite_note-defensenews.com-230]

In September 2021, the [Argentine government](67339.md) presented a draft budget for the fiscal year of 2022, which contained a request of US\$664 million for the acquisition of future fighter aircraft for the FAA.[^cite_note-\:3-234] However, multiple media outlets misinterpreted this action, erroneously reporting that the request for funds were for acquiring the JF-17 Block-III.[^cite_note-235] Argentina's [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Argentina\)) (*Ministerio de Defensa*) later clarified that the JF-17 had not been selected, asserting that the FAA was still evaluating five other aircraft as possible options.[^cite_note-\:3-234]

In May 2022, a delegation of the FAA evaluated the JF-17 Thunder in China.[^cite_note-236]

However, in October 2023, the United States approved the transfer of 24 second-hand [F-16 Block-15 MLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-16_Fighting_Falcon_variants) fighters previously owned by the [Royal Danish Air Force](548819.md) to Argentina, countering the Chinese offer; reportedly, the deal did not necessitate an approval from the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-237] Following the inauguration of the [Javier Milei administration](56599840.md) in Argentina in 2023, the decision to select the F-16 had reportedly materialised, leaving the JF-17 out of the contest.[^cite_note-238]

#### Malaysia

[Malaysia](3607937.md) had periodically indicated that it may be interested in purchasing the JF-17 for the [Royal Malaysian Air Force](1137609.md) (RMAF), as part of its efforts to replace its [MIG-29 fleet](185653.md); reports of Malaysian interest in the JF-17 emerged in 2015, although this was later denied.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245]

In March 2019, then-visiting [Malaysian PM](496731.md) [Mahathir bin Mohammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahathir_Mohamad) was accorded an aerial-display of the JF-17's at the 2019 [Pakistan Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Day_Parade); he was also briefed about the fighter by the PAF.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]

In June 2021, the RMAF formally released a tender for the supply of 18 light combat-aircraft — dubbed as the "Fighter Lead In Trainer-Light Combat Aircraft" (FLIT/LCA), in an effort to supplant its ageing [BAE Hawk 108/208](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Hawk) light-combat aircraft and its [MB-339CM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aermacchi_MB-339) trainer-aircraft.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249] The RMAF later issued a [Request for Proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Request_for_proposal) (RFP) to nine different aircraft-manufacturing conglomerates in July, with a submission-deadline of September 2021 (this would later be extended to October 2021).[^cite_note-theedgemarkets.com-250] The JF-17 was widely regarded to be a leading contender in the FLIT/LCA procurement initiative, along with the [HAL Tejas](749967.md) and the [KAI FA-50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle).[^cite_note-251]

However, in October 2021, the JF-17 was revealed to have abstained from participating in the FLIT/LCA tender; later reports confirmed that only six companies had responded to the RFP issued by the RMAF - the [KAI FA-50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle) ([Korea Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Aerospace_Industries)), the [HAL Tejas](749967.md) ([Hindustan Aeronautics Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Aeronautics_Limited)), the [HAIC L-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongdu_JL-10) ([China National Aero-Technology Import & Export Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Aero-Technology_Import_&_Export_Corporation)), the [Aermacchi M-346](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alenia_Aermacchi_M-346_Master) ([Leonardo S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.)), the [TAI Hürjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_H%C3%BCrjet) ([Turkish Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Aerospace_Industries)) and the [Mikoyan MiG-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_MiG-35) ([Rosoboronexport](3094695.md)).[^cite_note-theedgemarkets.com-250] The JF-17's unprecedented absence from the FLIT/LCA essentially ended all speculations regarding its participation in Malaysia.[^cite_note-theedgemarkets.com-250][^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254]

In December 2021, the JF-17 was reportedly re-offered to the RMAF, with an estimated price-discount of about 30%; however, these reports remain unconfirmed.[^cite_note-255] The RMAF eventually declined to purchase the JF-17 and proceeded instead to order 18 FA-50 Block 20 jets in March 2023.[^cite_note-256]

Also known as the JF-17 Operational Capability Upgrade (OCU), the project aims to enhance the JF-17 beyond the capabilities of the current JF-17C Block 3, which is the most advanced variant of the fighter.

## Accidents and incidents

Since its first flight in 2003 and operationalization in 2007, five JF-17s have crashed in accidents:

- **14 November 2011**: A PAF JF-17 Block 1 aircraft crashed during a routine training flight in the mountainous Mullan Mansoor region of [Attock District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attock_District) in the [Punjab Province](24751.md) while flying from [PAF Base Minhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAF_Base_Minhas). According to the official PAF report, the crash was caused by a technical malfunction. Pakistani news reported that the pilot, Squadron Leader Muhammad Hussain, ejected but was killed after his parachute failed to open, and that there were no civilian casualties reported on the ground. The pilot's body was discovered two kilometres from the crash site. This was the first known crash of a JF-17.

  [^cite_note-324]

  [^cite_note-325]

  [^cite_note-326]

  [^cite_note-327]

  [^cite_note-328]

- **27 September 2016:** A PAF JF-17 Block 1 aircraft crashed during [Exercise High Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_military_exercises#High_Mark) in the [Arabian Sea](2747.md). The pilot ejected successfully and was rescued from the sea. [Martin-Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin-Baker), the manufacturer of the JF-17's ejection seats, later tweeted that the 15 September 2020 crash was the first ejection from a JF-17.

  [^cite_note-329]

  [^cite_note-\:1-330]

- **15 September 2020:** A PAF JF-17 Block 1 aircraft crashed during a routine training flight near [Pindigheb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pindigheb), [Attock District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attock_District) in the [Punjab Province](24751.md). The pilot ejected successfully and no loss of life was reported on the ground.

  [^cite_note-331]

  &#x20;While the PAF did not identify the aircraft, ejection seat manufacturer Martin-Baker, whose seats are installed in the JF-17, said in a Twitter post, "a Pakistan Air Force JF-17 aircraft crashed earlier today during a routine training mission, the pilot ejected successfully," adding that this marked the first instance of an ejection from a JF-17 aircraft, which uses its Martin-Baker PK16LE ejection seats.

  [^cite_note-332]

  [^cite_note-\:1-330]

- **6 August 2021:** A PAF JF-17B Block 2 aircraft crashed during a routine training flight in [Attock District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attock_District) in the [Punjab Province](24751.md). Both pilots ejected successfully and no loss of life was reported on the ground.

  [^cite_note-333]

  [^cite_note-334]

  [^cite_note-335]

- **5 June 2024:** A PAF JF-17 Block 2 aircraft crashed during a routine training flight in [Jhang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhang_District) in the [Punjab Province](24751.md). The fighter jet reportedly belonged to [No. 14 Squadron "Tail Choppers"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._14_Squadron_PAF). The pilot successfully ejected. The crash was reported by the manufacturer of the ejection seat of the aircraft, Martin Baker.

  [^cite_note-336]

## In popular culture

The JF-17 Thunder has been heavily featured in the 2019 Pakistani war action film [Sherdil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherdil). Serving as the primary fighter for the protagonist, Flight Lieutenant Haris Mustafa, the indigenously built jet takes center stage. The movie’s high-stakes aerial combat sequences prominently showcase the multi-role fighter against Indian aircraft, highlighting its critical role in Pakistan’s modern defense capabilities.[^cite_note-363]

The JF-17 has earned the nickname ‘**Jeff** ’ among the [DCS World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Combat_Simulator) and other combat flight sim communities, the moniker is a playful phonetic anglicization derived directly from saying "JF" out loud.[^cite_note-364]
