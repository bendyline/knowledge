The **United States Fish and Wildlife Service** (**USFWS** or **FWS**) is a [U.S. federal government agency](58232.md) within the [United States Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior) which oversees the management of [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish), [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife), and natural [habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitats) in the United States. The mission of the agency is: *"working with others to conserve, protect, and enhance fish, wildlife, plants and their habitats for the continuing benefit of the American people"*.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

## History



[Game warden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_officer) [Paul Kroegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kroegel) with a [brown pelican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_pelican) at [Pelican Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelican_Island_National_Wildlife_Refuge), 1907

### Fish Commission and Bureau of Fisheries

The original ancestor of the agency was the **United States Commission on Fish and Fisheries**, more commonly referred to as the [United States Fish Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fish_Commission), created in 1871 by the [United States Congress](31756.md) with the purpose of studying and recommending solutions to a noted decline in the [stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_stocks) of [food fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_\(food\)).[^cite_note-4] [Spencer Fullerton Baird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Fullerton_Baird) was appointed to lead it as the first United States Commissioner of Fisheries.[^cite_note-5] In 1903, the Fish Commission was reorganized as the **United States Bureau of Fisheries** and made part of the [United States Department of Commerce and Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Commerce_and_Labor).[^cite_note-6]

When the Department of Commerce and Labor was split into the [United States Department of Commerce](70243.md) and the [United States Department of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Labor) in 1913, the Bureau of Fisheries was made part of the Department of Commerce.[^cite_note-7] Originally focused on [fisheries science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_science) and [fish culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_culture), the Bureau of Fisheries also assumed other duties; in 1906, the U.S. Congress assigned it the responsibility for the enforcement of [fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) and [fur seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_seal)-hunting regulations in the [District of Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Alaska),[^cite_note-afscearly-8] and in 1910 for the management and harvest of [northern fur seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_fur_seal), foxes, and other fur-bearing animals in the [Pribilof Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pribilof_Islands), as well as for the care, education, and welfare of the [Aleut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleut_people) communities in the islands.[^cite_note-pribiloftenders-9] In 1939, the Bureau of Fisheries moved from the Department of Commerce to the Department of the Interior.[^cite_note-10]

### Bureau of Biological Survey

The other ancestor of the agency began as the **Section of Economic Ornithology**, which was established within the [United States Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture) in 1885 and became the **Division of Economic Ornithology and Mammalogy** in 1886.[^cite_note-11] In 1896 it became the **Division of Biological Survey**. [Clinton Hart Merriam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Hart_Merriam) headed the Division for 25 years and became a national figure for improving the scientific understanding of birds and [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal) in the United States.

By 1905 with funding scarce, the Survey included in its mission the eradication of wolves, coyotes and other large predators. This garnered them the support of ranchers and western legislators resulting, by 1914, in a \$125,000 congressionally approved budget for destroying wolves, coyotes and other animals injurious to agriculture and animal husbandry.[^cite_note-\:0-12] Meanwhile, scientists like [Joseph Grinnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Grinnell) and Charles C. Adams, a founder of the [Ecological Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_Society_of_America), were promoting a [balance of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_nature). In 1924, at a conference organized by the [American Society of Mammalogists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Mammalogists), the debate generated a public split between those in the Survey, promoting eradication, and the mammalogists who promoted some sort of accommodation.[^cite_note-13] The Survey subsequently placed over 2 million poisoned bait stations across the west. The Survey then turned to the eradication of coyote,[^cite_note-\:0-12]<sup>: 124–126 </sup> coordinated through the [1931 Animal Damage Control Act](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/us-agriculture-animal-damage-control-act-chapter-17-miscellaneous-matters).

In 1934, the Division of Biological Survey was reorganized as the **Bureau of Biological Survey** and [Jay Norwood Darling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Norwood_Darling) was appointed its chief;. The same year, Congress passed the [Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_and_Wildlife_Coordination_Act), one of the oldest federal environmental review statutes.[^cite_note-14] Under Darling's guidance, the Bureau began an ongoing legacy of protecting vital natural habitat throughout the United States. In 1939, the Bureau of Biological Survey moved from the Department of Agriculture to the Department of the Interior.

### Fish and Wildlife Service



Patrol vehicles of the Fish & Wildlife Service in [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Alaska), 1950.

On June 30, 1940, the Bureau of Fisheries and the Bureau of Biological Survey were combined to form the Department of the Interior's **Fish and Wildlife Service**. In 1956, the Fish and Wildlife Service was reorganized as the **United States Fish and Wildlife Service** — which remained part of the Department of the Interior — and divided its operations into two bureaus, the **Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife** and the **Bureau of Commercial Fisheries**, with the latter inheriting the history and heritage of the old U.S. Fish Commission and U.S. Bureau of Fisheries.[^cite_note-auto-15]

In 1970, the [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](37876.md) was formed within the [Department of Commerce](70243.md). That year, the Bureau of Commercial Fisheries merged with the [saline water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saline_water) laboratories of the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife. This led to the creation of today's [National Marine Fisheries Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Marine_Fisheries_Service), which also acquired the [former fleet of the United States Fish and Wildlife Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_fleet_of_the_United_States_Fish_and_Wildlife_Service).[^cite_note-16]

The use of poisoned [bait stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bait_\(luring_substance\)) continued into the early 1970s. Although it resulted in the deaths of hundreds of thousands of [coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyote), this method failed to significantly reduce their population. Instead, due to their remarkable adaptability and resilience, coyotes migrated into a wider range of [habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat), even venturing into urban areas. Driven by growing environmental awareness in the late 1960s and early 1970s, [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) banned poisons used since the [Second World War](32927.md) and signed the [Endangered Species Act of 1973](532055.md).[^cite_note-\:0-12] In 1985 [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) reversed the poison killing ban and transferred the responsibility for predator control to the [Wildlife Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Services) program under the [Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Habitat conservation

The agency plays a vital role in wildlife and [habitat conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_conservation) through various key functions, including protecting [native species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_species), managing [migratory bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration) populations, restoring [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) to support [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem), conserving vital habitats like [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland), and overseeing wildlife efforts on [military bases](843566.md) per the [Sikes Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikes_Act).[^cite_note-\:1-2]

### National Wildlife Refuge System



[Biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist) examining a baby [alligator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_alligator) in the [Big Branch Marsh National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Branch_Marsh_National_Wildlife_Refuge)

The agency manages the National Wildlife Refuge System, guided by the [National Wildlife Refuge System Administration Act of 1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wildlife_Refuge_System_Administration_Act_of_1966), which consists of 570 [National Wildlife Refuges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wildlife_Refuge), encompassing a full range of habitat types, including [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland), [prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie), coastal and marine areas, and temperate, [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra), and [boreal forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_forest) spread across all 50 U.S. states. It also manages thousands of small [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) and other areas covering over 150 million acres.[^cite_note-19]

### National Monuments

The agency participates in the governance of six [National Monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_monument_\(United_States\)):

- The [Hanford Reach National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanford_Reach_National_Monument) in [Washington state](13015878.md);

- The [Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papah%C4%81naumoku%C4%81kea_Marine_National_Monument), a huge maritime area in the [Northwestern Hawaiian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Hawaiian_Islands);

- The [Aleutian Islands World War II National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands_World_War_II_National_Monument) in the [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands) in Alaska;

- The [Pacific Islands Heritage Marine National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Heritage_Marine_National_Monument), the largest [marine protected area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_protected_area) in the world;

  [^cite_note-20]

- The [Rose Atoll Marine National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Atoll_Marine_National_Monument) in [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa);

- The [Marianas Trench Marine National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianas_Trench_Marine_National_Monument), which includes undersea [mud volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_volcano), vents, [chemosynthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemosynthetic) organisms, and many of the deepest points on Earth.

### Endangered species

Further information: [United States Fish and Wildlife Service list of endangered mammals and birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fish_and_Wildlife_Service_list_of_endangered_mammals_and_birds)

The agency shares responsibility for administering the [Endangered Species Act of 1973](532055.md) with the [National Marine Fisheries Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Marine_Fisheries_Service). The latter is responsible for [marine species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology), while the agency oversees [freshwater fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_fish) and all other species. The two organizations jointly manage species that inhabit both marine and non-marine environments. To manage the listing process, a [listing priority number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listing_priority_number) is assigned to candidate species to reflect the relative urgency of listing them as [threatened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threatened_species) or [endangered](20110668.md) when an immediate listing is not feasible.[^cite_note-21] The agency publishes the quarterly *Endangered Species Bulletin*.

### National Conservation Training Center

The [National Conservation Training Center](https://www.fws.gov/program/national-conservation-training-center) trains employees and partners in the accomplishment of the agency's mission. The vast majority of fish and wildlife habitats are on state or private land not controlled by the [United States government](195149.md). Therefore, the agency's [Partners for Fish and Wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partners_for_Fish_and_Wildlife) program works closely with private groups such as [Partners in Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partners_in_Flight), [National Wildlife Refuge Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wildlife_Refuge_Association), and the [Landscape conservation cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_conservation_cooperatives) to promote voluntary [habitat conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_conservation) and restoration.[^cite_note-22]

## Migratory bird program



[Biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist) with a [Canada warbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_warbler) in [Silvio O. Conte National Fish and Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_O._Conte_National_Fish_and_Wildlife_Refuge)

The **Migratory Bird Program** aims to protect and conserve bird populations and habitats. It ensures ecological sustainability, enhances opportunities for [birdwatching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdwatching) and other outdoor activities, and promotes awareness of the importance of [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration). To achieve these goals, the program utilizes resources such as the [National Wetlands Inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wetlands_Inventory) to map and monitor critical wetland habitats. The program conducts surveys, coordinates conservation partnerships, offers conservation grants, develops policies, and manages conservation laws, such as the [Migratory Bird Conservation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_Bird_Conservation_Act), educates children, and provides resources for engaging with nature and birds.[^cite_note-23]

### Federal Duck Stamp

The agency organizes the annual art competition for the [Federal Duck Stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Duck_Stamp), formally known as the **Migratory Bird Hunting and Conservation Stamp**, a [collectable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectable) adhesive stamp required for [waterfowl hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfowl_hunting). It also allows access to [National Wildlife Refuges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wildlife_Refuge) without paying an admission fee.[^cite_note-24] Since 1934, sales of Federal Duck Stamps have generated over \$1.2 billion, which has enabled the conservation of more than 6 million acres of [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat). This makes the Duck Stamp one of the most successful [wetland conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland_conservation) revenue programs in history, with over 98% of the funds directly supporting the acquisition of wetlands and conservation easements for the [National Wildlife Refuge System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wildlife_Refuge).[^cite_note-25]

## Restoring fisheries



[Bozeman National Fish Hatchery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozeman_National_Fish_Hatchery)

The agency oversees the [National Fish Hatchery System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Fish_Hatchery_System) which includes [71 fish hatcheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_fish_hatcheries_in_the_United_States) and 65 conservation offices.[^cite_note-26] Originally created to reverse declines in lake and coastal fish stocks, the program subsequently expanded its mission to include the preservation of the genes of wild and hatchery-raised fish. The fish hatcheries contribute to the restoration of native fish populations, freshwater [mussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussel), and [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian) including populations of species listed under the [Endangered Species Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_Species_Act), and providing fish to benefit [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) and [nature reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

The **National Fish Passage Program** provides financial and technical resources to projects that promote the free movement of fish and aquatic life. Common projects include [dam removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dam_removal) and [fishway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishway) construction. Between 1999 and 2023, the program has worked with over 2,000 local partners to open 61,000 mi (98,000 km) of upstream habitat by removing or bypassing 3,400 aquatic barriers.[^cite_note-29] The agency also plays a key role both in protecting sensitive [marine coastal ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_coastal_ecosystem) under the [Coastal Barrier Resources Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Barrier_Resources_Act) and in conducting evaluations of the impacts on fish and wildlife resulting from proposed [water resources](18581463.md) development projects, in accordance with the [Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_and_Wildlife_Coordination_Act).

## Law enforcement



Officer checking a [fishing license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_license)

The [Office of Law Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fish_and_Wildlife_Service_Office_of_Law_Enforcement) enforces wildlife laws such as the [Marine Mammal Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Mammal_Protection_Act), the [Migratory Bird Treaty Act of 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_Bird_Treaty_Act_of_1918), and the [Lacey Act of 1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacey_Act_of_1900). The Refuge Law Enforcement officers safeguard National Wildlife Refuges, playing a vital role in preventing [habitat destruction](1707053.md).[^cite_note-30] The office also provides training to law enforcement officers, and collaborates with [tribal partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_\(Native_American\)) to conserve wildlife resources.&#x20;

The International Affairs Program coordinates national and global initiatives to protect, restore, and enhance wildlife and habitats, with a focus on species of international concern. It fulfills obligations under international [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty), such as [CITES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES). The program collaborates with private citizens, local communities, government agencies, conservation organizations, and other stakeholders to implement treaties and laws and conserve species worldwide.[^cite_note-31]

### Fish and Wildlife Forensic Laboratory

The agency operates the [Clark R. Bavin National Fish and Wildlife Forensic Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_R._Bavin_National_Fish_and_Wildlife_Forensic_Laboratory), the only [forensics](45710.md) laboratory in the world devoted to [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife) [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency). By treaty, it also is the official crime laboratory for [CITES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES) and [Interpol](14338129.md). The laboratory identifies the [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) or [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies) of pieces, parts, or products of an animal to determine its cause of death, help wildlife officers determine if a violation of law occurred in its death, and to identify and compare physical evidence to link suspects to the crime scene and the animal's death.

## Tribal relations

Pursuant to the *[Eagle feather law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_feather_law)* and the *[Bald and Golden Eagle Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_and_Golden_Eagle_Protection_Act)*, the agency administers the [National Eagle Repository](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Eagle_Repository) and the permit system for [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) religious use of eagle feathers.[^cite_note-32] These exceptions often only apply to Native Americans that are registered with the federal government and are enrolled with a federally recognized tribe.

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, the agency began to incorporate the research of tribal scientists into conservation decisions.[^cite_note-\:2-33] This came on the heels of Native American [traditional ecological knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_ecological_knowledge) gaining acceptance in the scientific community as a reasonable and respectable way to gain knowledge of managing the natural world.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Additionally, other natural resource agencies within the United States government, such as the [United States Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture), have taken steps to be more inclusive of tribes, native people, and tribal rights.[^cite_note-36] This has marked a transition to a relationship of more co-operation rather than the tension between tribes and government agencies seen historically. Today, these agencies work closely with tribal governments to ensure the best conservation decisions are made and that tribes retain their sovereignty.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-\:2-33]

## In popular culture

[Tom Lehrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Lehrer)'s satirical song, *[Poisoning Pigeons in the Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Evening_Wasted_with_Tom_Lehrer)*, gained notoriety in 1959 for its criticism against the agency's Animal Damage Control program's cruel practice of poisoning pigeons.[^cite_note-38] This program was in 1985 transferred to the [Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture) and renamed [Wildlife Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Services).[^cite_note-39]

In the 2017 [contemporary Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_Western) crime film *[Wind River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_River_\(film\))*, [Jeremy Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Renner) plays a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service [tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracking_\(hunting\)) who teams up with an FBI agent to solve a murder in the [Wind River Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_River_Indian_Reservation).[^cite_note-40]
