The **South Florida Detention Facility**, nicknamed '**Alligator Alcatraz'**,[^cite_note-Borresen_08222025-2][^cite_note-3] was an [immigration detention](27272402.md) facility located at [Dade-Collier Training and Transition Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dade-Collier_Training_and_Transition_Airport) inside [Big Cypress National Preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Cypress_National_Preserve) in [Ochopee, Florida](6275852.md), United States.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The [Florida Division of Emergency Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Division_of_Emergency_Management) detained non-citizens under [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) authority from July 2025 to June 2026[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] at an estimated cost of at least US\$1.4 billion in unnegotiated no-bid contracts.[^cite_note-Caputo_09042026-8]

The facility received significant media attention during the summer of 2025 due to its moniker, unique remote environment, political marketing effort, and references to concentration camps.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Ceballos_07012025-11][^cite_note-Closing-12] The [Governor of Florida](233840.md), [Ron DeSantis](36729527.md) invoked a standing immigration "[state of emergency](298608.md)" to seize a county-owned airfield and fast-track construction[^cite_note-13] without the usual environmental reviews or the traditional collaboration with local officials.[^cite_note-14] On July 1, 2025, President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and DHS Secretary [Kristi Noem](27663933.md) toured the facility for its opening.[^cite_note-NBC_News-15] In June 2026, all detainees had been transferred to other facilities and the vendors requested to vacate the camp,[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-17] before evaluation of the environmental impact.[^cite_note-Bayles08122026-18]

Criticism and lawsuits continue for the desecration of ancestral lands,[^cite_note-19] inhumane conditions faced by detainees,[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-MH10Jul-21][^cite_note-22] its environmental impact,[^cite_note-AP28Jun-23][^cite_note-AP-20250806-24][^cite_note-25] lack of [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process) or [order of removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removal_proceedings),[^cite_note-auto4-26] medical neglect,[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-auto9-28] costly missteps,[^cite_note-watchdog-29][^cite_note-30] funding,[^cite_note-auto7-31] and removing people to a third country without the third country's approval.[^cite_note-32] [Human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_United_States) organizations and legal experts say the camp did not meet the minimum standards of human treatment required for persons detained under international law due to the declared absence of sanitary facilities, medical care and access to legal advice with potential violations of the [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Civil_and_Political_Rights) (ICCPR) and the [United Nations Convention Against Torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_Against_Torture) (UNCAT).[^cite_note-33] An OIG report released in September 11, 2026 by the Inspector General of the Department of Homeland Security Office revealed widespread violations of detention standards, severe overcrowding, and unprecedented punitive practices, including small outdoor metal enclosures, across [immigration detention](27272402.md) sites in South Florida, including the Everglades camp.[^cite_note-Shockey_09142026-34] This OIG and other reports indicate that agents routinely placed people in outdoor cages the size of a telephone booth.[^cite_note-35]

## Location

Located in [the Everglades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Everglades), the [Big Cypress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Cypress) area was proposed to become the site of a new Miami Jetport and construction began in 1968 as [Everglades Jetport.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dade-Collier_Training_and_Transition_Airport) In 1969, [Marjory Stoneman Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjory_Stoneman_Douglas), author of *[The Everglades: River of Grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Everglades:_River_of_Grass)*, founded [Friends of the Everglades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Everglades). Construction was halted in 1970 due to efforts of Native Americans including [Buffalo Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Tiger), hunters, [environmental concerns related to the Big Cypress Swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Impact_of_the_Big_Cypress_Swamp_Jetport) and the cancellation of the [2707 Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_2707) program. By that point, one 10,500-foot (3,200 m) runway was built, the facility occupying 39 square miles (100 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-freeman-36]

Following continued efforts to federally protect the area, Big Cypress National Preserve became one of the first [National preserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_preserves) in the [United States National Park System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_areas_in_the_National_Park_System_of_the_United_States) on October 11, 1974.[^cite_note-nps-37] According to [Miami-Dade County Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County_Mayor) [Daniella Levine Cava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniella_Levine_Cava), since 2019, the federal and state governments had previously invested well over \$10 billion in Everglades restoration and protection.[^cite_note-AP-20250806-24] The Big Cypress National Preserve provides the [Miccosukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miccosukee), [Seminole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole) and Traditional people with permanent rights to occupy and use the land in traditional ways. In addition, they have first rights to develop income-producing businesses related to the resources and use of the preserve, such as guided tours.[^cite_note-38]

Big Cypress National Preserve was designated a [DarkSky International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DarkSky_International) park in 2016; the nation's first preserve to achieve "dark sky" status.[^cite_note-39]

During his [second tenure](78298108.md), President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and his administration pursued what Trump called for "huge camps"[^cite_note-Graziosi-40] where migrants would be held prior to [deportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_and_removal_from_the_United_States).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-Brownstein-42]

### Opening

On June 19, 2025, the "Alligator Alcatraz" was announced, which sources later officially named the "South Florida Detention Facility".[^cite_note-Borresen_08222025-2][^cite_note-43] According to the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement website, the official name is the "Florida Soft Sided Facility South".[^cite_note-44] Signs outside the camp indicated "Alligator Alcatraz" as the name.[^cite_note-Bridges_07082025-45][^cite_note-46]

On its opening on July 1, 2025, U.S. President Donald Trump and Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem joined DeSantis and other Florida state leaders. Trump said the compound "might be as good as the real Alcatraz," adding it's "a little controversial, but I couldn't care less."[^cite_note-NBC_News-15] Desantis repeated in July 2025 that all detainees had a final removal order, which Eunice Cho of the ACLU stated is "clearly inaccurate."[^cite_note-auto6-47]

### Moniker

The moniker alludes to both the local [American alligator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_alligator) population and the former maximum-security [Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcatraz_Federal_Penitentiary). In American slang, [alligator bait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alligator_bait) or gator bait is a [racial slur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_slur) for African Americans, implying they are worthless and expendable. Whatever the intentions, the moniker echoes a long tradition of using alligators in ways that have reinforced racist imagery and dehumanizing stereotypes according to Historian Mark V. Barrow, Jr. of [Virginia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Tech).[^cite_note-48] In 1919 the Florida Department of Agriculture and Florida State Live Stock Association explicitly discouraged any references to "alligators lying in wait for [pickaninnies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickaninny)", describing them as harmful for the image of the state and against the commercial interests of its citizens.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHughes2017-49] In 2020, the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida) decided to end the ["Gator Bait" chant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gator_Bait_chant) during athletic events due to "horrific racist imagery" associated with the phrase.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVan_Ness2020-50] The high-security Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary housed convicted criminals and was by far the most expensive prison in the United States, and many still perceived it as America’s most extreme jail.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDunbar199914-51]

DeSantis said the airstrip would serve as a "one-stop" shop using the on-site runway[^cite_note-Lowery_102425-52] for deportation and qualified National Guard members working as immigration judges. Data from the *Miami Herald* and *Tampa Bay Times* show it was used as a holding pen and transfer hub for immigrants still fighting their cases in immigration courts.[^cite_note-53] State officials said deportation flights began immediately after opening with a “surge” of immigration arrests, an ICE spokesperson answering, “Fire up the deportation planes.” [^cite_note-54] Flights included shuffle flights (in between US facilities), removal flights to a foreign country, and removal related flights (fuel stops and connections), with 50 such flights in 2025 and 108 flights between January and May 2026.[^cite_note-55] Shuffle flights increased in June 2026.[^cite_note-56] Court documents note deportations from the center included noncitizens of Mexico transported to the Mexican border, although Mexico refused their legal entry.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

### Closure

On June 16, 2026, DHS officials indicated that all detainees had been transferred to undisclosed locations.[^cite_note-59] Florida Emergency Management Director Kevin Guthrie instructed vendors to remove tents, fences, trailers and other structures from the site, thus initiating a "full demobilization" of the facility[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-\:0-16] and leaving 20 acres of asphalt added without permit to the original 35 acres.[^cite_note-Ryan_071726-61][^cite_note-Lowery_102425-52] Following this declaration, Miami-Dade Mayor Daniella Levine Cava said her administration would explore transferring county-owned land at the site to the [National Park Service](161535.md) or other Everglades restoration partners.[^cite_note-62] In July 2026, a Federal judge requested proof from DeSantis that the camp was closed in the context of a lawsuit brought by the American Civil Liberties Union's National Prison Project.[^cite_note-Mayet_071626-63]

Alligator Alcatraz is like [(Nazi) Germany](21212.md) in 1939, updated with 2026 rules."[^cite_note-89] [South African](17416221.md) of TV reality show and former lawyer, Peet Viljoen, who previously praised Trump and echoed claims about so-called “[white genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_genocide_conspiracy_theory)” in South Africa, was detained on a charge of aggravated grand retail theft and later transferred to the [California City Detention Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_City_Correctional_Facility).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

In October 2025, The *Miami Herald*,[^cite_note-92] as well as detainees' family and lawyers,[^cite_note-93] reported that the whereabouts of about two-thirds of over 1,800 detainees held at the facility in July could not be determined.[^cite_note-guardian-94] The average stay of the 21,000 persons detained on site was 16 days.[^cite_note-auto2-95]

## Reports of inhumane treatment

Detainees reported harsh conditions at the facility, citing limited access to water, insufficient food, and restrictions on the practice of their religion.[^cite_note-105] Detainees have described unsanitary conditions, including wastewater overflows and insect infestations, as well as inadequate access to medical care.[^cite_note-106] The area on which the facility is located is also subject to frequent bouts of extreme weather, including [yearly hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Florida_hurricanes_\(2000%E2%80%93present\)), heavy rainfall, and high heat.[^cite_note-107]

On July 21, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* described the facility, as well as Krome and a Miami jail, as being at the center of "a succession of alleged abuses at jails operated by Immigration and Customs Enforcement Agency (ICE) in the state since January, chronicled by the advocacy groups [Human Rights Watch](46530.md), Americans for Immigrant Justice, and Sanctuary of the South from interviews with detainees".[^cite_note-108] A month later, the outlet described reports of inhumane treatment and brutality at the camp as being commonplace.[^cite_note-Luscombe_08292025-109]

On July 22, people detained on site began a [hunger strike](222292.md) to protest what they consider to be inhumane and dangerous living conditions.[^cite_note-110] Some detainees reported maggots in the food and having to "dig the fecal matter out of the toilets with their bare hands" for lack of plumbing. The Florida Division of Emergency Management has denied the claims, but no independent inspections have been allowed.[^cite_note-111]

On August 29, three detainees said an uprising occurred at the facility in phone calls to Miami's Spanish language news channel Noticias 23. The incident allegedly occurred after several detainees shouted "freedom" after one received news a relative had died. The detainees described guards as indiscriminately beating detainees with batons and firing tear gas. The Florida Division of Emergency Management denied reports that the events had occurred.[^cite_note-Luscombe_08292025-109]

In December 2025, the report "Torture and Enforced Disappearances in the Sunshine State: Human Rights Violations at 'Alligator Alcatraz' and Krome in Florida" published by [Amnesty International](18947898.md) concluded that the camp's conditions, including routine and prolonged use of shackles and retention in a ["box"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_\(torture\)) described as a 2x2 foot cage-like structure "constitutes [torture](47702.md)".[^cite_note-auto3-112]

In 2026, an investigation over reports of abuse at the [Everglades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everglades) tent facility was filed by [U.S. Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senators) [Jon Ossoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Ossoff) of [Georgia](48830.md) and [Dick Durbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Durbin) of [Illinois](14849.md), focusing on allegations of [torture](47702.md).[^cite_note-113] After an unannounced visit in April 2026, Congresswoman [Debbie Wasserman Schultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Wasserman_Schultz) stated, everything about this detention camp "screams inhumane and unnecessary."[^cite_note-114] Controversy continues related to possible federal funding[^cite_note-115] for this first state-run facility within the context of [immigration to the United States](15051.md) and [deaths during ICE detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deaths_in_ICE_detention).[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

The *Miami Herald's* review of one year of emergency calls to 911 by concerned family and friends detail a lack of medical attention within the camp and a lack of communication about medical emergencies.[^cite_note-auto9-28]

In September 2026, a DHS Office of Inspector General report said the site used "small metal enclosures" covering 18 square feet that presented "significant risks" to detainee's health and safety as "calming areas."[^cite_note-118]

## Construction and operational costs

The total cost for the facility before dismantlement was estimated in June 2026 by *CBS News Miami* to be \$1.2 billion or over \$3,500 a day per detainee.[^cite_note-auto2-95] According to the [United States Government Accountability Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Government_Accountability_Office), ICE neglected standard practice by not entering into a contract or intergovernmental service agreement or negotiate costs with the state of Florida for the camp, leading to high operating costs.[^cite_note-watchdog-29] Florida officials initially announced a cost of US\$450 million a year to operate.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

Documents requested by Second Judicial Circuit Judge Angela Dempsey in March 2026 showed Florida had spent more than \$1.2 million per day to run the camp, with a daily [burn rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_rate) of \$3 million a day during its earliest weeks.[^cite_note-auto1-121] In July 2026, *CBS News Miami* calculated the average daily cost for the camp at \$3.4 million.[^cite_note-122]

Private companies[^cite_note-Ellenbogen-123][^cite_note-Bob-Norman-124] set up the facility and the [Florida National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_National_Guard) and private companies were deployed to secure the site.[^cite_note-TBT28Jun-125][^cite_note-Trump-126] The Florida Division of Emergency Management and the governor's office bypassed procurement and competitive bidding rules and selected IRG Global Emergency Management, who had given \$10,000 to Florida's Republican Party on June 24, 2025, for a \$1.1 million contract for "operational support services in support of migration efforts in the State," followed by two more contracts with Florida, totaling over \$5 million, for site shuttles, armory systems, on-site emergency services and air operations at the Ochopee site.[^cite_note-Ellenbogen-123] IRG is an offshoot of Access Restoration Services US, Inc., a major campaign donor to DeSantis and totaling nearly \$400,000 in donations to Republican coffers.[^cite_note-Bob-Norman-124] The Critical Response Strategies (CRS) company initially received a \$78 million no-bid contract from Florida emergency funds for staffing and some communications with lawyers.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

In early August 2025, the [Federal Emergency Management Agency](73797.md) confirmed reception of a grant application from state officials, later evaluated as a US\$1.49 billion grant.[^cite_note-auto1-121] In August 15, federal officials provided the Florida Division of Emergency Management (FDEM) with instructions related to the use of US\$608 million funds.[^cite_note-129] As of August 29, 2025, the State of Florida had incurred \$218 million of [sunk costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunk_costs) for the construction of the facility.[^cite_note-130] Its legality has been questioned by a coalition of 67 members of [Congress](31756.md), led by [Jeff Merkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Merkley) and Debbie Wasserman Schultz.[^cite_note-131]

In August 2025, U.S. district judge [Kathleen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_M._Williams) granted a [preliminary injunction](39343.md) halting construction and prohibiting the government from transferring any additional detainees to the site, which was later stayed by the Court of Appeals for the 11th Circuit allowing continued operations.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-guardian-94]

On December 30, 2025, Trump vetoed the bipartisan Miccosukee Reserved Area Act citing the immigration detention facility. The act would have transferred 30 acres of land in the Everglades to control by the [Miccosukee Tribe of Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miccosukee_Tribe_of_Indians).

State records show that \$34 million in public dollars had been spent on technology and IT support by April 2026, when the Florida Division of Emergency Management said that adding phones as required by a judge, would cost \$180,000 up front plus \$6,000 weekly. The DEM also reported having spent millions of dollars in legal fees statewide for immigration detention over three years and hundreds of thousands of dollars on private jet flights to and from the facility and food products.[^cite_note-133]

In February 2026, Florida House Republicans, led by Griff Griffits of [Panama City Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City_Beach), introduced a successful bill, supported by Democrats, that prohibits the use of Florida emergency funding for the camp, reserving the funds only for [natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disasters).[^cite_note-auto7-31] According to Transparency Florida, a government accountability website, FDEM filed a budget amendment May 6 notifying payments of \$45.3 million out of the Emergency Preparedness and Response Fund for invoices of the 2025-2026 year, before the bill passed in the Legislature. In total, FDEM has spent \$458.5 million in emergency funds on immigration enforcement in the past financial year.[^cite_note-134]

On May 7, 2026, *The New York Times* reported that federal and state officials were debating whether to close the facility owing to its high operational costs. It reported that a promised reimbursement worth \$608 million by the federal government for Florida to run the facility for a year had not been received by Florida officials.[^cite_note-Closing-12][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

According to interviews in May 2026 by the [The San Juan Daily Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_San_Juan_Daily_Star) newspaper, some private vendors hired by the state for the site's operations have been struggling to front costs as some invoices have not been paid in over 200 days.[^cite_note-137] In September 2026, the DeSantis administration had paid out nearly \$630 million to contractors, with about \$812 million in outstanding balances.[^cite_note-Caputo_09042026-8]

## Legal challenges

### Native American tribal rights

Miccosukee Tribe Chairman Talbert Cypress testified in a recent lawsuit that this is not the first time the tribe has had to [fight for its land and rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole_Wars), affirming, "we will always stand up for our culture, our sovereignty, and for the Everglades"[^cite_note-138] There are 15 remaining traditional Miccosukee and Seminole villages, recognised as [Indigenous people of the Everglades region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people_of_the_Everglades_region), in Big Cypress, as well as ceremonial and burial grounds and other gathering sites, Cypress testified before Congress in 2024."We live here. Our ancestors fought and died here. They are buried here," he said. "The Big Cypress is part of us, and we are a part of it."[^cite_note-139] The [Seminole Tribe of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole_Tribe_of_Florida) was likewise in opposition citing sacred lands.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

Republican Representative [Carlos Gimenez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Gim%C3%A9nez) of Florida sponsored a bill in July 2025 which would have transferred 30 acres of land in the Everglades to control by the [Miccosukee Tribe of Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miccosukee_Tribe_of_Indians) in order to avert "catastrophic flooding" and referring to the Miccosukee Tribe as "stewards of the Everglades." The Act reflected years of "bipartisan work and was intended to clarify land status and support basic protections for tribal members who have lived in this area for generations," wrote Chairman Cypress. According to Kevin Washburn, a law professor at [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_Berkeley) Law, "It is rare for an administration to veto a bill for reasons wholly unrelated to the merits of the bill." Washburn added that while denying land return to a tribe is a political act, Trump's move is "highly unusual."[^cite_note-142] On December 30, 2025, Trump vetoed the bipartisan Miccosukee Reserved Area Act citing the immigration detention facility.

The facility casts bright lights across sacred tribal lands where the Miccosukee people have conducted their annual [Green Corn Ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Corn_Ceremony) for many generations. The intense illumination disrupts the tribe's spiritual practices and the delicate nocturnal ecosystem that depends on natural light cycles.[^cite_note-143]

### Environmental challenge and injunction

In June 2025, [Betty Osceola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Osceola), a Miccosukee tribal judge and member of the Everglades Advisory Committee, organized Indigenous-led prayer gatherings and public demonstrations to highlight environmental and cultural concerns. Talbert Cypress, chairman of the [Miccosukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miccosukee) Business Council, noted that no environmental impact research had been done and that some Native villages were within 900 feet (270 m) of the camp's entrance.[^cite_note-144] On June 23, 2025, [Miami-Dade County Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County_Mayor) [Daniella Levine Cava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniella_Levine_Cava) wrote to the Florida Division and stated, "With the federal and state government investing well over \$10 billion since 2019 in Everglades restoration and protection, we would appreciate a detailed analysis and report on environmental impacts of this facility to the Everglades."[^cite_note-AP-20250806-24]

On June 27, 2025, a coalition led by [Friends of the Everglades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Everglades), the [Center for Biological Diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Biological_Diversity), and the [Miccosukee Tribe of Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miccosukee_Tribe_of_Indians), represented by [Earthjustice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthjustice) and attorney Paul Schwiep, filed suit in a federal court seeking an injunction until a full environmental review and public-comment period are completed.[^cite_note-APLawsuit-145] Plaintiffs argue that the project threatens the habitat of endangered species, including that of the [Florida panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_panther) and the [Florida bonneted bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_bonneted_bat), and violates both the [National Environmental Policy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Environmental_Policy_Act) (NEPA) and tribal cultural-resource protections.[^cite_note-146]

On August 7, 2025, U.S. District Judge [Kathleen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_M._Williams) temporarily halted construction at the facility for two weeks, while she considered if the detention center violates environmental laws. Witnesses testified that 20 acres (8 hectares) of new asphalt had been laid, while temporary tents, trailers, and other heavy equipment were at the airport.[^cite_note-147] Located in tropical wetlands, its infrastructure and sewage may be sources of both [water pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution) and [light pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_pollution).

Judge Williams later granted a preliminary injunction on August 21 that prohibits the government from transferring any additional detainees to the site or performing any more construction work. She also ordered the Trump administration to remove temporary fencing, industrial lighting, generators, sewage and waste receptacles from the site within 60 days.[^cite_note-148] Uthmeier stated that they plan to keep the ICE facility running, despite the court order.[^cite_note-149]

The [U.S. Court of Appeals for the 11th Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Court_of_Appeals_for_the_11th_Circuit), in a 2–1 decision early September, first stayed the injunction pending appeal, stating that because the federal government had yet to reimburse Florida's costs, federal law (the NEPA, which requires an environmental impact study) did not apply.[^cite_note-150] The State's request for federal reimbursement had been submitted on August 7, 2025, which it did not inform the courts of.[^cite_note-151] The Appellate Court granted a second stay, citing [the government shutdown](81225965.md).[^cite_note-152] On August 14, Friends of the Everglades filed suit to access Florida's undisclosed records of government financing for the camp under the [Government in the Sunshine Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_in_the_Sunshine_Act).[^cite_note-153] In April 2026, the US Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit ruled that the federal government could skip environmental review as the facility, considering it to be not "federally controlled", making it the first circuit court to rule on amendments Congress added to the [National Environmental Policy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Environmental_Policy_Act) in 2023.[^cite_note-154]

In March 2026, a state-commissioned environmental assessment report raised concerns about emissions of air pollutants exceeding regulatory thresholds of the [Clean Air Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Air_Act_\(United_States\)), including [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide) and [particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter) from vehicle use and continuously running generators.[^cite_note-155] On May 26, 2026, the Center for Biological Diversity filed a lawsuit for what it terms as "substantial, unpermitted pollution from diesel generators and other air-polluting equipment", that could lead to civil penalties for Florida of up to \$124,426 per day of violation. ### Civil liberties challenges

On July 16, 2025, the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union), the ACLU of Florida, and Americans for Immigrant Justice filed a [class action](7200.md) suit claiming the [Trump administration](78298108.md) violates the [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment) and [Fifth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) rights of people being detained, as well as the First Amendment rights of legal service organizations and law firms with clients held at the facility.[^cite_note-157] The civil rights groups' lawsuit alleges detainees are [being held without charges and are not being given access to their attorneys](13435705.md).[^cite_note-158] As part of this lawsuit that also contests the federal cancellation of bond hearings, [U.S. District Judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._District_Judge) [Rodolfo Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Ruiz) has requested all written agreements and contracts showing who has legal custody of the hundreds of detainees.[^cite_note-159]

Immigration attorneys described the facility's alternate system as a place "where the normal rules don't apply." Gov. Ron DeSantis falsely stated on July 25 that "Everybody here is already on a final removal order," a lie repeated by his director of emergency management, Kevin Guthrie.[^cite_note-auto5-160] Internal data show that the vast majority of detainees did not have final orders of removal from a judge before entering the facility (nearly 70% did not[^cite_note-auto5-160]), but they may have been deported as the facility's population fell to below 400 late August.[^cite_note-161]

In January 2026, a former detainee from the camp deported to [Colombia](5222.md) and a second who had requested asylum was deported to [Haiti](13373.md) both testified before a District judge that the camp agents did not respect their right to legal counsel guaranteed by the [Sixth Amendment to the United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution).[^cite_note-162] In April, lawyer Katherine Blankenship accused state and federal officials of failing to comply with a federal judge's preliminary injunction allowing detainees to phone their lawyer. Blankenship also declared that camp guards severely beat and [pepper-sprayed](157302.md) detainees.[^cite_note-163]

## Public response

Critics compare the facility to [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps), referring to it as "Alligator [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz)"[^cite_note-164] while others situate it within American concentration camps, including the [internment of German Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_German_Americans), the [internment of Italian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Italian_Americans), and the [internment of Japanese Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Americans)[^cite_note-New_Republic-165] at [Manzanar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manzanar) and elsewhere. Members of the [Republican Party](32070.md), including DeSantis,[^cite_note-166] have defended the facility, arguing it will help the [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement) (ICE) to cope with [the deportation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_in_the_second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump) of [the federal government](195149.md).[^cite_note-167] The camp has been labeled a "[black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole)" following the irregular disappearance of detainees.[^cite_note-168] [Alex Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Padilla), [Dick Durbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Durbin) and other Democratic U.S. Senators mentioned the camp in their open letter about the increase in deaths during ICE detention.[^cite_note-169]

At a weekly vigil at the camp entrance on September 21, Amy Fischer, Director of Refugee and Migrant Rights at [Amnesty International](18947898.md) USA, called the facility "a human rights disaster."[^cite_note-170] Amnesty International launched a campaign to shut down the site on July 23 in response to unsanitary conditions, lack of medical care, and minimal access to lawyers, considering that it "sets a dangerous precedent for how states can partner with the federal government to expand the reach of its mass detention and deportation machine."[^cite_note-171] "Amnesty International considers that detention conditions at both facilities amount to cruel, inhuman and degrading treatment. The use of prolonged solitary confinement at Krome and the use of the ['box'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_\(torture\)) at "Alligator Alcatraz" amount to torture or other ill-treatment."[^cite_note-auto3-112]

## Political responses

After the facility's announcement, the [Republican Party of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Florida) marketed "Alligator Alcatraz" merchandise, including hats, shirts, and koozies, the state of Florida handed out merchandise to conservative influencers, and Attorney General James Uthmeier sold similar items to raise election cash.[^cite_note-Ceballos_07012025-11] Critics found the fundraising pitch cruel and inaccurate as hundreds of people being held there are not facing any criminal charges and the facility could be described as a [concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camp).[^cite_note-172] Florida state Representative [Angela Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Nixon) called it a "modern-day concentration camp", explaining, “Donald Trump‘s blueprint for America has now become barbed wire and broken families.”[^cite_note-173]

On July 2, [Janelle Bynum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janelle_Bynum), [Maxwell Alejandro Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_Alejandro_Frost) and 22 other members of Congress wrote to Noem deploring that "detainees will be kept in tents with inadequate sanitation facilities and will face unbearable living conditions," including "exposure to deadly pathogens, constant threats from unpredictable flooding and extreme weather events, and daily temperatures averaging 90 degrees, with a heat index often over 100 degrees Fahrenheit."[^cite_note-174]

In July, [state Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_House_of_Representatives) [Anna Eskamani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Eskamani) and [state Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Senate) [Shevrin Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shevrin_Jones) joined lawmakers in suing DeSantis in order to gain access to the site. After their visit, they deplored the sanitary conditions, with Eskamani stating that the detention camp is "a political stunt with environmental damage and everyday lives being harmed. It needs to close immediately."[^cite_note-175]

Following an on-site visit on July 12, Representative [Debbie Wasserman Schultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Wasserman_Schultz) described the detainees as "essentially packed into cages, wall-to-wall humans, 32 detainees per cage". Congressional and state lawmakers on-site reportedly heard cries of *libertad*, meaning "freedom" in Spanish, from detainees. Lawmakers were not permitted to view the entire facility.[^cite_note-176]

Representative [Dan Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Goldman) of [New York, where ICE is a contentious topic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE_and_New_York_City_during_the_second_Trump_presidency), aided in the release of some 30 people on May 6, 2026, including at least one detained in the Everglades, stating, "our immigration system is being weaponized against those simply trying to follow the rules" and that he will "continue to use every tool in Congress to fight back and push for true accountability and reform."[^cite_note-177]

### Public opinion

Polling on July 4, 2025, by [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) found that 48% of Americans polled opposed the detention center, with 33% supporting it and 18% unsure, while 53% of independents polled opposed the facility.[^cite_note-178] In their poll on July 20, 28% of women and 42% of men approved the camp.[^cite_note-179] A poll among Florida voters from July 25 to 27 found that 34% of Floridians held a favorable opinion and 59% saying they disapprove of using the state of Florida's taxpayer-funded Emergency Preparedness and Response Fund to cover costs.[^cite_note-180]
