The **United States Semiquincentennial**[^cite_note-half-fivename-3] was the 250th [anniversary](477069.md) of the [United States Declaration of Independence](31874.md). Festivities marked various events leading up to the 250th [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)), July 4, 2026.&#x20;

Official festivities include [commemorative coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_coinage), [UFC Freedom 250](80931969.md) at the [White House](33057.md), the Great American State Fair on the [National Mall](167796.md), [Sail250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail250), a [Times Square Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball) drop event, the [Patriot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_Games_\(event\)), the [Freedom 250 Grand Prix](82275853.md), and the world's largest-ever fireworks display (also held on the National Mall).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Part of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) was held across the United States, with matches in [Philadelphia](50585.md) and [Houston](13774.md) on the date of the semiquincentennial.

Official planning for the celebrations began in 2016 with the congressional, non-partisan [United States Semiquincentennial Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_Commission) (America250). In 2025, federal resources were diverted to the president [Donald Trump](4848272.md)–aligned [White House Task Force on Celebrating America's 250th Birthday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Task_Force_on_Celebrating_America's_250th_Birthday) (Freedom 250) to promote and plan new events. Celebrations began with the [United States Army 250th Anniversary Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_250th_Anniversary_Parade) on June 14, 2025,[^cite_note-Daher_2025-1] with "America250" events formally starting on July 3, 2025.[^cite_note-Superville_07032025-6][^cite_note-Daher_2025-1] Several official events were criticized for being [politicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politicisation) and lacking financial transparency. Many celebrations were low-attendance, with some being disrupted by a [record-breaking heat wave](83587373.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Background

The [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress), meeting in [Philadelphia](50585.md), voted for the independence of the [United Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Colonies) by passing the [Lee Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Resolution) on July 2, 1776.[^cite_note-9] The [Declaration of Independence](31874.md), mainly written by [Committee of Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Five) member [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson), was proclaimed on July 4, the date on which the [anniversary of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)) is observed.[^cite_note-10]

There were no major government-sponsored 50th anniversary observances on July 4, 1826, which was the day that [Founding Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States) and former presidents [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams) and Thomas Jefferson both died. In 1876, the United States organized nationwide centennial observances centered on the [Centennial Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_Exposition) in Philadelphia,[^cite_note-Bykofsky-2017-11] in 1926, a [Sesquicentennial Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesquicentennial_Exposition) was held in Philadelphia, and in 1976, [United States Bicentennial observances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bicentennial) were held throughout the country.[^cite_note-Bykofsky-2017-11]

## Organization



Flag of United States Semiquincentennial









Federal legislation directs that semiquincentennial events receive special focus in the cities of [Boston](24437894.md), [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina), and [New York](645042.md) (*pictured, clockwise from top left*).

The United States Semiquincentennial Commission Act of 2016 directed the [United States government](195149.md) to issue [commemorative coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_coinage) and [postage stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps), and commission appropriately named [naval vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_vessels), in advance of the semiquincentennial.[^cite_note-12] In addition, specific activities—both officially organized and independently created—were planned. The legislation specifically directed the organization of events "in locations of historical significance to the United States" going on to list Boston, Philadelphia, New York City, and Charleston, South Carolina, as "leading cities".[^cite_note-13]

The Circulating Collectible Coin Redesign Act of 2020 allowed the [United States Mint](358818.md) to redesign any coins in 2026.[^cite_note-14] It called for a series of five designs for the [quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_\(United_States_coin\)), including one depicting women's contributions to independence.[^cite_note-15] 2025 changes by the [second Trump administration](78298108.md) ignored recommendations by the [Citizens Coinage Advisory Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Coinage_Advisory_Committee) and removed coins celebrating abolition, women's suffrage and the civil rights movement as part of a campaign against "[wokeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke)".[^cite_note-16]

America's Potluck was a nationwide communal meal event on July 5, 2026, the day after the semiquincentennial. Organized by the America250 Utah Commission and adopted across all 50 states and [Puerto Rico](23041.md),[^cite_note-17] the initiative invited neighbors, families and community groups to host or attend [potluck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potluck) meals to celebrate the anniversary.[^cite_note-utah-18][^cite_note-19]

Plans for the casting of a new public bell are being coordinated by the [National Bell Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bell_Festival). The bell will honor 250 years of women's contributions to U.S. history,[^cite_note-20] and will lead nationwide ringing tributes during the celebrations.[^cite_note-21]



The [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) was [signed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signing_of_the_United_States_Declaration_of_Independence) in [Independence Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Hall) in Philadelphia in 1776.

Celebrations were organized by two main organizations at the federal level: the non-partisan congressional [America250 Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_Commission) created in 2016 and backed by a nonprofit; and the [White House Task Force on Celebrating America's 250th Birthday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Task_Force_on_Celebrating_America's_250th_Birthday), chaired by [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and established via an executive order in 2025.[^cite_note-Daher_2025-1]

The America 250 Civics Education Coalition developed civics education programming to celebrate the semiquincentennial. The nonprofit [America First Policy Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_First_Policy_Institute) led the coalition of more than 40 conservative organizations, including [Turning Point USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Point_USA), the [Heritage Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_Foundation), and [Hillsdale College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsdale_College), with oversight from the [Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Education).[^cite_note-22]

In 2011, the non-profit organization USA250 was established in [Philadelphia](50585.md) to lobby for federal government support of the United States semiquincentennial and establish Philadelphia as the host city for events surrounding the semiquincentennial observances. In 2014, the [Philadelphia City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_City_Council) ordered a public hearing of the Committee on Parks, Recreation, and Cultural Affairs to investigate "the impact and feasibility of Philadelphia" hosting the United States Semiquincentennial in 2026, among other events.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The [United States Semiquincentennial Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_Commission) was subsequently established in July 2016,[^cite_note-26] and signed by president of the United States, [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama).[^cite_note-Lawrence-2018-27] The act was amended in December 2020,[^cite_note-28] to allow the commission to accept congressional funding, to add a justice of the Supreme Court to the commission, and to make additional technical changes.[^cite_note-29]

On November 15, 2017, the [United States Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior) issued a [request for proposals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Request_for_proposal) seeking a non-profit corporation to act as secretariat to the commission and lead a nationwide organization of observances.[^cite_note-30] The [American Battlefield Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Battlefield_Trust) was named the commission's non-profit partner to serve as Administrative Secretariat, tasked with preparing reports for Congress and helping raise funds for the anniversary observances. The trust "has distinguished itself in fundraising and managing high-profile commemorative events, and that expertise will be invaluable to the U.S.A. 250th Commemoration planning efforts," said then-[secretary of the interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Interior) [Ryan Zinke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Zinke).[^cite_note-31]

Daniel DiLella, CEO and President of Equus, a leading private equity real estate fund, was appointed Chairperson of the [Semiquincentennial Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiquincentennial_Commission) in April 2018, leading a 32-member body composed of members of Congress, private citizens, and federal officials.[^cite_note-32] The commission was tasked with developing a report containing recommendations for the president and Congress within the first two years of its formation. The commission will observe and commemorate not only the Revolution, but also the full history of the U.S. leading up to the 250th Anniversary.

On November 16, 2018, the 33 members of the United States Semiquincentennial were sworn in at Independence Hall in Philadelphia and convened their first organizing meeting to begin eight years of planning and organizing for the 250th national birthday celebration. Dilella estimated that the group would meet three or four times a year.[^cite_note-ABT-2018-33] The new commission consisted of 16 private citizens, including chairman DiLella; eight members of Congress and nine federal officials.[^cite_note-34] The day before the meeting, the [Daughters of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughters_of_the_American_Revolution), in partnership with the commission, held a tree-planting ceremony to introduce its Pathway of the Patriots along the Schuylkill River Trail. Project organizers expect to plant 250 new trees along the trail from the city to Valley Forge.[^cite_note-ABT-2018-33] After being named the non-profit partner in October 2018, the [American Battlefield Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Battlefield_Trust) provided financial and staff support to get the commission operational as well as initial legal support.[^cite_note-35]

In July 2021, the America250 Foundation made public its website as well as its marketing campaign, which initially consisted of advertisements in more than 4,559 locations in the four "leading cities," as well as a public service announcement aired on [Comcast NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast_NBCUniversal) networks that summer. Advertisements were displayed "\[from] [Times Square](31389.md) and the [Lincoln Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Tunnel) in New York to the [Walt Whitman Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Whitman_Bridge) in Philadelphia," with original content also being published on the campaign's social media accounts.[^cite_note-36]

In July 2022, President [Joe Biden](145422.md) designated former treasurer of the United States [Rosie Rios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosie_Rios) as chair of the U.S. Semiquincentennial Commission. Rios, who replaced Dan DiLella, was first appointed to the commission on January 11, 2018. DiLella will continue to serve as a commissioner.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] On October 3, 2022, Rios announced that CEO Joe Daniels had resigned. Daniels, who came from the [9/11 Memorial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_Memorial_Museum) in New York City, had served as America 250 CEO for one year.[^cite_note-39]

On August 1, 2024, the Semiquincentennial Commission announced that former presidents [George W. Bush](3414021.md) and [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama), as well as their spouses, former first ladies [Laura Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bush) and [Michelle Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Obama), will serve as the commission's honorary national co-chairs.[^cite_note-40]

By 2025, Trump was criticized for taking more control over the non-partisan congressional America250 organization. He installed [Fox News](11121.md) producer Ariel Abergel and Trump campaign officials as leaders, dropped contractors with ties to Democrats, partnered with conservative organization [PragerU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PragerU) to provide educational materials for festivities, and had America250 partner with a cryptocurrency event featuring his two sons.[^cite_note-Schuessler_07042025-41] The committee also partnered with conservative organization [Moms for Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moms_for_Liberty), and allowed its "America250" branding to be used at Trump rallies.[^cite_note-Scherer_08062025-42] Giant banners with Trump's face and "America250" branding were hung alongside [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) at the Department of Labor headquarters in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-43] A letter to Rios and Abergel by all of the Democratic lawmakers on the commission wrote that: "Unfortunately, recent America250 funded and branded events have been partisan and have not served America250's purpose and mission of uniting us as a nation."[^cite_note-Blackburn_1062025-44] In September 2025, the commission fired Abergel, with sources telling *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* that he had attempted to steer the direction of programming towards honoring Trump.[^cite_note-45]

By the end of 2025, \$10 million worth of funding towards America250 was diverted to Freedom 250's "Freedom Trucks". In early 2026, Representative [Bonnie Watson Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_Watson_Coleman) stated that America250 had only received \$25 million of its congressionally appropriated \$150 million, and was concerned the remainder would be reallocated to the Trump-aligned Freedom 250.[^cite_note-Vogel_02082026-46] The 250 Commission stated that it had enough money to continue with its original programming, which include "America's Field Trip" asking students across the country what America means to them, and the national volunteering effort "America Gives".[^cite_note-Fields_02102026-47]

### White House Task Force 250 / Freedom 250

Main article: [White House Task Force on Celebrating America's 250th Birthday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Task_Force_on_Celebrating_America's_250th_Birthday)

During his [2024 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign), [Donald Trump](4848272.md) pledged to throw a "spectacular birthday party" to honor the anniversary, by assembling a White House task force named "White House Task Force on Celebrating America's 250th Birthday".[^cite_note-48] The Task Force is separate from the bipartisan United States Semiquincentennial Commission with the White House-led "Task Force 250" chaired by Trump and his political appointees, and is described as more overtly political.[^cite_note-Bennett_11102025-49] Its funding arm, "Freedom 250", assisted Task Force 250's execution of Trump's proposals.[^cite_note-Waddick_12192025-50] In 2025, internal documents from the Interior Department instructed its staff to make Freedom 250 the "primary branding" for all national birthday events.[^cite_note-Vogel_02082026-46]

*The New York Times* described events held by Trump's Freedom 250 as having "lacked connection to American history", and were described as "tailored to Mr. Trump's political agenda and his penchant for spectacle, personal branding and legacy".[^cite_note-Vogel_02082026-46] The [Associated Press](18935732.md) described it as focused on "splashy events" compared to America250's focus on civic engagement and history.[^cite_note-Fields_02102026-47] Initial proposals included a "Great American State Fair" to be held in the [Iowa State Fairgrounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_Fairgrounds), featuring pavilions from all fifty states,[^cite_note-51] "Patriot Games" for high-school athletes, public monuments such as a [National Garden of American Heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Garden_of_American_Heroes), and other year-long celebrations and projects across the country.[^cite_note-52] On January 29, 2025, President Trump signed [Executive Order 14189](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_executive_orders_in_the_second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump), entitled "Celebrating America's 250th Birthday".[^cite_note-53] It also proposed a [UFC](169660.md) fight on the White House lawn.[^cite_note-Waddick_12192025-50]

Freedom 250 offered access to Trump and other benefits to donors who gave \$1 million to the organization. *The Times* described the organization as taking on a "Trumpian flare" and overshadowing years of planning work done by America250. While America250 is a stand-alone congressionally authorized nonprofit group, Freedom 250 "utilizes opaque corporate structures" and is "technically a limited liability company created by, and housed inside, the [National Park Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Park_Foundation)".[^cite_note-Vogel_02082026-46] US embassies promoted Freedom 250 and America250 to overseas donors to host lavish parties and events, with former diplomats and business executives describing an "aggressive" fundraising push that raised tens of millions of dollars. Freedom 250 spokeswoman Danielle Alvarez stated that the Freedom 250 name had been embraced across the administration, and suggested that embassies were using the name as shorthand for their own semiquincentennial initiatives not directly related to the group and that it did not receive foreign funding.[^cite_note-54]

Trump's formation of Task Force 250 was criticized by Democrats and some historians as an attempt to politicize the celebrations, with conservative author and political scientist [John J. Pitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Pitney) describing them as "an opportunity for people to curry Trump's favor" through donations. John Dichtl, president of the [American Association for State and Local History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_State_and_Local_History) criticized Task Force 250's decision to host a UFC fight on the White House lawn, writing "What does a (UFC) fight have to do with America's greatness?" A June 21 letter by Democrats on the bipartisan America250 commission questioned its relationship to the Trump-led Task Force 250 and its events.[^cite_note-Waddick_12192025-50]

In February 2026, Democratic senators launched a probe into Freedom 250's funding practices over its transparency and potential conflicts of interest.[^cite_note-55] During a February congressional hearing, Democratic Representative [Jared Huffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Huffman) accused the Trump administration of using Freedom 250 to "hijack the country's 250th anniversary and sell access, hide his donors and rewrite history" and that Republicans had "let him clean house and put loyalists on the board of the National Park Foundation, open the door to foreign, dark money donors to buy influence with zero oversight". Representative [Maxine Dexter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxine_Dexter) expressed concerns that the organization was using public money and mixing it with private donations, and that the structure of the organization made it difficult to trace where the money came from. The Interior Department has stated that the U.S. Semiquincentennial Commission receives money through an interagency agreement with the National Park Service, but did not respond to questions regarding the distribution of federal money to America250.[^cite_note-Fields_02102026-47] *[NOTUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOTUS_\(website\))* reported that as of April 29, 2026, Freedom 250 had received \$68.3 million from the [Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior).[^cite_note-56]

### Local

In 2016, Revolution 250, a non-profit group organized to plan commemorative events in Boston surrounding the semiquincentennial, was established.[^cite_note-57] According to the organization, it is a consortium of 56 groups,[^cite_note-AASLH-2019-58] including the [Society of the Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_the_Cincinnati), the [National Park Service](161535.md), the [Boston Tea Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Tea_Party) Museum, the [New England Historic Genealogical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Historic_Genealogical_Society), the [Suffolk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_University) history department, the Boston Downtown Business Improvement District, [The Bostonian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bostonian_Society), and others.[^cite_note-59]



Re-enactors portraying the [4th Regiment of Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Regiment_of_Foot) marching to the [Old Powder House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Powder_House) for the 250th anniversary commemoration, September 2024.

The incident known as the [Powder Alarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powder_Alarm) of 1774 in the city now known as [Somerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville,_Massachusetts) was commemorated by a reenactment in 2024, on a Sunday 250 years after the Sunday of the actual event.[^cite_note-60] The following day, activities in nearby [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) explored the consequences of this event.[^cite_note-61]

For the 250th [Evacuation Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_Day_\(Massachusetts\)) celebrations, commemorating the evacuation of British forces from the city of Boston following the [siege of Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Boston), early in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), on March 17, 2026, the NPS will carry out a \$25 million overhaul of the [Dorchester Heights Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorchester_Heights_Monument). The planned renovations, which will take approximately 18 months to complete, will include work on "taking apart and reassembling the top floors, placing granite slabs to mark where the 1776 fortifications sat, bringing the retaining walls, drainage and lighting up to date and giving the info panels a face lift."[^cite_note-Cotter-2022-62]

In honor of [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington)'s troops' final leg of the trip to secretly haul cannons from [Fort Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Hill,_Boston) to the [Fortification of Dorchester Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortification_of_Dorchester_Heights), artist Michael Dowling sought 100,000 Bostonians, a seventh of the city population, to write "a short story of belonging" on any piece of cloth, which will then be tied in a 4-mile (6.4 km) long rope, the same length as the covert journey.[^cite_note-Cotter-2022-62]

In 2017, the [Daughters of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughters_of_the_American_Revolution) announced a grant of \$380,000 to the city of [Philadelphia](50585.md) to plant 76 semiquincentennial commemorative trees at [Independence National Historical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_National_Historical_Park).[^cite_note-63] The actual planting of the trees occurred over the course of several years leading up to the semiquincentennial.[^cite_note-64] In 2018, at the state level, the Pennsylvania Commission for the United States Semiquincentennial Commission (A250PA) was formed.[^cite_note-AASLH-2019-58]

The commission prepared a [time capsule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_capsule) for burial in Philadelphia on July 4, 2026, which is scheduled for unearthing on July 4, 2276, the 500th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence.[^cite_note-Lawrence-2018-27]

In 2016, Philadelphia city planners announced "Vision 2026", a plan to redevelop [Old City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Philadelphia\)) in preparation for the semiquincentennial.[^cite_note-65]

On October 15, 2025, President Trump proposed a [Memorial Circle Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Triumphal_Arch) to be built in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) as part of celebrations to welcome people as they approach the [traffic circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_circle) on [Columbia Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Island_\(Washington,_D.C.\)) heading towards the [Arlington Memorial Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Memorial_Bridge) and into the west end of the [National Mall](167796.md).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The arch, named [United States Triumphal Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Triumphal_Arch), would be topped by a statue [personifying Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personification_of_Liberty).

## Events

### Washington, D.C.



*[Washington Crossing the Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Crossing_the_Delaware_\(1851_paintings\))* projected on the [Washington Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Monument) January 1, 2026

From December 31, 2025, to January 5, 2026, stories about how America was discovered, its independence, and future were projected on the sides of the [Washington Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Monument).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] The White House also sponsored the "Rededicate 250" prayer gathering on May 17, 2026.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] A temporary 5,000 seat [Ultimate Fighting Championship](169660.md) (UFC) arena was erected on the [South Lawn](15728461.md) of the [White House](33057.md) for [UFC White House](80931969.md) on June 14, 2026, the first professional sporting event on White House grounds.[^cite_note-74]

The semiquincentennial was the hottest recorded July 4 in Washington, D.C., at 102 °F (39 °C), breaking the previous record of 100 °F (38 °C), set in 1919. Several events were disrupted or canceled as a result of the high heat.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

On June 24, 2026, Trump opened the Great American State Fair on the [National Mall](167796.md). The fair was originally supposed to feature food and cultural booths from all [states and territories](12610470.md), along with displays from federal agencies. Connecticut, Illinois, Maine, Massachusetts, North Carolina, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, and Washington did not officially participate.[^cite_note-77]



View of the Great American State Fair



Salute to America event in Washington, D.C., July 5, 2026

A Ferris wheel was installed, and a scaled-down model of the proposed [United States Triumphal Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Triumphal_Arch) was displayed.[^cite_note-78] *The Washington Post* reported opening day attendance was "relatively sparse compared with past National Mall events".[^cite_note-79] On June 26, Trump claimed that the fair was "packed with happy people".[^cite_note-80] According to [Fox News](11121.md), only a few hundred people could be seen, but a reporter claimed there were many more people somewhere else.[^cite_note-81]

The fair was marred by multiple controversies and logistical issues. Power outages led to Ferris wheel malfunctions and caused booths to close due to lack of air conditioning.[^cite_note-Pequeno-2026-82] Multiple artists were slated to perform at an opening festival, but pulled out due to the event's association with Trump's political [Freedom 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Task_Force_on_Celebrating_America's_250th_Birthday) organization.[^cite_note-83] A video including the [Confederate flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_display_of_the_Confederate_battle_flag) was shown at the North Carolina exhibit, causing controversy.[^cite_note-Pequeno-2026-82][^cite_note-84] Extreme weather affected the fair on the day of the holiday. On July 4, the fair was evacuated due to storms, and 11 people were sent to the hospital as a result of heat exhaustion.[^cite_note-85]

Beginning at midnight on July 5, approximately 850,000 fireworks were launched over a span of 40 minutes from various sites in Washington, D.C., including the National Mall, [West Potomac Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Potomac_Park), and eight [Potomac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River) barges. This broke the record of nearly 811,000 fireworks, set by a Philippines megachurch in 2016. The city also briefly recorded the worst air quality of any major city in the world due to smog caused by the fireworks.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

An [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series) race, the [Freedom 250 Grand Prix](82275853.md), was ordered by President Trump on January 30, 2026. It was held on August 23 on a street course around part of the National Mall in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-89]

## Religious acts

In a historic act, the [United States Conference of Catholic Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Conference_of_Catholic_Bishops) (USCCB) consecrated the nation to the [Sacred Heart of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus) on June 11, 2026. This initiative took place within the context of the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence, with the purpose of praying for healing, peace, and unity for the country.[^cite_note-124]

The national event was held during the Bishops' Spring Plenary Assembly in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida), and was preceded by a national novena of prayer and various spiritual activities, including 250 hours of adoration and 250 works of mercy.[^cite_note-125]

The official prayer of consecration, written for the occasion, entrusted the country to the [Sacred Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Heart), asking for the healing of divisions, respect for human dignity, and the protection of religious freedom and life.

## Reactions



Frosted sugar cookies made to celebrate the United States Semiquincentennial

The launch of semiquincentennial celebrations occurred amidst political division and record-low approval ratings for the sitting president. These celebrations were described as featuring competing narratives over American history,[^cite_note-Schuessler_07042025-41][^cite_note-126] such as with conflicts over the [removal of Confederate monuments and memorials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removal_of_Confederate_monuments_and_memorials).[^cite_note-Smith_07042025-127] *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* described them as "shaping up not as mere metaphor but as a straightforward display of America's fractured state".[^cite_note-Bennett_11102025-49]

Trump's cost-cutting measures across the federal government were described as complicating celebrations, with funding cuts for the [National Endowment for the Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Humanities) leading to [grant terminations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_government_grant_pause) for several state humanities councils that were working on programming for the 250th celebration.[^cite_note-Superville_07032025-6] CNN reported that state and local planners expressed fears over the president's brand of politics and funding cuts, writing that they were "concerned about a lack of resources to put on their own events and fear that the overall tenor will turn partisan as opposed to unifying, leaving little room for meaningful reflection on the nation's past".[^cite_note-Blackburn_1062025-44]

Differences between the events and initiatives hosted by the bipartisan [America 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_Commission) and the Trump-aligned [Freedom 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_250) organizations caused public confusion by artists and performers over their association with the Trump administration.[^cite_note-Marquez_05292026-128] The [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported that Trump's own celebrations had "overshadowed" the bipartisan commission and that his second term's actions led to comparisons with King [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III) or a monarch, which Trump both rejected and endorsed.[^cite_note-129]

### Politicization and protests

See also: [United States Semiquincentennial Commission § Politicization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_Commission#Politicization)



Inner covers of a new limited-edition United States passport design for the Semiquincentennial featuring the [United States Declaration of Independence](31874.md), an 1818 [John Trumbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Trumbull) painting, and [Donald Trump](4848272.md)

Criticism of the celebrations for politicization and praising Trump and conservatism emerged over official programming decisions.[^cite_note-Politicization-130] Several artists who had been invited to perform at the event publicly announced their decision to decline participation. Their objections stemmed from a shared belief that what had originally been presented to them as a nonpartisan, neutral gathering had gradually become associated with President Trump and, in their view, taken on a political character.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] A June 2026 Reuters/Ipsos poll found a majority of Americans, including three-quarters of Democrats and half of Republicans, believed events celebrating the country's 250th anniversary had grown too political.[^cite_note-133]

*[Axios](52436164.md)* described them as taking on a "[MAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_America_Great_Again) bent" since January 2025, with Trump coming up with and announcing more ideas for celebrations throughout the year. These events included a "soft launch" of America250 festivities during [Trump's military parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_250th_Anniversary_Parade) in June 2025 that became overshadowed by the millions across the country who protested on the same day during the [No Kings protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Kings_protests_\(June_2025\)).[^cite_note-Daher_2025-1][^cite_note-Ewing_06142025-134][^cite_note-135] *The Economist* described the Trump administration's March 2025 executive order requesting an audit of federal historical sites and the Smithsonian Museum to remove "divisive narratives" viewed as promoting [DEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEI) and "leftist" ideologies, such as ordering the removal of signs mentioning slavery at national parks, as an example of the nation's history becoming a "partisan battleground" ahead of the festivities.[^cite_note-Bennett_11102025-49]

During the opening announcement for America250 in [Des Moines, Iowa](9163.md), on July 3, 2025, Trump criticized Democrats, saying "I hate them" and that "I cannot stand them, because I really believe they hate our country",[^cite_note-Scherer_08062025-42] and also said those who voted against his [One Big Beautiful Bill Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act) "hate our country".[^cite_note-Superville_07032025-6] He also mocked former president [Joe Biden](145422.md)'s speaking style, repeated his [lie of a stolen 2020 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_lie#Donald_Trump's_false_claims_of_a_stolen_election), [criticized the "fake news" gear and memorabilia.[^cite_note-Superville_07032025-6][^cite_note-Smith_07042025-127] *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* described Trump's July 4 speech at [Mount New York City Mayor [Zohran Mamdani](64618347.md) delivered a speech at Washington's old desk, criticizing [Trumpism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism) and inequality, and said America was not [exceptional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_exceptionalism) because it was more powerful but because Americans collectively worked towards its founding ideals.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

The White House stated it has sent letters to state governors and ambassadors to display its "Founder's Museum" exhibit made in partnership with PragerU in capitols, schools, and embassies. The exhibit received criticism from historians for blurring history with AI-generated fiction and having historical characters repeat the phrase "facts don't care about your feelings" popularized by conservative commentator [Ben Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shapiro).[^cite_note-140] Freedom 250's "Freedom Trucks" exhibit was funded by \$10 million worth of congressionally authorized funding diverted from America 250, and was criticized as being made by conservative [Hillsdale College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsdale_College) and PragerU and prominently featuring quotes by Trump and a video he filmed inside the Oval Office.[^cite_note-Vogel_02082026-46]

### Currency featuring Donald Trump

#### Trump commemorative coin

Further information: [United States Semiquincentennial coinage § Trump dollar proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial_coinage#Trump_dollar_proposal)

The decision of the US Mint to create a two-headed commemorative \$1 coin of Trump was widely criticized by historians as antithetical to America's anti-monarchical ethos. It was described as coming in spite of a 2005 congressional authorization that stated coins should not "bear the image of a living former or current president". Treasury Secretary [Scott Bessent](38532044.md) stated that a 2020 law allowed "designs emblematic of the United States semiquincentennial" and that "there is no profile more emblematic for the front of this coin than that of our serving President, Donald J. Trump." *The New York Times* highlighted passages from the law which also stated "No head and shoulders portrait or bust of any person, living or dead, and no portrait of a living person may be included in the design on the reverse of any coin", which would make such a decision likely illegal.[^cite_note-Barry_11092025-153]

In early 2026, the Mint proposed an additional "review and discussion of a Semiquincentennial Gold Coin" that would feature Trump to the [Citizens Coinage Advisory Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Coinage_Advisory_Committee).[^cite_note-Barry_02282025-154] Acting chairman Donald Scarinci criticized the decision, writing that:

> For 250 years, since that great document was signed — with a few controversial exceptions — no nation on earth has issued coins with the image of a democratically elected leader during the time of their service. Only those nations ruled by kings or dictators display the image of their sitting ruler on the coins of the realm. God bless America, and may God preserve our nation.[^cite_note-Barry_02282025-154]

The only other president to be depicted on coinage despite federal law[^cite_note-155]<sup>: 221 </sup> was during the 150th anniversary celebrations in 1926, when coinage was made depicting then sitting President [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge) as part of the [United States Sesquicentennial coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Sesquicentennial_coinage).[^cite_note-156]

#### Trump signature and \$250 banknote



Obverse of a *Series 2025* \$100 note with the signature of the U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md)

In April 2026, Trump appointees began printing [\$100 bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_one-hundred-dollar_bill) with Trump's signature to replace the Secretary of the Treasury and produced mock-ups for a \$250 banknote bearing Trump's portrait to celebrate the nation's 250th anniversary. The moves were criticized for breaking federal law according to currency experts.[^cite_note-157] Additionally, no new U.S. currency can be adopted without the explicit approval of Congress.[^cite_note-158]
