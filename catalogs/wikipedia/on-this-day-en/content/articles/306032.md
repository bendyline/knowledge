**Stade** (German pronunciation: [\[ˈʃtaːdə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c0/De-Stade.ogg/De-Stade.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c0/De-Stade.ogg/De-Stade.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German_language): *Stood*), officially the **Hanseatic City of Stade** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Hansestadt Stade*, [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German_language): *Hansestadt Stood*), is a city in [Lower Saxony](18435.md) in northern [Germany](11867.md). First mentioned in records in 934, it is the seat of the [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_\(district\)) (*Landkreis*) which bears its name. It is located roughly 45 km (28 mi) to the west of [Hamburg](13467.md) and belongs to that city's [wider metropolitan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Metropolitan_Region). Within the area of the city are the urban districts of Bützfleth, Hagen, Haddorf and Wiepenkathen, each of which have a council (*Ortsrat*) of their own with some autonomous decision-making rights.

Stade is located in the [lower regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederelbe) of the river [Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe). It is also on the [German Timber-Frame Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Timber-Frame_Road).

## History

The first human settlers came to the Stade area in 30,000 BC.

### Medieval period

[Swedish](5058739.md) and [Danish](419786.md) [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) under [Eric the Victorious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_the_Victorious) conquered Stade and looted the town during the 990s. Many prominent [Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxons) were taken back as slaves by Swedish troops. A majority of Vikings withdrew after taking plenty of plunder. A minor part of the Swedish and Danish forces stayed but were later defeated by reinforcements sent by the emperor.[^cite_note-4]

Since 1180 Stade belonged to the [Prince-Archbishopric of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Archbishopric_of_Bremen). In early 1208 King [Valdemar II of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_II_of_Denmark) and his troops conquered Stade. In August Valdemar II's cousin being in enmity with the king, the then Prince-Archbishop [Valdemar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_of_Denmark_\(bishop\)) reconquered the city only to lose it soon after again to Valdemar II.[^cite_note-5] In 1209 Emperor [Otto IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) persuaded his ally Valdemar II to withdraw into the north of the Elbe, and the deposed Prince-Archbishop Valdemar took Stade.

On 2 May 1209 Otto IV granted important [town privileges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_privileges) to Stade. Otto IV confirmed the [burghers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie) to be personally free and recognised them constituting a political entity of their own law, the *burgenses and optimi cives* of Stade.[^cite_note-6] Property within the municipal boundaries could not be subjected to feudal overlordship and was to be freely inherited without feudal claims to reversion. Fair juridical procedures were constituted and maximal fines fixed. Otto IV obliged himself to prevent burghers from being taken as hostages and to liberate captured burghers.

After Otto IV had changed his mind and reinvested Prince-Archbishop Valdemar with the [See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see) in 1211, Valdemar II recaptured Stade. In 1213 Otto's elder brother Count Palatine [Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V,_Count_Palatine_of_the_Rhine) of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Palatine_of_the_Rhine), reconquered Stade for the Prince-Archbishop. In 1215 Henry repelled another Danish attack on Stade. In the winter of 1216 Valdemar II and his Danish troops, unable to take the city of Stade, ravaged the [County of Stade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Stade). From then on Stade remained a part of the Prince-Archbishopric of Bremen.



1572 drawing of Stade. "S. Cosmus" and "S. Wilhat" refer to [St. Cosmae et Damiani Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian,_Stade) and St. Wilhadi, respectively.

From the 13th century to the late 17th century, Stade was a prominent member of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League), but was later eclipsed by [Hamburg](13467.md). In 1611 the city signed a contract with [Sephardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardim) Jews, allowing the foundation of a community. In 1613, [Johann Friedrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Frederick,_Duke_of_Schleswig-Holstein-Gottorp), Administrator of the Prince-Archbishopric, followed by settling [Ashkenazic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazim) Jews in the city, but during the turmoil of Catholic conquest and Lutheran reconquest the last archival traces of Jews date from 1630. In 1648, by the [Treaty of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Westphalia), the Prince-Archbishopric of Bremen underwent a constitutional transformation from a [prince-bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-bishopric) into a monarchy, the [Duchy of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bremen). The duchy and the neighbouring [Principality of Verden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Verden), colloquially referred to as [Bremen-Verden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen-Verden), were granted by the Treaty of Westphalia as an appanage to the Swedish crown. Stade, already under Swedish occupation since 1645, was a part of the [Swedish](5058739.md) province of Bremen-Verden-Wildeshausen from 1645 to 1712, and some of the buildings built by the Swedes are still in use today.

### Swedish rule



Stade in 1640 (drawing by [Matthäus Merian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matth%C3%A4us_Merian))

Stade's heyday lasted until the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War). In 1628 [Tilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_t%E2%80%99Serclaes_von_Tilly) conquered the town; shortly thereafter, [Sweden](5058739.md) took possession of it until 1636. After a period of Danish occupation, Sweden finally recaptured it in 1643 and was also officially granted possession of it, together with the [Archbishopric of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Bremen), in the [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia). Two-thirds of the town were razed in the great town fire on 26 May 1659. The town was rebuilt again to the same plan.

From 1675 to 1676, in the [Swedish-Brandenburg War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish-Brandenburg_War), Swedish Stade was conquered during a [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen-Verden_Campaign) by [Denmark-Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark-Norway) and several states of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) and remained in allied hands until the end of that war in 1679. Stade, as the headquarters of the Swedish Stadhalter, was besieged from early April 1676 to 13 Aug 1676. In the wake of the [Treaty of Saint Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_\(1679\)) in 1679, Stade was once again awarded to Sweden.

The Elbe customs station near Stade, in Brunshausen at the mouth of the Schwinge, played special role in trading on the River Elbe from the period of the archbishopric. In 1663, the Swedes stationed an Elbe customs frigate as a permanent patrol ship. This arrangement continued to exist under various rulers until 1850 and the customs station on the Schwinge fieldworks itself existed until 1865.[^cite_note-Graewe-7]

Swedish sovereignty ended in 1712. Danish troops besieged the town in the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) and shelled it from 29 August to 7 September 1712 that destroyed 152 houses, a quarter of the built-up area.

During the Swedish times Stade was the capital of the province.

### Danish rule

In 1712 Denmark-Norway conquered Stade and the whole of Bremen-Verden. Stade remained Bremen-Verden's capital also after the Danes ceded it to the [Electorate of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Hanover) in 1715. When in 1823 Bremen-Verden was replaced by new administrative forms, Stade continued to be the capital of the [Stade region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_\(region\)).

In 1355 and in 1712, Stade suffered from the [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) epidemic, which killed at least 30–40% of the city's population.

### Late modern period

In 1757 following the [French Invasion of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Hanover_\(1757\)), the [Hanoverian Army of Observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoverian_Army_of_Observation) under [Prince William, Duke of Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William,_Duke_of_Cumberland), and the [Privy Council of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Hanover) (government) took shelter in Stade. Cumberland prepared to defend the town before agreeing the [Convention of Klosterzeven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Klosterzeven) which brought about a temporary [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice).

By the end of the 17th century Ashkenazi Jews reappeared in Stade. In 1842 the [Kingdom of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hanover) granted equal rights to Jews and promoted building up Jewish congregations and a regional superstructure ([rabbinate](51273.md)) within a nationwide scope. The Jews in Stade regarded this as progress and a burden alike, because prior they had not employed any rabbi and religion teacher due to the implied financial burden. In 1845 – according to the new law – a land-rabbinate, under [Land-Rabbi](51273.md#18th%E2%80%9319th_centuries) Joseph Heilbut, was established in the city, serving 16 Jewish congregations, which were founded over the years in the whole Stade Region, with altogether 1,250 Jews in 1864 (highest number ever reached). The local authorities now requested, that the Jewish congregations establish synagogues and Jewish education for the pupils.



New harbor during the 1894 flooding



Former synagogue of Stade

In 1849 Stade's synagogue opened, but had to close due to financial restrictions in 1908. And a teacher for [Jewish religion](15624.md) and Hebrew was employed (after 1890 Stade's community couldn't afford a teacher any more). From 1903 on the Jewish community of Stade was granted public subsidies to continue functioning. The Stade Region stayed a Jewish diaspora, and from 1860 on Stade's land-rabbinate was never staffed again, but served alternately by one of the other three Hanoverian land-rabbinates. Labour migration and emigration[^cite_note-8] to urban centres outside the Stade Region and Jewish demography rather led to a reduction of the number of Jews in the *Stade Region* (786 in 1913, 716 in 1928).[^cite_note-9] However, most of the remaining Jews were deported during the Nazi reign.

During [World War II](32927.md), a Nazi court jail was operated in Stade.[^cite_note-10] During the war, Stade remained completely untouched by Allied bombings.



Stade nuclear power plant (offline) in 2006

In past decades, Stade has economically benefited significantly from the presence of chemical and aerospace industry at the [Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) river, most notably [Dow Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Chemical) and [Airbus](26220236.md). Also by the Elbe at Stade is the decommissioned [Stade Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Nuclear_Power_Plant), which was connected to the power grid from 1972 to 2003. By the time the plant was brought offline, it was Germany's second oldest reactor. Following Germany's 2002 decision to phase out nuclear power generation, Stade was the first German plant to be affected; it was closed down permanently on 14 November 2003. Close to the former nuclear plant there is an inactive oil-fired power station, the [Schilling Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schilling_Power_Station).

In 2026, [a mass shooting](83579203.md) occurred at a youth welfare centre in Stade; 6 people were killed.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Sights

The Old Town of Stade is home to a variety of notable historic buildings; among the most notable are the [St. Cosmae et Damiani Lutheran Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Cosmae_et_Damiani_\(Stade\)), the Wilhadi Lutheran Church, the city hall, the *Schwedenspeicher* and the *Zeughaus*.

Located near to Stade are the gigantic pylons of [Elbe Crossing 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe_Crossing_1) and [Elbe Crossing 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe_Crossing_2); the Elbe Crossing 2 pylons are the tallest in Europe and the sixth-tallest in the world.

## Transport

In late 2007, line S3 of the [S-Bahn Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn_Hamburg) was extended to Stade. Trains depart [Stade station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_station) every 20 minutes (at peak times), arriving at [Hamburg central station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Hauptbahnhof) in roughly one hour.
