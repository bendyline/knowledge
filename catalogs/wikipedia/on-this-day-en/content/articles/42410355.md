# Paraguay at the FIFA World Cup

The **[FIFA World Cup](11370.md)** is an international [association football](10568.md) competition contested by the [men's national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_national_association_football_teams) of the members of *Fédération Internationale de Football Association* ([FIFA](11049.md)), the sport's global governing body. The championship has been awarded every four years since the first tournament in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), except in 1942 and 1946, due to [World War II](32927.md).

[Paraguay](804025.md) have appeared in the finals of the World Cup on nine occasions, the first being at the very first finals in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), where they finished in the ninth position. Their farthest advancement in the tournament was in [2010](168079.md), when they reached the quarter-finals for the first time. They made a ninth World Cup appearance at the [2026 tournament](36581929.md).

## Tournament summary

Paraguay competed at three World Cups by 1958, and after a long gap they re-appeared in 1986. Later, they achieved a streak of four participations: South Africa 2010 was Paraguay's fourth consecutive trip to the World Cup final tournament, having previously qualified for the final in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup). After a poor qualifying campaign, however, Paraguay failed to qualify for the tournament in 2014, missing out on the chance to play in a World Cup hosted on their own continent, in Brazil. Three times they survived the first round of the international football tournament, with the highlight being in [2010](168079.md) where they reached the quarter-finals.

### 1958 FIFA World Cup

Main article: [1958 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup)

For the 1958 World Cup, Paraguay surprisingly qualified ahead of Uruguay (beating them 5–0 in the decisive game) with a team that contained a formidable attacking lineup with stars such as [Juan Bautista Agüero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Ag%C3%BCero), [José Parodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Parodi), [Juan Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Romero_\(soccer_player\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Cayetano Ré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayetano_R%C3%A9) and [Florencio Amarilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florencio_Amarilla). In their first game in Sweden, Paraguay were 3–2 up against [France](250197.md) in a game they lost 7–3. A 3–2 win over [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) and a 3–3 draw with [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) saw Paraguay finish third in their group. &#x20;

### 1986 FIFA World Cup

Main article: [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md)

Paraguay ended a 28-year absence from the World Cup in 1986 with a team starring [Roberto Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Fern%C3%A1ndez_\(footballer,_born_1954\)) in goal; [Cesar Zabala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Zabala), [Rogelio Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogelio_Delgado) and [Juan Bautista Torales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Torales) in defense; [Jorge Amado Nunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Amado_Nunes) and [Vladimiro Schettina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimiro_Schettina) in midfield; midfield playmaker [Romerito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romerito) and strikers [Roberto Cabañas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Caba%C3%B1as), [Ramón Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Hicks) and [Rolando Chilavert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolando_Chilavert) (older brother of [José Luis Chilavert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Chilavert)). In first round matches, Paraguay defeated [Iraq](1039652.md) (1–0, goal Romerito) and then tied [Mexico](808402.md) (1–1, goal Romerito) and Belgium (2–2, both goals Roberto Cabañas). They reached the second round where they were beaten 3–0 by [England](9904.md).[^cite_note-4] &#x20;

### 1998 FIFA World Cup

Main article: [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup)

A new generation of players helped end the World Cup drought in grand fashion, as the Albirroja reached the 1998 World Cup in France by qualifying in second place in South America, behind [Argentina](454699.md). The first round matches were against [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) (0–0), [Spain](609825.md) (0–0), and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) (3–1; goals [Celso Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celso_Ayala), [Miguel Ángel Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_%C3%81ngel_Ben%C3%ADtez_Pav%C3%B3n) and [José Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Cardozo)). Paraguay qualified to the second round (round of 16) to be defeated in a thrilling match against hosts and eventual World Champions [France](250197.md). France only scored through [Laurent Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Blanc) in the 114th minute, during the second half of overtime (making it the first golden goal scored in a World Cup). Paraguay's central defending duo ([Carlos Gamarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Gamarra) and [Celso Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celso_Ayala)) and goalkeeper José Luis Chilavert were selected for the all-star World Cup team. &#x20;

### 2002 FIFA World Cup

Main article: [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup)

Paraguay returned to the world's greatest stage once more in the 2002 World Cup. In their first match, Paraguay tied [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) 2–2 (goals: [Roque Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Santa_Cruz) and [Francisco Arce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Arce)). Paraguay lost to Spain in the second game (1–3) and finally defeated [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team) (3–1; goals [Nelson Cuevas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Cuevas), twice, and [Jorge Luis Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Campos)) to qualify for the second round. [Germany](250204.md) ended Paraguay's dreams in the World Cup with an 88-minute goal. &#x20;

### 2010 FIFA World Cup

Main article: [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md)

In their fourth consecutive World Cup appearance, Paraguay eventually reached the quarter-finals. Drawn into [Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F) along with defending champions Italy, [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team) and [New Zealand](1023035.md), they opened their tournament with a draw against the Italians, in a game where they took the lead thanks to a first-half goal from [Antolín Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antol%C3%ADn_Alcaraz) but ultimately finished 1-1. In their second match, they beat Slovakia 2–0 with goals from [Enrique Vera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Vera) and [Christian Riveros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Riveros). Their final group match saw a goalless draw with New Zealand, clinching first place in Group F and setting up a date with [Japan](993546.md). Another goalless draw with the Japanese after 120 minutes went to penalties, which Paraguay won 5–3, advancing them for the first time in their history to the World Cup round of eight. In the quarter-finals, they met powerhouses Spain, to whom they lost 1–0 in a game where a goal by [Nelson Valdez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Valdez) was controversially called an offside by the referee. The game also featured each team being awarded a penalty, both of which were contained, first by Spain's [Iker Casillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iker_Casillas) and then Paraguay's [Justo Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justo_Villar) (both were also team captains for the game).

The *Albirroja* arrived back from South Africa on Monday, July 5 at 3\:30 AM. Upon arrival, they were greeted by over 3,000 fans at the airport and were decorated by the [President of Paraguay](720557.md). [Gerardo Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardo_Martino) announced that he would take some time to decide his future, although the [Paraguayan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_Football_Association) has offered him a four-year contract to continue at the helm. [Roque Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Santa_Cruz) also announced that this would be his last World Cup, but that he may play one more tournament, the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) in Argentina in 2011, which he ended up playing in. &#x20;

Paraguay and Japan met at the [Loftus Versfeld Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loftus_Versfeld_Stadium) in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) on 29 June 2010. The match was decided by a penalty shoot-out after the score was locked at 0–0 for 120 minutes. Paraguay won the shoot-out and progressed to its first ever World Cup quarter-final.[^cite_note-9] The match was a generally unexciting affair, as Japan adopted a defensive posture while Paraguay itself maintained a solid defence. The first half produced the occasional chance on goal with [Lucas Barrios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Barrios) having a shot saved shortly before a long-distance shot from [Daisuke Matsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisuke_Matsui) hit the crossbar of Paraguay's goal. The second half was similar, with either side producing occasional chances to score rather than periods of dominance. The result of the deadlock was extra time, which continued goalless. A penalty shoot-out ensued, in which [Yūichi Komano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%ABichi_Komano) missed a spot kick for Japan. Paraguay scored all five of its penalties, clinching the win and passage to the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-10] After the match, Japan head coach [Takeshi Okada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Okada) resigned and [Shunsuke Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunsuke_Nakamura) retired from international football.[^cite_note-11]

On 3 July 2010, Spain defeated Paraguay 1–0 to secure entry to the semi-finals where they would meet Germany. It was the first time that Spain had progressed to the semi-final of a World Cup since 1950; while for the defeated Paraguay, the quarter-final appearance was also the country's best ever performance.[^cite_note-BBCSpaPar-13]

The first half of the match finished goalless, although both sides had chances to score and Paraguay's [Nelson Valdez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Haedo_Valdez) had a goal ruled out as offside. The match suddenly became eventful in the second half due to a string of penalty kicks. First, [Óscar Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Cardozo) was pulled down by [Gerard Piqué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Piqu%C3%A9) in Spain's penalty area and Paraguay was awarded a penalty. Cardozo took the penalty himself but it was saved by Spain's goalkeeper Iker Casillas. Spain soon after launched an attack at the other end of the field, in which [David Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Villa) was ruled by the referee to have been brought down by Antolín Alcaraz. [Xabi Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xabi_Alonso) stepped up to take the penalty kick and seemed to have scored, only for the referee to order it be retaken because of encroachment by a Spanish player into the penalty area before the kick was taken. Xabi Alonso's retake was saved by Paraguayan goalkeeper Justo Villar. As a result, the score remained 0–0 after the three penalty kicks. Spain, however, ultimately managed to take the lead in the 82nd minute: David Villa collected a rebounded shot off the post from [Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_\(footballer,_born_July_1987\)), to score himself off the post. The goal turned out to be the winner for Spain.[^cite_note-BBCSpaPar-13] After the match, Spain coach [Vicente del Bosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_del_Bosque) conceded that his side were not playing at their best and were starved of possession. He also noted his view that Spain's next opponents Germany were the best team at the World Cup.[^cite_note-14] Paraguay coach Gerardo Martino stated he would be leaving his position at the end of his contract.[^cite_note-15]
