The **Ministry of Education** (**MOE**; [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 教育部; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Jiàoyùbù*; [Pe̍h-ōe-jī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%CC%8Dh-%C5%8De-j%C4%AB): *Kàu-io̍k-pō͘*; [Zhuyin Fuhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bopomofo): ㄐㄧㄠˋ ㄩˋ ㄅㄨˋ; [Pha̍k-fa-sṳ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pha%CC%8Dk-fa-s%E1%B9%B3): *Kau-yuk Phu*) is the cabinet-level ministry of the Republic of China ([Taiwan](25734.md)) responsible for formulating educational policies, managing public schools, and overseeing local educational administrative agencies.

## History

See also: [History of education in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_education_in_Taiwan)

The Taiwanese education ministry's origin goes back to the [Ministry of Education, Science, Sports and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education,_Science,_Sports_and_Culture) under the [Imperial Japanese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), which took over Taiwan in 1895. During [Japanese colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_under_Japanese_rule), school attendance among Taiwanese children increased from 3.8% in 1904 to 71.3% in 1943, and literacy became widespread in Taiwan.[^cite_note-1] Modern schools were formed with widespread establishment of primary schools while higher schooling for Taiwanese people remained rare and secondary schools and colleges were mostly for Japanese nationals. In special cases, some Taiwanese were able to pursue higher education, and many traveled to Japan for further studies.

The current government of Taiwan, officially known as the Republic of China (ROC), was formed in [mainland China](55079.md) in 1912. After the [retreat of the ROC government to Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_the_government_of_Republic_of_China_to_Taiwan) in 1949, the ROC Ministry of Education was re-established in [Taipei](57648.md).

In 2022, in response to complaints from higher education institutions about the weekly cap on inbound visitors, the MOE reserved extra slots for foreign students to ensure they are not prevented from entering Taiwan.[^cite_note-2]

## International partnerships

In 2017, the Ministry of Education (MOE) launched the [New Southbound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Southbound_Policy) Talent Development Program to promote educational exchange with India.[^cite_note-3]

In November 2023, [Montana](19978.md) governor [Greg Gianforte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Gianforte) announced that the Montana Office of the Commissioner of Higher Education signed a new [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) with the Taiwanese MOE to create a [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) language program at the [University of Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montana) in [Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula) and an educational exchange program at [Montana Technological University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Technological_University). This partnership followed a first wave of Montana Tech students who, after a 2022 MOU, studied at the [Minghsin University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minghsin_University_of_Science_and_Technology).[^cite_note-4]

In December 2023, the MOE hosted a delegation from the [University of Scranton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Scranton) led by [Joseph G. Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_G._Marina) to explore partnerships with Taiwanese universities. The trip followed a series of exchanges between the university and the MOE, leading to Taiwanese cultural programs, lectures, and film festivals in Scranton starting in 2010.[^cite_note-5]
