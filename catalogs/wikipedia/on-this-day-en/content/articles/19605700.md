**East Asia** is a geocultural region of [Asia](689.md). It includes [China](5405.md), [Japan](15573.md), [Mongolia](19271.md), [North Korea](21255.md), [South Korea](27019.md), and [Taiwan](25734.md),[^cite_note-Kort_2005_7-2][^cite_note-RAND-3] plus two [special administrative regions of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China): [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md). The economies of [China](19284336.md), [Japan](15578.md), [South Korea](27024.md), and [Taiwan](30092.md) are among the world's largest, fastest-growing, and most prosperous. East Asia borders [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia) to the north, [Southeast Asia](28741.md) to the south, [South Asia](21566765.md) to the southwest, and [Central Asia](6742.md) to the west. To its east is the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md).

East Asia, especially the [Chinese civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China), is regarded as one of the earliest [cradles of civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization#China). Other ancient civilizations in East Asia that still exist as independent countries in the present day include the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Japan), [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Korea), and [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mongolia) civilizations. Various other civilizations existed as independent polities in East Asia in the past but have since been absorbed into neighbouring civilizations in the present day, such as [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tibet), [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Manchuria), and [Ryukyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Ryukyu_Islands) ([Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island)), among many others. Taiwan has a relatively young [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Taiwan) in the region after the [prehistoric era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Taiwan); originally, it was a major site of [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) civilization prior to colonization by European colonial powers and China from the 17th century onward. For thousands of years, China was the leading civilization in the region, exerting influence on its neighbours.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Historically, societies in East Asia have fallen within the [Chinese sphere of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cultural_sphere), and East Asian vocabularies and scripts are often derived from [Classical Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Chinese) and [Chinese script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_character). The [Chinese calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calendar) serves as the root from which many other East Asian calendars are derived.

Major [religions in East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_religions) include [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Buddhism) (mostly [Mahayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana_Buddhism)),[^cite_note-8] [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) and [Neo-Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confucianism), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism),[^cite_note-\:2-9] [ancestral worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_veneration_in_China), and [Chinese folk religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion) in Mainland China, [Hong Kong](13404.md), [Macau](19068.md) and [Taiwan](25734.md), [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) in Japan, and [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Korea) and [Musok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_shamanism) in Korea.[^cite_note-Salkind_2008_56-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] [Tengerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_shamanism) and [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) are prevalent among [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) and [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) while other religions such as [Shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_shamanism) are widespread among the indigenous populations of northeastern China such as the [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people).[^cite_note-13] The major [languages in East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_East_Asia) include [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), [Japanese](15606.md), and [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language). The major [ethnic groups of East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_East_Asia) include the [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) in China and Taiwan, [Yamato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_people) in Japan, [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans) in North and South Korea, and [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_people) in Mongolia. There are 76 officially-recognized [minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group) or [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) ethnic groups in East Asia; [55 native to mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_China) (including [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), Manchus, [Chinese Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China), Tibetans, [Uyghurs](31783.md), and [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people) in the [frontier regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_China)), 16 native to the [island of Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan) (collectively known as [Taiwanese indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_indigenous_peoples)), 1 native to the Japanese island of [Hokkaido](58092.md) (the [Ainu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainu_people)) and 4 native to [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Mongolia#Ethnicity_and_languages) ([Turkic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples)). The [Ryukyuan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_people) are an unrecognized ethnic group indigenous to the [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md) in southern Japan, which stretch from [Kyushu](16914.md) to Taiwan. There are also several unrecognized indigenous ethnic groups in mainland China and Taiwan.

East Asia is inhabited by around 1,648 million people, making up nearly 33% of the population in continental Asia and 20% of the global population.[^cite_note-14] The region is home to major world metropolises such as [Beijing](18603746.md)–[Tianjin](50821.md), [Busan](68115.md)–[Daegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu)–[Ulsan](323111.md)–[Changwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changwon), [Chongqing](171752.md), [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou)-[Hong Kong](13404.md)-[Macau](19068.md), [Osaka](52230.md)–[Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto)–[Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe), [Seoul](19159283.md)-[Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon), [Shanghai](27643.md), [Taipei](57648.md), and [Tokyo](30057.md). Although the coastal and riparian areas of the region form one of the world's most populated places, the population in [Mongolia](19271.md) and [Western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China), both landlocked areas, is very sparsely distributed, with Mongolia having the [lowest population density of a sovereign state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_by_population_density). As of 2025, the overall population density of the region is 133 inhabitants per square kilometre (340/sq mi), about three times the world average of 45/km<sup>2</sup> (120/sq mi).[^cite_note-15]

## History

Main article: [History of East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_East_Asia)

### Ancient era

China was the first region settled in East Asia and was undoubtedly the core of East Asian civilization from where other parts of East Asia were formed. The various other regions in East Asia were selective in the Chinese influences they adopted into their local customs. Historian [Ping-ti Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping-ti_Ho) referred to China as the cradle of Eastern civilization, in parallel with the [cradle of Middle Eastern civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization) along the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent) encompassing [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) and [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt)[^cite_note-16] as well as the [cradle of Western civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_Western_civilization) encompassing [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece).

Chinese civilization emerged early, and prefigured other East Asian civilisations. Throughout history, imperial China would exert cultural, economic, technological, and political influence on its neighbours.[^cite_note-Ball_2005_104-17][^cite_note-Amy_Chua,_Jed_Rubenfeld_2014_121-18][^cite_note-Kang_2012_33–34-19] Succeeding Chinese dynasties exerted enormous influence across East Asia culturally, economically, politically and militarily for over two millennia.[^cite_note-Kang_2012_33–34-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-2000years-21] The [tributary system of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China) shaped much of East Asia's history for over two millennia due to Imperial China's economic and cultural influence over the region, and thus played a huge role in the history of East Asia in particular.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Warren_I._Cohen_2000-23][^cite_note-Amy_Chua,_Jed_Rubenfeld_2014_121-18] Imperial China's cultural preeminence not only led the country to become East Asia's first literate nation in the entire region, it also supplied Japan and Korea with Chinese loanwords and linguistic influences rooted in their writing systems.[^cite_note-24]

Under [Emperor Wu of Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Han), the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) made China the regional powerhouse in East Asia, projecting much of its imperial influence onto its neighbours.[^cite_note-Kang_2012_33–34-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen200060-25] Han China hosted the largest unified population in East Asia, the most literate and urbanised as well as being the most economically developed, as well as the most technologically and culturally advanced civilization in the region at the time.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Cultural and religious interaction between the Chinese and other regional East Asian dynasties and kingdoms occurred. China's impact and influence on Korea began with the Han dynasty's [northeastern expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Gojoseon) in 108 BC when the Han Chinese conquered the northern part of the Korean peninsula and established a province called [Lelang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelang_Commandery). Chinese influences were transmitted and soon took root in Korea through the inclusion of the Chinese writing system, monetary system, rice culture, philosophical schools of thought, and Confucian political institutions.[^cite_note-28] Jomon society in ancient Japan incorporated wet-rice cultivation and metallurgy through its contact with Korea. Starting in the fourth century AD, Japan adopted [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters), which remain integral to the [Japanese writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_writing_system). Utilizing the Chinese writing system allowed the Japanese to conduct their daily activities, maintain historical records and give form to various ideas, thoughts, and philosophies.

### Medieval era

Map of Asia

Map showing the boundary of the 13th-century [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) compared to today's [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols)

During the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), China exerted its greatest influence on East Asia as various aspects of [Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture) spread to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_influence_on_Japanese_culture) and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_influence_on_Korean_culture).[^cite_note-lockard1999p2-3-29][^cite_note-lockard1999p7-30] The establishment of the medieval Tang dynasty rekindled the impetus of Chinese expansionism across the geopolitical confines of East Asia. Similar to its [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) predecessor, Tang China reasserted itself as the center of East Asian geopolitical influence during the early medieval period which spearheaded and marked another [golden age in Chinese history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_golden_age). In addition, Tang China also managed to maintain control over [northern Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Nanyue) and [northern Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo%E2%80%93Tang_War).[^cite_note-31]

As full-fledged medieval East Asian states were established, Korea by the fourth century AD and Japan by the seventh century AD, Japan and Korea actively began to incorporate Chinese influences such as [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism), the use of [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters), [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_architecture), state institutions, [political philosophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_philosophy), religion, urban planning, and various [scientific and technological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science_and_technology_in_China) methods into their culture and society through direct contacts with Tang China and succeeding Chinese dynasties.[^cite_note-lockard1999p2-3-29][^cite_note-lockard1999p7-30][^cite_note-32] Drawing inspiration from the Tang political system, Prince [Naka no Ōe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Tenji) launched the [Taika Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taika_Reform) in 645 AD where he radically transformed Japan's political bureaucracy into a more centralized bureaucratic empire.[^cite_note-lockard1999p8-33] The Japanese also adopted [Mahayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana) Buddhism, Chinese style architecture, and the imperial court's rituals and ceremonies, including the orchestral music and state dances had Tang influences. Written Chinese gained prestige and aspects of Tang culture such as [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_poetry), [calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calligraphy), and [landscape painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_painting#China) became widespread.[^cite_note-lockard1999p8-33] During the [Nara period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_period), Japan began to aggressively import Chinese culture and styles of government which included Confucian protocol that served as a foundation for Japanese culture as well as political and social philosophy.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-Tang6-35] The Japanese created laws adopted from the Chinese legal system that was used to govern in addition to the traditional [kimono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimono), which was inspired from Chinese traditional *[hanfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanfu)* during the 8th century.

### Modern era



A 17th-century-map showing [Qing China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) and [Tokugawa Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate)

For many centuries, most notably from the 7th to the 14th centuries, China stood as East Asia's most advanced civilization and foremost military and economic power, exerting its influence as the transmission of advanced Chinese cultural practices and ways of thinking greatly shaped the region up until the 19th century.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-Ellington_2009_21-38] From the 3rd century through the 18th century, diplomatic and trade relations between China and other East Asian countries and the steppe kingdoms was governed through a tributary system.[^cite_note-\:02-39]<sup>: 13–14 </sup> Under this system, the Chinese emperor received tribute from other rulers and in return received political benefits (like recognition or non-aggression agreements) or physical gifts, like porcelain and silks.[^cite_note-\:02-39]<sup>: 14 </sup> Through this system, the Chinese emperor conferred legitimacy on other rulers.[^cite_note-\:02-39]<sup>: 14 </sup>

As East Asia's connections with Europe and the Western world strengthened during the late 19th century, China's power began to decline.[^cite_note-Ball_2005_104-17][^cite_note-40] By the mid-19th century, the weakening [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) became fraught with political corruption, obstacles and stagnation that was incapable of rejuvenating itself as a world power in contrast to the industrializing Imperial European colonial powers and a rapidly modernizing Japan.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] The United States Commodore [Matthew C. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_C._Perry) would [open Japan to Western influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakumatsu), and the country would expand in earnest after the 1860s.[^cite_note-Tindall_2009_926-43][^cite_note-April_2007_163-44][^cite_note-45] Around the same time, the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration) in Japan sparked rapid societal transformation from an isolated feudal state into East Asia's first industrialised nation.[^cite_note-Batty_2005-46][^cite_note-April_2007_163-44] The modern and militarily powerful Japan would galvanise its position in the Orient as East Asia's greatest power with a global mission poised to advance to lead the entire world.[^cite_note-Batty_2005-46][^cite_note-Goldman_2000_3-47] By the early 1900s, the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) succeeded in asserting itself as East Asia's most dominant geopolitical force.[^cite_note-Goldman_2000_3-47]



[Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_imperialism_in_Asia) and [spheres of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_of_influence) in East Asia and Oceania, circa 1914

With its newly found international status, Japan began to challenge the European colonial powers and took on a more active role within the East Asian geopolitical order and world affairs at large.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen2000273-48] Flexing its political and military might, Japan soundly defeated the Qing dynasty during the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War) as well as defeating Russia in the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War) in 1905; the first major military victory in the modern era of an East Asian power over a European one.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-Tindall_2009_926-43]

During [World War I](4764461.md), European military presence in East Asia decreased.[^cite_note-\:03-52]<sup>: 34 </sup> Japan viewed this as an opportunity to increase its power in China and shortly after the war began, occupied [Germany's concessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_concessions_in_China) in [Shandong](105032.md).[^cite_note-\:03-52]<sup>: 34 </sup> In December 1914, Japan made its [Twenty-One Demands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-One_Demands) to China.[^cite_note-\:03-52]<sup>: 34 </sup> The [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) under [Yuan Shikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Shikai) conceded to most of the demands in 1915, and subsequent treaties and agreements further increased Japan's semi-colonial power in China.[^cite_note-\:03-52]<sup>: 34 </sup>

Japan hegemony was the heart of an empire that would include [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_under_Japanese_rule) and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_Korea).[^cite_note-Batty_2005-46] During [World War II](32927.md), Japanese expansionism with its imperialist aspirations through the [Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_East_Asia_Co-Prosperity_Sphere) would incorporate Korea, Taiwan, much of eastern China and Manchuria, Hong Kong, and Southeast Asia under its control establishing itself as a maritime colonial power in East Asia.[^cite_note-53]

### Contemporary era

See also: [Pacific Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Century)

After a century of exploitation by the European and Japanese colonialists, post-colonial East Asia saw the [defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) and [occupation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan) by the victorious Allies. The end of World War II did not result in east Asian countries obtaining independence or national unification.[^cite_note-\:02-39]<sup>: 4 </sup> Independence and national unification were primary concerns for the first generation of east Asian post-World War II leaders.[^cite_note-\:02-39]<sup>: 4 </sup>

The [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War) resumed after the defeat of the Japanese, with the Communists defeating the Nationalist Republic of China government. The [government of the Republic of China retreated to Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_the_government_of_the_Republic_of_China_to_Taiwan) and the [People's Republic of China was proclaimed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) on 1 October 1949.

Post-war, the [Korean peninsula was partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Korea), leading to the development of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea ([North Korea](21255.md)) and the Republic of Korea ([South Korea](27019.md)). The [Korean War](16772.md) (1950–1953) increased regional and international tensions.[^cite_note-\:12222-54]<sup>: 163 </sup> The northeast part of East Asia hardened along communist and anti-communist lines.[^cite_note-\:12222-54]<sup>: 163 </sup> South Korea, Taiwan, and the United States have increased their ties.[^cite_note-\:12222-54]<sup>: 163 </sup>

During the latter half of the 20th century, the region would see the [post war economic miracle of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_post-war_economic_miracle), which ushered in three decades of unprecedented growth, only to experience an [economic slowdown during the 1990s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Decade_\(Japan\)), but nonetheless Japan continues to remain a global economic power. East Asia would also see the [economic rise of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Hong_Kong), [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_of_the_Han_River), and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Miracle), in addition to the respective handovers of [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Hong_Kong) and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Macau) near the end of the 20th century.

The onset of the 21st century in East Asia led to the integration of [China into the global economy](19284336.md) through its entry in the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_and_the_World_Trade_Organization) while also enhancing its [emerging international status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China's_peaceful_rise) as a potential world power reinforced with its aim of restoring its historical established significance and enduring international prominence in the world economy.[^cite_note-Kort_2005_7-2][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-Paul_2012_114-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Dahlman-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-Dahlman-58][^cite_note-60]

As of at least 2022, the region is more peaceful, integrated, wealthy, and stable than at any time in the previous 150 years.[^cite_note-\:Ma\&Kang-61]<sup>: 183 </sup>

## Definitions



Three sets of possible boundaries for the [Central Asia](6742.md) region that overlap with conceptions of East Asia

In common usage, the term "East Asia" typically refers to a region including [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China), [Japan](15573.md), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea) and [Mongolia](19271.md).[^cite_note-Paul_2012_114-56][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuaHu20143-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

[China](5405.md), Japan, and Korea represent the three core countries and civilizations of traditional East Asia, as they once had a shared written language, a shared culture, and a shared Confucian societal value system (involving shared Confucian philosophical tenets) once instituted by Imperial China.[^cite_note-Prescott_2015_3-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-Yoshimatsu_2014_1-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-Hazen_2005_1-73] Other usages define China, Japan, North Korea, South Korea and Taiwan as countries that constitute East Asia based on their geographic proximity as well as historical and modern cultural and economic ties, particularly with [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_influence_on_Japanese_culture) and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_influence_on_Korean_culture) in having retained strong cultural influences that originated from China.[^cite_note-Prescott_2015_3-69][^cite_note-Hazen_2005_1-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Some scholars include [Vietnam](202354.md) as part of East Asia as it has been considered part of the greater [Chinese cultural sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cultural_sphere). Though Confucianism continues to play an important role in Vietnamese culture, Chinese characters are no longer used in its written language and many scholarly organizations classify Vietnam as a Southeast Asian country.[^cite_note-Prescott_2015_6-77][^cite_note-Miller_2007_xi-78][^cite_note-afe.easia.columbia.edu-79] Mongolia is geographically north of China yet Confucianism and the Chinese writing system and culture had limited impact on Mongolian society. Thus, Mongolia is sometimes grouped with Central Asian countries such as [Turkmenistan](198149.md), [Kyrgyzstan](170131.md), and [Kazakhstan](16642.md).[^cite_note-Prescott_2015_6-77][^cite_note-Miller_2007_xi-78] [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) and [Tibet](31516.md) are sometimes seen as part of Central Asia (see also [Greater Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Central_Asia)).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

Broader and looser definitions by international agencies and organisations such as the [World Bank](36752.md) refer to East Asia as the "three major Northeast Asian economies, i.e. China, Japan, and South Korea", as well as Mongolia, North Korea, the [Russian Far East](173135.md), and [Siberia](29639.md).[^cite_note-83] The [Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_Foreign_Relations) includes the Russia Far East, Mongolia, and [Nepal](171166.md).[^cite_note-Northeast_Asia-84] The World Bank also acknowledges the roles of Chinese special administrative regions [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md), as well as [Taiwan](25734.md), a country with limited recognition. The Economic Research Institute for Northeast Asia defines the region as "China, Japan, the Koreas, Nepal, Mongolia, and eastern regions of the [Russian Federation](25391.md)".[^cite_note-85]

### Alternative definitions

See also: [Pacific Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Asia)

In the context of business and economics, "East Asia" is sometimes used to refer to the geographical area covering ten [Southeast Asian](28741.md) countries in [ASEAN](106539.md), [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China), Japan, and Korea. However, in this context, the term "[Far East](11672.md)" is used by the Europeans to cover ASEAN countries and the countries in East Asia. On rare occasions, the term is also sometimes taken to include [India](14533.md) and other South Asian countries that are not situated within the bounds of the Asia-Pacific, although the term [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) is more commonly used for such a definition.[^cite_note-93]

Observers preferring a broader definition of "East Asia" often use the term [Northeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Asia) to refer to China, the Korean Peninsula, and Japan, with the region of [Southeast Asia](28741.md) covering the ten [ASEAN](106539.md) countries. This usage, which is seen in economic and diplomatic discussions, is at odds with the historical meanings of both "East Asia" and "Northeast Asia".[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] The [Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_Foreign_Relations) of the United States defines Northeast Asia as Japan and Korea.[^cite_note-Northeast_Asia-84]

## Climate



East Asia map of [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification).

East Asia is home to many climatic zones. It also has unique weather patterns such as the [East Asian rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_rainy_season) and the [East Asian Monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Monsoon).[^cite_note-97]

### Climate change

Main article: [Climate change in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Asia)



Climate change is expected to exacerbate [heat stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_stress) over at the [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain), which is particularly vulnerable as widespread [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) results in very moist air. There is a risk that agricultural labourers will be physically unable to work outdoors on hot summer days at the end of the century, particularly under the scenario of greatest emissions and warming.

[^cite_note-Kang2018-98]

Like the rest of the world, East Asia has been getting warmer due to climate change, and there had been a measurable increase in the frequency and severity of [heatwaves](293270.md).[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99]<sup>: 1464 </sup> The region is also expected to see the intensification of its monsoon, leading to more flooding.[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99]<sup>: 1459 </sup> China has notably embarked on the [sponge cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge_cities) program, where cities are designed to increase the area of [urban green spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_green_space) and [permeable pavings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permeable_paving) in order to help deal with [flash floods](244391.md) caused by greater precipitation extremes.[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99]<sup>: 1504 </sup> Under high-warming scenarios, "critical health thresholds" for heat stress during the 21st century will be at times breached,[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99]<sup>: 1465 </sup> in areas like the [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain).[^cite_note-Kang2018-98]

China, Japan and the Republic of Korea are expected to see some of the largest economic losses caused by sea level rise.[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99] The city of [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) is projected to experience the single largest *annual* economic losses from sea level rise in the world, potentially reaching US\$254 million by 2050. Under the highest climate change scenario and in the absence of adaptation, cumulative economic losses caused by sea level rise in Guangzhou would exceed US\$1 trillion by 2100.[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99] [Shanghai](27643.md) is also expected to experience annual losses of around 1% of the local GDP in the absence of adaptation.[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99] The [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) basin is a sensitive and biodiverse ecosystem, yet around 20% of its species may be lost throughout the century under 2 °C (3.6 °F) and \~43% under 4.5 °C (8.1 °F).[^cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-99]<sup>: 1476 </sup>

## Culture

Further information: [Sinosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosphere)

Main category: [Culture of East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Culture_of_East_Asia)

The culture of East Asia, has been [deeply influenced by China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosphere), as it was the civilization that had the most dominant influence in the region throughout the ages that ultimately laid the foundation for East Asian civilization. The vast knowledge and ingenuity of Chinese civilization and the classics of Chinese literature and culture were seen as the foundations for a civilized life in East Asia. [Imperial China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_China) served as a vehicle through which the adoption of Confucian ethical philosophy, Chinese calendar system, political and legal systems, architectural style, diet, terminology, institutions, religious beliefs, [imperial examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_examination#East) that emphasised a knowledge of Chinese classics, political philosophy and cultural value systems, as well as historically sharing a common writing system reflected in the histories of [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_writing_system) and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_writing_system).[^cite_note-Goscha_2016-123][^cite_note-Kang_2012_33–34-19][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-Reischauer-126][^cite_note-Hazen_2005_1-73]

The Imperial Chinese tributary system was the bedrock of network of trade and foreign relations between China and its East Asian tributaries, which helped to shape much of East Asian affairs during the ancient and medieval eras. Through the tributary system, the various dynasties of Imperial China facilitated frequent economic and cultural exchange that influenced the cultures of Japan and Korea and drew them into a Chinese international order.[^cite_note-Amy_Chua,_Jed_Rubenfeld_2014_121–122-127] The Imperial Chinese tributary system shaped much of East Asia's foreign policy and trade for over two millennia due to Imperial China's economic and cultural dominance over the region, and thus played a huge role in the history of East Asia in particular.[^cite_note-Warren_I._Cohen_2000-23][^cite_note-Amy_Chua,_Jed_Rubenfeld_2014_121–122-127] The relationship between China and its cultural influence on East Asia has been compared to the historical influence of [Greco-Roman civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_civilization) on classical Western civilisation.[^cite_note-Reischauer-126][^cite_note-Amy_Chua,_Jed_Rubenfeld_2014_121–122-127][^cite_note-Goscha_2016-123]

Since the late 19th century, the initially unequal encounter with [Western influences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) has also shaped East Asia, primarily in Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan.[^cite_note-128]

### Sports



Japan vs. Mongolia in baseball at the [2014 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Asian_Games)

[Baseball](3850.md) is one of the main sports in East Asia, having been introduced through mid-19th century American contact and further spread by the Japanese Empire.[^cite_note-131] The game has gained [millions of fans in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_in_China) since the 2010s.[^cite_note-132]

In [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics), Japan was the first Asian nation to hold the [Olympic Games](22576.md) after two unsuccessful 1940 attempts in [Sapporo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Winter_Olympics) and [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Summer_Olympics) were cancelled due to World War II.[^cite_note-133] [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) saw Japan and South Korea host the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), marking the first time two nations served as hosts of the competition together, as well as the first World Cup in Asia.[^cite_note-134] Between 2018 and 2022, three Olympic Games were held in the region: [Pyeongchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyeongchang_County) hosted the Winter Games in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Winter_Olympics), Tokyo held the Summer Games in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics), and Beijing became the first city to host both Summer and Winter Olympics after hosting the Winter Games in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics), having run the Summer Games in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] The Tokyo 2020 and Beijing 2022 events were significantly scaled back due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Asia), with the Tokyo Games being postponed until 2021.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

Main article: [East Asian Youth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Youth_Games)

Following the discontinuation of the [East Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Games) after the 2013 edition, the [East Asian Youth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Youth_Games) were established as their successor. Organized by the East Asian Games Association (EAGA) they are held every four years among athletes from East Asian countries and territories of the [Olympic Council of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Council_of_Asia) (OCA), as well as [Guam](11974.md), a member of the [Oceania National Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_National_Olympic_Committees). Although the inaugural edition was originally scheduled for [Taichung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taichung), Taiwan, in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_East_Asian_Youth_Games), it was cancelled; the first edition to be held took place in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_East_Asian_Youth_Games).

It is one of five Regional Games of the OCA. The others are the [Central Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asian_Games), the [Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Games) (SEA Games), the [South Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Games), and the [West Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asian_Games).
