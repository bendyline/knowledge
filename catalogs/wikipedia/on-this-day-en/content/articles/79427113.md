The **77th Primetime Emmy Awards** honored the best in American [prime time](24973.md) television programming from June 1, 2024, until May 31, 2025, as chosen by the [Academy of Television Arts & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Television_Arts_&_Sciences).[^cite_note-2] The awards ceremony was held live on September 14, 2025, at the [Peacock Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_Theater) in [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles), California, and was preceded by the [77th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Primetime_Creative_Arts_Emmy_Awards) on September 6 and 7. During the ceremony, [Emmy Awards](6579025.md) were presented in 26 categories. The ceremony was produced by Jesse Collins Entertainment, directed by Alex Rudzinski, and broadcast in the United States by [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) and [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+). [Nate Bargatze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Bargatze) hosted the event.

At the main ceremony, *[The Studio](72258571.md)* won four awards, including [Outstanding Comedy Series](4719642.md), and *[The Pitt](77660135.md)* won three awards, including [Outstanding Drama Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Drama_Series). *[Adolescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence_\(TV_series\))* led all shows with six wins, including [Outstanding Limited or Anthology Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Limited_or_Anthology_Series). Other winning programs included *[Hacks](67541962.md)*, *[Last Week Tonight with John Oliver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Week_Tonight_with_John_Oliver)*, and *[Severance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_\(TV_series\))* with two wins each, and *[Andor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andor)*, *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert)*, *[The Penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Penguin_\(TV_series\))*, *[Slow Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Horses)*, *[SNL50: The Anniversary Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_50th_Anniversary_Special)*, *[Somebody Somewhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somebody_Somewhere_\(TV_series\))*, and *[The Traitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Traitors_\(American_TV_series\))* with one each. Including Creative Arts Emmys, *The Studio* led all shows with 13 wins, while [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO), alongside its partner [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max), and [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) led all networks with 30 wins each.

The nominations for the 77th Primetime Emmy Awards were announced on July 15, 2025, at the Television Academy's Wolf Theatre in [North Hollywood, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hollywood,_Los_Angeles), hosted by actor [Harvey Guillén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Guill%C3%A9n) and actress [Brenda Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Song), along with Television Academy chair [Cris Abrego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cris_Abrego).[^cite_note-3] The nominees for [Outstanding Reality Competition Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Reality_Competition_Program) and [Outstanding Talk Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Talk_Series) were revealed on *[CBS Mornings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Mornings)* a few hours prior.[^cite_note-4] Including nominations at the [77th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Primetime_Creative_Arts_Emmy_Awards), *[Severance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_\(TV_series\))* led all programs with 27 nominations, followed by *[The Penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Penguin_\(TV_series\))* with 24 nominations.[^cite_note-5] *[The Studio](72258571.md)* became the most-nominated first-year comedy series in the awards' history with 23 nominations; the [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV_\(streaming_service\)) comedy surpassed the record held by the sports comedy *[Ted Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lasso)*, which received 20 nominations in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/73rd_Primetime_Emmy_Awards). The series also tied *[The Bear](71135592.md)*'s 23 nominations in the [previous year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) for most nominations for a comedy series in a single season.[^cite_note-Studio_Record-6]

[Sterling K. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_K._Brown), nominated for [Outstanding Lead Actor in a Drama Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Drama_Series) for his role in *[Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_\(2025_TV_series\))*, tied with [Andre Braugher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Braugher) and [Don Cheadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cheadle) for most nominations by a Black male performer with 11 nominations each.[^cite_note-7] At age 77, [Kathy Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Bates) became the oldest nominee for [Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Drama_Series) for her performance in *[Matlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matlock_\(2024_TV_series\))*; she surpassed the record previously held by [Angela Lansbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Lansbury) who was nominated at age 70 in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) for her role in *[Murder, She Wrote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder,_She_Wrote)*.[^cite_note-8] At age 21, [Bella Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Ramsey) was the youngest two-time nominee for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series for their performance in *[The Last of Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_of_Us_\(TV_series\))*.[^cite_note-Variety_Emmys_Diversity_Report-9] Ramsey was also the first openly [non-binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary) person to be nominated more than once for an acting Emmy.[^cite_note-10] At age 83, for his role on *[Shrinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrinking_\(TV_series\))*, [Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford) became the second oldest nominee for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series behind [Alan Arkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Arkin), who was nominated in that category at age 86 in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) for his work on *[The Kominsky Method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kominsky_Method)*.[^cite_note-11] [Ayo Edebiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayo_Edebiri), nominated for both [Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](3621935.md) and [Outstanding Directing for a Comedy Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Directing_for_a_Comedy_Series) for her work on *The Bear*, was the first Black woman nominated for both acting and directing in the same year. At age 29, Edebiri also became the youngest Black woman to receive three acting nominations in her career.[^cite_note-Variety_Emmys_Diversity_Report-9] For the first time in Emmys history, the nominees for [Outstanding Writing for a Comedy Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Writing_for_a_Comedy_Series) had at least one woman included in each of the writing teams.[^cite_note-Variety_Emmys_Diversity_Report-9] [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) and its streaming service [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max) led all networks and platforms with 142 nominations; [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) came in second place with 120 nominations.[^cite_note-12]

The winners were announced on September 14, following the Creative Arts Emmys on September 6 and 7. With 30 total wins each, HBO and its streaming service HBO Max tied with Netflix to lead all networks and platforms.[^cite_note-13] Combined with its nine Creative Arts Emmys, *The Studio* was the most awarded comedy in a single year with thirteen wins, breaking *The Bear*'s record of eleven from the previous year.[^cite_note-14] It also surpassed the record for most wins by a freshman comedy series which was previously set by *The Bear* with ten awards in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards).[^cite_note-The_Studio-15] Seth Rogen won four awards for the aforementioned program to tie [Moira Demos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moira_Demos), [Dan Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Levy_\(Canadian_actor\)), and [Amy Sherman-Palladino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Sherman-Palladino) for the most wins for an individual in one year.[^cite_note-16] *[The Pitt](77660135.md)* became the first [medical procedural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_drama) to win Outstanding Drama Series since *[ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ER_\(TV_series\))* in 1996.[^cite_note-17] [Tramell Tillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramell_Tillman) was the first Black performer to win Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Drama Series for his work on *Severance*.[^cite_note-18] At age 15, for his role in *[Adolescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence_\(TV_series\))*, [Owen Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Cooper) became the youngest male acting winner in Emmy history, breaking a record previously held by [Scott Jacoby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Jacoby_\(actor\)) who won Outstanding Performance by an Actor in a Supporting Role in a Drama in [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) at age 16 for *[That Certain Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Certain_Summer)*.[^cite_note-19]

Winners are listed first, highlighted in **boldface**, and indicated with a double dagger (‡).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] For simplicity, producers who received nominations for program awards, as well as nominated writers for [Outstanding Writing for a Variety Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Writing_for_a_Variety_Series), have been omitted.

### Bob Hope Humanitarian Award

The [Bob Hope Humanitarian Award](1193824.md) was presented to married actors [Ted Danson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Danson) and [Mary Steenburgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Steenburgen), in recognition of "a lifetime of extraordinary philanthropy, activism and unwavering commitment to global good". This marked the first time the award was presented to a couple.[^cite_note-23]

## Ceremony information

Nate Bargatze in 2017.

[Nate Bargatze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Bargatze) hosted the ceremony.

On March 5, 2025, the [Academy of Television Arts & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Television_Arts_&_Sciences), also known as the Television Academy, announced that the 77th Primetime Emmy Awards would be held on September 14.[^cite_note-CBS_Emmys-29] [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) broadcast the ceremony as part of a rotating deal among the "Big Four" networks ([ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company), CBS, [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company), and [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC)) signed in 2018.[^cite_note-30] Additionally, it was announced that the ceremony would be available live and on-demand via [Paramount Skydance](79917576.md)'s streaming service [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+).[^cite_note-CBS_Emmys-29] The ceremony was produced by Jesse Collins Entertainment (Jesse Collins, Dionne Harmon, and Jeannae Rouzan-Clay) for the third consecutive year.[^cite_note-31] On April 16, comedian [Nate Bargatze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Bargatze) was announced as the ceremony's host.[^cite_note-32]

During a press conference held on September 11 promoting the show, producer Collins revealed that the ceremony would feature reunions in tribute to the 25th anniversaries of [The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW) and [WB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_WB) comedy-drama series *[Gilmore Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilmore_Girls)* and CBS reality competition series *[Survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_\(American_TV_series\))*, and the 35th anniversary of NBC's police procedural franchise *[Law & Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_&_Order_\(franchise\))*. Additionally, the telecast would pay tribute to the 40th anniversary of the NBC sitcom *[The Golden Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Golden_Girls)* with country singer [Reba McEntire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire) and [Little Big Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Big_Town)'s [Karen Fairchild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Fairchild) and [Kimberly Schlapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberly_Schlapman) performing the series' theme song, "[Thank You for Being a Friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thank_You_for_Being_a_Friend)".[^cite_note-33] [Eric Dane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Dane) and [Jesse Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Williams_\(actor\)) were initially scheduled as co-presenters to honor the 20th anniversary of ABC medical drama *[Grey's Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey's_Anatomy)*, but Dane was absent from the ceremony while Williams presented alone. According to Collins, he told reporters for *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* after the awards ceremony, "I just was told that he wasn't able to make it, and fortunately, Jesse was able to present by himself."[^cite_note-34] The following month, Dane revealed during an interview with *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* that he was recovering from a fall he suffered in his kitchen shortly before the ceremony. He had previously been diagnosed with [ALS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALS) in April 2025.[^cite_note-35]

In an effort to shorten winners' acceptance speeches and have the telecast run on time, Bargatze revealed that he would donate \$100,000 to the [Boys & Girls Club of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_&_Girls_Clubs_of_America). However, for every second an award winner's speech went over the 45-second limit \$1,000 would be deducted from the donation.[^cite_note-36] Similarly, for every second under the limit another \$1,000 would be added.[^cite_note-37] At the end of the show, the sum of money slated be donated fell \$60,000 below zero due to several winners' speech going over the time limit.[^cite_note-38] As a result, Bargatze stated that CBS would donate the full \$100,000 to the charity, and that he would add an additional \$250,000.[^cite_note-39]

Several other individuals were involved with the production of the telecast. Alex Rudzinski and [Rickey Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickey_Minor) served as director and musical director for the ceremony, respectively.[^cite_note-40] *[Entertainment Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Tonight)* co-anchors [Kevin Frazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Frazier) and [Nischelle Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nischelle_Turner) hosted a [red carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_carpet) pre-show preceding the telecast.[^cite_note-41] Comedians [Mikey Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikey_Day), [James Austin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Austin_Johnson), and [Bowen Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowen_Yang) participated in an opening comedy sketch with Bargatze parodying how [Philo Farnsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philo_Farnsworth) invented television, similar to the "Washington's Dream" sketch featured in a [50th season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_season_50) episode of *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*.[^cite_note-42]

### Category and rule changes

On January 8, 2025, the Television Academy announced rule changes that affected the directing and guest performer awards. Starting with the 77th ceremony, individuals or directing teams are permitted to submit multiple episodes for consideration in the directing categories, provided that the episodes are from different programs. In previous years, directors or directing teams could only submit one entry per category.[^cite_note-43] Additionally, any performer who previously won or has been nominated in the lead or supporting acting categories would be ineligible to submit a performance of the same character in the same series for consideration in the guest performance categories in subsequent years.[^cite_note-44]

On July 22, 2025, it was announced that [Outstanding Writing for a Variety Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Writing_for_a_Variety_Series) would be presented at the main ceremony, replacing [Outstanding Writing for a Variety Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Writing_for_a_Variety_Special), which was presented at the Creative Arts ceremony held on September 7.[^cite_note-45] Two weeks later, the Television Academy moved the presentation for [Outstanding Variety Special (Live)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Variety_Special_\(Live\)) to the main ceremony as well.[^cite_note-46]

### Critical reviews and viewership

The broadcast generally received mixed to negative reviews from critics.[^cite_note-47] Aramide Tinubu, writing for *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))*, found the onslaught of surprise winners adding to the excitement, but criticized host Bargatze's performance and wrote, "He lacked the enthusiasm and gumption needed to lead a three-hour-long award program and often seemed stunned to be in the spotlight." She also noted that the decision to have a forty-five second acceptance speech rule to caused winners to hurriedly rush through their speeches while contrasting that with presenters' introductions that seemed to drag down the ceremony.[^cite_note-48] Daniel Feinberg of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* wrote, "It was an ill-conceived mess, punctuated by well-deserved wins and emotional and effective speeches, but rarely helped by Bargatze's consistently uneasy performance."[^cite_note-49] Brian Lowry of *[TheWrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheWrap)* commented, "Unfortunately, racing through the speeches came at too high a cost for the show itself, one that played out in uneven pacing and awkward moments strewn throughout." He concluded that due to the time limit, the ceremony was "an otherwise bland affair, which, one suspects, is just the way the Television Academy wanted it."[^cite_note-50]

In a more positive review, Robert Lloyd of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* complimented the show and wrote of host Bargatze, "If your goal was to avoid insulted celebrities, social media outrage or petulant notes from the White House, you could have done no better than to hire Bargatze, a clean, calm, classical, noncontroversial, nonpolitical, very funny, very successful comedian." He also reserved praise for several presenters, including [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert), [Brad Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Garrett), and [Ray Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Romano).[^cite_note-51] Matt Roush of *[TV Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Guide_\(magazine\)#TV_Insider)* wrote, "There was a sense that for all of the ribbing, this Emmys broadcast was a pleasurable celebration of an ever-evolving medium." He also gave high marks toward Bargatze, but criticized the acceptance speech 45-second rule as tiresome.[^cite_note-52] David Nemetz of *[TVLine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVLine)* wrote, "These Emmys were strangely anti-TV at times, with a self-deprecating tone that bordered on masochistic. But they also showcased the best that TV has to offer, with surprise wins and stirring speeches that reminded us of the sheer quantity of top-notch programming we're blessed to have at our fingertips right now."[^cite_note-53]

The ceremony was viewed by 7.59 million people in the United States, making it the most-viewed Emmys in [four years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/73rd_Primetime_Emmy_Awards), representing a 10% increase over the previous year's ceremony.[^cite_note-Emmy_Ratings-54] It also achieved a 1.28 rating among adults ages 18–49.[^cite_note-Emmy_Ratings-54] Ratings figures included those who watched the telecast on CBS or streamed it on Paramount+.[^cite_note-55]
