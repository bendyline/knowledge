**Veneto**,[^cite_note-10] officially the **Region of Veneto**,[^cite_note-11] is one of the 20 [regions of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy), located in the [north-east of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Italy). It is the 4th most populous region in Italy, with a population of 4,857,460 as of 2026.[^cite_note-population-3] [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) is the region's capital while [Verona](263991.md) is the largest city.

Veneto was part of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) until the 5th century AD. Later, after a [feudal period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism), it was part of the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) until 1797. Venice ruled for centuries over one of the largest and richest [maritime republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics) and trade empires in the world. After the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) and the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), the [former Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Province) was combined with [Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy_\(historical_region\)) and re-annexed to the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) as the [Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy%E2%80%93Venetia), until that was [merged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification) with the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) in 1866, as a result of the [Third Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Italian_War_of_Independence) and of a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite_of_Veneto_of_1866).

Besides [Italian](14708.md), most inhabitants also speak [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Since 1971, the [Statute of Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Veneto) has referred to the region's citizens as "the Venetian people".[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Article 1 defines Veneto as an "autonomous Region", "constituted by the Venetian people and the lands of the provinces of Belluno, Padua, Rovigo, Treviso, Venice, Verona, and Vicenza", while maintaining "bonds with Venetians in the world". Article 2 sets forth the principle of the "self-government of the Venetian people" and mandates the Region to "promote the historical identity of the Venetian people and civilisation". Despite these affirmations, approved by the [Italian Parliament](1417859.md), Veneto is not among the [autonomous regions with special statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy#Autonomous_regions_with_special_statute), unlike its north-eastern and north-western neighbours, [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli-Venezia_Giulia) and [Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](246766.md) respectively.

Veneto is home to a notable nationalist movement, known as [Venetian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_nationalism) or Venetism. The region's largest party is [Liga Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Veneta), a founding component of [Lega Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Nord). The current [President of Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Veneto) is [Alberto Stefani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Stefani) (Liga Veneta–Lega Nord), elected in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Venetian_regional_election). An [autonomy referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Venetian_autonomy_referendum) took place in 2017: 57.2% of Venetians turned out, 98.1% voting "yes" to "further forms and special conditions of autonomy".

Having been for a long period in history a land of mass emigration, Veneto is today one of the greatest immigrant-receiving regions in the country, with foreign-born residents making up 13.9% of the population, the 7th-largest share among all regions.[^cite_note-foreignborn-17]

## History

### Venetic period

Between the 2nd and 1st millennium BC, the region was inhabited by the [Euganei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euganei). According to ancient historians, who perhaps wanted to link Venetic origins to legend of Roman origins in [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy), the [Veneti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Veneti) (often called the *Palaeoveneti*) came from [Paphlagonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphlagonia) in [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) at the time of the [Fall of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War) (12th century BC), led by prince [Antenor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenor_\(mythology\)), a comrade of [Aeneas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeneas). Other historians link Venetic origins with [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts).

In the 7th–6th centuries BC the local populations of Veneto entered into contact with the [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization) and the [Greeks](42056.md). Venetic culture reached a high point during the 4th century BC. These ancient Veneti spoke [Venetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetic_language), an [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages) language akin to, but distinct from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) and the other [Italic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italic_languages). Meanwhile, the Veneti prospered through their trade in [amber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber) and breeding of horses. [Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Este), [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), [Oderzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oderzo), [Adria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adria), [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza), [Verona](263991.md), and [Altino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altinum) became centres of Venetic culture. Over time, the Veneti began to adopt the dress and certain other customs of their [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) neighbours.



The [Tetrarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrarchy) were the four co-rulers who governed the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) as long as [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian)'s reform lasted. Here they are portrayed embracing, in a posture of harmony, in a porphyry sculpture dating from the 4th century, produced in [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia), located today on a corner of [St Mark's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mark's_Basilica) in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice).&#x20;

### Roman period

During the 3rd century BC, the [Veneti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Veneti), together with the [Cenomani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenomani_\(Cisalpine_Gaul\)) [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) on their western border, sided with the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), as [Rome](25458.md) expanded and struggled against the [Insubres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubres) and [Boii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boii_\(Cisalpine_Gaul\)) ([Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts)). During the [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War) (218 – 202 BC), the Veneti even sent a contingent of soldiers to fight alongside the Romans against Hannibal and the invading Carthaginians. These Venetians were among those slaughtered at the [Battle of Cannae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cannae) (216 BC).

In 181 BC a Roman [triumvirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumvirate) of [Publius Cornelius Scipio Nasica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publius_Cornelius_Scipio_Nasica_\(consul_191_BC\)), [Caius Flaminius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Flaminius_\(consul_187_BC\)), and [Lucius Manlius Acidinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Manlius_Acidinus) founded a Latin [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonies_in_antiquity) at [Aquileia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquileia) as a base to protect the territory of the Veneti from incursions of the hostile [Carni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carni) and [Histri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istria). From then on, Roman influence over the area increased. In 169 BC 1,500 more colonising families were sent by Rome to Aquileia. In 148 BC the *[Via Postumia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Postumia)* was completed connecting Aquileia to [Genoa](47332321.md). In 131 BC, the *Via Annia* joined [Adria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adria) to Patavium (modern [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua)) to Altinum to Concordia to Aquileia.

The [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) gradually transformed its alliance with the Veneti into a relationship of dominance. After the 91 BC Italic rebellion, the cities of the Veneti, together with the rest of *Transpadania*, were granted partial rights of [Roman citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_citizenship) according to the *[Lex Pompeia de Transpadanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Pompeia_de_Transpadanis)*. Later in 49 BC, by the *[Lex Roscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Roscia)* granted full Roman citizenship to the Veneti. The *[Via Claudia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Claudia_Augusta)* would be completed in AD 46 to connected Altinum, Tarvisium (modern Treviso), Feltria (modern [Feltre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feltre)), and Tridentum (modern Trento). From Tridentum it continued northwards to Pons Drusus and further to Augusta Vindelicorum (modern [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg)), and southwards from Trento to Verona and Mutina (modern [Modena](62418.md)).

After the [Battle of Philippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Philippi) (42 BC) ended the [Roman Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_civil_wars), the lands of the Veneti, together with the rest of [Cisalpine Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Gaul), ceased to be a province. Between 8 and 6 BC, [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) reorganized *Italia* into 11 *regions.* The territory of modern Veneto along with [Istria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istria), modern [Friuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli) and [Trentino-Alto Adige](246766.md) and eastern [Lombardy](43807.md) (including its cities of [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua), [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona), [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia), and [Sondrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sondrio)) became Region X (*Venetia et Histria*). Aquileia, although not officially the capital was the chief municipium of the region.[^cite_note-Venetiae-18] Meanwhile, under the [Pax Romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Romana), Patavium developed into one of the most important cities of northern Italy. Other Venetic cities such as Opitergium (modern [Oderzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oderzo)), Tarvisium, Feltria, Vicetia (modern Vicenza), Ateste (modern Este), and Altinum (modern Altino) adopted the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) language and the culture of Rome. By the end of the 1st century AD Latin had displaced the original [Venetic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetic_language).

In 166 AD the [Quadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadi) and [Marcomanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcomanni) invaded Venetia. It was the beginning of many barbarian invasions. [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius) retaining the regions of *Italia*, superimposed another layer of administration by ascribing Regions X and XI to the district of *Transpadana* under a *iuridicus*. The end of the 3rd c. brought further administrative changes when [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian) abolished the regions and districts and established *provinciae*. Thus, Region X (*Venetia et Histria*) became Province VIII (*Venetia et Histria*), being enlarged in the west up to the [Adda River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adda_River) governed by a *corrector* until 363 and from 368 to 373 by a *consularius* seated at Aquileia. *Venetia et Histria* remained one of the 16 Provinces of Italy in the 5th century when both [Alaric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaric_I) the Goth and then [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila_the_Hun) and the Huns devastated the area. Attila laid siege to Aquileia and turned it into a ruin in 452 AD. Many of the mainland inhabitants sought protection in the nearby lagoons which would become [Grado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grado,_Italy) in the east and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) more to the west. On the heels of the Huns came the Ostrogoths who not only invaded, but also settled down in the region, especially near Treviso where the penultimate king [Totila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totila) was born.[^cite_note-Venetiae_2-19]

During the mid-6th century, [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) reconquered Venetia for the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). An Exarch was established at [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna) while a [military tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tribune) was set up in Oderzo. Greek-Byzantine rule did not last long. Starting in 568 AD, the Lombards crossed the [Julian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Alps). These invaders subdivided the territory of Venetia into numerous feuds ruled by Germanic dukes and counts, essentially creating the division of Veneto from Friuli.

The invasion provoked another wave of migration from the mainland to the Byzantine controlled coast and islands. In 643 AD the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) conquered the Byzantine base at Oderzo and took possession of practically all of Veneto (and Friuli) except for Venice and Grado. The 36 Lombard duchies included the Venetian cities of [Ceneda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Veneto), Treviso, Verona, and Vicenza. A reminder of Lombard rule can be seen in the place names beginning with the word *Farra*.

### Middle Ages



The defence of the [Carroccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroccio) during the [battle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano) (1176) by [Amos Cassioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Cassioli) (1832–1891)

By the middle of the 8th century, the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) had assumed political control of the region and the mainland of Veneto became part of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire). Though politically dominant, these Germanic invaders were gradually absorbed into the Venetian population over the centuries. In the late 9th century, [Berengar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berengar_I_of_Italy), [Margrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrave) of the [March of Friuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Friuli) was [elected king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elective_monarchy) of Italy. Under his tumultuous reign, the March of Friuli was absorbed into the [March of Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Verona) so that Verona's territory contained a large portion of Roman Venetia.

In the 10th century, the mainland of Veneto, after suffering raids from the [Magyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people) and the [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples), was incorporated into the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). Gradually, the communes of the mainland grew in power and wealth. In 1167 an alliance (called the [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League)) was formed among the Venetian cities such as Padua, Treviso, Vicenza, and Verona with other cities of [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) to assert their rights against the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor).

The emperor [Frederick I, Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa) (reigned 1155–1190) conducted six military campaigns in Italy, which was under his [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). Originally he wanted to confront the [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily) in the south. However, his intervention in Italy was opposed by several Italian cities, particularly [Milan](36511.md), which he had partially destroyed during his second campaign. A dispute with [Pope Alexander III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III) (1159–1181) developed because Frederick endorsed [antipope Victor IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_\(1159%E2%80%931164\)), who had been elected in opposition to Alexander. Opposition against Frederick in northern Italy grew and the [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League), a league formed by several cities, fought him. Frederick was defeated at the [Battle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano) in 1176. Preliminary peace negotiations took place at [Anagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagni) (the Peace of Anagni) in 1176.[^cite_note-20] Negotiations involving all the concerned parties to reach a formal peace treaty took place in Venice where a conference was scheduled for July 1177. The [doge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge_\(title\)) [Sebastiano Ziani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastiano_Ziani) (1172–1178) was to act as an intermediary.

The [Second Treaty of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Constance) in 1183 confirmed the [Peace of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Venice) of 1177 in which the cities agreed to remain part of the Empire as long as their jurisdiction over their own territories was not infringed upon. The league was dissolved at the death of [Emperor Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in 1250. This period also witnessed the founding of the second oldest [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) in Italy, the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) founded in 1222. Around this time, [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) also served as home to [St. Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua), the beloved Saint called simply "il Santo" ("the Saint") by the inhabitants of the town.

### Venetian Republic

Main articles: [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and [History of the Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Republic_of_Venice)



An 18th-century view of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) by [Canaletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaletto)

As the barbarians were interested in the wealth of the mainland, part of the Venetian population sought refuge on some of the isolated and unoccupied islands in the lagoon, from which the city of *Venetiae* or [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) was born. After a period of [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) domination in 8th century, Venice became an independent maritime [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) ruled by its elected [doge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge_\(title\)).

The Republic became a commercial superpower and its influence lasted through the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) and the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance). In fact, the [Venetian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) enjoyed 1100 years of uninterrupted influence throughout the [Mediterranean](19006.md). By the 16th century, the Venetian Republic dominated Veneto, [Friuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli), parts of [Lombardy](43807.md) and [Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagna), [Istria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istria), [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia), the [Ionian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Islands) of Corfu, Cefalonia, Ithaca and Zante. From the 13th to 17th centuries, it held the island of [Crete](6591.md) and from the mid-15th to mid-16th century, the island of [Cyprus](5593.md).

Venetian mainland holdings led to Venetian involvement in European and in particular, Italian politics. Cities had to be fortified, two impressive examples are Nafplio in Peloponnese and [Palmanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmanova) in Friuli. The wise rule and prosperity brought by the "Serenissima" (most serene republic) made the cities of the *terra firma* willing subjects. Eastern Islands served as useful ports for Venetian shipping. However, as the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) grew more powerful and aggressive, Venice was often put on the defensive. Ottoman control of the eastern Mediterranean and the discoveries of sea routes to Asia around Africa and of the [Americas](29833.md) had a debilitating effect on the Venetian economy.

In 1797, [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) invaded the territory of the [Venetian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice). Overwhelmed by more powerful forces, [Doge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge_of_Venice) [Ludovico Manin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovico_Manin) resigned and retired to his villa at Passariano in Friuli and the thousand year old Republic disappeared as an [independent state](23604120.md). This proved very unpopular in the mainland cities where sympathies were strong with the Republic of Venice. By the [Treaty of Campoformio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Campo_Formio) signed on 17 October 1797, part of the Venetian mainland was handed over to [Francis II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) and a western part was annexed to the French backed [Cisalpine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic). The territory soon reverted to Napoleon in 1801.

### Habsburg rule



The [Napoleonic Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Kingdom_of_Italy) from 1806 to 1810 included [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia) that had belonged to Venice until 1797.

In 1805–1806, Veneto was conquered by [Napoleon's armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) and included in the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)). During 1809, the region revolted against the French-Italian rule,[^cite_note-21] supporting the advancing Austrian troops during the [War of the Fifth Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Fifth_Coalition). It was mainly a peasant revolt, less organised than the nearby [Andreas Hofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Hofer)'s revolt, while urban national guard troops fought on the French-Italian side. After the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), 1814–1815, Venetia was the eastern half of the [Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy%E2%80%93Venetia), a separate kingdom within the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire).

During the 1848 [First Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Italian_War_of_Independence), Venetia rose against the central Austrian government, forming the [Republic of San Marco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_San_Marco). After 17 months, it sought annexation to the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_\(1720%E2%80%931861\)), forming an Italian confederation against Austria, and using the Italian tricolour in its flag. However, the other Italian states left the war in May 1848, and Sardinia surrendered (August 1848, then March 1849),  Venetia stood alone. It surrendered on 24 August 1849, when the [Siege of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Venice_\(1848\)) ended.[^cite_note-Encyc_Rev_1848-22]

The Austrian imperial government was unpopular among upper and middle classes, because of [Metternich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metternich)'s anti-[liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) politics, turned by Emperor [Franz Joseph I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I) into [neo-absolutism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I#Imperial_absolutism,_1848%E2%80%931860) after 1848, and for not granting Lombardo–Venetia any real autonomy: It was considered less than a [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state); nevertheless, it was appreciated (especially among lower classes) for its efficient and honest administration; by 1848–1849,  there would be no further revolt against the Austrian rule.

### United Italy

See also: [Plebiscite of Veneto of 1866](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite_of_Veneto_of_1866)



The 13th-century [Castel Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Brando) in [Cison di Valmarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cison_di_Valmarino), [Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treviso)



King [Victor Emmanuel II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_II) of Italy entering [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) during the [Third Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Italian_War_of_Independence) (1866) among a profusion of [tricolour flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy)

Venetia remained under Austrian control until the [Austro-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Prussian_War) in 1866, when the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) joined on the Prussian side and was promised Venetia in exchange for its assistance. Austria offered to sell Venetia to Italy, but the Italy refused, seeing it as a dishonourable choice. This resulted in a new southern front for Austria, the [Third Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Italian_War_of_Independence).

Once the wars ended, the [Treaty of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Vienna_\(1866\)) ceded the region to neutral France, but left the fortresses under Austrian control for a time. Following protests, the Austrians left, and the French ceded it to Italy on 20 October. A referendum – where only 30% of the adult population voted (as was customary in that period), and did so under government pressure[^cite_note-23] – was held on 21–22 October, and ratified the handover, with a 99.99% majority for Italy.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-Borsetto-25][^cite_note-26]

Uneven economic development reduced many to poverty, making the 19th century, and the first half of the 20th, a period of emigration. Millions of Venetians left their homes and their native land, to seek opportunities in other parts of the world. Many settled in South America (especially in [Brazil](3383.md)), and others in [Australia](4689264.md), [Canada](5042916.md), and the [United States of America](3434750.md).

Those who remained in Veneto would experience the turmoil of two [world wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_war). In 1915, Italy entered the [First World War](4764461.md) on the side of the [France](5843419.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) (after extricating itself from its alliance with [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary)). Veneto became a major battlefront. After Italian forces suffered an enormous defeat at [Caporetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobarid) in November 1917, the combined Austro-Hungarian and German forces advanced – almost unhindered – through Veneto, towards Venice, until reaching the [Piave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piave_\(river\)) River. The [Battle of the Piave River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Piave_River) prevented these invading troops from advancing further, and was celebrated in *[La Leggenda del Piave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Leggenda_del_Piave)*. Between 24 October and 3 November 1918, Italy launched the decisive [Battle of Vittorio Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vittorio_Veneto), whose outcome assured Italy's victory.

During the [fascist era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism), due to the [nationalist policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_nationalism#Fascism_and_World_War_II_\(1922_to_1945\)) the Venetian language, as other local languages, was banned in public spaces.[^cite_note-27] Between 1943 and 1945, Veneto belonged to the [Italian Social Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic), while the province of Belluno was part of the [Prealpine Operations Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prealpine_Operations_Zone). Many towns in the region were bombed by [the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) during the Second World War. Most hit were Treviso and Vicenza, along with the industrial area around [Marghera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marghera). The [Armistice of Villa Giusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Villa_Giusti) which ended warfare between [Italy](14532.md) and [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), was signed at [Villa Giusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Giusti) near Padua.

After the Second World War, many Venetians emigrated to Western European countries; in many of these places, their descendants have maintained the use of their ancestral Venetian dialects.

## Geography

### Geomorphology



Relief map of Veneto

Veneto is the 8th largest region in Italy, with a total area of 18,398.9 km<sup>2</sup> (7,103.9 sq mi). It is located in the north-eastern part of Italy and is bordered to the east by [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli-Venezia_Giulia), to the south by [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md), to the west by [Lombardy](43807.md) and to the north by [Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](246766.md). In its northernmost corner it also borders [Austria](26964606.md).

The north–south extension of Veneto is 210 km (130 mi) from the [Austrian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria%E2%80%93Italy_border) to the mouth of the [River Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)). By area, 29% of its surface is mountainous ([Carnic Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnic_Alps), eastern [Dolomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomites) and [Venetian Prealps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Prealps)). The highest massif in the Dolomites is the [Marmolada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmolada)-massif at 3,342 m (10,965 ft). Other dolomitic peaks are the [Tre Cime di Lavaredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tre_Cime_di_Lavaredo) and the [Pale di San Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_group). The Venetian Prealps are not as high and range between 700 m (2,300 ft) and 2,200 m (7,200 ft). A distinctive landmark of the Pre-alps are the cave formations, including chasms and sink holes; the [Spluta Della Preta](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spluga%20della%20Preta), situated in the [Monti Lessini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lessinia) chain in the province of [Verona](263991.md), has an explored depth of 985 m (3,232 ft), being the deepest cave in Italy. Fossil deposits are also abundant there.

The [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley), covering 57% of Veneto, extends from the [mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountains) to the [Adriatic sea](23275478.md), broken only by some low hills: [Euganean Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euganean_Hills), [Berici Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berici_Hills), [Colli Asolani](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colli%20Asolani) and [Montello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montello_\(TV\),_Italy), which constitute the remaining 14% of the territory. The plain itself is subdivided into the higher plain (gravel-strewn and not very fertile) and the lower plain (rich in water sources and arable terrain). The lower plain is both a mainstay of [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) production and the most populated part of the region.



The [Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige) in [Verona](263991.md)

Several rivers flow through the region: the [Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)), [Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige), [Brenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenta_\(river\)), [Bacchiglione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacchiglione), [Livenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livenza), [Piave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piave_\(river\)), and [Tagliamento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagliamento). The eastern shore of the largest lake in Italy, [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda), belongs to Veneto. The coastline covers approximately 200 km (120 mi), of which 100 km (62 mi) are beaches.

The coasts of the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md) are characterised by the [Venetian Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Lagoon), a flat terrain with ponds, [marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh) and islands. The [Po Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)) to the south features sandbars and dunes along the coastline. The inland portion contains cultivable land recently reclaimed by a system of [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) and [dykes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levee). [Fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) ponds have been created there as well. The delta and the lagoon are a stopping-point for [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration).

### Climate

The climate changes significantly from one area to another: while it is continental on the plains, it is milder along the Adriatic coast; around the [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda) and in the hilly areas. The lowlands are often covered by thick fog, in winter; precipitations that are scarce – 750 mm per year – close to the [river Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Po), but are more abundant – from 750 to 1100 mm per year – at higher altitudes; the highest values – up to 3200 mm per year – are recorded in the Bellunese Prealps, near [Mount Pasubio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pasubio?action=edit\&redlink=1) and on the [Asiago plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiago_plateau).

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Veneto)



[Palazzo Balbi, Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Balbi,_Venice), the seat of the regional government

Veneto is a [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy). The [President of Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Veneto), colloquially nicknamed Governor or even *Doge* in remembrance of Venice's traditional head of state, is also the head of the [Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Veneto). [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) is exerted by the [Regional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Veneto), the local parliament. The [Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Veneto) (i.e. the law establishing and regulating the regional institution, which was first promulgated on 22 May 1971), uses the term "people" for Venetians, but, like in the case of [Sardinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinian_people), this is not a legal recognition of any differences from other Italian citizens. Moreover, the region is not granted a form of autonomy comparable to that of neighbouring [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli-Venezia_Giulia) and [Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](246766.md).[^cite_note-29] This is the reason why many municipalities have held referendums in order to be united to these regions. According to [Robert D. Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Putnam), the "institutional performance" of Veneto's regional government is higher than average in Italy and Veneto belongs to the "civic North".[^cite_note-30]

Traditionally a very [Catholic](606848.md) region, Veneto was once the heartland of [Christian Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democracy_\(Italy\)), which won a record 60.5% of the vote in the [1948 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Italian_general_election_in_Veneto), polled above 50% in each and every general and regional election until 1983 and governed the region since its establishment in 1970 to 1994. After that, Veneto has been a stronghold of the [centre-right coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_coalition_\(Italy\)), which has governed the region since 1995, under President [Giancarlo Galan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giancarlo_Galan) ([Forza Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forza_Italia_\(1994\))/[The People of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_of_Freedom), 1995–2010), [Luca Zaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Zaia) ([Liga Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Veneta)–[Lega Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Nord), 2010–2025) and [Alberto Stefani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Stefani) (Liga Veneta–[Lega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_\(political_party\)), 2025–present). In the [2025 regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Venetian_regional_election) Liga Veneta was the largest party with 36.3% of the vote, followed by the three main Italian parties, the [Brothers of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_of_Italy) (18.7%), the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Italy\)) (16.6%), and [Forza Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forza_Italia_\(2013\)) (6.3%).

### Venetian nationalism



[Flag of the Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Republic_of_Venice)



[Flag of Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Veneto)

[Venetian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_nationalism) is a regionalist/nationalist [political movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_movement) which gained prominence in Veneto during the 1970s and 1980s, demanding more autonomy, a [special statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_with_special_statute) or even independence, and promoting Venetian culture, [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language) and history.[^cite_note-31] This is the political background in which the [Liga Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Veneta) (LV) was launched in 1980. Other regionalist/nationalist groupings, including [Liga Veneta Repubblica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Veneta_Repubblica) (LVR), [North-East Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-East_Project) and the avowed separatist [Veneto State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneto_State), [Venetian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Independence) (IV) and [Plebiscito.eu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscito.eu), emerged but they have never touched the popularity of the LV, which was a founding member of [Lega Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Nord) in 1991. Currently, other than the LV, [Resist Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resist_Veneto) and the LVR are represented in the Council.

IV and other alike groups have been long proposing a referendum on the independence of Veneto from [Italy](14532.md). After the [Regional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Veneto) approved a resolution on self-determination (with an explicit reference to a referendum) in November 2012,[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] a referendum bill was proposed in April 2013.[^cite_note-34] Plebiscite 2013 organised an [online referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Venetian_independence_referendum), with no official recognition, for 16–21 March 2014.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] According to organisers, turnout was 63.2% (2.36 million voters) and 89.1% of participants (56.6 of all eligible voters) voted yes.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Several news sources, however, contested these results, saying that participants were at most 135,000 (3.6% of eligible voters) based on public independent web traffic statistics.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] On 22 October 2017 an official [autonomy referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Venetian_autonomy_referendum) took place in Veneto: 57.2% of Venetians participated and 98.1% voted "yes".

Veneto has 4,857,460 inhabitants as of 2026, ranking it as the 5th most populated region in Italy. Veneto is also the 5th most densely populated region at 264.8 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup>. This is particularly true in the provinces of [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) and [Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treviso), where the inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup> are above 300. [Belluno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belluno) is the least densely populated province, with 54.6 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup>. 49.4% of the population are male, and 50.6% are female. Minors make up 14.4% of the population, and seniors make up 25.3%.[^cite_note-population-3]

Like the other regions of [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) and [Central Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Italy), though with a certain time lag, Veneto has been experiencing a phase of very slow population growth caused by the dramatic fall in fertility. The overall population has so far been increasing – though only slightly – due to the net immigration started at the end of the 1980s, after more than 20 years of massive exodus from the poorer areas of the region.

### Migration

Nearly 3 million Venetians left their country between 1861 and 1961 to escape poverty.[^cite_note-46] Many emigrated to [Brazil](3383.md) and [Argentina](18951905.md). After [World War II](32927.md), they moved to other European countries. In 2008, there were 260,849 Venetian citizens living outside of Italy (5.4% of the region's population), the largest number was found in Brazil, with 57,052 Venetians, followed by [Switzerland](26748.md), with 38,320, and Argentina, with 31,823. There are several million people of Venetian descent around the world, particularly in Brazil, in the states of [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md), [Santa Catarina](256305.md), [São Paulo](229422.md) and [Paraná](229411.md). Local names in [Southern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Region,_Brazil) such as Nova Schio, [Nova Bassano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Bassano), [Nova Bréscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Br%C3%A9scia), Nova Treviso, [Nova Veneza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Veneza,_Santa_Catarina), Nova Pádua and Monteberico indicate the Venetian origin of their inhabitants.[^cite_note-47] In recent years, people of Venetian descent from Brazil and Argentina have been migrating to Italy.[^cite_note-48]

Due to the impressive [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) of the last two decades, Veneto has turned into a land of immigration and has been attracting more and more immigrants since the 1990s.

### Religion



[St Mark's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mark's_Basilica), the seat of the [Patriarch of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice). It is one of the best known examples of [Italo-Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Byzantine) architecture.

[^cite_note-49]

Veneto converted to [Christianity](5211.md) during Roman rule. The region venerates as its patrons the 2nd-century bishop [St. Hermagoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermagoras_of_Aquileia) and his deacon St. Fortunatus, both of Aquileia and both martyrs. [Aquileia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquileia) became the metropolitan see of Venetia. Aquileia had its own [liturgical rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_liturgy) which were used throughout the dioceses of Veneto until the [later Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages) when the [Roman Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rite) replaced the [Aquileian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquileian_Rite). By the 6th century the [bishop of Aquileia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Aquileia) claimed the title of [patriarch](175409.md). Rejection of the [Second Council of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Constantinople) (553) led to a [schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schism_of_the_Three_Chapters) wherein the bishops of Aquileia, [Liguria](71864.md), [Aemilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aemilia_\(Roman_province\)), [Milan](36511.md) and of the [Istrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istrian) peninsula all refused to condemn [the Three Chapters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-Chapter_Controversy) leading to the churches of Veneto to break communion with the [Church of Rome](606848.md).[^cite_note-50] The invasion of the non-Catholic Lombards in 568 only served to prolong the schism until 606 and then finally 699 when the [Synod of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_de_synodo_ticinensi) definitively ended the schism.[^cite_note-51]

In 2004, over 95% of the population claimed to be [Roman Catholic](606848.md). The region of Veneto along with the regions of Friuli and Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol form the ecclesiastical region of Triveneto under the [Patriarchate of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice). The Patriarchate of Venice is an archdiocese and [metropolitan see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of an ecclesiastical region which includes suffragan episcopal sees of Adria-Rovigo, Belluno-Feltre, Chioggia, Concordia-Pordenone, Padua, Treviso, Verona, Vicenza, and [Vittorio Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Veneto).[^cite_note-52]

The [Archdiocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) of Venice was elevated to an honorary [Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate) by the pope on 8 October 1457 when the [Patriarchate of Grado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Grado), a successor to the [Patriarchate of Aquileia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Aquileia_\(Episcopal\)), was suppressed. The first patriarch of Venice was St. [Laurence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Giustiniani), a nobleman of the Giustiniani family.

During the 20th century the patriarchs were usually appointed [cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholicism\)), and three cardinal patriarchs, [Giuseppe Sarto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_X), [Angelo Roncalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII), and [Albino Luciani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_I), were elected pope: Pius X, John XXIII, and John Paul I, respectively. The [Patriarchate of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice) claims [St. Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_the_Evangelist) the Evangelist as its patron. The same saint, symbolised by a winged lion, had become the typical symbol of the Venetian Republic and is still represented on many civic symbols.

## Economy



United Colors of [Benetton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benetton_Group) store in [Parma](24231.md), [Italy](14532.md)

Under Austrian rule, Veneto's agriculturally-based economy suffered, which later led to mass emigration. But, since the 1970s it has seen impressive development, thanks to the so-called "Veneto development model" that is characterised by strong [export-oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export-oriented_industrialization) entrepreneurship in traditional [economic sectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy) (€64.47 billion of exports in 2019 [^cite_note-53]) and close social cohesion[^cite_note-54] – making it actually the third richest region in terms of total GDP (€166.4 billion) after [Lombardy](43807.md) and [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

Geography and historical events have determined the present social and economic structure of the region, centred on a broad belt running from east to west. The plain and the Alpine foothills are the most developed areas in contrast to the [Po delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)) and the mountainous areas, with the exception of the surroundings of [Belluno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belluno). This is why the [Alps](981.md) and the province of [Rovigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovigo) are suffering more than other areas, from a trend of declining and [ageing population](720684.md).

### Agriculture



Glera grapes on the vine in the [Prosecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecco) zone, pre-veraison

Though its importance has been decreasing for the past 20–30 years, agriculture continues to play a significant role in the regional economy. The [agricultural sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) of Veneto is among the most productive in Italy. However, it is still characterised by an intensive use of labour rather than capital, due to the specialisation in [market gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_gardening), fruit-growing and vine-growing throughout the plain and the foothills, requiring very much handicraft. In the south and in the extreme east of the region, [grain crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal) are more common and land holdings are larger than in the rest of the region; mechanisation is more advanced here. The cattle stock, although declining, still represented 15% of the national stock.[^cite_note-regportraits1-57] [Fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing) is also still important in coastal areas.

The main agricultural products include [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [green peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pea), vegetables, apples, cherries, [sugar beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet), forage, tobacco, hemp. Moreover, Veneto is one of Italy's most important wine-growing areas, producing [wines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_wine), such as [Prosecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecco), [Valpolicella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valpolicella), and [Soave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soave_\(wine\)). Overall, Veneto produces more bottles of [DOC wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominazione_di_origine_controllata) than any other area in Italy. The [Amarone della Valpolicella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarone_della_Valpolicella), a wine from the hills around Verona, is made with high-selected grapes and is among the more expensive red wines in the world.

### Industry



View of [Marghera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marghera)

In the last 30–40 years industrialisation transformed the appearance of the landscape, especially in the plains.

The regional [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) is especially made of small and medium-sized businesses, which are active in several sectors: food products, wood and furniture, leather and footwear, textiles and clothing, gold jewelry, but also chemistry, metal-mechanics and electronics. This has led to the establishment of a strongly export-orientated system of industries.

Typical of Veneto is the partition of the territory into industrial districts, which means that each area tends to specialise in a specific sector. The province of Venice hosts large metallurgical and chemical plants in [Marghera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marghera) and [Mestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestre), but is also specialised in glass handicraft ([Murano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murano)). The province of Belluno hosts the so-called eyeglasses district, being the largest world manufacturer [Luxottica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxottica) a firm domiciled at Agordo. Other important firms are [Safilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safilo), De Rigo, Marcolin.

Veneto is home to a thriving [fashion industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion) with brands such as [Benetton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benetton_Group), [Bottega Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottega_Veneta), [Geox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geox), [Diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_\(brand\)), [Golden Goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Goose), [Calzedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calzedonia), [Pal Zileri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pal_Zileri), [Dainese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainese), [Lotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotto_Sport_Italia), [Marzotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marzotto), [Tecnica Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecnica_Group) all originating from the area.

Other large Venetian companies are [Aprilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aprilia), [AGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGV_\(helmet_manufacturer\)), [Campagnolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campagnolo), [De'Longhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De'Longhi), [Fedrigoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedrigoni), [Laverda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laverda), [Permasteelisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permasteelisa), [Pinarello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinarello), [Wilier Triestina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilier_Triestina), [Zamperla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamperla).

During the last 20 years, a large number of Venetian companies relocated their plants (especially the most dangerous and polluting productions) in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), especially [Romania](25445.md). The Romanian city of [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara) is also called "the newest Venetian province".[^cite_note-58]

### Art and architecture



[Scrovegni Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrovegni_Chapel) in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) contains a [fresco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresco) cycle by [Giotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giotto), an important masterpiece of [Western art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_art).

The Middle Ages stimulated the creation of monumental works such as the complex of churches on the island of [Torcello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torcello), in the Venetian lagoon, with the [Cathedral of Santa Maria Assunta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torcello_Cathedral) founded in 639, its bell tower erected in the 11th century and the adjacent *Martyrium of Santa Fosca* built around the 1100, notable for the mosaics. They saw the construction of the [Basilica of San Zeno Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Zeno) in [Verona](263991.md), which was Veneto's main centre for that esthetic movement and we note, by the mixture of styles that Verona was an important crossroads to the north of [Europe](9239.md). Examples of [Gothic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_art), in addition to the Venetian church of [Santa Maria Gloriosa dei Frari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_Gloriosa_dei_Frari) and that of Santi Giovanni e Paolo, are the [Scaliger Tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaliger_Tombs) in the historical centre of Verona.

While in Veneto [Byzantine art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Art) was important, an element of innovation was brought to Padua by [Giotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giotto), bearer of a new pictorial tradition: that of Tuscany. Towards the 1302 he was commissioned by [Enrico Scrovegni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Scrovegni) to paint the family chapel, now known just by the name of [Scrovegni Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrovegni_Chapel), one of the most important artistic monuments of [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) and Veneto. The influences of the contribution of Giotto were felt immediately, as in the frescoes of [Giusto de' Menabuoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giusto_de'_Menabuoi) in the [Baptistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptistry) near the [Cathedral of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Padua) and those of [Altichiero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altichiero) in the [Basilica of Saint Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Anthony_of_Padua).



[Giorgione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgione)'s *[The Tempest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tempest_\(Giorgione\))*

After a phase of development of [Gothic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_art), with the creation of important works including the [Ca' d'Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca'_d'Oro) and the [Doge's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge's_Palace,_Venice) in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), and the churches of [Santa Maria Gloriosa dei Frari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frari) and of [Saints John and Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo,_Venice) in Venice, the influence of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) ushered in a new era. In addition to [Donatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatello), an important Venetian Renaissance artist was [Andrea Mantegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Mantegna) (1431–1506), whose most important work in Veneto is perhaps the *[San Zeno Altarpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Zeno_Altarpiece_\(Mantegna\))*, found in Verona. With the mainland expansion of the Venetian Republic and the consolidation of its institutions, there was also an artistic development of exceptional stature: Mantegna, [Vittore Carpaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittore_Carpaccio), [Giovanni Bellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Bellini_\(painter\)), [Cima da Conegliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cima_da_Conegliano), [Pordenone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pordenone) laid the foundations for what would be the age of Venetian painting.

[Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) was a cradle of the Venetian Renaissance, Where influences from [Tuscany](21967242.md) and [Umbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbria) filtered north. Amongst the Renaissance artists who worked there were [Donatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatello), who worked on an altar of the [Basilica of Saint Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Anthony), and [Pisanello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisanello), whose works are mainly in [Verona](263991.md), for example, the fresco of [Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George) in the [Church of St. Anastasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St._Anastasia).



The [Prato della Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato_della_Valle) in Padua, a work of [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance) architecture

In the first phase with Carpaccio and Bellini, the influences of international painting were still evident and the references to [Flemish art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_art) were numerous. Artists of the successive phase included [Giorgione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgione), [Titian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titian), [Sebastiano del Piombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastiano_del_Piombo) and [Lorenzo Lotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Lotto). Giorgione and Titian developed an original and innovative style, which characterised the painters of the [Venetian school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_school_\(art\)) rather than other traditions. Giorgione's enigmatic style infused his work with allegory, and he created his paintings with less reliance on a preparatory drawing than previous painters. This innovation was looking for the imitation of natural phenomena by creating atmospheres with the colours and shifting the emphasis from the pursuit of artistic perfection. *[The storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tempest_\(Giorgione\))* (1506–1508), now in the [Accademia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallerie_dell'Accademia) in Venice, is an example of this use of colour, where the mixture colour and texture continue indefinitely without preparatory drawing for the painting work gives a special atmosphere.

Titian, born in Belluno [Pieve di Cadore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieve_di_Cadore), brought forward the use of this technique without pictorial design, creating masterpieces such as the *[Assumption of the Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_the_Virgin_\(Titian\))* (1516–1518),[^cite_note-dteofmkng-67] an [altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar) made by imposing visible sizes on the main altar of the Basilica of [Santa Maria Gloriosa dei Frari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Gloriosa_dei_Frari) in Venice, a work whose suggestion is due to the use of colour. At the end of his long life, he had acquired fame and commissions across the continent.

[Tintoretto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tintoretto) (1518–1594) recast Roman Mannerism in a Venetian style, less linear, and with more use of colour to distinguish forms, highlighting the bright prospects for its operations, giving unusual deformations of perspective, to increase the sense of tension in the work.[^cite_note-68] His studio was prolific. Palaces and churches of Venice abound with his paintings. The [Scuola Grande di San Rocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Grande_di_San_Rocco) alone sports 66 paintings by this painter. The [San Giorgio Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Giorgio_Maggiore) houses a huge canvas by him depicting the *Last Supper*.

[Paolo Veronese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Veronese) (1528–1588) was about as prolific as Tintoretto, with works that celebrated the Venetian state,[^cite_note-Rosand,_107-69] as well as decorating houses of Venetian nobles. He decorated large portions of the [Palazzo Ducale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge's_Palace) and the decoration of many villas [Palladian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Palladio), including [Villa Barbaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Barbaro).

[Jacopo Bassano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Bassano) (1517–1592) and [Lorenzo Lotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Lotto) were active in the mainland, and reflected some of the influences of [Milanese painters](36511.md) with the introduction of images taken from real life, enriched by a touch of drama.



[Villa Cornaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Cornaro)

In architecture, [Andrea Palladio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Palladio) (1508–1580), born in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), completed some highly influential works, including [Villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladian_Villas_of_Veneto) in the mainland, in [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Vicenza), [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Padua) and [Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Treviso). In Venice, he designed the [Basilica of San Giorgio Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Giorgio_Maggiore), the [Il Redentore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Redentore), and [Zitelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zitelle) on the island of [Giudecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giudecca). Palladian Villa architecture, in masterpieces such as [Villa Emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Emo), [Villa Barbaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Barbaro), [Villa Capra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Capra), and [Villa Foscari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Foscari), evoked the imagined grandeur of antique classical Roman villas. This aesthetic, through his publications, proved popular and underwent a revival in the [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) period. In his villas, the owner shall permit the control over production activities of the surrounding countryside by structuring the functional parts, such as [porch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porch), close to the central body. In the case of Villa Badoer, the open barn, formed by a large circular colonnade, enclosing the front yard in front of the villa allows you to create a space that recalls the ancient idea of the [Forum Romanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_Romanum), and bringing all campaign activities to gravitate in front of the villa itself.



*[Antonio Canova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Canova)'s [Psyche Revived by Love's Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyche_Revived_by_Cupid's_Kiss)*

The research style of Palladio has created an architectural movement called [Palladianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladianism), which has had strong following in the next three centuries, inspiring architects, some of them his direct students, including [Vincenzo Scamozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Scamozzi), after the death of the teacher who completed several works, including the first [Teatro Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Olimpico) in [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza).



The Church of [Santa Maria della Salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Salute) in Venice

The 18th-century [Venetian school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_School_\(art\)) comprises many artists. Important painters include [Giambattista Tiepolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Tiepolo), his son [Giandomenico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giandomenico_Tiepolo), [Giambattista Piazzetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Piazzetta), [Niccolò Bambini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Bambini), [Pietro Longhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Longhi), [Marco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Ricci) and [Sebastiano Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastiano_Ricci), [Sebastiano Bombelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastiano_Bombelli), [Gianantonio Fumiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianantonio_Fumiani), [Gaspare Diziani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspare_Diziani), [Rosalba Carriera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalba_Carriera), and the architect/painter [Girolamo Mengozzi Colonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girolamo_Mengozzi_Colonna).

Sculptors include [Morlaiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianmaria_Morlaiter), [Filippo Parodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Parodi), [Bernard Torretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Torretti) and his nephew [Giuseppe Torretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Torretti), and at the end of the republic [Antonio Canova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Canova). Some other important artists are the architects [Girolamo Frigimelica Roberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girolamo_Frigimelica_Roberti), [Giorgio Massari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Massari), [Scalfarotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Scalfarotto), and [Tommaso Temanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommaso_Temanza); the carver [Andrea Brustolon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Brustolon); playwrights [Carlo Goldoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Goldoni) and [Gaspare Gozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspare_Gozzi); the poets [Alessandro Labia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Labia?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [George Whisker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Whisker?action=edit\&redlink=1); and composers [Benedetto Marcello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedetto_Marcello) and [Antonio Vivaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Vivaldi).

[Giovanni Battista Tiepolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo) (1696–1770), described as "the greatest decorative painter of eighteenth-century Europe, as well as its most able craftsman."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevey1980193-70] was a [painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting) and [printmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printmaker), who together with [Giambattista Pittoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Pittoni), [Canaletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaletto), [Giovan Battista Piazzetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovan_Battista_Piazzetta), [Giuseppe Maria Crespi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Maria_Crespi) and [Francesco Guardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Guardi) formed the ultimate group of traditional great Venetian [old master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_master) painters of that period. [Perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_\(graphical\)) played a central role in Tiepolo's representations, and was forced beyond the usual limits in his ceiling decorations depicting levitating figures viewed from below.

Another peculiar feature of Venetian art is [landscape painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_painting), which sees in [Canaletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Canal) (1697–1768) and [Francesco Guardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Guardi) (1712–1793) the two leading figures. Canaletto's rigorous perspective studies make for an almost "photographic" reality, in contrast to Guardi's more subjective [capriccios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capriccio_\(art\)).

[Antonio Canova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Canova) (1757–1822), born in [Possagno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possagno), was the greatest of the [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) artists.[^cite_note-71] The [Temple of Possagno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Canoviano), which he designed, financed, and partly-built himself,[^cite_note-72] is among landmarks of neo-classical architecture. His most important works include *[Psyche Revived by Love's Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyche_Revived_by_Cupid's_Kiss)* and *[The Three Graces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Graces_\(Canova\))*.

After the [fall of the Republic of Venice in 1796](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice#Fall), every city in Veneto created its own form of art. Important was, however, the role of [Accademia di Belle Arti in Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_di_Belle_Arti_\(Venezia\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was able to attract many young artists from the surrounding territory.

Among the many artists which were important in modern ages were [Guglielmo Ciardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Ciardi), who incorporated the experience of [macchiaioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macchiaioli) movement, uniting the typical colour of the classic Venetian school, and yet bringing out from his paintings a chromatic essence, [Giacomo Favretto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Favretto), who too as Ciardi, enhanced the colour, which was sometimes very pronounced, painter [Frederick Zandomeneghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Zandomeneghi), who deviates from the tradition of Venetian colouring to venture in a style similar to French impressionism, and finally [Luigi Nono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Nono_\(painter\)), whose works feel realistic, even if, in addition to painting genre scenes, includes portraits of finity for psychological enhancement.

### Language

Main article: [Venetian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language)



[Venetian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language) distribution in [Triveneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triveneto):&#x20;

&#x20; Areas where Venetian is spoken

&#x20; Areas where Venetian is spoken alongside other languages ([Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language), [Emilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_language), [Friulian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friulian_language), [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language), [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language), [Chakavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakavian_language), [Istriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istriot_language) and formerly [Dalmatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatian_language)) and areas of linguistic transition (with [Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language) and with Emilian)

&#x20; Areas of influence of Venetian (over Lombard and over [Ladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladin_language))

Most of the people of Veneto speak [Italian](14708.md), along with widespread usage of local varieties of the [Venetian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language). Within Venetian there are distinct sub-groups centered on the major cities, and distinctions are also found between rural and urban dialects and those spoken in northern mountainous areas and on the plain.[^cite_note-74]

Venetian dialects are classified as [Western Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Romance_languages). Linguists identify five major types of Venetian: an Eastern or Coastal (Venice) group, a Central (Padua, Vicenza, Polesine) group, a Western (Verona) group, a North-Central (Treviso) group, and a Northern (Belluno, Feltre, Agordo, Cadore, [Zoldo Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoldo_Alto)) group of dialects. All dialects are mutually intelligible to varying degrees, are descended from [Vulgar Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulgar_Latin) and influenced to varying degrees by Italian. Venetian is first attested as a written language in the 13th century. It is also spoken outside of Veneto, such as in [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli-Venezia_Giulia) (including in Trieste, see [Triestine dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triestine_dialect)), Istria in Croatia and Slovenia and Dalmatia in Croatia, [Brazil](3383.md) ([Talian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talian_dialect)) and [Mexico](3966054.md) ([Chipilo Venetian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipilo_Venetian_dialect)).

The language of Venice enjoyed substantial prestige in the days of the [Venetian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Republic), when it attained the status of a [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) in the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean). Notable Venetian-language authors include the playwrights [Carlo Goldoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Goldoni) (1707–1793) and [Carlo Gozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Gozzi) (1720–1806), while [Ruzante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Beolco) (1502–1542) is best known for his rustic comedies "cast in mainland peasant *Pavan* 'Paduan'".[^cite_note-75]

[Ladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladin_language), also Romance, is spoken in parts of the [province of Belluno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Belluno), especially in the municipalities of [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md), [Livinallongo del Col di Lana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livinallongo_del_Col_di_Lana) and [Colle Santa Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colle_Santa_Lucia), while [Cimbrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimbrian_language) (Germanic) is spoken in two villages (Roana and Giazza respectively) of the [Seven Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sette_Comuni) and the [Thirteen Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Communities). These are two historical groups of villages of Cimbric origin, which for a long time formed two distinct "commonwealths" under the rule of the Republic of Venice, among others. Furthermore, in the area around [Portogruaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portogruaro) people speak [Friulian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friulian_language).

As the region does not enjoy a special status of autonomy, [minority languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_language) are not granted any form of official recognition. A motion to recognise Venetian as an official [regional language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_language) has been approved by the [regional Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Veneto).[^cite_note-76]

### Literature

Main article: [Venetian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_literature)

[Venetian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_literature), referring to works written in the Venetian language, experienced an initial period of splendour in the 16th century with the success of artists such as [Ruzante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruzante). It then reached its zenith in the 18th century, thanks especially to dramatist [Carlo Goldoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Goldoni). Subsequently, literary production in Venetian declined, following the [collapse of the Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice#Fall). Nevertheless, during the 20th century there was a literary revival featuring lyrical poets such as [Biagio Marin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biagio_Marin) of Grado.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Venetian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_cuisine)



Waiter pouring [Prosecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecco)

Cuisine is an important part of the culture of Veneto, and the region is home to some of the most recognisable dishes, desserts and wines in Italian, European and worldwide cuisine.

#### Wines and drinks

Main article: [Venetian wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_wine)

Veneto is an important wine-growing area producing: [Soave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soave_\(wine\)), [Bardolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardolino_DOC), [Recioto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_wine), [Amarone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarone), [Torcolato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_wine), [Prosecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecco), [Tocai Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenache), [Garganega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garganega), [Valpolicella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valpolicella), [Verduzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verduzzo), [Raboso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raboso_\(grape\)), [Moscato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_\(grape_and_wine\)), [Cabernet Franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabernet_Franc), [Pinot Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinot_noir), [Pinot Grigio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinot_gris), and [Merlot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlot). Homemade [wine making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winemaking) is widespread. After making wine, the alcohol of the [pressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressed_\(wine\)) grapes is distilled to produce [grappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappa) or *graspa*, as it is called in the local language.

[Prosecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecco) is a dry [sparkling wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparkling_wine).[^cite_note-DuBose-77][^cite_note-Kinssies-78] It is made from the glera grape, a white grape formerly known as Prosecco,[^cite_note-79] which is traditionally grown in an area near [Conegliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conegliano) and [Valdobbiadene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdobbiadene), in the hills north of [Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treviso).[^cite_note-DuBose-77] The name of *Prosecco* is derived from the northern Italian village of Prosecco (Trieste), where this grape variety is believed to have originated.[^cite_note-Kinssies-78][^cite_note-NYT-80]

Spritz, in the Venetian language also called "spriss" or "spriseto" depending on the area, usually consists of equal parts of sparkling wine, [Aperol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aperol) and sparkling water. Campari may also be used instead of Aperol.

#### Cheeses



[Asiago cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiago_cheese) and crackers

Cheeses of Veneto include: [Asiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiago_cheese) ([PDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_Geographical_Status)), [Piave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piave_\(cheese\)) (PDO), [Monte Veronese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Veronese) (PDO), [Morlacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morlacco), [Grana Padano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano) (PDO).

#### Salamis and meats

The [sopressa vicentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopressa) ([PDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_Geographical_Status)) is an aged salami, cylindrical in shape and prepared with raw, quality pork meat. It may or may not include [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic) in its ingredients and comes in medium and large sizes. [Prosciutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosciutto) Veneto Berico-Euganeo (PDO) is obtained from the fresh [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat) of a top breed of adult hogs. The aroma is delicate, sweet and fragrant.

#### Vegetables

[Radicchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicchio) rosso di [Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treviso) ([PGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_Geographical_Status)) is a peculiar vegetable with a faintly [bitter taste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_tastes) and a crunchy texture. The production area encompasses many town districts in the provinces of Treviso, Padua and Venice. The radicchio Variegato di [Castelfranco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelfranco_Veneto) (PGI) has a delicate and slightly sweet taste and a crunchy texture. [Veronese Vialone Nano Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vialone_Nano) from [Verona](263991.md) (PGI) is a type of rice with short, plump grains, which have a creamy consistency when cooked. They are commonly used in [risotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risotto) dishes and have a high starch content. The [Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean) of [Lamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamon) (PGI) is particularly prized for its delicate flavour and extremely tender skin. The White [Asparagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asparagus) of [Cimadolmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimadolmo) (PGI) has a peculiar scent and a very delicate taste. The White Asparagus of [Bassano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassano_del_Grappa) is a typical product of the northern part of the [province of Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Vicenza). The [San Zeno di Montagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Zeno_di_Montagna) ([Verona](990199.md)) [chestnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut) has Protected Geographical Status.

A slice of [tiramisù](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiramis%C3%B9)

### Music

Main articles: [Music of Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Veneto) and [Music of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Venice)



[Rovigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovigo), chiesa della Beata Vergine del Soccorso, named *La Rotonda*, 27 september 2022: [Giuliano Carella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliano_Carella) conducted the orchestra *[I Solisti Veneti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Solisti_Veneti)* in *La tromba, regina degli ottoni* (The trumpet, queen of the brass).

The city of Venice in Italy has played an important role in the development of the [music of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Italy). The Venetian state—i.e. the medieval Maritime Republic of Venice—was often popularly called the "Republic of Music", and an anonymous Frenchman of the 17th century is said to have remarked that "In every home, someone is playing a musical instrument or singing. There is music everywhere."[^cite_note-84]

In [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), musical ensembles such as the *Amici della Musica di Padova*, the *[Solisti Veneti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Solisti_Veneti)* and the Padova-Veneto Symphony are found. Concerts are often held in the historic [Loggia Comaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggia_Comaro?action=edit\&redlink=1), built in 1524. As well, the city is the site of the Teatro delle Maddalene, the Teatro delle Grazie, the Giuseppe Verdi Theater, and the [Cesare Pollini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Pollini?action=edit\&redlink=1) music conservatory.

[Rovigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovigo) is the site of the [Teatro Sociale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Sociale,_Rovigo?action=edit\&redlink=1), built in 1819. In the 20th century it was the venue for the career beginnings of [Tullio Serafin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullio_Serafin), [Beniamino Gigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beniamino_Gigli) and [Renata Tebaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renata_Tebaldi). The town of Rovigo is also the site of the [Francesco Venezze music Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovigo_Conservatory_of_Music).

The city of [Verona](263991.md) is the site of the Roman amphitheatre known as the "Arena" which has been hosting musical events since the 16th century, but more recently the spectacular outdoor staging of [Verdi's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi) [Aida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aida), an event staged for the first time in 1913. The city also has the Felice Evaristo Dall'Abaco music conservatory;

#### Theatres



[Teatro La Fenice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_La_Fenice)



Teatro Salieri

- **[Teatro La Fenice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_La_Fenice)** is the main opera house of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice). Repeatedly destroyed by fire and later rebuilt, it is home to an important opera season and to the International Festival of contemporary music. Teatro La Fenice in Venice was designed in 1790 by [Gian Antonio Selva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Antonio_Selva) for the society of the Venetian artistocracy, and the Venetian Theatre was built rapidly despite the many controversies about its location and its rational structure and neoclassical style.

- **[Teatro Malibran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Malibran)** is a Venetian theatre. In the 17th and 18th centuries it bore the name of Theater St. John Chrysostom.

- **[Teatro Stabile del Veneto "Carlo Goldoni"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Stabile_del_Veneto_%22Carlo_Goldoni%22?action=edit\&redlink=1)** corresponds to the ancient Teatro Vendramin, also known as San Salvador or San Luke, and was opened in 1622. The Teatro Carlo Goldoni is located in the vicinity of the [Rialto bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rialto_bridge), in the historical centre of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice). It is a film set, with rooms structured in four tiers of boxes, stalls and galleries. With a total of 800 seats, the stage is off 12 Underground and 11.20 deep and is [framed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_foundation?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron). The Teatro Goldoni season hosts Prose organised by the Teatro Stabile del Veneto "Carlo Goldoni", the review of Children's Theatre, opera, concerts, ballets and other events in the concession.

- **[Teatro Verdi (Padua)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Verdi_\(Padua\))** is the main theatre in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua). Commissioned by a society formed specifically for the purpose of establishing a major theatre in the city, it was built by the Paduan architect [Giovanni Gloria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Gloria?action=edit\&redlink=1) (c. 1684–1753) to designs by Antonio Cugini (1677–1765), an architect from [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia). It opened in 1751, and was known as the *Teatro Nuovo* until 1884. Substantial restoration work was carried out in 1847, 1884 and 1920. Currently the Teatro Verdi is the operational headquarters of the Teatro Stabile del Veneto.

- **[Teatro Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Olimpico)** is a theatre designed by the Renaissance architect [Andrea Palladio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Palladio) in 1580 and located in [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza). It is generally considered the first example of a modern indoor theatre set. The realisation of the theatre, in a pre-existing medieval complex, was commissioned by the Olympic Palladio for the staging of classical plays. Its construction began in 1580 and was inaugurated on 3 March 1585, after the realisation of the stationery scenes of [Vincenzo Scamozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Scamozzi). These wooden structures are the only of the Renaissance to be extant, however, they are still excellent condition. The theatre is still the seat of performances and concerts and has been included in 1994 in the list of World Heritage Sites' s [UNESCO](21786641.md), as other works by Palladio to [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza).

- **[Philharmonic Theater (Verona)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philharmonic_Theater_\(Verona\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)** is the main opera house of [Verona](263991.md). It is owned by the [Accademia Filarmonica di Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Filarmonica_di_Verona), since its foundation, but is used by the foundation of the Arena as the site of the opera season in winter.

- **[Teatro Filarmonico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Filarmonico)** in Verona (built in the XVIII century)

- **[Roman Theatre of Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_of_Verona)** is Verona's main arena, located in the northern part of the city at the foot of Colle San Pietro. This [theatre](20913771.md) was built at the end of [BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Christ), a period in which [Verona](263991.md) has seen from the monumental St. Peter of the hill. Before, it was built between the Stone Bridge and Gates of the embankments, which were built on Tyrol parallel to the theatre itself, in order to defend against the possible flooding of river. It is considered the largest Roman theatre in the north of [Italy](14532.md). Today it is used for theatrical and operatic productions during the summer.

[Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza) and the [Palladian Villas of Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladian_Villas_of_Veneto) are a number of [Palladian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladio) villas which are [World Heritage Sites](44940.md). UNESCO inscribed the site on the [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_List) in 1994.[^cite_note-91] At first the site was called "Vicenza, City of Palladio" and only buildings in the immediate area of Vicenza were included. Various types of buildings were represented including the [Teatro Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Olimpico), *[palazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo)* and a few [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa). Most of Palladio's surviving villas lay outside the site. However, in 1996 the number of [Palladian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladian) villas included in the site was expanded to include those in other parts of Veneto. The site was given its present name.

The term villa was used to describe a country house. Often rich families in Veneto also had a house in town called a palazzo. In most cases the owners named their palazzi and villas with the family surname, hence there is both a [Palazzo Chiericati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Chiericati) in Vicenza and a [Villa Chiericati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Chiericati) in the countryside, similarly there is a [Palazzo Foscari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca'_Foscari) in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) and a [Villa Foscari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Foscari) in the countryside. Somewhat confusingly there are multiple [Villas Pisani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Pisani_\(disambiguation\)), including two by Palladio.

There are these sorts of villas all over the Venetian plain, but especially in the provinces of [Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treviso), [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice). The date of construction of these villas ranges from the 15th to the 19th century. There are approximately five thousand Ville Venete, of which 1,400 are declared of historical and monumental interest.

Apart from the numerous Palladian villas, of which 24 are protected by [UNESCO](21786641.md), there are many villas spread across Veneto, mainly from the 16th, 17th and 18th centuries. ### Mountains



A ski resort in [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md)

[Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md) is situated in the province of Belluno and is one of the most exclusive mountain locations in Europe together with Kitzbühel in Austria and [St. Moritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Moritz) in Switzerland. It was scene of [the 1956 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Winter_Olympics) and hosted once again in [2026](38416091.md) alongside [Milan](36511.md). To the north there are the [Tre Cime di Lavaredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tre_Cime_di_Lavaredo), said to be a symbol of the Italian [Dolomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomites).

- [Auronzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auronzo_di_Cadore) is in the upper [Cadore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadore).

- [Arabba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livinallongo_del_Col_di_Lana) lies between the [Sella group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sella_group) and the [Marmolada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmolada).

### Thermal baths



Thermal baths in [Abano Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abano_Terme)

The [thermal baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_baths) of [Abano Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abano_Terme) are an important [tourist attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_attraction). [Montegrotto Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montegrotto_Terme) and [Recoaro Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoaro_Terme) are other resorts.

### Beaches

[Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice)'s [Lido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lido_di_Venezia) is an 11-kilometre-long [sandbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal), visited by many tourists every [summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer).

[Jesolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesolo) is one of the most important [seaside resorts](293990.md) on the Adriatic coast, just a few kilometres far from [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice). Every year Jesolo gives accommodation to over 4.5 million tourists.

[Caorle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caorle) has often received awards for one of the cleanest beaches in Italy. [Bibione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibione), [Eraclea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eraclea) and [Sottomarina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sottomarina) are resorts too. Albarella island is a [private island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_island) on the Lido. Alberoni Beach is set in a [nature reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve).
