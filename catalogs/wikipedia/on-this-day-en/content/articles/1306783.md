The **Royal Anglian Regiment** is an [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) [regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment) of the [British Army](4887.md). It consists of two Regular [battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) and one [Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Reserve_\(United_Kingdom\)) battalion. The modern regiment was formed in 1964, making it the oldest of the [line regiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_regiment) now operating in the British Army, and can trace its history back to 1685. The regiment was the first of the large infantry regiments and is one of the six regiments of the [Queen's Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Division).

## History

### Formation

The regiment was formed on 1 September 1964 as the first of the new [large infantry regiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_regiment), through the amalgamation of the four regiments of the [East Anglian Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglian_Brigade):[^cite_note-2]

- 1st (Norfolk and Suffolk) Battalion from the 1st Battalion of the [1st East Anglian Regiment (Royal Norfolk and Suffolk)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_East_Anglian_Regiment)

- 2nd (Duchess of Gloucester's Own Lincolnshire and Northamptonshire) Battalion – 1st Battalion, [2nd East Anglian Regiment (Duchess of Gloucester's Own Royal Lincolnshire and Northamptonshire)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_East_Anglian_Regiment)

- 3rd (16th/44th Foot) Battalion – 1st Battalion, [3rd East Anglian Regiment (16th/44th Foot)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_East_Anglian_Regiment)

- 4th (Leicestershire) Battalion – 1st Battalion, [Royal Leicestershire Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Leicestershire_Regiment).

The Royal Anglian Regiment was established to serve as the [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County) regiment for the following counties:

- [Bedfordshire](69769.md)

- [Cambridgeshire](6288.md)

- [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex)

- [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire)

- [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire)

- [Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire)

- [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk)

- [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire)

- [Rutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutland)

- [Suffolk](27886.md)

Initially formed of seven battalions (four [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_army) and three [Territorial Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Reserve_\(United_Kingdom\))), the regiment was reduced in 1975 with the loss of the 4th (Leicestershire) Battalion to three regular battalions and three TA. The regiment was reduced again in 1992 to two regular and two TA battalions with the loss of the 3rd (16th/44th Foot) and 5th Battalions.[^cite_note-beds-3]

The remaining Territorial battalion of the regiment, the [East of England Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_England_Regiment) was re-designated on 1 April 2006 as the [3rd Battalion, Royal Anglian Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Battalion,_Royal_Anglian_Regiment) as part of the reforms.[^cite_note-beds-3]

### Early operational history

#### Northern Ireland

The regiment carried out a series of tours-of-duty throughout "[the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles)" in [Northern Ireland](21265.md).[^cite_note-4]

#### Yugoslav Wars

During the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars), the 2nd Battalion was deployed to [Bosnia](3463.md) in April 1994 as part of the [United Nations](31769.md) peacekeeping force [UNPROFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNPROFOR).[^cite_note-5] During the tour, Corporal Andrew Rainey became one of the first ever non-officers to be awarded the [Military Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Cross), for his actions during a heavy contact between 3 Platoon, A Company and a [Bosnian Serb Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Serb_Army) unit on the confrontation line in the north of the [Maglaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maglaj) Finger.[^cite_note-6]

#### Croatia

In 1995 the 1st Battalion was sent to Croatia as part of 24 Airmobile Brigade between July and October of that year. The Vikings returned to the UK having suffered no casualties.[^cite_note-7]

#### Sierra Leone

Shortly after British forces intervened in [Sierra Leone](27308.md) during [its civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Civil_War), the 2nd Battalion briefly joined the IMATT force in June 2000 to help train the [Sierra Leonean armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Sierra_Leone).[^cite_note-8]

### Recent operational history

#### Afghanistan

##### Operation Fingal



Royal Anglian Regiment in Afghanistan

In March 2002, a majority of the 1st Battalion were sent to [Afghanistan](737.md) as part of [Operation Fingal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fingal) which involved military leadership and the provision of a 2,000-strong contingent to coalition forces in Afghanistan. The battalion was based in the capital [Kabul](16826.md) as part of the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) (ISAF). The following February, the 2nd Battalion's A Company were posted to Kabul and were replaced by C Company in June.[^cite_note-9]

##### Operation Herrick

From March to September 2007, as part of [12th Mechanised Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Armoured_Infantry_Brigade_\(United_Kingdom\)), 1st Battalion was deployed to Afghanistan as part of [Operation Herrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Herrick) 6. They were stationed in [Helmand Province](457235.md). This deployment was the subject of the [Sky One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_One) documentary *[Ross Kemp in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Kemp_in_Afghanistan)*, broadcast in January/February 2008. A book, *Attack State Red*, published by Penguin in 2009, was written by [Richard Kemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kemp), a former commanding officer, about the battalion on this tour. The fighting attracted much media attention due to the ferocity of the combat, with soldiers often having to resort to using [bayonets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayonets). The battalion suffered nine casualties during its tour, five from attacks and four accidental.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

In a reported [friendly fire](202484.md) incident, on 23 August 2007, one of a pair [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) [F-15E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15E_Strike_Eagle) fighter aircraft called in to support a patrol of the 1st Battalion in Afghanistan dropped a bomb on the same patrol, killing three men, and severely injured two others. It was later revealed that the British [forward air controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_air_controller) who called in the strike had not been issued a noise-cancelling headset, and in the confusion and stress of the battle incorrectly confirmed one wrong digit of the co-ordinates mistakenly repeated by the pilot, and the bomb landed on the British position 1,000 metres away from the enemy. The coroner at the soldiers' inquest stated that the incident was due to "flawed application of procedures" rather than individual errors or "recklessness".[^cite_note-12]



A patrol from the 1st Battalion in contact with insurgents during Operation Herrick 6

With very little notice the 1st Battalion would deploy again to Helmand Province in late 2009 where soldiers from 1st Battalion saw action guarding checkpoints in the Nad-e-Ali area, in central Helmand province later in the year.[^cite_note-13] Deploying alongside them were elements of the sister 3rd Battalion, who deployed to Afghanistan as part of [11 Light Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_Light_Brigade) in October 2009.[^cite_note-14]

The 1st Battalion and elements of 3rd Battalion again deployed to Afghanistan as part of 12th Mechanised Brigade in March 2012 as part of Operation Herrick 16 in the closing stages of the conflict.[^cite_note-15]



The Duke of Gloucester presents medals to members of the 1st Battalion on 1 August 2019, following [Operation Toral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Toral) 7; red and yellow roses are worn in the soldiers' hats to mark [Minden Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minden_Day)

#### Iraq

In 2005, 1st Battalion undertook a tour in Iraq as part of [Operation Telic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Telic) 6 where the battle group was responsible for the [Basra](57580.md) Rural South area of operations. C (Essex) Company was detached to act as a Brigade Operations Company and was involved in several high-profile arrest operations.[^cite_note-16]

In Spring 2006, 2nd Battalion deployed to Iraq as part of Op Telic 8 and formed Basra City South Battlegroup. C (Northamptonshire) company was detached to operate as part of Force Reserve and was involved in many high-profile arrest and strike operations. During the tour the regiment mourned the loss of two soldiers; on 13 May 2006 Privates Joseva Lewaicei and Adam Morris died as a result of injuries sustained from a roadside bomb attack in Basra. A third soldier was badly injured.[^cite_note-17]

#### Cyprus

In autumn 2017 the 2nd Battalion Royal Anglian Regiment deployed to [Cyprus](5593.md), assuming the role of Regional Standby Battalion and was held at very high readiness to deploy anywhere in the world.[^cite_note-gear-18] In August 2019 it returned to [Kendrew Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendrew_Barracks) in Rutland.[^cite_note-kendrew-19]

In summer 2021 the 1st Battalion deployed to Cyprus and, as with the 2nd Battalion in the years before, assumed the role of Regional Standby Battalion to be ready to deploy anywhere in the world. Elements of the battalion were deployed at short notice on [Operation Pitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pitting), helping to recover British nationals and Afghan refugees following the fall of Kabul to the Taliban.[^cite_note-20]

### Recent history



A [Foxhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocelot_\(vehicle\)) of 2 Royal Anglian on exercise at the [Castlemartin Training Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlemartin_Training_Area), 2021

On 9 October 2019, 100 men from C and D companies marched through [Haverhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverhill,_Suffolk) and received the [freedom of the town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_city). The battalion was led by their commanding officer [Lieutenant Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Colonel) Phillip Moxey [MBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Most_Excellent_Order_of_the_British_Empire).[^cite_note-21] In 2020 during the [2019–20 coronavirus pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_coronavirus_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom), members of the regiment helped assist the [NHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS) for testing of COVID-19 patients, and provided checkpoints throughout [London](17867.md) in collaboration with the [Grenadier Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadier_Guards).[^cite_note-22] 1 Royal Anglian also helped build [NHS Nightingale London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS_Nightingale_London), a temporary critical care hospital.[^cite_note-23]

## Battalion nicknames

The battalion nicknames are as follows: \


**The Vikings – 1st Battalion**

The nickname stems from the late 1960s when the commanding officer of the 1st Battalion, The 1st East Anglian Regiment Lt Col A F Campbell, MC said it described the Nordic influence on the counties of Norfolk and Suffolk. The nickname stuck, was adopted by the 1st Battalion on formation, and is still widely used today.[^cite_note-32]

**The Poachers – 2nd Battalion**

This stems from the Regimental March, *[The Lincolnshire Poacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lincolnshire_Poacher)*, of one of their predecessors, the Royal Lincolnshire Regiment.[^cite_note-33]

**The Steelbacks – 3rd Battalion**

This was the nickname of the 58th Regiment of Foot, later the 2nd Battalion the Northamptonshire Regiment. It was subsequently the nickname of the former 5th (Volunteer) Battalion, Royal Anglian Regiment. It stems from Private Hovenden of the 58th being described as a 'Steelback' after a flogging in 1813.[^cite_note-34]

**The Pompadours – Disbanded 3rd Battalion**

This was the nickname of the 56th (West Essex) Regiment of Foot, an infantry regiment in the British Army, active from 1755 to 1881. The regiment was originally uniformed with a deep crimson [facing colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facing_colour), which in 1764 was changed to purple. During the 18th century the fugitive nature of the dye required to produce this unusual military colour produced varying shades.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The colour was often called "pompadour", from which the regiment's nickname of "The Pompadours" came.[^cite_note-37] The reasons for the name of the colour are unclear; it is often said that the shade was [Madame de Pompadour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_de_Pompadour)'s [favourite colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_preferences). Some soldiers of the regiment preferred to claim that it was the colour of her [underwear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergarment).[^cite_note-redcoat-38]

**The Tigers – Disbanded 4th Battalion**

This was the nickname of the Leicestershire Regiment (Royal Leicestershire Regiment after 1946), a line infantry regiment with a history going back to 1688. In 1804 the regiment moved to [India](14533.md),[^cite_note-39] and remained there until 1823.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECannon184837-40] In 1825 the regiment was granted the badge of a "royal tiger" to recall their long service in the sub-continent.[^cite_note-42]

### Uniform

Uniforms are as follows:[^cite_note-pamphlet-43]\


Cap badge

The cap badge consists of the [garter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter) star, a silver star of eight points, denoting the royal status of the regiment. Mounted upon which, is the castle of [Gibraltar](7607314.md) with a scroll inscribed 'ROYAL ANGLIAN' in gold. The Suffolks, Essex and Northamptonshire Regiments served at the [Great Siege of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Siege_of_Gibraltar) from 1779 to 1783, the key identifies that Gibraltar was the "key to the Mediterranean".

[^cite_note-guide-44]

Beret

The 1st Battalion the Royal Norfolk Regiment joined [24th Guards Independent Brigade Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24th_Infantry_Brigade_\(United_Kingdom\)) in 1942; the Guards officers wore a [khaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaki) beret and the Norfolks adopted it at the time. The beret went out of use after the war but was re-adopted in 1960, and has continued in use. It was adopted by the rest of the Royal Anglian Regiment in 1970 and the wearing of it extended to [warrant officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer). The complete regiment went to khaki berets in 1976.

[^cite_note-guide-44]

Black patch

The black patch behind the cap badge commemorates the burial of [Sir John Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Moore_\(British_Army_officer\)) at [Corunna in 1809](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Corunna) by officers and men of the 9th of Foot (which became the Norfolk Regiment), the rearguard of the withdrawing British Expeditionary Force.

[^cite_note-guide-44]

Tactical recognition flashes (TRFs)

The 1st Battalion wear the red and yellow Minden Flash. It originates from the Suffolk Regiment who adopted it after [Dunkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkirk_evacuation); it symbolises the red and yellow of the roses at the [Battle of Minden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Minden). The 2nd Battalion wear the black, yellow, black of the [Bedfordshire and Hertfordshire Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedfordshire_and_Hertfordshire_Regiment). The 3rd Battalion wear the Regimental colours, blue, red and yellow.

Eagle

The Salamanca Eagle is worn on the left sleeve on No. 1 and No 2 dress tunics. It is a replica of the French 62nd Regiment's [French Imperial Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Imperial_Eagle) standard which was captured by the 2/[44th (East Essex) Regiment of Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/44th_\(East_Essex\)_Regiment_of_Foot) at the [Battle of Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Salamanca) during the [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War). The Eagle is mounted on a backing of Pompadour purple, representing the 3rd Battalion The Royal Anglian Regiment (The Pompadours) disbanded in 1992. The British Army captured a total of six of Napoleon's eagles (two at Salamanca, two at Madrid and two at [Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo)), four of which were taken with bayonet. The original eagle is still held in the [Essex Regiment Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_Regiment_Museum#Regimental_museum) and is displayed in the 1st Battalion on Salamanca Day.

[^cite_note-guide-44]

Collar badges

The 1st Battalion wear a figure of [Britannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia) superimposed on a castle and key. Britannia was awarded to the Norfolk Regiment for service at the [Battle of Almansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Almansa) and the castle was the cap badge of the Suffolk Regiment, commemorating the siege of Gibraltar. The 2nd Battalion wear the [sphinx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphinx) with underneath a scroll inscribed 'TALAVERA'. The sphinx is from the Royal Lincolnshire Regiment, for service in Egypt, 'TALAVERA' is from the [48th (Northamptonshire) Regiment of Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48th_\(Northamptonshire\)_Regiment_of_Foot)'s role at [that 1809 battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Talavera). The 3rd Battalion wear the cap badge as their collar badge.

Lanyards

The lanyard of the 1st Battalion is yellow from the Royal Norfolk Regiment. The 2nd Battalion wear black to commemorate the Northamptonshire Regiment. The 3rd Battalion wears the black and primrose yellow lanyard of the former Bedfordshire and Hertfordshire Regiment.

Buttons

All ranks wear the Royal Leicestershire Regiment buttons, a tiger surrounded by an unbroken laurel wreath. The tiger is in memory of the regiment's service in India, the wreath commemorates the [Battle of Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Princeton) in the [American War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_War_of_Independence).

[^cite_note-guide-44]

### Band

The first trace of a band within the Northamptonshire Regiment was recorded in 1798, during which a "German bandmaster was engaged". Military bands have continued in this regiment over the years after Territorial Army battalions of the former regiments were affiliated were merged. In 1986, the first women were recruited into the band from the [Women's Royal Army Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Royal_Army_Corps). In 1996, the 5th Battalion of the Royal Anglian Regiment was reorganized with the regimental council electing to keep the band, under the name of the Band of the Royal Anglian Regiment following the amalgamation of the Regular Royal Anglian bands into the Bands of the Queen's Division.[^cite_note-45]

### The Royal Bermuda Regiment



A [Permanent Staff Instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Staff_Instructor) (PSI), seconded from the Royal Anglians, with senior [Non-Commissioned Officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Commissioned_Officer) of the [Royal Bermuda Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bermuda_Regiment). The PSI wears a Royal Bermuda Regiment cap badge on a Royal Anglian khaki beret. The Royal Anglian Regiment provides a PSI to each of the Royal Bermuda Regiment's companies, as well as to the companies of its own [Territorial Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Army_\(United_Kingdom\)) [battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion).



The badge of the Royal Anglian Regiment (right) and of one of its predecessors, the [Royal Lincolnshire Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Lincolnshire_Regiment) (left), the Royal Bermuda Regiment (bottom), and both of its predecessors, the [Bermuda Militia Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Militia_Artillery) ([Royal Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Artillery)) and the [Bermuda Volunteer Rifle Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Volunteer_Rifle_Corps) (second and third from left).

The Royal Anglian Regiment has a unique relationship with the [Royal Bermuda Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bermuda_Regiment) (RBR), a Territorial battalion of the [British Overseas Territory](207114.md) of [Bermuda](3460.md) (which was designated an [Imperial fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress) until the 1950s,[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] with the [Royal Naval Dockyard, Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Dockyard,_Bermuda) reduced to a base in 1951, the naval [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_America_and_West_Indies_Station) of the [America and West Indies Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_and_West_Indies_Station) abolished with the station in 1956,[^cite_note-50] and the [Bermuda Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Garrison) Headquarters and all regular units and detachments of the garrison withdrawn in 1957).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Although the Royal Bermuda Regiment is usually described as an affiliated regiment, its relationship to the regiment is more akin to that of one of Royal Anglian's own TA battalions. One of the two units that amalgamated in 1965 to form the Royal Bermuda Regiment, the [Bermuda Volunteer Rifle Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Volunteer_Rifle_Corps), sent two contingents (the first in June, 1915, merging with the second in 1916) to the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\)), to support the [Lincolnshire Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Lincolnshire_Regiment), during the First World War. It also sent two contingents (in 1940 and 1944) during the Second World War. The relationship would continue as both units were amalgamated in the 1960s, with Royal Anglian Regiment officers and warrant officers making contributing to the Royal Bermuda Regiment's Permanent Staff since it was formed by amalgamation in 1965 (providing the adjutant, and later the staff officer, permanent staff instructors and other roles from second-in-command to training wing warrant officer).[^cite_note-53] Senior NCOs were also loaned to the Royal Bermuda Regiment for the duration of its annual recruit camps, with one attached to each [platoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platoon) of its training company.[^cite_note-54]

## Popular culture

In 1989, the Band and fifty members of the 3rd Battalion were featured in the opening and closing sequences of BBC historical sitcom *[Blackadder Goes Forth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackadder_Goes_Forth)* with the band, men and actors [Rowan Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowan_Atkinson), [Hugh Laurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Laurie), [Stephen Fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Fry), [Tony Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Robinson) and [Tim McInnerny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_McInnerny) dressed in First World War period uniforms marching to "[The British Grenadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_British_Grenadiers)" and the *Blackadder* theme song. It was shot on location at the former [Colchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester) Cavalry Barracks.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-LookEast-56]
