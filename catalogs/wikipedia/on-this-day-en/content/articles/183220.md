A **first minister** is any of a variety of leaders of government [cabinets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)). The term literally has the same meaning as "prime minister".

In the United Kingdom, the title of *first minister* is used to refer to the political leader of a [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution) national government, such as the [devolved governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_in_the_United_Kingdom) of [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland), [Wales](602076.md), and [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Office_\(Northern_Ireland\)), or of a dependent territory.

In Australia and Canada, the term is used to refer collectively to the Prime Minister and the state or provincial premiers.

## Australia

In [Australia](4689264.md), a first minister is one of the [heads of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_Government), including the [Prime Minister of Australia](24117.md) and the [Premiers and chief ministers of the Australian states and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiers_and_chief_ministers_of_the_Australian_states_and_territories).

## Canada

Further information: [Premier (Canada)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_\(Canada\))

In [Canada](5042916.md), a first minister is any of the Canadian first [ministers of the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Crown), otherwise known as [heads of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_Government), including the [Prime Minister of Canada](24135.md) and the [provincial and territorial](75763.md) [premiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier). The title is used in such formulae as "[first ministers' meetings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_ministers'_meetings)".

In [Newfoundland and Labrador](21980.md), the [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) self-governing region of [Nunatsiavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunatsiavut) provides for a [first minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Nunatsiavut) responsible to the Nunatsiavut Assembly.

## Norway

The [head of government of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government_of_Norway) was called *first minister* ([Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language): *førstestatsråd*) between 1814 and 1873, while it was in [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union) [with Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway). In 1893, 12 years prior to the [dissolution of the union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden_in_1905), it was changed to [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister) (*statsminister*).

## United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, the term *first minister* was once used interchangeably with [prime minister](24150.md), such as when [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) stated: "I did not become His Majesty's First Minister so that I might oversee the liquidation of the British Empire!"

Since 1999 the term has been used to describe the leaders of the [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_in_the_United_Kingdom) governments of [Scotland](26994.md),[^cite_note-1] [Wales](69894.md) and [Northern Ireland](21265.md). See

- [List of first ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_heads_of_government_in_the_United_Kingdom_and_dependencies#Government_of_the_United_Kingdom)

- [First Minister of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland)

- [First Minister of Wales](602076.md)

- [First Minister and deputy First Minister of Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_and_Deputy_First_Minister_of_Northern_Ireland)

## Other

In [Australia](4689264.md), the [premiers and chief ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiers_and_chief_ministers_of_the_Australian_states_and_territories) are the first ministers of the [states and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_states_and_territories) respectively.

In [Germany](11867.md), the first minister in each federal state is known as the [Minister President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_President) (*Ministerpräsident*). A similar term exists in countries with some historic German influence, e.g. [Latvia](17514.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md) etc.

In [Malaysia](3607937.md), the first minister for each state with a Malay ruler is known as the [Menteri Besar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menteri_Besar). Meanwhile, the heads of government in the four states without a monarch are called [chief ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_minister).

[George Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Cadle_Price) held the office of First Minister of [British Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Honduras) from 1961 until 1964, when it became self-governing and the title was changed to [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier). He continued as Premier after the [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) changed its names to [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), and then as [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister) after Belize gained full independence in 1981.
