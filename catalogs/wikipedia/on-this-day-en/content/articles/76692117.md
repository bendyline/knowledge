The **2025 NBA Finals** was the [championship series](890793.md) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s (NBA) [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NBA_season) and conclusion to [the season's playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs). The [best-of-seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-of-seven) series ended with the [Western Conference](28141073.md) champion [Oklahoma City Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_season) defeating the [Eastern Conference](1305847.md) champion [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Indiana_Pacers_season) in seven games. The Thunder's [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) was voted the [NBA Finals Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player) (MVP), becoming the first [Canadian](19851291.md) to win the award.[^cite_note-1] The series began on June 5 and ended on June 22 with the first NBA Finals Game 7 since [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-schedule-2]

With a league-best regular season record of 68–14, the Thunder held [home-court advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home-court_advantage) in the Finals. They came into the series as heavy favorites against the 50–32 Pacers.[^cite_note-3] The Pacers set an NBA postseason record with five 15-point comebacks.[^cite_note-4] One was in Game 1 of the Finals, when [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton) made a game-winning two-point jump shot with 0.3 seconds left. The teams split the first six games, forcing a decisive [Game 7](15664872.md), in which Haliburton tore his right [Achilles tendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon) in the first quarter.[^cite_note-5] The Thunder's victory secured their second championship in franchise history, after their [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_NBA_Finals) title as the [Seattle SuperSonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics), and their first since their [2008 move to Oklahoma City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics_relocation_to_Oklahoma_City).

The 2025 Finals crowned the NBA's seventh unique champion in seven years, the longest stretch in league history.[^cite_note-6] The 2025 Finals was also the first since [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_NBA_Finals) in which both cities were seeking their first NBA championship.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

The Thunder's victory was the first championship in any major professional sport for the state of Oklahoma.[^cite_note-10]

## Background

### General

This was the first playoff match-up between Oklahoma City and Indiana. Oklahoma City won both of its regular season games against Indiana.[^cite_note-11] The Thunder had not lost a game at home to an Eastern Conference team since losing to the Pacers on March 12, 2024.[^cite_note-12] Both teams were in the Finals after missing the playoffs two seasons prior.[^cite_note-13] This was the smallest NBA Finals in terms of total market size, with the previous lowest mark belonging to [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) and [San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_San_Antonio_Spurs_season) in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-14] The total television market size comprises fewer than two million households with Indianapolis being the 22nd largest and Oklahoma City being the 26th largest out of 28 markets league-wide.[^cite_note-15] The 2025 Finals also set another milestone; it was the first Finals without a luxury tax team since the [salary cap](3280233.md) era began in 2002.[^cite_note-16]

Coincidentally, both teams traded former star player [Paul George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_George) in trades that yielded their best players, [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) and [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton) to the Thunder and the Pacers, respectively.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] On the eve of free agency in 2017, the Pacers traded George to the Thunder for [Victor Oladipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Oladipo) and [Domantas Sabonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domantas_Sabonis).[^cite_note-20] Sabonis, along with other players, was dealt to the [Sacramento Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) for Haliburton and other pieces before the 2022 trade deadline.[^cite_note-21] For Oklahoma City, George starred for the team, finishing third in [NBA Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player) (MVP) and [Defensive Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Defensive_Player_of_the_Year) voting in [2018–19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_season), but the team fell in the first round in both his seasons with the club. On July 10, 2019, the Thunder traded George to the [Los Angeles Clippers](72892.md) for Gilgeous-Alexander, [Danilo Gallinari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilo_Gallinari), five first-round draft picks, and the rights to swap two other first-round picks (with one of them used to select [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams)).[^cite_note-22]

The Thunder entered the NBA Finals as the second-largest betting favorite in the previous 20 years, with only the [2018 Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Golden_State_Warriors_season) having shorter pre-Finals odds since 2005.[^cite_note-23] The 18-game difference in wins between the teams was the largest among NBA Finals participants since the [1981 Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_NBA_Finals) between the 62-win [Celtics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_Boston_Celtics_season) and 40-win [Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_Houston_Rockets_season).[^cite_note-24]

### Indiana Pacers

Main article: [2024–25 Indiana Pacers season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Indiana_Pacers_season)



[Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) made his second NBA Finals appearance after winning a championship as a member of the [2019 Toronto Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Toronto_Raptors_season).

From October through December, Indiana was a .500 team or worse, hitting a low point after a loss to [Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Charlotte_Hornets_season) on December 8 to put their record at 10–15.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Star point guard [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton) struggled during this time, saying he was having personal off-court problems.[^cite_note-27] They did not get going until January, when they posted a 10–2 record. The Pacers went 20–9 from the [All-Star break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_All-Star_Game) until the end of the season.[^cite_note-28] During the turnaround, Haliburton found his rhythm and his stats improved greatly; the team got healthier (starters Haliburton, [Andrew Nembhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Nembhard), and [Aaron Nesmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Nesmith) all had injuries at the beginning of the year); and their defense improved dramatically, ranking ninth in defensive efficiency after early December.[^cite_note-29] Steady veteran forward [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) earned his third career All-Star Game appearance, while Haliburton earned his second straight [All-NBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-NBA) third-team honor.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The Pacers' offensive rating ranked second in the league, and they led the league in assist percentage and [true shooting percentage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_shooting_percentage) as a team.[^cite_note-32] Another strength was their depth, as no Pacers player averaged more than 33.6 minutes per game during the regular season.[^cite_note-33]

The team finished with a record of 50–32, marking Indiana's first 50-win season since [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Indiana_Pacers_season) and earning the fourth seed in the Eastern Conference playoffs. In the [First Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs#\(4\)_Indiana_Pacers_vs._\(5\)_Milwaukee_Bucks), the Pacers met the [Milwaukee Bucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Milwaukee_Bucks_season), a rematch from [the previous year's first round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NBA_playoffs#\(3\)_Milwaukee_Bucks_vs._\(6\)_Indiana_Pacers). They won the series in five games, highlighted by a 7-point, 40-second comeback in overtime in Game 5.[^cite_note-34] They then upset the 64-win, top-seeded Cleveland Cavaliers in the [Eastern Conference Semifinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs#\(1\)_Cleveland_Cavaliers_vs._\(4\)_Indiana_Pacers) in five games, which was again highlighted by an unlikely comeback when they won Game 2 on a Haliburton game-winning three after being down 7 points with 46 seconds left.[^cite_note-35] The Pacers' third improbable comeback was against the [New York Knicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_New_York_Knicks_season) in Game 1 of the [Eastern Conference Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs#\(3\)_New_York_Knicks_vs._\(4\)_Indiana_Pacers): down 14 with 2\:45 left, the Pacers won in overtime. Indiana was the first team in the play-by-play era ([1996–97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_NBA_season)–present) to win a game where they were trailing by 14 points or more with 2\:45 left in the game.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In their second straight conference finals, they beat the Knicks in six games. After a 10–15 start, Indiana went 52–21 for the rest of the season.[^cite_note-38]

This was the Pacers' second NBA Finals appearance. Their previous appearance was in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NBA_Finals), a loss to the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season).[^cite_note-39] The Pacers won three [ABA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association) championships, but the NBA does not incorporate ABA statistics into its official records. Pacers coach [Rick Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Carlisle), a lead assistant to head coach [Larry Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Bird) on the [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_Indiana_Pacers_season) team, sought his second championship as head coach and third overall. Carlisle won a ring as a player on the [1986 Celtics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Boston_Celtics_season) and as a head coach on the [2011 Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Dallas_Mavericks_season).[^cite_note-40] Siakam returned to the NBA Finals for the second time after winning a championship in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_NBA_Finals) with the [Toronto Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Toronto_Raptors_season).[^cite_note-41] The only other players on the roster with Finals experience were Nesmith and December trade acquisition [Thomas Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bryant_\(basketball\)); Nesmith played a small role on the [2022 Celtics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Boston_Celtics_season) and Bryant won a championship with the [2023 Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Denver_Nuggets_season).[^cite_note-42]

### Oklahoma City Thunder

Main article: [2024–25 Oklahoma City Thunder season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_season)



[Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) became the first player to win the regular season MVP and an NBA Finals in the same season since [Steph Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steph_Curry) in [2014–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014-15_NBA_season).

In the 2024–25 season, Oklahoma City had its best season in franchise history, finishing 68–14 to secure the top seed in the [Western Conference](28141073.md) for a second straight year.[^cite_note-43] They did so in dominant fashion, outscoring opponents by 12.9 points per game during the regular season, more than half a point better than the previous record, set by the [1971–72 Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) when Los Angeles outscored opponents by 12.3 points per game.[^cite_note-44] They were led by the regular season [MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player) [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md), who led the league with 32.7 points per game while also averaging 6.4 assists and five rebounds.[^cite_note-45] Third-year player [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams) continued his ascent as one of the league's best forwards, improving in every major statistical category from last season, becoming an [All-Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_All-Star_Game) for the first time, and making the third team [All-NBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-NBA_Team).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] After losing to the [Dallas Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Dallas_Mavericks_season) in the 2024 [Western Conference semifinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NBA_playoffs#\(1\)_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_vs._\(5\)_Dallas_Mavericks), the team acquired veteran depth pieces [Isaiah Hartenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Hartenstein) (via free agency) and [Alex Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Caruso) (via trade). Gilgeous-Alexander, Williams, Hartenstein, Caruso, second-year center [Chet Holmgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Holmgren), and guard [Luguentz Dort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luguentz_Dort) formed a stalwart defensive unit as OKC led the league in most defensive categories;[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Dort was also rewarded with an [All-Defensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_All-Defensive_Team) first team honor, while Williams was voted to the All-Defensive second team.[^cite_note-51] Rounding out the roster were sharpshooters [Aaron Wiggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Wiggins) and [Isaiah Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Joe); second-year player guard [Cason Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cason_Wallace), another great defensive player; and reserve big man [Jaylin Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaylin_Williams_\(basketball,_born_2002\)), who filled in for Holmgren during his two-month absence with a hip injury. For putting this roster together, Thunder executive vice president and general manager [Sam Presti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Presti) was named the 2024–25 [NBA Executive of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Executive_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-52] Head coach [Mark Daigneault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Daigneault), who was named [Coach of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Coach_of_the_Year) the previous season, came in fourth for the award this season.[^cite_note-53] OKC finished 16 games better than the [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Houston_Rockets_season), the second-best team in the Western Conference. This margin between a first and second seed was the largest in NBA history.[^cite_note-54]

In the playoffs, the Thunder dispatched the [Memphis Grizzlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Memphis_Grizzlies_season) in a four-game sweep in the [First Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs#\(1\)_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_vs._\(8\)_Memphis_Grizzlies). In the Western Conference Semifinals, they defeated the veteran [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Denver_Nuggets_season) in [seven games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs#\(1\)_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_vs._\(4\)_Denver_Nuggets), with a blowout home win in the [seventh game](15664762.md). They won the [Western Conference finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs#\(1\)_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_vs._\(6\)_Minnesota_Timberwolves) handily against the [Minnesota Timberwolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Minnesota_Timberwolves_season) in five games. The Thunder entered the Finals 8–1 at home, with their only defeat coming on an [Aaron Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Gordon) game-winning three in the final seconds of Game 1 of the second round against Denver.[^cite_note-55]

This was the franchise's fifth NBA Finals appearance overall and second since the team's relocation to Oklahoma City. Their last appearance was in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_NBA_Finals), when they lost to the [Miami Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Miami_Heat_season).[^cite_note-athletic_05292025-56] The franchise also made three Finals appearances as the [Seattle SuperSonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics), winning one title in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_NBA_Finals). Caruso was the only player or coach on the roster with Finals experience, as he won a championship with the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season).[^cite_note-57] The Thunder became the youngest team to make the NBA Finals since the [1977 Portland Trail Blazers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Portland_Trail_Blazers_season).[^cite_note-58]

[Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton)'s jumpshot with 0.3 seconds left won the game for Indiana.

The Thunder jumped out to an early lead and forced a record 19 Indiana turnovers in the first half, taking a 57–45 advantage into halftime.[^cite_note-59] In the fourth quarter, the Pacers trimmed an initial 15-point deficit with 9\:42 left into a one-possession game with under two minutes left.[^cite_note-60] After a coach's challenge by [Rick Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Carlisle) on a [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) out-of-bounds call was ruled unsuccessful with 22 seconds remaining, the Thunder were up 110–109, but [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md)'s midrange jumper missed, and Indiana gained possession. Carlisle opted not to call a timeout to give the Thunder a chance to set their defense, allowing the Pacers to push in transition. [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton), who had already made clutch shots this postseason against Milwaukee, Cleveland, and New York, nailed a 21-foot pull-up jump shot over [Cason Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cason_Wallace) to put Indiana up 111–110 with 0.3 seconds left, marking their first lead of the entire game. The Thunder's final lob attempt failed, giving the Pacers another improbable come-from-behind playoff victory.[^cite_note-61]

Gilgeous-Alexander led all scorers with 38 points, marking the third-most points in a Finals debut behind [Allen Iverson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Iverson) and [George Mikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mikan).[^cite_note-espn-g1-62] [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams) and [Luguentz Dort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luguentz_Dort) combined for 32 points for the Thunder, while [Chet Holmgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Holmgren) struggled with just six points on 2-of–9 shooting from the field. Siakam led the scoring for the Pacers with 19 points and 10 rebounds. All five Indiana starters finished in double figures, with [Aaron Nesmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Nesmith) and Haliburton tallying double-doubles, and [Obi Toppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obi_Toppin) adding 17 points in a productive night off the bench. This was just the Thunder's second home loss of the entire playoffs, the last coming in a 121–119 loss in Game 1 of the conference semifinals against the [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Nuggets).[^cite_note-espn-g1-62] According to ESPN analytics, the Thunder held a 96.4% win probability with a nine-point lead and 2\:52 remaining. This game marked the Pacers' fourth comeback this postseason in which their opponent had at least a 95% chance of winning with under three minutes left; each game included a game-tying or go-ahead basket by Haliburton.[^cite_note-63]

Haliburton's go-ahead shot with 0.3 seconds left marked the latest game winner in an NBA Finals game since [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan)'s buzzer-beating shot in Game 1 of the [1997 Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-64] The Pacers in Game 1 joined the [1999 Spurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_San_Antonio_Spurs_season) in Game 2 of the [Western Conference Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NBA_playoffs#\(1\)_San_Antonio_Spurs_vs._\(2\)_Portland_Trail_Blazers) and the [2002 Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) in Game 4 of the [Western Conference Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NBA_playoffs#\(1\)_Sacramento_Kings_vs._\(3\)_Los_Angeles_Lakers) as teams to win a playoff game despite leading for only 20 seconds or less.[^cite_note-65] Indiana also tied the [1992 Chicago Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Chicago_Bulls_season) in [Game 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_NBA_Finals#Game_6) and the [2011 Dallas Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Dallas_Mavericks_season) in [Game 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NBA_Finals#Game_2)—the latter also coached by Carlisle—for the largest NBA Finals fourth-quarter comeback.[^cite_note-66] This was Oklahoma City's first home loss to an Eastern Conference team since March 12, 2024, also to the Pacers.[^cite_note-67] After the game, Thunder star Shai Gilgeous-Alexander said, "It's a 48-minute game, and \[the Pacers] teach you that lesson more than anyone else in the league the hard way."[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

In a Game 2 win, [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) became the 12th player to score a combined 3,000 points in the regular season and playoff.

[^cite_note-70]

The Thunder defeated the Pacers 123–107 to tie the series, winning their first Finals game since their Game 1 win in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_NBA_Finals) against the [Miami Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Miami_Heat_season).[^cite_note-OKCPacersG2-71] The Thunder took a six-point lead in the opening quarter.[^cite_note-\:0-72] In the second quarter, the Thunder capitalized on a 19–2 run, jumping out to a 23-point lead before finishing with an 18-point lead at halftime. For the rest of the game, Indiana was unable to cut the lead back down to single digits.[^cite_note-OKCPacersG2-71] [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton) attempted to spark the Pacers in the beginning of the fourth quarter, but a Thunder response left Indiana unable to bring the deficit under 19 points for the first six minutes before they ceded defeat.[^cite_note-\:0-72]

[Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) followed up his 38-point Finals debut by leading all Thunder scorers with 34 points (in addition to eight assists and five rebounds), totaling 72 points across two games. He surpassed [Allen Iverson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Iverson) (71 points, 2001) for the most combined points scored in a player's first two Finals games. Gilgeous-Alexander became the 12th player to score a combined 3,000 points in the regular season and playoffs. This also marked his ninth consecutive home playoff game with at least 30 points, tying an NBA record previously set by [Wilt Chamberlain](255645.md).[^cite_note-73] Jalen Williams and Chet Holmgren came back from poor offensive performances in Game 1 with 19 and 15 points, respectively.[^cite_note-\:0-72] The Thunder became the first team to have five or more 15+ point scorers in a Finals game since the [2019 Toronto Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Toronto_Raptors_season).[^cite_note-74] Haliburton led the Pacers with 17 points, while [Myles Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myles_Turner) and [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) added 16 and 15, respectively. For the second straight game, none of the Pacers reached 20 points; they became the first team since the [2013 Miami Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Miami_Heat_season) to have no players score 20 points in two straight Finals games.[^cite_note-OKCPacersG2-71]

[Bennedict Mathurin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennedict_Mathurin) scored a playoff career-high 27 points off the bench to give the Pacers a 2–1 series lead.

In a tightly contested game in which neither team led by double digits, the Pacers surged in the fourth quarter to reclaim the series lead. The Thunder took an early nine-point lead behind hot starts by Holmgren and Dort, who combined for 22 of the team's 32 points in the first quarter.[^cite_note-75] But Indiana's bench shifted the momentum in the second quarter. [Bennedict Mathurin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennedict_Mathurin) scored 14 points in the frame, while [T. J. McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._J._McConnell) energized the team with four assists and three steals.[^cite_note-76] The game remained close, and a 6–0 Thunder run in the final minute of the third quarter gave Oklahoma City a five-point edge entering the fourth. From there, the Pacers took control. Indiana shot 13-of-21 from the field in the final period, while limiting the Thunder to just 35% shooting and forcing five turnovers.[^cite_note-77] With the score tied at 98 and under seven minutes to play, [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton) scored or assisted on 10 of the Pacers' 14 points during a decisive 14–6 run that gave them an eight-point lead with 1\:09 remaining—a lead they did not relinquish.[^cite_note-78]

Mathurin finished with a game-high 27 points on 9-of-12 shooting in 22 minutes off the bench, marking the most points by a reserve in the NBA Finals since [Jason Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Terry) scored 27 in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NBA_Finals). Mathurin was supported by McConnell (five steals, five assists) and [Obi Toppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obi_Toppin) (six rebounds, two blocks), helping Indiana dominate Oklahoma City 49–18 in bench points.[^cite_note-79] Haliburton nearly recorded a triple-double with 22 points, 11 assists, and 9 rebounds, while [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) added 21 points, giving the Pacers three 20-point scorers in the game after having none in the first two games. [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams) led the Thunder with 26 points on 50% shooting. [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) contributed 24 points but struggled with turnovers, posting four assists against six turnovers, marking his first postseason game with a negative assist-to-turnover ratio. Holmgren added 20 points, going 6-of-9 inside the arc but missing all six of his three-point attempts.[^cite_note-80]

The Pacers won their tenth straight game following a loss, dating back to March 15. The Thunder, who went 61–2 when leading going into the fourth quarter in the regular season, fell to 1–2 in such situations in the Finals.[^cite_note-81]

Much like their second round series against the [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Denver_Nuggets_season), the Thunder won a crucial Game 4 to avoid a 3–1 deficit. The Thunder rallied in the 4th quarter after being down by 10 points.[^cite_note-82] The Thunder moved to 18–2 after a defeat in both the regular season and playoffs, tying the [1986–87 Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) for the best winning percentage in NBA history after a loss in a season.[^cite_note-83]

[Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) delivered, scoring 15 of his 35 points in the final 4\:38, capping Oklahoma City's rally from a 10-point, second-half deficit. [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams) added 27, [Alex Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Caruso) had 20 and [Chet Holmgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Holmgren) finished with 14 points and 15 rebounds. Down the stretch in the fourth quarter, Holmgren's defense proved vital against [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton).[^cite_note-84] The Thunder won the game despite a season-low three 3-pointers and no assists from Gilgeous-Alexander for the first time all season.[^cite_note-85]

The Pacers, who had a 10-point lead deep into the third quarter,[^cite_note-86] were unable to pull away. [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) scored 20 for Indiana, but only took one shot in the fourth quarter. Haliburton had 18 points and 7 assists, while [Obi Toppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obi_Toppin) continued his stellar play off the bench with 17 points.[^cite_note-87] The Pacers had chances at the end of the game, but [Bennedict Mathurin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennedict_Mathurin) missed 3-of-4 free throws and committed two away from the play fouls in the last 23.1 seconds.[^cite_note-88] Haliburton made Finals history for the most points scored in a 3-game span (53) without a free throw attempt, surpassing [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant)'s mark set in the [2000 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-89]

[Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams) scored a career playoff-high 40 points to help the Thunder prevent a second comeback attempt by the Pacers and secure a 3–2 series lead.

Oklahoma City halted an Indiana fourth-quarter comeback attempt to take a 3–2 lead.[^cite_note-90] [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams) scored a career playoff-high 40 points and [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) added 31 in the Thunder's 120–109 win. It was the 10th time the Thunder star duo combined for more than 70 points in a game. Williams was 14-of-24 from the field, while Gilgeous-Alexander added 10 assists after having none in game 4. The pair combined to score or assist on 103 points, the most by a duo in an NBA Finals game in the past 50 years, according to ESPN Research.[^cite_note-91] Through five games, Gilgeous-Alexander and Williams had 291 combined points, the fourth-most by a duo through the first five games of an NBA Finals since the [NBA-ABA merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA-ABA_merger) in 1977.[^cite_note-92] Off the bench, [Aaron Wiggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Wiggins) added 14 points and [Cason Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cason_Wallace), who was benched for [Isaiah Hartenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Hartenstein) in Game 4, had 4 steals and scored 11 points; [Alex Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Caruso) also had 4 steals for an Oklahoma City defense that forced 22 Pacers' turnovers. After a rough game shooting the ball from three in Game 4, Williams (3-of-5), [Luguentz Dort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luguentz_Dort) (3-of-6), Wiggins (4-of-7), and Wallace (3-of-4) led the 3-point shooting for OKC, who shot 43.8% from beyond the arc as a team.[^cite_note-93]

Similar to their previous two games in Oklahoma City, the Pacers got off to a rough start. [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton) strained his right calf in the first quarter and was not the same the rest of the game, not making a field goal and only scoring four points.[^cite_note-dopirak_0622025-94][^cite_note-95] Down by as many as 18 late in the second quarter, the Pacers got within five late in the third quarter. With Haliburton hampered, back-up point guard [T. J. McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._J._McConnell) led the rally, scoring 13 points in just under seven minutes.[^cite_note-96] The Pacers took that momentum into the fourth quarter, as [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) made a pair of free throws with 9\:19 left to get Indiana within four and then made a 3-pointer a minute later to cut the score to 95–93. But that was the closest Indiana got, as OKC ratcheted up the pressure, outscoring Indiana 21–8 over the next five minutes.[^cite_note-97] Siakam finished with 28 points on 9-of-15 shooting, and McConnell scored 18 points overall. This was the first time [this postseason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs) that the Pacers trailed in a playoff series.[^cite_note-98]

Six Pacers scored in double figures, including [Obi Toppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obi_Toppin), who led the team with 20 points.

A balanced Pacers effort on both sides of the ball helped extend the series and force a [Game 7](15664762.md) in the NBA Finals for the first time since [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals). The Thunder picked up where they left off in Game 5 with an early 10–2 run to start the game. The Pacers responded with a 24–7 run, highlighted by [Andrew Nembhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Nembhard) and [Obi Toppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obi_Toppin) scoring 16 of the 24 points and Indiana's defense forcing four turnovers.[^cite_note-nba-g7-99] The first quarter ended with the Pacers leading 28–25, the lowest scoring output in the first quarter of the series for the Thunder.[^cite_note-100] With 5\:22 left in the second quarter, Indiana held their largest lead of the Finals when the score was 45–33 after an [Aaron Nesmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Nesmith) 3-pointer.[^cite_note-101] The Pacers stretched that to 51–35, completing a 17–2 run over a span of 5\:13 from a score of 34–33.[^cite_note-102] The quarter ended with a [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) mid-range buzzer beater over [Alex Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Caruso); 40 seconds earlier, Siakam also had a [poster dunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posterized) on [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams), off a no-look feed from [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton).[^cite_note-103] At halftime, Indiana held a commanding 64–42 lead. For Oklahoma City, Williams and [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md) had 31 of the Thunder's 42 points.[^cite_note-104]

Oklahoma City had a chance to get back into the game at the start of the third quarter, as the Pacers missed their first seven shots of the quarter. But the Thunder also started the quarter cold, also missing their first seven shots, and no points were scored in the first 3\:53 of play.[^cite_note-105] Indiana ended the third the same way they ended the second, with a buzzer-beater, this time by [Ben Sheppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Sheppard_\(basketball\)), which made the score 90–60. With a big lead, the Pacers rested Haliburton's calf injury for most of the second half. Toppin led the Pacers in scoring with 20 points off the bench. Five other Pacers scored in double figures. As a team, they forced 21 turnovers, including 8 by Gilgeous-Alexander, a career high for the league's MVP. Oklahoma City shot only 26.7% from beyond the arc, and at −40, Jalen Williams had the worst +/- in NBA Finals history for a single game.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

[Chet Holmgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Holmgren) set a record for most blocks (5) in a Game 7 of the NBA Finals.

[^cite_note-108]

This was the 20th [Game 7](15664762.md) in NBA Finals history[^cite_note-109] and the first Game 7 of an NBA Finals in Pacers history, while the Thunder, then known as the [Seattle SuperSonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics), lost a Game 7 in the [1978 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_NBA_Finals) at home to the [Washington Bullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Bullets).[^cite_note-110]

With five minutes left in the first quarter, [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton), already playing with a right calf strain, tore his right Achilles tendon on a non-contact drive to the basket.[^cite_note-dopirak_0622025-94] The entire Pacers team left the bench to huddle around Haliburton, who got up and was helped off the court;[^cite_note-111] he was ruled out of the game a few minutes later.[^cite_note-112] Before the injury, Haliburton had made 3-of-4 three-pointers.[^cite_note-113] Despite Indiana losing who many felt was their best player, the Pacers had a 48–47 lead at halftime.[^cite_note-114]

In the third quarter, Oklahoma City broke through; three consecutive three pointers midway into the third quarter gave them a 65–56 lead. [Chet Holmgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Holmgren) and [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams), who both struggled in the first half, combined for 16 points in the quarter. A lone bright spot for Indiana in the third was [T. J. McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._J._McConnell), who scored the last 12 points for the Pacers, but they were down by 13 at the end of the quarter, 81–68. Indiana's poor play continued over into the fourth quarter, as they did not score a basket until [Pascal Siakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Siakam) made a mid-range jump shot with 7\:29 left. The Pacers cut Oklahoma City's lead to 10, but that was the smallest margin for the rest of the game. The Thunder's three key players (Gilgeous-Alexander, Williams, and Holmgren) led the team to a 103–91 victory, winning the second title in franchise history. Gilgeous-Alexander scored 29 points and 12 assists, Holmgren recorded 18 points, eight rebounds, and five blocks, and Williams scored 20 points.[^cite_note-115] With 103 points scored, the Thunder became the first team to score 100 points or more in a Finals Game 7 since the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) and [Detroit Pistons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Pistons) did so in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

Gilgeous-Alexander was voted the [Finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player); he was the first player to win a [regular season MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player) and Finals MVP in the same season since [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NBA_season). He also became the fourth player to win the [league scoring title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NBA_annual_scoring_leaders) along with regular season and Finals MVPs in one season, and the first since [Shaquille O'Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaquille_O'Neal) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_NBA_season).[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]

This was the first championship for the state of Oklahoma in a [major professional sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada).[^cite_note-120] The Thunder were the second-youngest team to win an NBA championship, behind the [1977 Portland Trail Blazers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Portland_Trail_Blazers_season).[^cite_note-121] By the end of the series, they also became the first team since the [2015–16 Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Golden_State_Warriors_season) to have a combined total of 84 or more wins in the regular season and playoffs.[^cite_note-122]

## Sponsorship

This was the final year of a multiyear partnership dating back to [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_Finals) in which the internet television service [YouTube TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_TV) was the [presenting sponsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_sponsor) of the NBA Finals.[^cite_note-125]

## Media coverage

The Finals was televised in the United States by [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_ABC) (including local affiliates [KOCO-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOCO-TV) in Oklahoma City and [WRTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WRTV) in Indianapolis) for the 23rd consecutive year. Analyst [Richard Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Jefferson) joined play-by-play announcer [Mike Breen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Breen), co-analyst [Doris Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Burke), and sideline reporter [Lisa Salters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Salters) after [JJ Redick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JJ_Redick) left to become head coach of the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers).[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-coverage-127] Breen called his record-extending 20th NBA Finals series on television.[^cite_note-128] Salters was absent for Games 2–4 due to her mother's health issues, which was revealed by Breen during Game 2.[^cite_note-129] [ESPN Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_Radio) reporter [Jorge Sedano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Sedano) filled in for her for the television coverage, while reporter Vanessa Richardson filled in for Sedano for the radio coverage of Games 2–4 before Salters returned for Game 5.[^cite_note-130] The series was broadcast on ESPN Radio with play-by-play announcer [Marc Kestecher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Kestecher), analyst [P. J. Carlesimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._J._Carlesimo), and Sedano.[^cite_note-coverage-127]



[Doris Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Burke) was demoted following the Finals.

The NBA and ABC faced criticism from viewers and sportswriters over a perceived lack of emphasis on the "pageantry" and magnitude of the NBA Finals in its media coverage. This included ABC's coverage not showing player introductions, which had not been done since 2013. The national anthem played only in the later matchups, notably when [Tony Award](54741.md) winning actress [Kristin Chenoweth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristin_Chenoweth) performed. There was also a lack of branding on the court such as the NBA Finals logo or [Larry O'Brien Trophy](2070674.md). This had not been done since 2014, when the NBA discontinued the decals due to player safety issues. This contrasted with the [NBA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Cup), in which dedicated court designs were used for each team. Sports media website *[Awful Announcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awful_Announcing)* called ABC's coverage "auraless" for these and other reasons.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133] In an apparent response to the criticism, ESPN began to display images of the trophy and NBA Finals logo on the court via [virtual insertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_advertising) beginning in Game 2 (which itself was criticized by viewers as a low-quality copout),[^cite_note-134] and showed player introductions during Game 5.[^cite_note-135] After the Finals, Doris Burke was demoted to the B team, while [Tim Legler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Legler) was promoted to team with Jefferson and Breen.[^cite_note-136]

### Viewership

Game 1 was the third least-watched NBA Finals Game 1 since 1988. Only the [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals) and [2021 Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NBA_Finals), which were both delayed by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), had a lower-rated opener.[^cite_note-137] NBA commissioner [Adam Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Silver) defended the ratings before Game 3, saying, "People compare us to 20 years ago, but Games 1 and 2 are the highest-rated programs in May and June so far on television".[^cite_note-138] Game 7 averaged 16.35 million viewers and peaked with 19.28 million, marking the most-watched NBA game since Game 6 of the [2019 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_NBA_Finals). Despite the strong finish, the series as a whole averaged just 10.27 million viewers, making it the least-watched Finals since 2021—and, excluding the COVID-delayed 2020 and 2021 editions, the lowest average since [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals). Moreover, Game 7 was the least-watched of its kind in modern ratings annals.[^cite_note-139] Despite that, Game 7 was the most-ever internationally watched NBA Finals game on [NBA League Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_League_Pass), and the Finals averaged 10 million viewers or more per game.[^cite_note-140] The NBA also generated a record-breaking 5 billion views across all social media platforms during the Finals, up 215% from 2024.[^cite_note-141]

## Aftermath

A day after Game 7, it was revealed that [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton) tore his [Achilles tendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon); he therefore missed the entire [2025–26 NBA season](79260681.md).[^cite_note-150] ESPN sportswriter [Brian Windhorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Windhorst) said of the injury, "I'd hate to even say this, but he might have scored 40 points in this game. We'll never know, but he had nine points in seven minutes. The Thunder were looking a little shaky. It's possible he has a game that is an all-time classic."[^cite_note-151] In an unlucky coincidence, Haliburton was the third player to wear the number "0" and tear his Achilles tendon in the [2025 NBA playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs), after [Damian Lillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damian_Lillard) and [Jayson Tatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayson_Tatum).[^cite_note-152]

The victory parade and championship celebration for the Thunder was held in downtown Oklahoma City on June 24, concluding outside of the [Paycom Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paycom_Center).[^cite_note-153] According to the Oklahoma Voice, around 500,000 people attended.[^cite_note-154]

Without Haliburton and a various injuries to other key players, the Pacers had their worst-ever start to a season in [2025–26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Indiana_Pacers_season), beginning 1–12, which was also the worst for any team in NBA history that made the previous season's NBA Finals.[^cite_note-155] They finished with a 19–63 record, the worst in franchise history.

Meanwhile, the [2025–26 Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_season) went 24–1 in their first 25 games, tying the [2015–16 Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Golden_State_Warriors_season) for the best 25-game start in NBA history.[^cite_note-156] They finished 64–18, just four games off the pace they set in [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_season) despite All-Star forward [Jalen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalen_Williams) missing much of the season due to a recurring hamstring strain. They swept through the first two rounds of the [NBA playoffs](79524481.md) before meeting the Spurs, led by [Victor Wembanyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Wembanyama), in the Western Conference Finals. The Thunder lost the series in seven games at home, failing in their quest to become the first team since the [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Golden_State_Warriors_season)–[2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Golden_State_Warriors_season) Warriors to repeat as champion.[^cite_note-157]
