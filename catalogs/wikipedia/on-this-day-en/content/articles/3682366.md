[Long track speed skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_track_speed_skating) has been featured as a sport in the [Winter Olympics](34069.md) since the [first winter games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Winter_Olympics) in 1924. Women's events were added to the Olympic program for the first time in [1960 Squaw Valley Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Winter_Olympics).

## History

The [governing body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_sport_federations) for speed skating, the [International Skating Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Skating_Union) (ISU), was included in the list of recognized federations when the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) was founded, but was first discussed seriously for the [1908 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics) in [London](17867.md). No speed skating events were contested, although figure skating – also governed by the ISU – was on the programme. The preliminary calendar for the [1916 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_Summer_Olympics), to be held in [Berlin](3354.md), listed a 3-event allround competition,[^cite_note-Kluge_1994,_page-1] but these Games were cancelled because of [World War I](4764461.md).

The International Winter Sports Week in [Chamonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamonix), retro-actively dubbed the [1924 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Winter_Olympics), contained five speed skating events. Uncommon for the time, it not only included an all-round competition, but also awarded medals for the individual distances: 500 m, 1500 m, 5000 m and 10000 m. The all-round event was dropped before the [1928 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Winter_Olympics), even though it remained the only [World Championship format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Speed_Skating_Championships) in the sport until the 1970s; single distance World Championships were not established until 1996.

The [1932 speed skating events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_skating_at_the_1932_Winter_Olympics) were held according to the North American rules, meaning the skaters competed in small packs of skaters (the format paved the way for the development of [short track speed skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating)), instead of the common against-the-clock format. These Games in [Lake Placid, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Placid,_New_York), also saw the first female speed skaters at the Olympics in a set of demonstration events, with all victories being achieved by North American athletes. Women's events were also set to be held at the [1940 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Winter_Olympics),[^cite_note-Kluge_1994,_page-1] which were cancelled. After the [war](32927.md), they were withdrawn again by the decision of the IOC until the 1960 Olympics in Squaw Valley, when the women skated 500 m, 1000 m, 1500 m and 3000 m.

Following the introduction of World Sprint Championships in the early 1970s, the 1000 m for men was added in [Innsbruck 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Olympics), while the women's 5000 m, reinstated by the ISU as an official distance in 1981, made its Olympic debut in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Winter_Olympics). The latest addition to the Olympic speed skating programme is the team pursuit, which was added for the [2006 Turin Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics). Its inclusion was remarkable as it had not yet been contested at a senior World Championship in the form skated at the Olympics at the time of inclusion. It had however been contested at a senior World Championship, the Dutch team won the 2005 title in [Inzell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisstadion_Inzell), but in that form they only had to skate once and be the fastest, while the Olympic form required three starts.

At the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics), [Haralds Silovs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haralds_Silovs) became the first athlete in Olympic history to participate in both [short track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating_at_the_2010_Winter_Olympics) (1500m) and [long track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_skating_at_the_2010_Winter_Olympics) (5000m) speed skating, and the first to compete in two different disciplines on the same day.[^cite_note-star-2][^cite_note-post-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]
