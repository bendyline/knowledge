**John Clarke** (born 10 February 1942) is a British experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and Professor Emeritus at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^cite_note-1] He is known for his various works on measurement devices based on [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity). [Steven Girvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Girvin) has called Clarke "the godfather of superconducting electronics."[^cite_note-2]&#x20;

In the 1980s, Clarke led a research team that included [John M. Martinis](62226822.md) and [Michel Devoret](51263177.md).[^cite_note-\:1-3] Their discoveries in [macroscopic quantum phenomena](35543722.md) using the [Josephson effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect) earned them the [Nobel Prize in Physics](81280496.md) in 2025.[^cite_note-\:1-3]&#x20;

## Education and career

John Clarke was born on 10 February 1942 in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), England.[^cite_note-Nobel-4][^cite_note-5] He attended [The Perse School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perse_School), before embarking on a [Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Science_Tripos) degree at [Christ's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ%27s_College,_Cambridge), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge).[^cite_note-timesnownews-6] He graduated with a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.A.) in Physics in 1964, and then studied for a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Physics in the [Royal Society Mond Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Museums_Site) at the [University of Cambridge](25978572.md).[^cite_note-berkeley-7]&#x20;

In 1965, Clarke became one of the first students to enter the newly founded [Darwin College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_College,_Cambridge), and was the first president of the Darwin College students' association.[^cite_note-8] While conducting his doctoral work under [Brian Pippard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Pippard), Clarke developed a very sensitive [voltmeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltmeter), which he later called "SLUG" (Superconducting Low-inductance Undulatory Galvanometer).[^cite_note-timesnownews-6][^cite_note-berkeley-7] He obtained his doctorate in 1968.[^cite_note-berkeley-7] Clarke has said at various times that his work was influenced by Nobel laureate [Brian Josephson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Josephson), who predicted the [Josephson effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect) in 1962 and was also a previous student of Pippard.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]&#x20;

After completing his doctorate, Clarke gained a postdoctoral research position at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), and subsequently worked at Berkeley for his whole academic career, as Assistant Professor (1969), Associate Professor (1971), and as Professor of Physics (1973–2010).[^cite_note-Christs-11] In 1969, Clarke also joined [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory), eventually retiring as a faculty senior scientist in the Materials Sciences Division in 2010.[^cite_note-LBNL-12]&#x20;

Clarke's association with the University of Cambridge continued, after he moved to the United States.[^cite_note-Christs-11] In 1972, he was elected a Fellow of Christ's College; in 1989, he was a visiting fellow at [Clare Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Hall,_Cambridge), and in 1998 was elected a by-fellow of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge).[^cite_note-Christs-11] Clarke was awarded a [D.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) from the University of Cambridge in 2003.[^cite_note-Christs-11] He was elected an Honorary Fellow of Christ's College in 1997, and of Darwin College in 2023.[^cite_note-Christs-11]&#x20;

## Research

Clarke's research focuses on [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity) and superconducting electronics, particularly in the development and application of [superconducting quantum interference devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQUID) (SQUIDs), which are ultrasensitive detectors of [magnetic flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux).[^cite_note-RS-13][^cite_note-Ford2005-14][^cite_note-Lakhani2025-15]&#x20;

In 1985, Clarke, [John M. Martinis](62226822.md) (his Ph.D. student), and [Michel Devoret](51263177.md) (a postdoctoral researcher at the time) demonstrated the quantum behaviour of a [Josephson junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect).[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-\:2-16] They showed that at low temperature, a macroscopic electronic state associated with superconductors underwent [quantum tunnelling](643769.md) at zero voltage.[^cite_note-17] The same year, by sending [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave) pulses of the system, the [resonances showed quantised energy levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_qubit#Quantized_energy_levels).[^cite_note-18] This experiment was the first evidence of [circuit quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_quantum_electrodynamics), that would become later the basis for [superconducting quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing).[^cite_note-\:3-19][^cite_note-20] The work, which was recognized with the [Nobel Prize in Physics](52497.md) in 2025, was largely funded by the Office of Basic Energy Sciences in the [United States Department of Energy](62866.md). [^cite_note-APS-21]&#x20;

Clarke has also worked in the application of SQUIDs configured as quantum-noise limited amplifiers to search for the [axion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axion), a possible component of [dark matter](8651.md).[^cite_note-RS-13]&#x20;

## Recognition

Clarke obtained an [Alfred P. Sloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan) fellowship (1970)[^cite_note-22] and a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) (1977).[^cite_note-23]&#x20;
