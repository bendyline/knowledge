**Edan Alexander** (born December 29, 2003) is an American-Israeli soldier and former [prisoner of war](25008.md) who became the last known living [American citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_citizen) held hostage by [Hamas](13913.md) in the [Gaza war hostage crisis](75025451.md). His captivity and eventual release after 584 days received extensive [international media coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_coverage_of_the_Gaza_war) and political attention. His abduction during the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), his dual U.S.-Israeli citizenship, and his role as an Israeli soldier made his case particularly significant. Alexander's release was the result of high-profile negotiations involving the [U.S. government](195149.md) and was marked by meetings with President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) at the [White House](33057.md). The negotiations and advocacy to secure his freedom became emblematic of international efforts to resolve the hostage crisis. Upon his return, Alexander received an official welcome in his hometown of [Tenafly, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenafly,_New_Jersey), with hundreds gathering to celebrate his return.

## Background

Alexander was born in [Tel Aviv](31453.md), Israel, on December 29, 2003, to [Israeli](181296.md) parents.[^cite_note-1] The family moved to the U.S. when he was a baby.[^cite_note-k896-2] Alexander spent summers visiting Israeli relatives including his grandparents.[^cite_note-h069-3] After high school in his hometown of Tenafly, Alexander returned to [Israel](9282173.md) in 2022. He joined [Garin Tzabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garin_Tzabar), a program of the Israel Scouts, and enlisted in the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md).[^cite_note-w609-4] Alexander serves in the [Golani Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golani_Brigade) of the [Infantry Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Corps_\(Israel\)).[^cite_note-5]

## Capture and captivity

During the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) on Israel, Hamas militants seized Alexander from his military base. He had volunteered to stay there over the Jewish [Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbath).[^cite_note-c422-6] By his account, he faced off with almost 30 militants by himself before he was kidnapped.[^cite_note-c852-7] Alexander was one of six Americans taken into captivity by Hamas.[^cite_note-w609-4]

Alexander was held in underground tunnels, Hamas safe houses, mosques, schools, and tents of displaced [Palestinians](23267.md). He was once transferred by a militant that was disguised as a woman, going through a busy Gaza market. He reported suffering hunger in captivity, losing a quarter of his body weight after being served only pitta, hummus, rice and canned food, which his father said was reflective of the [food scarcity in Gaza](75828824.md).[^cite_note-c852-7] He described captivity as “year of hell.”[^cite_note-8] He was kept with [Matan Zangauker](81382917.md) for some time, as well as with [Itzik Elgarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itzik_Elgarat?action=edit\&redlink=1), whose body was returned in February 2025 after his death due to a heart attack as he was subjected to [torture](47702.md) during interrogations.[^cite_note-v556-9] According to [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_people) hostages' accounts, Alexander advocated for them with their Hamas captors in English, explaining that the Thais were migrant workers, not Israelis.[^cite_note-h069-3]

In November 2024, Hamas released a video of Alexander in which he pleaded for help[^cite_note-c422-6] and said he did not want to "end up dead like my fellow USA citizen, [Hersh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_and_killing_of_Hersh_Goldberg-Polin)".[^cite_note-v020-10]

Alexander said he had been held in a cage in a [Hamas underground tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas_underground_tunnel) with his hands and feet bound.[^cite_note-v343-11] Alexander was [handcuffed, beaten](76400472.md) and interrogated during his time in captivity, according to his father, who added, “His whole body has bedbug bites. His skin is in terrible condition.”[^cite_note-r792-12]

In April 2025, [Abu Obeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Obeida), spokesman for Hamas’ military wing, the [Qassam Brigades](1703333.md), said Hamas had lost contact with the group holding Alexander.[^cite_note-c614-13]

## Release

In May 2025, Alexander entered Israel from [Gaza](12047.md) in a deal made directly between Hamas and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-j139-14] He was seen with masked Hamas fighters as they handed him over to Red Cross workers in Khan Younis.[^cite_note-b875-15] Unlike prior hostage release ceremonies, he was not paraded in front of a crowd.[^cite_note-r427-16]

Alexander was given a [Star of David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_David) necklace by [Steve Witkoff](40901053.md), U.S. special envoy to the Middle East, who had played an active role in the negotiations.[^cite_note-l694-17] The necklace had belonged to Witkoff's late son, Andrew.[^cite_note-g106-18] Other negotiators credited by President Trump included Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) and US Special Envoy for Hostage Response [Adam Boehler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Boehler).[^cite_note-y643-19] Alexander's family had met with the President multiple times while they were advocating for his release.[^cite_note-u214-20]

## Post-release activities



Alexander with President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office), 2025

Alexander returned to large crowds in his hometown of Tenafly, [New Jersey](21648.md). Congressman [Josh Gottheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Gottheimer), a Jewish [New Jersey Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Democrat), described his return as "a huge day worthy of great celebration across our state.”[^cite_note-u755-21] Alexander was greeted by President Trump at the White House on July 3, 2025.[^cite_note-r792-12] His family urged the Israeli government to continue efforts to free the 58 remaining hostages in Gaza.[^cite_note-r603-22] Alexander also visited [the grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohel_\(Chabad-Lubavitch\)) of [Menachem Mendel Schneerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Mendel_Schneerson), also known as the Lubavitcher Rebbe, in New York. While in captivity, Alexander had seen television footage of his parents praying there for his return together with President Trump.[^cite_note-w033-23]

At a September 2025 gala hosted by [Friends of the Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Israel_Defense_Forces) (FIDF) in New York, Alexander announced he would return to Israel to serve in the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF).[^cite_note-24]
