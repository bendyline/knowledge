**Touring car racing** is a form of [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport) [racing](1022.md) featuring [production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_vehicle) cars, modified for competition. The discipline emphasizes close racing, balanced performance, and manufacturer diversity, with cars that still resemble their road-going counterparts. Originating in Europe in the mid-20th century, touring car racing has since expanded globally through rule sets such as [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_A), [Super Touring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Touring), and the [FIA TCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_Touring_Car) formula. Major championships include the [Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Tourenwagen_Masters) (DTM), [British Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Touring_Car_Championship) (BTCC) and [Supercars Championship](54162.md).[^cite_note-FIAOverview2-1][^cite_note-MotorsportNetwork20242-2] It has both similarities to and significant differences from [stock car racing](28349.md), which is popular in the [United States](3434750.md).

While the cars are not as fast as those in [formula](1618495.md) or [sports car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_car_racing) races, their similarity both to one another and to fans' own vehicles makes for well-supported racing. The lesser use of aerodynamics means following cars have a much easier time passing than in [open-wheel racing](1618495.md), and the more substantial bodies of the cars makes the subtle bumping and nudging for overtaking much more acceptable.

As well as short *sprint* races, many touring car series include one or more [endurance races](699018.md), which last from 3 to 24 hours and are tests of reliability and [pit crews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pit_crew) as much as speed and consistency.

## Characteristics



A [Chevrolet Cruze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Cruze) touring car

Touring car racing started in the mid twentieth century as a long-format style of competition that took place on public roads between numerous towns. The cars were crewed by a driver and, because of their unreliability, a mechanic who carried tools and spares. The legacy of these beginnings can still be seen in modern touring and GT cars – the driver sits offset from the centreline of the car and there is space for a second seat (although they are rarely fitted any more).

While rules vary from country to country and series to series, most series require that the competitors start with a standard [car body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_frame#Unibody), but virtually every other component may be allowed to be heavily modified for racing, including engines, suspension, brakes, wheels and tires. Aerodynamic aids are sometimes added to the front and rear of the cars. Regulations are usually designed to limit costs by banning some of the more exotic technologies available (for instance, many series insist on a *control tire* that all competitors must use) and keep the racing close (sometimes by [ballast weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballast_weight) where winning a race requires the winner's car to be heavier for subsequent races).

Touring cars share some similarity with American [stock car racing](28349.md). Touring cars are, at least notionally, derived from production cars as most stock car racing outside of [NASCAR](21114.md), including [street stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_car_racing) and [late model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_model) racing, continue to be. NASCAR circuits have since moved to a custom platform independent of production cars, unlike touring car circuits.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Touring car racing is also referred to as *saloon car racing*.[^cite_note-5]

## Series of competition

### FIA TCR World Tour



2018 WTCR Race of Hungary

*Worldwide*

Main articles: [TCR World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_World_Tour), [World Touring Car Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Touring_Car_Cup), and [World Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Touring_Car_Championship)

Modern World Touring Car Championship (WTCC) started in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_World_Touring_Car_Championship_season), evolving from the reborn [European Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Touring_Car_Championship). The series merged with the [TCR International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_International_Series) and became the World Touring Car Cup (WTCR) starting from [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_World_Touring_Car_Cup). In [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_TCR_World_Tour), the WTCR was replaced by the [TCR World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_World_Tour).

Running at major international racing facilities, this series is supported by [BMW](3772.md), [SEAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEAT) and [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet). The latter fields a works team, whereas the other two only sell racing kits to be installed on their cars, providing technical support to their customers. In 2011, [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Cars) also entered the championship, fielding a one-car team as an evaluation for a possible heavier commitment to the series. The World Touring Car Championship features 1.6-litre cars built to *Super 2000* regulations based on FIA [Group N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_N).

Following the trend of recent FIA rules, cost control is a major theme in the technical regulation. In 2011 the rules concerning the engine capacity have changed, switching from 2000 cc to 1600 cc turbo engines. Cars equipped with the old 2000 cc engines are still eligible in the championship. Many technologies that have featured in production cars are not allowed, for example: variable valve timing, variable intake geometry, [ABS braking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-lock_braking_system) and [traction control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traction_control_system).

### British Touring Car Championship



2016 BTCC Brands Hatch

*United Kingdom*

Main article: [British Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Touring_Car_Championship)

The British Touring Car Championship (BTCC) currently competes at nine circuits in the UK with cars built to [Next Generation Touring Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Touring_Car) specification, with ballast being used to equalise performance. From [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_British_Touring_Car_Championship_season), cars that ran to the BTCC's own [Next Generation Touring Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Touring_Car) specification were eligible to compete in a phased move away from [Super 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_2000) regulations. Cars are 2.0-litre [saloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedan_\(automobile\)), [station wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_wagon) and [hatchbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatchback) with over 350 bhp (260 kW) and can be front or rear-wheel drive. During the [2016 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_British_Touring_Car_Championship_season) manufacturer team entries came from BMW, [Subaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru), [MG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MG_Cars), and [Honda](13729.md). Since BTCC budgets have been kept relatively low, there is a strong independent and privateer presence in the championship. Manufacturers represented by privateers include [Vauxhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vauxhall_Motors), [Ford](30433662.md), [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota), [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen), Chevrolet, and [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi).

Prior to 2001 the BTCC was contested by cars built to 2.0-litre supertouring regulations and had in its heyday up to nine different manufacturers. [Joachim Winkelhock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Winkelhock) stated on several occasions that it was the best touring car championship in the world, and many champions of that era now race in the [World Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Touring_Car_Championship) (WTCC). Between 2002 and 2006 the BTCC ran its own Touring class with Super Production/Super 2000 cars making up the numbers; the Touring class was phased out (only privateers are eligible to run old Touring cars) with the intention of a pure Super 2000 series. The introduction of the [Next Generation Touring Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Touring_Car) specification, from [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_British_Touring_Car_Championship_season), started a phased transition from Super 2000 cars in an effort to cut costs and improve the sport.

### DTM



DTM at [Hockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockenheim) in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_DTM_season)

*Germany/Europe*

Main article: [Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Tourenwagen_Masters)

The DTM series, the initials standing for *Deutsche Tourenwagen Meisterschaft* until 1996, then following a hiatus, revived as *Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters* in 2000, features advanced purpose built 2.0-litre [four-cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-cylinder) turbo-powered [space frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_frame) machines, covered with largely [carbon fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fibre) bodyshapes resembling the manufacturers' road machine (although the roof and roof pillars do originate from the production car).[^cite_note-6] In order to lower costs, the engine power is limited to 610 hp (450 kW),[^cite_note-7] and transmissions, brakes and tyres (Hankook) are standard parts for all. Also, dimensions and aerodynamics are equalised. The approx. 985 kg (2,172 lb) (without driver) DTM cars corner incredibly quickly and wear spectacular bodykits incorporating huge wheel arches and diffusers, plus a drag-reduction system (DRS) designed to encourage overtaking.

More than 20 works-backed entries of the [Opel Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opel_Astra), [Audi TT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_TT) and [Mercedes-Benz CLK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_CLK) contested the revived 2000 DTM series but a serious issue developed for the series when Opel pulled out ahead of the 2006 season.[^cite_note-8] BMW would eventually replace Opel as the series' third manufacturer for 2012,[^cite_note-9] while [Mercedes-AMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-AMG) withdrew at the end of 2018.[^cite_note-10] Mercedes-AMG was replaced by a privately funded [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin) [Vantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin_Vantage_DTM) program that did not last beyond the 2019 season.[^cite_note-11]

For the 2019 season, major technical changes occurred. Turbochargers were reintroduced in accordance with new regulations impacting engines and power outputs, as all cars are now required to have 2.0-litre 4-cylinder single turbo engines, replacing the 4.0L V8s that had been used since the series' revival in 2000. Engine power was increased from \~500 hp to 610 hp, with an extra 30 hp available as part of a [push-to-pass system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push-to-pass) available to drivers for the first time.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Downforce levels were also reduced to encourage overtaking and increase top speeds to 300 km/h (186 mph) in order to improve the racing spectacle.[^cite_note-14]

In 2019, DTM formed a partnership with the Japanese sports car series [Super GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_GT) which runs a near identical set of rules and regulations in its GT500 class. Honda, Toyota – represented via [Lexus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus), and [Nissan](21240.md) each entered a wildcard entry for the final race of the 2019 season at the [Hockenheimring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockenheimring). The cars entered were a [Nissan GT-R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_GT-R) (R35), a [Lexus LC 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus_LC_500) and a [Honda NSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_NSX_\(second_generation\)). For the weekend of the 22nd-24 November, DTM sent three BMW M4s and four Audi RS5s to take part in a non-championship race at [Fuji Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Speedway) along with the full GT500 grid, labelled the 'SUPER GT x DTM Dream Race'. Aston Martin withdrew from the event as they intended to focus on developing the Vantage package for 2020, however this never eventuated as the program was ended.[^cite_note-15]

Audi announced in late April 2020 that they would be discontinuing their involvement in the series after the end of the 2020 season, following the same path Mercedes-Benz did after the end of the 2018 season; focusing on electric motorsport, most notably [Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_E). This will leave BMW as the sole manufacturer left, putting the series' future in serious jeopardy.[^cite_note-16]

### Nürburgring NLS Endurance Racing Series

*Germany*

Main article: [Nürburgring Langstrecken Serie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring_Langstrecken_Serie)

Since 1997, and nowadays still on the over 20 kilometres (12 mi) long famous old [Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring) and other circuits worldwide, in average over 150 touring cars compete in the NLS series of ten typically four-hour-long races. Cars range from old 100 hp (75 kW) road legal compacts to 500 hp (370 kW) [Porsche 996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996) and even modified DTM cars (1,250 kg (2,760 lb)). Most entrants of the 24 Hours Nürburgring collect experience here.

### Scandinavian Touring Car Championship



[Alx Danielsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alx_Danielsson) driving a [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn) in the STCC – Racing Elite League

*Sweden/Denmark*

Main article: [Scandinavian Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Touring_Car_Championship)

Between [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Swedish_Touring_Car_Championship_season) and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Swedish_Touring_Car_Championship_season) the [Swedish Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Touring_Car_Championship) contained various races in [Sweden](5058739.md) and a few in [Denmark](76972.md). The most successful car makes were Volvo, BMW, Audi, and Nissan. In 2010 the championship merged with the [Danish Touringcar Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Touringcar_Championship) to form the [Scandinavian Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Touring_Car_Championship). The [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Swedish_Touring_Car_Championship_season) champion was [Robert Dahlgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Dahlgren), because he had achieved the best results from selected races in the Danish and Swedish championships. [Rickard Rydell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickard_Rydell) and [Johan Kristoffersson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Kristoffersson) won the championship in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Scandinavian_Touring_Car_Championship_season) and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Scandinavian_Touring_Car_Championship_season), in a [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Cruze) and a [VW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Scirocco), respectively. In 2013 the series merged with the [TTA – Racing Elite League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TTA_%E2%80%93_Racing_Elite_League) to form the [2013 STCC – Racing Elite League season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_STCC_%E2%80%93_Racing_Elite_League_season), starring 17 drivers for Volvo, BMW, [Saab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_Automobile), [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn), [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Dacia), and Honda.

### Supercars Championship

*Australia* and *New Zealand*

Main article: [Supercars Championship](54162.md)





Gen3 regulation Supercars used since 2023.

Formerly the [Australian Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Touring_Car_Championship), Supercars are recognised internationally as the 'fastest touring cars in the world' racing at speeds that can reach nearly 300 km/h. They are also the most expensive touring cars in the world with each car costing in excess of \$1 million (AUD) which includes bespoke \$250,000 (AUD) 5.0-litre [V8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_engine) engines producing approximately 635 hp (473 kW).[^cite_note-17] The current formula was devised in 1993 (based on Group A regulations) and branded as 'V8 Supercars' in 1997 and 'Supercars' in 2016. The series features grids of approximately 25 cars, although selected events feature wildcard entries which add to the grid. The cars are currently based on the [Ford Mustang GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang_\(sixth_generation\)) and [Holden Commodore (ZB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Commodore_\(ZB\))[^cite_note-18] The minimum weight for a Supercar including driver is 1,395 kg (3,075 lb).[^cite_note-19] The Commodore will be replaced by a [Chevrolet Camaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Camaro) in 2022.[^cite_note-20] The race cars themselves are derived from production body panels and [space frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_frame) chassis. Both body styles feature an aerodynamic package incorporating large front and rear wings designed to ensure equal aerodynamic performance between the two vehicle types.[^cite_note-21]

The series incorporates the world famous [Bathurst 1000](1072273.md) race as a championship round. Because of the longer distance, regulations require two drivers per car for this race. This also applies to [The Bend 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bend_500) & the [Gold Coast 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_600). These events make up the [Pirtek Enduro Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirtek_Enduro_Cup), which is a championship-within-a-championship where the driver combination with most points collected over these three endurance races wins a trophy.

In Australia, Supercars enjoys a strong support base that is still driven in large part by the tribal Ford versus [Holden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden) battle.[^cite_note-22] Over 200,000 total spectators attend the four-day [Adelaide 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_500) and [Bathurst 1000](1072273.md) events, and the [2019 Bathurst 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bathurst_1000) drew a maximum of 2.36 million television viewers across the country.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] This compares favourably with other major sporting events such as the [AFL Grand Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Grand_Final) with 2.2 million TV viewers in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFL_Grand_Final).[^cite_note-25] Supercars is also popular in [New Zealand](4913064.md),[^cite_note-26] with a regular round formerly held in the country (previously held at [Pukekohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukekohe_Park_Raceway)) being the only international event on the series calendar. Attempts at further international expansion were made in [China](5405.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), the [Middle East](19323.md), and the [United States](3434750.md) during the 2000s and 2010s, none of which have survived.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

As the series has grown, major international motorsport organisations have become involved such as [Team Penske](2321219.md), [Andretti Autosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andretti_Autosport), [United Autosports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Autosports) and [Triple Eight Race Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Eight_Race_Engineering_\(Australia\)).

Both Ford and Holden financially and technically supported their favoured teams and took an active role in promotion of the series from its beginning, but began to wind back and ultimately withdraw their financial commitments approximately in line with the decline in sales and eventual discontinuation of the [Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Falcon_\(Australia\)) in 2016[^cite_note-32] and [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Commodore) in 2020 (the two models that exclusively competed in the V8 formula from 1993 to 2012).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Ford withdrew all financial support after [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_International_V8_Supercars_Championship),[^cite_note-36] and Holden cut most of its support back to only the [Red Bull Holden Racing Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Eight_Race_Engineering) from 2017.[^cite_note-37] Holden was shut down as a brand during 2020, ending its factory involvement in Supercars after the [2020 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Supercars_Championship),[^cite_note-38] while Ford returned for the [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Supercars_Championship) season with the Mustang project.[^cite_note-39] The Commodore will be replaced with a [Chevrolet Camaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Camaro), which will be sold in Australia by [General Motors Specialty Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_Specialty_Vehicles).

Other manufacturers have also appeared in the series, including Nissan with [Kelly Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Racing),[^cite_note-40] Volvo with [Garry Rogers Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Rogers_Motorsport),[^cite_note-41] and [Mercedes-Benz](18564164.md) in a non-factory-supported program from [Erebus Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erebus_Motorsport).[^cite_note-42] With Kelly Racing's switch to Ford Mustangs for 2020[^cite_note-43] after a year of running its Nissan Altimas privately,[^cite_note-44] Supercars reverted to a two-make Ford vs. Holden competition.
