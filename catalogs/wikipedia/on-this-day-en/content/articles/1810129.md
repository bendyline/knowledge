**_La Stampa_** (lit. 'The Press') is an Italian [daily newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper#Daily). Headquartered in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) and owned by the [Agnelli family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnelli_family), *La Stampa* is one of Italy's oldest and most prominent national newspapers.[^cite_note-lapo-1][^cite_note-2]

## History

In February 1867, the paper was founded by journalist and novelist Vittorio Bersezio,[^cite_note-lapo-1][^cite_note-sabi-3] with the name *Gazzetta Piemontese*.[^cite_note-esi-4] In 1895, the newspaper was bought, and by then edited, by [Alfredo Frassati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Frassati?action=edit\&redlink=1) (father of [Pier Giorgio Frassati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Giorgio_Frassati)), who gave it its current name and a national perspective.[^cite_note-esi-4] For criticizing the 1924 murder of the socialist [Giacomo Matteotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Matteotti), he was forced to resign and sell the newspaper to [Giovanni Agnelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Agnelli_\(entrepreneur,_born_1866\)), who cofounded the automobile manufacturer [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_S.p.A.).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-esi-4] The financier [Riccardo Gualino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccardo_Gualino) also took a share.[^cite_note-6]

Until the late 1970s and early 1980s, when Italy underwent a nationalization process, *La Stampa* and *Corriere della Sera* were not real national daily newspapers, as their geographical area of circulation was mostly limited to [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) for *La Stampa* and [Lombardy](43807.md) for *Corriere della Sera*; thus, both papers shared a readership that was linked to its place of residence and its social class, mostly from the industrialist class and financial circles.[^cite_note-\:0-7] The paper is now owned by [GEDI Gruppo Editoriale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEDI_Gruppo_Editoriale).[^cite_note-phum-8] The former contributors of *La Stampa* include Italian novelist [Alberto Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Moravia).[^cite_note-9] In 2004, [Angelo Agostini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Agostini_\(1959%E2%80%932014\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) categorized *La Stampa* as an institution daily (*quotidiano-istituzione*), alongside *[Corriere della Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corriere_della_Sera)*, in contrast to the agenda daily (*quotidiano-agenda*) such as *[la Repubblica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Repubblica)*, and the activist daily (*quotidiano-attivista*) such as *[Il Foglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Foglio)*, *[Libero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libero_\(newspaper\))*, and *[l'Unità](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Unit%C3%A0)*.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

*La Stampa*, based in Turin,[^cite_note-lvz-12] was published in [broadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadsheet) format,[^cite_note-adsm-13] until November 2006, when the paper began to be published in the [Berliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berliner_\(format\)) format.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] *La Stampa*launched a [website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Website) in 1999[^cite_note-sabi-3] as well as a project called *Vatican Insider*, the latter of which run by the daily newspaper and has among its staff several [Vatican affairs analysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaticanologist).[^cite_note-17] Since 26 May 2006, it has published the monthly magazine *Specchio+*. From 26 January 1996 to 7 April 2006, it was called *Specchio*, which was published as a weekly supplement, a general interest magazine.[^cite_note-18] In September 2012, *La Stampa* moved to its new headquarters in Turin, leaving its historical editorial building.[^cite_note-sabi-3] [Mario Calabresi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Calabresi?action=edit\&redlink=1) was the [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of the daily.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-anbu-20][^cite_note-21]

On 9 April 2013, an explosive device was sent by an [insurrectionary anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrectionary_anarchist) group – the [Informal Anarchist Federation](27167495.md) – to the offices of *La Stampa*;[^cite_note-nra-22] it did not detonate.[^cite_note-nra-22] In June 2017, during the celebration for its 150 years of activity, *La Stampa* hosted the international conference "The Future of Newspaper", where many great actors of the news industry discussed the future prospects for the news agencies. Among them [John Elkann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Elkann), editor of *La Stampa*; [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos), owner of *[The Washington Post](102226.md);* Louis Dreyfus, CEO of *[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde);* and [Mark Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Thompson_\(media_executive\)), CEO of *[The New York Times](30680.md)*.[^cite_note-23]

In April 2020, [Maurizio Molinari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurizio_Molinari) was replaced by Massimo Giannini (former journalist of *la Repubblica* and [Radio Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Capital)). Under his guide, *La Stampa* moved to a more marked [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left) position.[^cite_note-24]

## Circulation

The 1988 circulation of *La Stampa* was 560,000 copies.[^cite_note-phum-8] In 1997, the paper had a circulation of 376,493 copies.[^cite_note-lvz-12] Its circulation was 399,000 copies in 2000,[^cite_note-25] and 409,000 copies in 2001.[^cite_note-adsm-13] The circulation of the paper was 330,000 copies in 2003,[^cite_note-bbc-26] and 345,060 copies in 2004.[^cite_note-27] Its 2007 circulation was 314,000 copies.[^cite_note-28] In 2012, the circulation was 256,203 copies.[^cite_note-29] In May 2023, an average circulation was 87,143 copies.[^cite_note-\:0-7]
