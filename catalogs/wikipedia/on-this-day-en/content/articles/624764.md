**Kryvyi Rih**[^cite_note-4] is a city in [central Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ukraine). It hosts the administration of [Kryvyi Rih Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Raion) and its subordinate [Kryvyi Rih urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_urban_hromada) in [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md).[^cite_note-admreform_2020_kryvyirih-5] The city is part of the [Kryvyi Rih Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Metropolitan_Region). Its population is estimated at 603,904 (2022 estimate),[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-6] making it the [seventh-most populous city in Ukraine](4077166.md) and the second largest by area. Kryvyi Rih is claimed to be the longest city in Europe.[^cite_note-arnika-7][^cite_note-bryzetskyi-8]

Located at the confluence of the [Saksahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saksahan) and [Inhulets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhulets_river) rivers, Kryvyi Rih was founded as a military [staging post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_station) in 1775. Urban-industrial growth followed [Belgian](3343.md), [French](5843419.md) and [British](31717.md) [investment](63121.md) in the exploitation of the area's rich [iron-ore](145352.md) deposits, generally called [Kryvbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvbas), in the 1880s. Kryvyi Rih gained city status in 1919.

[Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)-era industrialisation built [Kryvorizhstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvorizhstal) in 1934, the largest integrated metallurgical works in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). After [World War II](32927.md), Kryvyi Rih experienced renewed growth through to the 1970s. The economic dislocation associated with the break-up of the Soviet Union contributed to high unemployment and a large-scale exodus from the city in the 1990s. The [privatization](24661.md) of [Kryvorizhstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvorizhstal) in 2005 was followed by increased foreign and private investment which helped finance urban regeneration. Beginning in 2017, there were major labour protests and strikes.

Since the beginning of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in February 2022, Kryvyi Rih has been the target of [frequent Russian missile strikes](72034098.md). It was a focus of the [southern Ukraine campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine_campaign), but the closest ground advance by [Russia](25391.md) stalled some 50 kilometres (31 mi) to the south of the city before it was turned back in March 2022.

Kryvyi Rih (Кривий Ріг), which in Ukrainian literally means 'crooked [horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_\(anatomy\))' or 'curved cape', was the name originally given in the 18th century to the general area of the present city by [Zaporozhian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Cossacks). According to local legend, the first village in the area was founded by a "crooked" ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) slang for 'one-eyed') Cossack named *Rih* (literally, 'horn'). The name likely derives from the shape of the landmass formed by the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the river [Saksahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saksahan) with the [Inhulets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhulets).[^cite_note-9]

When governed from [St Petersburg](24320051.md) under the [Tsars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Tsars) (1775-1917), the settlement was identified in Russian as Кривой Рог (Krivoy Rog).[^cite_note-10]

## History

### Early history

See also: [Liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidation_of_the_Zaporozhian_Sich)

In 1734 the Cossack [Zaporozhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Sich) (or Host) incorporated the area within the Inhul Palanka division of their *de-facto* republic. A list of villages and winter camps (zymivnyky) from that time mentions Kryvyi Rih. In 1770, Kryvyi Rih was again recorded as the camp of the Zaporozhian Sich.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-\:7-12]

In May 1775, after the end of the [Russian-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-Turkish_War), [Russian authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) established Kryvyi Rih as a staging post, in the tradition of the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) [yam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_\(route\)), on the roads to the Russian garrisons of [Kremenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk), [Kinburn foreland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinburn_Spit) and [Ochakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochakiv).[^cite_note-13] In August 1775, on the direct order of [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great), the Sich was [forcibly dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidation_of_the_Zaporozhian_Sich). The Cossack lands were annexed to the Russian province of [Novorossiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Russia_Governorate) and distributed among the Russian and Ukrainian gentry.[^cite_note-Zap-14]

The early 19th century saw the construction of the first stone houses (1828), and three water mills.[^cite_note-15] In 1860, the village was designated a [township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Township).[^cite_note-encdict-16]

### Industrial growth

[Alexander Pol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pol) is credited with discovering the Kryvyi Rih Iron Ore Basin ([Kryvbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvbas)).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] This stimulated the development of a local mining industry.[^cite_note-19] In 1884, [Alexander III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_III_of_Russia) started the Catherine Railway Kryvyi Rih and the Kryvbas mines to [Dnipro](989795.md). By 1902, the line had extended a further 505 km to the coal-mining region of [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Donets_Basin-21]

In 1880, with 5 million [francs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franc) of [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(economics\)), Pol founded the "French Society of Kryvyi Rih Ores". In 1882 16.4 thousand tons of ore were extracted from [open-cast operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-pit_mining) on the outskirts of town by 150 workers. The first underground mine of the basin began operations in 1886.[^cite_note-22] In 1892, the *Hdantsivka* [ironworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironworks) was started. Ore began to be processed locally, spawning new metallurgical enterprises spurred by substantial western, and in particular Belgian,[^cite_note-\:10-23] investment. At the same time Kryvyi Rih ore began to feed the German metallurgical industry in [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia).[^cite_note-Donets_Basin-21]

At the end of the 19th century the tallest building was the Central Synagogue, built by a thriving Jewish community of artisans, merchants and traders.[^cite_note-encdict-16] In 1905, the community was subject to [pogroms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogroms), in which the authorities were complicit. Many Jewish people left the area, emigrating to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-24]

The surrounding mines attracted prospectors looking to turn a quick profit.[^cite_note-25] The [supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_\(economics\)) of mined ore soon exceeded [demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand). Many mines had to cut employment or temporarily suspend operations. Workers, many drawn from the Russian-speaking north (from [Great Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Russia)),[^cite_note-26] laboured in harsh conditions with no security. Work in the mines induced lung cancer, [tuberculosis](30653.md) and [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma).[^cite_note-27] In protest, workers began to develop ideas about socialism and democracy. Labour unrest resulted in several terrorist attacks and in widespread [strikes](155134.md).[^cite_note-28]

The [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) interrupted access to the export markets, and many workers were drafted into the military.

### Ukrainian War of Independence

In the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence), Kryvyi Rih changed hands several times.

A council of [*soviet* of Soldiers and Worker's Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_democracy) was formed in 1917. January 1918 saw the first attempt by the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) to establish the authority in the town of the new [Soviet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Soviet_Union) in Moscow.[^cite_note-kryvyirih.dp.ua-29][^cite_note-30] In February 1918, the Bolsheviks proclaimed the [Donetsk-Krivoy Rog Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk%E2%80%93Krivoy_Rog_Soviet_Republic), but then in March conceded the territory under the terms of the [Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk) to the German-controlled [Ukrainian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State).[^cite_note-31]

After the [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Army) and their [Austro-Hungarian allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army) withdrew in November 1918, the town was successively occupied by the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic), the counter-revolutionary [Volunteer Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_Army) of [General Denikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Denikin) (the "Whites"),[^cite_note-kryvyirih.dp.ua-29] the anarchist [Revolutionary Insurgent Army of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Insurgent_Army_of_Ukraine) (the [*Makhnovshchina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Insurgent_Army_of_Ukraine))[^cite_note-32] and, from 17 January 1920, by the Bolshevik Red Army.[^cite_note-\:5-33]

Afterwards it was administratively part of the [Katerynoslav Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katerynoslav_Governorate) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] In 1922 the region was incorporated into the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), a constituent republic of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).

### Soviet era

#### Industrialisation and collectivisation



[AEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEG_\(German_company\)) power station built in 1930

The town, with a population of 22,571, was now designated a city. Mine operations were revived, and in 1924 a 55.3 km (34.4 mi) water-supply system was laid underground. In the summer of 1927, 10,000 people began to work on the [Dnieprostroi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Hydroelectric_Station), a huge dam on the [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River) in [Zaporizhzhia](1873579.md), whose hydro-electric power drove Kryvyi Rih's industrialisation.[^cite_note-37] The first [Mining Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_National_University) opened in 1929. The Medical and [Pedagogical Institutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Pedagogical_Institute) were founded.[^cite_note-38]

In line with [Stalin's plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_plans_of_the_Soviet_Union) for break-neck industrialisation, in 1931 the foundation of the Kryvyi Rih Metallurgical Plant, the future [Kryvorizhstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvorizhstal), was laid.[^cite_note-39] The first blast furnace of the metallurgical works produced steel three years later. The city grew rapidly.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-40] In the surrounding countryside, industrialisation was accompanied [by the collectivisation of agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivization_in_the_Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). The dispossession of the peasants and the confiscation of their harvests induced the *[Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor)* or Great Famine of 1932–33.[^cite_note-41]

By 1941, at over 200,000, the population of the industrial city had increased almost tenfold.[^cite_note-\:6-42]

#### German occupation

During World War II, Kryvyi Rih was occupied by the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) from 15 August 1941 to 22 February 1944. It was administered for most of that period as part of the [Reichskommissariat Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine). In advance of the Germans, industrial plant and machine operators were evacuated to [Nizhny Tagil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Tagil) in the Urals.[^cite_note-\:5-33] An initial toleration of Ukrainian cultural activity and propaganda by the pro-German [Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Ukrainian_Nationalists) in the city ended in January and February 1942 with the arrest and execution of the leading Ukrainian activists.[^cite_note-43]



[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) soldiers operating [10.5 cm leFH 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10.5_cm_leFH_18). Svobody Street, 1942

In 1939, 12,745 [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Ukraine) had lived in Kryvyi Rih, comprising about 6% of the total population. Those who did not leave the city during the organized evacuation were systematically concentrated and murdered by the Nazi occupiers during the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust). The first mass killing of two to three hundred by an [Einsatzkommando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzkommando) occurred at the end of August 1941 at a brick works. On 14–15 October a combination of SS, German police and Ukrainian auxiliaries murdered 7,000 more at an iron ore mine. Children were thrown into the pits alive.[^cite_note-44]



Soldiers arresting people, 1942

During [World War II](32927.md), the Stalag 338 [prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) was relocated from [Kietrz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kietrz) to Kryvyi Rih.[^cite_note-ushm-45] The number of POWs ranged from 5,219 in May 1942 to 23,977 in September 1942, and at least 800 POWs died in the camp.[^cite_note-ushm-45] In November 1943, the camp was moved to [Voznesensk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voznesensk).[^cite_note-ushm-45]

Hitler had repeatedly stressed the crucial importance of this area: "The Nikopol manganese is of such importance, it cannot be expressed in words. Loss of Nikopol (on the [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River), today's southwest of Zaporizhzhia) would mean the end of war."[^cite_note-46] The German bridgehead on the left bank of the Dnieper gave the German command a base in order to restore the land connection with their forces locked in the Crimea.[^cite_note-47] During the first half of January 1944,[^cite_note-48] Soviet troops made repeated attempts to eliminate the Nikopol-Krivoy Rog enemy group. The [Nikopol–Krivoy Rog Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikopol%E2%80%93Krivoy_Rog_Offensive) did not succeed in breaking into the city until the end of February. Although the greater part of city was destroyed, a special 37th Red Army detachment prevented the German demolition of the power stations in the city and the [Saksahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saksahan) dams.[^cite_note-49]

#### Post-war

After the war, people lived among the ruins while rebuilding the housing stock. The housing shortage was met by innovative technological solutions, and temporary barracks and houses were quickly built.



Dimitrova Street, like many in the city center, is lined with dozens of [Stalinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_architecture) buildings

In the late 1940s, re-construction was accompanied by [Stakhanovite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stakhanovite_movement) propaganda:[^cite_note-50] Pre-war iron ore production was restored by 1950.[^cite_note-\:5-33] In 1961 this was supplemented by new mines and by the [Central and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Iron_Ore_Enrichment_Works), [Northern Iron Ore Enrichment Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Iron_Ore_Enrichment_Works).[^cite_note-ReferenceB-51] By the end of the Soviet era, Kryvbas was producing 42% of the USSR's and 80% of [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) ore.[^cite_note-52]

At the beginning of the 1960s, the city received a signature 185m-tall, guyed tubular steel TV mast.[^cite_note-53] Housing stock was replaced and expanded with several large [Khrushchyovkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrushchyovka) apartment complexes. Urban planning incorporated broad tree-lined avenues with trams lines running down their center.[^cite_note-54]

On June 16–18, 1963, increased food prices triggered protests in the city, estimated to involve between 1,000 and 6,000 people.[^cite_note-55] After an ex-serviceman who had interacted with the police was severely beaten, there was rioting. Moscow sent in troops. While the authorities admitted to 4 dead and 15 wounded, witnesses report that soldiers killed at least 7, and that over 200 people were hospitalised with injuries.[^cite_note-56] Fifteen hundred people received prison sentences.[^cite_note-\:5-33]



City Hall was built in the year of the city's 200th jubilee.

In 1975, the city's two-hundredth anniversary was marked by the development of the Jubilee mine and adjacent residential area, and by the construction of a new city administration building and park. In September 1976, the Krivorozh wool spinning factory was commissioned.[^cite_note-\:5-33]

In last years of the Soviet Union, and following a sharp reduction in spending on cultural, sports and youth service, the city witnessed neighbourhood-based gang violence—the so-called "war of Runners".[^cite_note-57] The era of *[Perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika)* was also marked by the emergence of independent trade unions, and of new civic and political organisations.[^cite_note-58]

The former [Krivoi Rog Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krivoi_Rog_Air_Base) is located nearby.

### In independent Ukraine

#### Redevelopment and politics

In a [national referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum) on 1 December 1991, Ukrainian independence was approved by 90% of the votes cast in Kryvyi Rih's [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md).[^cite_note-NS-59] The first 25 years of independence was a period of economic dislocation and adjustment. The population of the city decreased by almost 100,000, from a peak of 780,000 in the late 1980s.[^cite_note-\:8-60][^cite_note-\:6-42]



[Nativity of the Theotokos Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_All-holy_Theotokos_\(Kryvyi_Rih\)), 1886, restored in the 2000s

[^cite_note-61]

Assisted by [Metinvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metinvest), investment followed the 2005 [privatization](24661.md) of [Kryvorizhstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvorizhstal).[^cite_note-62] There was extensive redevelopment including new shopping and entertainment centers.[^cite_note-63] In July 2020 as part of the administrative reform of Ukraine, Kryvyi Rih Municipality and the Kryvyi Rih Raion came under a common city authority.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] The city remains the second most important in the Dnipropetrovsk region after [Dnipro](989795.md). Krivyi Rih has two independent universities, and several institutes and technical schools.[^cite_note-\:5-33]

Until the events of [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) in 2014 and their aftermath, in local and national elections Kryvyi Rih favored Russian-friendly candidates belonging first, in the 1990s, to the [Communist Party of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Ukraine) and then, in the new century, the [Party of Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Regions). In 2010 the city elected Party of Regions Yuriy Vilkul mayor, and helped [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) to victory in the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_presidential_elections). After the [Euromaidan events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan), which were accompanied by demonstrations and clashes in the city centre,[^cite_note-66] support began to ebb from the Party of Regions. [Petro Poroshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko), who insisted that Russian separatists in the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) "don't represent anybody",[^cite_note-separatists-67] was supported in the presidential election of 2014. Vilkul was re-elected mayor in 2015, but amidst large-scale protests alleging electoral fraud.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

In the 2019 Ukrainian presidential election the city supported its native son [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md),[^cite_note-\:9-70] who defeated Poroshenko in the second round in April.[^cite_note-ukrpravda-71][^cite_note-\:3-72][^cite_note-\:4-73] In the July 2019 elections for the [Ukrainian Rada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Council_of_Ukraine), candidates for [Zelenskyy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelensky)'s [Servant of the People Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servant_of_the_People) won the city's three parliamentary seats. However, in December 2020, the Servant of the People candidate for mayor, Dmytr Shevchuk, lost to Kostantin Pavlov of the pro-Russian [Opposition Platform — For Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_Platform_%E2%80%94_For_Life).[^cite_note-74]

On 15 August 2021, Pavlov was found dead in the entrance to his home, a gun lying next to his body.[^cite_note-75] In September, reporting on an investigation that included a search of the home of the former, and now acting, mayor, Yyriy Vilkul,[^cite_note-76] the Minister of Internal Affairs [Denys Monastyrsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denys_Monastyrsky) suggested that Pavlov may have committed suicide against the backdrop of a large-scale audit of the city's budget.[^cite_note-77]

#### Labour protests

Beginning in 2017, Kryvyi Rih had major labour unrest. In May 2017, coordinated protest actions began at the city's main plants, Kryvyi Rih Iron Ore Plant, Evraz-Sukha Balka and AMKR. Employees stopped work, held public meetings and occupied administration offices. Noting that they were receiving one of the lowest wages across the global industry, the metalworkers raised the demand for a monthly wage of US\$1,000/Euros. The conflict stopped after an agreement was reached to gradually raise wages, on average by 50%. In 2018, protest erupted again triggered by the fatal result of underinvestment in plant and safety. On the night of 3–4 March 2018, the roof collapsed at AMKR's converter shop, killing a 25-year-old worker.[^cite_note-78]

In May 2018, the [ArcelorMittal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal) steel plant ground to a halt as workers refused to guide trains along the factory's self-enclosed supply chain until they received monthly pay of 1,000 euros. Management brought in employees from state-owned railway company Ukrzaliznytsia to run the factory, breaking the strike but leaving the central dispute in place. An underlying problem, according to ArcelorMittal's chief procurement officer, was a labour shortage. Skilled workers were emigrating to [Poland](22936.md), [Czechia](5321.md), and to other countries. But the plant's upper management saw costs associated with the higher salaries that might retain workers as an unacceptable threat to an ambitious, multibillion-dollar factory modernization project.[^cite_note-\:0-79]

[Kryvorizhstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvorizhstal), Ukraine's largest integrated steel company, had been privatised in 2005 in a publicly televised auction. This was after the incoming government of President [Viktor Yushchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko) cancelled a 2004 auction that had seen the company sold at a much lower price, to a consortium that included the son-in-law of ex-President [Leonid Kuchma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kuchma). The Indian-owned international steel conglomerate [Mittal Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittal_Steel_Company) proved successful with a bid of \$4.8 billion, equivalent to a fifth of Ukraine's national budget.[^cite_note-80] In 2006, Mittal took over its international rival, Arcelor, to form [ArcelorMittal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal) headquartered in [Luxembourg City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_City).[^cite_note-81] Over the next five years, the company said it invested more than \$5 billion in its Kryvyi Rih operations.[^cite_note-\:2-82]

On 15 October 2020, in an action that began with 393 miners occupying mine-shafts,[^cite_note-\:1-83] 18 iron-ore miners came to the surface after spending a total of 43 days underground to protest pay and conditions. The mine administration had introduced piecework wages for most jobs underground, linking people's daily income to the amount of ore mined. In response to this, and to above-ground worker blockades, plant management made concessions on wages, benefits and health and safety.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-\:2-82]

#### Russian invasion

Main article: [Kryvyi Rih strikes (2022–present)](72034098.md)

On the first day of the [invasion of Ukraine by Russia](79400268.md), 24 February 2022, there were air strikes against military targets in the city, causing evacuations of residents in the district of Makulan.[^cite_note-85]

On 27 February, city mayor [Oleksandr Vilkul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Vilkul) was appointed the head of the military administration of Kryvyi Rih.[^cite_note-20220227Vikul1429610-86] According to Vilkul, the day previously — the second day the war — the Russian military had attempted an air assault. An [Ilyushin Il 76](229943.md) transport had approached an abandoned Soviet-era air base just east of the city. Carrying more than 100 paratroopers with orders to capture the airfield as an “air bridge”, it was forced to abort its mission, 300 metres from landing. Local defenders had blocked the runway with mechanical equipment.[^cite_note-\:9-70][^cite_note-\:10-23] The following day, according to Vilkul, a Russian column of 300 military vehicles approaching from the south was turned back after ten days of fighting. As an industrial center that accounted for fully 10% of Ukraine's GDP, Vilkul was convinced that Kryvyi Rih was a prime target for Russian forces.[^cite_note-87]

In the third week of the war, Russian troops broadened their offensive across Ukraine and were again advancing toward Kryvyi Rih from the south.[^cite_note-88] On 10 March, two rockets struck the [Kryvyi Rih International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_International_Airport) in [Lozovatka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozovatka?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-89] On 12 March, Metinvest shuttered an open pit iron ore mine in the city, and sent the huge trucks used at the mine to block key roads to slow the Russian advance.[^cite_note-90] In its 15 March briefing, the [Ukrainian Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Ministry_of_Defence) stated that the movement by "occupation troops" toward Kryvyi Rih had been stopped.[^cite_note-91]

According to Russian sources, the invaders faced extensive improvised fortifications and minefields.[^cite_note-92] On 29 March 2022, Vilkul said that the line of contact was no longer on the border with [Dnipropetrovsk region](494909.md), but 40–60 km south in the [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md).[^cite_note-93] He was confident that running 120 km north to south, the longest city in Europe could not be surrounded.[^cite_note-\:10-23]



A residential building in Kryvyi Rih after a Russian rocket attack on the night of 13 June 2023

On 30 March, ArcelorMittal which at the beginning of the month had idled its steelmaking operations in Kryvyi Rih citing concern for the safety and security of its 26,000 workers and for its assets,[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] announced that it was preparing to restart production.[^cite_note-96]

At the end of May 2022, responding to Russian rocket and missile strikes, Ukrainian forces made limited counterattacks south of Kryvyi Rih.[^cite_note-97] The southern [Inhulets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhuletskyi_District) and [Radushne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radushne) districts remain exposed to Russian shelling with civilian losses.[^cite_note-98] At dawn on 25 August, Kryvyi Rih was hit by [cluster munitions](79745.md).[^cite_note-99]

On 14 September 2022, Russian missiles struck the dam of the Karachun reservoir, located upstream of Kryvyi Rih along the Inhulets, causing rising water levels in the Inhulets and water shortage in the city.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-\:92-101][^cite_note-\:82-102] The strike against President Zelenskyy's home town—an attempt, he suggested, to flood the city—came after his visit to towns in the Kharkiv region regained in [Ukraine's first major counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_Kharkiv_counteroffensive).[^cite_note-103]

In 2025, the third year of the war, the city has been subjected to large-scale [kamikaze drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_attack_drone) strikes.[^cite_note-104] On 4 April, a [Iskander-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander-M) [ballistic missile strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Kryvyi_Rih_playground_strike) hit a residential area, including a playground killing 20 people, including 9 children. It was the deadliest single strike involving children since the beginning of the full scale invasion.[^cite_note-CNNKryvyiRihStrike-105][^cite_note-ohchr-turk-106] In 2026, repeated missile strikes halted primary steel production at ArcelorMittal.[^cite_note-107]

The city of Kryvyi Rih is governed by the [Kryvyi Rih Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_City_Council). It is a city community that is designated as a separate district within its [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast).

Administratively, the city is divided into [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_districts_of_Ukraine) (*raions*). There are 7 districts: Metalurhiinyi, Tsentralno-Miskyi, Ternivskyi, Saksahanskyi, Inhuletskyi, Pokrovskyi and Dovhyntsivskyi. Small townships, Avanhard, Horniatske, Ternuvatyi Kut, Kolomiitseve and Novoivanivka were added to the city.[^cite_note-108]

In 1775, the Inhulets Povit (territory) of [Novorossiysk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Governorate) was established on lands of the Inhulets palanca, after the abolition of the [Zaporozhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Sich). In 1775/1776 it was part of [Kherson Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson_Governorate). In 1783, the povit centre became Kryvyi Rih, and it was renamed "Kryvyi Rih Povit". In 1860, Kryvyi Rih received the status of township within the [Kherson Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson_Governorate). In 1919, the township was granted city status in [Yekaterinoslav Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Governorate) and, later, [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md). As a result of the administrative reform in 1923, Kryvyi Rih povit converted to Kryvyi Rih county. In 1930, it became an independent administrative unit of Ukraine.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

Kryvyi Rih has four single-mandate [parliamentary constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) entirely within the city, through which members of parliament (MPs) are elected to represent the city in [Ukraine's national parliament](844993.md). At the [2014 Ukrainian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Ukrainian_parliamentary_election), they were won by [Petro Poroshenko Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko_Bloc) and independent candidates with representation being from Yuri Pavlov, Andriy Halchenko, [Konstantin Usov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Usov) respectively.[^cite_note-111]

In multimember districts, the city voted for the [Opposition Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_Bloc), a union of all political forces that did not endorse [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan). In the [2019 Ukrainian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ukrainian_parliamentary_election) the party of [Ukrainian President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_President) and Kryvyi Rih native [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md), [Servant of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servant_of_the_People_\(political_party\)), took three of the city's seats. The fourth was taken by Dmytro Shpenov, previously of the Opposition Bloc, standing as an independent.[^cite_note-ukrpravda-71][^cite_note-\:3-72][^cite_note-\:4-73]

## Culture



Local history museum with one of the biggest flower clocks in Europe

[^cite_note-112]

Kryvyi Rih has a thriving theatre, circus and dance scene, and is home to a number of large performance venues. There are also the Doll Theatre and Movement Theatre. The first theater was the Coliseum, built in 1908. The New Theatre of Vyzenberh and Hrushevskyy followed in 1911, at the corner of Lenina and Kalynychenko streets. Kryvbas Theatre began its activities in 1931, and three years later was incorporated with the Shevchenko Theater.

Kryvyi Rih is noted as the birthplace [Eugenie Gershoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenie_Gershoy). She emigrated to the United States with her family in 1903, and there became an American sculptor and watercolorist. Gershoy's work is in the collections of the [Whitney Museum of American Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Museum_of_American_Art), the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art), and the [Smithsonian American Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_American_Art_Museum). Her papers are held at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University).[^cite_note-113] Indie band [Brunettes Shoot Blondes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunettes_Shoot_Blondes), folk musician [Eduard Drach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Drach), actress [Helena Makowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Makowska), and dancer [Vladimir Malakhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Malakhov_\(dancer\)) originated from the city.

The first film screenings were conducted in the city in the early 1920s. In 1934 Lenin Cinema was built. Today there are three movie theaters: Olympus, [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa_Kino) and [Multiplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplex_Cinemas).[^cite_note-114] The Kryvyi Rih Circus features large-scale exhibition space where fairs are held.[^cite_note-115] A remnant of Soviet heritage are [Palaces of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture), located in every district of the city.

The local historical museum celebrates Cossack history, the industrial heritage of the area and its role in the Soviet State. The municipally owned Art Gallery houses a collection of local paintings.[^cite_note-116]

The nightlife of the city has expanded significantly since the 2000s. Big clubs such as Hollywood[^cite_note-117] and Sky have attracted touring DJs and pop and rap performers. Another major scene of the city is the Palace of Youth and Students of the [Kryvyi Rih National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_National_University) (KNU).[^cite_note-118] The most popular fast-food, [McDonald's](2480627.md), is located at [Kvartal 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Block) neighborhood.

[Ukrainian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine) is found adjacent to a range of Jewish and popular American foods: [bagels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel), [cheesecake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheesecake), [hot dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_dog), [shawarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawarma) and [pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizza). [Japanese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cuisine) and other Asian restaurants, [hookahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hookah), [sandwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwich) joints, [trattorias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trattoria) and [coffeehouses](370815.md) have become ubiquitous. Other well-known places include City Pub and Prado Cafe. The city is home to the annual [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music) Turbofly festival.[^cite_note-119] [Rock](25423.md) music, a tradition in Ukraine, is an important part of the city's life and is hosted in few small pubs.

## Landmarks

Kryvyi Rih's buildings display a variety of architectural styles, ranging from [eclecticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism) to [contemporary architecture](3970948.md). The widespread use of red brick and block apartments characterize the city. Much of the architecture in the city was built during its prosperous days as a center for the ore trade. Just outside the immediate city center is a large number of former factories. Some have been totally destroyed. Others are in desperate need of restoration.

[Stalinist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_architecture) was the predominant style of postwar apartments, of 5 to 7 stories. City Hall is the best example of [The decree On liquidation of excesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_architecture).[^cite_note-120] [Khrushchyovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrushchyovka) are a type of low-cost, concrete-paneled or brick three- to five-storied apartment building which was developed in the USSR during the early 1960s. They are named after [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), then premier of the Soviet government. Dozens of these aging buildings around the city are now past their design lifetime.

Kryvyi Rih has six [microdistricts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microdistrict).

The city has many [Christian churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_\(Moscow_Patriarchate\)), the most notable being the Savior Transfiguration Cathedral of the Ukrainian Orthodox church in union with the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church). It is the base of the [Kryvyi Rih Eparchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Eparchy), which was established in July 1996.[^cite_note-121] A Roman Catholic chapel located in the old town, Pokrova church, Mykhailivska church and Christmas church were destroyed in the 1930s during the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge), never to be used as a church again.[^cite_note-122] Beside the Russian jurisdiction, in 2009 to 2011 there existed parallel eparchy of the [Orthodox Church of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Ukraine) (as the [Ukrainian Orthodox Church – Kyivan Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_%E2%80%93_Kyivan_Patriarchate)). Domination of the pro-Russian political party [Party of Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Regions) in Kryvyi Rih expelled that organization.

In 2010, the [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism_in_Ukraine) built a new, large synagogue.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

Large parks hold many of Kryvyi Rih's public monuments. There are numerous [socialist realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_realism)-style monuments installed in the Soviet years to honor Cossacks, [Olexander Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olexander_Paul), [Taras Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko) (2), [Bohdan Khmelnytsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohdan_Khmelnytsky) (3, since 1954), Vasili Marguelov, [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin), [Fyodor Sergeyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Sergeyev), [Mikhail Lermontov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Lermontov), and [Maxim Gorky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Gorky). The few [Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin) monuments were destroyed during [euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) events in 2014.[^cite_note-125] Dozens of [cenotaphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenotaph) and memorials to [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) soldiers were erected. A [Sukhoi Su-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-15) is on display near the Aviator Club, a [Yakovlev Yak-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-40) at the [National Aviation University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aviation_University), Vyzvolennia Square holds a [IS3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IS_tank_family) tank, and a [Russian locomotive class Ye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_locomotive_class_Ye) is placed near the [railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Main_Station).

## Geography



MOPR/MODR ([International Red Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Red_Aid)) city area rocks



Red-colored ground is common because of the [iron oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_oxide).

Located 316 kilometres (200 mi) south of [Kyiv](585629.md), the city is the biggest by size within the [Central Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ukraine) and being situated in the right-bank portion of [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md). In addition, the city is located right on the conditional border where [Dnieper Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Upland) transitions to [Black Sea Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Lowland). The Dnieper Upland is part of the [Ukrainian Crystalline Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Shield) which in the region contains number of [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metals) deposits such as iron and uranium ores and [hard rock deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock_deposits?action=edit\&redlink=1) like [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite). Combination of the crystalline shield with the near flowing river of [Dnieper](380138.md) causes the plain-relief river looking as mountainous and running through rapids ([Dnieper rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_rapids)).

Also, the city is often claimed to be the longest in Europe. It is said up to 100 km[^cite_note-arnika-7] or even 126 kilometres (78 mi) [^cite_note-bryzetskyi-8] from north to south. While the city is strikingly elongated on a map, in reality the greatest distance between two points within city limits is 66.1 km. The longer figures result from drawing a line precisely following the heavily indented city limits from north to south.[^cite_note-131]

The city area is not contiguous, with part of the [Inhulets District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhuletskyi_District) being an exclave to the city proper. There isn't a continuous built up area along the full length of the city. Kryvyi Rih's shape is influenced by the [ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore) [deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposition_\(geology\)) which lie parallel to it and which have been the city's mining mainstay. The city centre is on the east bank of the [Inhulets River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhulets_River), near its confluences with its tributary river [Saksahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saksahan). Both Inhulets and Saksahan change their river course in the area often bending in each direction. The [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the river [Saksahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saksahan) with the [Inhulets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhulets) supposedly gave the name for the city by forming a geographical crooked horn ("kryvyi rih").[^cite_note-132]

The city is set in the rolling steppe land surrounded by fields of sunflowers and grain. A short distance east of the city center, there is an area along a small lake where glacial boulders were deposited. As a result, this area was never cultivated and contains one of the few remaining patches of wild [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) vegetation in the area. The city's [environmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment) and [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction) [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety) is a growing problem due to abandoned [mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) and [polluted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) ore-processing [waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste).

### Climate

Kryvyi Rih has a dry warm [hot-summer continental climate (*Dfa*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate#Koppen_Dfa) within to the Köppen climate classification system, like much of southern Ukraine. This tends to generate warm summers and cold winters with relatively low precipitation. Snowfalls are not common in the city, due to the urban warming effect. Districts that surround the city receive more snow and roads leading out of the city can be closed[^cite_note-133] due to snow.[^cite_note-134]

Historically, the population of Kryvyi Rih began to increase rapidly during the [Interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period), peaking at 197,000 in 1939.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-138] From then the population began to decrease rapidly. Foreign workers arrived, and there was increased building of social housing estates by the Kryvyi Rih City Council after the Second World War, such as Sotsmisto and Soniachnyi.[^cite_note-139]

The 2014 estimate for the population of Kryvyi Rih was 654,900 (8th in Ukraine). This was a decrease of 4,348 since the 2013 estimate. Since 2001, the population has grown by 48,001. In 2013, deaths exceeded births by 3,589. The [net migration rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_migration_rate) is 234 (negative).[^cite_note-ReferenceC-138][^cite_note-140]

According to the [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR) and City Council, 7,000 people have fled to Kryvyi Rih from [Donetsk](562951.md) and [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk) since the beginning of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) in 2014, not including those who did not register as asylum seekers.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

Kryvyi Rih historically had a Christian majority-population. It has numerous churches, particularly in the city centre. The well-known Saviour Transfiguration Cathedral in Saksahan Raion is an [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_\(Moscow_Patriarchate\)) administrative centre, and the bishop of the [Kryvyi Rih Eparchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Eparchy) has his main residence here. The town has a school of [icon painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon_painting). The patron saint of the city is [Saint Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas), as well as bishops Onufry and Porphyry.[^cite_note-147]

This was long a centre of [Jewish](25955086.md) population. Its Central Synagogue was the tallest building in town in the late 19th century. The majority of the region's Jews live here, and a significant Jewish community has been re-established. Beis Shtern Shtulman Synagogue[^cite_note-148] opened in 2010 in the city centre. In the early twentieth century, the city had two synagogues, located on Kaunas street. As part of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Kharkiv-Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Kharkiv-Zaporizhzhia), the city has the Kostel of Mary Mother of Jesus. Kryvyi Rih is also home to Evangelical Christians, CEF, and [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas) communities.

In terms of ethnicity, ethnic [Ukrainians](145144.md) account for the majority of the local population. Jews constituted one of the largest [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minority).[^cite_note-149] As of 2018, approximately 71% of Kryvyi Rih's residents were ethnic [Ukrainians](145144.md) and 27% ethnic [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-150]

Large immigrant groups include people from [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), [Poland](22936.md), [Moldova](19260.md) and [Azerbaijan](746.md), as well as [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) and [Roms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people).[^cite_note-151] Numerous [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_people) students come to the city to attend local universities.[^cite_note-152] Central city and Dovhuntsevskyi Raion are centres of population for ethnic minorities.

The [Kryvyi Rih Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Metropolitan_Region) (KMR) had a population over 1,010,000 in 2010. In addition to Kryvyi Rih, the KMR (factually) includes far more than five [raions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_raions_of_Ukraine), and numerous territories in central and southern parts of Ukraine. The KMR is the sixth-largest within Ukraine.

## Economy

In 2020 Kryvyi Rih's share of [Ukraine's national GDP](31829.md) was about 7%.[^cite_note-www\.segodnya.ua/regions/dnepr/dmitriy-shevchik-krivoy-rog-vnosit-nemaluyu-chast-v-vvp-ukrainy-155] In mid-2014, Kryvyi Rih had an [IPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_production_index) of ₴41.6bn[^cite_note-Kryvyi_Rih_City_Council-156][^cite_note-Економічний_прогноз._Кривий_Ріг-157] about \$3bn,[^cite_note-xe.com-158] with 17.9% growth. Exports reached \$2.520m (a 4.9% decrease), Import – \$276m. The city has received \$4.899m of foreign investments, mainly from [Germany](11867.md), [Cyprus](5593.md), [Netherlands](21148.md), and the [UK](31717.md).

The [average wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_wage) in September 2021 was ₴10.258 (\$384).[^cite_note-159] Official unemployment throughout 2018 averaged 0.95%.[^cite_note-160]



[ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal_Kryvyi_Rih)

Processing and the [mining industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_industry) – are the two largest sectors of Kryvyi Rih's economy. Rest fraction is about 50%. The city has over 53 plants, mines and factories.[^cite_note-161] [ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal_Kryvyi_Rih), owned by [ArcelorMittal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal) since 2005, is the largest private company by revenue in Ukraine,[^cite_note-162] producing over 7 million tonnes of crude steel, and mined over 17 million tonnes of iron ore. As of 2011, the company employed about 37 000 people. The 4 Iron Ore Enrichment Works of [Metinvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metinvest) are a large contributors to the UA's [balance of payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_payments). Other giants of the city are the [Evraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evraz) mining company and [HeidelbergCement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HeidelbergCement).[^cite_note-Kryvyi_Rih_City_Council-156][^cite_note-Економічний_прогноз._Кривий_Ріг-157]

## Transport

Local public transportation in Kryvyi Rih includes the [Metrotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Metrotram) (subway), [buses](4146.md)[^cite_note-Kryvyi_Rih_City_Council-156][^cite_note-Економічний_прогноз._Кривий_Ріг-157] and [minibus lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshrutka), [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Kryvyi_Rih) (in operation since 1957, the system presently comprises 23 routes), [trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Tramway) (one of the world's largest tram networks, operating on 88.1 kilometres (54.7 miles) of total route; as of 2014, it was composed of 13 lines) and, [taxi](22026933.md).

The publicly owned and operated Kryvyi Rih Metrotram is the fastest, the most convenient and affordable network that covers most, but not all, of the city. The Metrotram is continuously expanding towards the city limits to meet growing demand, currently has two lines with a total length of 18.7 kilometres (11.6 miles) and 11 stations. Despite its designation as a "[metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_\(rapid_transit\)) [tram](30733.md)" and its use of [tram cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram_car) as rolling stock, the Kryvyi Rih Metrotram is a complete [rapid transit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit_system) with enclosed stations and tracks separated both from roads and from the city's conventional tram lines. City public transport serviced 66m persons in the first part of 2014.[^cite_note-Kryvyi_Rih_City_Council-156][^cite_note-Економічний_прогноз._Кривий_Ріг-157]

In May 2021, Kryvyi Rih became the first city in Ukraine to introduce free travel in public transport for its citizens.[^cite_note-251819KryvyiRihCard-163] In order not to pay for municipal transport one must show a special electronic "Kryvyi Rih Card".[^cite_note-251819KryvyiRihCard-163]

The historic [tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Tramway),[^cite_note-ReferenceB-51] once a well maintained and widely used method of transport, is now gradually being phased out in favor of buses and trolleybuses.

The [taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) system is expansive but not regulated. In particular, the taxi fare per kilometer is not regulated. There is a fierce competition between private taxi companies.

[Kryvyi Rih International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_International_Airport) is the airport that serves the city. It is located 17.5 km (10.9 Miles) northwest of the city of Kryvyi Rih.

## Education



[Kryvyi Rih National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_National_University)'s original building

[Kryvyi Rih National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_National_University), a major institution, was originally formed as a college and Mining Institute in 1929. It gained university status in 1982. [Kryvyi Rih Pedagogical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Pedagogical_Institute) was founded in 1930 as an Institute of Vocational Training, and is the oldest pedagogical institution in Kryvyi Rih, reorganized as a Pedagogical Institute. In 2011 the Cabinet of Ukraine founded Kryvyi Rih National University by uniting the Mining Institute, Pedagogical University, Economic Institute of [Kyiv National Economic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_National_Economic_University) and Department of the [National Metallurgical Academy of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Metallurgical_Academy_of_Ukraine).

Other institutions include the local Department of Dnipropetrovsk State University of Internal Affairs, campuses of [Zaporizhzhia National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_National_University), [National University Odesa Law Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_Odesa_Law_Academy) and [Interregional Academy of Personnel Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregional_Academy_of_Personnel_Management), college of [National Aviation University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aviation_University).

In 2014 Donetsk Tugan-Baranovsky National University of Economics and Trade was evacuated to Kryvyi Rih after a start of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-164]

There are 149 general secondary schools and 150 nursery schools and kindergartens in Kryvyi Rih.[^cite_note-Kryvyi_Rih_City_Council-156] There are evening schools for adults, musical, art, sports and specialist technical schools.

## Sport



[Metalurh Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalurh_Stadium_\(Kryvyi_Rih\)), the home ground of [FC Kryvbas Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Kryvbas_Kryvyi_Rih_\(1959%E2%80%932013\))

[FC Hirnyk Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Hirnyk_Kryvyi_Rih) was a [football](10568.md) club founded in 1925 based in Kryvyi Rih, dissolved in 2020.

Kryvyi Rih was home to another football team, [FC Kryvbas Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Kryvbas_Kryvyi_Rih). The team was founded as FC Kryvyi Rih in 1959. The next year it was part of the republican sports society Avanhard. After a couple of years, it changed to Hirnyk, before obtaining current its name in 1966. Kryvbas debuted in the [Ukrainian Premier League](2322171.md) in the [1992–93 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Vyshcha_Liha). They had been in the top league since their debut, with their best finish in third place in the 1998–99 and 1999–2000 seasons.

At the end of the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Ukrainian_Premier_League) the team finished in 7th place. Due to financial difficulties the club declared itself bankrupt in June 2013. [FC Kryvbas-2 Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Kryvbas-2_Kryvyi_Rih) was the reserve team of Kryvbas. In 1998 the club entered into the professional leagues to compete in the [Second League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Second_League). In 3 seasons the club moved to the Amateur Level before competing one last time in [Second League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Second_League).

[SC Kryvbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Kryvbas) is a professional basketball club. Achievements of the team are winning the Ukrainian Basketball League in 2009 and winning the Higher League in 2003 and 2004. Since 2010 the team is active in the [Ukrainian Basketball SuperLeague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Basketball_SuperLeague).

The city is famous for its annual autorally. It was also the birthplace of the Ukrainian tennis players [Valeria Bondarenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeria_Bondarenko), [Alona Bondarenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alona_Bondarenko) and [Kateryna Bondarenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kateryna_Bondarenko).
