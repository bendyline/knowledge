The **1970 FIFA World Cup** was the ninth edition of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the quadrennial international [football](10568.md) championship for [men's senior national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_national_association_football_teams). Held from 31 May to 21 June in Mexico, it was the first World Cup tournament held outside Europe and South America, and also the first hosted in North America. Teams representing 75 nations from all six populated continents entered the competition, and its [qualification rounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) began in May 1968. Fourteen teams qualified from this process to join host nation [Mexico](808402.md) and defending champions [England](9904.md) in the 16-team final tournament. [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team) and [Morocco](1024865.md) made their debut appearances at the final stage.

In the [tournament final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_Final), [Brazil](149286.md) won 4–1 against [Italy](362466.md), another FIFA two-time champion (and [UEFA Euro 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968) winner). Brazil also overcame another two-time champion and [South American champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_South_American_Championship) [Uruguay](679782.md), by 3–1 in the semi-final, and [defending champions](61629.md) [England](9904.md) 1–0 in the group stage as they were also eliminated by [West Germany](250204.md) in the quarter-finals. This is currently the only time that the winning team defeated the European and South American champions alongside the tournament's defending champions.

The win gave Brazil their third World Cup title, which allowed them to permanently keep the [Jules Rimet Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy#Jules_Rimet_Trophy), and a [new trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy) was introduced in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup). The victorious team, led by [Carlos Alberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Torres) and featuring players such as [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9), [Gérson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rson), [Jairzinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jairzinho), [Rivellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivellino) and [Tostão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tost%C3%A3o), is often cited as the greatest football team of all time.[^cite_note-BBC_review-1][^cite_note-Brazil_poll-2][^cite_note-Independent-3] They achieved a perfect record of wins in all six games in the finals, as well as winning all [their qualifying fixtures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification#Group_2_3).[^cite_note-Perfect_record-4]

Despite the issues of altitude and high temperature,[^cite_note-5] the finals largely produced attacking football which created an average goals per game record not since bettered by any subsequent World Cup Finals.[^cite_note-Castrol-6][^cite_note-Brasil_2014-7][^cite_note-Technical_Report-8] With the advancements in satellite communications, the 1970 Finals attracted a new record television audience for the FIFA World Cup as games were broadcast live around the world[^cite_note-9] and, in a few cases, in colour—the first time that that was the case.[^cite_note-FIFA_overview-10][^cite_note-11]

## Host selection

Further information: [FIFA World Cup hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts)

Mexico was chosen as the host nation on 8 October 1964 vote at the [FIFA](11049.md) congress in [Tokyo](30057.md), where the only other bid submitted was by Argentina, which would go on to host the [1978 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-Hosting-12]

The 1970 selection of Mexico as host would mark several distinctions. It was the first World Cup that was staged in North America and the first to be staged outside of South America and Europe. Further distinction would come to Mexico at the [1986 World Cup](157231.md) when it became the first country to host the FIFA World Cup twice after stepping in as a substitute for Colombia, which was struggling financially.[^cite_note-Hosting-12]

A total of 75 teams entered the 1970 FIFA World Cup, and 73 were required to qualify. Due to rejected entries and withdrawals, 68 teams eventually participated in the qualifying stages, including eight for the first time.[^cite_note-Qualifying-13][^cite_note-Qualifying_stats-14] [Mexico](808402.md) as the host nation and [England](9904.md) as reigning World Cup champions were granted automatic qualification, with the remaining 14 finals places divided among the continental confederations.[^cite_note-Qualifying-13]

Eight places were available to teams from [UEFA](32332.md) (Europe), three for [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) (South America), one for [CAF](715265.md) (Africa), one for a team from either the [AFC](715249.md) or the [OFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) (Asia/Oceania), and one for [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) (North and Central America and Caribbean).[^cite_note-Qualifying-13]

A place in the finals for an African team was guaranteed for the first time: this came in response to the events of [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF,_AFC_and_OFC\)), where all 15 entered African nations boycotted the qualifying in protest after FIFA – which had combined the qualifying for Africa, Asia and Oceania into one group for only one place at the finals – declined CAF's requests to change the qualifying format and the allocation of places, citing competitive and logistical issues.[^cite_note-African_history-15][^cite_note-Qualifying_draw-16][^cite_note-17] Thus, while [Morocco](1024865.md) was the first African team to qualify for the World Cup since [Egypt](1022953.md) in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), they were the first to be assured qualification via their own continental zone, since Egypt had to play against [the British-occupied Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine_national_football_team) to qualify for the 1934 tournament.

The draw for the qualifying stages was conducted on 1 February 1968,[^cite_note-Qualifying_stats-14] with matches beginning in May 1968 and the final fixtures being concluded in December 1969. [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team), quarter-finalists at the [previous tournament](61629.md), were disqualified during the process after refusing to play in Israel for political reasons.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Glanville-19] [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team) qualified for the finals after beating [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) in a [play-off match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification#Group_2_4), which was the catalyst for a four-day conflict in July 1969 known as the [Football War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_War).[^cite_note-20]

Half of the eventual qualifying teams had also been present at the previous World Cup, but three teams qualified for the first time: [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team) and [Morocco](1024865.md), while [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team), [Belgium](679716.md) and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) made their first World Cup appearances since [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), [1954](60523.md) and [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) respectively. Czechoslovakia returned to the World Cup stage after missing out in [1966](61629.md).[^cite_note-Stat_Kit-21][^cite_note-22]

## Venues

Five stadiums in five cities were selected to host the World Cup matches. Alternative venues in [Hidalgo state](224021.md) and the port city of [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veracruz,_Veracruz) were also considered.[^cite_note-23] Each group was based solely in one city with exception of Group 2, which was staged in both [Puebla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puebla,_Puebla) and [Toluca](17551726.md). Aside from the [Estadio Luis Dosal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesio_Diez_Stadium), all the stadia had only been constructed during the 1960s, as Mexico prepared to host both the World Cup and the [1968 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-24] The stadium construction cost for the four smaller venues were around US\$11 million, while the final stadium in Mexico City cost US\$20 million.[^cite_note-25]

All sites were in Central Mexico, on the [Mexican Plateau](12631416.md); none were below 1,500 metres (4,900 ft) above sea level. The altitude of the venues varied and [the importance of acclimatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_high_altitude_on_humans) was strongly considered by all the participating teams. As a result, in contrast to the previous tournament staged in England, most teams arrived in the region well in advance of their opening fixtures [to prepare for this factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_training).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Some teams had already experienced the local conditions when competing in the [football competition at 1968 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1968_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-Technical_Report-8] At an elevation in excess of 2,660 metres (8,730 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_mean_sea_level), [Toluca](17551726.md) was the highest of the venues; [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara) was the lowest at 1,500 m (4,920 ft). In addition to the altitude, all five locations had hot and rainy weather where temperatures would regularly go past 32 °C (90 °F).

Of the five stadia used for the 32 matches played, the largest and most utilised venue was the [Azteca Stadium](1834902.md) in Mexico City, which hosted 10 total matches including the final and match for third place, and all of Group 1's matches (which included all of host Mexico's matches). The Jalisco Stadium in Guadalajara hosted eight matches including all of Group 3's matches and a semi-final. The Nou Camp Stadium in Leon hosted seven matches, which consisted of all of Group 4's matches and a quarter-final match. The Luis Dosal stadium in Toluca hosted four matches, and Cuauhtémoc stadium in Puebla hosted three matches and was the only stadium of the five used for this tournament not to host any knockout rounds.

## Format



An [Adidas Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Telstar) football used at the 1970 World Cup matches.

The 16 qualified teams were divided into four groups of four. Each group was played as a [single round-robin](939742.md) in which each team would play each of the others in their group once. Teams were ranked by the number of points earned from their matches (two points were awarded for a win and one for a draw), and goal difference was used as the primary tie-breaker; if two teams finished level on both points and goal difference, lots were drawn to separate them. This method was used to separate the top two teams in Group 1 (Soviet Union and Mexico),[^cite_note-28] but it was not used to separate Bulgaria and Morocco in Group 4, despite them finishing level on points and goal difference, as they were both eliminated anyways.

The top two teams from each group progressed to the knockout stage. In all knockout matches, extra time was played if the teams were drawn after 90 minutes. If teams were still tied after extra-time, drawing of lots would have been used in all matches other than the final. FIFA did not announce in advance what would happen if the final itself were to be drawn after extra time.[^cite_note-29] In the event, all knockout matches were decided in either regular time or extra time.

## Final draw

Although it was reported in the build-up to the final draw that seedings would be used, as had been the case at the previous two World Cup Finals,[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-Seeding_criteria_negotiaions-32] the FIFA Organising Committee ultimately announced on the same day of the draw, that there would be no seeding of teams due to failed attempts to reach a compromise for the seeding criteria.[^cite_note-FIFA_seeding-33] Instead, the 16 qualified teams were divided into four pots comprising 'geographical groupings' decided by a vote of the FIFA Organising Committee, which for the composition of pot 1, 3 and 4 also took into account the teams' strengths and even political considerations.[^cite_note-Seeding_criteria_negotiaions-32] The tournament structure ensured that teams grouped within the same pot would not meet each other before the quarter-finals at the earliest. The system of politically decided pots hereby ensured that Israel and Morocco would not be drawn to face each other after Morocco had earlier threatened to withdraw from the tournament, as they had done from the Olympic football tournament two years earlier,[^cite_note-34] if that were the case.[^cite_note-35]

The draw was staged in [Mexico City](18987.md), Mexico on 10 January 1970 in the [Maria Isabel Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheraton_Mexico_City_Maria_Isabel_Hotel), which served as FIFA's headquarters during the competition.[^cite_note-Seeding_criteria_negotiaions-32][^cite_note-36] The teams were drawn into the four groups, which had their locations defined in advance: Group 1 being staged in Mexico City, Group 2 in [Puebla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puebla,_Puebla) and [Toluca](17551726.md), Group 3 in [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara) and Group 4 in [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Guanajuato). Ahead of the draw, the FIFA regulations had predetermined that the hosts [Mexico](808402.md) would be in Group 1 and so based in the capital city, and that [England](9904.md) as holders would be based together with Group 3 in Guadalajara,[^cite_note-Seeding_criteria_negotiaions-32][^cite_note-Draw_of_Groups-37] the tournament's second largest stadium.[^cite_note-Dawson-38] Keeping the defending champion ([holders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy#Winners)) apart from meeting the hosts in the group stage either by seeding or predetermined group positions, was a practiced tradition throughout the history of the FIFA World Cup, with [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1954](60523.md) being the only two exceptions.[^cite_note-FIFA_seeding-33]

The 10-year-old daughter of [Guillermo Cañedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Ca%C3%B1edo_de_la_B%C3%A1rcena), president of the [Mexican Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Football_Federation) and chief of the [FIFA Organising Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Organisers?action=edit\&redlink=1), drew out the teams from four silver cups, so that each of the four groups featured one drawn team respectively from pot 1, pot 2, pot 3 and pot 4.[^cite_note-Draw-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-Draw_of_Groups-37]

## Tournament review



Juanito was the official tournament mascot.

### Group stage

Following the opening ceremony host nation [Mexico](808402.md) faced the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team); this was the last time until the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) that the host nation's first match rather than the World Cup holders' began the tournament.[^cite_note-42] Both this opening match of Group 1 and many others during the competition kicked off at noon for the benefit of European television schedules, meaning play under the midday sun.[^cite_note-FIFA_overview-10] The match produced a goalless draw, prompting some media to predict the entire tournament would be played at the slow tempo that featured in this game given the conditions involved.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] Following the half-time interval [Anatoliy Puzach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Puzach) became the first substitute to be used in FIFA World Cup history as the Soviets made use of the new competition rule.[^cite_note-Stat_Kit-21] Both teams won their remaining two games to progress from the group at the expense of [Belgium](679716.md) and World Cup debutants [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team).

Group 2 was the lowest-scoring of the groups with only six goals in its six matches as [Uruguay](679782.md), [reigning South America champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_South_American_Championship), and [Italy](362466.md), the [reigning European champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968), edged past [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team). Sweden would have progressed if they had produced a two-goal victory against Uruguay in their final game, but it was not until the final minute that they scored the only goal of the game.[^cite_note-45] Hours before the game FIFA elected to replace the scheduled referee after bribery rumours – later dismissed by FIFA[^cite_note-Lots_draw-46] – arose in Mexico.[^cite_note-47] The 1–0 result meant that Uruguay advanced, to be joined by Italy after they avoided defeat in the group finale against Israel.[^cite_note-48]

Owing to the lack of a seeding system, Group 3 allowed the reigning World Cup holders [England](9904.md) to be paired together with the two-time former champion [Brazil](149286.md), considered by many the pre-tournament favourites for the trophy.[^cite_note-FIFA_seeding-33] England's preparations were hampered by the arrest of their captain [Bobby Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Moore) in Colombia for [allegedly stealing a bracelet from a jeweller's shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_Bracelet);[^cite_note-49] the charges were later dropped.[^cite_note-50] The attitude of their manager [Alf Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Ramsey) and the English media in general was perceived by many locals as unfriendly and xenophobic toward Mexico's hosting of the competition, which meant the English team received a largely hostile response during the competition.[^cite_note-Dawson-38][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-English_win_first_World_Cup_match-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-Times_review-56]

With both having won their opening games – against [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team) and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team), respectively[^cite_note-4_June-57][^cite_note-58] – Brazil met England in the group's most famed match.[^cite_note-59] Although [Gordon Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Banks) in the English goal denied [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) from close range with a reflex save that Pelé himself cited as the greatest of his career,[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] a second-half goal from [Jairzinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jairzinho) won the match for Brazil, after which England squandered several excellent opportunities to equalise.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] Both teams then won their final group games to progress to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-64]

Play in Group 4 began with [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) taking a two-goal lead against [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team), but a second half comeback gave the South Americans a 3–2 victory.[^cite_note-65] [Morocco](1024865.md), the first [African World Cup representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_nations_at_the_FIFA_World_Cup) since 1934,[^cite_note-African_history-15] also began strongly by taking the lead against the 1966 runners-up [West Germany](250204.md), but the Germans came back to win 2–1.[^cite_note-4_June-57] West Germany also went behind against Bulgaria in their second match, but a [Gerd Müller](172313.md) hat-trick helped them recover and win 5–2; the eventual [Golden Boot winner](3482503.md#Golden_Boot) Müller hit another [hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hat-tricks) – the only hat-tricks of the entire tournament.[^cite_note-66] – to win the group against Peru.[^cite_note-67]

### Knockout stage

#### Quarter-finals

Mexico and the Soviet Union had finished tied at the top of Group 1 on both points and goal difference, meaning that the drawing of lots was required to rank them. On 12 June, the draw allocated the Soviet Union the group winners' berth, meaning that they would face Uruguay at the [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md), while the host nation were paired against Italy in the smaller [Toluca venue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesio_Diez_Stadium).[^cite_note-Lots_draw-46][^cite_note-68] Mexican officials unsuccessfully appealed to FIFA to stage their game in the capital to avoid traffic problems.[^cite_note-Lots_draw-46] The hosts took the lead against Italy with a [José Luis González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Gonz%C3%A1lez_D%C3%A1vila) goal, but his teammate [Javier Guzmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Guzm%C3%A1n) equalised with an [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal#Association_football) before half-time. Italy then dominated the second half to progress to the semi-finals with a 4–1 win.[^cite_note-69] The Soviet Union were also eliminated in their quarter-final when a [Víctor Espárrago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Esp%C3%A1rrago) header three minutes from the end of extra-time sent Uruguay through.[^cite_note-70] The Soviets had stopped play during Uruguay's attack leading to the goal, believing that the ball had crossed the touchline.



Official poster

The all-South America tie in [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara) was the highest-scoring of the four quarter-finals as Brazil recorded a 4–2 triumph over Peru. The match is considered to be one of the most entertaining matches in World Cup history: Brazil shot 27 times; Peru, 22. Next came a rematch of [the previous World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) between England and West Germany that took place in [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Guanajuato). The reigning champions took a two-goal lead, but [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) halved the deficit when his low shot beat England's second-choice goalkeeper [Peter Bonetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bonetti), who was playing after [Gordon Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Banks) suffered food poisoning the day before.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Eight minutes from time, an [Uwe Seeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uwe_Seeler) header levelled the score. England's [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) then had an apparently legitimate goal ruled out for offside.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] An extra-time goal from [Gerd Müller](172313.md) brought (West) Germany's first-ever competitive victory over England.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

#### Semi-finals



Plaque commemorating the "[Game of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_v_West_Germany_\(1970_FIFA_World_Cup\))"

All four of the semi-finalists were former world champions, with the line-up guaranteeing a final between Europe and South America. In the all-South American tie, controversially switched from the capital to the lower altitude of Guadalajara,[^cite_note-77] Brazil came from behind to defeat Uruguay 3–1 and earn the right to contest their fourth World Cup Final. Two Brazilian goals in the final 15 minutes decided a match that had been evenly-matched until that point.[^cite_note-78] The all-European meeting between Italy and West Germany produced a match regarded by many as one of the greatest World Cup games of all time. Having led from the eighth minute through [Roberto Boninsegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Boninsegna)'s strike, Italy were pegged back in injury time when sweeper [Karl-Heinz Schnellinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Heinz_Schnellinger) scored his only international goal.[^cite_note-79] Extra-time brought five more goals as the lead swung between the two sides until [Gianni Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianni_Rivera) gave the *Azzurri* a decisive 4–3 lead.[^cite_note-80] The match subsequently became known as the "[Game of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_v_West_Germany_\(1970_FIFA_World_Cup\))",[^cite_note-Game_of_the_Century-81] and today has a plaque outside the Estadio Azteca to commemorate it. West Germany went on to defeat Uruguay 1–0 in the match for third place.[^cite_note-82]

#### Final

In [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_final), Brazil opened the scoring when [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) headed in a cross from [Rivellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivellino) in the 18th minute, but [Roberto Boninsegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Boninsegna) equalised for Italy after a series of blunders in the Brazilian defence.[^cite_note-Final-83] The match remained level until the 65th minute when a powerful shot from [Gérson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rson) restored the Brazilians' lead. Further goals from [Jairzinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jairzinho) and [Carlos Alberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Torres) rewarded Brazil's attacking play and secured a 4–1 victory and a record third World Cup triumph, which earned them the right to permanently keep the [Jules Rimet Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy#Jules_Rimet_Trophy).[^cite_note-Final-83][^cite_note-84]

### Legacy

Both the [Brazilian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_at_the_1970_FIFA_World_Cup) that were crowned champions of the 1970 World Cup and the tournament itself have become regarded as among the very finest in the history of the FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-BBC_review-1][^cite_note-Brazil_poll-2][^cite_note-Herald-85][^cite_note-86] In contrast to the more physical style of play that had dominated the previous two tournaments, the 1970 Finals are noted for the attacking play adopted by most teams.[^cite_note-Castrol-6][^cite_note-Brasil_2014-7][^cite_note-Technical_Report-8]



[Adidas Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Telstar) of [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup)

For the first time at a World Cup Finals, referees could issue [yellow and red cards](3090014.md) (a system that is now commonplace at every level of football worldwide),[^cite_note-87] yet, in contrast to the previous tournaments (besides the 1950 edition) and all subsequent tournaments to date, no player was expelled from play.[^cite_note-88] The officiating of the opening match, commentated by some media as overly strict,[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] set a standard of discipline that instead helped protect skillful players in accordance with FIFA's stated wish.[^cite_note-Times_review-56][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

The eventual champions Brazil, led by [Carlos Alberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Torres), and featuring [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9), [Clodoaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clodoaldo), [Gérson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rson), [Jairzinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jairzinho), [Rivellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivellino), and [Tostão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tost%C3%A3o), is often cited as the greatest-ever World Cup team.[^cite_note-BBC_review-1][^cite_note-Brazil_poll-2][^cite_note-Independent-3] They won all of their six games on the way to the title, and had also won every one of [their qualifying fixtures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification#Group_2_2).[^cite_note-Perfect_record-4] [Jairzinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jairzinho)'s feat of not only scoring in every finals match, but also winning each finals match likewise has yet to be equalled.[^cite_note-94] Coach [Mário Zagallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Zagallo) became the first man to win the World Cup as both a player ([1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup)) and coach.[^cite_note-95]

This was the first World Cup to use the [Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Telstar) ball from [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) (who have supplied every World Cup match ball to date), introduced as the "Telstar Erlast" for the [1968 European Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_European_Football_Championship). The Telstar was the first World Cup ball to use the now-familiar [truncated icosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_icosahedron) for its design, consisting of 12 black [pentagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagon) and 20 white [hexagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagon) panels.[^cite_note-fifalist-96][^cite_note-soccerballworld-97] The 32-panel configuration had been introduced in 1962 by [Select Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Select_Sport),[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-Bernsen-99] and was also used in the official logo for the 1970 World Cup.[^cite_note-100] The black-and-white pattern, to aid visibility on [black and white television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_and_white_television) broadcasts (which was still commonplace then, as colour television was rare in many parts of the world), was also well established before the Telstar.[^cite_note-Bernsen-99][^cite_note-101] The name came from the [Telstar communications satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telstar), which was roughly spherical and dotted with solar panels, somewhat similar in appearance to the football.[^cite_note-fifalist-96]

### Merchandise

Forming a partnership with [FIFA](11049.md) in 1970, [Panini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panini_Group) published its first FIFA World Cup [sticker album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticker_album) for the 1970 World Cup, initiating a global craze for collecting and trading stickers.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] In 2017, a complete 1970 World Cup Panini sticker album signed by [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) sold for a record £10,450.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

&#x20; Group stage

The first round, or group stage, saw the 16 teams divided into four groups of four teams. Each group was a [round-robin](939742.md) of six games, where each team played one match against each of the other teams in the same group. Teams were awarded two points for a win, one point for a draw and none for a defeat. The teams finishing first and second in each group qualified for the quarter-finals, while the bottom two teams in each group were eliminated from the tournament.

**Tie-breaking criteria**

1. Greater number of points in all group matches

2. Goal difference in all group matches (replacing the previous usage of [goal average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference))

3. Drawing of lots by the FIFA Organising Committee

## Knockout stage

Main article: [1970 FIFA World Cup knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage)

The eight teams that had advanced from the group stage entered a [single-elimination](939680.md) style tournament, which also featured a match for third place contested between the two losing semi-finalists. In this knockout stage (including the final), if a match was level at the end of 90 minutes, [extra time](633242.md#Association_football) of two periods (15 minutes each) would be played. In matches prior to the final, if the score was still level after extra time then a coin toss by the referee would determine the winner. If the final was still level after 120 minutes' play then the match would instead be [replayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replay_\(sports\)) at a later date.
