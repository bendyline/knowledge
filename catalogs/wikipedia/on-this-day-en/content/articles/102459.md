A **one-party state**, **single-party state**, **one-party system** or **single-party system** is a governance structure in which only a single [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) controls the ruling system.[^cite_note-1] In the one-party state, all opposition parties are either outlawed or have limited and controlled participation in [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election). The expression "[de facto](43594.md) one-party" is sometimes used to describe a [dominant-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant-party_system) that, unlike a one-party state, allows (at least nominally) multiparty elections, but the existing practices or balance of [political power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_power) effectively prevent the [opposition](3379016.md) from winning power.[^cite_note-2]

Membership in the ruling party tends to be relatively small compared to the population;[^cite_note-3] however, this is not a universal rule, as some one-party states, such as [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire) under [Mobutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko), made the entire citizenry members of the party. Rather, they give out private goods to fellow [elites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite) to ensure continued support. One-party, compared to dominant-party [dictatorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship), structure themselves unlike [democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracies). They also turn into multi-party democracies at a lower rate than dominant-party dictatorships.[^cite_note-Political_Order_and_One-Party_Rule-4] While one-party states prohibit opposition parties, some allow for [independent candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_candidates) to stand for election in competition with party candidates. Therefore, they place elites and sympathetic candidates in key administrative races.[^cite_note-dx.doi.org-5] For example, the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) exercises political control by infiltrating village administrations.[^cite_note-6] They view these positions as crucial for gathering [information on the population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_on_the_population) and maintaining a presence in the far reaches of their borders.[^cite_note-The_Single-Party_Dictator's_Dilemma_2011-7]

One-party states recognize the trade-off between election victory and gathering valuable data. To account for this, the governments have been observed placing local nobility in easy-to-win races.[^cite_note-8] One-party states have also been observed using elections to ensure that only the most popular elites get chosen to office.[^cite_note-Elections_Under_Authoritarianism-9] They also gather data from elections to indicate if a local official is performing poorly in the eyes of the residents.[^cite_note-Elections_Under_Authoritarianism-9] This gives locals the opportunity to monitor local officials and communicate satisfaction with the local government.[^cite_note-Elections_Under_Authoritarianism-9] Throughout the country, members of the one party hold key political positions.[^cite_note-Political_Order_and_One-Party_Rule-4] In doing so, the party avoids committing outright fraud and rather sustains their power at the local level with strategic appointment of elites.[^cite_note-The_Single-Party_Dictator's_Dilemma_2011-7] Data on one-party governments can be difficult to gather given their [lack of transparency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_transparency).[^cite_note-dx.doi.org-5]
