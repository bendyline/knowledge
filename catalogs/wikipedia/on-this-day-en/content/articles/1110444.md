**Sirmaur district** is the southernmost district in the [Himalayan](37966.md) state of [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), [India](14533.md). It is largely mountainous and rural, with 90% of its population living in villages. The district capital is the town of [Nahan](1698715.md). Some other towns include [Paonta Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paonta_Sahib), Lana Palar, Tuheri, Bhawan, Sirmaur, Shamra, UchaTikker and Suketi - the last being known for [Shivalik Fossil Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivalik_Fossil_Park). Culturally, it is a part of the historical [Mahasu region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahasu_\(region\)).[^cite_note-2]

## Geography

There are seven [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) in this district: [Nahan](1698715.md), Renuka, [Kamrau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamrao), [Shillai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shillai), [Rajgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajgarh,_Himachal_Pradesh), Pachhad, and [Paonta Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paonta_Sahib). The [Giri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giri_River) divides the district into two almost equal parts: Giripar and Giriaar. The major towns are Nahan, Paonta Sahib, Rajgarh, and Shillai. Rajgarh is the biggest village of Sirmour district.

## Demographics

According to the [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), Sirmaur district has a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 529,855, which placed it 542nd in India (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-districtcensus-5] The district had a population density of 188 inhabitants per square kilometre (490/sq mi).[^cite_note-districtcensus-5] Its [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) over the decade 2001–2011 was 15.61%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-5] Sirmaur had a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 915 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) for every 1000 males,[^cite_note-districtcensus-5] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 79.98%. 10.79% of the population lived in urban areas. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes made up 30.34% and 2.13% of the population respectively.[^cite_note-districtcensus-5]

As of the 2011 census, 39% of the population of the district identified their first language as [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), 34% as [Pahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari_languages), and 20% as [Sirmauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirmauri_language). There were also speakers of [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) (3.88%), [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) (0.94%), [Haryanvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryanvi_language), (0.43%), and [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_languages) (0.42%).[^cite_note-languages-7]

## [Simbalbara National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simbalbara_National_Park)

Declared a national park in 2010, Simbalbara National Park is located in Sirmaur district. It covers an area of around 27.88 sq.km. The fauna of Simbalbara National Park includes, [Goral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goral), [Spotted Deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chital), Sambhar, [Himalayan Black Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_black_bear), [Hanuman Langurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman_langur) and [Indian Muntjacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_red_muntjac). The flora includes Sal forests and lush green pastures.
