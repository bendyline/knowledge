The **Guided Bomb Unit‐72 (GBU‐72)**, also known as the **Advanced 5K (A5K) Penetrator**, is a [bunker busting](167751.md) bomb developed in the United States.

## Description

The GBU-72 is a 5,000 lb (2,300 kg) class precision-guided [bunker-busting](167751.md) bomb which uses a [JDAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JDAM) guidance kit.[^cite_note-Trevithick-4]

The GBU-72 resembles an enlarged GBU-31/B JDAM fitted with a bunker-busting [BLU-109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLU-109_bomb) or BLU-137/B warhead.[^cite_note-Trevithick-4] It is fitted with the GBU-31/B JDAM’s [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS) and [INS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system) tail-mounted guidance kit and has long fins on either side of the bomb's underside.[^cite_note-Jennings-2][^cite_note-Trevithick-4]

No official data is available for how deep the GBU-72 can penetrate before exploding, although the weapon's program manager has been quoted as saying that its "lethality is expected to be substantially higher compared to similar legacy weapons like the [GBU-28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-28)".[^cite_note-Jennings-2][^cite_note-Axe-3] The capabilities of the later versions of the GBU-28 remain classified, but the earliest versions could reportedly penetrate 150 ft (46 m) of earth and more than 15 ft (4.6 m) of reinforced concrete.[^cite_note-Trevithick-4]

The GBU-72 can be dropped by the US Air Force's [B-1Bs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-1B) and [F-15Es](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15E), and the Israeli Air Force's [F-15Is](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15I).[^cite_note-Axe-3][^cite_note-Barrowclough-Magnay-5]

## History

The evaluation of the GBU-72 at [Eglin Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglin_Air_Force_Base)[^cite_note-Jennings-2][^cite_note-Silver-6] included ground-based tests such as detonating the bomb’s warhead inside barriers to measure its blast and other effects, and test flights planned by the [780th Test Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/780th_Test_Squadron?action=edit\&redlink=1) and carried out by the [40th Flight Test Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_Flight_Test_Squadron) between July and October 2021 that included confirming "the weapon could safely release from the aircraft and validate a modified 2,000-pound joint-direct-attack-munition tail kit’s ability to control and navigate a 5,000-pound weapon."[^cite_note-Jennings-2][^cite_note-Trevithick-4][^cite_note-Silver-6][^cite_note-\:0-7]

The initial testing phase ended on 7 October 2021, when an [96th Test Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/96th_Test_Wing) [McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle](495724.md) released the GBU-72 (known as the GBU-72 Advanced 5K Penetrator) over the [Eglin Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglin_Air_Force_Base) range. The program moved on to more JDAM integration test flights and developmental and operational testing in 2022.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

In May 2024 it was reported that as part of joint American-British strikes during the [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md), the US Air Force used a GBU-72 to destroy an underground [Houthi](17240332.md) facility in [Yemen](350939.md).[^cite_note-CBS-8]

In September 2024 it was reported that the Israeli Air Force deployed up to ten GBU-72s on 27 September 2024 to kill [Hezbollah](13919.md)'s Secretary-General, [Hassan Nasrallah](313436.md), in his underground headquarters complex in Beirut.[^cite_note-Barrowclough-Magnay-5][^cite_note-Rasheed-etal-9] Subsequent reporting stated 2,000 lb (910 kg) class JDAM-equipped [BLU-109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLU-109) bunker-busting bombs were likely used.[^cite_note-Goodwin-etal-10][^cite_note-Cheeseman-etal-11]

In March 2026, multiple media outlets reported that the [United States Air Force](32090.md) employed GBU-72/B in strikes against hardened Iranian anti-ship missile sites near the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) during the [Iran-war](82533582.md). [United States Central Command](338482.md) confirmed the use of 5,000-pound class penetrator munitions in the operation, but did not specify the weapon system. Some reports described the strikes as the first combat use of the GBU-72/B, although earlier unconfirmed reports had suggested possible use in Yemen in 2024.[^cite_note-12]
