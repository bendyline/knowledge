Disputes and clashes in [Syria](7515849.md) have continued following the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) in December 2024, which many sources consider the end of the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md). Numerous actors are involved, including clashes between the newly-formed [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government) and later the [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md) led by the former [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) of [Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham](53015755.md) (HTS) and incumbent president [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md), [Assad](36629941.md) loyalists, [Alawite](436616.md) groups, and [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) insurgents; clashes between elements of the Turkish-backed [Syrian National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Army) (SNA) and the government on one side, and formerly the Kurdish-led [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) (SDF) on the other; as well as an [Israeli invasion and occupation](78549398.md) of the demilitarized buffer zone in southwestern Syria.

## Background

For a chronological guide, see [November 2024–present](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Syrian_civil_war_\(November_2024%E2%80%93present\)) timeline.

See also: [Fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md), [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government), [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md), [2024 Syrian opposition offensives](78460726.md), and [Operation Dawn of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dawn_of_Freedom)



[Syrian opposition offensives](78460726.md) which overthrew Assad's regime in 11 days

On 27 November 2024, a coalition of opposition groups called the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command_\(Syria\)),[^cite_note-6] led by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham and the [Southern Operations Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Operations_Room), launched [a major offensive](78460726.md)[^cite_note-7] against the Syrian Army and other pro-government forces in [Aleppo](7444618.md), [Idlib](7445043.md), [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama_Governorate) and [Homs Governorates](7445154.md).[^cite_note-8] This was followed by other rebel offensives from the [Southern Operations Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Operations_Room), the SDF and the Syrian Free Army which all began seizing Syrian government territory in the country's south and east.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] On 29 November, rebel forces [entered Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aleppo_\(2024\)) as Syrian Army positions collapsed across the country.[^cite_note-13] On 7 December, rebel forces [entered Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Damascus_\(2024\)) and the next day, on 8 December, Bashar al-Assad was reported to have fled the capital.[^cite_note-14] The Syrian Army confirmed Assad was no longer in power and had fled the country,[^cite_note-Syrian_rebels_topple_Assad-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] resulting in the [collapse of his regime](78548066.md) and ending over 60 years of Ba'athist rule and 54 years under the [Assad dynasty](27334130.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Assad and his family fled to [Moscow](19004.md) and were granted asylum in Russia.[^cite_note-Syrian_rebels_topple_Assad-15][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The fall of Assad has been said to mark the end of the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).[^cite_note-end_of_war-26]

Syrian prime minister [Mohammad Ghazi al-Jalali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Ghazi_al-Jalali) recognized the transfer of power to the [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government), which established [a caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government) in Damascus with [Mohammed al-Bashir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_al-Bashir) serving as the prime minister.[^cite_note-27] Ahmed al-Sharaa, the leader of the Syrian Salvation Government and [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) of Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, became *de facto* leader of Syria.[^cite_note-28]

On 8 December 2024, the day that the Assad regime fell, Israel [invaded southern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Syria), subsuming the [Golan Heights](60364960.md) [buffer zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Disengagement_Observer_Force) and capturing [Quneitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quneitra), [Mount Hermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hermon), and surrounding towns and villages, while also carrying out a bombing campaign against Syrian military bases. Israel has since maintained a military occupation of the buffer zone.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] The Turkish-backed SNA launched [an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij_offensive_\(2024\)) against the SDF, which ended with the capture of [Manbij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij) on 11 December.

At the [Syrian Revolution Victory conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Revolution_Victory_Conference) held in Damascus on 29 January 2025, the new government appointed al-Sharaa as president of Syria during the transitional phase and announced the dissolution of several armed militias and their integration into the [Syrian Army](3423927.md) under the [Ministry of Defense](2774942.md).

## North and East Syria

### SDF–SNA clashes

Main articles: [Operation Dawn of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dawn_of_Freedom) and [Turkish–Syrian National Army offensive in Northern Syria (2024–2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish%E2%80%93Syrian_National_Army_offensive_in_Northern_Syria_\(2024%E2%80%932025\))

Clashes broke out between SDF and SNA forces as SDF forces began to enter government-controlled towns in northern Aleppo, which government forces were retreating from due to the HTS-led offensive on [Aleppo](159244.md) from [Idlib](2528679.md).[^cite_note-ANFnovember2024-31] On 1 December 2024, SNA captured the towns of [as-Safirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Safirah), [Khanasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanasir) and the [Kuweires airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuweires_Military_Aviation_Institute), while clashes occurred between SNA and SDF in the Sheikh Najjar district of Aleppo city.[^cite_note-SOHR1DecCapture-32]

Main articles: [Manbij offensive (2024)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij_offensive_\(2024\)) and [2024 Kobani clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kobani_clashes)

On 6 December 2024, the SNA launched an offensive targeting the SDF-controlled city of [Manbij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij).[^cite_note-33] As the last SDF-controlled area west of the Euphrates, Manbij represented a crucial strategic point for Turkey's goal of pushing the SDF eastward beyond the river to enable the SNA to advance toward [Kobani](44162675.md).[^cite_note-fdd20241210-34] According to the SOHR and the pro-SDF thinktank [Foundation for Defense of Democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_for_Defense_of_Democracies) (FDD), Turkey conducted drone strikes on SDF positions in Manbij.[^cite_note-fdd20241210-34] On 9 December 2024, the SOHR reported, the SDF withdrew from most of Manbij after intense fighting with the SNA and Turkish airstrikes on Qarqozaq bridge.[^cite_note-35] On 11 December, [Mazloum Abdi](59386296.md), commander-in-chief of the SDF, announced that SDF soldiers "will be withdrawn from the area as soon as possible" following a US-brokered ceasefire agreement.[^cite_note-Barron's-36] On 12 December 2024, a truce mediated by the [United States](3434750.md) was announced, though it was ended days later.[^cite_note-37] On 17 December 2024, the truce was extended by a week.[^cite_note-38] Turkish Armed Forces launched airstrikes [in the vicinity of Kobani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kobani_clashes) later that month.[^cite_note-SOHR_TR_attack_Tal_Mishtanour2-39]

Main article: [East Aleppo offensive (2024–2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Aleppo_offensive_\(2024%E2%80%932025\))

On 23 December 2024, the SDF's [Manbij Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij_Military_Council) (MMC) [launched a counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Aleppo_offensive_\(2024%E2%80%932025\)) in the eastern countryside of Aleppo to regain control of positions around the [Tishrin Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tishrin_Dam) and to gain further territory along the [Euphrates River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_River).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Fighting was halted following the signing of the [10 March agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#10_March_agreement), between the SDF and the Syrian transitional government.

### 10 March agreement

Main article: [Syrian peace process § 10 March agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#10_March_agreement)

On 10 March 2025, the SDF signed the [10 March agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#10_March_agreement) with the [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government), agreeing to integrate into Syria's state institutions, securing [minority rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_rights), establishing a [ceasefire](592591.md) throughout Syria, and retaining limited authority over North and East Syria.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-\:132-43]

### SDF–Syrian transitional government clashes

Main article: [SDF–Syrian transitional government clashes (2025–2026)](81146732.md)

Delays in implementing the 10 March agreement, with both sides accusing each other of obstruction, along with the cancellation of the SDF–STG talks in Paris on 25 July and again on 9–10 August, heightened hostilities between the SDF and the Syrian transitonal government, which ultimately led to clashes between the two.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-Hawar_negotiations_stalled-46][^cite_note-\:16-47]

The first clashes were reported on 2 August 2025, near [Dayr Hafir](35490900.md) and [al-Khafsah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khafsah).[^cite_note-\:1-48][^cite_note-\:2-49] On 12 August, one Syrian Army soldier was killed in clashes with the SDF near [Dayr Hafir](35490900.md).[^cite_note-\:4-50][^cite_note-1STGKilled12August-51][^cite_note-\:8-52] In addition the government's [Ministry of Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_\(Syria\)) claimed that two civilians were killed and three injured.[^cite_note-53] Coinciding with the clashes the [Foundation for Defense of Democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_for_Defense_of_Democracies) (FDD) warned of a 'wider conflict' if the [US](3434750.md) does not remain fully engaged in preventing the collapse of the March agreement.[^cite_note-\:1-48]

#### Aleppo clashes

Main article: [Aleppo clashes (2025–2026)](82109129.md)

On 6 October 2025, clashes erupted between forces affiliated with the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) and government troops in the neighborhoods of Ashrafiyah and [Sheikh Maqsoud](42696554.md). Following the confrontation, [government forces](27479.md) closed all roads leading into the two neighborhoods, effectively restricting movement and access.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Residents protested the closures, demanding freedom of movement, and some demonstrations were met with [tear gas](1202646.md) and [live fire](3366570.md) by security forces.[^cite_note-56] Heavy exchanges of fire, including small arms and medium weapons, were reported in the neighborhoods, resulting in casualties on both sides and displacing some families.[^cite_note-57] Kurdish authorities accused the government-aligned forces of attempting to infiltrate the neighborhoods and targeting civilians.[^cite_note-58] The [Syrian Ministry of Defense](2774942.md) stated that army movements in northern and [northeastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria) respond 'to repeated SDF attacks on civilians and security forces'.[^cite_note-59] Calm returned to the Kurdish-majority neighborhoods of Sheikh Maqsoud and Ashrafiyah after a preliminary agreement between Syrian government forces and the Syrian Democratic Forces.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

#### Offensive on SDF Territory

Main article: [2026 northeastern Syria offensive](82106178.md)

### Anti-ISIS operations

Main article: [Eastern Syria insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Syria_insurgency)

Operations against ISIS sleeper cells continued after the fall of the Assad regime. In eastern Syria in particular, the SDF, in coordination with [the Global Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Global_Coalition), carried out 79 security operations, resulting in the arrest of 203 ISIS members, including senior figures, in 2025. These operations also led to the killing of more than 14 ISIS members, including commanders.[^cite_note-62]

Main articles: [2025 Palmyra ambush](81852348.md) and [2025–2026 United States airstrikes in Syria](81885282.md)

On 13 December 2025, two U.S. soldiers and an U.S. civilian interpreter were killed by an alleged Islamic State gunman in the city of [Palmyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmyra_\(modern\)), central Syria. The gunman was shot dead by security forces during the attack.[^cite_note-63] According to the Syrian Interior Ministry, the attacker was a new recruit in Syria's internal security forces who was suspected of Islamic State ties.[^cite_note-64]
