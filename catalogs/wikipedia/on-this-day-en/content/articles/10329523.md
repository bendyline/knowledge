# Stabilization fund

A **stabilization fund** is a mechanism set up by a government or [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) to insulate the domestic economy from large influxes of revenue, as from commodities such as oil. A primary motivation is maintaining a steady level of government revenue in the face of major commodity price fluctuations (hence the term "stabilization"), as well as the avoidance of [inflation](38286.md) and associated atrophy of other domestic sectors ([Dutch disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_disease)). This generally involves the purchase of foreign denominated debt, especially if the goal is to prevent [overheating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overheating_\(economics\)) in the domestic economy. The notion may overlap with [sovereign wealth fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_wealth_fund).

Examples of such funds include:

- [Stabilization Fund of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilization_Fund_of_the_Russian_Federation)

- [Petroleum Fund of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_Fund_of_Norway) (SPF)

- [Chile](5489.md)'s [Copper Stabilization Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Stabilization_Fund) (CSF)

- [Oman](22316.md)'s [State General Reserve Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_General_Reserve_Fund) (SGRF)

- [Kuwait](7515890.md)'s [Reserve Fund for Future Generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Fund_for_Future_Generations) (RFFG)

- [Papua New Guinea](22943.md)'s Mineral Resources Stabilization Fund (MRSF)

- [Venezuela](32374.md)'s Macroeconomic Stabilization Fund (MSF)

- [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) [Abu Dhabi Fund for Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_Fund_for_Development)

- [Central Bank of Iran](3961876.md)'s Oil Stabilization Fund

Stabilization funds do not necessarily have to revolve around large commodity revenue. Such national funds might instead seek to influence currency [exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate) without affecting domestic money supply. Such examples include:

- [European Financial Stability Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Financial_Stability_Facility)

- United Kingdom's [Exchange Equalisation Account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_Equalisation_Account)

- United States's [Exchange Stabilization Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_Stabilization_Fund)
