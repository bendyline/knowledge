**Dar es Salaam** (English: [/ˌdɑːr.ɛs.səˈlɑːm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), Swahili: [\[ɗɑr‿ɛs‿sɑˈlɑːm\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swahili) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/3d/Sw-ke-Dar_es_Salaam.flac/Sw-ke-Dar_es_Salaam.flac.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/3d/Sw-ke-Dar_es_Salaam.flac/Sw-ke-Dar_es_Salaam.flac.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): دَار السَّلَام, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Dār as-Salām*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Abode of Peace') is the largest city and financial hub of [Tanzania](30118.md) and capital of the [Dar es Salaam Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam_Region). It is located on the [Swahili coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_coast). With a population of over 7 million people, Dar es Salaam is the largest city in [East Africa](260987.md) by [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_\(human_biology\)) and the [fifth-largest in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Africa_by_population). Dar es Salaam remains an important economic centre and one of the fastest-growing cities in the world.[^cite_note-The_Guardian_-_Where_is_the_fastest_growing_city_in_the_world?-3] Experts predict that the city's population will grow to over 10 million before 2030.[^cite_note-4]

The city was founded in the mid-19th century and served as the main administrative and commercial centre of [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa), [Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_\(territory\)) and Tanzania. The decision was made in 1974 to move the capital to [Dodoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodoma) which was officially completed in 1996.[^cite_note-5]

Dar es Salaam is Tanzania's most prominent city for arts, fashion, media, film, television and finance. It is the capital of the co-extensive Dar es Salaam Region, one of Tanzania's [31 administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Tanzania) and consists of five [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Tanzania): [Kinondoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinondoni_District) in the north; [Ilala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilala_District) in the centre; [Ubungo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubungo) and [Temeke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temeke) in the south; and [Kigamboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigamboni) in the east across the [Kurasini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurasini) estuary.

## History

Further information: [History of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Zanzibar)



Image of the port of Dar es Salaam from the book *Von Unseren Kolonien* by Ottomar Beta in the year 1908

In the 19th century, 'Mzizima ([Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language) for "healthy town") was a coastal fishing village on the periphery of [Indian Ocean trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade).[^cite_note-UNHSP-6][^cite_note-Brennan-7] In 1865 or 1866, [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sultans_of_Zanzibar) [Majid bin Said of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majid_bin_Said_of_Zanzibar) began building a new city very close to Mzizima[^cite_note-Brennan-7] and named it Dar es Salaam. The name is commonly translated from Arabic as "abode (home) of peace", from *dar* ("house"), and *es salaam* ("of peace").[^cite_note-Brennan-7] Dar es Salaam fell into decline after Majid's death in 1870, but was revived in 1887 when the [German East Africa Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa_Company) established a station there. The town's growth was facilitated by its role as the administrative and commercial centre of [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa) and industrial expansion following the construction of the [Central Railway Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Line_\(Tanzania\)) in the early 1900s.[^cite_note-8]

In the [East African campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_campaign_\(World_War_I\)) of [World War I](4764461.md), [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) and [Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) forces captured [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa). The [Royal Navy](26061.md) bombarded the city with the [monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_\(warship\)) [*Mersey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Mersey_\(1914\)) on 21 July 1916 and [battleship](4054.md) [HMS *Vengeance*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Vengeance_\(1899\)) on 21 August.[^cite_note-9] The German colonial authorities surrendered the city on 3 September.[^cite_note-10] German East Africa became the British [Tanganyika Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_Territory).

Dar es Salaam remained the administrative and commercial centre. Under British [indirect rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_rule), European areas such as [Oyster Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_Bay,_Tanzania) and African areas (e.g., [Kariakoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariakoo) and [Ilala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilala_District)) developed separately from the city centre. The city's population also included a large number of [workers from British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_Tanzania), many of whom came to take advantage of trade and commercial opportunities. After World War II, Dar es Salaam experienced a period of rapid growth.[^cite_note-\:3-11]

Political developments, including the formation and growth of the [Tanganyika African National Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_African_National_Union), led to Tanganyika's independence from colonial rule in December 1961. Dar es Salaam continued to serve as its capital, even when Tanganyika and the [People's Republic of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Zanzibar) merged to form Tanzania in 1964. In 1973, provisions were made to relocate the capital to [Dodoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodoma), a more centrally located city in the interior. The relocation process to Dodoma was completed, although Dar es Salaam continued to be the location of most government offices.[^cite_note-\:3-11]

&#x20;\


&#x20;In 1967, the [Tanzanian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Tanzania) declared the *[ujamaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujamaa)* policy, which made Tanzania lean towards [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism). The move hampered the potential growth of the city as the government encouraged people not to move into cities and instead remain in Ujamaa socialist villages. By the 1980s, the policy failed to combat the increasing poverty and hunger that Tanzania faced, and had delayed necessary development. This situation led to the liberalisation policy of the 1980s that essentially ended socialism and silenced its proponents within Tanzania's government through political repression.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Ngowi-13]



Dar es Salaam in the 1930s, with the [Old Boma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Boma,_Dar_es_Salaam) and [St. Joseph's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph's_Cathedral,_Dar_es_Salaam) prominently in view

Until the late 1990s, Dar es Salaam was not regarded in the same echelon as Africa's leading cities like [Cairo](6293.md), [Nairobi](21482.md), [Johannesburg](58893.md), [Lagos](85232.md), or [Addis Ababa](52593.md). During the 2000s, businesses opened and prospered; growth expanded in the construction sector, with new multi-storey buildings, bridges and roads;[^cite_note-allafrica.com-14] [Tanzanian banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_banks_in_Tanzania) headquartered in the city became better regulated; and the [Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam_Stock_Exchange) expanded. The port is prominent for [entrepot trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-exportation) with landlocked countries like [Rwanda](25645.md), [Burundi](21490998.md), [Zambia](34415.md), and the eastern portion of the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md). The city's skyline features tall buildings, among them the 35-storey PSPF Tower (finished in 2015) and the [Tanzania Ports Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Ports_Authority) (TPA) Tower, the tallest in the country (completed in 2016).[^cite_note-15]

A number of historical buildings and elements of urban planning, such as parts of the harbour and streets going back to colonial times, still exist. The [Old Boma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Boma,_Dar_es_Salaam), one of the city's oldest buildings, was built in 1866–67 by [Majid bin Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majid_bin_Said_of_Zanzibar), [sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) of [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar), and enlarged under German rule. The Botanical Gardens now are close to the [National Museum of Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Tanzania). The present-day [State House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikulu) goes back to Majid bin Said, and were the seat of the German and later the British colonial governments. Along with the [Azania Front Lutheran Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azania_Front_Lutheran_Church), built between 1899 and 1902,[^cite_note-16] and the [Roman Catholic](606848.md) [St. Joseph's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph's_Cathedral,_Dar_es_Salaam), constructed around the same period,[^cite_note-17] [Ocean Road Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Road_Hospital) are early historical buildings in Dar es Salaam.[^cite_note-18]

## Geography

Dar es Salaam is located at 6°48' S, 39°17' E (−6.8000, 39.2833),[^cite_note-19] on a natural harbour on the coast of East Africa, with sandy beaches in some areas.

#### Kinondoni

Kinondoni is the most populated of the districts. It houses half of the city's population and several high-income suburbs.



Tanzanite Bridge

- Masaki, Oyster Bay and [Ada Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Estate) are the high-income suburbs located along the central beach. During the Colonial Era, they were the major European suburbs of the city. Diplomats and expatriates currently reside in these areas. Oyster Bay Beach (also known as Coco Beach) is the only white sandy beach east of Kinondoni.

- Mikocheni and Regent Estate are also suburbs within the district. These are high and middle-income areas with Mikocheni B enjoying a higher population density than Mikocheni A and Regent Estate. According to the 2012 census, the Mikocheni ward had a population of 32,947.

- [Msasani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msasani) is a peninsula to the northeast of the city centre and home to expatriates from the United Kingdom and other western countries. It contains a mixture of traditional shops and western-oriented resorts and stores including the redevelopment of the former Msani Slipway shipyard by architect [Antoni Folkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Folkers).

- Mbezi Beach is the beachfront suburb located along the northern Dar es Salaam Beach. It contains several tourist hotels, residences and a kite-surfing area by Upepo Avenue.

- Sinza, Kijitonyama, Magomeni, [Kinondoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinondoni) and [Mwenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwenge,_Dar_es_Salaam) are more ethnically mixed than the areas above and are located west of Dar es Salaam's Central Business District.

#### Ilala

The administrative district of Ilala contains almost all government offices, ministries, and the Central Business District. It is the transportation hub of the city, as the [Julius Nyerere International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Nyerere_International_Airport), Central Railway Station and Tazara Railway Station are all within the district's boundaries. The residential areas are mainly middle- to high-income, among them:



The [Askari Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askari_Monument) along Samora Avenue marks the exact centre of Dar es Salaam, in the [Ilala district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilala_district).

- Upanga and [Kisutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisutu) have the highest concentration of Asian communities within Dar es Salaam, with residents of Indian and Arabic descent. These areas contain colonial houses and mansions built in Indian, Arabic and European styles. Upanga is divided into [Upanga East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanga_East) and [Upanga West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanga_West).

- [Kariakoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariakoo) is the shopping district of the city: shops, bazaars, and merchants sell products from foodstuffs to hardware. The Kariakoo Market contains the only underground section of the city. It is the major supply point of the food consumed by all Dar es Salaam residents.

- Tabata, Segerea and Ukonga are located slightly farther west from the city centre.

- Ilala, among the middle-income suburbs very near to the city centre, is marked by the [Askari Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askari_Monument) and suffers from gang activity.

Temeke is the main industrial district of the city, where manufacturing (both heavy and light industry) is located. To the east is the Port of Dar es Salaam, the largest port in the country. Temeke is believed to have the largest concentration of low-income residents due to industry. It is home to military and police officers as well as port officials.

- Kurasini, located on the harbour, contains Dar es Salaam Port, the Police College, the Mgulani Police Barracks and the [Dar es Salaam International Trade Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam_International_Trade_Fair) grounds. The main residents are police officers and port officials.



[Kigamboni Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigamboni_Bridge) at night

- Chang'ombe is one of the few higher-income areas in Temeke. It has maintained this status due to the presence of African high colonial officers and some industry owners from the Colonial Era. Chang'ombe houses the Dar es Salaam University College of Education, the National Stadium and Uhuru Stadium.

- Temeke, Mtoni, Tandika, Kijichi, and Mbagala are middle to low-income suburbs, of which the last is the largest suburb in the entire district.

#### Ubungo

The Ubungo terminal serves as a transportation link to most large Dar es Salaam urban nodes. The [narrow-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge) commuter rail runs from there to the city centre, with ten [level crossings](193595.md) along the route.

This district is characterised with a lot of potential social and economic centres such as industries i.e. Urafiki textile industry, bus station and various institutes and universities such as National Institute of Transport(NIT)

#### Kigamboni

[Kigamboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigamboni) (also known as South Beach), a beachfront suburb on a peninsula, is home to an economically diverse population. Access to the suburb is mainly by ferry, although the [Kigamboni Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigamboni_Bridge) provides an alternative.

### Climate

Dar es Salaam experiences tropical climatic conditions, typified by hot and humid weather throughout much of the year due to its proximity to the equator and the warm Indian Ocean. It has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Aw*/*As*). Annual rainfall is approximately 1,150 millimetres or 45 inches, and in a normal year there are two rainy seasons: the "long rains" in April and May, and the "short rains" in November and December.

#### Climate change

A 2019 paper published in [PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) estimated that under [Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Concentration_Pathway#4.5), a "moderate" scenario of [climate change](5042951.md) where global warming reaches \~2.5–3 °C (4.5–5.4 °F) by 2100, the climate of Dar es Salaam in the year 2050 would most closely resemble the current climate of [Barquisimeto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquisimeto) in [Venezuela](32374.md). The annual temperature and temperatures of the warmest month would increase by 1.3 °C (2.3 °F), while the temperature of the coldest month would go down by 0.1 °C (0.18 °F).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] According to [Climate Action Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Tracker), the current warming trajectory appears consistent with 2.7 °C (4.9 °F), which closely matches RCP 4.5.[^cite_note-33]

Moreover, according to the 2022 [IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report), Dar es Salaam is one of 12 major African cities ([Abidjan](21371767.md), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Algiers](1644.md), [Cape Town](6653.md), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Dakar](44251.md), Dar es Salaam, [Durban](313650.md), [Lagos](85232.md), [Lomé](57067.md), [Luanda](18402.md) and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo)) which would be the most severely affected by the future [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). It estimates that they would collectively sustain cumulative damages of US\$65 billion under RCP 4.5 and US\$86.5 billion for the high-emission scenario RCP 8.5 by the year 2050. Additionally, RCP 8.5 combined with the hypothetical impact from [marine ice sheet instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability)[^cite_note-34] at high levels of warming would involve up to US\$137.5 billion in damages, while the additional accounting for the "low-probability, high-damage events" may increase aggregate risks to US\$187 billion for the "moderate" RCP4.5, US\$206 billion for RCP8.5 and US\$397 billion under the high-end ice sheet instability scenario.[^cite_note-35] Since sea level rise would continue for about 10,000 years under every scenario of climate change, future costs of sea level rise would only increase, especially without adaptation measures.[^cite_note-36]

## Demographics



Urban area

Dar es Salaam is the most populous city in Tanzania and the fifth most populous in Africa.[^cite_note-natgeo2019-39] In 2020, the population was estimated to be 8 million.[^cite_note-brinkhoff2020-40] Muslims make up 70% of the population in Dar es Salaam.[^cite_note-\:2-41]

When the 2012 national census was taken, the city had a population of 4,364,541, about ten percent of the country's total. The average private household size was 3.9 persons compared to the national average of 4.7. Less than half of the city's residents were married, with a rate lower than any other region in the country. The literacy rate in the city was 96%, while the national average was 78%. Between the 2002 and 2012 censuses, the city's 5.6% average annual growth rate was the highest in the country.[^cite_note-42]

More than three-quarters of the city's population live in [informal settlements](63298152.md).[^cite_note-natgeo2019-39] In 2018, Dar es Salaam scored 0.699 (medium category) on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI). The city's HDI has increased every year since 1992, and it ranked higher than any other region in the country except for one.[^cite_note-globaldata2018-43]

Dar es Salaam is the second-fastest-growing city in the world and could have a population as high as 15.9 million by 2030.[^cite_note-natgeo2019-39] The population was estimated at 20,000 in 1900, 93,000 in 1957 and 273,000 in 1967.[^cite_note-44]

## Economy and infrastructure



The [Bank of Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Tanzania)



The high rise buildings of Dar es Salaam

Dar es Salaam is Tanzania's most important city for both business and government. The city contains high concentrations of trade and other services and manufacturing compared to other parts of the country, which has about 65 percent of its population in rural areas.[^cite_note-46] Downtown includes small businesses, many of which are run by traders and proprietors whose families originated in the Middle East and the Indian subcontinent—areas of the world with which the settlements of the Tanzanian coast have had long-standing trading relations.



Street view of [Kariakoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariakoo) ward

The Dar es Salaam Central Business District is the largest in Tanzania and comprises the [Kisutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisutu), [Kivukoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kivukoni), [Upanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanga_East) and [Kariakoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariakoo) areas.[^cite_note-47] The downtown area is located in the [Ilala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilala_District) district. Kivukoni is home to the Tanzania Central Bank, The [Bank of Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Tanzania), the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange and the city's important Magogoni fish market.[^cite_note-allafrica.com-14] With businesses and offices, Kisutu is the location of Dar es Salaam central railway station, the PSPF Towers, and the TPA Tower.

Dar es Salaam is undergoing major construction and development. The 35-storey [PSPF Twin Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSPF_Twin_Towers?action=edit\&redlink=1) are the second tallest building in the city and the country.[^cite_note-48] The city has major infrastructural challenges, including an outdated transport system and occasional power rationing.



The Tanzania Ports Authority headquarters

### Financial services

The [Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam_Stock_Exchange) (DSE) is the country's first stock market. The headquarters of the [Natural Gas Revenue Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Gas_Revenue_Fund) is also here.

### Retail

Dar es Salaam hosts the [Mlimani City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlimani_City) shopping mall, the City Mall in the Kisutu area, Quality Center Mall, GSM Pugu Shopping Mall, GSM Msasani Mall, and Dar Free Market Mall.

On a natural harbour on the Indian Ocean, Dar es Salaam is one of the hubs of the [Tanzanian transportation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Tanzania), as the main railways and several highways originate in or near the city to provide convenient transportation for commuters.

### Local public transport

Public minibus share taxis (*[dala dala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dala_dala)*) are the most common form of transport in Dar es Salaam and are often found at the major bus terminals of [Makumbusho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makumbusho), Ubungo and other areas of the city. However, since the introduction of the motorcycle transit business known as "bodaboda", most people prefer it,[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] allowing them to get into the city faster as compared with the minibuses, which encounter heavy traffic. Other types of transport include motorcycles and [bajaj](593765.md) (auto rickshaws).

#### Bus

The government has been introducing a metro bus system, [Dar es Salaam bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam_bus_rapid_transit) (*mwendo kasi* in Kiswahili). The metro buses are managed by UDA-RT, a partnership between Usafiri Dar es Salaam (UDA) and the government.

The bus rapid-transit system Phase 1 has been completed by UDA-RT and began operation on 10 May 2016.[^cite_note-BRT_project-51] The first section runs between Kimara in the northwest to Kivukoni on the northern headland of the harbour.[^cite_note-52] Phase 1 was funded by the [World Bank](36752.md), [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank) and the Tanzanian government.[^cite_note-53]

#### Metro

Dar es Salaam will have a [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system, currently undergoing a feasibility study conducted by [Mota-Engil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mota-Engil) and [Dar Rapid Transit Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_Rapid_Transit_Agency).

### Maritime transport

#### Port

The [Port of Dar es Salaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Dar_es_Salaam) is Tanzania's busiest, handling 90% of the country's cargo. It is located in the Kurasini administrative ward of Temeke District southeast of the city's central business district. Due to a huge influx of cargo and the slow pace of expansion, a new cargo port 60 km (37 mi) northwest of Dar es Salaam is proposed at [Bagamoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagamoyo_Port).[^cite_note-54]

#### Ferry

MV *Kigamboni* ferries run between southeast of Kivukoni and northwest of [Kigamboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigamboni) in Dar es Salaam.[^cite_note-55]

## Culture

### Art

The [Tingatinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingatinga_\(painting\)) painting style originates from Dar es Salaam. The Nyumba ya sanaa ("House of Art") is a cultural centre, workshop and retail outlet dedicated to Tanzanian art, showcasing and promoting Tanzanian craftsmanship. Prominent Tanzanian sculptor [George Lilanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lilanga) has donated some of his works to the centre, including decorations of the building's main entrance.

### Music



A traditional African dance in Dar es Salaam

The music scene in Dar es Salaam is divided among several styles. The longest-standing style is live dance music ([muziki wa dansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muziki_wa_dansi)) played by bands such as [DDC Mlimani Park Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDC_Mlimani_Park_Orchestra) and Malaika Musical Band. [Taarab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taarab), which was traditionally popular in Zanzibar has also found a niche. However, it remains small compared both to dance music and "[Bongo Flava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzanian_hip_hop)", a broad category representing the Tanzanian take on hip hop and rhythm and blues that has quickly become the most popular locally produced music. The rap music scene is also present.[^cite_note-56] Traditional music, which locally refers to tribal music, is still performed, but typically only on family-oriented occasions such as weddings.

In the 1970s, the Ministry of National Youth Culture aimed to create a national culture stressing the importance of music. Dar es Salaam became the music centre in Tanzania, with the local radio showcasing new bands and dominating the music and cultural scene. With this *ujamaa* (family) mentality governing culture and music, a unified people's culture was created, leading to the rise of hip hop culture.[^cite_note-Lemelle-57] Throughout the years, the radio in Dar es Salaam has played a major role in the dissemination of music, because many people do not have television; cassettes are more common than CDs.

### Tourism



National Museum of Tanzania



Beach on the peninsula of Kigamboni, Dar es Salaam

Dar es Salaam has two of the five museums that make up the [National Museum of Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Tanzania) consortium, namely the National Museum proper and the Makumbusho Cultural Centre & Village Museum. The National Museum is dedicated to the history of Tanzania; most notably, it exhibits some of the bones of *[Paranthropus boisei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranthropus_boisei)* that were among the findings of [Louis Leakey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Leakey) at [Olduvai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olduvai_Gorge). In 2016, there was a breakthrough discovery in Northern Tanzania by a scientist, from the University of Dar es Salaam, of footprints thought to be of a hominid that predates *Homo sapiens*. The Makumbusho Cultural Centre & Village Museum,[^cite_note-58] located in the outskirts of the city on the road to Bagamoyo, showcases traditional huts from 16 different Tanzanian ethnic groups. There are also examples of traditional cultivation, as well as daily traditional music and dance shows. Close to the National Museum are also the [botanical gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden), with tropical plants and trees.

There are beaches on the [Msasani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msasani) peninsula north of Dar es Salaam and in Kigamboni to the south. [Bongoyo Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongoyo_Island) can be reached by boat from the Msasani Slipway.

## Sports

### Stadium

Dar es Salaam is the sports centre of Tanzania and hosts the second-largest stadium in East and Central Africa, the [National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Mkapa_National_Stadium), which can accommodate up to 60,000 people.[^cite_note-61]



Aerial view of the Tanzania National Main Stadium, with the Kurasini estuary in the background

The Tanzanian National Stadium hosts football clubs based in Dar es Salaam: [Young Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Africans_S.C.) and [Simba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simba_S.C.). It also hosts other Tanzanian football clubs and international matches. A new stadium in Dodoma with a much larger capacity has been proposed by the government as a donation from Morocco.

Apart from the National Stadium, the city is home to two other stadiums: the [Uhuru Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Stadium), the Karume Memorial Stadium and [Chamazi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamazi_Stadium). The Uhuru Stadium is used mainly for local tournaments and political gatherings, whilst the Karume Memorial Stadium is situated west of [Kurasini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurasini) and home to the [Tanzania Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Football_Federation). ### Boxing

Boxing is a popular sport in Tanzania and Dar es Salaam hosts numerous boxing galas organised throughout the year. Tanzanian professional boxer [Francis Checka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Cheka) is the current World Boxing Federation (WBF) Super Middleweight Champion.

## Media

### Newspapers



The head office of Tanzania Telecommunications Company Ltd at Extelecom Building in Samora Avenue, east of [Kisutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisutu)

Newspapers in Dar es Salaam are often sold by vendors weaving through stationary traffic at road intersections. English-language newspapers, with online versions, include *The Citizen* and *The Guardian*. Swahili dailies *Tanzania Daima* and *Mwananchi* are also available. *Business Times* is the only financial and economic newspaper in the city; it was established in 1988 and became the first private newspaper in Tanzania. *Business Times* owns *Majira*, another Swahili newspaper.

### Television stations

Dar es Salaam is home to ITV,[^cite_note-64] Sibuka,[^cite_note-65] Channel Ten Television Station (formerly Dar es Salaam Television \[DTV]) and Azam TV, a subscription-based service from the Azam group of companies.

Television station Ayo TV[^cite_note-66] is based in Ubungo, Dar es Salaam, as is the [Tanzania Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Broadcasting_Corporation).

### Internet access



The ship-like building of [Airtel Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airtel_Tanzania) headquarters in Dar es Salaam

Installation of the [trans-Indian Ocean backbone cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEACOM_\(African_cable_system\)) (SEACOM) in 2009 has, in theory, made Internet access much more readily available in Dar es Salaam in particular and in East Africa in general. However, roll-out to end-users is currently slow. Telephone-line coverage provided by the [Tanzania Telecommunications Company Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Telecommunications_Company_Limited) is limited,[^cite_note-67] prices are high, and long contracts are required for purchase of bandwidth for small Internet service providers. The expressed aim of the SEACOM cable is to enable East Africa to develop economically through increased online trading.

[Internet cafés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_caf%C3%A9) are found in the city centre, and free Wi-Fi hotspots are available in various government and nongovernment institutions as well as public transport.

Mobile-telephone access to the Internet via [4G](486547.md) is still relatively expensive, though [5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G) is making its way through major cities and towns as of 2022 with plans to go nationwide in the advanced stages.

### Radio

Dar es Salaam's first radio station began operation in the early 1950s with "little more equipment than a microphone and a blanket hung over a wall..." This project was overseen by [Edward Twining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Twining).[^cite_note-Gunther-68]

## Environment

Since the 1990s,[^cite_note-69] Dar es Salaam has experienced heavy and frequent flooding due to intense rainfall.[^cite_note-\:0-70][^cite_note-71] The city is especially vulnerable to flooding, due to its lowland [coastal](5236.md) orientation and the fact that the [Msimbazi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msimbazi_River) flows through the city. The situation has worsened over the years, both due to [climate change](5042951.md) and the expansion of city pavement, which increases [surface runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff).[^cite_note-72]

In 2019, flooding displaced 1,215 households. Between 2017 and 2018, the city experienced seven floods.[^cite_note-\:0-70] The [World Bank](36752.md) estimates that exposure to floods has impacted about 2 million people, or 25% of the population in Dar es Salaam.[^cite_note-\:0-70] Flooding incidents destroy bridges and roads, disrupt transportation, increase risk of diseases such as [cholera](7591.md) and skin infection, and are a barrier to reducing [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty).[^cite_note-\:0-70]

## Education

Dar es Salaam has the highest literacy rate in the country, with 93.9% of the residents being literate,[^cite_note-73] compared to the national average of 77.9%.[^cite_note-\:1-74] The city's [GER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_enrolment_ratio) lacks in comparison to the rest of the country with 96.1%, which is however still sufficient. On the other hand, its secondary [GER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_enrolment_ratio) is one of the highest in the country (61.8%).[^cite_note-\:1-74]
