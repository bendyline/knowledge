# Mali–Senegal border



Map of the Mali-Senegal border

The **Mali–Senegal border** is 489 km (or 304 mi) in length and runs from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Mauritania](18778516.md) in the north to the tripoint with [Guinea](12176.md) in the south.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border begins in the north at the tripoint with Mauritania at the confluence of the [Senegal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_River) and [Falémé River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fal%C3%A9m%C3%A9_River). It then follows the latter for some distance southwards, before proceeding overland for a stretch, before rejoining the Falémé, which it then follows down to the tripoint with Guinea.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2]

## History

[France](5843419.md) had begun settling on the coast of modern [Senegal](27278.md) in the 17th century, gradually extending their rule further inland during the mid-1800s onward.[^cite_note-IBS151-3][^cite_note-4] The areas east of the Falémé river (i.e. roughly modern Mali, [Burkina Faso](3470.md) and [Niger](21373.md)) were originally under Senegalese administration as [Upper Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Senegal), but were split off as [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan) in 1893.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] Both Senegal and French Sudan later became constituent of the federal colony of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa) (*Afrique occidentale française*, abbreviated AOF).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-IBS151-3] The boundary between French Sudan and Senegal was drawn up on 16 June 1895.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2][^cite_note-IBS151-3]

As the movement for decolonisation grew in the post-[Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) era, France gradually granted more political rights and representation for their sub-Saharan African colonies, culminating in the granting of broad internal autonomy to French West Africa in 1958 within the framework of the [French Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Community).[^cite_note-6] Eventually, in 1960, both Senegal and [Mali](19127.md) were granted full independence, originally as the short-lived [Mali Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Federation).
