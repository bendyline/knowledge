The **national federation of civil protection** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *fédération nationale de protection civile*), or the **FNPC**, is a French humanitarian and first aid [voluntary association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_association).

Its logo, a blue triangle on white and orange ground, comes from the international distinctive sign of civil defence,[^cite_note-PA1-A66-1] which is a [protective sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protective_sign) under the protection of the four [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions) of 1949.

This [voluntary association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_association) is not to be confused with the French [Sécurité Civile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_Civile), which is a government agency, and is only one of the many French civil protection associations.

## History

### From Civil Defense to Civil Protection

At the end of [World War I](4764461.md), the high number of civilian casualties highlighted the need to upgrade the protection of people during conflicts.

In 1933, the first [voluntary organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_organization) to protect civilians during war time was founded with the support of [Gaston Doumergue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Doumergue) (UNDA : UNION NATIONALE POUR LA DEFENSE AERIENNE ET POUR LA PROTECTION DES POPULATIONS CIVILES). In 1938, at the dawn of [World War II](32927.md), the French government developed [civil defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_defense) (*défense passive*) policies.[^cite_note-2] Their goal was to build [blast shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_shelter) and warning networks to keep people safe from [airstrikes](323167.md), the UNDA become COMITE NATIONAL DE DEFENSE AERIENNE ET DE SAUVETAGE PUBLIC.

After the war, the *défense passive* missions became more and more related to non military threats like [natural disasters](58921.md). The 27 of January 1950, the UNPC (UNION NATIONALE DE PROTECTION CIVILE), is born.

In 1951, a national service for civil protection in the [ministry of the Interior](871703.md) was established. This government agency then became the French [Sécurité Civile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_Civile) in 1975.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

However, this nationalization did not hamper volunteering which developed through local civil protection societies from 1958.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

### Local societies and the national federation

In 1965, there were local civil protection societies in 26 [departments](38523.md). Following a call from Prime minister [Georges Pompidou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Pompidou), the national federation of civil protection (*Fédération nationale de protection civile*) was constituted on December 14, 1965, in Paris, in order to manage these local societies and to raise popular awareness on [civil defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_defense).[^cite_note-50-ans-au-service-des-citoyens-4] The first federal president was [Léon Robine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Robine?action=edit\&redlink=1).

In 1968, there were local societies in 54 [departments](38523.md) and 31,701 volunteers. One year later, a decree entitled the French Civil Protection as an association acting in the public interest.[^cite_note-\:1-5]

During the 1980s, the Civil Protection became the society which delivered the second highest number of [first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid) trainings in France. Its volunteers also led awareness campaigns on several topics such as [first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid), domestic accidents, [HIV](14170.md) and road safety.[^cite_note-50-ans-au-service-des-citoyens-4]

In the early 1990s, the national federation of civil protection was unifying 94 local civil protection associations, but also other societies like the [French White Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_White_Cross?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [RATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RATP_Group) first aid society or the [national civil defense amateur radio society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_civil_defense_amateur_radio_society?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-50-ans-au-service-des-citoyens-4] Since 1993, the French Civil Protection is entitled to deliver official first aid training and certifications.[^cite_note-6]

### Mission broadening

From 1991 to 2009, [Louis Lareng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Lareng), the founder of the [French emergency medical service](9581361.md), was elected president of the national federation of civil protection. Under his guidance, the scope of civil defense societies widened from their traditional [first aid stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station) missions to [disaster response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_response) and [emergency response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_response).[^cite_note-50-ans-au-service-des-citoyens-4] Since then, the French civil protection has developed [civil defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_defense) partnerships with national and local authorities.&#x20;

## Current organization

The French Civil Protection is a [voluntary association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_association) acting in the public interest,[^cite_note-\:1-5] with a government certification to carry civil defense missions. In 2018, there were 32,000 volunteers acting in more than 500 local societies spread into 97 [departments](38523.md).[^cite_note-9] The federation is led by a board of directors with 24 members and by François Richez who has been the federal president since 2019.

### National level

The national federation is the organization in charge of managing the 97 societies spread in [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) and [overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_France) [departments](38523.md). Headquarters are located in [Asnières-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asni%C3%A8res-sur-Seine).

### Departmental and local levels

In each [department](38523.md), there can only be one association labeled by the national federation as a "departmental association of civil protection" (*association départementale de protection civile, or ADPC*). Each departmental association is in charge of one or several local societies (*antennes*) which are the operational units of the organization.

For historical reasons, there is only one departmental association for the [departments](38523.md) of [Paris](22989.md), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) and [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne).[^cite_note-\:2-10]

In [overseas territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_France), departmental associations can have different names, like the Polynesian federation of civil protection (*fédération polynésienne de protection civile*) from [French Polynesia](10737.md). However, their organization is the same as in [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France).

The French Civil Protection has no local association in some [departments](38523.md) like [Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes_\(d%C3%A9partement\)), [Corse-du-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corse-du-Sud), [Haute-Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Corse), [Haute-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Loire), [Eure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eure_\(d%C3%A9partement\)), [Lozère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loz%C3%A8re_\(d%C3%A9partement\)) or [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana).[^cite_note-\:2-10]

### Protection Civile Paris Seine

Its history and status are specific.

Until 1998, the [Paris Police Prefecture](1386763.md) was hiring temporary and volunteer rescuers in the interdepartmental civil protection service (*service interdépartemental de protection civile*). Their missions included [emergency response](146330.md) for the [Paris Fire Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Fire_Brigade). Then the service was disbanded and replaced by the Civil Protection of Paris, a local society managed by the National Federation of Civil Protection. It was then renamed *Protection Civile Paris Seine.*

## Missions

The French Civil protection has a government certification to carry 4 types of missions:[^cite_note-\:2-10]

- [first aid stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station) management;

- [first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid) training;

- humanitarian and social support;

- [emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_response) and [disaster response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_response).

### First aid stations

Main article: [First aid station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid_station)

Provisional [first aid stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station) (*dispositifs prévisionnels de secours*) are established during cultural, political or sportive events to provide medical first response to the participants. In 2016, the French Civil Protection carried 18,200 [first aid stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station).[^cite_note-\:3-12]

There are different configurations to adapt to the scale of the event:[^cite_note-ref_dps-13]

1. warning and [first aid station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station): 2 [certified first responders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certified_first_responder);

2. small scale provisional [first aid station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station): 4 to 12 certified first responders;

3. medium scale provisional [first aid station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station): 13 to 36 certified first responders;

4. large scale provisional [first aid station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_station): at least 37 certified first responders.

### First aid training

The French Civil Protection delivers public basic first aid training (*prévention et secours civiques de niveau 1*, or *PSC1*).[^cite_note-\:2-10] The association is also entitled to train its own members to become a [certified first responder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certified_first_responder) at the *PSE1* and *PSE2* levels. Specialized trainings as nautical first aid are also delivered.[^cite_note-\:5-16]

In 2016, 2,600 certified trainers from the French Civil Protection trained 110,000 people to first aids.[^cite_note-\:5-16]

### Humanitarian and social support

The French Civil Protection is entitled to carry a broad scope of humanitarian and support missions[^cite_note-\:2-10] including [assistance to the homeless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_services) and post-disaster response.

In 1999, the Federation contributed to an international humanitarian operation to support refugees from the [Kosovo war](16760.md) by managing the [Stenkovec refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenkovec_camp) in [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Macedonia).[^cite_note-senat_qSEQ990516653-17]

### Emergency response

The [Île-de-France](1716427.md), [Bas-Rhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas-Rhin) and [Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord_\(French_department\)) local societies are entitled to take part in [emergency and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_response) [disaster response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_response) missions[^cite_note-\:2-10] for the [prefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France), the [French emergency medical service](9581361.md) and the [fire services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_services_in_France).

#### Notable missions

French Civil Protection teams participated in the [emergency response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_response) after the 1999 [MV Erika sinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Erika),[^cite_note-nouvelobs_OBS4793-18] the 2001 [Toulouse chemical factory explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse_chemical_factory_explosion)[^cite_note-ina_InaEdu01836-19] and the 2015 [Germanwings Flight 9525 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanwings_Flight_9525).[^cite_note-\:3-12]

During the [November 2015 Paris attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2015_Paris_attacks), 130 first responders contributed to manage the crisis, to evacuate the victims to local hospitals, and to provide first aid to the witnesses.[^cite_note-\:3-12]

After the [2016 Nice truck attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Nice_truck_attack), 162 volunteers provided [emergency response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_response) and [psychological first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_first_aid) to the victims, their relatives and witnesses.[^cite_note-\:3-12]

## Equipment

### Vehicles

In 2016, the Federation owned a fleet of 1,230 vehicles, including 400 ambulances, light vehicles, boats, mobile headquarters vehicles, off-road vehicles and bicycles. The vehicles are white, with a large orange line on which the Federation logo is displayed and the words "PROTECTION CIVILE" are written in blue. Thin blue lines surround the orange line, except in Paris.

As the French Civil Protection is certified for [emergency response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_response) missions, its vehicles can be entitled to have blue [emergency vehicle lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_vehicle_lighting) and a two-tone siren.

### Uniform

Volunteers must wear [combat boots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_boot) and the Federal uniform during their missions. The trousers are blue with a vertical orange band on each side and reflective tape. The white, blue and orange top displays the logo and the words "PROTECTION CIVILE" on the back and on the right shoulder.[^cite_note-20]

Since 2012, the uniform has been in accordance with the European standard for high visibility clothing, number NF EN 471.

During some missions, first responders can wear an [*F2* helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPECTRA_helmet).
