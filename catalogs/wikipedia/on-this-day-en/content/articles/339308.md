The **Chinese Grand Prix** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 中国大奖赛; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Zhōngguó Dàjiǎngsài*) is a round of the [Formula One World Championship](10854.md). The event was held every year from [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Chinese_Grand_Prix) until [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Chinese_Grand_Prix) before it was suspended from 2020 to 2023 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_China). The event resumed in 2024 and is contracted to be held until 2030.[^cite_note-1]

It is currently held at the [Shanghai International Circuit](952143.md), [Jiading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiading_District), [Shanghai](27643.md). Designed by [Hermann Tilke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Tilke) and opened in 2004, the [US\$](18717338.md)240 million Shanghai course was the most expensive Formula One circuit facility[^cite_note-Shanghaigo-2] until the \$6 billion Abu Dhabi course opened five years later.[^cite_note-3] The 5.451 km (3.387 mi) track features one of the trickiest corner combinations on the Formula One calendar: Turn 1 and 2, a demanding 270-degree, right-handed corner combination whose radius decreases as the corner progresses.

## History

In the early 1990s, the [Chinese government](23239.md) began seeking to host an F1 race. After the [Zhuhai International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai_International_Circuit) was opened in 1996 in the city of [Zhuhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai) in [Guangdong](65109.md) Province, southern [China](5405.md), a race there was provisionally added to the 1999 F1 World Championship calendar.[^cite_note-4] However, the track failed to meet [FIA](248019.md) standard resulting in the race being cancelled.[^cite_note-5]

In 2002, it was announced that the management of the [Shanghai International Circuit](952143.md), with assistance from the organizers of the [Macau Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_Grand_Prix), had signed a seven-year contract with [Formula One Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_Group) to host the Chinese Grand Prix from 2004 until 2011.[^cite_note-Shanghaigo-2] The first Chinese Grand Prix, held on 26 September 2004, was won by [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari)'s [Rubens Barrichello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubens_Barrichello). The following year, it hosted the final round of the Formula One championship, in which the newly crowned world champion [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) won and claimed the constructor's title for [Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One). In 2006, the Chinese Grand Prix was won by [Michael Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schumacher), his last victory in Formula One.

In November 2008, the [BBC](19344654.md) reported that a senior race official, Qiu Weichang, had suggested that the money-losing race might be cancelled. Following a similar announcement about the [French Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Grand_Prix), Qiu said that the race's future was under consideration, and a decision would be made in 2009.[^cite_note-6]

2010 came and went with no formal announcement of an extension to the initial seven-race deal struck in 2004. However, immediately after the 2010 Shanghai race [Bernie Ecclestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Ecclestone), who manages the contracts with the various circuits, said of the 2011 calendar, "We are not dropping anything. \[It's] 20 races – getting ready for 25".[^cite_note-7]

It was only in February 2011 that a deal was agreed between F1 and the organisers of the Chinese round of the world championship. Reasons for the delay appear to have been over the fee paid to F1 to host the race. After racking up losses year after year, the organisers of the race refused to pay the fee required, reported to be amongst the highest paid to host an F1 race. F1 bosses appear to have reduced the fee and the new agreement to host an F1 race ran to 2017.[^cite_note-8]

In September 2017, a new three-year contract to host the race was announced, keeping the race on the calendar until 2020.[^cite_note-formula1-2020-9][^cite_note-speedcafe-2020-10] In 2019 it hosted the 1000th round of the Formula One World Championship.

The [2020 Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_Grand_Prix), scheduled for 19 April, was postponed and later cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-11] Plans for [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Formula_One_World_Championship), [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Formula_One_World_Championship) races were announced and later cancelled.[^cite_note-12] The Grand Prix returned to the Formula One calendar in 2024.[^cite_note-2024_calendar-13]

## Support races

In 2004, [Formula BMW Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_BMW_Asia), [Porsche Carrera Cup Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Asia) and [Asian Formula Renault Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Formula_Renault_Challenge) supported the inaugural Chinese Grand Prix. The latter was not retained in 2005. In 2008, [GP2 Asia Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Asia_Series) became a support event while Formula BMW Asia was renamed Formula BMW Pacific. 2009 saw Porsche Carrera Cup Asia as the only support event. It remained this way until [TCR International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_International_Series) became a support event in 2015, although this lasted only one year. In 2019, the [China Formula Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Formula_Grand_Prix) and [Shell Helix FIA F4 Chinese Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F4_Chinese_Championship) became support events. [F1 Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1_Academy) became a support event since 2025.
