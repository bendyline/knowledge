The **Northeastern United States** (also referred to as the **Northeast**, the **East Coast**,[^cite_note-11] or the **American Northeast**) form one of the four [census regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_of_the_United_States) defined by the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau). Located on the [Atlantic coast](89126.md) of North America, the region borders [Canada](5042916.md) to its north, the [Southern United States](179553.md) to its south, the [Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) to its west, and the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to its east.

The Northeast is one of the four regions defined by the U.S. Census Bureau for the collection and analysis of statistics.[^cite_note-Census-1] The Census Bureau defines the region as including the six [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) states of [Connecticut](6466.md), [Maine](19977.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), [New Hampshire](21134.md), [Rhode Island](25410.md), and [Vermont](32578.md), and three lower Northeast states of [New Jersey](21648.md), [New York](8210131.md), and [Pennsylvania](23332.md). Some expanded definitions of the region include [Mid-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)) locations such as [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware), [Maryland](18858.md), [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia), and [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)[^cite_note-Hobbs-12][^cite_note-Hudson-13][^cite_note-McIlwraith-14][^cite_note-Shelley-15][^cite_note-USDA-16]

The region is the base for the [Northeast megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_megalopolis), which includes many of the nation's largest metropolitan areas, including [New York City](645042.md), [Philadelphia](50585.md), and [Boston](24437894.md). The megalopolis makes up 67% of the region's total population of 57,609,148. The [gross domestic product](12594.md) of the region was \$5.1 trillion as of 2022 and contains some of the [most developed states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_Human_Development_Index) based on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index), with every state above the national average.[^cite_note-\:5-17][^cite_note-18] It is also the most densely populated region in the United States, with 320 people per square mile (120 people/km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-Zelinksy1-19][^cite_note-Hobbs-12] The U.S. Census Bureau defines the Northeast United States as having a total area of 181,324 mi<sup>2</sup> (469,630 km<sup>2</sup>), making it the smallest region of the United States by total area. The coastal waters of the Northeast megalopolis constitute one of the [fastest-warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) urban ocean [shorelines](5236.md) in the world.[^cite_note-NYCFastestWarmingUrbanShorelineWorld-20]

## History

### Indigenous people

Main article: [Indigenous peoples of the Northeastern Woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Northeastern_Woodlands)

Anthropologists recognize the "Northeastern Woodlands" as one of the [cultural regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) that existed in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md) at the time of [European colonists](7299.md) in the 15th and later centuries. Most did not settle in North America until the 17th century. The cultural area, known as the "[Northeastern Woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Woodland)", in addition to covering the entire Northeast U.S., also covered much of what is now Canada and other regions of what is now the [eastern United States](509486.md).[^cite_note-21]

Among the many tribes inhabiting this area were those that made up the [Iroquois nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquois) and the numerous [Algonquian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_peoples).[^cite_note-Native-22] In the United States of the 21st century, 18 [federally recognized tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federally_recognized_tribes_by_state) reside in the Northeast.[^cite_note-wildlife-23] For the most part, the people of the Northeastern Woodlands, on whose lands European fishermen began camping to dry their codfish in the early 1600s, lived in villages, especially after being influenced by agricultural traditions of the Ohio and Mississippi valley societies.[^cite_note-24]

### Colonial history

Main article: [History of New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_England)



*Embarkation of the Pilgrims*, an 1857 portrait by [Robert Walter Weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Walter_Weir)



*Penn's Treaty with the Indians*, a 1772 portrait by [Benjamin West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_West)

All of the [U.S. states](18618239.md) making up the Northeastern region were among the original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies), though Maine and Vermont were part of other colonies before the United States became independent in the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution). The two cultural and geographic regions that form parts of the Northeastern region have distinct histories. The first European explorer known to have explored the Atlantic shoreline of the Northeast since the [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_the_Americas) was [Giovanni da Verrazzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_da_Verrazzano) in 1524. His ship *La Dauphine* explored the coast from what is now known as Florida to New Brunswick.

The first Europeans to settle and colonize New England were [Pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrims_\(Plymouth_Colony\)) from [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England), who landed in present-day Massachusetts in 1620.[^cite_note-Arenstam-25] The Pilgrims arrived on the ship *[Mayflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayflower)* and founded [Plymouth Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Colony) so they could practice religion freely.[^cite_note-Arenstam-25] Ten years later, a larger group of [Puritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritan) settled north of Plymouth Colony in [Boston](24437894.md) to form [Massachusetts Bay Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony).[^cite_note-26] In 1636, colonists established [Connecticut Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Colony)[^cite_note-Kelly_2019-27] and [Providence Plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Plantations).[^cite_note-Kelly_2020-28]

Providence was founded by [Roger Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Williams), who was banished by Massachusetts for his beliefs in freedom of religion, and it was the first colony to guarantee all citizens freedom of worship. [Anne Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hutchinson), who was also banished by Massachusetts, formed the town of [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Rhode_Island). Providence, Portsmouth and two other towns ([Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island) and [Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick,_Rhode_Island)) consolidated to form the [Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Rhode_Island_and_Providence_Plantations).[^cite_note-Kelly_2020-28]

[Henry Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hudson) explored the area of present-day New York in 1609 and claimed it for the Netherlands. His journey stimulated Dutch interest, and the area became known as [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland). In 1625, the city of [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam) (the location of present-day New York City) was designated the capital of the province.[^cite_note-29] The Dutch New Netherland settlement along the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) and, for a time, the [New Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Sweden) settlement along the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) divided the English settlements in the north and the south. In 1664, [Charles II of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) formally annexed New Netherland and incorporated it into the [English colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_colonial_empire).[^cite_note-Radford-30] The territory became the colonies of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York) and [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-Radford-30] New Jersey was originally split into [East Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jersey) and [West Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Jersey) until the two were united as a royal colony in 1702.[^cite_note-Radford-30]

[New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) played a prominent role in early American education. Starting in the 17th century, the larger towns in New England opened [grammar schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar_schools), the forerunner of the modern [high school](554992.md). The first public school in the English colonies was the [Boston Latin School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Latin_School), founded in 1635.[^cite_note-31] In 1636, the colonial [legislature of Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature_of_Massachusetts) founded [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College), the oldest [institution of higher learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education) in the United States.[^cite_note-32]

In 1681, [William Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn), who wanted to give [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) a land of religious freedom, founded [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) and extended freedom of religion to all citizens.[^cite_note-33]

Penn strongly desired access to the sea for the [Province of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) and leased what then came to be known as the "Lower Counties on the Delaware" from the [Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_England).[^cite_note-hod-34] Penn established representative government and briefly combined his two possessions under one General Assembly in 1682.

By 1704, the province of Pennsylvania had grown so large that their representatives wanted to make decisions without the assent of the Lower Counties and the two groups of representatives began meeting on their own, one at [Philadelphia](50585.md), and the other at [New Castle, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Castle,_Delaware). Penn and his heirs remained proprietors of both and always appointed the same person Governor for their province of Pennsylvania and their territory of the Lower Counties. The fact that Delaware and Pennsylvania shared the same governor was not unique. From 1703 to 1738, both New York and New Jersey shared a governor.[^cite_note-35] Massachusetts and New Hampshire also shared a governor for some time.[^cite_note-36]

### American Revolution

Further information: [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) and [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War)



The [Battle of Trenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trenton), fought in New Jersey in December 1776 following [Washington's covert crossing of the Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington's_crossing_of_the_Delaware_River) during the night of December 25, 1776, represented an inspiring victory for Washington's [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) and began to turn the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in the Americans' favor.

The beginnings of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) were in the Northeast, specifically in Massachusetts. The [Battles of Lexington and Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord) in northeast of Boston were the first military engagements between the Revolutionaries and the British.[^cite_note-Daughan_2018-37] Many of the major battles of the revolution were fought in the Northeast. The British [evacuated Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Boston) in early 1776 and moved to [capture New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_at_Kip's_Bay).[^cite_note-McCullough_2005-38]

The revolutionaries were pushed to the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) before suddenly moving forward against the British in the [Battles of Trenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trenton) and [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Princeton).[^cite_note-McCullough_2005-38] A stalemate was reached in 1778, between the British and American Revolutionaries and continued until the end of the war in 1783.[^cite_note-39] The war moved to southern states and eventually conclude with the [Battle of Yorktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Yorktown) in Virginia.[^cite_note-McCullough_2005-38]

#### Formation of the United States of America

The idea of an independent United States of America, with the designs of its government, was created primarily in the Northeast in a series of declarations, constitutions, and documents. The [Continental Congresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress) met in Philadelphia, which produced the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) and [the Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation). Following the American Revolution, the [capital of the newly formed United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) moved around in the states of [Pennsylvania](23332.md), [New Jersey](21648.md), and [New York](8210131.md). It was based in [New York City](645042.md) from 1785 until 1790, when it was moved to [Congress Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Hall) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), where it remained for a decade, until 1800, when the construction of the new national capital of [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) was completed.[^cite_note-40]

The [Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(United_States\)) was held in Philadelphia, where the [new United States Constitution](31644.md) was drafted in 1787.[^cite_note-41] Six of the [first 13 states to ratify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Ratification_of_the_Constitution) the new constitution were in the Northeast, with the last of the original 13, Rhode Island, ratifying the constitution in 1790. Vermont was admitted in 1791 as the 14th state. The [first Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_United_States_Congress) convened in [Federal Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Hall) in New York City in March 1789.[^cite_note-42]

### Early and mid-19th century



The [Battle of Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg), fought in and around [Gettysburg, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg,_Pennsylvania), was the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War)'s bloodiest battle; the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army)'s victory at Gettysburg represented a turning point in the war in the Union's favor.

Following the revolution the Northeast saw small skirmishes like the [Whiskey Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskey_Rebellion) in western parts of Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-Berkin_2017-43] Many northeastern states continued trading with the British and other European powers. Tensions between the United States and Europe (specifically Britain) soured in the lead-up to the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812).

This caused certain trade merchants to [meet in Hartford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_Convention) to propose seceding from the United States.[^cite_note-44] The [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812) saw less fighting in the Northeast and instead more fighting in western and southern areas. A failed [invasion of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Canada_\(1812\)) and the occupation of Maine were some of the major conflicts during the war.[^cite_note-45] The war ended in 1815 and most of the Northeast has not seen any major conflict since then.

The [American Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution_in_the_United_States) was launched in [Blackstone Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone_Valley) in [Rhode Island](25410.md) and [Massachusetts](1645518.md), where [textile mills](2162735.md) spread across New England, and in eastern [Pennsylvania](23332.md), where coal, steel, and industrialization launched the nation's manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-46]

After the end of the War of 1812, industry boomed in the Northeast in the early and middle parts of the 19th century. With the construction of railroads and canals crossing the northeast and the rise of western territories and resources from the south, the Northeast experienced the development of new industries and a fast-growing population. Many of the coastal cities, including [Boston](24437894.md), [New York City](645042.md), and [Philadelphia](50585.md), served as ocean trade ports for American goods.

Cities, including [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), [Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), [Pittsburgh](25101.md), [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), and [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York), were settled and emerged as major industrial centers.[^cite_note-47]

By 1860, New York City, based on its present-day boundaries, was the first U.S. city to reach a population exceeding one million.[^cite_note-Jackson_1995-48] Due to the settlement of the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) and [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains), agriculture collapsed in the Mid-Atlantic and New England, with many farms being abandoned by the end of the century, returning to rural forest.

Conflicts with the south over the [spread of slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states) became a large factor in the start of the American Civil War, between the United States (western and Northeastern states) and [the Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) (southeastern states). The admission of Maine as a free state in exchange for [Missouri](19571.md) becoming a slave state as part of the [Missouri Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Compromise) in 1820 settled the final boundaries of the Northeastern states.[^cite_note-49]

The [Mason-Dixon line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason%E2%80%93Dixon_line) was established as the border of slavery, following the border of Pennsylvania and Delaware/Maryland.[^cite_note-50] [Abolitionist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States) started in the Northeast and Midwest and became prominent towards the mid-19th century, these groups advocated the shrinking or banning of [slavery in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States). Some Northeastern states still had small amounts of slaves into the 1850s, though some banned it during the decade.

The [election of 1860](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_United_States_presidential_election) led to the start of the Civil War; southern states seceded from the United States in late 1860 and early 1861. States like [Maryland](18858.md) and [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) remained in the union, even with slavery still legal. For the first two years, the [eastern theater of the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_theater_of_the_American_Civil_War) remained in [Virginia](32432.md) and Maryland, but in 1863 the war reached its northeastern most extent in [Gettysburg, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg,_Pennsylvania). The [Battle of Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg) is considered a turning point in the Civil War, seeing the end of the Confederate push northwards.[^cite_note-51]

While all Northeastern states remained in the United States during the war, conflicts did arise, like the [New York draft riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_draft_riots) in 1863.[^cite_note-52] The war ended in 1865 with the United States taking back control of Southern states.

### Industrial Revolution and modern times



[Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy,_Manhattan) in [Lower Manhattan](455646.md), c. 1900



Northeastern United States in 1908 from *The Harmsworth atlas and Gazetter*

Following the Civil War, the Northeast saw a large economic boom and became one of the most industrialized regions in the world. Many technological innovations were made in the Northeast during this time. The [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution) saw the northeast grow massively, even more so than before the Civil War. Many cities in the Northeast exploded in population, with cities like Philadelphia and New York climbing over one million people, while other cities like Buffalo, Boston, and Pittsburgh rose above half a million during this time.

New York City eventually grew to become one of the largest cities in the world by 1900. With the American involvement in both World Wars, the Northeast became a large base of war production, with the [Brooklyn Naval Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Navy_Yard) producing many navy ships.[^cite_note-53] Many worker strikes occurred in the states, including the [Homestead strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_strike) in 1892.[^cite_note-54] Many of these cities saw a peak population and industrial output in the aftermath of [World War II](32927.md) in the 1950s.[^cite_note-55]

Starting in the 1950s and continuing into the 21st century, a large [industrial decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialization) in the Northeast resulted in a depopulation of many Northeastern cities, many of which had not yet recovered from it into the 21st century. This led to the rise of programs of [urban renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal) and demolition of large parts of Northeastern cities during the mid and late 20th century.[^cite_note-Sugrue_2014-56] There has also been a large population shift to the [Sun Belt states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Belt) starting in the 1960s.[^cite_note-57]

[New York state](8210131.md) lost its claim to being the most populated state after it was surpassed by [California](5407.md) in the 1970s. Some Northeastern cities, including New York City, have recovered from their decline in the mid-20th century.[^cite_note-Jackson_1995-48] Many new information and service industries have risen in the northeast, which has led to a boom in the 21st century in some cities in the Northeast like Boston, New York, and Philadelphia. Some other cities like [Hartford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut), Syracuse, and Buffalo still are declining though in the 21st century.[^cite_note-58] [Hurricane Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy) impacted much of the northeast in 2012, severely damaging much of the coast and causing flooding inland. The hurricane directly impacted [New Jersey](21648.md) and caused large amounts of flooding in New York City.[^cite_note-59]

Although the first settlers of New England were motivated by religion, since the 21st century, [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) had become one of the least religious parts of the United States. In a 2009 [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gallup_Organization) survey, less than half of residents in [Maine](19977.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), [New Hampshire](21134.md), and [Vermont](32578.md) reported religion as an important part of their daily life.[^cite_note-60] In a 2010 Gallup survey, less than 30% of residents in Vermont, New Hampshire, Maine, and Massachusetts reported [attending church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_attendance) weekly, giving them the lowest church attendance among U.S. states.[^cite_note-61]

## Geography

[Montauk Point Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montauk_Point_Lighthouse) on the east end of [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island)

The vast area from central [Virginia](32432.md) to northern [Maine](19977.md), and from western [Pennsylvania](23332.md), from [Pittsburgh](25101.md) in the west to the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) in the east, have all been loosely grouped into the Northeast at one time or another. The [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau)'s definition of the Northeast includes nine states: Maine, [New York](8210131.md), [New Jersey](21648.md), [Vermont](32578.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), [Rhode Island](25410.md), [Connecticut](6466.md), [New Hampshire](21134.md), and Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-Census-1][^cite_note-63]

The region is often subdivided into [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England), the six states east of New York state and the [Mid-Atlantic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)) of New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania and areas to the south. This definition has been essentially unchanged since 1880 and is widely used as a standard for data tabulation.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-widely-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

The U.S. Census Bureau has acknowledged the obvious limitations of this definition and the potential merits of a proposal created after the 1950 census,[^cite_note-68] that would include changing regional boundaries to include [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware), [Maryland](18858.md), and [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) with the Mid-Atlantic states, but ultimately decided that "the new system did not win enough overall acceptance among data users to warrant adoption as an official new set of general-purpose State groupings. The previous development of many series of statistics, arranged and issued over long periods of time on the basis of the existing State groupings, favored the retention of the summary units of the current regions and divisions."[^cite_note-GARM-69] The U.S. Census Bureau confirmed in 1994 that it would continue to "review the components of the regions and divisions to ensure that they continue to represent the most useful combinations of states and state equivalents."[^cite_note-GARM-69]

Many organizations and reference works follow the Census Bureau's definition for the region.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] In the history of the United States, the [Mason–Dixon line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason%E2%80%93Dixon_line) between Pennsylvania ([the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_United_States)) and Maryland ([the South](179553.md)) traditionally divided the regions,[^cite_note-73] but in modern times, various entities define the Northeastern United States in somewhat different ways.

The [Association of American Geographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Geographers) divides the Northeast into two divisions: "New England", which is the same as the Census Bureau; and it has the same "Middle States" but adds Delaware.[^cite_note-74] Similarly, the [Geological Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Society_of_America) defines the Northeast as these same states but with the addition of Maryland and the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia).[^cite_note-75]

The narrowest definitions include only the states of New England.[^cite_note-76] Other more restrictive definitions include New England and New York as part of the Northeast United States, but exclude Pennsylvania and New Jersey.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

### Topography

While most of the Northeastern United States lie in the physiographic region of the [Appalachian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains), some are also part of the [Atlantic coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_plain), which extends south to the southern tip of [Florida](18933066.md). The coastal plain areas include [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod) in Massachusetts, [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island) in New York, and most of [New Jersey](21648.md), and are generally low and flat with sandy soil and long tidal marsh waterways.[^cite_note-Hobbs-12] The highlands, including the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(United_States\)) and the Appalachian Mountains, are heavily forested, ranging from rolling hills to summits greater than 6,000 feet (1,800 m), and pocked with many lakes.[^cite_note-Hobbs-12] The highest peak in the Northeast is [Mount Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Washington_\(New_Hampshire\)) in [New Hampshire](21134.md) at 6,288 feet (1,917 m).[^cite_note-85]

### Land use

As of 2012, forest-use covered approximately 60% of the Northeastern states, including Delaware, Maryland, and Washington, D.C., about twice the national average. About 11% was cropland and another 4% grassland pasture or range. There is also more urbanized land in the Northeast (12%) than in any other U.S region.[^cite_note-USDA-16]

Many parks on a state and national level cover the inland parts of the region. Large parks include the [Adirondack Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adirondack_Park) in northeastern New York, [Green Mountain National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Mountain_National_Forest) in Vermont, [White Mountain Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Mountain_National_Forest) in northern New Hampshire, [Baxter State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baxter_State_Park) in northern Maine, [Acadia National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia_National_Park) on the eastern coast of Maine, [Allegheny National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_National_Forest) in northwestern Pennsylvania, and [Catskill Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catskill_Park) in southern New York. There are also some parks closer to the shore, though these are usually smaller and squeezed in-between urbanized areas. These include the [Palisades Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Palisades_\(Hudson_River\)) in New Jersey, [Fire Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Island_National_Seashore) in Long Island, and the [Cape Cod shoreline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod_National_Seashore) in Massachusetts. The Northeast has 72 [National Wildlife Refuges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wildlife_Refuge), encompassing more than 500,000 acres (780 sq mi; 2,000 km<sup>2</sup>) of habitat and designed to protect some of the 92 different threatened and [endangered species](20110668.md) living in the region.[^cite_note-wildlife-23]

### Climate

The climate of the Northeastern United States varies from the northernmost state of [Maine](19977.md) to its southernmost state in [Maryland](18858.md). The region's climate is influenced by its positional western to eastern flow of weather in the lower middle latitudes in the United States. In summer the subtropical high ([Bermuda High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_High)) moves toward the East Coast, this pumps warm and sultry air toward the Northeast (less so in the far northern areas of northern New York, Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine). Summers are normally warm in northern areas to hot in southern areas. Frequent (but brief) thundershowers are common on hot summer days from Connecticut south to Maryland.

In winter, the subtropical high retreats southeastward, and the polar jet stream moves south bringing colder air masses from up in [Canada](5042916.md) and more frequent storm systems to the region. Winter often brings both rain and snow as well as surges of both warm and cold air. In the southern part of the Northeast from coastal Rhode Island southwest to eastern Maryland, the Appalachians partially protect these locations from the extreme cold coming from the west and the interior of North America.[^cite_note-Climate-86]

The basic climate of the Northeast can be divided into a colder and snowier interior, including western Maryland, most of Pennsylvania, most of [North Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jersey), [Upstate New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_New_York), and most of New England, and a milder coastal plain region from [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod) and southern [Rhode Island](25410.md) southward, including [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), [Southern Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Connecticut), [New York City](645042.md), central and southern [New Jersey](21648.md), part of the Pennsylvania portion of the [Philadelphia metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_metropolitan_area) including [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware), and most of [Maryland](18858.md).

In the latter region the [hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) ranges from 7a to 8a. Annual mean temperatures range from the low-to-mid 50s F from Maryland to southern Connecticut, to the 40s F in most of New York State, New England, and northern Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-Climate-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

Most of the Northeast has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfa/Dfb/occasional Dfc/Dc*). The northernmost portion of the [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) zone (*Cfa/Do*) begins at [Martha's Vineyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha's_Vineyard) and [extreme SW Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerly,_Rhode_Island) and extends southwestward down the coastal plain to central and [southern Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Maryland). The [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) zone (*Cfb/Do*) only exists on [Block Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island) and [Nantucket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantucket). It is the only area of the Northeast where all months average between 0 and 22 °C (32 and 72 °F). [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod) borders this zone and warm-summer humid continental (*Dfb/Dc*).

Ethnic origins in the Northeast

As of the [2020 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population of the region was 57,609,148, representing 17.38% of the nation's total population.[^cite_note-auto-6] With an average of 345.5 people per square mile, the Northeast is 2.5 times as densely populated as the second-most dense region, the [South](179553.md). Since the last century, the [U.S. population has been shifting away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_growth_rate) from the Northeast and [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) toward the South and [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States).[^cite_note-91]

The region's racial composition as of 2020 was 64.42% [white](273285.md), 11.51% African American, 0.51% Native American, 7.25% Asian, 0.04% Pacific Islander, 8.17% from other races, and 8.10% from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race were 15.27% of the population.[^cite_note-92] There were 22,418,883 households and 14,189,719 families in 2021. Of the 22,418,883 households, 27.7% included children under the age of 18.[^cite_note-93]

In 2021, the region's the population's age distribution was 20.5% under age 18, 57.36% from 18 to 62, and 22.1% who were 62 years of age or older. The median age was 40.5 years. For every 100 females, there were 96.4 males. For every 100 women ages 18 and over, there were 94.3 men.[^cite_note-94]

The median income for a household in the region in 2021 was \$77,142, and the median income for a family was \$97,347. About 11.9% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 16.0% of those under age 18 and 10.4% of those age 65 or over.[^cite_note-95]

The two U.S. Census Bureau divisions in the Northeast, [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) and the [Mid-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)), rank second and first respectively among the [9 divisions in population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density) according to the 2013 population estimate. The [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_states) region (233.1) was very close behind New England (233.2). Due to the faster growth of the South Atlantic region, it will take over the #2 division rank in population density in the next estimate, dropping New England to 3rd position. New England is projected to retain the number 3 rank for many, many years, as the only other lower-ranked division with even half the population density of New England is the [East North Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_North_Central_states) division (192.1) and this region's population is projected to grow slowly.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-census.gov-96]

As of 2012, the Northeast U.S. accounts for approximately 23% of the nation's [gross domestic product](12594.md).[^cite_note-\:5-17] Due to its vast population and diverse landscapes, the Northeast has a large and robust economy, ranging from financial services in Manhattan, to agriculture in [Central Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_Pennsylvania).

### New York City

Main article: [Economy of New York City](3848717.md)



The [New York Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange_Building) in [Lower Manhattan](455646.md) is the largest stock exchange in the world by market capitalization.

As of 2021, the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) is estimated to produce a [gross metropolitan product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_metropolitan_product) (GMP) of \$2.1 trillion US dollars, ranking it [first in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._metropolitan_areas_by_GDP) If the New York metropolitan area were a [sovereign state](23604120.md), it would have been the [eighth-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) in the world. [Manhattan](45470.md) is considered the world's financial center, with many large banks based in Manhattan and some of the largest stock exchanges on [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street), like the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md), it is so prominent that the term "Wall Street" is usually synonymous with finance. Many other companies are based in New York City area, either in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), [downtown Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Brooklyn), [Long Island City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_City), or the various suburbs, like [Stamford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford,_Connecticut) or [White Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Plains,_New_York). Some of the largest companies based in New York City area include, [Verizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verizon_Communications), [J.P. Morgan Chase](231001.md), [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup), [MetLife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetLife), [PepsiCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PepsiCo), [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM), [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia), [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs), and [Pfizer](62304.md). Several technology companies have been founded in New York, or moved their headquarters to New York from other places.

[New York City](645042.md) is the nation's most populated city, and the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) including and surrounding it is the nation's most populated metropolitan region, contributing to a sizable shopping economy, including many large shopping malls and department stores based in the area, such as [Macy's on 34th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy's_Herald_Square), [Fifth Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue), and [American Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream_Meadowlands) in [East Rutherford, New Jersey](136878.md), the [Palisades Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palisades_Center) in [West Nyack, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Nyack,_New_York), and the [SoNo Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_SoNo_Collection) in [Norwalk, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwalk,_Connecticut). The [Port of New York and New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey), one of the nation's largest ports, is located on [New York Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Harbor).

### Other regions

Many national and state parks in the region generate lots of tourism, especially during fall months. The logging industry is especially prominent in Maine, making up a large part of [Northern Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Maine_Woods)'s economy.

#### Major stations



[Grand Central Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Terminal) in New York City, a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark) and the second-busiest train station in the nation after [New York Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Penn_Station), also in New York City

- [30th Street Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Street_Station) in Philadelphia. Served by all SEPTA Regional Lines, Amtrak, NJ Transit's [Atlantic City Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City_Line), it is the third-busiest [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) station and 11th-busiest train station in North America with over four million passengers in 2019.

- [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York_City\)) in New York City is served by some [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) lines, and some [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md) and [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) trains. It is the [busiest train station in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_railway_stations_in_North_America), with over 10 million passengers in 2019, along with 27 million passengers from NJ Transit and 69 million from Long Island Rail in 2017.

- [Grand Central Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Terminal) in New York City is served by Metro North and some Long Island Rail trains (beginning in January 2023). Grand Central Terminal had over 67 million annual passengers in 2017 and is the second-busiest train station in the nation and third-busiest in North America.

- [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(New_Haven\)) in [New Haven, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut) is served by [New Haven Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven_Line), Hartford Line, and Shoreline East along existing [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) train lines. It had 350,000 annual Amtrak passengers in 2017.

- [South Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Station) in [Boston](24437894.md) is served by southern MBTA commuter lines and Amtrak, and was the seventh-busiest train station in North America with nearly 29 million passengers as of 2017.

- [North Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Station) in Boston is served by northern MBTA commuter lines and the [Downeaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downeaster_\(train\)) on Amtrak. It had six million MBTA users in 2012 and 152,000 Amtrak passengers in 2021.

### History



The [Jersey City Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Railroad_of_New_Jersey_Terminal), a major rail and ferry connection between New Jersey and New York City.



The Cross-Bronx Expressway (I-95) is an urban freeway which was built using [slum clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum_clearance_in_the_United_States) policies in the 1950s and 1960s. Today it is one of the most congested highways in the nation. It is regarded as a major cause for [urban decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_decay) in the Bronx.

[^cite_note-107]

The Northeast has been a place for many firsts in transportation in the US, from the first commercial railroad in the US in [Milton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton,_Massachusetts) ([Granite Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite_Railway)), first rapid transit system ([MBTA Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(MBTA\))),[^cite_note-108] the first limited access road was the [Bronx River Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River_Parkway), opened in 1922,[^cite_note-109] New York is also where the first urban freeway was built in the late-1930s.[^cite_note-110] ([FDR Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDR_Drive)) The northeast would also be home to some of the first major [freeway revolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_revolt) in [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village),[^cite_note-\:1-111] and would see the first major highway teardown ([Miller Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Highway)) in the 1970s.[^cite_note-112]

Before European settlement, most of the Northeast was loosely connected by Native American trails, some of which would be incorporated into early-European settlement roads and turnpikes. One major early road was the [Boston Post Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Post_Road), connecting New York City and Boston along the Connecticut and Rhode Island coasts.[^cite_note-113] Later these roads would be included in the [King's Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Highway_\(Charleston_to_Boston\)), spanning most of the east coast. Smaller turnpikes would also connect cities across the northeast. These roads would prove essential to moving goods across the English colonies in the 18th century and would later play a large part in the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution).[^cite_note-114]

The region saw a boom in canal-building in the early-19th century, with a major canal being the [Erie Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Canal), opened in 1825, connecting the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) to the Hudson River and Atlantic Ocean through [Western New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_York).[^cite_note-115] The first railroads would be built in the late-1820s and would explode in mileage in the mid to late 19th century.[^cite_note-116] Places like Philadelphia, New York, Boston, Newark, and Pittsburgh would become large water and rail hubs during the Industrial Revolution and would see tremendous booms in population and use.[^cite_note-117]

Many large rivers in the northeast like the Hudson and Delaware would be slowly crossed with bridges starting in the 1800s, with the first fixed crossing of the Hudson River south of Albany being the [Poughkeepsie Railroad Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkway_over_the_Hudson), opened in 1889.[^cite_note-118] The [Delair Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delair_Bridge), which would connect Philadelphia with New Jersey was opened six years later in 1896.[^cite_note-119] The first crossing of the Hudson River into New York City would be the series of Hudson River PATH tunnels, being opened in 1908 and 1909.[^cite_note-120] The first major vehicle tunnel would be the Holland Tunnel, opened up in 1927.[^cite_note-121]

The start of highway construction would be the [Bronx River Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River_Parkway) and [Long Island Motor Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Motor_Parkway), both of which started construction in the early-1900s.[^cite_note-122] The rise of [Robert Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Moses) in New York would see the construction of many major road bridges and highways crossing the city and metro area. East River Drive (eventually renamed FDR Drive), was built along the corresponding river in Manhattan.[^cite_note-123] The mid-20th century would see the rise of urban and suburban [freeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway) and the decline of passenger and freight rail, with many lesser used tracks being abandoned or torn up during this time.[^cite_note-124] It would also see the original [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(1910%E2%80%931963\)) demolished in Midtown Manhattan during the mid-1960s.[^cite_note-125] The construction of the [Cross-Bronx Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Bronx_Expressway) in New York, [Central Artery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Artery) in Boston, and the [Vine Street Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_676) in Philadelphia tore up many ethnic and minority neighborhoods in the name of [urban renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal).[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-\:2-129] Many other highways were proposed during this era, like the [Lower Manhattan Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Manhattan_Expressway) and the [Inner Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_695_\(Massachusetts\)) in Boston, which were not built due to fierce highway revolts and rising costs.[^cite_note-\:1-111][^cite_note-\:2-129][^cite_note-130] After the major highway revolts and rise of environmental concerns, new highway and interstate projects were mostly cancelled or shortened in the Northeast by the 1990s.

Despite the lack of new major road projects in the Northeast, the region has still continued to grow in population, resulting in the rise of alternative forms of transport like [HOV lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-occupancy_vehicle_lane) or [commuter rails](6286.md). This has led to the Northeast having one of the highest transit usage percentages in North America, with the Long Island Railroad being the most used commuter rail in the continent.[^cite_note-131] One exception was the [Big Dig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Dig), a major road project that would tear down the former elevated Central Artery (I-93) and instead [tunnel it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Neill_Tunnel) (and widen). It would also construct a [new Charles River bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_P._Zakim_Bunker_Hill_Memorial_Bridge) and the [Ted Williams Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Williams_Tunnel) (I-90). This would end up becoming one of the costliest construction projects in the world, costing \$21 billion adjusted to 2020 inflation.[^cite_note-132] The former highway's path would become the [Rose Kennedy Greenway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Fitzgerald_Kennedy_Greenway), a large public park. The [Sheridan Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Route_895) (former I-895) was also rebuilt into a boulevard in the late-2010s.[^cite_note-133] [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York) has torn down the [Inner Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Loop_\(Rochester\)) due to low traffic and to reunify neighborhoods in downtown and to create developable space.[^cite_note-134]

One geographer, [Wilbur Zelinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbur_Zelinsky), asserts that the Northeast region lacks a unified cultural identity,[^cite_note-Zelinksy1-19] but has served as a "culture hearth" for the rest of the nation.[^cite_note-135] Several much smaller geographical regions within the Northeast have distinct cultural identities.[^cite_note-Zelinksy1-19]

### Landmarks

Almost half of the [National Historic Landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark) maintained by the National Park Service are located in the Northeastern United States.[^cite_note-136]

### Religion

According to a 2009 [Gallup poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_\(company\)), the Northeastern states differ from most of the rest of the U.S. in religious affiliation, generally reflecting the descendants of immigration patterns of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, with many Catholics arriving from Ireland, Italy, French Canada - Quebec, Portugal and east-central Europe. Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York and New Jersey are the only states in the nation where [Catholics](606848.md) outnumber [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) and other [Christian denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination). More than 20% of respondents in Maine, New Hampshire, and Vermont declared no religious identity.[^cite_note-137] Compared to other U.S. regions, the Northeast, along with the Pacific Northwest, has had the lowest regular religious service attendance and the fewest people for whom religion is an important part of their daily lives as of 2015.[^cite_note-138]

Notable golf tournaments in the Northeastern United States include [The Northern Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Northern_Trust), [Travelers Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelers_Championship), and [Atlantic City LPGA Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City_LPGA_Classic). The [US Open](82729.md), held in New York, is one of the four Grand Slam tennis tournaments.

Notable Northeastern motorsports tracks include [Watkins Glen International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watkins_Glen_International), [Pocono Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocono_Raceway), [New Hampshire Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_Motor_Speedway) and [Lime Rock Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_Rock_Park), which have hosted [Formula One](10854.md), [IndyCar](30446232.md), [NASCAR](21114.md) and [International Motor Sports Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Motor_Sports_Association) races. Also, drag strips such as [Englishtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Bridge_Township_Raceway_Park), [Epping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Dragway) and [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Grove_Raceway) have hosted [NHRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHRA) national events. [Belmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Park) at New York hosts the [Belmont Stakes](62398.md) horse races, which is part of the [Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing](19116721.md).

The region has also been noted for the prevalence of the traditionally Northeastern sports of [ice hockey](14790.md) and [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse).[^cite_note-141]
