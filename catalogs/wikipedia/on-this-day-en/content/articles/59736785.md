**NetBlocks** is a [watchdog organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchdog_organization) that monitors [cybersecurity](7398.md) and the [governance of the Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_governance).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] The service was launched in 2017 to monitor [Internet freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_freedom).[^cite_note-3]

## Activities

### Projects

NetBlocks publishes original reporting on Internet governance and [sustainable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy), providing tools to the public to observe possible Internet restrictions and to estimate the economic consequences of network disruptions.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] NetBlocks has established a high level of trust in communities around the world, facilitating the spread of information during emergencies and [Internet censorship](11056386.md) events, according to peer-reviewed research published in the scientific journal *[Nature](43427.md)*.[^cite_note-6]

### Events

On 25 November 2017, NetBlocks and the [Digital Rights Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Rights_Foundation) provided information about the nationwide [censorship of Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_of_Facebook), [Twitter](30642008.md), [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_of_YouTube) and other social media services by the [Pakistani government](1897833.md) following the [Tehreek-e-Labaik protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Faizabad_sit-in).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

During the [2018–2019 Sudanese protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932019_Sudanese_protests), NetBlocks stated that the Sudanese government maintains "an extensive Internet censorship regime" following the [censorship of social media websites in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_Sudan).[^cite_note-REUTnet-10] Following the [2019 Gabonese coup d'état attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Gabonese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt), NetBlocks monitored censorship in the country.[^cite_note-11] The cost of the three-day Internet shutdown following the [Zimbabwean fuel protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_fuel_protests) was also calculated to cost [Zimbabwe](34399.md) an estimated \$17 million.[^cite_note-12]

The [block of Wikipedia in Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_of_Wikipedia_in_Venezuela) and [other censorship incidents](60360839.md) during the [Venezuelan presidential crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_presidential_crisis) were also monitored by NetBlocks, with several international media outlets covering the situation with NetBlocks' work.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

In July 2020, as the [Somalian Parliament](36817385.md) [passed a motion of no confidence](53281313.md#Motion_of_no_confidence_and_resignation) in [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_somalia) [Hassan Ali Khaire](53281313.md), NetBlocks reported that Internet access had been disrupted impeding media coverage of political and public reactions to events on the ground, presenting evidence contradicting network operator [Hormuud Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormuud_Telecom)'s claim that the outage was due to "windy conditions."[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

From February 2022, NetBlocks set up a reporting initiative providing extensive coverage on the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), documenting Russian efforts to disable communications at nuclear sites and in conflict zones.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

## Reception

Visiting NetBlocks' website used to trigger monitoring checks against websites to see if they were blocked for that visitor. However, these checks were done without the user's consent. A researcher, Collin Anderson, argued that this could potentially present a risk to users. NetBlocks said it removed the checks in 2020 because the data "just wasn't that good," [*Wired*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\)) reported.[^cite_note-wired20210714-24]

Anderson set up a website criticizing NetBlocks at the domain "netblocks.fyi". In September 2020, NetBlocks filed a complaint against Anderson with the [World Intellectual Property Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Intellectual_Property_Organization). The [mailing list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_mailing_list) OTF-Talk reacted negatively to the response, and afterwards subscribers demanded NetBlocks "release its tools as [open source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software), make its methodologies open to audit, and publish its measurement data so it could be scrutinised". NetBlocks won the complaint in November 2020 and took over the domain.[^cite_note-wired20210714-24][^cite_note-25] Anderson subsequently moved the site to netblocked.org.[^cite_note-wired20210714-24]
