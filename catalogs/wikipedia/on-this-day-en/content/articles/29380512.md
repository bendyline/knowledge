**Swedes** ([Swedish](26689.md): *svenskar*), or **Swedish people**, are an [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) native to [Sweden](5058739.md), who share a common ancestry, [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Sweden), [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sweden), and [language](26689.md). Swedes mostly inhabit Sweden but have also migrated to other countries, especially the other [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries). A substantial [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_diaspora) in other countries include the [Swedish Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Americans) in the [United States](3434750.md) and [Canada](5042916.md).

The Swedish language is one of the official languages of [Finland](10577.md) and the [Finland-Swedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland-Swedes) – having Finnish national identity – are an officially recognized linguistic minority.[^cite_note-31]

## Etymology

Further information: [Name of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Sweden)

The English term "Swede" has been attested in English since the late 16th century and is of [Middle Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Dutch) or Middle [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) origin.[^cite_note-32] In [Swedish](26689.md), the term is *svensk*, which is from the name of *[svear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svear)* (or Swedes), the people who inhabited [Svealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svealand) in eastern central [Sweden](5058739.md),[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] and were listed as *Suiones* in [Tacitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacitus)' history *[Germania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_\(book\))* from the first century AD. The term is believed to have been derived from the [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language) [reflexive pronominal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_pronoun) root, \**s(w)e*, as the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *suus*. The word may have meant "one's own (tribesmen)". The same root and original meaning is found in the ethnonym of the Germanic tribe [Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi), preserved to this day in the name [Swabia](616237.md).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-pokorny-36][^cite_note-37]

### Origins

Like other [Scandinavians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_peoples), Swedes are considered to have descended from [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans) [Battle Axe culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Axe_culture) and also the [Pitted Ware culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitted_Ware_culture).[^cite_note-38] Prior to the first century AD there is no written evidence and [historiography is based solely on various forms of archeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_Northern_Europe). The [Proto-Germanic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language) is thought to have originated in the arrival of the Battle Axe culture in Scandinavia[^cite_note-39] and the Germanic tribal societies of Scandinavia were thereafter surprisingly stable for thousands of years.[^cite_note-40] The merger of the Battle Axe and Pitted Ware cultures eventually gave rise to the [Nordic Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Bronze_Age) which was followed by the [Pre-Roman Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Roman_Iron_Age). Like other [North Germanic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_peoples), [Swedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedes_\(tribe\)) likely emerged as a distinct ethnic group during this time.[^cite_note-41]



Stone slab from [The King's Grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King's_Grave) in southern Sweden, [Nordic Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Bronze_Age), 1400 BC

Swedes enters written proto-history with the *[Germania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_\(book\))* of [Tacitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacitus) in 98 AD. In [Germania 44, 45](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Germania#XLIV) he mentions the Swedes (*[Suiones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedes_\(tribe\))*) as a powerful tribe (*distinguished not merely for their arms and men, but for their powerful fleets*) with ships that had a [prow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prow) in both ends ([longships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longship)). Which kings (*kuningaz*) ruled these Suiones is unknown, but [Norse mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_mythology) presents a long line of legendary and semi-legendary kings going back to the last centuries BC. As for literacy in Sweden itself, the [runic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runic_alphabet) was in use among the south Scandinavian elite by at least the second century AD, but all that has survived from the Roman Period is curt inscriptions on artefacts, mainly of male names, demonstrating that the people of south Scandinavia spoke [Proto-Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Norse) at the time, a language ancestral to Swedish and other [North Germanic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_languages).

### Migration Age and Vendel Period

The migration age in Sweden is marked by the [extreme weather events of 535–536](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather_events_of_535%E2%80%93536) which is believed to have shaken Scandinavian society to its core. As much as 50% of the population of Scandinavia is thought to have died as a result and the emerging [Vendel Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendel_Period) shows an increased militarization of society.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Several areas with rich burial gifts have been found, including well-preserved boat inhumation graves at [Vendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendel) and [Valsgärde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valsg%C3%A4rde), and tumuli at [Gamla Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamla_Uppsala). These were used for several generations. Some of the riches were probably acquired through the control of mining districts and the production of iron. The rulers had troops of mounted elite warriors with costly armour. Graves of mounted warriors have been found with stirrups and saddle ornaments of birds of prey in gilded bronze with encrusted garnets. The [Sutton Hoo helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Hoo_helmet) very similar to helmets in Gamla Uppsala, Vendel and Valsgärde shows that the [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) elite had extensive contacts with Swedish elite.[^cite_note-44]



The Vendel I helmet, at the [Swedish Museum of National Antiquities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Museum_of_National_Antiquities).

In the sixth century [Jordanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes) named two tribes, which he calls the *Suehans* and the *Suetidi*, who lived in [Scandza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandza). The Suehans, he says, have very fine horses just as the Thyringi tribe (*alia vero gens ibi moratur Suehans, quae velud Thyringi equis utuntur eximiis*). The Icelander [Snorri Sturluson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snorri_Sturluson) (1179–1241) wrote of the sixth-century Swedish king [Adils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adils) (Eadgils) that he had the finest horses of his days. The Suehans supplied black fox-skins for the Roman market. Then Jordanes names the *Suetidi* which is considered to be the Latin form of *[Svitjod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svitjod)*. He writes that the Suetidi are the tallest of men—together with the [Dani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes_\(tribe\)), who were of the same stock. Later he mentions other Scandinavian tribes as being of the same height.

Originating in semi-legendary Scandza (believed to be somewhere in modern [Götaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6taland), Sweden), a [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) population had crossed the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) before the second century AD. They reaching [Scythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythia) on the coast of the [Black Sea](3386.md) in modern Ukraine, where Goths left their archaeological traces in the [Chernyakhov culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernyakhov_culture). In the fifth and sixth centuries, they became divided as the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) and the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths), and established powerful successor-states of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) and Italy respectively.[^cite_note-45] [Crimean Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Gothic) communities appear to have survived intact in the [Crimea](163045.md) until the late-18th century.[^cite_note-46]

&#x20;The Swedish [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age) lasted roughly between the eighth and 11th centuries. During this period, it is believed that the Swedes expanded from eastern Sweden and incorporated the [Geats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geats) to the south.[^cite_note-47] It is believed that Swedish Vikings and [Gutar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutar) mainly travelled east and south, going to Finland, the Baltic countries, Russia, [Belarus](3457.md), Ukraine the [Black Sea](3386.md) and further as far as [Baghdad](4492.md). Their routes passed [through the Dnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trade_Route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks) down south to [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), on which they did numerous raids. The [Byzantine Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor) [Theophilos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophilos_\(emperor\)) noticed their great skills in war and invited them to serve as his personal bodyguard, known as the [varangian guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangian_guard). The Swedish Vikings, called "[Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rus_\(people\))" are also believed to be the founding fathers of [Kievan Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus). The Arabic traveller [Ibn Fadlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Fadlan) described these Vikings as following:&#x20;

> I have seen the Rus as they came on their merchant journeys and encamped by the [Itil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga). I have never seen more perfect physical specimens, tall as date palms, blond and ruddy; they wear neither tunics nor caftans, but the men wear a garment which covers one side of the body and leaves a hand free. Each man has an axe, a sword, and a knife, and keeps each by him at all times. The swords are broad and grooved, of Frankish sort.

— [^cite_note-48]

&#x20;The adventures of these Swedish Vikings are commemorated on many [runestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runestone) in Sweden, such as the [Greece Runestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_Runestones) and the [Varangian Runestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangian_Runestones). There was also considerable participation in expeditions westwards, which are commemorated on stones such as the [England Runestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_Runestones). The last major Swedish Viking expedition appears to have been the ill-fated expedition of [Ingvar the Far-Travelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingvar_the_Far-Travelled) to [Serkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serkland), the region south-east of the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). Its members are commemorated on the [Ingvar Runestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingvar_Runestones), none of which mentions any survivor. What happened to the crew is unknown, but it is believed that they died of sickness.

#### Kingdom of Sweden

It is not known when and how the 'kingdom of Sweden' was born, but the [list of Swedish monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swedish_monarchs) is drawn from the first kings who ruled both [Svealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svealand) (Sweden) and [Götaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6taland) (Gothia) as one province with [Erik the Victorious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Segers%C3%A4ll). Sweden and Gothia were two separate nations long before that into antiquity. It is not known how long they existed, but *[Beowulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beowulf)* described semi-legendary [Swedish-Geatish wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish-Geatish_wars) in the sixth century.

#### Cultural advances

During the early stages of the Viking Age, a centre of trade in northern Europe developed at [Birka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birka) on the island of [Björkö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rk%C3%B6,_Eker%C3%B6), not far from where Stockholm was later constructed, in mid-latitude Sweden. Birka was founded around 750 AD as a trading port by a king or merchants trying to control trade.[^cite_note-\:0-49] Birka was the [Baltic](3335.md) link in the [Dnieper Trade Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_trade_route) through [Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga) (*Aldeigja*) and [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Land) (*Holmsgard*) to the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate).[^cite_note-50] It was abandoned c. AD 975, around the same time [Sigtuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigtuna) was founded as a Christian town some 35 km to the northeast. It has been estimated that the population in Viking Age Birka was between 500 and 1000 people.[^cite_note-\:0-49] Archaeological finds indicate that Birka was still wealthy in the 9th and 10th centuries. Thousands of graves, coins, jewelry and other luxury items have been found there.[^cite_note-51] [St. Ansgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Ansgar) is usually credited for introducing Christianity in 829, but the new religion did not begin to fully replace [paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism) until the 12th century. During the 11th century, Christianity became the most prevalent religion, and from 1050 Sweden is counted as a Christian nation. The period between 1100 and 1400 was characterized by internal power struggles and competition among the Nordic kingdoms. Swedish kings also began to expand the Swedish-controlled territory in Finland, creating conflicts with the Rus who no longer had any connection with Sweden.[^cite_note-52]

#### Feudal institutions in Sweden

Except for the province of [Skåne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C3%A5ne), on the southernmost tip of Sweden which was under Danish control during this time, [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism) never developed in Sweden as it did in the rest of Europe.[^cite_note-53] Therefore, the peasantry remained largely a class of free farmers throughout most of Swedish history. [Slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) (also called [thralldom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrall)) was not common in Sweden,[^cite_note-54] and what slavery there was tended to be driven out of existence by the spread of Christianity, the difficulty in obtaining slaves from the lands east of the Baltic Sea, and by the development of cities before the 16th century[^cite_note-55] Indeed, both slavery and [serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom) were abolished altogether by a decree of King [Magnus IV Eriksson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_IV_Eriksson) in 1335. Former slaves tended to be absorbed into the peasantry and some became laborers in the towns. Still, Sweden remained a poor and economically backward country in which barter was the means of exchange. For instance, the farmers of the province of [Dalsland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalsland) would transport their butter to the mining districts of Sweden and exchange it there for iron, which they would then take down to the coast and trade the iron for fish they needed for food while the iron would be shipped abroad.[^cite_note-56]

#### Late Middle Ages



[Gustav Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Vasa) addressing men from Dalarna in Mora. Idealized depiction by [Johan Gustaf Sandberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Gustaf_Sandberg), 1836

In 1397, Sweden joined Norway and Denmark to form the Scandinavian [Kalmar Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union).[^cite_note-57] The union monarch [Christian II of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_II_of_Denmark) ordered a massacre in 1520 of Swedish nobles at Stockholm. This came to be known as the "[Stockholm blood bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_blood_bath)" and stirred the Swedish nobility to [new resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_War_of_Liberation) and, on 6 June (now Sweden's national holiday) in 1523, they made [Gustav Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Vasa) their king.[^cite_note-58] This is sometimes considered as the [foundation of modern Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_of_modern_Sweden). Shortly afterwards he rejected [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) and led Sweden into the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation). Economically, Gustav Vasa broke the monopoly of the Hanseatic League over Swedish Baltic Sea trade.[^cite_note-59]

The Hanseatic League had been officially formed at [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck) on [the sea coast](3335.md) of [Northern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1356. The Hanseatic League sought civil and [commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce) privileges from the princes and royalty of the countries and cities along the coasts of the Baltic Sea.[^cite_note-60] In exchange they offered a certain amount of protection. Having their own navy the Hansa were able to sweep the Baltic Sea free of pirates.[^cite_note-61] The privileges obtained by the Hansa included assurances that only Hansa citizens would be allowed to trade from the ports where they were located. They also sought agreement to be free of all customs and taxes. With these concessions, Lübeck merchants flocked to Stockholm, Sweden and soon came to dominate the economic life of that city and made the port city of Stockholm into the leading commercial and industrial city of Sweden.[^cite_note-62] Under the Hanseatic trade two-thirds of Stockholm's imports consisted of textiles and one third of salt. Exports from Sweden consisted of iron and copper.[^cite_note-63]

However, the Swedes began to resent the monopoly trading position of the Hansa (mostly German citizens) and to resent the income they felt they lost to the Hansa. Consequently, when Gustav Vasa or [Gustav I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_I) broke the monopoly power of the Hanseatic League he was regarded as a hero to the Swedish people. History now views Gustav I as the father of the modern Swedish nation. The foundations laid by Gustav would take time to develop. Furthermore, when Sweden became more developed, after breaking the trade monopoly of the Hanseatic League, and entered its golden era, the fact that the peasantry had traditionally been free meant that more of the economic benefits flowed back to them rather than going to a feudal landowning class.[^cite_note-64] This was not the case in other countries of Europe like Poland where the peasantry was still bound by serfdom and a strong feudalistic land owning system.

### Swedish Empire



The [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) between 1560 and 1815

See also: [History of Sweden (1611–1648)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sweden_\(1611%E2%80%931648\)), [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire), [Swedish overseas colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_overseas_colonies), [Sweden and the Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_and_the_Great_Northern_War), [Absolute Monarchy in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_Monarchy_in_Sweden), and [Union between Sweden and Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway)

During the 17th century Sweden emerged as a European [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Power). Before the emergence of the Swedish Empire, Sweden was a very poor and scarcely populated country on the fringe of European civilization, with no significant power or reputation. Sweden rose to prominence on a continental scale during the tenure of king [Gustavus Adolphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus_Adolphus_of_Sweden), seizing territories from Russia and [Poland–Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) in multiple conflicts, including the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War).

During the Thirty Years' War, Sweden conquered approximately half of the Holy Roman states. Gustav Adolphus planned to become the new [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor), ruling over a united Scandinavia and the Holy Roman states, but he died at the [Battle of Lützen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_L%C3%BCtzen_\(1632\)) in 1632. After the [Battle of Nördlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_N%C3%B6rdlingen_\(1634\)), Sweden's only significant military defeat of the war, pro-Swedish sentiment among the German states faded. These German provinces excluded themselves from Swedish power one by one, leaving Sweden with only a few northern German territories: [Swedish Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Pomerania), [Bremen-Verden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen-Verden) and [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar). The Swedish armies may have destroyed up to 2,000 castles, 18,000 villages and 1,500 towns in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_history_of_Germany), one-third of all German towns.[^cite_note-Population-HLS-65]

In the middle of the 17th century Sweden was the third largest country in Europe by land area, only surpassed by Russia and Spain. Sweden reached its largest territorial extent under the rule of [Charles X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_X_of_Sweden) after the [treaty of Roskilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Roskilde) in 1658.[^cite_note-HayesPSH-66][^cite_note-67] The foundation of Sweden's success during this period is credited to [Gustav I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_I)'s major changes on the Swedish economy in the 16th century, and his introduction of [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism).[^cite_note-GusEB-68] In the 17th century, Sweden was engaged in many wars, for example with Poland–Lithuania, with both sides competing for territories of today's [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states). The Polish–Swedish War (1626–1629) ended with a ceasefire in [Stary Targ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stary_Targ) ([Truce of Altmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truce_of_Altmark)) on 26 September 1629 that was in favour of the Swedes, to whom Poland ceded the larger part of [Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonia) together with its important port of [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga). The Swedes later conducted a series of invasions into the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, known as the [Deluge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deluge_\(history\)).[^cite_note-69]

One-third of the Finnish population died in the devastating [famine](51329.md) that struck the country in 1696.[^cite_note-70] Famine also hit Sweden, killing roughly 10% of Sweden's population.[^cite_note-71] After more than half a century of almost constant warfare, the Swedish economy had deteriorated. It became the lifetime task of Charles' son, [Charles XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_XI_of_Sweden), to rebuild the economy and refit the army. His legacy to his son, the coming ruler of Sweden [Charles XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_XII_of_Sweden), was one of the finest arsenals in the world, a large standing army and a great fleet. Sweden's largest threat at this time, Russia, had a larger army but was far behind in both equipment and training.



Death of [Gustav II Adolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_II_Adolf) at the [Battle of Lützen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_L%C3%BCtzen_\(1632\))

After the [Battle of Narva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Narva_\(1700\)) in 1700, one of the first battles of the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War), the Russian army was so severely decimated that Sweden had an open chance to invade Russia. However, Charles did not pursue the Russian army, instead turning against Poland–Lithuania and defeating the Polish king [Augustus II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_II) and his Saxon allies at the [Battle of Kliszow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kliszow) in 1702. This gave Russia time to rebuild and modernize its army.

After the success of invading Poland, Charles decided to make an invasion attempt of Russia which ended in a decisive Russian victory at the [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) in 1709. After a long march exposed to [cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack) raids, Russian Tsar [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great)'s [scorched-earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched-earth) techniques and [the extremely cold winter of 1709](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Frost_of_1709), the Swedes stood weakened with a shattered morale and enormously outnumbered against the Russian army at Poltava. The defeat meant the beginning of the end for the Swedish Empire.



The [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) in 1709. In the years following Poltava, Russia occupied all the Swedish annexations on the Baltic coast and even Finland.

Charles XII attempted to invade Norway 1716; however, he was shot dead at [Fredriksten fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredriksten) in 1718. The Swedes were not militarily defeated at Fredriksten, but the whole structure and organization of the Norwegian campaign fell apart with the king's death, and the army withdrew.

Forced to cede large areas of land in the [Treaty of Nystad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nystad) in 1721, Sweden also lost its place as an empire and as the dominant state on the Baltic Sea. With Sweden's lost influence, Russia emerged as an empire and became one of Europe's dominant nations. As the war finally ended in 1721, Sweden had lost an estimated 200,000 men, 150,000 of those from the area of present-day Sweden and 50,000 from the [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Finland#17th_century) part of Sweden.[^cite_note-72]

In the 18th century, Sweden did not have enough resources to maintain its territories outside Scandinavia, and most of them were lost, culminating with the 1809 loss of eastern Sweden to Russia which became the highly autonomous [Grand Duchy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland) in [Imperial Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia).

In interest of reestablishing Swedish dominance in the Baltic Sea, Sweden allied itself against its traditional ally and benefactor, France, in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars). Sweden's role in the [Battle of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leipzig) gave it the authority to force Denmark-Norway, an ally of France, to cede Norway to the King of Sweden on 14 January 1814 in exchange for northern German provinces, at the [Treaty of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kiel). The Norwegian attempts to keep their status as a sovereign state were rejected by the Swedish king, [Charles XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_XIII). He launched a military campaign against Norway on 27 July 1814, ending in the [Convention of Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Moss), which forced Norway into a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway) with Sweden under the Swedish crown, which lasted until 1905. The 1814 campaign was the last war in which Sweden participated as a combatant.[^cite_note-Local_2014-73]

### Modern history

See also: [Modernization of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernization_of_Sweden) and [Swedish emigration to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_emigration_to_the_United_States)



Swedish emigrants boarding ship in [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg) in 1905

There was a significant population increase during the 18th and 19th centuries, which the writer [Esaias Tegnér](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esaias_Tegn%C3%A9r) in 1833 attributed to "peace, [vaccine](32653.md), and [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potatoes)".[^cite_note-MagocsiOntario1999-74] Between 1750 and 1850, the population in Sweden doubled. Sweden was hit by the last natural caused famine in Europe, the [Famine of 1867-69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_famine_of_1867-1869) killed thousands in Sweden. According to some scholars, mass emigration to America became the only way to prevent famine and rebellion; over 1% of the population emigrated annually during the 1880s.[^cite_note-Einhorn-75] Nevertheless, Sweden remained poor, retaining a nearly entirely agricultural economy even as Denmark and Western European countries began to industrialize.[^cite_note-Einhorn-75][^cite_note-76]

Many looked towards America for a better life during this time. It is believed that between 1850 and 1910 more than one million Swedes moved to the United States.[^cite_note-77] In the early 20th century, more Swedes lived in [Chicago](6886.md) than in [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg) (Sweden's second largest city).[^cite_note-78] Most Swedish immigrants moved to the [Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States), with a large population in [Minnesota](19590.md), with a few others moving to other parts of the United States and Canada.

Despite the slow rate of industrialization into the 19th century, many important changes were taking place in the agrarian economy because of innovations and the large population growth.[^cite_note-Koblik9-10-79] These innovations included government-sponsored programs of [enclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclosure), aggressive exploitation of agricultural lands, and the introduction of new crops such as the potato.[^cite_note-Koblik9-10-79] Because the Swedish peasantry had never been enserfed as elsewhere in Europe,[^cite_note-80] the Swedish farming culture began to take on a critical role in the Swedish political process, which has continued through modern times with modern Agrarian party (now called the Centre Party).[^cite_note-81] Between 1870 and 1914, Sweden began developing the industrialized economy that exists today.[^cite_note-82]

Strong grassroots movements sprung up in Sweden during the latter half of the 19th century ([trade unions](17626.md), [temperance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperance_movement) groups, and independent religious groups), creating a strong foundation of democratic principles. In 1889 The Swedish Social Democratic Party was founded. These movements precipitated Sweden's migration into a modern parliamentary democracy, achieved by the time of World War I. As the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) progressed during the 20th century, people gradually began moving into cities to work in factories and became involved in [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) unions. A communist revolution was avoided in 1917, following the re-introduction of [parliamentarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentarism), and the country saw comprehensive [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization) reforms under the joint Liberal-Social Democrat cabinet of [Nils Edén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nils_Ed%C3%A9n) and [Hjalmar Branting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Branting), with universal and equal suffrage to both houses of parliament enacted for men in 1918 and for women in 1919. The reforms were widely accepted by King [Gustaf V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_V_of_Sweden), who had previously ousted [Karl Staaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Staaff)'s elected Liberal government in the [Courtyard Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard_Crisis) because of differences in defence policy.

### World Wars

See also: [Sweden during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_during_World_War_II)



Swedish soldier during World War II

Sweden remained officially neutral during World War I and most of World War II, with the exception of the [Winter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War) when it declared itself [non-belligerant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-belligerant).[^cite_note-83] It's neutrality during the rest of the war has also been disputed.[^cite_note-Koblik303-313-84][^cite_note-85] Sweden was under German influence for much of the war, as ties to the rest of the world were cut off through blockades.[^cite_note-Koblik303-313-84] The Swedish government felt that it was in no position to openly contest Germany,[^cite_note-NB313-319-86] and therefore made some concessions.[^cite_note-87] Sweden also supplied steel and machined parts to Germany throughout the war. However, Sweden supported Norwegian resistance, and in 1943 helped rescue Danish Jews from deportation to [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps).[^cite_note-p433-88] Sweden also supported Finland in the [Winter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War) with volunteers and [materiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materiel).[^cite_note-r538-89]

Toward the end of the war, Sweden began to play a role in humanitarian efforts and many refugees, among them many Jews from Nazi-occupied Europe, were saved partly because of the Swedish involvement in rescue missions at the internment camps and partly because Sweden served as a haven for refugees, primarily from the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries) and the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states).[^cite_note-NB313-319-86] Nevertheless, internal and external critics have argued that Sweden could have done more to resist the Nazi war effort, even if risking occupation although doing so would likely have resulted in even greater number of casualties and prevented many humanitarian efforts.[^cite_note-NB313-319-86]

### Post-war era

Sweden was officially a neutral country and remained outside [NATO](21133.md) or [Warsaw pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_pact) membership during the [Cold War](325329.md), but privately Sweden's leadership had strong ties with the United States and other western governments.

Following the war, Sweden took advantage of an intact industrial base, social stability and its natural resources to expand its industry to supply the rebuilding of Europe.[^cite_note-NB335-339-90] Sweden was part of the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan) and participated in the Organization of Economic Co-operation and Development ([OECD](33853117.md)). During most of the post-war era, the country was governed by the [Swedish Social Democratic Party](202978.md) largely in cooperation with [trade unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Trade_Union_Confederation) and industry. The government actively pursued an internationally competitive manufacturing sector of primarily large corporations.[^cite_note-svensteinmo-91]



Sweden joined the European Union in 1995 and signed the [Lisbon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Treaty) in 2007.

Sweden, like countries around the globe, entered a period of economic decline and upheaval, following the oil embargoes of 1973–74 and 1978–79.[^cite_note-92] In the 1980s pillars of Swedish industry were massively restructured. Shipbuilding was discontinued, wood pulp was integrated into modernized paper production, the steel industry was concentrated and specialized, and mechanical engineering was robotized.[^cite_note-93]

Between 1970 and 1990 the overall tax burden rose by over 10%, and the growth was low compared to other countries in Western Europe. The marginal income tax for workers reached over 80%. Eventually government spent over half of the country's [gross domestic product](12594.md). Sweden GDP per capita ranking declined during this time.[^cite_note-svensteinmo-91]

### Recent history

See also: [History of Sweden (1989–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sweden_\(1989%E2%80%93present\))



Swedes celebrating [Midsummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsummer) in 2010



Swedish national costumes

A bursting [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) bubble caused by inadequate controls on lending combined with an international recession and a policy switch from anti-unemployment policies to anti-inflationary policies resulted in a fiscal crisis in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-94] Sweden's GDP declined by around 5%. In 1992, there was a run on the currency, with the central bank briefly increasing interest to 500%.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

The response of the government was to cut spending and institute a multitude of reforms to improve Sweden's competitiveness, among them reducing the [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) and [privatising](24661.md) public services and goods. Much of the political establishment promoted EU membership, and the Swedish referendum passed with 52% in favour of joining the EU on 13 November 1994. Sweden joined the [European Union](9317.md) on 1 January 1995.

After over 200 years of neutrality, Sweden joined NATO on 7 March 2024, in response to the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-l861-97] Sweden has had extensive cooperation with other European countries in the area of defence technology and defence industry. Among others, Swedish companies export weapons that are used by the American military in Iraq.[^cite_note-98] Sweden also has a long history of participating in international military operations, including most recently, [Afghanistan](737.md), where Swedish troops are under NATO command, and in EU sponsored peacekeeping operations in [Kosovo](17391.md), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), and [Cyprus](5593.md). Sweden held the chair of the [European Union](9317.md) from 1 July to 31 December 2009.

The native language of nearly all Swedes is [Swedish](26689.md) (*svenska* [\[ˈsvɛ̂nːska\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e3/Sv-svenska.ogg/Sv-svenska.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e3/Sv-svenska.ogg/Sv-svenska.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) a [North Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_languages) language, spoken by approximately 10 million people,[^cite_note-speakers-99] predominantly in Sweden and parts of Finland, especially along its coast and on the [Åland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85land) islands. It is, to a considerable extent, [mutually intelligible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutually_intelligible_languages) with [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language) and to a lesser extent with spoken [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language) (see especially "[Classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language#Classification)"). Along with the other North Germanic languages, Swedish is a descendant of [Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse), the common language of the [Germanic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) living in Scandinavia during the [Viking Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age). It is the largest of the North Germanic languages by numbers of speakers.

[Standard Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Swedish), used by most Swedish people, is the national language that evolved from the Central Swedish dialects in the 19th century and was well established by the beginning of the 20th century. While distinct regional [varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(linguistics\)) descended from the older rural [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect) still exist, the spoken and [written language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Written_language) is uniform and standardized. Some dialects differ considerably from the standard language in [grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar) and [vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocabulary) and are not always mutually intelligible with Standard Swedish. These dialects are confined to rural areas and are spoken primarily by small numbers of people with low [social mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_mobility). Though not facing imminent [extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct_language), such dialects have been in decline during the past century, despite the fact that they are well researched and their use is often encouraged by local authorities.

## Genetics

According to recent genetic analysis, both mtDNA and Y chromosome polymorphisms showed a noticeable [genetic affinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_affinity) between Swedes and other [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) ethnic groups.[^cite_note-hpgl.stanford.edu-100]

[Paternally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrilineage), through their [Y-DNA haplogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Y-chromosome_DNA_haplogroups), the Swedes are quite diverse and show strongly of [Haplogroup I1d1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I1_\(Y-DNA\)) at over 40% of the population tested in different studies, followed by [R1a1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1a) and [R1b1a2a1a1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1b) with over 20% each and [haplogroup N1c1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_N_\(Y-DNA\)) with over 5% at different regional variance. The rest are within haplogroups [J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J_\(Y-DNA\)) and [E1b1b1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E3b) and other less common ones.[^cite_note-auto-101]

[Maternally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilineage), through their [mtDNA haplogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mitochondrial_DNA_haplogroup), the Swedes show very strongly of [haplogroup H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_H_\(mtDNA\)) at 25–30%, followed by [haplogroup U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_U_\(mtDNA\)) at a 10% or more, with [haplogroup J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J_\(mtDNA\)) and [T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_T_\(mtDNA\)), [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_K_\(mtDNA\)) at around 5% each.

Among Swedes, there are regional differences. The clearest difference is between northern and southern Swedes, but the differences are more clinal than sharp when looking at the entire population.[^cite_note-102] This population structure of Swedes is largely explained by the mixing of Swedes with Finno-Ugric people in the northern parts of the country.[^cite_note-103]

The largest area inhabited by Swedes, as well as the earliest known original area inhabited by their linguistic ancestors, is in the country of Sweden, situated on the eastern side of the [Scandinavian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Peninsula) and the islands adjacent to it, situated west of the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) in [northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe). The Swedish-speaking people living in near-coastal areas on the north-eastern and eastern side of the Baltic Sea also have a long history of continuous settlement, which in some of these areas possibly started about a [millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium) ago. These people include the Swedish-speakers in [mainland Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Finland) – speaking a Swedish dialect commonly referred to as [Finland Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_Swedish) (finlandssvenska which is part of the East-Swedish dialect group) and the almost exclusively Swedish-speaking population of [Åland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85land) speaking in a manner closer to the adjacent dialects in Sweden than to adjacent dialects of Finland Swedish. [Estonia](28222445.md) also had an important [Swedish minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Estonians) which persisted for about 650 years on [the coast and isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiboland). Smaller groups of historical descendants of 18th–20th-century Swedish emigrants who still retain varying aspects of Swedish identity to this day can be found in the [Americas](29833.md) (especially [Minnesota](19590.md) and [Wisconsin](33127.md); see [Swedish Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Americans)) and in Ukraine.

Currently, Swedes tend to emigrate mostly to the Nordic neighbour countries (Norway, Denmark, Finland), English speaking countries (United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, New Zealand), Spain, France and Germany.[^cite_note-104]

Historically, the Kingdom of Sweden had a much larger territory, especially during "The Era of Great Power" ([Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire)) in 1611–1718. Finland was a part of Sweden until 1809.[^cite_note-Svenskfinland_2019-105] Since there was no separate Finnish nationality at those times, it is not unusual that sources predating 1809 refer both to Swedes and Finns as "Swedes". This is particularly the case with [New Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Sweden), where some of the Swedish settlers were of Finnish origin.

Questionnaire surveys of Swedish embassies conducted by Swedes Worldwide, a non-profit organization, have shown a steady growth of Swedes living outside Sweden. The 2022 survey indicates an approximate 685,000 Swedes living abroad, up from 660,000 in 2015 and 546,000 in 2011.[^cite_note-SVIV2022-106]
