The **Bosnia and Herzegovina national football team**[^cite_note-3] represents [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) competitions. It is governed by the [Football Association of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) following country's independence from [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) in 1992.

The team played its first official international match on 30th November 1995 against [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_national_football_team) in [Tirana](31075.md), coming just nine days after the signing of the [General Framework Agreement for Peace in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement).

Bosnia and Herzegovina has reached two major tournaments in its history to date; the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), where they were eliminated in the group stage and earned their first win over [Iran](272865.md), and most recently in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), where the team advanced to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Bosnia and Herzegovina is currently the only national team in the world to have qualified twice for the World Cup while failing to ever qualify for its [continental championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship), joining the former [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) as one of only two national teams in the world to have appeared in a World Cup without ever competing in their continental championship.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

The team's highest [FIFA World Ranking](719095.md) was 13th, which they achieved in August 2013.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## History

Main article: [History of the Bosnia and Herzegovina national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_national_football_team)

From the [creation of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_of_Yugoslavia) in 1918 to the [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia) in 1992, players from the modern-day [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) lined up for [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team), but following their [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Bosnian_independence_referendum) and devastating [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War), a new football nation arose from the ashes.

Following acceptance into [FIFA](11049.md), Bosnia and Herzegovina entered [1998 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_1) to compete for a place in a major competition. Bosnia and Herzegovina finished fourth in a group that included [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_national_football_team), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) (reached [Quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Quarter-finals)), [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) ([finishing third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Match_for_third_place)) and [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] During [2002 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_7), Bosnia and Herzegovina also finished in fourth in a group with [Spain](609825.md) (eventual tournament [Quarter-finalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup#Quarter-finals)).

This early period was followed by Bosnia and Herzegovina coming very close to [qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_qualifying_Group_2) directly for their first major competition, [UEFA Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004), narrowly missing out by a single goal against Denmark (needing a victory to top the group).[^cite_note-Uefa.com-12] Bosnia and Herzegovina failed to make the grade in the [2006 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_7), despite being unbeaten at home (drawing twice with Spain). The national team then experienced double heartbreak, bowing out twice in the playoffs to [Portugal](576328.md). First 2–0 on aggregate in the [2010 FIFA World Cup play-offs decider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round) (having been grouped with [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) champion and eventual [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) winners Spain during qualifying phase) and then 6–2 on aggregate in the [UEFA Euro 2012 play-offs decider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualifying_play-offs) after finishing second [by a point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualifying_Group_D) to [France](250197.md).[^cite_note-Spain_-_Bosnia-Herzegovina_Match_Report_WC_Q_2006-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### 2014 FIFA World Cup

See also: [2014 FIFA World Cup Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F)

Bosnia and Herzegovina qualified for the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), their first major tournament, by beating [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_national_football_team) in October 2013 during [UEFA qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_G) that also included [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_national_football_team) and [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] At the tournament, Bosnia and Herzegovina finished third in a group which included [Argentina](454699.md) and won their first match 3–1 over Iran.[^cite_note-20] [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) ([2013 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) champion) pipped them for second place in the group with a 1–0 win marred with controversy following an incorrectly-disallowed goal by [Edin Džeko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edin_D%C5%BEeko) in the first half.[^cite_note-21]

In the [UEFA Euro 2016 qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying_Group_B), Bosnia and Herzegovina finished third behind [Belgium](679716.md) and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team). After a bad start to the qualifiers with a surprise 2–1 defeat at home against [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_national_football_team) and managing just two points in four games, [Safet Sušić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safet_Su%C5%A1i%C4%87) was dismissed and replaced by [Mehmed Baždarević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Ba%C5%BEdarevi%C4%87).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] With five wins in their remaining six matches, including victories over Wales and Israel along with three clean sheets Bosnia and Herzegovina ultimately failed to qualify after a [two legged playoff encounter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying_play-offs) with the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Bosnia and Herzegovina failed to make back-to-back FIFA World Cups after failing to [qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_H) to the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md).

Bosnia and Herzegovina won their [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League_B#Group_3) group and guaranteed a [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying_play-offs#Path_B) berth for [UEFA Euro 2020 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying). Still, they finished fourth in their [UEFA Euro 2020 qualifying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying_Group_J) with the manager [Robert Prosinečki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Prosine%C4%8Dki), missing out on direct qualification and entered the playoffs,[^cite_note-28] and with new manager [Dušan Bajević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Bajevi%C4%87), they ultimately missed out on yet another Euro after losing to Northern Ireland on [penalties](511302.md).[^cite_note-29] They finished bottom of Group 1 in the [2020–21 UEFA Nations League A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Nations_League_A), thus relegating them to the [2022–23 UEFA Nations League B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Nations_League_B).



Bosnia and Herzegovina's squad in an international friendly against [Austria](695226.md) in 2015

Following several years of disappointment, Bulgarian [Ivaylo Petev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivaylo_Petev) was named as Bosnia and Herzegovina's new manager, marking the first time a Bosnia and Herzegovina manager was not from the [former Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Yugoslavia).[^cite_note-30] In their first campaign under Petev, Bosnia and Herzegovina were drawn into a group with [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) champions [France](250197.md) and kept their chances of qualification alive until the final two matches; ultimately failing to qualify for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md). He also led the team to winning Group 3 of the [2022–23 UEFA Nations League B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Nations_League_B), thus earning promotion back to [2024–25 UEFA Nations League A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League_A) and guaranteed a play-off berth for the [UEFA Euro 2024 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying).[^cite_note-31] They suffered disappointment in their [UEFA Euro 2024 qualifying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying_Group_J) that saw three managers: [Faruk Hadžibegić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faruk_Had%C5%BEibegi%C4%87),[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] [Meho Kodro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meho_Kodro)[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] and [Savo Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savo_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87) pick up only one win each for a total of 9 points and entered the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying_play-offs#Path_B). Bosnia and Herzegovina failed to qualify for [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) after conceding two late goals, losing 2–1 to [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team).[^cite_note-36]

### 2026 FIFA World Cup

In April 2024, former national team captain [Sergej Barbarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergej_Barbarez) was appointed manager.[^cite_note-37] He debuted in friendlies against [England](9904.md) and [Italy](362466.md). After relegation from the [2024–25 UEFA Nations League A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League_A) (posting their worst loss in history to [Germany](250204.md)), Bosnia and Herzegovina played well in their [2026 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_H). Following their victory over [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team) at home on 15 November,[^cite_note-38] Barbarez's side needed to defeat [Austria](695226.md) in the final group match to qualify directly for the [World Cup](36581929.md). Despite holding on to a 1–0 lead until the 77th minute, the match ended in a draw. As a result, Barbarez's team finished second in the group, two points behind Austria, securing a spot in the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round#Path_A).[^cite_note-39] In the playoffs, they defeated both Wales and Italy on penalties, qualifying for the 2026 World Cup and marking their second appearance as an independent nation.[^cite_note-auto-40]

## Team image

### Nickname

A popular nickname of all Bosnian national teams is "Zmajevi" (the Dragons),[^cite_note-46] in reference to the military commander [Husein Gradaščević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husein_Grada%C5%A1%C4%8Devi%C4%87), the "Dragon of Bosnia", who led an [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_uprising_\(1831%E2%80%931832\)) against the Ottoman Empire in the 19th century.[^cite_note-47] The term was originally popularised by football TV commentator [Mustafa Mijajlović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Mijajlovi%C4%87) during the [Belgium](679716.md) vs. Bosnia and Herzegovina (2–4) [2010 WC qualification game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_5) on 28 March 2009.

In both local and foreign media, they are sometimes referred to as the *Golden Lilies*, which was the original nickname given to all of the country's national teams by fans after [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Bosnian_independence_referendum), in reference to the official state insignia at the time (the [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and the [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)), which itself referred to the [golden lily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Lily_\(Bosnia_and_Herzegovina\)), the historical state insignia that was featured on the coat of arms of the ruling Bosnian medieval [Kotromanić dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotromani%C4%87_dynasty).[^cite_note-Golden_Lilies_blossoming-48][^cite_note-49]

### Home stadiums

Currently, the national team uses [Bilino Polje Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilino_Polje_Stadium) as its main home ground.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The stadium has a capacity of 15,292.[^cite_note-\:0-57] The stadium has a grass surface.[^cite_note-\:0-57] There are plans for a new stadium at the same site.[^cite_note-58]

Other stadiums Bosnia and Herzegovina has used are [Grbavica Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Grbavica) and [Koševo City Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%A1evo_City_Stadium).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

Its training ground for domestic matches is the [Bosnia and Herzegovina FA Training Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_FA_Training_Centre) in [Zenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenica), which was opened in 2013 in conjunction with [UEFA](32332.md).[^cite_note-63]

### Supporters

See also: [BHFanaticos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BHFanaticos)

A large number of the national team's supporters come from [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe) and [Western Europe](33800.md), [North America](21139.md), and [Australia](4689264.md),[^cite_note-64] particularly among their sizeable [Bosnian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_diaspora) communities.[^cite_note-65] Most fans are members of BHFanaticos, Belaj Boys, BHLegion, Armija Zmajeva (Dragons Army) and Ljuti Krajišnici groups.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

Bosnia and Herzegovina's traditional kit colours are blue and white, taken from the [country's flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina).

The team kit is currently produced by Spanish sports company [Kelme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelme_\(company\)).[^cite_note-KELME_NOVI_TEHNIČKI_SPONZOR_NS/FS_BIH-69] The general sponsor of the team is [m\:tel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mtel_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina).[^cite_note-70] Also sponsoring the team are [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola), ASA Osiguranje, and [Privredna banka Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privredna_banka_Sarajevo).
