Presidential elections were held in [Guinea](12176.md) on 28 December 2025. They are the first to be held in the country since the [2021 Guinean coup d'état](68651369.md) that installed general [Mamady Doumbouya](68651894.md) as president.[^cite_note-africanews-1] Doumbouya won by a landslide margin amid criticism by opposition groups and the United Nations over the conduct of the election.

## Background

The election month was announced by Prime Minister [Bah Oury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah_Oury) at the [Africa CEO Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_CEO_Forum) in [Abidjan](21371767.md), Ivory Coast on 12 May 2025.[^cite_note-africanews-1] In June 2025, the ruling military junta established the Directorate General of Elections (DGE) to oversee the election.[^cite_note-2] The election was scheduled for 28 December 2025 by a decree read by Doumbouya on national television on 27 September 2025.[^cite_note-3]

## Electoral system

Candidates were required to submit a deposit of 875 million Guinean francs (\$100,000).[^cite_note-4] Candidates were also required to be residing in Guinea and aged between 40 and 80 years.[^cite_note-africanews2-5] A runoff election is required should no candidate win a majority.[^cite_note-f24-6]

## Candidates

A total of 11 people submitted their candidacies before the Supreme Court by the deadline of candidate registration on 3 November 2025. These included:[^cite_note-africanews2-5]

- [Mamady Doumbouya](68651894.md), incumbent president.

  [^cite_note-candidates-7]

- [Lansana Kouyaté](5996620.md), former prime minister.

  [^cite_note-candidates-7]

- [Ousmane Kaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ousmane_Kaba?action=edit\&redlink=1), former minister.

  [^cite_note-candidates-7]

- [Amadou Thierno Diallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_Thierno_Diallo?action=edit\&redlink=1), former minister.

  [^cite_note-africanews3-8]

- [Ben Youssouf Keita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Youssouf_Keita?action=edit\&redlink=1), member of the national assembly.

  [^cite_note-africanews3-8]

On 8 November, the Supreme Court released a provisional list of nine candidates which included Doumbouya and [Yero Baldé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yero_Bald%C3%A9?action=edit\&redlink=1) from the [Democratic Front of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Front_of_Guinea?action=edit\&redlink=1) but excluded Kouyaté and Kaba.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-balde-10]

## Conduct

Doumbouya's campaign highlighted major infrastructure projects and reforms launched under his rule, while Yero Baldé ran on themes of anti-corruption and economic growth.[^cite_note-balde-10]

Nearly 12,000 police officers were mobilized to provide security during the election. A day before the vote, authorities said that security forces "neutralised" an armed group with "subversive intentions threatening national security" after gunshots were reported in the Sonfonia neighborhood of [Conakry](56638.md).[^cite_note-balde-10]

Several opposition groups called for a boycott of the election, with exiled opposition leader [Cellou Dalein Diallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellou_Dalein_Diallo) calling the vote "an electoral charade" aimed at giving legitimacy to "the planned confiscation of power". United Nations rights commissioner [Volker Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Turk) said the campaign was marred by severe restrictions, "intimidation of opposition actors, apparently politically motivated enforced disappearances, and constraints on media freedom" that threatened to undermine the credibility of the election.[^cite_note-11]

Following the election, opposition candidate [Faya Millimono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faya_Millimono) said the vote was marred by "systematic fraudulent practices" and restrictions on observers monitoring the voting and counting processes.[^cite_note-f24-6] Another candidate, Yero Balde, alleged that [ballot stuffing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot_stuffing) had taken place and that his representatives were prevented from accessing vote-counting centres. The [National Front for the Defence of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_for_the_Defence_of_the_Constitution) also said that a "huge majority of Guineans chose to boycott the electoral charade".[^cite_note-12] Balde subsequently filed a petition before the Supreme Court accusing the DGE of electoral fraud, but withdrew the complaint on 3 January 2026.[^cite_note-13]

## Results

The DGE declared Mamady Doumbouya as the winner on 30 December, having won 86.72% of the vote, followed by Yéro Baldé with 6.59%.[^cite_note-AP1-14][^cite_note-AP2-15] The results were validated by the Supreme Court on 4 January 2026.[^cite_note-16]

## Aftermath

Doumbouya was inaugurated as the civilian president of Guinea on 17 January 2026. On 23 January, the [African Union](10634933.md) lifted sanctions imposed on Guinea following the [2021 Guinean coup d'état](68651369.md), citing the transition to civilian rule under the 2025 election.[^cite_note-18] ECOWAS also lifted sanctions and reinstated Guinea's membership on 30 January 2026.[^cite_note-19]
