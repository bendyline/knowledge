The **Mausoleum of Imam Ali** ([Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): مقام علی, romanized: *Maqām ʿAlī*), also known as the **Blue Mosque** ([Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): مسجد کبود), is a [mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum) and [mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mosques_in_Afghanistan) complex located in [Mazar-i-Sharif](20517.md), the [capital](181337.md) and largest [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Afghanistan) of [Balkh Province](457218.md) in northern [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-Dupree-1] It is a popular [tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Afghanistan) attraction in the middle of the city, which is visited all year long by all people regardless of religion or sect.[^cite_note-2]

The complex purportedly houses the tomb of Caliph [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali), also known as the first [Imam](15240.md) of [Shia Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Muslims) (r. 656–661). Many pilgrims annually celebrate [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz) (New Year) at the site. At the annual [Janda Bala ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz_in_Afghanistan), a flag used to be raised in honor of Ali and pilgrims would touch the flag for supposed good luck in the [New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz_in_Afghanistan).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] This particular ceremony has been officially halted in recent years.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)

The earliest surviving source stating Ali to be buried in Balkh is *Tuhfat al-Albab* of the Andalusian traveller [Abu Hamid al-Gharnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hamid_al-Gharnati) (d. 1170).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcChesney201427-8] Abd al-Ghafur Lari wrote that [Muhammad al-Baqir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Baqir), the fifth Shia imam, assigned [Abu Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Muslim) the task of transferring Ali's body to [Khurasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan), though this is likely apocryphal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcChesney201430–31-9] The land around the site was formerly a memorial mosque dedicated to [Ali al-Balkhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_al-Balkhi), a [Hanafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi) Muslim scholar and statesman who died in the same place.[^cite_note-10]

The first structure of the site dates back to the [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire) era. It was a shrine built by Sultan [Ahmad Sanjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Sanjar) in 1136.[^cite_note-Dupree-1] In the 13th century, the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) under [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) invaded Balkh, where they massacred the Balkhi population and destroyed their places of worship. The shrine built by Sanjar was destroyed by the Mongols in the year 1220.[^cite_note-ArchNet-11] The Mongols were led to believe that there was a significant amount of treasure under it.[^cite_note-Dupree-1]

In the 15th century, the [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty) [amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir), [Sultan Husayn Bayqara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Husayn_Bayqara), ordered a reconstruction of the destroyed building.[^cite_note-ArchNet-11]

In later years, various rulers made repairs and endowments, including the [Shaybanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaybanids) emir [Abd al-Mu'min ibn Abd Allah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Mu'min_ibn_Abd_Allah_Khan?action=edit\&redlink=1), who built a dome. Later, [Berdi Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdi_Beg), the [Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(title\)) of the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde) who reigned from 1357 to 1359, added several decorations to the building. In the modern era, a plan was created to renovate the whole complex in 1910.[^cite_note-ArchNet-11]

[Sher Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Ali_Khan), Emir of Afghanistan in 1860s, and some of his relatives are also buried at the site.[^cite_note-Dupree-1] During the [2025 Balkh earthquake](81496310.md), the structure's [minaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minaret) was partially damaged and cracks formed in the walls. Afghanistan's [Ministry of Information and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Culture_\(Afghanistan\)) immediately pledged to "assess and repair the damage".[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]
