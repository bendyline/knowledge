"**Mainland China**"[^cite_note-1] is a [geopolitical](163225.md) term defined as the territory under direct administration of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC) in the aftermath of the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War). In addition to the geographical mainland, the geopolitical sense of the term includes islands such as [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan), [Chongming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongming_Island), and [Zhoushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhoushan).[^cite_note-Mainland_Affairs_Council-2]

By convention, the following territories are excluded from the term:

- [Special administrative regions of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China), which are regarded as subdivisions of the country, but retain distinct administrative, judicial and economic systems from those on the mainland:

  [^cite_note-3]

  - [Hong Kong](13404.md), formerly a [British colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong)

  - [Macau](19068.md), formerly a [Portuguese colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau)

- [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan), along with [Penghu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penghu), [Kinmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinmen), [Matsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsu_Islands) and other minor islands, are collectively known as the [Taiwan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Area), where has been the major territorial base of the [government of Taiwan (ROC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China) since 1950. The ROC still claims the "Mainland Area" as its constitutionally defined territory.

In Taiwan, the term is often used to refer to all territories administered by the PRC.[^cite_note-cons.judicial.gov.tw-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] The term is widely used in Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan as well as internationally, including by many [Overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) communities and diasporas.

## Background

In 1949, the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) and the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) had largely defeated the [Kuomintang](16903.md) (KMT)'s [National Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Army) in the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War). This forced the Kuomintang to relocate the government and institution of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) to the relative safety of [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan), an island which was [placed under its control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_San_Francisco#Fate_of_Taiwan_and_other_Japanese_overseas_territories) after the [surrender of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) at the end of [World War II](32927.md) in 1945. With the establishment of the People's Republic of China on 1 October 1949, the CCP-controlled government saw itself as the sole legitimate government of China,[^cite_note-7] competing with the claims of the Republic of China, whose authority is now limited to [Taiwan and other islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Area_of_the_Republic_of_China). This resulted in a situation in which [two co-existing governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Chinas) competed for [international legitimacy and recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_and_the_United_Nations) as the "government of China". With the democratisation of Taiwan in the 1990s and the rise of the [Taiwanese independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_independence) movement, some people began simply using the term "China" instead.[^cite_note-8]

Due to their status as colonies of foreign states during the establishment of the People's Republic of China in 1949, the phrase "mainland China" excludes [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md).[^cite_note-9] Since the return of Hong Kong and Macau to Chinese sovereignty in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_sovereignty_over_Hong_Kong) and [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_sovereignty_over_Macau), respectively, the two territories have retained their legal, political, and economic systems. The territories also have their distinct identities. Therefore, "mainland China" generally continues to exclude these territories, because of the "[one country, two systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_country,_two_systems)" policy adopted by the Chinese government towards the regions.[^cite_note-LegCo1-10] The term is also used in economic indicators, such as the [IMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Management_Development) Competitiveness Report. International news media often use "China" to refer only to the mainland of the People's Republic of China.

## Political use

### People's Republic of China

The Exit and Entry Administration Law of the People's Republic of China ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 中华人民共和国出境入境管理法) defines two terms in Chinese that are translated to "mainland":[^cite_note-11]

- *Dàlù* (大陆; 大陸), which means 'the continent'.

- *Nèidì* (内地; 內地), literally 'inland' or 'inner land'. It excludes Hong Kong and Macau.

  [^cite_note-12]

In the PRC, usage of the two terms is not strictly interchangeable. To emphasise the [One-China policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-China_policy) and not give the ROC "equal footing" in [cross-strait relations](2596399.md), the term must be used in PRC's official contexts with reference to Taiwan (with the PRC referring to itself as the "mainland side" dealing with the "Taiwan side"). In fact, the PRC government mandates that journalists use "Taiwan" and "the Mainland" (Dàlù) as corresponding concepts.[^cite_note-13]

But in terms of Hong Kong and Macau, the PRC government refers to itself as "the Central People's Government".[^cite_note-14] In the People's Republic of China, the term 内地 (Nèidì, 'inland') is often contrasted with the term 境外 ('outside the border') for things outside the mainland region.[^cite_note-15] Examples include "Administration of Foreign-funded Banks" (中华人民共和国外资银行管理条例; 中華人民共和國外資銀行管理條例) or the "Measures on Administration of Representative Offices of Foreign Insurance Institutions" (外国保险机构驻华代表机构管理办法; 外國保險機構駐華代表機構管理辦法).[^cite_note-LegCo1-10]

[Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan) is an island, but is nevertheless commonly considered to be part of the "mainland" politically, because its government, legal and political systems do not differ from the rest of the PRC. Nonetheless, Hainanese people still refer to the geographic mainland as "the mainland" and call its residents "mainlanders".[^cite_note-16]

Before 1949, the Kinmen and Matsu islands, were jointly governed with the rest of Fujian Province under successive Chinese governments. The two territories are generally considered to belong to the same historical region, Fujian Province, which has been divided since 1949 as a result of the civil war. However, because they are not controlled by the PRC, they are not included as part of "mainland China".

Some Internet platforms like [Bilibili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili) bans the term "mainland China" on their website and considered its use promoting separatism and undermining the integrity of national sovereignty. They require that the term should be replaced as "Chinese mainland", "China's mainland" and "the mainland of China".[^cite_note-17] According to the [Foreign Ministry of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Ministry_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), "Chinese mainland" is the correct term because "mainland China" supposedly implies that there are other Chinas.[^cite_note-18]

### Hong Kong and Macau

[Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md) have been territories of the PRC since 1997 and 1999 respectively. However, due to the [One Country, Two Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Country,_Two_Systems) policy, the two regions maintain a degree of autonomy, hence they are not governed as part of mainland China.

Geographically speaking, Hong Kong and Macau are both connected to mainland China in certain areas (e.g. the north of the [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories)). Additionally, the islands contained within Hong Kong (e.g. [Hong Kong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Island)) and Macau are much closer to mainland China than Taiwan and Hainan, and are much smaller.

In Hong Kong and Macau, the terms "mainland China" and "mainlander" are frequently used for people from PRC-governed areas (i.e. not Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau). The Chinese term *Neidi* (內地), meaning the *inland* but still translated *mainland* in English, is commonly applied by [SAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China) governments to represent non-SAR areas of PRC, including Hainan province and coastal regions of mainland China, such as "Constitutional and Mainland Affairs" (政制及內地事務局)[^cite_note-19] and Immigration Departments.[^cite_note-20] In the [Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_and_Hong_Kong_Closer_Economic_Partnership_Arrangement) (as well as the [Mainland and Macau Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_and_Macau_Closer_Economic_Partnership_Arrangement)) the CPG also uses the Chinese characters 内地 "inner land", with the note that they refer to the "customs territory of China".[^cite_note-CEPA-21]

References to the PRC and other lost continental territories as the mainland began appearing in Taiwan state documents as early as 1954.[^cite_note-法规ROC4-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Legal definitions followed in the 1990s. The 1991 [Additional Articles of the Constitution of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_Articles_of_the_Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_China) stated that "the handling of people's rights and obligations and other affairs between the free area and the mainland can be specially stipulated by law", and used the legal term "mainland area" without defining its geographical boundaries.[^cite_note-法规ROC6-25] The 1992 Regulations on the Relations between the People in Taiwan and the Mainland defined "Taiwan" as areas controlled by the ROC and "mainland" as "the territory of the Republic of China".[^cite_note-法规ROC5-26] The related [Cross-Strait Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-Strait_Act) called those under PRC jurisdiction - excluding those in Hong Kong and Macau - as "people of the mainland area", and used "[free area of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_area_of_the_Republic_of_China)" to describe areas under ROC control.[^cite_note-ROC_Constitution-27] The issue on the mainland's territory also stated in the [Judicial Yuan](378790.md) Interpretation No. 328 in 1993.[^cite_note-cons.judicial.gov.tw-4][^cite_note-法规ROC10-28] In 2012, the [Supreme Court of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Republic_of_China)'s judgment #900 labeled the [Macao Special Administrative Region](19068.md) as the "Mainland's Macau Area".[^cite_note-法规ROC11-29] The 2002 amendments to the [executive order](210541.md) *Enforcement Rules for the [Act Governing Relations between Peoples of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-Strait_Act)* defined the mainland as areas claimed but not controlled by the ROC, corresponding to "areas under control of the [Chinese Communists](7175.md)" (within the *[de facto](43594.md)* [borders of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_China)).[^cite_note-Mainland_Affairs_Council-2][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Views of the term "mainland China" (中國大陸) vary on Taiwan. During the [Dangguo era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dang_Guo), the KMT had previously referred to the territories under the control of the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) by several different names, e.g. "(territory controlled by the) [Communist bandits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_bandit)", "occupied/unfree area (of China)", "Communist China" (as opposed to either "Nationalist China" or "Democratic China"), "Red China" (as opposed to "Blue China"), and "mainland China (area)".[^cite_note-32] In modern times, many of these terms have fallen out of use. The terms "mainland China" (中國大陸) or "the mainland" (大陸) still remain in popular use, but some also simply use the term "China" (中國). The former term is generally preferred by the [Pan-Blue Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Blue_Coalition) led by the KMT, while the latter term is preferred by the [Pan-Green Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Green_Coalition) led by the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party) (DPP), which opposes the term "mainland" and its suggestion that Taiwan is part of China. This has caused many political debates.[^cite_note-Wachman-33][^cite_note-34]

## Usage of excluding SARs

**Mainland China** (**中國內地**), simply **Mainland** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): **內地**; lit. 'Inland')[^cite_note-35], is a political and legal term referring to the areas under the direct jurisdiction of the People's Republic of China, excluding the regions of [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md).[^cite_note-HKc5973-36] It is used in the [People's Republic of China](5405.md).[^cite_note-37] It This term is often used in contexts where the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau are mentioned, and in some cases, it is synonymous with "mainland China" (中國大陸). Nevertheless, in Taiwan, "mainland China" is also often used to refer to all territories administered by the People's Republic of China, including Hong Kong and Macau.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Legal uses

#### Mainland China

On 30 December 1954, the [Supreme People's Court of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_People's_Court) addressed a marital issue between a spouse residing in Inland and another residing in Hong Kong or Macau. The court used the term "mainland" in its official response, "Reply of the Supreme People's Court Regarding the Handling Opinions on Marital Issues with One Spouse Residing in Mainland and the Other in Hong Kong or Macau." This terminology arose from a divorce case between a person from Hong Kong and another from Zhoushan.[^cite_note-法规C12-41]

On 6 December 1984, in a reply by the Supreme People's Court titled "Reply of the Supreme People's Court on the Issue of Whether Civil Cases Involving Hong Kong and Macau Compatriots Holding '[British National Overseas Passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_National_\(Overseas\)_passport)' Issued by the Hong Kong British Authorities and Identity Cards Issued by the Macau Portuguese Authorities in Mainland Chinese People's Courts Should Be Treated as Foreign-Related Cases," it was pointed out that holders of such documents are considered Chinese citizens, not recognizing them as having British or Portuguese nationality; hence their civil cases in Mainland Chinese courts should not be treated as foreign-related cases.[^cite_note-法规C22-42]

On 30 June 2012, the Eleventh National People's Congress passed Article 89 of the "Exit and Entry Administration Law of the People's Republic of China," which states: "Exiting refers to traveling from Mainland China to other countries or regions, from Mainland China to the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, the Macau Special Administrative Region, or from the Mainland China to the Taiwan region".[^cite_note-法规C3甲3-43] This law symmetrically aligns "Mainland China" with the Hong Kong and Macau Special Administrative Regions,[^cite_note-法规C3甲3-43] and in English versions, both are uniformly translated as "Chinese Mainland".[^cite_note-法规C3乙2-44]

Additionally, the term "Mainland residents" is commonly used in contexts related to immigration control, tourism, and marriage, such as individual travel from Hong Kong and Macau, and [Mainland Travel Permits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Travel_Permit_for_Hong_Kong_and_Macao_Resident) for Hong Kong and Macau residents.[^cite_note-2020covid2-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

#### Hong Kong

In 1997, the [Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress) passed the "Decision of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress on Handling the Existing Laws of Hong Kong in Accordance with Article 160 of the Basic Law of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China", which required that any references in Hong Kong laws to "Mainland", "Taiwan", "Hong Kong", and "Macau" should be interpreted as referring to parts of the People's Republic of China.[^cite_note-法规H12-49] The Hong Kong Interpretation and General Clauses Ordinance stipulates that "'China' refers to the People's Republic of China", and "'the People's Republic of China' includes the Hong Kong and Macau Special Administrative Regions".[^cite_note-法规H22-50]

In 2003, the [Ministry of Commerce](638506.md) and the [Financial Secretary of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Secretary_\(Hong_Kong\)) signed the "[Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_and_Hong_Kong_Closer_Economic_Partnership_Arrangement)" defining "mainland" as "all the tariff territory of the People's Republic of China".[^cite_note-HKc5973-36][^cite_note-51]

On 1 April 2006, the "Mainland Affairs Liaison Office", under the Constitutional Affairs Bureau, was established, and on 1 July 2007, during the administration of Donald Tsang, it was renamed the "[Constitutional and Mainland Affairs Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_and_Mainland_Affairs_Bureau)".[^cite_note-报道H12-52]

#### Macau

In 1999, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPC) passed the "Decision of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress on Handling the Existing Laws of Macau in Accordance with Article 145 of the Basic Law of the Macau Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China." This decision required that any references in Macau's laws that mentioned Mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau, either individually or together, should be interpreted as referring to parts of the People's Republic of China.[^cite_note-法规M12-53] Law No. 1/1999 of Macau, also known as the "Reunification Law", restated relevant parts of the decision in its Annex IV.[^cite_note-法规M42-54]

In 2003, the Ministry of Commerce and Macau's Secretary for Economy and Finance signed the "[Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA) between Mainland and Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_and_Hong_Kong_Closer_Economic_Partnership_Arrangement)" which defined "mainland" as "all the tariff territory of the People's Republic of China".[^cite_note-法规M23-55]

### Usage in entertainment industry

In order to differentiate from the entertainment industries of Europe, America, Taiwan, Japan, Korea, and Hong Kong, the entertainment industry in Mainland China is often referred to as the "Mainland entertainment industry" or simply "Mainland entertainment". Artists who have developed their careers within the People's Republic of China are also referred to as "mainland artists".[^cite_note-56]
