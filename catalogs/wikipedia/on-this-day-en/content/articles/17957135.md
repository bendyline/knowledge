**Egyptian National Police** or **ENP** is the national police force of [Egypt](8087628.md) responsible for [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement). It is a department in the [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Egypt\)).

## National organization

The [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Egypt\)) divides the functions of the police and public security among four Deputy Ministers of Interior while the Minister of Interior himself retained responsibility for [National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency_\(Egypt\)) (Qitaa' al-amn al-watani),[^cite_note-1] investigations and overall organization.

There are four Deputy Ministers:

- [Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Security) responsible for [public safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_safety) (inc. [Municipal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Police)), travel, [Immigration](4599312.md), [passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport), [port security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_security), and [criminal investigation](5236980.md).

- [Special Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Police) responsible for prison administration, the [Central Security Forces](4317752.md), [civil defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_defense), police transport, police communications, [traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic) [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police), Tourism and Antiquities Police and the [Presidential police](335341.md).

- Personnel Affairs was responsible for police-training institutions, personnel matters for police and civilian employees, and the Policemen's Sports Association.

- Administrative and Financial Affairs responsible for general administration, budgets, supplies, and legal matters.

## Regional organization

The **Egyptian National Police (ENP)** is structured into a hierarchical and geographically distributed system that ensures national coverage and efficient command. The police force operates under the authority of the **[Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior)** and is organized at multiple regional levels to manage law enforcement across the country.

### [Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate)-level Command

Egypt is divided into **27 governorates (muhafazat)**, and each governorate has its own **Police Directorate**, headed by a **Director of Security**. This director is responsible for all police operations within the governorate, including crime prevention, investigations, traffic control, and civil defense.

- **Each Police Directorate oversees multiple departments**, such as:&#x20;

  - [Criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal) Investigations

  - Public Security

  - Traffic Police

  - Central Security Forces (in coordination with national command)

### District and Precinct-level Organization

Each governorate is subdivided into **districts (markaz)**, which contain **urban precincts and rural police stations**. These smaller units report to the governorate's Police Directorate and handle:

- **[Community policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_policing)**

- **[Local crime investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime)**

- **Routine [patrols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol) and traffic regulation**

### Specialized Units and Regional Deployment

In addition to general law enforcement, **specialized units** are deployed regionally to respond to specific threats and maintain public order:

- **[Anti-terrorism units](83392.md)**

- **[Drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_prohibition) enforcement teams**

- **[Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) and Antiquities Police** (especially in regions with major archaeological sites like Luxor and Aswan)

- **Environmental and border protection units** in regions such as the Sinai Peninsula and the Western Desert

### Central Security Forces (CSF)

Although part of the Ministry of Interior, the **[Central Security Forces](4317752.md)** are deployed regionally and support the ENP during:

- **[Public unrest](329906.md)**

- **Counter-terror operations**

- **Major events and demonstrations**

## Training

All of the commissioned officers were graduates of the [Police Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Police_Academy?action=edit\&redlink=1), established by [Abdelkarim Darwish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelkarim_Darwish) at [Cairo](6293.md), where, after high school, they had to complete four years at the academy, or after College degree, completing a period of 12 to 18 months. The Police Academy is a modern institution equipped with laboratory and physical-training facilities. The police force also sent some officers abroad for schooling.

The Police Academy offers a four-year program which includes: security administration, [criminal investigation](5236980.md), [military drills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_drill), civil defense, [fire fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_fighting), [forensic medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_medicine), communications, [cryptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptology), [first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology), [anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy), and foreign languages ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)). Also included are: political orientation, [public relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_relations), and military subjects (such as infantry and cavalry training), [marksmanship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marksmanship), leadership, and field exercises. Graduates receive a [bachelor](188874.md) of police studies degree and are commissioned [lieutenants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant), while those who held degrees from other colleges are commissioned as first lieutenants.

Advanced officer training was given at the academy's Institute for Advanced Police Studies, completion of which was required for advancement beyond the rank of [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel). The academy's three-month course for enlisted personnel is conducted in a military atmosphere but emphasizes police methods and techniques.

Some police officers, especially the special operations officers, are well trained by the [Egyptian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Armed_Forces) in Al-Sa'ka Military School.

## Uniforms and equipment

### Ranks

Egyptian police rank insignia are the same as those used by the Egyptian Army. Commissioned police ranks resemble [those of the Egyptian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Army_ranks). The highest-ranking Egyptian police officer is a [major General](36301471.md) and officer ranks descend only to first lieutenant. Enlisted police ranks include [master sergeant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_sergeant), [sergeant](219424.md), [corporal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporal), and [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_\(rank\)).

### Police uniforms

Egyptian police uniforms are similar to the Egyptian Army's service uniform for the ground forces, which is [khaki drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaki_drill) cotton. However, enlisted police personnel wear a black wool [bush jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_jacket) and trousers in the winter and a white cotton bush jacket and [trousers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trousers) in the summer. Certain police personnel also wear a blue or black [beret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beret).

### Equipment

Egyptian law enforcement police officers generally carry either the [M\&P357](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M\&P357), [CZ 75B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CZ_75) or [Glock pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glock_pistol) when on regular duty, however, heavy arms are always available at police stations. These include [submachine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submachine_gun), [assault rifles](18950865.md), [shotguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotguns) and [carbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbines), while special units may also have additional weapons like [Flash bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_bang_grenade) and stinger [tear gas](1202646.md) grenades, [H\&K USP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H\&K_USP) series and [SIG Sauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_Sauer) series [pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistols), [Heckler & Koch MP5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_MP5), [Heckler & Koch UMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_UMP) and [FN P90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_P90) submachine guns, [M14 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M14_rifle), the [Remington 700P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remington_700P) and some [.50 caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.50_caliber) sniper rifles.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

The Tourism and Antiquities Police cover tourist destinations like historical sites, museums, hotels, etc. Places such as the [Great Pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) of [Giza](144514.md), [Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Egypt) [Giza](144514.md), [Egyptian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Museum), etc. in [Cairo](6293.md), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) – [Qaitbay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaitbay) Citadel and the [Serapium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serapium) Temple and [Pompey's Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey's_Pillar_\(column\)), etc., going through all cities in Egypt carrying same weapons as law enforcement police.[^cite_note-4]

### Transport



Egyptian National Police [Iveco Eurocargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iveco_Eurocargo).

Unlike in many other countries, the Egyptian police extensively use [SUVs](47696.md). The Egyptian produced [Jeep](15658.md) vehicle used to be the most common police car in Egypt but in recent years, other similar vehicles have also come into police use. SUVs are known for their capabilities to move around in any sort of terrain. Depending upon the location, the police vehicles may have individual revolving lights ([strobe lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strobe_lights)) or [light bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_bar), [sirens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siren_\(noisemaker\)) etc. An extensive modernisation drive has ensured that these vehicles are equipped with wireless sets in communication with a central control room. Traffic Police vehicles generally also have equipment like speed [radars](25676.md), breath analysers and emergency [first aid kits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid_kit). Color schemes of police vehicles vary according to their location and which directorate they belong to.

For traffic regulation and patrolling in cities, [motorcycles](19876.md) are also used. This is because of increasing congestion in cities where the heavier bikes would prove to be unwieldy when compared to the nimbler handling the newer bikes were capable of. The bikes are provided with two-way radios, strobes and sirens and are generally painted white. Some cities make use of [sedans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedan_\(automobile\)) as patrol vehicles or high speed 'interceptors' on highways. Of late, the various police forces are on a modernisation drive, upgrading and revamping their fleet with new vehicles.

## Relations with public

The police in Egypt lost some prestige during the [2011 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_Revolution). According to one source the Egyptian police, "once feared by civilians, are now seen as leftover elements of Mubarak's regime and treated with little respect. Pulled off the streets after violently cracking down on protests in January, they are now trying to reshape their role in the post-Mubarak Egypt."[^cite_note-csm-5] [Police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality) is credited with being one of the causes of the revolution,[^cite_note-6] and as of June 2011 several police officers are being tried for the killing of "hundreds of demonstrators" during the revolution. According to government statistics, 90 police stations have been burnt since the start of the revolution.[^cite_note-csm-5] The government has taken steps to address public concerns and police demoralization.[^cite_note-csm-5] In response to a planned July 8 rally protesting the release on bail of police accused of murdering protesters during the revolution, interim interior minister [Mansour el-Essawy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_el-Essawy) promised to purge up to 700 corrupt senior police officers. However protesters complained that five months after the revolution where almost 1000 people were killed, only one officer has been convicted of wrongdoing and he has not yet been incarcerated.[^cite_note-7]

### July 2013 coup d'état

Mass demonstrations took place for and against President [Mohamed Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Morsi) on 30 June 2013, marking the one-year anniversary of Mohamed Morsi's inauguration. The police, along with the military, had made it clear that they were with anti-government protestors by carrying out a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Egyptian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) on 3 July.

### Sit-in dispersals

The [August 2013 Rabaa massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2013_Rabaa_massacre) by police to remove pro-government protesters from sit-ins being held in [Rabaa Al-Adawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabaa_Al-Adawiya_Mosque) and Al-Nahda Square in support of President [Mohamed Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Morsi) resulted in rapidly escalating violence that eventually led to the death of over 900 people, with at least 3,994 injured.[^cite_note-8] The police attempted to defend their actions by claiming to disperse the sit-ins with the least possible damage.[^cite_note-9]

### Treatment of women

According to writer [Ahdaf Soueif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahdaf_Soueif), since 2005 the police have routinely grabbed women protesters and torn "their clothes off and beat them, groping them at the same time. The idea was to insinuate that females who took part in street protests wanted to be groped." To protect against this, many female protesters wear "layers of light clothing, no buttons, [drawstring pants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawstring_pants) double-knotted".[^cite_note-10]
