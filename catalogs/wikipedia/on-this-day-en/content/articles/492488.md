**Hicham El Guerrouj** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): هشام الݣروج; [Berber languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages): ⵀⵉⵛⴰⵎ ⵍⴳⵔⵔⵓⵊ, romanized: *Hisham El Gerruj*; born 14 September 1974) is a retired Moroccan [middle-distance runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-distance_runner) who is the current [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_records_in_athletics) holder in the [1500 metres](2707254.md).[^cite_note-3] He formerly held the world records in the [2000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_metres) and [mile](5227492.md). At the [2004 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics), he became the first man since [Paavo Nurmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paavo_Nurmi) to win gold medals in both the 1500 m and [5000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5000_metres) at the same Olympic Games.

Regarded as one of the greatest middle-distance runners of all time,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] he recorded six of the 10 fastest 1500 m performances in history[^cite_note-All-time_men's_best_1500_m-7] and four of the 10 fastest performances in the mile as of June 2026.[^cite_note-worldathletics.org-8] El Guerrouj's mile world record of 3\:43.13 lasted for 27 years (1999–2026). He and [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen) are the only men to have run faster than 3\:27 for the 1500 m and 3\:44 for the mile. El Guerrouj is also the only man to have broken 3\:27 in the 1500 m more than once, achieving the feat five times.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

El Guerrouj won four consecutive world titles in the 1500 m, taking gold at the [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), and [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres) [World Athletics Championships](218926.md). He was named [World Athlete of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athlete_of_the_Year) three times[^cite_note-All-time_men's_best_1500_m-7][^cite_note-worldathletics.org-8] and was inducted into the [International Association of Athletics Federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Athletics_Federations) (IAAF) [Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_Hall_of_Fame) in November 2014.[^cite_note-11]

## Early life and career

Born in [Berkane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkane), Hicham El Guerrouj comes from a family of farmers. He initially pursued soccer and basketball in his sporting endeavors, but his spark in the sport of athletics came at the age of 13, when a local coach recognized his talent and encouraged him to pursue the sport.[^cite_note-12] According to El Guerrouj, his urban upbringing and close proximity to a sports stadium allowed him to watch athletics competitions often, fueling his passion for running.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-13]

El Guerrouj's first international triumph was at age 18, when he came third in the [5000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5000_metres) of the [1992 Junior World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_World_Junior_Championships_in_Athletics) in Seoul, behind [Haile Gebrselassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Gebrselassie) of Ethiopia and [Ismael Kirui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismael_Kirui) of [Kenya](188171.md). A year later, he was the #2 man on the Moroccan team at the [World Junior Cross Country Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_IAAF_World_Cross_Country_Championships_%E2%80%93_Junior_men's_race).

In 1994, he was a member of the Moroccan team in the [1994 IAAF World Road Relay Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_IAAF_World_Road_Relay_Championships), which won the race in world record time.[^cite_note-14]

El Guerrouj rose to international prominence in the mid-1990s with near-record times in the [1500 metres](2707254.md) and mile. At the age of 20 he finished second in the 1500 metres to then world record holder [Noureddine Morceli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noureddine_Morceli) at the [1995 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_World_Championships_in_Athletics) in Gothenburg. In 1996 after setting a new personal best in the 1500 metres of 3\:29.59 in [Stockholm](26741.md), he was considered one of the favourites for the [Olympic](22576.md) [gold](526488.md).

## 1996 Atlanta Olympics – 1999 season

El Guerrouj competed in his first Olympic Games in 1996 at [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics). Running the 1500 metres final, as he was moving into position to challenge for the lead,[^cite_note-15] he fell with 400 m to go and finished last in 12th place. He had been expected to challenge the world record holder and three-time [World champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_champion), [Noureddine Morceli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noureddine_Morceli).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

One month later, at the Grand Prix final in Milan, El Guerrouj became the first runner to defeat Morceli over 1500 m in four years.[^cite_note-18] In the following years, El Guerrouj became the only middle distance runner to win four consecutive [world titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_World_Championships_in_Athletics) in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres),[^cite_note-19] [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres),[^cite_note-20] [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), and [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres).[^cite_note-21]

El Guerrouj set two [world indoor records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_indoor_world_records_in_athletics) at the start of the 1997 season, starting with a 1500 m record of 3\:31.18 at the [Sparkassen Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparkassen_Cup_\(athletics\)), which was not beaten until 22 years later, in 2019 by [Samuel Tefera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Tefera). He also set a new indoor world record of 3\:48.45 in the mile run at the [Indoor Flanders meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_Flanders_meeting) a few weeks later, which stood until [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) bettered the mark in 2019.

In 1998 in [Rome](25458.md), El Guerrouj broke Morceli's 1500 m world record (3\:27.37) with a time of 3\:26.00. With an average pace of 54.93 seconds per lap, this record still stands today, and is the first performance in the 1500 m where the pace averaged under 55 seconds per lap.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

In 1999, also in Rome, El Guerrouj broke the world record in the [mile](5227492.md) set by Morceli in 1993, with a time of 3\:43.13, completing the first 1500 metres in 3\:28.21. [Noah Ngeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Ngeny) of Kenya, who ran second, was also under the previous world record with a time of 3\:43.40, which remained the second place world record until 2026.[^cite_note-26] This was the first time in over 40 years that two men had bettered the mile world record in the same race,[^cite_note-27] neither of which had been broken until 2026 when [Josh Kerr](54894149.md) beat El Guerrouj's mile record in London, UK.[^cite_note-28]

Later that season he set a new world record over [2000 m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_m) in [Berlin](3354.md) at 4\:44.79, bettering the previous mark set by Morceli by more than three seconds, that has since been broken by [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen), who ran 4\:43.13 in 2023.[^cite_note-29] In 1999 El Guerrouj also ran what was then the second fastest [3000 m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3000_m) ever in [Brussels](3708.md), with a time of 7\:23.09.

## 2000 Sydney Olympics – 2003 season

At the [Sydney Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics), El Guerrouj was favourite to take gold but finished second in the 1500 metres, behind [Noah Ngeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Ngeny), a talented Kenyan runner at the peak of his career who had run as El Guerrouj's pacemaker when El Guerrouj ran his 1500m world record in Rome in 1998.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

El Guerrouj successfully defended his 1500 m title in the [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_World_Championships_in_Athletics) and [2003 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_World_Championships_in_Athletics) and came close to breaking his own 1500 m record in [Brussels](3708.md) in 2001 with a time of 3\:26.12. He also won 3 consecutive [IAAF Golden League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_Golden_League) prizes in 2001, 2002 and 2003. He was the only [middle distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_distance_track_event) athlete to produce the winning streak necessary to be entitled for a share of the jackpot of 50 kilograms (1,608 troy ounces) of [gold](12240.md) (2000–2002) or US\$1 million (1998–1999, 2003–present). As of 2011 he was the only athlete to have won it three times in a row.[^cite_note-33]

In 2003, El Guerrouj set a personal best of 12\:50.24 in the [5000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5000_metres), which is the 26th fastest ever in the event. Later in the year, at the [World Track & Field Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_World_Championships_in_Athletics), he finished a close second to Kenyan [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge) in the 5000 metres, adding a silver to the gold he had previously won in the 1500 metres.

## 2004 Athens Olympics and retirement



El Guerrouj and Carlos García at the 2004 Summer Olympics in Athens

After a relatively poor start to the 2004 season that included slow times and an 8th-place finish in a 1500-metre race in Rome, El Guerrouj entered both the 1500 metres and the 5000 metres at the [2004 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics) in [Athens](1216.md), Greece.

Only 20 days before the Olympic final, 2000 Olympic bronze medalist [Bernard Lagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lagat) ran the fastest 1500 m in 2004 (3\:27.40), narrowly defeating El Guerrouj (3\:27.64) at the [Weltklasse Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weltklasse_Z%C3%BCrich) meet on August 6. On August 24, in the final straight of the Olympic 1500m final, El Guerrouj beat Lagat by 0.12 seconds, winning the gold medal. In the finale, entering the home straight El Guerrouj led, only to be overtaken by Lagat- and then El Guerrouj re-took the lead a few strides from the line.[^cite_note-34]

Four days later, El Guerrouj won the 5,000 m final with a time of 13\:14.39, preventing [Kenenisa Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenenisa_Bekele) from achieving the 5000 m/10000 m distance double, last achieved by Ethiopian [Miruts Yifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miruts_Yifter) in [1980 Moscow Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-35]

El Guerrouj became the first man in 80 years to win both 1500m and 5000m titles in the same Olympics, previously achieved only by the "[Flying Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Finn)" [Paavo Nurmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paavo_Nurmi) in 1924.

On 16 May 2005, El Guerrouj was nominated for the [Laureus World Sports Award for Sportsman of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award_for_Sportsman_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Having fulfilled his sporting ambitions & due to injuries from his years of high intensity training, El Guerrouj never competed internationally again after the 2004 Olympics and announced his retirement on May 22, 2006.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Awards and honors

His sporting career is marked by numerous recognitions such as the award for [humanitarian effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarianism) from [the International Association of Athletics Federations (IAAF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF), which he received in 1996. He is also a [UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UNICEF_Goodwill_Ambassadors). El Guerrouj was named [IAAF World Athlete of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_World_Athlete_of_the_Year) in 2001, 2002 and 2003 after remaining unbeaten in more than 20 races, becoming the first man to win the award in consecutive years. He was also named best athlete of the year by the athletics journal *[Track and Field News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_and_Field_News)* in 2002. In 2003, he was elected as a member of the [IAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF) Athletes Committee.

On September 7, 2004, El Guerrouj was decorated with the "Cordon de Commandeur" by [King Mohammed VI of Morocco](226113.md). In the same year, he was awarded the [Prince of Asturias Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Asturias_Awards).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

He was a member of the [International Olympic Committee Athletes' Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee) from 2004 to 2012.

Hicham El Guerrouj is today an Ambassador for [Peace and Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Sport), a [Monaco](19261.md)-based international organization,[^cite_note-42] as well as a member of its "Champions for Peace" club, a group of 54 famous elite athletes committed to serving peace in the world through sport.

## Personal life

During the [1996 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics), after his devastating fall in the [1500 metres final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), El Guerrouj received a call from [King Hassan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_II_of_Morocco), then the [King of Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Morocco), who told him, "Do not cry. You are a champion in the eyes of the Moroccan people."[^cite_note-ReferenceA-13] In response to this call, El Guerrouj later said, "After the call by His Majesty, it was another el-Guerrouj who was born. There is no similarity to the el-Guerrouj before this call and the el-Guerrouj right now."[^cite_note-43]

On 27 September 2003, El Guerrouj married Najoua Lahbil.[^cite_note-44] They have a daughter named Hiba.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-13]

El Guerrouj is an admirer of [Roger Bannister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Bannister), whom he considers to be his "spiritual father." El Guerrouj met Bannister in 2000, and attended a 70-year anniversary event of Bannister's first sub-four minute mile on 6 May 2024 in [Oxford, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford).[^cite_note-athleticsweekly.com-45][^cite_note-46] In 2019, he also attended the World Athletics Heritage Mile Night in Monaco, which was organized by [Sebastian Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Coe).[^cite_note-47]

In 2024, reflecting on the differences between the mile and 1500 metres, El Guerrouj stated the following:[^cite_note-athleticsweekly.com-45]

> "My favourite is 1500m. It's part of my heart. I competed in it a lot and I know every metre of this race. The mile is completely different. If you are not strong physically and mentally, you cannot run it well."

El Guerrouj attended the opening ceremony of the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics), and was also a torchbearer.[^cite_note-48] He also attended the 2024 Wanda [Diamond League](21778161.md) Final in [Brussels](1111662.md) on 13 September, where he greeted [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen) at the finish line of the 1500 m.[^cite_note-49]
