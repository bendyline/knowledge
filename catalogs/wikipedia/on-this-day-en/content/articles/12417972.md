**Head constable** was a rank used in some [British](31717.md) and [British colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) forces, and is still used in the [Indian police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_India) and [Central Armed Police Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Armed_Police_Forces).

## England and Wales

Originally, head constable was the normal title for the chief officer of a [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough) police force in England and Wales. Throughout the later 19th century and early 20th century, this title was superseded by [chief constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_constable) in most forces. A few smaller borough forces and the [Liverpool City Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_City_Police) retained it until it was finally abolished under the [Police Act 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Act_1919). However, [Winchester City Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_City_Police) appears to have retained the title until 1943, when it was amalgamated with [Hampshire Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_Constabulary).

## India

Further information: [List of police ranks in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_police_ranks_in_India)

Head constable in the [Indian police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_India) is equivalent to [sergeant](219424.md) in police forces in other countries. Head constables wear three point-down [chevrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_\(insignia\)) on their sleeves or three bars on their [epaulettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epaulette).[^cite_note-1] In [Kerala Police](52132287.md), when serving in the local police, a head constable is designated as a senior civil police officer (SCPO).[^cite_note-2]

In some state police forces, such as the [Odisha Police](23398194.md), the rank of [havildar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havildar) is used and is considered equivalent to the rank of head constable.[^cite_note-3] In the [Kerala Police](52132287.md), however, the rank of havildar exists only in the Armed Police cadre.[^cite_note-4]

## Ireland and colonial police forces

In the [Royal Irish Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Constabulary), [Royal Ulster Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Ulster_Constabulary) (until its reorganisation in 1970), and some colonial forces such as the [Palestine Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Police), head constable was a rank between the sergeant and [inspector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector) grades, roughly equivalent to a [warrant officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer) in the Army. In colonial forces, it was usually a rank held by Europeans only. Some colonial forces also had a higher rank of head constable major.
