The **Social Democrats** ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Socialdemokratiet* [\[soˈɕɛˀlte̝moˌkʰʁɑˀtɪət\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Danish), lit. 'The Social Democracy', **S**) is a [social democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy) political party in Denmark.[^cite_note-Nordsieck-12][^cite_note-Merkel-13] A member of the [Party of European Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_European_Socialists), the Social Democrats have 38 out of 179 members of the [Folketing](429743.md) (following the [latest Danish general election](72208286.md) held in 2026) and three out of fourteen [MEPs](258909.md) elected from Denmark.

Founded by [Louis Pio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pio) in 1871, the party first entered the [Folketing](429743.md) in the [1884 Danish Folketing election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1884_Danish_Folketing_election). By the early 20th century, it had become the party with the largest representation in the Folketing, a distinction it would hold for 77 years. It first formed a government after the [1924 Danish Folketing election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Danish_Folketing_election) under [Thorvald Stauning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvald_Stauning), the longest-serving [Danish Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Denmark) of the 20th century. During Stauning's government which lasted until the [1926 Danish Folketing election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Danish_Folketing_election), the Social Democrats exerted a profound influence on [Danish society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_society), laying the foundation of the Danish [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state). From 2002 to 2016, the party used the name *Socialdemokraterne* in some contexts.[^cite_note-16] The party was a member of the [Labour and Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_and_Socialist_International) from 1923 to 1940. A member of the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International) until 2017, the party withdrew to join the [Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance), founded in 2013.

The party was the [major coalition partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorning-Schmidt_I_Cabinet) in government from the [2011 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Danish_general_election) until the [2015 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Danish_general_election), with then-party leader [Helle Thorning-Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helle_Thorning-Schmidt) as [Prime Minister](273720.md). After losing power in the 2015 election, Thorning-Schmidt was succeeded as party leader on 28 June 2015 by the former Vice Leader [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md), who shifted the party back to the [political left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_left) on economics, while criticising mass [immigration](4599312.md).[^cite_note-The_Guardian-17][^cite_note-Politico-18] Frederiksen led the party to win the [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Danish_general_election), [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Danish_general_election), and [2026 Danish general election](72208286.md) forming [a single-party minority government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_I_Cabinet) from 2019 to 2022, [a majority grand-coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_II_Cabinet) with the centre-right [Venstre](403109.md) and the centrist [Moderates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderates_\(Denmark\)) from 2022 to 2026 and a [centre-left minority coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_III_Cabinet) since 2026.&#x20;

## Overview

The party traces its own history back to the International Labour Association, founded in 1871 and banned in 1873, loosely re-organised in the Social Democratic Labour Party which in 1876 issued the Gimle program, but as a formal political party it was first founded from 11–12 February 1878 as the **Social Democratic Federation**. This name was formally carried by the party for almost a hundred years, although in practice it also used a number of other names until it changed its name to Social Democracy in 1965. At a congress in [Aalborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg) in 2002, the party changed its name to the Social Democrats, but from 2016 again only Social Democracy is used.[^cite_note-name1-14][^cite_note-name2-15]

The party has the letter *A* as a symbol, but the abbreviation *S* is often used in the media. The party's classic symbol is a [red rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_rose_\(politics\)) and in recent times an *A* in a red circle. Aside from the classical socialist [red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_\(politics\)) colour,[^cite_note-19] the party has recently adopted a more light red colour called [competition orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(colour\)).[^cite_note-20] The party was a member of the [Labour and Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_and_Socialist_International) between 1923 and 1940.[^cite_note-21] It is now a member of the [Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance), an association of progressive social-democratic parties.[^cite_note-PA-22] The Social Democrats are also a member of the [Party of European Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_European_Socialists) while the party's [MEPs](258909.md) sit in the [Socialists & Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance_of_Socialists_and_Democrats) group.

## History

### 19th century



[Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) pioneer [Louis Pio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pio), founder and first leader of the Social Democrats (1871–1872) and 1875–1877)&#x20;

The party was founded as the International Labour Association of Denmark on 15 October 1871 by [Louis Pio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pio), [Harald Brix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Brix?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Paul Geleff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Geleff).[^cite_note-23] The goal was to organise the emerging [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) on a [democratic and socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism) basis. The [industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation) of Denmark had begun in the mid-19th century and a period of rapid [urbanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanisation) had led to an emerging class of [urban workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletariat). The social-democratic movement emerged from the desire to give this group [political rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_rights) and [representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_\(politics\)) in the [Folketing](429743.md), the Danish [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament). In 1876, the party held an annual conference, adopting the first party manifesto.[^cite_note-Danmarkshistorien-24]

&#x20;The stated policy was as follows:&#x20;

> The Danish Social Democratic Labour Party works in its national form, but is convinced of the international nature of the labour movement and ready to sacrifice everything and fulfill all obligations to provide: Freedom, equality and brotherhood among all nations.

In 1884, the party had their first two members of parliament elected, namely [Peter Thygesen Holm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thygesen_Holm?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Chresten Hørdum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chresten_H%C3%B8rdum?action=edit\&redlink=1).

### 20th century



[Thorvald Stauning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvald_Stauning), the party's first Prime Minister (1924–1926 and 1929–1942) on his 1935 *[Stauning or Chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stauning_or_Chaos)* election poster

In 1906, the party created the Social Democratic Youth Association, lasting until 1920 when the [Social Democratic Youth of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Youth_of_Denmark) and current party's [youth wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_wing) was founded. In the [1924 Danish Folketing election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Danish_Folketing_election), the party won the majority with 36.6 percent of the vote and its first government was put in place with [Thorvald Stauning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvald_Stauning) as [Prime Minister](273720.md).[^cite_note-25] That same year, he appointed [Nina Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Bang) as the world's first female minister, nine years after [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) had been given in Denmark. Stauning stayed in power until his death in 1942, with his party laying the foundations for the Danish [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) based on a close collaboration between labor unions and the government.[^cite_note-26]

In January 1933, Stauning's government entered into what was then the most extensive settlement yet in [Danish politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_politics), namely the [Kanslergade settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanslergadeforliget) ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Kanslergadeforliget*) with the liberal party [Venstre](403109.md).[^cite_note-27] The settlement was named after Stauning's apartment in Kanslergade in [Copenhagen](5166.md) and included extensive [agricultural subsidies](171866.md) and reforms of the legislation and administration in the social sector.[^cite_note-28] In 1935, Stauning was reelected with the famous slogan "[Stauning or Chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stauning_or_Chaos)".[^cite_note-29] Stauning's second cabinet lasted until the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) [occupation of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Denmark) in 1940, when the cabinet was widened to include all political parties for a [national unity government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_unity_government) and the Danish government pursued a collaborative policy with the German occupiers. Through the 1940s and until 1972, most of [Denmark's Prime Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Denmark) were from the party.[^cite_note-30]

### Poul Nyrup Rasmussen government coalition: 1993–2001



Social Democrats election poster for the October 1945 general election

The Social Democrats' [social policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_policy) through the 1990s and continuing in the 21st century involved a significant [redistribution of income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistribution_\(economics\)) and the maintenance of a large state apparatus with collectively financed core [public services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_services) such as [public healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_healthcare), [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) and [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure). Social Democrats-led coalition governments (the [I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Poul_Nyrup_Rasmussen_I), [II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Poul_Nyrup_Rasmussen_II), [III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Poul_Nyrup_Rasmussen_III) and [IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Poul_Nyrup_Rasmussen_IV) Cabinets of [Poul Nyrup Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poul_Nyrup_Rasmussen)) implemented the system known as [flexicurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexicurity) (flexibility and social security), mixing strong Scandinavian [unemployment benefits](378430.md) with deregulated employment laws, making it easier for employers to fire and rehire people in order to encourage [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) and reduce unemployment.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-Danmarkshistorien-24]

The Cabinets of Poul Nyrup Rasmussen maintained a parliamentary majority during the period from 1993 to 2001 by virtue of their support from the [Socialist People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_People's_Party_\(Denmark\)) and the [Red–Green Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red%E2%80%93Green_Alliance_\(Denmark\)).[^cite_note-32] Towards the end of the 1990s, a [trade surplus](4810.md) of 30 billion [kroner](236323.md) (US\$4.9 billion) turned into a [deficit](4810.md). To combat this, the government increased taxes, limiting [private consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_\(economics\)). The 1998 initiative, dubbed the Whitsun Packet (Danish: *Pinsepakken*) from the season it was issued, was not universally popular with the electorate; it may have also been a factor in the Social Democrats' defeat in the [2001 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Danish_general_election).

### In opposition: 2001–2011

After being defeated by the [Liberal Party](403109.md) in the 2001 Danish general election, the party chairmanship went to former [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Minister_of_Denmark) and [foreign minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Minister_of_Denmark) [Mogens Lykketoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogens_Lykketoft). Following another defeat in the [2005 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Danish_general_election), Lykketoft announced his resignation as party leader and at an extraordinary congress on 12 March it was decided that all members of the party would cast votes in an election of a new party leader. The two contenders for the leadership represented the two wings in the party, with [Helle Thorning-Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helle_Thorning-Schmidt) being viewed as [centrist](39619035.md) and [Frank Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Jensen) being viewed as slightly more [left-leaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics). On 12 April 2005, Thorning-Schmidt was elected as the new leader.[^cite_note-DenStoreDanske-33]

### Helle Thorning-Schmidt government coalition: 2011–2015

In the [2011 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Danish_general_election), the Social Democrats gained 44 seats in Parliament, the lowest number since 1953.[^cite_note-34] Nonetheless, the party succeeded in establishing a minority government with the [Danish Social Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Social_Liberal_Party) and the [Socialist People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_People's_Party_\(Denmark\)). The incumbent centre-right coalition led by the [Liberal Party](403109.md) lost power to a centre-left coalition led by the Social Democrats, making Thorning-Schmidt the country's first female prime minister. The Danish Social Liberal Party and the Socialist People's Party became part of the three-party centre-left coalition government. The new parliament convened on 4 October. The government rolled back anti-immigration legislation enacted by the previous government[^cite_note-35] and passed a tax-reform with support from the liberal-conservative opposition.[^cite_note-ThorningTaxreform-36] The tax reform raised the top tax threshold, which had previously applied to over half the working population. The aim of the tax reform was to increase labour output to fend off a projected labour shortage within the next decades. The stated goal was to entice Danes to work more in order to compensate for the decreasing workforce by lowering tax on wages and gradually lowering welfare payments to those outside of the labour market to increase the economic benefit of working relative to receiving welfare.[^cite_note-37]

On 3 February 2014, the Socialist People's Party left the government in protest over the sale of shares in the public energy company [DONG Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DONG_Energy) to the investment bank [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs).[^cite_note-38] Because of the government's minority status and of its dependency on the support of the Danish Social Liberal Party, the government had to jettison many of the policies that the Social Democrats–Socialist People's Party coalition had given during the campaign. Although critics have accused the government of breaking its promises, other studies argue that it accomplished half of its stated goals, blaming instead poor public relations strategies for its increasingly negative public image.[^cite_note-39] The government pursued a centrist compromise agenda, building several reforms with support from both sides of the parliament. This caused friction with the supporting Red–Green Alliance, who were kept outside of influencing decisions.[^cite_note-ThorningTaxreform-36]

### In opposition: 2015–2019

In the [2015 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Danish_general_election), the Social Democrats gained seats and became the biggest party in Parliament again since 2001, yet lost the government because the right-wing parties had a majority. The results of the 2015 election and the defeat of the left-bloc led Thorning-Schmidt to resign as prime minister on election night and making way for the next leader [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md).[^cite_note-40] Under Frederiksen, the Social Democrats voted in favor of a law allowing Danish authorities to confiscate money, jewellery and other valuable items refugees crossing the border may have as long as those valuables have no sentimental value,[^cite_note-41] despite harsh condemnation from the [United Nations Human Right Council](635790.md)[^cite_note-42] and widespread comparisons between the plan and the treatment of [Jews](25955086.md) in [Nazi-occupied Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi-occupied_Europe).[^cite_note-43] The law had been used 17 times in the first six years.[^cite_note-44]

Similarly, the Social Democrats voted for [a law banning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burqa_ban) wearing of [burqas](165826.md) and [niqabs](1322219.md), while abstaining during a vote on a law on mandatory handshakes irrespective of religious sentiment at citizenship ceremonies and on a plan to house criminal [asylum seekers](276005.md) on an island used for researching contagious animal diseases. Frederiksen has also backed the right-wing populist [Danish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_People's_Party) in their paradigm shift push to make [repatriation](1481067.md) rather than [social integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_integration) the goal of asylum policy. She has called for a cap on non-Western immigrants, expulsion of asylum seekers to a reception centre in North Africa and forced labour for immigrants in exchange for benefits. Labeling foreign policies of Europe as too [economic liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberal), Frederiksen has criticised other social democratic parties for losing their voters' trust by failing to prevent globalisation chipping away at labour rights, increasing inequality and exposing them to uncontrolled immigration.[^cite_note-45]

### 2019–present: Frederiksen I, II and III



[Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) during a debate with [Martin Lidegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Lidegaard) and [Mona Juul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mona_Juul_\(Danish_politician\)) in [Nyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyborg), January 2026

In the [2019 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Danish_general_election), the Social Democrats gained one further seat and the opposition red bloc of [left-wing](18499.md) and [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left) parties (the Social Democrats, the Danish Social Liberal Party, the Socialist People's Party and the Red–Green Alliance along with the [Faroese](17238607.md) [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)) and [Greenland](12118.md)'s [Inuit Ataqatigiit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit_Ataqatigiit) and [Siumut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siumut)) won a majority of 93 out of 179 seats in the Folketing while support for the [Danish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_People's_Party) and the [Liberal Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Alliance_\(Denmark\)) collapsed, costing [Lars Løkke Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_L%C3%B8kke_Rasmussen) his majority. With the result beyond doubt on election night, Rasmussen conceded defeat and Frederiksen has been commissioned by [Queen Margrethe II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrethe_II_of_Denmark) to lead the negotiations to form a new government.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

On 27 June 2019, Frederiksen was successful in forming the [Frederiksen Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_I_Cabinet), an exclusively Social Democrats minority government supported by the red bloc, becoming the second woman in the role after Thorning-Schmidt as well as the youngest prime minister in Danish history at the age of 41.[^cite_note-48] Despite having run on an [anti-immigration](6491626.md) stance during the election, Frederiksen shifted her stance on immigration by allowing more foreign labour and reversing government plans to hold foreign criminals offshore after winning government.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

In the [2022 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Danish_general_election), the Social Democrats gained 2 more seats with more than twice the number of seats of any other party.[^cite_note-52] This led to the formation of the [Frederiksen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_II_Cabinet) cabinet, which included the Social Democrats, the Liberal Party and the newly formed [Moderates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderates_\(Denmark\)), being a cross-bloc government. This marked the first time in more than 40 years that the Social Democrats and the Liberal Party, two historical political rivals, have been together in a government.[^cite_note-53]

In the [2025 Danish local elections](74940717.md), the Social Democrats suffered significant losses, and losing in [Copenhagen](5166.md) after more than 100 years.[^cite_note-54] In the [2026 Danish general election](72208286.md), the Social Democrats lost 12 seats, their worst result since 1903. However, the Social Democrats remained the largest party in the Folketing.[^cite_note-55]

Since its foundation, the motto of the party has been "[Liberty, Equality, Fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libert%C3%A9,_%C3%A9galit%C3%A9,_fraternit%C3%A9)" and these values are still described as central in the party program. In the political program, these values are described as being consistent with a focus on solidarity with the poorest and social welfare to those who need it, with individual responsibility in relation to other members in society and with an increased involvement in the [European Union](9317.md) project.[^cite_note-56]

As well as adopting more [left-leaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-leaning) economics, the party has become increasingly sceptical of [immigration](4599312.md) from the late 2010s. The party believes that immigration has had negative consequences for much of the population, a more pressing issue since at least 2001 after the [11 September attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_September_attacks) which intensified during the [2015 European migrant crisis](46415102.md). It also returned to a more sceptical view of [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism), arguing that its adherence to [Third Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Way) politics, its application of [centrist](39619035.md) and [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal) economics, and supporting unrestricted [economic globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_globalisation) contributed to the party's poor electoral performance in the late 2000s and early 2010s.[^cite_note-The_Guardian-17][^cite_note-Politico-18]

The party is described as [anti-globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalisation), [anti-immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-immigrant), and critical of Islam. According to the party, Muslim immigrants do not respect the Danish judicial system, Muslim women abstain from working for religious reasons, and they are also subject to "massive social control" in their immigrant communities.[^cite_note-57] In a biography written before becoming the prime minister in 2019, [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) wrote: "For me, it is becoming increasingly clear that the price of unregulated globalisation, mass immigration and the free movement of labour is paid for by the lower classes."[^cite_note-58] Frederiksen combined opposition to immigration and support for [social cohesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_cohesion) with criticism of [globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalisation), wealth inequality and erosion of workers' rights.[^cite_note-59] Economist [Mark Blyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Blyth) wrote that under Frederiksen, the Danish Social Democrats have "torn up the neoliberal rule book" in favor of combining increased government spending with immigration controls.[^cite_note-60]

The party had factions that promoted anti-immigration policies since the 1980s, but it was Mette Frederiksen that completed the party's right-turn on immigration. Since then, Social Democrats have been supporting strict controls of immigration, arguing that it is integral to "a new class struggle" as it protects the national culture, rural communities and the working-class identities of Denmark.[^cite_note-61] The party's proposals include expulsion of asylum seekers, a cap on non-Western immigration, and a requirement for immigrants to work at least 37 hours per week before being given a right to social and welfare benefits.[^cite_note-axford-11] It links immigration to sexual violence, trafficking and erosion of Danish culture.[^cite_note-catalano-62] The party also legally obliged rejected asylum applicants to reside in "return centres" and be excluded from the Danish labour market.[^cite_note-dostal8-63] The party justified the strict migration policy as a defence of Denmark's strong welfare state, with the 2026 party programme stating that "we must distinguish between those who can and want to (belong to) Denmark, and those who do not."[^cite_note-64]

Social Democrats adopted a conservative stance on cultural diversity, supporting repatriation rather than integration, postulating a ban on burqas and niqabs, and voting in favour of a law allowing Danish authorities to confiscate valuable items from refugees crossing the border.[^cite_note-axford-11] It also implemented a ban on room prayers in schools, strict requirements for family reunions, and a "ghetto plan", where immigrant areas designated as "ghettos" were given special regulation such as doubled punishments for certain crimes, easier access to personal information of residents for state authorities, and obligatory day care institutions for children (with non-attendance resulting in welfare grants cuts).[^cite_note-65] The party is considered to have "moved strongly to the center" on other cultural issues;[^cite_note-66] it makes no mention of gender and racial equality in its ideological declarations.[^cite_note-catalano-62]

These stances were compared to the ones of [radical right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_\(Europe\)) parties and argued to have made them mainstream, with critics suggesting that the far-right was slowed at a high cost in terms of tolerance, as also reflected in polling in a country that was more tolerant compared to other European countries. Rune Stubager, a political science professor at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University), argued that the move had prevented them "from growing beyond a certain segment of the electorate ... like we've seen in other countries where mainstream parties have not tightened immigration regulation to the same extent".[^cite_note-67] Valur Ingimundarson of the [University of Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iceland) wrote that the Social Democrats had "revised their immigration policy in such a radical way that it echoes many of the core tenets of the right-wing [Danish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_People's_Party)", and "embraced the anti-immigration and anti-refugee stance of the populist right".[^cite_note-68] [Josef Joffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Joffe) described the party's new stance as a combination of a "harsh anti-immigrant—but generous social—policy", and argued that by adopting such platform, the Social Democrats had "put on rightish clothes".[^cite_note-69] *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy)* argued that the party "adopted the far-right's anti-immigration stance", citing their rejection of the [United Nations](31769.md) annual quota of refugees resettled, support prison sentences for immigrant parents who take their children on extended visits to their home country, closing asylum centers, advocating detaining asylum seekers offshore and establishing facilities in North African countries instead.[^cite_note-70]

Since then, the party is described as a "[left conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_conservative)" social democratic party that "places itself to the left on issues related to welfare and redistribution, and right on cultural and value‐related issues".[^cite_note-etzerodt-10] Its ideology was also described as a combination of "populism, socialism and [cultural conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_conservatism)",[^cite_note-71] and Social Democrats are compared to the [Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht_Alliance), a German "left-conservative" party that split from [Die Linke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Linke).[^cite_note-72] The party was also described as [welfare chauvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_chauvinist),[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-enggist-74][^cite_note-75] as well as [nativist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_\(politics\)).[^cite_note-76] The party is thus seen to have taken a "nationalist and anti-immigrant turn", and increasingly came to be "perceived as a right-wing party".[^cite_note-77] Frederiksen argued that her party had previously "underestimated the challenge of mass migration" and that the "economic policy and foreign policy in Europe have been too liberal."[^cite_note-dostal8-63]

## Political leadership

The party leader since 2015 is [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md). She succeeded [Helle Thorning-Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helle_Thorning-Schmidt), who stepped down after the left bloc's defeat in the [2015 Danish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Danish_general_election). Deputy Party Leaders are [Lennart Damsbo-Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lennart_Damsbo-Andersen) and [Christian Rabjerg Madsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Rabjerg_Madsen). The Secretary General is [Annette Lind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annette_Lind).[^cite_note-78]

## Election results

The Social Democrats governed Denmark for most of the 20th century, with a few intermissions such as the [Conservative People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_People's_Party_\(Denmark\))-led government of [Poul Schlüter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poul_Schl%C3%BCter) in the 1980s. It continued to be Denmark's largest party until 2001 when [Anders Fogh Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Fogh_Rasmussen)'s liberal [Venstre](403109.md) party gained a landslide victory, becoming the largest party and forming a centre-right government. From 2015 and onwards, The Social Democrats have again been the largest party in Denmark. The Social Democrats returned to government from 2011 to 2015 and since 2019.

## Youth wings

Main articles: [Frit Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frit_Forum) and [Social Democratic Youth of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Youth_of_Denmark)

The Social Democratic Youth of Denmark (Danish: *Danmarks Socialdemokratiske Ungdom*) is the Social Democrats' youth wing. It was founded on 8 February 1920 and is an independent organization from the Social Democrats. This allows them to formulate their own policies and make their own campaigns. Prominent Social Democrats beginning their political work in the Social Democratic Youth include prime ministers [Hans Hedtoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Hedtoft), [H. C. Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._C._Hansen), [Jens Otto Krag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Otto_Krag), [Anker Jørgensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anker_J%C3%B8rgensen) and [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md), as well as ministers [Per Hækkerup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_H%C3%A6kkerup) and [Morten Bødskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morten_B%C3%B8dskov).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Frit Forum is the Social Democrats' student organization. It was founded in 1943 in [Copenhagen](5166.md). It has since 1973 been organizationally part of Social Democratic Youth. Prominent members previously leading Frit Forum include prime minister [Poul Nyrup Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poul_Nyrup_Rasmussen) and other leaders of the Social Democrats [Mogens Lykketoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogens_Lykketoft) and [Svend Auken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svend_Auken).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]
