A partial [lunar eclipse](18298.md) occurred at the Moon's [ascending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit on Friday, August 28, 2026,[^cite_note-\:0-1] with an umbral [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitude_of_eclipse) of 0.9319. A lunar eclipse occurs when the [Moon](19331.md) moves into the [Earth's shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_shadow), causing the Moon to be darkened. A partial lunar eclipse occurs when one part of the Moon is in the Earth's umbra, while the other part is in the Earth's penumbra. Unlike a [solar eclipse](5024887.md), which can only be viewed from a relatively small area of the world, a lunar eclipse may be viewed from anywhere on the night side of Earth. Occurring about 5.8 days after [apogee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (08\:20 UTC, August 22) and about 9.7 days before perigee (20\:26 UTC, September 6), the Moon's apparent diameter was less than average.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

This lunar eclipse was the last of an almost [tetrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrad_\(astronomy\)), with the others being on [March 14, 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2025_lunar_eclipse) (total), [September 8, 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2025_lunar_eclipse) (total), and [March 3, 2026](16448588.md) (total).

## Visibility

The eclipse was completely visible over [North](21139.md) and [South America](26769.md), seen rising over the central [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) and setting over [Africa](5334607.md), [Europe](9239.md), and the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-4]

Storms were forecasted to impact the [East Coast](89126.md) in the United States, which led to no visibility of partiality.[^cite_note-5]

## Eclipse season

See also: [Eclipse cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_cycle)

This eclipse is part of an [eclipse season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_season), a period, roughly every six months, when eclipses occur. Only two (or occasionally three) eclipse seasons occur each year, and each season lasts about 35 days and repeats just short of six months (173 days) later; thus two full eclipse seasons always occur each year. Either two or three eclipses happen each eclipse season. In the sequence below, each eclipse is separated by a [fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnight).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-\:0-1]

### Half-Saros cycle

A lunar eclipse will be preceded and followed by solar eclipses by 9 years and 5.5 days (a [half saros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saros_\(astronomy\)#Relationship_between_lunar_and_solar_saros_.28sar.29)).[^cite_note-15] This lunar eclipse is related to two total solar eclipses of [Solar Saros 145](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Saros_145).[^cite_note-16]
