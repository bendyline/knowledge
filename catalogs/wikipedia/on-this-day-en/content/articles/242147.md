**Ubon Ratchathani** ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): อุบลราชธานี, pronounced [\[ʔù.bōn râːt.tɕʰā.tʰāː.nīː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Thai); [Northeastern Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Thai_language): อุบลราชธานี, pronounced [\[ʔǔ.bōn lâːt.sā.tʰa᷇ː.ni᷇ː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Lao)), often shortened to **Ubon** (อุบลฯ), is one of Thailand's seventy-six [provinces](204637.md). It lies in [lower northeastern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan#Administrative_divisions) (also called [Isan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan)). Ubon is about 630 km (390 mi) from Bangkok.[^cite_note-5] Neighboring provinces are (from west clockwise) [Sisaket](292014.md), [Yasothon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasothon_province), and [Amnat Charoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnat_Charoen_province).

Five inscriptions of the sixth and seventh centuries come from the province, and one of the hospital inscriptions of the Angkorian king [Jayavarman VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayavarman_VII) was found near the city. The province meets [Laos](17752.md) and [Cambodia](334751.md) on the [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong) and in the [Dângrêk Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A2ngr%C3%AAk_Mountains), and its national parks include [Pha Taem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pha_Taem_National_Park), known for its prehistoric rock paintings. To the north and east it borders [Salavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salavan_province) and [Champasak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champasak_province) of [Laos](17752.md), to the south [Preah Vihear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preah_Vihear_province) of [Cambodia](334751.md).

## History

Five inscriptions from the province belong to a corpus of twenty-four that Chaowanee Lekkla refers to Zhēnlà: Pak Nam Mun I and II, Pak Dom Noi, Tham Phu Manai and Ban Tung Lung, with a sixth in Wat Supattanaram. Brick ruins and a broken *linga* on a moonstone-shaped pedestal were found at Tham Phu Manai.[^cite_note-lekkla2019-6]<sup>: 89–95 </sup> She places the Mun-river group 290 km north of [Sambor Prei Kuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambor_Prei_Kuk) by the [Mun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mun_River).[^cite_note-lekkla2019-6]<sup>: 97 </sup>

One of the seven Sanskrit inscriptions of *śaka* 1108, that is 1186, recording hospital foundations of the Angkorian king [Jayavarman VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayavarman_VII) (r. 1181–1218) was found near Ubon; the others of the group come from the [Nakhon Ratchasima](204656.md), [Chaiyaphum](239573.md) and [Surin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surin_province) regions and from Sai Fong on the Mekong, in what is now [Laos](17752.md).[^cite_note-13th-7]<sup>: 50 </sup>

The city was founded in the late 18th century by Thao Kham Phong, descendant of Phra Wo and Phra Ta, who escaped from King Siribunsan of Vientiane into Siam Kingdom during the reign of [King Taksin the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Taksin_the_Great). Later Thao Kham Phong was appointed to be "Phra Pathum Wongsa," the first ruler of [Ubon Ratchathani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubon_Ratchathani_Province). In 1792, [Ubon Ratchathani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubon_Ratchathani_Province) became a province, and was also the administrative center of the monthon Isan.

Until 1972, [Ubon Ratchathani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubon_Ratchathani_Province) was the largest province of [Thailand](30128.md) by area. [Yasothon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasothon_Province) was split off from [Ubon Ratchathani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubon_Ratchathani_Province) in 1972, followed by [Amnat Charoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnat_Charoen_Province) in 1993.

Before it became a province. Ubon Ratchathani was the administrative center of the monthon Isan, of which monthon Ubon was split off. In 1925 it became part of monthon Nakhon Ratchasima, with the abolishment of the monthon in 1933 the province became a first level subdivision of the country.

## Symbols

The [provincial seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seals_of_provinces_of_Thailand) shows a [lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphaeaceae) flower in a pond. This alludes to the meaning of the name of the province, which translates to 'royal city of the lotus flower'. The provincial flower is the lotus (*[Nymphaea lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphaea_lotus)*). The [provincial tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Thai_provincial_trees) is the Yang-na (*[Dipterocarpus alatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipterocarpus_alatus)*). [Black-eared catfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasius_larnaudii) (*[Pangasius larnaudii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasius_larnaudii)*) is the provincial aquatic animal.

## Economy

Ubon Ratchathani's economy is largely agricultural. It is the nation's leading rice-producing province. It earns more than 10 billion baht a year from rice sales.[^cite_note-8] It is also an important grower of casava.

Around 50% of the workforce in Ubon Ratchathani are in the agricultural sector.

It also generates billions of baht from manufacturing.

Ubon Ratchathani is also along the Meekong with a major crossing into Laos at Chongmek. It also borders Cambodia. This makes it important for both trade and as a tourism gateway.

## Geography

At [Khong Chiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khong_Chiam_District) the [Mun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mun_River), the biggest river of the [Khorat Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorat_Plateau), joins the [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong), which forms the northeastern boundary of Thailand with Laos. It is called "Maenam Song Si" or the "Mun River alluvium" because the brown water from Mekong River mixes with the blue water of the Mun. It is about 84 km (52 mi) from [Ubon Ratchathani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubon_Ratchathani) city centre.[^cite_note-9]

The borders of Thailand, Laos and Cambodia meet in the [Dângrêk Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A2ngr%C3%AAk_Mountains). The area is promoted as the "[Emerald Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_Triangle_\(Southeast_Asia\))", in contrast to the "[Golden Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_\(Southeast_Asia\))" in the north of Thailand. "Emerald" refers to the largely intact monsoon forests there. The total forest area is 2,808 km<sup>2</sup> (1,084 sq mi) or 18 percent of provincial area.[^cite_note-AREA-1]

- Phu Chong–Na Yoi National Park is in the mountainous southern region of the province.

- Kaeng Tana National Park is in [Khong Chiam District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khong_Chiam_District).

  [^cite_note-11]

- Pha Taem National Park, plateaus and hills dominate the park landscape. The sheer cliffs here are a result of earthquakes. The interesting places in the national park are Pha Taem and Pha Kham. On the cliffs surfaces are numerous prehistoric cave paintings from 3,000 to 4,000 years ago. These paintings depict scenes of fishing, rice farming, figures of people, animals, hands and geometric designs that depict life during the pre-historic time and reflect the ancient lifestyle of the people who lived there.

### Local government

As of 26 November 2019 there are:[^cite_note-16] one Ubon Ratchathani Provincial Administration Organisation (*_ongkan borihan suan changwat_*) and 60 municipal (*thesaban*) areas in the province. Ubon Ratchathani has city (*[thesaban nakhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_nakhon)*) status. Chaeramae, Det Udom, Phibun Mangsahan and Warin Chamrap have town (*[thesaban mueang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_mueang)*) status. Further 54 subdistrict municipalities (*[thesaban tambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_tambon)*). The non-municipal areas are administered by 179 Subdistrict Administrative Organisations – SAO (*ongkan borihan suan tambon*).
