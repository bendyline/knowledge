**Kansas** ([/ˈkænzəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e9/En-us-Kansas.ogg/En-us-Kansas.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e9/En-us-Kansas.ogg/En-us-Kansas.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*KAN-zəss*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-11] is a landlocked [state](18618239.md) in the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) region of the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-12] It borders [Nebraska](21647.md) to the north, [Colorado](5399.md) to the west, [Oklahoma](22489.md) to the south, and [Missouri](19571.md) to the east. Kansas is named after the [Kansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_River), in turn named after the [Kansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaw_people) people.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Its [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) is [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas), and its [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Kansas) is [Wichita](114932.md); however, the largest urban area is the bi-state [Kansas City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_metropolitan_area) surrounding [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), which straddles the border of Kansas and Missouri.

For thousands of years, what is now known as Kansas was home to numerous and diverse [Indigenous tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Indians). The first settlement of non-indigenous people in Kansas occurred in 1827 at [Fort Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Leavenworth). The pace of settlement accelerated in the 1850s, in the midst of political wars over the [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) debate. When it was officially opened to settlement by the U.S. government in 1854 with the [Kansas–Nebraska Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas%E2%80%93Nebraska_Act), conflict between abolitionist [Free-Staters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-Stater_\(Kansas\)) from [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) and pro-slavery settlers from neighboring Missouri broke out over the question of whether Kansas would become a [free state or a slave state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states), in a period known as [Bleeding Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeding_Kansas). On January 29, 1861,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Kansas entered the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) as a free state, hence the unofficial nickname "The Free State". Passage of the [Homestead Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Acts) in 1862 brought a further influx of settlers, and the booming cattle trade of the 1870s attracted some of the Wild West's most iconic figures to western Kansas.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

As of 2015, Kansas was among the most productive agricultural states, producing high yields of wheat, corn, [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), and [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean).[^cite_note-21] In addition to its traditional strength in agriculture, Kansas possesses an extensive aerospace industry. Kansas, which has an area of 82,278 square miles (213,100 square kilometers) is the [15th-largest state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area), the [36th most-populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) of the 50 states, with a population of 2,940,865[^cite_note-22] according to the 2020 census, and the [10th least densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_the_United_States_by_population_density). Residents of Kansas are called *Kansans* or *Jayhawkers*.[^cite_note-23] [Mount Sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sunflower) is Kansas's highest point at 4,039 feet (1,231 meters).[^cite_note-24]

Kansas is generally considered to be the geographic center of the [contiguous United States](83759.md), with [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon,_Kansas) being [approximately the center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_center_of_the_contiguous_United_States).

## Etymology

The name *Kansas* derives from the [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages) term *Akansa*, referring to the [Quapaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quapaw) people. These were a [Dhegiha Siouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhegiha_Siouan)-speaking people who settled in [Arkansas](1930.md) around the 13th century. The stem -*kansa* is named after the [Kaw people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaw_people), also known as the *Kansa*, a federally recognized Native American tribe.[^cite_note-25] The [tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federally_recognized_tribes)'s name (natively *_kką\:ze_*) is often said to mean "people of the (south) wind", although this was probably not the term's original meaning.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## History

Main article: [History of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kansas)



Samuel Seymour's 1819 illustration of a [Kansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaw_people) lodge and dance is the oldest drawing known to have been done in Kansas.

Before [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas), Kansas was occupied by the [Caddoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddoan) [Wichita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_people) and later the [Siouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouan) [Kaw people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaw_people). The Indigenous people in the eastern part of the state generally lived in villages along the river valleys. Those in the western part of the state were semi-nomadic and hunted large herds of [bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison). The first European to set foot in present-day Kansas was the Spanish [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) [Francisco Vázquez de Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_V%C3%A1zquez_de_Coronado), who explored the area in 1541.

Between 1763 and 1803, the territory of Kansas was integrated into [Spanish Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Louisiana). During that period, Governor [Luis de Unzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_Unzaga) 'le Conciliateur' promoted expeditions and good relations with the [Amerindians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindians). Explorer Antoine de Marigny and others continued trading across the [Kansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_River), especially at its confluence with the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), tributaries of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River).[^cite_note-28]

In 1803, most of modern Kansas was [acquired by the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_territorial_acquisitions) as part of the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase). Southwest Kansas, however, was still a part of Spain, Mexico, and the [Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas) until the conclusion of the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) in 1848, when these lands were [ceded to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Cession). From 1812 to 1821, Kansas was part of the [Missouri Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Territory). The [Santa Fe Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Trail) traversed Kansas from 1821 to 1880, transporting manufactured goods from [Missouri](19571.md) and silver and furs from [Santa Fe, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe,_New_Mexico). [Wagon ruts from the trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Trail_Remains) are still visible in the prairie today.

In 1827, [Fort Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Leavenworth) became the first permanent settlement of white Americans in the future state.[^cite_note-29] The [Kansas–Nebraska Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas%E2%80%93Nebraska_Act) became law on May 30, 1854, establishing [Nebraska Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Territory) and [Kansas Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Territory), and opening the area to broader settlement by whites. [Kansas Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Territory) stretched all the way to the Continental Divide and included the sites of present-day [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver), [Colorado Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado), and [Pueblo](24158.md).

### Bleeding Kansas and the Civil War

The first non-military settlement of Euro-Americans in Kansas Territory consisted of [abolitionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States) from [Massachusetts](1645518.md) and other [Free-Staters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-Stater_\(Kansas\)) who founded the town of Lawrence and attempted to stop the spread of slavery from neighboring Missouri.

Missouri and [Arkansas](1930.md) continually sent settlers into Kansas Territory along its eastern border to sway votes in favor of slavery prior to Kansas statehood elections. Directly presaging the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) these forces collided, entering into skirmishes and guerrilla conflicts that earned the territory the nickname [Bleeding Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeding_Kansas). These included [John Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_\(abolitionist\))'s [Pottawatomie massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottawatomie_massacre) of 1856.

Kansas was [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) as a free state on January 29, 1861, making it the 34th state to join the United States. By that time, the violence in Kansas had largely subsided, but during the Civil War, on August 21, 1863, [William Quantrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Quantrill) led several hundred of his supporters on a raid into [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas), destroying much of the city and killing nearly 200 people. He was roundly condemned by both the conventional [Confederate military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army) and the partisan rangers commissioned by the [Missouri legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_legislature). His application to that body for a commission was flatly rejected due to his pre-war criminal record.[^cite_note-30]

### Settlement and the Wild West

Passage of the [Homestead Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Acts) in 1862 accelerated settlement and agricultural development in the state. After the Civil War, many veterans constructed homesteads in Kansas. Many African Americans also looked to Kansas as the land of "[John Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_\(abolitionist\))" and, led by [freedmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedmen) like [Benjamin "Pap" Singleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_%22Pap%22_Singleton), began establishing black colonies in the state. Leaving southern states in the late 1870s because of increasing discrimination, they became known as [Exodusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exodusters).

At the same time, the [Chisholm Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisholm_Trail) was opened and the [Wild West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Old_West) era commenced in Kansas. Storied lawman [Wild Bill Hickok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Bill_Hickok) was a deputy marshal at [Fort Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Riley) and a marshal at [Hays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hays,_Kansas) and [Abilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abilene,_Kansas). [Dodge City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_City,_Kansas) was home to both [Bat Masterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat_Masterson) and [Wyatt Earp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyatt_Earp), who worked as lawmen in the town. The [Dalton Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_Gang) robbed trains and banks throughout Kansas and the Southwest and maintained a hideout in [Meade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meade,_Kansas). During its 19th-century peak, Dodge City shipped up to 500,000 Texas cattle annually, cementing its reputation as the "Queen of the Cowtowns".

### 20th century

In response to demands of [Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodists) and other [evangelical Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Protestants), in 1881 Kansas became the first U.S. state to adopt a constitutional amendment [prohibiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition) all [alcoholic beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Kansas), which was repealed in 1948. Anti-saloon activist [Carrie Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Nation) vandalized her first saloon in [Kiowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiowa,_Kansas) in 1900. In 1922, suffragist [Ella Uphay Mowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Uphay_Mowry) became the first female gubernatorial candidate in the state when she ran as "Mrs. W\.D. Mowry". She later stated: "Someone had to be the pioneer. I firmly believe that some day a woman will sit in the governor's chair in Kansas."[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

Kansas suffered severe environmental damage in the 1930s due to the combined effects of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) and the [Dust Bowl](59749.md), and large numbers of people left southwestern Kansas in particular for better opportunities elsewhere.[^cite_note-34] The outbreak of [World War II](32927.md) spurred rapid growth in aircraft manufacturing near Wichita in the so-called [Battle of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kansas), and the aerospace sector remains a significant portion of the Kansan economy to this day.

## Geography

Kansas is bordered by [Nebraska](21647.md) to the north; [Missouri](19571.md) to the east; [Oklahoma](22489.md) to the south; and [Colorado](5399.md) to the west. The state is divided into [105 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Kansas) with [628 cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Kansas), with its largest county by area being [Butler County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_County,_Kansas).[^cite_note-35] Kansas is located [equidistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equidistant) from the Pacific and Atlantic oceans. The [geographic center of the 48 contiguous states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_center_of_the_contiguous_United_States) is in [Smith County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_County,_Kansas) near [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon,_Kansas). Until 1989, the [Meades Ranch Triangulation Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meades_Ranch_Triangulation_Station) in [Osborne County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osborne_County,_Kansas) was the geodetic center of North America: the central reference point for all maps of North America. The geographic center of Kansas is in [Barton County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_County,_Kansas).

### Geology

Main article: [Geology of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Kansas)

Kansas is underlain by a sequence of horizontal to gently westward [dipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_and_dip) [sedimentary rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock). A sequence of [Mississippian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_age), [Pennsylvanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvanian_\(geology\)) and [Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian) rocks outcrop in the eastern and southern part of the state. The state's western half has exposures of [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) through Tertiary sediments, the latter derived from the [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) of the [uplifted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_uplift) [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) to the west. These are underlain by older Paleozoic and Mesozoic sediments which correlate well with the outcrops to the east. The state's northeastern corner was subjected to [glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaciation) in the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) and is covered by [glacial drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_drift) and [loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess).

### Topography

The western third of the state, lying in the [great central plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) of the United States, has a generally flat or undulating surface, while the eastern third has many hills and forests; the middle third is generally hilly, with more sparse vegetation than the eastern third.[^cite_note-36] The land gradually rises from east to west. Its altitude ranges from 684 ft (208 m) along the [Verdigris River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdigris_River) at [Coffeyville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffeyville,_Kansas) in [Montgomery County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Kansas), to 4,039 ft (1,231 m) at [Mount Sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sunflower), 0.5 miles (0.80 kilometers) from the Colorado border, in [Wallace County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_County,_Kansas). It is a common misconception that Kansas is the flattest state in the nation—in 2003, a tongue-in-cheek study famously declared the state "flatter than a pancake".[^cite_note-37] Kansas has a maximum topographic relief of 3,360 ft (1,020 m),[^cite_note-38] making it the 23rd flattest U.S. state measured by maximum relief.[^cite_note-39]

### Rivers

See also: [List of rivers of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Kansas)



[Spring River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_River_\(Missouri\)), Kansas

Around 74 mi (119 km) of the state's northeastern boundary is defined by the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River). The [Kansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_River) (locally known as the Kaw), formed by the junction of the [Smoky Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoky_Hill_River) and [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_River) rivers at appropriately named [Junction City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction_City,_Kansas), joins the Missouri River at [Kansas City](115009.md), after a course of 170 mi (270 km) across the northeastern part of the state.

The [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River) ([pronunciation varies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River#Pronunciations)), rising in [Colorado](5399.md), flows with a bending course for nearly 500 mi (800 km) across the western and southern parts of the state. With its tributaries, (the [Little Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Arkansas_River), [Ninnescah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninnescah_River), Walnut, [Cow Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow_Creek_\(Kansas\)), [Cimarron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimarron_River_\(Arkansas_River\)), Verdigris, and the [Neosho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neosho_River)), it forms the southern drainage system of the state.

Kansas's other rivers are the [Saline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saline_River_\(Kansas\)) and Solomon Rivers, tributaries of the Smoky Hill River; the [Big Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Blue_River_\(Kansas\)), [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River_\(Kansas\)), and [Wakarusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakarusa_River), which flow into the Kansas River; and the [Marais des Cygnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marais_des_Cygnes_River), a tributary of the Missouri River. Spring River is located between [Riverton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverton,_Kansas) and [Baxter Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baxter_Springs,_Kansas).

### Climate



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Kansas, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](42778673.md)



Clouds in northeastern Kansas



Kansas summer wheat and storm panorama

In the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Kansas has three climates: humid continental, semi-arid steppe, and humid subtropical. The eastern two-thirds of the state (especially the northeastern portion) has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate), with cool to cold winters and hot, often humid summers. Most of the precipitation falls during both the summer and the spring. The [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA) [hardiness zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) for Kansas range from Zone 5b (−15 °F to −10 °F) in the North to Zone 7a (0 °F to 5 °F) in the South.[^cite_note-46]

The western third of the state—from roughly the [U.S. Route 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_83) corridor westward—has a [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) climate. Summers are hot, often very hot, and generally less humid. Winters are highly changeable between warm and very cold. The western region receives an average of about 16 inches (410 millimeters) of precipitation per year. [Chinook winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_wind) in the winter can warm western Kansas all the way into the 80 degrees Fahrenheit (27 degrees Celsius) range.

The south-central and southeastern portions of the state, including the [Wichita](114932.md) area, have a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) with hot and humid summers, milder winters, and more precipitation than elsewhere in Kansas. Some features of all three climates can be found in most of the state, with droughts and changeable weather between dry and humid not uncommon, and both warm and cold spells in the winter.

Temperatures in areas between U.S. Routes 83 and [81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_81), as well as the southwestern portion of the state along and south of [U.S. 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_50), reach 90 °F (32 °C) or above on most days of June, July, and August. High humidity added to the high temperatures sends the [heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index) into life-threatening territory, especially in Wichita, [Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchinson,_Kansas), [Salina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salina,_Kansas), [Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell,_Kansas), [Hays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hays,_Kansas), and [Great Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Bend,_Kansas). Temperatures are often higher in [Dodge City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_City,_Kansas), [Garden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City,_Kansas), and [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal,_Kansas), but the heat index in those three cities is usually lower than the actual air temperature.

Although temperatures of 100 °F (38 °C) or higher are not as common in areas east of U.S. 81, higher humidity and the [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island) effect lead most summer days to heat indices between 107 and 114 °F (42 and 46 °C) in [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas), [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas), and the [Kansas City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_metropolitan_area). Also, combined with humidity between 85 and 95 percent, dangerous heat indices can be experienced at every hour of the day.

Precipitation ranges from about 47 inches (1,200 mm) annually in the state's southeast corner to about 16 inches (410 mm) in the southwest. Snowfall ranges from around 5 inches (130 mm) in the fringes of the south, to 35 inches (890 mm) in the far northwest. Frost-free days range from more than 200 days in the south, to 130 days in the northwest. Thus, Kansas is the country's ninth or tenth sunniest state, depending on the source. Western Kansas is as sunny as parts of California and [Arizona](21883824.md).

Kansas is prone to severe weather, especially in the spring and the early-summer. Despite the frequent sunshine throughout much of the state, due to its location at a climatic boundary prone to intrusions of multiple air masses, the state is vulnerable to strong and severe thunderstorms. Some of these storms become [supercell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercell) thunderstorms; these can produce some [tornadoes](37530.md), occasionally those of [EF3](3724479.md) strength or higher. Kansas averages more than 50 tornadoes annually.[^cite_note-Annual_Average_Number_of_Tornadoes-47] Severe thunderstorms sometimes drop some very large [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail) over Kansas as well. Furthermore, these storms can even bring in flash flooding and damaging straight line winds.

According to NOAA, the all-time highest temperature recorded in Kansas is (121 °F or 49.4 °C) on July 24, 1936, near [Alton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alton,_Kansas) in [Osborne County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osborne_County,_Kansas), and the all-time low is −40 °F (−40 °C) on February 13, 1905, near [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon,_Kansas) in [Smith County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_County,_Kansas). Alton and Lebanon are approximately 50 miles (80 km) apart.

Kansas's record high of 121 °F (49.4 °C) ties with [North Dakota](21651.md) for the fifth-highest record high in an American state, behind California (134 °F or 56.7 °C), [Arizona](21883824.md) (128 °F or 53.3 °C), [Nevada](21216.md) (125 °F or 51.7 °C), and [New Mexico](21649.md) (122 °F or 50 °C).

Kansas has 627 [incorporated cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation). By state statute, cities are divided into three classes as determined by the population obtained "by any census of enumeration". A city of the third class has a population of less than 5,000, but cities reaching a population of more than 2,000 may be certified as a city of the second class. The second class is limited to cities with a population of less than 25,000, and upon reaching a population of more than 15,000, they may be certified as a city of the first class. First and second class cities are independent of any [township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_township) and are not included within the township's territory.

### Life expectancy

The residents of Kansas have a life expectancy near the U.S. national average. In 2013, males in Kansas lived an average of 76.6 years compared to a male national average of 76.7 years and females lived an average of 81.0 years compared to a female national average of 81.5 years. Increases in life expectancy between 1980 and 2013 were below the national average for males and near the national average for females. Male life expectancy in Kansas between 1980 and 2014 increased by an average of 5.2 years, compared to a male national average of a 6.7-year increase. Life expectancy for females in Kansas between 1980 and 2014 increased by 4.3 years, compared to a female national average of a 4.0 year increase.[^cite_note-61]

Using 2017–2019 data, the [Robert Wood Johnson Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wood_Johnson_Foundation) calculated that life expectancy for Kansas counties ranged from 75.8 years for [Wyandotte County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyandotte_County,_Kansas) to 81.7 years for [Johnson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County,_Kansas). Life expectancy for the state as a whole was 78.5 years.[^cite_note-Robert_Wood_Johnson-62] Life expectancy for the United States as a whole in 2019 was 78.8 years.[^cite_note-Mortality-63]

#### Northeast Kansas



[University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) Strong Hall in Lawrence

The northeastern portion of the state, extending from the eastern border to [Junction City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction_City,_Kansas) and from the Nebraska border to south of Johnson County is home to more than 1.5 million people in the Kansas City (Kansas portion), Manhattan, Lawrence, and Topeka [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area). [Overland Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overland_Park,_Kansas), a young city incorporated in 1960, has the largest population and the largest land area in the county. It is home to [Johnson County Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County_Community_College).

Olathe is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) and home to [Johnson County Executive Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County_Executive_Airport). The cities of Olathe, [Shawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee,_Kansas), [De Soto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Soto,_Kansas) and [Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardner,_Kansas) have some of the state's fastest growing populations. The cities of Overland Park, [Lenexa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenexa,_Kansas), Olathe, De Soto, and Gardner are also notable because they lie along the former route of the [Santa Fe Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Trail). Among cities with at least one thousand residents, [Mission Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Hills,_Kansas) has the highest median income in the state.

Several institutions of higher education are located in Northeast Kansas including [Baker University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_University) (the oldest university in the state, founded in 1858 and affiliated with the [United Methodist Church](32304.md)) in Baldwin City, [Benedictine College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine_College) (sponsored by [St. Benedict's Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Benedict's_Abbey) and [Mount St. Scholastica Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine_Sisters_of_Mount_St._Scholastica) and formed from the merger of St. Benedict's College (1858) and Mount St. Scholastica College (1923)) in Atchison, [MidAmerica Nazarene University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MidAmerica_Nazarene_University) in Olathe, [Ottawa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_University) in Ottawa and Overland Park, Kansas City Kansas Community College and KU Medical Center in Kansas City, and KU Edwards Campus in Overland Park. Less than an hour's drive to the west, [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas) is home to the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), the largest public university in the state, and [Haskell Indian Nations University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskell_Indian_Nations_University).

To the north, [Kansas City](115009.md), with the second-largest land area in the state, contains a number of diverse ethnic neighborhoods. Its attractions include the [Kansas Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Speedway), [Sporting Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_Kansas_City), [Kansas City Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Monarchs_\(American_Association\)), and [The Legends at Village West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legends_at_Village_West) retail and entertainment center. Nearby, Kansas's first settlement [Bonner Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonner_Springs,_Kansas)[^cite_note-autogenerated1-64] is home to several national and regional attractions including the [Providence Medical Center Amphitheater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_Wireless_Amphitheater_\(Bonner_Springs,_Kansas\)), the [National Agricultural Center and Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Agricultural_Center_and_Hall_of_Fame), and the annual [Kansas City Renaissance Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Renaissance_Festival). Further up the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), the city of [Lansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansing,_Kansas) is the home of the state's first maximum-security prison. Historic [Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavenworth,_Kansas), founded in 1854, was the first incorporated city in Kansas. North of the city, [Fort Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Leavenworth) is the oldest active Army post west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River). The city of [Atchison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atchison,_Kansas) was an early commercial center in the state and is well known as the birthplace of [Amelia Earhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_Earhart).



The [Kansas Statehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Statehouse)

To the west, nearly a quarter million people reside in the Topeka metropolitan area. [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas) is the state capital and home to [Washburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washburn_University) and [Washburn Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washburn_Institute_of_Technology). Built at a [Kansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_River) crossing along the old [Oregon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Trail), this historic city has several nationally registered historic places. Further westward along [Interstate 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_70_\(Kansas\)) and the Kansas River is [Junction City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction_City,_Kansas) with its historic limestone and brick buildings and nearby [Fort Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Riley), well known as the home to the [U.S. Army](32087.md)'s [1st Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) (nicknamed "the Big Red One"). A short distance away, the city of [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan,_Kansas) is home to [Kansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_University), the second-largest public university in the state and the nation's oldest land-grant university, dating back to 1863. South of the campus, [Aggieville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggieville) dates back to 1889 and is the state's oldest shopping district of its kind.

#### South Central Kansas



Downtown Wichita in the winter

In south-central Kansas, the [Wichita metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_metropolitan_area,_Kansas) is home to more than 600,000 people.[^cite_note-65] [Wichita](114932.md) is the largest city in the state in terms of both land area and population. 'The Air Capital' is a major manufacturing center for the aircraft industry and the home of [Wichita State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_State_University). Before Wichita was 'The Air Capital' it was a Cowtown.[^cite_note-gowichita1-66] With a number of nationally registered historic places, museums, and other entertainment destinations, it has a desire to become a cultural mecca in the Midwest. Wichita's population growth has grown by double digits and the surrounding suburbs are among the fastest growing cities in the state. The population of [Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddard,_Kansas) has grown by more than 11% per year since 2000.[^cite_note-2006SubEst-67] Other fast-growing cities include [Andover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andover,_Kansas), [Maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize,_Kansas), [Park City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_City,_Kansas), [Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby,_Kansas), and [Haysville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haysville,_Kansas).

Wichita was one of the first cities to add the city commissioner and city manager in their form of government.[^cite_note-gowichita1-66] Wichita is also home of the nationally recognized [Sedgwick County Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedgwick_County_Zoo).[^cite_note-gowichita1-66]



The [Cosmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmosphere), the world-renowned space museum in Hutchinson

Up river (the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River)) from Wichita is the city of [Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchinson,_Kansas). The city was built on one of the world's largest salt deposits (of what would form [Strataca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strataca)), and it has the world's largest and longest wheat elevator. It is also the home of [Kansas Cosmosphere and Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Cosmosphere_and_Space_Center), Prairie Dunes Country Club and the [Kansas State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_Fair). North of Wichita along [Interstate 135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_135_\(Kansas\)) is the city of [Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Kansas), the former western terminal of the [Santa Fe Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Railroad) and trailhead for the famed [Chisholm Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisholm_Trail). To the southeast of Wichita are the cities of [Winfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winfield,_Kansas) and [Arkansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_City,_Kansas) with historic architecture and the [Cherokee Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_Strip_\(Kansas\)) Museum (in Ark City). The city of [Udall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udall,_Kansas) was the site of the deadliest [tornado](37530.md) in Kansas on May 25, 1955; it killed 80 people in and near the city.[^cite_note-68]

#### Southeast Kansas



Pittsburg

[Southeast Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Kansas) has a unique history with a number of nationally registered historic places in this coal-mining region. Located in [Crawford County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawford_County,_Kansas) (dubbed the Fried Chicken Capital of Kansas), [Pittsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg,_Kansas) is the largest city in the region and the home of [Pittsburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg_State_University). The neighboring city of [Frontenac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontenac,_Kansas) in 1888 was the site of the worst mine disaster in the state in which an underground explosion killed 47 miners. "[Big Brutus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brutus)" is located 1.5 miles (2.4 km) outside the city of [West Mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Mineral,_Kansas). Along with the restored fort, historic [Fort Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Scott,_Kansas) has a national cemetery designated by President Lincoln in 1862. The region also shares a Media market with [Joplin, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joplin,_Missouri), a city in Southwest Missouri.

#### Central and North-Central Kansas



Cedar Point Mill, built in 1875 in Cedar Point, on the National Register of Historic Places

[Salina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salina,_Kansas) is the largest city in central and north-central Kansas. South of Salina is the small city of [Lindsborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsborg,_Kansas) with its numerous [Dala horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalecarlian_horse). Much of the architecture and decor of this town has a distinctly Swedish style. To the east along [Interstate 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_70_\(Kansas\)), the historic city of [Abilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abilene,_Kansas) was formerly a trailhead for the [Chisholm Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisholm_Trail) and was the boyhood home of President [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower), and is the site of his [Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenhower_Presidential_Center) and the tombs of the former president, First Lady and son who died in infancy. To the west is [Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas,_Kansas), the Grassroots Art Capital of Kansas.

#### Northwest Kansas

Milky Way over Monument Rocks, Kansas, US

Kansas's [Monument Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Rocks_\(Kansas\)) at night

Westward along the Interstate, the city of [Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell,_Kansas), traditionally the beginning of sparsely populated northwest Kansas, was the base of former U.S. Senator [Bob Dole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dole) and the boyhood home of U.S. Senator [Arlen Specter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlen_Specter). The city of [Hays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hays,_Kansas) is home to [Fort Hays State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Hays_State_University) and the Sternberg Museum of Natural History, and is the largest city in the northwest with a population of around 20,001.

Two other landmarks are located in smaller towns in [Ellis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_County,_Kansas): the "Cathedral of the Plains" is located 10 miles (16 km) east of Hays in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Kansas), and the boyhood home of [Walter Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Chrysler) is 15 miles (24 km) west of Hays in [Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis,_Kansas). West of Hays, population drops dramatically, even in areas along I-70, and only two towns containing populations of more than 4,000: [Colby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby,_Kansas) and [Goodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodland,_Kansas), which are located 35 miles (56 km) apart along I-70.

#### Southwest Kansas



The chalk rock badlands and cliffs in western Kansas, near Dighton

[Dodge City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_City,_Kansas), famously known for the cattle drive days of the late 19th century, was built along the old [Santa Fe Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Trail) route. The city of [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal,_Kansas) is located along the southern Santa Fe Trail route. The first wind farm in the state was built east of [Montezuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montezuma,_Kansas). [Garden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City,_Kansas) has the [Lee Richardson Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Richardson_Zoo). In 1992, a [short-lived secessionist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Kansas) advocated the secession of several counties in southwest Kansas.[^cite_note-69]

#### Around the state



Gove County Badlands

Located midway between Kansas City, Topeka, and Wichita in the heart of the Bluestem Region of the [Flint Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint_Hills), the city of [Emporia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emporia,_Kansas) has several nationally registered historic places and is the home of [Emporia State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emporia_State_University), well known for its Teachers College. It was also the home of newspaper man [William Allen White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Allen_White).

## Demographics

### Population



Population density map of Kansas in 2020



Kansas [population pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_pyramid)

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) estimates that the population of Kansas was 2,913,314 on July 1, 2019, a 2.11% increase since the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census) and an increase of 58,387, or 2.05%, since 2010.[^cite_note-PopEstUS-70] This includes a natural increase since the last census of 93,899 (246,484 births minus 152,585 deaths) and a decrease due to net migration of 20,742 people out of the state. [Immigration](15051.md) from outside the United States resulted in a net increase of 44,847 people, and migration within the country produced a net loss of 65,589 people.[^cite_note-2006StateEstComp-71] At the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), its population was 2,937,880.

In 2018, the top countries of origin for Kansas's immigrants were [Mexico](3966054.md), [India](14533.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), [Guatemala](17238567.md) and [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-72]

The population density of Kansas is 52.9 people per square mile.[^cite_note-73] The [center of population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population) of Kansas is located in [Chase County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_County,_Kansas), at [38°27′N 96°32′W  /  38.450°N 96.533°W  / 38.450; -96.533](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Kansas\&params=38_27_N_96_32_W_region\:US-KS_type\:landmark), approximately 3 miles (4.8 km) north of the community of [Strong City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_City,_Kansas).[^cite_note-74]

The focus on labor-efficient grain-based agriculture—such as a large wheat farm that requires only one or a few people with large [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_machinery) to operate, rather than a [vegetable farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_farming) that requires many people—is causing the [de-population of rural areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depopulation_of_the_Great_Plains) across Kansas.[^cite_note-\:0-75]

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 2,397 [homeless](19394651.md) people in Kansas.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

As of 2004, the population included 149,800 foreign-born (5.5% of the state population). The ten largest reported ancestry groups, which account for nearly 90% of the population, in the state are: [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American) (33.75%), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) (14.4%), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) (14.1%), [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_ancestry) (7.5%), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American) (4.4%), [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_American) (4.2%), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_American) (2.5%), [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_American) (2.4%), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American) (1.8%), and [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_American) (1.5%).[^cite_note-95] Kansas is also home to a large Czech community.[^cite_note-96]

Mexicans are present in the southwest and make up nearly half the population in certain counties. Many African Americans in Kansas are descended from the [Exodusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exodusters), newly freed blacks who fled the South for land in Kansas following the Civil War.[^cite_note-97]

There is a growing Asian community in Kansas. Since 1965, more and more Asian families have moved to Kansas from countries such as the Philippines, China, Korea, India, and Vietnam.[^cite_note-98]

### Native Americans



Kansas serves as the home to four federally recognized sovereign Native American nations. The preponderance of the state's Native American population resides on reservations.[^cite_note-101]

### Language

English is the most-spoken language in Kansas, with 91.3% of the population speaking only English at home as of the year 2000. 5.5% speak Spanish, 0.7% speak German, and 0.4% speak Vietnamese.[^cite_note-102]

[Kansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansa_language) represents the indigenous language spoken by the Kansa people, a Native American nation historically associated with the state of Kansas.[^cite_note-103]

## Economy

See also: [Kansas locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_locations_by_per_capita_income), [Golden Triangle of Meat-packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_of_Meat-packing), and [List of Kansas companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kansas_companies)

Kansas's total GDP (Gross Domestic Product) was \$241.3 billion in 2025.[^cite_note-110] The state's [per capita personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_personal_income) was \$69,510 in 2025, which ranked 33rd among U.S. states, and [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) was measured at \$68,925.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-u858-112]

As of 2023, Kansas had a total of 76,139 employer establishments.[^cite_note-115] In 2025, [small businesses](276495.md) made up 99.1% of all businesses in Kansas and employed 48.9% of the state's work force.[^cite_note-116]

In 2015, the job growth rate was 0.8%, among the lowest rates in America with only "10,900 total nonfarm jobs" added that year.[^cite_note-kansascity_2015-117] As of May 2025, the state's unemployment rate was 3.8%.[^cite_note-118]

The State of Kansas had a \$350 million budget shortfall in February 2017.[^cite_note-WP_2017_revolt-119] In February 2017, S\&P downgraded Kansas's credit rating to AA−.[^cite_note-NYT_S\&P_ratings_2017-120]

Nearly 90% of Kansas's land is devoted to agriculture.[^cite_note-\:0-75] The state's agricultural outputs are cattle, sheep, wheat, [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans), cotton, [hogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), and salt. As of 2018, there were 59,600 farms in Kansas, 86 (0.14%) of which are [certified organic farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_certification).[^cite_note-\:0-75] The average farm in the state is about 770 acres (more than a square mile), and in 2016, the average cost of running the farm was \$300,000.[^cite_note-\:0-75]

By far, the most significant agricultural crop in the state is wheat. Eastern Kansas is part of the [Grain Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_Belt), an area of major grain production in the central United States. Approximately 40% of all winter wheat grown in the U.S. is grown in Kansas.[^cite_note-\:0-75] Roughly 95% of the wheat grown in the state is [hard red winter wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_red_winter_wheat).[^cite_note-\:0-75] During 2016, farmers of conventionally grown wheat farmed 8.2 million acres and harvested an average of 57 bushels of wheat per acre.[^cite_note-\:0-75]

The industrial outputs are transportation equipment, commercial and private aircraft, food processing, publishing, chemical products, machinery, apparel, petroleum, and mining.

The state's economy is also heavily influenced by the aerospace industry. Several large aircraft corporations have manufacturing facilities in Wichita and Kansas City, including [Spirit AeroSystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_AeroSystems), [Bombardier Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Aerospace) ([LearJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LearJet)), and [Textron Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textron_Aviation) (a merger of the former [Cessna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna), [Hawker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Aircraft), and [Beechcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft) brands). [Boeing](18933266.md) ended a decades-long history of manufacturing in Kansas between 2012 and 2013.

Major companies headquartered in Kansas include the [Garmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmin) ([Olathe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olathe,_Kansas)), [YRC Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YRC_Worldwide) (Overland Park), and [Koch, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch,_Inc.) (with national headquarters in [Wichita](114932.md)).

Kansas is also home to three major military installations: [Fort Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Leavenworth) (Army), [Fort Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Riley) (Army), and [McConnell Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McConnell_Air_Force_Base) (Air Force). Approximately 25,000 active duty soldiers and airmen are stationed at these bases which also employ approximately 8,000 civilian DoD employees. The U.S. Army Reserve also has the 451st Expeditionary Sustainment Command headquartered in Wichita that serves reservists and their units from around the region. The Kansas Air National Guard has units at [Forbes Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Field) in Topeka and the 184th Intelligence Wing in Wichita. The Smoky Hill Weapons Range, a detachment of the Intelligence Wing, is one of the largest and busiest bombing ranges in the nation. During World War II, Kansas was home to numerous Army Air Corps training fields for training new pilots and aircrew. Many of those airfields live on today as municipal airports.

### Energy

See also: [List of power stations in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Kansas)



NRC workers at the Wolf Creek Nuclear Power Plant in [Burlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_Kansas)



Kansas electricity production by type

Kansas has vast renewable resources and is a top producer of [wind energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Kansas) in the US, with an installed capacity of about 6,100 [Megawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts) (MW) from nearly 3,200 wind turbines in 2019. Wind generated the [largest share of electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Kansas) from the state at 41%. An additional 700 MW of capacity was scheduled to come online during 2020. Kansas is also a leading national producer of renewable [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol) and [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel) fuels at nearly 600 million gallons per year.[^cite_note-122]

Kansas is one of 28 states with a nuclear power plant, Wolf Creek in Burlington, which houses a Westinghouse pressurized water reactor. The plant came online in 1985, and cost over \$5 billion to construct. It is owned by [Evergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergy).

Kansas is ranked eighth in [U.S. petroleum extraction](19086358.md). Production has experienced a steady decline as the state's limited economical reserves especially from the [Anadarko Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadarko_Basin) are depleted. Since [oil prices](5137675.md) bottomed in 1999, oil production in Kansas has remained fairly constant, with an average monthly rate of about 2.8 million barrels (450,000 cubic meters) in 2004. The [recent higher prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_price_increases_since_2003) have made [carbon dioxide sequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration) and other oil recovery techniques more economical.

Kansas is also ranked eighth in U.S. natural gas production. Production has steadily declined since the mid-1990s with the gradual depletion of the [Hugoton Natural Gas Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugoton_Natural_Gas_Area)—the state's largest field which extends into Oklahoma and Texas. In 2004, slower declines in the Hugoton gas fields and increased [coalbed methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalbed_methane) production contributed to a smaller overall decline. Average monthly production was over 32 billion cubic feet (0.91 cubic kilometers).

### Taxes

Main article: [Kansas Senate Bill Substitute HB 2117](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Senate_Bill_Substitute_HB_2117)

Tax is collected by the [Kansas Department of Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Department_of_Revenue).

Revenue shortfalls resulting from lower than expected tax collections and slower growth in personal income following a 1998 permanent tax reduction have contributed to the substantial growth in the state's debt level as bonded debt increased from \$1.16 billion in 1998 to \$3.83 billion in 2006. Some increase in debt was expected as the state continues with its 10-year Comprehensive Transportation Program enacted in 1999.

In 2003, Kansas had three income brackets for income tax calculation, ranging from 3.5% to 6.45%.

The state sales tax in Kansas is 6.15%. Various cities and counties in Kansas have an additional local sales tax. Except during the 2001 [recession](25382.md) (March–November 2001), when monthly sales tax collections were flat, collections have trended higher as the economy has grown and two rate increases have been enacted. If there had been no change in sales tax rates or in the economy, the total sales tax collections for 2003 would have been \$1,797 million, compared to \$805.3 million in 1990. However, they instead amounted to \$1,630 million an inflation-adjusted reduction of 10%. The state sales tax is a combined destination-based tax, meaning a single tax is applied that includes state, county, and local taxes, and the rate is based on where the consumer takes possession of the goods or services. Thanks to the destination structure and the numerous local special taxing districts, Kansas has 920 separate sales tax rates ranging from 6.5% to 11.5%.[^cite_note-123] This taxing scheme, known as "[Streamlined Sales Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streamlined_Sales_Tax)" was adopted on October 1, 2005, under the governorship of [Kathleen Sebelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Sebelius).[^cite_note-124] Groceries are subject to sales tax in the state. All sales tax collected is remitted to the state department of revenue, and local taxes are then distributed to the various taxing agencies.

As of June 2004, [Moody's Investors Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's_Investors_Service) ranked the state 14th for net tax-supported debt per capita. As a percentage of personal income, it was at 3.8%—above the median value of 2.5% for all rated states and having risen from a value of less than 1% in 1992. The state has a statutory requirement to maintain cash reserves of at least 7.5% of expenses at the end of each fiscal year; however, lawmakers can vote to override the rule, and did so during the most recent budget agreement.

During his campaign for the 2010 election, Governor [Sam Brownback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Brownback) called for a complete "phase out of Kansas's income tax".[^cite_note-theatlantic_tax_experiment_dead_2017-125] In May 2012, Governor Brownback signed into law the **Kansas Senate Bill Substitute HB 2117**.[^cite_note-HB2117-126] Starting in 2013, the "ambitious tax overhaul" trimmed income tax, eliminated some corporate taxes, and created [pass-through income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow-through_entity) tax exemptions, he raised the sales tax by one percent to offset the loss to state revenues but that was inadequate. He made cuts to education and some state services to offset lost revenue.[^cite_note-stltoday_2013-127] The tax cut led to years of budget shortfalls, culminating in a \$350 million budget shortfall in February 2017. From 2013 to 2017, 300,000 businesses were considered to be pass-through income entities and benefited from the tax exemption. The tax reform "encouraged tens of thousands of Kansans to claim their wages and salaries as income from a business rather than from employment."[^cite_note-WP_2017_revolt-119]

The economic growth that Brownback anticipated never materialized. He argued that it was because of "low wheat and oil prices and a downturn in aircraft sales".[^cite_note-theatlantic_tax_experiment_dead_2017-125] The state general fund debt load was \$83 million in fiscal year 2010 and by fiscal year 2017 the debt load sat at \$179 million.[^cite_note-128] In 2016, Governor Brownback earned the title of "most unpopular governor in America". Only 26 percent of Kansas voters approved of his job performance, compared to 65 percent who said they did not.[^cite_note-129] In the summer of 2016 [S\&P Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_Global) Ratings downgraded Kansas's credit rating.[^cite_note-NYT_S\&P_ratings_2017-120] In February 2017, S\&P lowered it to AA−.[^cite_note-NYT_S\&P_ratings_2017-120]

In February 2017, a bi-partisan coalition presented a bill that would repeal the pass-through income exemption, the "most important provisions of Brownback's overhaul", and raise taxes to make up for the budget shortfall. Brownback vetoed the bill but "45 GOP legislators had voted in favor of the increase, while 40 voted to uphold the governor's veto."[^cite_note-WP_2017_revolt-119] On June 6, 2017, a coalition of Democrats and newly elected Republicans overrode \[Brownback's] veto and implemented tax increases to a level close to what it was before 2013.[^cite_note-theatlantic_tax_experiment_dead_2017-125] Brownback's tax overhaul was described in a June 2017 article in *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)* as the United States' "most aggressive experiment in conservative economic policy".[^cite_note-theatlantic_tax_experiment_dead_2017-125] The drastic tax cuts had "threatened the viability of schools and infrastructure" in Kansas.[^cite_note-theatlantic_tax_experiment_dead_2017-125]

### Highways

See also: [List of state highways in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_highways_in_Kansas), [List of U.S. Highways in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._Highways_in_Kansas), and [List of Interstate Highways in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Interstate_Highways_in_Kansas)

Kansas welcome sign on [K-96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-96_\(Kansas_highway\))

Kansas is served by two [Interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) with one [beltway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road), two [spur routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spur_route), and three [bypasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bypass_\(road\)), with over 874 miles (1,407 km) in all. The first section of Interstate in the nation was opened on [Interstate 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_70_in_Kansas) (I-70) just west of [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas) on November 14, 1956.[^cite_note-130]

I-70 is a major east–west route connecting to [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver), Colorado and [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri). Cities along this route (from west to east) include [Colby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby,_Kansas), [Hays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hays,_Kansas), [Salina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salina,_Kansas), [Junction City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction_City,_Kansas), [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas), [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas), [Bonner Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonner_Springs,_Kansas), and [Kansas City](115009.md).

[I-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35_in_Kansas) is a major north–south route connecting to [Oklahoma City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City), Oklahoma and [Des Moines, Iowa](9163.md). Cities along this route (from south to north) include [Wichita](114932.md), [El Dorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Dorado,_Kansas), [Emporia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emporia,_Kansas), [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa,_Kansas), and Kansas City (and suburbs).

Spur routes serve as connections between the two major routes. [I-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_135_\(Kansas\)), a north–south route, connects I-35 at Wichita to I-70 at Salina. [I-335](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_335_\(Kansas\)), a southwest–northeast route, connects I-35 at Emporia to I-70 at Topeka. I-335 and portions of I-35 and I-70 make up the [Kansas Turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Turnpike). Bypasses include [I-470](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_470_\(Kansas\)) around Topeka, [I-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_235_\(Kansas\)) around Wichita, and [I-670](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_670_\(Kansas\)) in downtown Kansas City. [I-435](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_435_\(Kansas\)) is a beltway around the [Kansas City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_metropolitan_area) while [I-635](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_635_\(Kansas%E2%80%93Missouri\)) bypasses through Kansas City.

[U.S. Route 69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_69_in_Kansas) (US-69) travels south to north, from Oklahoma to Missouri. The highway passes through the eastern section of Kansas, traveling through [Baxter Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baxter_Springs,_Kansas), [Pittsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg,_Kansas), [Frontenac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontenac,_Kansas), [Fort Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Scott,_Kansas), [Louisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisburg,_Kansas), and the Kansas City area.

Kansas also has the country's third largest state highway system after Texas and California. This is because of the high number of counties and [county seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) (105) and their intertwining.

In January 2004, the [Kansas Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Department_of_Transportation) (KDOT) announced the new Kansas [511](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-1-1) traveler information service.[^cite_note-131] By dialing 511, callers will get access to information about road conditions, construction, closures, detours and weather conditions for the state highway system. Weather and road condition information is updated every 15 minutes.

### Aviation

See also: [List of airports in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Kansas)

The state's only major commercial ([Class C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airspace_class_\(United_States\))) airport is [Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower National Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_Dwight_D._Eisenhower_National_Airport), located along [US-54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_54_\(Kansas\)) on the western edge of the city. [Manhattan Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Regional_Airport) in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan,_Kansas) offers daily flights to [Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas/Fort_Worth_International_Airport) and Chicago's [O'Hare International Airport](248449.md), making it the second-largest commercial airport in the state.[^cite_note-132] Most air travelers in northeastern Kansas fly out of [Kansas City International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_International_Airport), located in [Platte County, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platte_County,_Missouri), as well as [Topeka Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka_Regional_Airport) in the state's capital.

In the state's southeastern part, people often use [Tulsa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_International_Airport) in [Tulsa, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa,_Oklahoma) or [Joplin Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joplin_Regional_Airport) in [Joplin, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joplin,_Missouri). For those in the far western part of the state, [Denver International Airport](263648.md) is a popular option. Connecting flights are also available from smaller Kansas airports in [Dodge City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_City_Regional_Airport), [Garden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City_Regional_Airport), [Hays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hays_Regional_Airport), [Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchinson_Municipal_Airport_\(Kansas\)), [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Mid-America_Regional_Airport), or [Salina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salina_Municipal_Airport).

Dotted across the state are smaller regional and municipal airports, including the [Lawrence Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Municipal_Airport_\(Kansas\)), which houses many aircraft for the city of Lawrence and the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), Miami County Airport, Wamego Airport, [Osage City Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osage_City_Municipal_Airport), which is the headquarters of *Skydive Kansas*, [Garden City Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City_Regional_Airport), [Manhattan Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Regional_Airport), and [Dodge City Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_City_Regional_Airport).

### Rail

See also: [List of Kansas railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kansas_railroads)



1915–1918 Kansas railroad map

Up through the mid-20th century, railroads connected most cities in Kansas. During [World War II](32927.md), less profitable links were abandoned for [scrap metal drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_home_front_during_World_War_II), then additional mileage was reduced as passenger service was halted caused by the wide spread use of automobiles and trucking on the expanding highway system.

For passenger service, currently the *[Southwest Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Chief)* [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) route runs through the state on its route from Chicago, Illinois to Los Angeles, California on the [BNSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF) railway. Stops in Kansas include [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas), [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas), [Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Kansas), [Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchinson,_Kansas), [Dodge City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_City,_Kansas), and [Garden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City,_Kansas).[^cite_note-133] An [Amtrak Thruway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Thruway) connects Newton to the *[Heartland Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartland_Flyer)* in [Oklahoma City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City), Oklahoma.[^cite_note-134] There has been proposals to modify the Amtrak routing through Kansas, such as: removing rail service from the *[Southwest Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Chief)* between [Albuquerque, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico) and Dodge City,[^cite_note-135] and extending rail service for the *[Heartland Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartland_Flyer)* from Oklahoma City to Newton with new stops at [Arkansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_City,_Kansas) and [Wichita](114932.md).[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

For freight service, there are three [Class I railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_I_rail_carrier) in Kansas: [BNSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF), [Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific), and [CPKC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Kansas_City); as well as many [shortline railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortline_railroad).[^cite_note-138]

## Law and government

### State and local politics

See also: [Government of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Kansas) and [Political party strength in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_strength_in_Kansas)

The executive branch consists of one officer and five elected officers. The governor and [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Kansas) are elected on the same [ticket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticket_\(election\)). The attorney general, secretary of state, state treasurer, and state insurance commissioner are each elected separately.

The [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) [Kansas Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Legislature) consists of the [Kansas House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_House_of_Representatives), with 125 members serving two-year terms, and the [Kansas Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Senate), with 40 members serving four-year terms.[^cite_note-139]

The [judicial branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) of the state government is headed by the [Kansas Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Supreme_Court). The court has seven judges. A vacancy is filled by the Governor picking one of three nominees selected by the nine-member [Kansas Supreme Court Nominating Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Supreme_Court_Nominating_Commission).[^cite_note-140] The board consists of five Kansas lawyers elected by other Kansas lawyers and four members selected by the governor.[^cite_note-141]

### Political culture

Since the 1930s, Kansas has remained one of the most socially conservative states in the nation. The 1990s brought the defeat of prominent Democrats, including [Dan Glickman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Glickman), and the [Kansas State Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_Board_of_Education)'s 1999 decision to eliminate [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) from the state teaching standards, a decision that was later reversed.[^cite_note-142] In 2005, voters accepted a constitutional amendment to ban [same-sex marriage](92656.md). The next year, the state passed a law setting a minimum age for marriage at 15 years.[^cite_note-143] Kansas's path to a solid Republican state has been examined by journalist and historian [Thomas Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Frank_\(political_commentator\)) in his 2004 book *[What's the Matter with Kansas?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What's_the_Matter_with_Kansas%3F_\(book\))*.[^cite_note-144]

### 19th-century state politics

Starting in 1887 Kansas women could vote in city elections and [hold certain offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first_women_mayors_in_the_United_States#Kansas).[^cite_note-145]

### 20th-century state politics

Kansas was the first state to institute a system of [workers' compensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_compensation) (1910) and to regulate the [securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_\(finance\)) industry (1911). Kansas also permitted [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) in 1912, almost a decade before the federal constitution was amended to require it.[^cite_note-146] Suffrage in all states would not be guaranteed until ratification of the [19th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) in 1920.

The [council–manager government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government) model was adopted by many larger Kansas cities in the years following World War I while many American cities were being run by [political machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_machine) or [organized crime](22625.md), notably the [Pendergast Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Pendergast) in neighboring [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri). Kansas was also at the center of *[Brown v. Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Board_of_Education) of [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas)*, a 1954 Supreme Court decision that banned racially segregated schools throughout the U.S.; many Kansas residents opposed the decision, and it led to protests in Topeka after the verdict.[^cite_note-147]

The state backed Republican Presidential Candidates [Wendell Willkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Willkie) and [Thomas E. Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_E._Dewey) in [1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_United_States_presidential_election_in_Kansas) and [1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_United_States_presidential_election_in_Kansas), respectively, breaking ranks with the majority of the country in the election of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt). Kansas also supported Dewey in [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_United_States_presidential_election_in_Kansas) despite the presence of incumbent president [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman), who hailed from [Independence, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence,_Missouri), approximately 15 miles (24 km) east of the Kansas–Missouri state line. After Roosevelt carried Kansas in [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election_in_Kansas), only one Democrat has won the state since, [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election_in_Kansas).

In 2008, Democrat Governor [Kathleen Sebelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Sebelius) vetoed permits for the construction of new coal-fired energy plants in Kansas, saying: "We know that greenhouse gases contribute to climate change. As an agricultural state, Kansas is particularly vulnerable. Therefore, reducing pollutants benefits our state not only in the short term—but also for generations of Kansans to come."[^cite_note-149] However, shortly after Mark Parkinson became governor in 2009 upon Sebelius's resignation to become Secretary of U.S. Department of Health & Human Services, Parkinson announced a compromise plan to allow construction of a coal-fired plant.

In 2010, Republican [Sam Brownback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Brownback) was elected governor with 63 percent of the state vote. He was sworn in as governor in 2011, Kansas's first Republican governor in eight years. Brownback had established himself as a conservative member of the U.S. Senate in years prior, but made several controversial decisions after becoming governor, leading to a 23% approval rating among registered voters – the lowest of any governor in the United States.[^cite_note-150] In May 2011, much to the opposition of art leaders and enthusiasts in the state, Brownback eliminated the Kansas Arts Commission, making Kansas the first state without an arts agency.[^cite_note-151] In July 2011, Brownback announced plans to close the Lawrence branch of the Kansas Department of Social and Rehabilitation Services as a cost-saving measure. Hundreds rallied against the decision.[^cite_note-152] Lawrence City Commission later voted to provide the funding needed to keep the branch open.[^cite_note-153]

Democrat [Laura Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Kelly) defeated former Secretary of State of Kansas [Kris Kobach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Kobach) in the [2018 election for Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Kansas_gubernatorial_election) with 48.0% of the vote.[^cite_note-NYT_Laura_Kelly-154][^cite_note-Wichita_Eagle_Laura_Kelly-155]

In August 2022, Kansas voters rejected the controversial [Value Them Both Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kansas_Value_Them_Both_Amendment), which would have eliminated the right to an abortion in the state constitution. The vote was the first referendum on abortion since [Roe v. Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_v._Wade) was overturned earlier that summer, and the result was hailed as a landmark victory for pro-choice advocates in the traditionally socially conservative state.[^cite_note-156]

In a 2020 study, Kansas was ranked as the 13th hardest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-157]

### National politics

See also: [Kansas's congressional delegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas's_congressional_delegations) and [United States presidential elections in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_elections_in_Kansas)



[Charles Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Curtis) ([R](32070.md)) was born near Topeka and served as a State Legislator, Congressman and Senator, before becoming Vice President (1929–33). He is the only Native American elected to the Executive Branch (he was born into the [Kaw Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaw_Nation)).



The state's current delegation to the [Congress of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_United_States) includes [Republican](32070.md) Senators [Jerry Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Moran) of [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan,_Kansas), and [Roger Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Marshall) of [Great Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Bend,_Kansas). In the [House of Representatives](19468510.md), Kansas is represented by Republican Representatives [Tracey Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracey_Mann) of [Quinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinter,_Kansas) ([District 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Congressional_District_of_Kansas)), [Jake LaTurner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_LaTurner) of [Pittsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg,_Kansas) ([District 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Congressional_District_of_Kansas)), [Ron Estes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Estes) of [Wichita](114932.md) ([District 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Congressional_District_of_Kansas)), and Democratic Representative [Sharice Davids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharice_Davids) of [Kansas City](115009.md) ([District 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Congressional_District_of_Kansas)) Davids is the second Native American to represent Kansas in Congress, after Republican [Charles Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Curtis) ([Kaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaw_people)).

Historically, Kansas has been strongly Republican, dating from the [Antebellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antebellum_era) age when the Republican Party was created out of the movement opposing the extension of slavery into Kansas Territory. Kansas has not elected a [Democrat](5043544.md) to the U.S. Senate since the 1932 election, when [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) won his first term as president in the wake of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression). This is the longest Senate losing streak for either party in a single state. Senator [Sam Brownback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Brownback) was a candidate for the Republican party nomination for president in 2008. Brownback was not a candidate for re-election to a third full term in 2010, but he was elected Governor in that year's general election. Moran defeated Tiahrt for the Republican nomination for Brownback's seat in the August 2010 primary, then won a landslide general election victory over Democrat Lisa Johnston.

The only non-Republican presidential candidates Kansas has given its electoral vote to are Populist [James Weaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Baird_Weaver) and Democrats [William Jennings Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan) (once), [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson), Franklin Roosevelt (twice), and [Lyndon Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson). In 2004, [George W. Bush](3414021.md) won the state's six electoral votes by an overwhelming margin of 25 percentage points with 62% of the vote. The only two counties to support Democrat [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) in that election were [Wyandotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyandotte_County,_Kansas), which contains [Kansas City](115009.md), and [Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County,_Kansas), home to the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), located in [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas). The 2008 election brought similar results as [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) won the state with 57% of the votes. Douglas, Wyandotte, and [Crawford County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawford_County,_Kansas) were the only counties in support of President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama).[^cite_note-158]

Abilene was the boyhood home to Republican president [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower), and he maintained lifelong ties to family and friends there. Kansas was the adult home of two losing Republican candidates (Governor [Alf Landon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Landon) in [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election) and Senator Bob Dole in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_States_presidential_election)).

The *New York Times* reported in September 2014 that as the Democratic candidate for Senator has tried to drop out of the race, independent [Greg Orman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Orman) has attracted enough bipartisan support to seriously challenge the reelection bid of Republican Pat Roberts:

Kansas politics have been roiled in recent years. The rise of the Tea Party and the election of President Obama have prompted Republicans to embrace a purer brand of conservatism and purge what had long been a robust moderate wing from its ranks. Mr. Roberts has sought to adapt to this new era, voting against spending bills that included projects for the state that he had sought.

### State laws

See also: [Alcohol laws of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Kansas)

The [legal drinking age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_drinking_age) in Kansas is 21. In lieu of the state retail sales tax, a 10% Liquor Drink Tax is collected for liquor consumed on the licensed premises and an 8% Liquor Enforcement Tax is collected on retail purchases. Although the sale of cereal [malt beverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malt_beverage) (also known as [3.2 beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3.2_beer)) was legalized in 1937, the first post-[Prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States) legalization of alcoholic liquor did not occur until the [state's constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Constitution) was amended in 1948. The following year the [Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Legislature) enacted the Liquor Control Act which created a system of regulating, licensing, and taxing, and the Division of Alcoholic Beverage Control (ABC) was created to enforce the act. The power to regulate cereal malt beverage remains with the cities and counties. Liquor-by-the-drink did not become legal until passage of an amendment to the state's constitution in 1986 and additional legislation the following year. As of November 2006, Kansas still has 29 [dry counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_county) and only 17 counties have passed liquor-by-the-drink with no food sales requirement.[^cite_note-160] Today there are more than 2,600 liquor and 4,000 cereal malt beverage licensees in the state.[^cite_note-161]

On May 12, 2022, Gov. Laura Kelly signed legislation (Senate Bill 84) that legalizes sports betting in the state, making Kansas the 35th state to approve sports wagering in the US. This would give the four state-owned casinos the right to partner with online bookmakers and up to 50 retailers, including gas stations and restaurants, to engage in sports betting.[^cite_note-162]

### Music

### Literature

See also: [Kansas Notable Book Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Notable_Book_Awards)

The state's most famous appearance in literature was as the home of Dorothy Gale, the main character in the novel *[The Wonderful Wizard of Oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wonderful_Wizard_of_Oz)* (1900). [Laura Ingalls Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Ingalls_Wilder)'s *[Little House on the Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_House_on_the_Prairie_\(novel\))*, published in 1935, is another well-known tale about Kansas.

Kansas was also the setting of the 1965 best-seller *[In Cold Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Cold_Blood)*, described by its author [Truman Capote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Capote) as a "nonfiction novel". Mixing fact and fiction, the book chronicles the events and aftermath of the 1959 murder of a wealthy farmer and his family who lived in the small West Kansas town of [Holcomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holcomb,_Kansas) in [Finney County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finney_County).

The fictional town of [Smallville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallville_\(comics\)), Kansas is the childhood home of Clark Kent/[Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman) in American comic books published by [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics). Also Keystone City is a Kansas city where [The Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flash) works and lives.

The science fiction novella *[A Boy and His Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Boy_and_His_Dog)*, as well as the [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Boy_and_His_Dog_\(1975_film\)) based on it,[^cite_note-NYT-164] take place in post-apocalyptic [Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas).

The winner of the 2011 [Newbery Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbery_Medal) for excellence in children's literature, *[Moon Over Manifest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Over_Manifest)*, tells the story of a young and adventurous girl named Abilene who is sent to the fictional town of Manifest, Kansas, by her father in the summer of 1936. It was written by Kansan [Clare Vanderpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Vanderpool).

[Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas) is the setting for a number of science fiction writer [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn_\(author\))'s novels.

### Film theaters



The Orpheum, a historic movie theater in Wichita



The Plaza Cinema in [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa,_Kansas) is the oldest operating movie theater in the world.



Fox Theater, [Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchinson,_Kansas)

The first film theater in Kansas was the [Patee Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patee_Theater?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas). Most theaters at the time showed films only as part of [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville) acts but not as an exclusive and stand alone form of entertainment. Though the [Patee family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patee_family?action=edit\&redlink=1) had been involved in [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville), they believed films could carry the evening without other variety acts, but to show the films it was necessary for the Patee's to establish a generating plant (back in 1903 Lawrence was not yet fully electrified). The Patee Theater was one of the first of its kind west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River). The specialized equipment like the [projector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projector) came from New York City.[^cite_note-168]

Kansas has been the setting of many award-winning and popular American films, as well as being the home of some of the oldest operating cinemas in the world. The Plaza Cinema in Ottawa, Kansas, located in the northeastern portion of the state, was built on May 22, 1907, and it is listed by the [Guinness Book of World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_Book_of_World_Records) as the oldest operating cinema in the world.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] In 1926, The Jayhawk Theatre, an [art-deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art-deco) movie house in Topeka opened its doors for the first time to movie going audiences, and today, in addition to screenings of independent films, the theatre acts as a venue for plays and concerts. The Fox Theater in Hutchinson was built in 1930, and was placed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in 1989.[^cite_note-171] Like the other theaters listed here, The Fox still plays first run movies to this day.

#### History

The history of professional sports in Kansas probably dates from the establishment of the [minor league baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league_baseball) Topeka Capitals and [Leavenworth Soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavenworth_Soldiers) in 1886 in the [Western League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_League_\(1885%E2%80%931899\)).[^cite_note-Evans-175][^cite_note-Madden-176] The African-American [Bud Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bud_Fowler) played on the Topeka team that season, one year before the "[color line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_color_line)" descended on professional baseball.[^cite_note-Madden-176]

In 1887, the Western League was dominated by a reorganized Topeka team called the [Golden Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka_Golden_Giants_\(1887\)): a high-priced collection of major leaguer players, including [Bug Holliday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_Holliday), [Jim Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Conway_\(baseball\)), [Dan Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Stearns), [Perry Werden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_Werden) and [Jimmy Macullar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Macullar), which won the league by 15.5 games.[^cite_note-Madden-176] On April 10, 1887, the Golden Giants also won an exhibition game from the defending [World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1886_World_Series) champions, the [St. Louis Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Browns_\(NL\)) (the present-day Cardinals), by a score of 12–9. However, Topeka was unable to support the team, and it disbanded after one year.

The first night game in the history of professional baseball was played in Independence on April 28, 1930, when the Muscogee (Oklahoma) Indians beat the Independence Producers 13–3 in a minor league game sanctioned by the Western League of the Western Baseball Association with 1,500 fans attending the game. The permanent lighting system was first used for an exhibition game on April 17, 1930, between the Independence Producers and House of David semi-professional baseball team of Benton Harbor, Michigan with the Independence team winning 9–1 before a crowd of 1,700 spectators.[^cite_note-177]

### College



[David Booth Kansas Memorial Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Booth_Kansas_Memorial_Stadium) is the oldest football stadium west of the Mississippi River, and one of the oldest standing football stadiums in the country. Built in 1921, it is home to the Kansas Jayhawks football team.

See also: [List of college athletic programs in Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_college_athletic_programs_in_Kansas)

The history of intercollegiate of athletics in the state dates back to 1866, with the establishment of the University of Kansas baseball team, which competed against local area teams and schools in the Kansas State Fair. The Jayhawks baseball team is one of the oldest intercollegiate baseball programs in the United States.[^cite_note-178]

The governing body for intercollegiate sports in the United States, the [National Collegiate Athletic Association](60706.md) (NCAA), was headquartered in [Johnson County, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County,_Kansas) from 1952 until moving to Indianapolis in 1999.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

#### NCAA Division I schools



[Allen Fieldhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Fieldhouse) at [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) in [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas)



Tyler Field in [Eck Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eck_Stadium) at [Wichita State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_State_University) in [Wichita](114932.md)

While there are no franchises of the four major professional sports within the state, many Kansans are fans of the state's major college sports teams, especially the [Jayhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Jayhawks) of the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) (KU), and the [Wildcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_Wildcats) of [Kansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_University) (KSU or "K-State"). The teams are rivals in the [Big 12 Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_12_Conference).

Both KU and K-State have tradition-rich programs in men's basketball. The Jayhawks are a perennial national power, ranking first in all-time victories among NCAA programs. The Jayhawks have won six national titles, including NCAA tournament championships in 1952, 1988, 2008, and 2022. They also were retroactively awarded national championships by the [Helms Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helms_Foundation) for 1922 and 1923. K-State also had a long stretch of success on the hardwood, lasting from the 1940s to the 1980s, making four [Final Fours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Four) during that stretch. In 1988, KU and K-State met in the [Elite Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Eight), KU taking the game 71–58. After a 12-year absence, the Wildcats returned to the NCAA tournament in 2008, and advanced to the Elite Eight in 2010 and 2018. KU is fifth all-time with 15 Final Four appearances, while K-State's four appearances are tied for 17th.

Conversely, success on the [gridiron](18951490.md) has been less frequent for both KSU and KU. However, there have been recent breakthroughs for both schools' football teams. The Jayhawks won the [Orange Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Bowl_\(game\)) for the first time in three tries in 2008, capping a 12–1 season, the best in school history. And when [Bill Snyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Snyder) arrived to coach at K-State in 1989, he turned the Wildcats from one of the worst college football programs in America,[^cite_note-181] into a national force for most of the 1990s and early 2000s. The team won the [Fiesta Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_Bowl) in 1997, achieved an undefeated (11–0) regular season and No. 1 ranking in 1998, and took the [Big 12 Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_12_Conference) championship in 2003. After three seasons in which K-State football languished, Snyder came out of retirement in 2009 and guided them to the top of the college football ranks again, finishing second in the Big 12 in 2011 and earning a berth in the [Cotton Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Bowl_Classic), and winning the Big 12 again in 2012.

[Wichita State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_State_University), which also fields teams (called the [Shockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_State_Shockers)) in [Division I](669931.md) of the [NCAA](60706.md), is best known for its baseball and basketball programs. In baseball, the Shockers won the [College World Series](536107.md) in 1989. In men's basketball, they appeared in the Final Four in 1965 and 2013, and entered the 2014 NCAA tournament unbeaten. The school also fielded a [football](18951490.md) team from 1897 to 1986. The Shocker football team is tragically known for a [plane crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_State_University_football_team_plane_crash) in 1970 that killed 31 people, including 14 players.

#### NCAA Division II schools

Notable success has also been achieved by the state's smaller schools in football. [Pittsburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg_State_University), an NCAA Division II participant, has claimed four national titles in football, two in the NAIA and most recently the 2011 NCAA Division II national title. Pittsburg State became the winningest NCAA Division II football program in 1995. PSU passed Hillsdale College at the top of the all-time victories list in the 1995 season on its march to the national runner-up finish. The Gorillas, in 96 seasons of intercollegiate competition, have accumulated 579 victories, posting a 579–301–48 overall mark.

[Washburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washburn_University), in Topeka, won the [NAIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Intercollegiate_Athletics) Men's Basketball Championship in 1987. The [Fort Hays State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Hays_State_University) men won the 1996 NCAA Division II title with a 34–0 record, and the Washburn women won the 2005 NCAA Division II crown. St. Benedict's College (now Benedictine College), in Atchison, won the 1954 and 1967 Men's NAIA Basketball Championships.

The [Kansas Collegiate Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Collegiate_Athletic_Conference) has its roots as one of the oldest college sport conferences in existence and participates in the NAIA and all ten member schools are in the state of Kansas. Other smaller school conferences that have some members in Kansas are the [Mid-America Intercollegiate Athletics Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-America_Intercollegiate_Athletics_Association) the [Midlands Collegiate Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands_Collegiate_Athletic_Conference), the [Midwest Christian College Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_Christian_College_Conference), and the [Heart of America Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_America_Athletic_Conference). Many [junior colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_college) also have active athletic programs.

[Emporia State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emporia_State)'s women's basketball team, under head coach [Brandon Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Schneider), who is now serving as the women's basketball coach at the University of Kansas, has seen success as well. In 2010 the team won the NCAA Division II National Championship. Emporia State and Washburn in Topeka share a heated rivalry in all sports, mostly due to the close proximity of both cities.

#### Junior colleges

The [Kansas Jayhawk Community College Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Jayhawk_Community_College_Conference) has been heralded as one of the best conferences in all of [NJCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJCAA) football, with [Garden City Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City_Community_College), [Independence Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Community_College), and [Butler Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_Community_College) all consistently in contention for national championships.

### High school

The [Kansas State High School Activities Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_High_School_Activities_Association) (KSHSAA) is the organization which oversees interscholastic competition in the state of Kansas at the high school level. It oversees both athletic and non-athletic competition, and sponsors championships in several sports and activities.

### Rivalry with Missouri



Map of Kansas and Missouri with [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City) metro counties

Kansas and [Missouri](19571.md) are two bordering U.S. [states](18618239.md) with a long and tumultuous history. The relationship between these two states has its roots in [Bleeding Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeding_Kansas), but mutual distrust has continued off and on since then, [even in sporting contexts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_War_\(Kansas%E2%80%93Missouri_rivalry\)).[^cite_note-182] These states also share the [Kansas City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_metropolitan_area), where both states each have a city named Kansas City on either side of the Missouri River. The bitterness sown during Bleeding Kansas lingers in the [Border War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_War_\(Kansas%E2%80%93Missouri_rivalry\)#Background) between the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) and the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri).[^cite_note-183] The two states compete economically, mainly at the border which is also called a Border War.[^cite_note-184] In 2019, the governors of the two states signed an agreement to stop offering financial incentives to pull business across the border.[^cite_note-Hardy2019-185] In 2022, the governor of Kansas said that agreement did not include enticement of the [Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) football team moving its arena from Missouri to Kansas.[^cite_note-186] In 2024, the Kansas House of Representatives and Kansas State Senate passed legislation that would give professional sports programs north of \$1 billion in STAR bonds for the development of stadiums and entertainment districts in [Kansas City, Kansas](115009.md). The Chiefs and Royals both hired lobbyists in 2024 that appealed to the Kansas Legislature. As of March 2025, the Chiefs haven't commented directly on a move to Kansas, but the Royals have expressed interest in building a riverfront stadium on the junction between the Kansas River and Missouri River. Kansas House of Representatives member Sean Tarwater said that talks with the Chiefs were intensifying in February 2025, and that Kansas was close to "bringing over at least one team, maybe two".[^cite_note-187] In June 2024, an architecture firm in Kansas released a video featuring 3D renderings of a potential design for a domed stadium in Kansas City, Kansas.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]
