**Escape and Plasma Acceleration and Dynamics Explorers** (**ESCAPADE**) is a spacecraft mission to [Mars](14640471.md) consisting of two spacecraft known as ***Blue*** and ***Gold***, which launched in November 2025.[^cite_note-berkeley1-5][^cite_note-nssdc1-4][^cite_note-Space.com1-6] The mission is designed to demonstrate low-cost planetary space exploration. The twin spacecraft will study Mars' [magnetosphere](20479.md) and how [solar wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_wind) contributed to the loss of most of the planet's [atmosphere](4548379.md) over [Solar System](26903.md) history. The mission is led by [UC Berkeley's Space Sciences Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Sciences_Laboratory) with Dr. Robert Lillis as Principal Investigator. It is part of [NASA](18426568.md)'s [SIMPLEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Innovative_Missions_for_Planetary_Exploration) program.[^cite_note-sn-20230413-7][^cite_note-sn-20240425-8]

## Spacecraft



Both ESCAPADE spacecraft unboxed at Astrotech

Each identical ESCAPADE spacecraft has a dry mass of \~200 kg (440 lb), with a wet mass of 535 kg (1,179 lb). The [spacecraft bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft_bus) is based on the [Rocket Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Lab) Explorer spacecraft bus and is approximately 1.20 × 1.65 × 1.09 m (3 ft 11 in × 5 ft 5 in × 3 ft 7 in). The spacecraft is powered by two [solar array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panels_on_spacecraft) wings that when fully deployed is approximately 4.88 × 1.65 × 1.09 m (16.0 × 5.4 × 3.6 ft). The spacecraft buses were drastically enlarged from the original mission that was supposed to hitch a ride on *[Psyche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyche_\(spacecraft\))*. After a change in launch vehicle made it impossible for the smaller dual orbiters to reach Mars while attached to *Psyche*, a separate launcher had to be used which enabled larger spacecraft buses. The original dimensions for each ESCAPADE spacecraft were 90 kg (200 lb) and 60 × 70 × 90 cm (24 × 28 × 35 in).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Chemical propulsion is provided by thrusters developed by [Arianespace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianespace).[^cite_note-11] [Reaction wheels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_wheel), [inertial measurement units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_measurement_unit), and [star trackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_tracker) are used to maintain orientation, with [cold gas thrusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_gas_thruster) to desaturate[^cite_note-desaturation-12] the wheels. Communications are in [X-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-band) via a 60 cm (24 in) diameter [dish antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dish_antenna). A 200 cm (79 in) boom extends above the spacecraft, hosting the EMAG and ELP mNLP sensors.[^cite_note-nssdc1-4]

## Science

The science goals of ESCAPADE are to:[^cite_note-nssdc1-4]

- understand the processes controlling the structure of Mars' hybrid magnetosphere and how it guides ion flows

- understand how energy and momentum are transported from the solar wind through Mars' magnetosphere

- understand the processes controlling the flow of energy and matter into and out of the collisional atmosphere

There are four science experiments onboard each identical spacecraft: EMAG, EESA, ELP, and VISIONS.

**EMAG**, which was developed by the [NASA Goddard Spaceflight Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Goddard_Spaceflight_Center), is a [magnetometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetometer) that will measure DC [magnetic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field) up to 1000 [nT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotesla), mounted at the end of the boom to reduce magnetic noise from the spacecraft.&#x20;

**EESA** is an [electrostatic analyzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatic_analyzer) developed at UC Berkeley to measure the energies, fluxes, and masses of suprathermal ions from 2 [eV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronvolt) to 20 keV and energies and fluxes of suprathermal electrons from 3 eV to 10 keV. It is mounted on the upper deck of the spacecraft bus, with a 240° x 120° field of view for electrons and 247.5° x 90° field of view for ions.&#x20;

**ELP**, developed by the Space and Atmospheric Instrumentation Lab at [Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embry-Riddle_Aeronautical_University), is a Langmuir probe consisting of three separate sensors: the multi-needle [Langmuir probe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langmuir_probe) (mNLP) consists of 4 thin needles mounted in two pairs \~3/4 way up the boom and measures thermal electron density; the two planar ion probes (PIPs) are mounted on the instrument deck and measure thermal ion density, and the floating potential probe (FPP) is also mounted on the spacecraft deck and measures changes in relative spacecraft [electrostatic potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatic_potential).&#x20;

**VISIONS** (Visible and Infrared Observation System), developed by [Northern Arizona University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona_University), is a dual wavelength camera that captures images in both visible and infrared wavelength ranges simultaneously. VISIONS will uniquely be able to capture Mars in one frame unlike other Mars orbiters that are closer to the planet which will make it possible to study seasonal changes in the size of the polar caps and capture images of Mars' [aurorae](49658.md). In addition to its primary science mission it is also a technology demonstration to show how low-cost [commercial-off-the-shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial-off-the-shelf) (COTS) products can operate in an interplanetary mission.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Trajectory

The design of the ESCAPADE mission has been developed by Advanced Space LLC[^cite_note-16] and originally called for ESCAPADE to be [injected directly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_injection_trajectory) into an interplanetary trajectory.[^cite_note-nssdc1-4] After several delays and revisions, based on the final mission plan, ESCAPADE launched to a staging orbit about a million miles away, curving around the Earth-Sun L<sub>2</sub> [Lagrange point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_point) on November 13, 2025. It will loiter there, studying [space weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weather) until late 2026, when the [Mars transfer window](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_Mars#Launch_windows) opens. Then ESCAPADE will change course to dive within 600 km of Earth for a [gravity assist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_assist) and execute its escape burns to place it on a [trajectory to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_transfer_orbit).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

ESCAPADE will execute one major and several minor propulsive course correction maneuvers during its 11-month cruise to Mars. The twin probes will reach Mars a few days apart and each will fire its engines for about 11 minutes to enter a highly [elliptical orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_orbit). Over the following six months, the orbit will be lowered and circularized until it reaches the nominal science orbit.[^cite_note-19]

Once the nominal science orbit is achieved by mid-2028, Science Campaign A will begin which involves both spacecraft flying in the same orbit at varying distances from one another, \~170 × 8,400 km (110 × 5,220 mi) (5.66 hours) with an inclination of 65 degrees. This will last approximately six months.&#x20;

At the conclusion of Science Campaign A, *Blue* will lower its [apoapsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoapsis) to 7,000 km (4,300 mi) and *Gold* will raise its apoapsis to 10,000 km (6,200 mi) to begin Science Campaign B. With different orbital periods (4.9 and 6.6 hours), the orbits will precess (due to [Mars' non-uniform gravity field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_of_Mars)) at different rates and thus separate, allowing simultaneous measurements of distant parts of the Mars magnetosphere. This campaign will operate for approximately five months until the end of the nominal science mission.[^cite_note-nssdc1-4]

## Timeline

The EscaPADE mission made a spacecraft to study the loss of Mars’s atmosphere. The mission is led by the University of California, Berkeley, and is collaborating with Advanced Space and Rocket Lab. The spacecraft launched on November 13, 2025, from [Launch Complex 36A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Launch_Complex_36) at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Florida. The spacecraft is planned to enter Mars orbit by September 2027.[^cite_note-20]

The [Small Innovative Missions for Planetary Exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Innovative_Missions_for_Planetary_Exploration) ("SIMPLEx") program[^cite_note-trials2023-21] was intended as a program to select small, low-cost space missions to fly as secondary payloads on other NASA missions. As a ride-along mission on another launch, not a main mission, they were intended to have low cost and tolerate a higher level of risk than other NASA missions.

ESCAPADE was originally selected from a competition to be a low-cost ride-along "mission of opportunity" to hitch a ride to Mars with the [*Psyche*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyche_\(spacecraft\)) spacecraft, and drop off as the spacecraft made a fly-by of Mars. Thus, it had an anticipated launch cost of nearly nothing. However, the launch of the *Psyche* mission was awarded to Falcon Heavy, meaning that *Psyche* will make a higher-energy flyby of Mars, and ESCAPADE mission plans could not be updated in time for the launch.[^cite_note-sn-20200918-22] Rather than cancel the mission, NASA announced that it would go on a different launch vehicle, and requested bids from industry for the launch.

In February 2023, [Blue Origin](887418.md) won the contract to launch the mission on the first flight of the [New Glenn](51585988.md) launch vehicle in October 2024, at a cost of \~\$20 million.[^cite_note-sn-20240425-8] The entire cost of ESCAPADE was \$75 million, with \$55 million being the cost for the spacecraft buses and science instruments and \$20 million to cover the cost for the New Glenn rocket launch. For comparison NASA's [MAVEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAVEN) mars orbiter launched in 2013 cost \$582 million to build and launch and the failed 1999 [Mars Polar Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Polar_Lander) cost \$165 million to build and launch. ESCAPADE's smaller cost is similar to [ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO)'s \$74 million [Mars Orbiter Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Orbiter_Mission).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-NSF-20251105-24]

In August 2024 the spacecraft arrived at the launch site.[^cite_note-sn-20240819-25] On September 6, 2024, the launch was delayed by NASA to spring 2025 at the earliest due to schedule and technical issues involved with the mission's [New Glenn](51585988.md) launch vehicle, which was set to debut with the launch of these spacecraft.[^cite_note-nasa2024062024-26] NASA said: "The agency's decision to stand down was based on a review of launch preparations and discussions with Blue Origin, the Federal Aviation Administration, and Space Launch Delta 45 Range Safety Organization, as well as NASA's Launch Services Program and Science Mission Directorate. The decision was made to avoid significant cost, schedule, and technical challenges associated with potentially removing fuel from the spacecraft in the event of a launch delay, which could be caused by a number of factors."[^cite_note-27]

A launch attempt on November 9, 2025, was scrubbed due to weather conditions.[^cite_note-NSF-20251109-28] On November 10, the Federal Aviation Administration temporarily banned commercial rocket launches during the daytime due to the [ongoing government shutdown](81225965.md).[^cite_note-29] However, the FAA granted Blue Origin a waiver for this launch.[^cite_note-30] The second launch attempt on November 12 was scrubbed due to [solar storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_storm).[^cite_note-Space.com2-31][^cite_note-sfn-20251112-32][^cite_note-33] Finally, the mission successfully launched at 3\:55\:01 PM on November 13, 2025.[^cite_note-NYT-20251113-34][^cite_note-Space.com1-6][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]
