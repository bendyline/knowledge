**Martial law** is the replacement of civilian government by military rule and the suspension of civilian legal processes for military powers.[^cite_note-britannica-1][^cite_note-2] Martial law can continue for a specified amount of time, or indefinitely, and standard civil liberties may be suspended for as long as martial law continues.[^cite_note-britannica-1][^cite_note-3] Most often, martial law is declared in times of war or emergencies such as civil unrest and natural disasters.[^cite_note-4] Alternatively, martial law may be declared in instances of [military coups d'état](25914154.md).[^cite_note-5]

## Overview

Despite the fact that it has been declared frequently throughout history, martial law is still often described as largely elusive as a legal entity.[^cite_note-Moore2017-6] Across history, the phrase "martial law" has been used to refer to a "wide variety of actions, practices, or roles for the military", and thus lacked (and currently lacks) a concrete definition.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] References to martial law date back to 1628 England, when [Sir Matthew Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Hale_\(jurist\)) described martial law as, "no Law, but something indulged rather than allowed as a Law."[^cite_note-Moore2017-6] Despite being centuries old, this quote remains true in many countries around the world today. Most often, the implementation of martial law arises from necessity rather than legal right, and while some countries have provisions explicitly permitting the use of martial law, many do not.[^cite_note-Moore2017-6] For countries that do not explicitly permit the declaration of martial law, but where martial law has been declared, the legal justification for it is often the common law [doctrine of necessity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine_of_necessity), or some variation of it.

### Common law doctrine of necessity

One legal theory most frequently associated with martial law is the common law doctrine of necessity.[^cite_note-Moore2017-6] While many countries – for example, the United States – do not have the explicit constitutional right to declare martial law, scholars often interpret the law of the United States to allow for the implementation of martial law in times of necessity.[^cite_note-brennancenter-10] Countries such as Pakistan have implemented this rationale as well.[^cite_note-11]

## Use

Martial law can be used by governments to enforce their rule over the public, as seen in multiple countries listed below. Such incidents may occur after a [coup d'état](25914154.md) ([Thailand in 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Thai_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Thai_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), and [Egypt in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Egyptian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)); when threatened by popular protest (China, [Tiananmen Square protests of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989)); to suppress political opposition ([martial law in Poland in 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Poland)); or to stabilize insurrections or perceived insurrections. Martial law may be declared in cases of major [natural disasters](58921.md); however, most countries use a different legal construct, such as a [state of emergency](298608.md).

Martial law has also been imposed during conflicts, and in cases of occupations, where the absence of any other civil government provides for an unstable population. Examples of this form of military rule include post–[World War II](32927.md) reconstruction in Germany and Japan; the recovery and reconstruction of the former [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) during the [Reconstruction Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_Era) in the United States of America following the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War); and German occupation of northern France between 1871 and 1873 after the [Treaty of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Frankfurt_\(1871\)) ended the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War). The [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) commonly used martial law when [insurgencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgencies) occurred in its [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonies), such as the [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine) in [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine).[^cite_note-12]

Typically, the imposition of martial law accompanies [curfews](297681.md); the suspension of [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)), [civil rights](37071.md), and [habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus); and the application or extension of [military law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_law) or [military justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_justice) to civilians. Civilians defying martial law may be subjected to [military tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tribunal) ([court-martial](58625.md)).

## By country

### Africa

#### Egypt

See also: [Emergency law in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_law_in_Egypt)



Martial law in Egypt: Egyptian tanks used in a checkpoint near midtown Tahrir during the [2011 Egyptian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_revolution).

In Egypt, [states of emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_law_in_Egypt) were in effect almost continuously from 1967 to 2021. A state of emergency gives military courts the power to try civilians and allows the government to detain for renewable 45-day periods and without court orders anyone deemed to be threatening state security. Public demonstrations are banned under the legislation.

During [Hosni Mubarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosni_Mubarak)'s presidency, parliament had renewed emergency laws every three years since they were imposed. The legislation was extended in 2003 and were due to expire at the end of May 2006; plans were in place to replace it with new anti-terrorism laws. After the [Dahab bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Dahab_bombings) in April of that year, it was renewed for another two years.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In May 2008 there was a further extension to June 2010.[^cite_note-15] In May 2010, the state of emergency was further extended, albeit with a promise from the government to be applied only to "Terrorism and Drugs" suspects. On 10 February 2011, during the [uprising against his rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_revolution), Mubarak promised the deletion of the relevant constitutional article regarding the emergency law in an attempt to please the mass number of protesters that demanded him to resign. The following day, he stepped down and handed control of the country to the [Supreme Council of the Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_of_the_Armed_Forces). It meant that the presidential executive powers, the parliamentary legislative powers and the judicial powers all transferred directly to the military system which may delegate powers back and forth to any civilian institution within its territory.

The military issued in its third announcement the "end of the State of Emergency as soon as order is restored in Egypt". Before martial law, the Egyptian parliament under the constitution had the civilian power to declare a state of emergency. When in martial law, the military gained all powers of the state, including to dissolve the parliament and suspend the constitution as it did in its fifth announcement. Under martial law, the only legal framework within the Egyptian territory was the numbered announcements from the military. These announcements could for instance order any civilian laws to come back into force. The military announcements (communiqués) were the *de facto* only constitution and legal framework for the Egyptian territory. It meant that all affairs of the state were bound by the [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions).

Under the current president [Abdel Fattah el-Sisi](36703624.md), a series of [constitutional amendments passed in 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Egyptian_constitutional_referendum) granted the military the authority to intervene in national politics to "preserve the constitution and democracy, protect the basic principles of the state and its civil nature, and protect the people's rights and freedoms". The armed forces were separately granted policing responsibilities to arrest civilians and "protect public and vital facilities".[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

#### Mauritius

Mauritius is known as being a "Westminster" style of democracy but a peculiar system that was imposed in Mauritius during a period of civil unrest in 1968 as an emergency measure, has never been repealed and is still used by the police force there to this day. The system, which has no apparent foundation in the constitution of Mauritius, enables the police to arrest without having to demonstrate reasonable suspicion that a crime has been carried out but simply on the submission of "provisional information" to the magistrate. The accused is then placed on remand or bail and required to report to the police or the court on a regular basis, sometimes every day. There are examples of this system being used to intimidate or coerce individuals in civil litigations.[^cite_note-Raouf_Gulbul-18]

### Americas

#### Canada

See also: [War Measures Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Measures_Act)

The *War Measures Act* was a [Parliament of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) [statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute) that allowed the government to assume sweeping emergency powers, stopping short of martial law, i.e., the military did not administer justice, which remained in the hands of the courts. The act was invoked three times: during [World War I](4764461.md), [World War II](32927.md), and the [October Crisis of 1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Crisis_of_1970). In 1988, the *War Measures Act* was replaced by the *[Emergencies Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergencies_Act)*, which saw its first invocation in February 2022 amidst the [Freedom Convoy protests](69876497.md).

During the [colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), martial law was proclaimed and applied in the territory of the [Province of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec_\(1763%E2%80%931791\)) during the [invasion of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Canada_\(1775\)) by the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in 1775–1776. It was also applied twice in the [Province of Lower Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada) during the [1837–1838 insurrections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada_Rebellion). On 5 December, following the events of November 1837, martial law was proclaimed in the district of [Montreal](7954681.md) by Governor [Gosford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Acheson,_2nd_Earl_of_Gosford), without the support of the [Legislative Assembly of Lower Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Lower_Canada). It was imposed until 27 April 1838. Martial law was proclaimed a second time on 4 November 1838, this time by acting Governor [John Colborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Colborne), and was applied in the district of Montreal until 24 August 1839.[^cite_note-19]

#### United States

Main article: [Martial law in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_the_United_States)

See also: [Habeas corpus in the United States § Federal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus_in_the_United_States#Federal_law), and [Suspension Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_Clause)

In the United States, martial law has been declared for a state or other locality under various circumstances including after a direct foreign attack (Hawaii after the [Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_attack_on_Pearl_Harbor); New Orleans during the [Battle of New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_New_Orleans)); after a major disaster (Chicago after the [Great Chicago Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chicago_Fire) of 1871; San Francisco after the [earthquake of 1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_of_1906)); and in response to chaos associated with protests and mob action (San Francisco during the [1934 West Coast waterfront strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_West_Coast_waterfront_strike); Montgomery, Alabama, following the mob actions against the [Freedom Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Riders#Mob_violence_in_Montgomery)). It has also been declared by renegade local leaders seeking to avoid arrest or challenges to their authority ([Nauvoo, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauvoo,_Illinois) by Joseph Smith during the [Illinois Mormon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Mormon_War) and [Utah](31716.md) by Governor Brigham Young during the [Utah War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_War)).[^cite_note-20]

The martial law concept in the United States is closely tied with the right of *[habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus)*, which is in essence the right to a hearing on lawful imprisonment, or more broadly, the supervision of law enforcement by the judiciary. The ability to suspend *habeas corpus* is related to the imposition of martial law.[^cite_note-21] Article 1, Section 9 of the [U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Constitution) states, "The Privilege of the Writ of *Habeas Corpus* shall not be suspended, unless when in Cases of Rebellion or Invasion the public Safety may require it." There have been many instances of the use of the military within the borders of the United States, such as during the [Whiskey Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskey_Rebellion) and in the South during the [Civil Rights Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Movement), but these acts are not tantamount to a declaration of martial law.

In [United States law](27552742.md), martial law is limited by several court decisions handed down between the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) and [World War II](32927.md). In 1878, [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_United_States) passed the [Posse Comitatus Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posse_Comitatus_Act), which, depending on the circumstances, can forbid U.S. military involvement in domestic law enforcement without congressional approval.([18 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_18_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 1385](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1385), original at 20 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large) [152](https://legislink.org/us/stat-20-152))

The legality of the implementation of martial law was examined in 1866, in the court case *Ex parte Milligan*, 71 U.S. 2 (1866).[^cite_note-ex-parte-milligan-22] Through this case, the Supreme Court established that trying civilians in military tribunals was unconstitutional unless there were no civilian courts available.[^cite_note-ex-parte-milligan-22] Today, the ability to declare martial law over the United States is not explicitly granted in the Constitution.[^cite_note-23] Despite this, martial law has been declared at least 68 times in the United States.[^cite_note-brennancenter-10]

There are two main schools of thought regarding the declaration of martial law. First, some scholars argue that the ability to declare martial law is a constitutional power vested in Congress, and in some cases of emergency, the President.[^cite_note-Lieber2019-24] The second school of thought believes that the power to declare martial law in the United States is not expressed in any law, but rather arises as a matter of necessity and in the interests of "national self-preservation".[^cite_note-Lieber2019-24] As it stands today, there is no explicit provision in the Constitution granting powers to any specific body of government to declare martial law.

Historically, martial law has been declared in response to national emergencies in the United States. In Hawaii, for example, martial law was instituted following the attack on Pearl Harbor.[^cite_note-pearlharbor-25] The Supreme Court evaluated the legality of declaring martial law in Hawaii in the court case *Duncan v. Kahanamoku*, 327 U.S. 304 (1946).[^cite_note-duncan-v-kahanamoku-26] Here, the Supreme Court held that although Hawaii was not yet a state, the legality of declaring martial law must be analyzed as though Hawaii was one.[^cite_note-duncan-v-kahanamoku-26] As a result, the United States determined that the safety of the residents of Hawaii was their responsibility, and martial law was implemented throughout the Hawaiian Islands.[^cite_note-pearlharbor-25]

In June 1970, martial law was effected in the small university-town of [Isla Vista, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Vista,_California) next to the [University of California at Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Santa_Barbara) at the acme of the 1969–1970 student [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests) against the [Vietnam War](32611.md). Student [demonstrations](6787314.md) had engendered aggressive police [repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression), which reached an apex when students burned down the [Isla Vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Vista) [Bank of America](347756.md) building. One student, Kevin Moran, who was working to put out the flames, was shot and killed by police.[^cite_note-27] In response, [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor) [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) deployed the [national guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_guard) in Isla Vista and effected a 6am to 6pm [curfew](297681.md).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] This set of events was one of the rare instances of full-scale martial law being effected in the United States outside of wartime.

#### China

Further information: [Martial law in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Taiwan) and [White Terror (Taiwan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Terror_\(Taiwan\))

In China, martial law in the [Beiyang government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiyang_government) could be dated back to the final year of the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). The outline of a 1908 draft constitution—modeled on [Japan](15573.md)'s [Meiji Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Constitution)—included provisions for martial law. The [Provisional Government of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_the_Republic_of_China_\(1912\)) promulgated the [Provisional Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_China) in March 1911, which authorized the [President](61155.md) to declare martial law in times of emergency. The *Martial Law Declaration Act* were issued by the [Nationalist Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Government) later in 1920s and amended in 1940s.

Martial law was declared in 1947 in [Taiwan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_\(Republic_of_China_province\)) after the [February 28 incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_28_incident).

During the [Chinese civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_civil_war), the [Nationalist](16903.md)-led central [government of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China) declared martial law in most provinces on 10 Dec 1948. Martial law was declared again in Taiwan Province on 19 May 1949, effective the following day. At the end of 1949, the Nationalist government lost most territories to the communists and retreated to Taiwan. They used the perceived need to suppress Communist activities in Taiwan was utilised as a rationale for not lifting martial law, until thirty-eight years later, on 15 July 1987, martial law in Taiwan was lifted. Taiwan's period of martial law was "the longest imposition of martial law by a regime anywhere in the world" at that time,[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-reuters20110421-41] but has since been surpassed by Brunei and Syria. Kinmen and Matsu didn't end their martial law period until 7 Nov 1992, making their total length of martial period 43 years.

Martial law was [imposed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_4_martial_law) in [Beijing](18603746.md) in 1989 following the [Tiananmen Square protests of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989) by the Communist-ruled government on mainland China.

#### India

The sole mention of Martial Law in the [Indian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) is in Article 34 which gives Parliament the power to indemnify persons in respect of acts done in territories where martial law was in force and to legitimize such actions. But in the text itself, there is no mention of any grants of power to declare martial law.[^cite_note-42] The [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) in [Puttaswamy v. Union of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puttaswamy_v._Union_of_India) declared that certain rights of life and liberty are [natural rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law), which cannot be curbed by the state, but also upheld the inherent right of issuing writs by courts.[^cite_note-Firstpost_rightful_place-43] As the power of martial law is given through an [Enabling Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act) of the Parliament, it is inherently subject to the above decision.&#x20;

During the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), martial law was effectively declared in the [Defense of India Act, 1915](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_of_India_Act_1915) and the [Defense of India Act, 1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_of_India_Act,_1939). It was also declared in most of the Punjab during 1919 as a response to tensions caused by the [Amritsar Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jallianwala_Bagh_massacre). These tensions were caused due to the controversial [Rowlatt Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowlatt_Act).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

On 18 May 2003, during a military activity in [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh), under the order of the [president](10011631.md), [Indonesian Army](1711324.md) Chief imposed martial law for a period of six months to eliminate [Acehnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acehnese_people) separatists.

#### Iran

On 7 September 1978, in response to public demonstrations protesting the perceived government involvement in the death of the son of Ayatollah Khomeini, [Mostafa Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Khomeini), [Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah) [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](20065598.md) appointed Chief of Army Staff General [Gholam Ali Oveisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gholam_Ali_Oveisi) as the military governor of the capital city of [Tehran](57654.md).[^cite_note-MacroHistory:_World_History-47] On 8 September, the government effectively declared martial law on the capital along with several other cities throughout the country, after which further protests erupted that lead to the army opening fire on a group of protesters in Tehran's Jaleh Square on the same day. Estimates on the number of casualties vary; However, according to Iranian human rights activist [Emadeddin Baghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emadeddin_Baghi), the number of people killed was 88 of which 64 were gunned down in Jaleh Square.[^cite_note-48] The day is often referred to as [Black Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Friday_\(1978\)). Unable to control the unrest, the Shah dissolved the civil government headed by Prime Minister [Jafar Sharif-Emami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jafar_Sharif-Emami) on 6 November and appointed General [Gholam Reza Azhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gholam_Reza_Azhari) as the prime minister whom ultimately failed in his efforts to restore order to the country. As he was preparing to leave the country, the Shah dissolved the military government and appointed [Shapour Bakhtiar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapour_Bakhtiar), a reformist critic of his rule, as the new prime minister on 4 January 1979. Bakhtiar's government fell on 11 February and gave rise to the Islamic Republic and the creation of a new constitution.[^cite_note-MacroHistory:_World_History-47]

Article 79 of the [Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran) forbids the proclamation of martial law without the approval of the [Islamic Consultative Assembly](383276.md).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-WIPO-50]

#### Myanmar

On 1 February 2021, democratically elected members of Myanmar, known as the [National League for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_for_Democracy), were overthrown by Myanmar's military, called the [Tatmadaw](20394.md).[^cite_note-Campbell2023-51] This military placed their power in a [military junta](66589972.md).[^cite_note-hrw20230227-52] Five days following the coup, factory workers around Yangon (a region in Myanmar) held protests against the coup regime.[^cite_note-Campbell2023-51] On 14 March, security forces killed upwards of sixty-five protestors in the town of Hlaingtharyar.[^cite_note-hrw20230227-52] As a result, the military junta declared martial law over the region of Yangon, including over the majority of industrial zones.[^cite_note-hrw20230227-52] As of 22 February 2023, Myanmar's military junta has declared martial law over a total of 50 townships in the regions of Chin, Kachin, Karen, Karenni, Mon States, Yangon and Mandalay.[^cite_note-hrw20230227-52] News reports indicate that since the military coup in February 2021, and the subsequent declaration of martial law in Myanmar regions, military tribunals have sentenced more than 100 people to death.[^cite_note-hrw20230227-52] Reports further indicate that a total of three townships in Myanmar's northwest reside under the "executive and judicial jurisdiction of regional military commander Maj. Gen. Than Htike," who has since been sanctioned by the European Union for alleged human rights violations since Myanmar's declaration of martial law.[^cite_note-hrw20230227-52]

#### Pakistan

Main article: [Military coups in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Pakistan)

Martial law was declared in [Pakistan](23235.md) on 7 October 1958, by President [Iskander Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander_Mirza) who then appointed General [Muhammad Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(general\)) as the Chief Martial Law Administrator and Aziz Ahmad as Secretary General and Deputy Chief Martial Law Administrator. However, three weeks later General Ayub—who had been openly questioning the authority of the government before the imposition of martial law—deposed Iskandar Mirza on 27 October 1958 and assumed the presidency that practically formalized the militarization of the political system in Pakistan. Four years later a new document, Constitution of 1962, was adopted. The second martial law was imposed on 25 March 1969 by Yahya Khan, when President Yahya Khan abrogated the Constitution of 1962 and Ayub Khan handed over power to the Army Commander-in-Chief, General [Agha Mohammad Yahya Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan). On assuming the presidency, General Yahya Khan acceded to popular demands by abolishing the one-unit system in West Pakistan and ordered general elections on the principle of one man one vote.

The civilian martial law was imposed by [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto), the first civilian to hold this post in Pakistan after the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War). On 21 December 1971, Bhutto took this post as well as that of President. It was the first civilian martial law.

The third was imposed by the General [Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Zia-ul-Haq) on 5 July 1977. After several tumultuous years, which witnessed the secession of [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) to form Bangladesh, politician [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto) took over in 1971 as the first civilian martial law administrator in recent history, imposing selective martial law in areas hostile to his rule, such as the country's largest province, [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_\(Pakistan\)). Following widespread [civil disorder](329906.md), General Zia overthrew Bhutto and imposed martial law in its totality on 5 July 1977, in a bloodless [coup d'état](25914154.md). Unstable areas were brought under control through indirect [military action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_action), such as Balochistan under Martial Law Governor, General [Rahimuddin Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahimuddin_Khan). Civilian government resumed in 1988 following General Zia's death in an aircraft crash.

On 12 October 1999, the government of Prime Minister [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif) was dissolved, and the Army took control once more. A fourth martial law was imposed. General [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) took the title of [Chief Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Executive) until the [President of Pakistan](419460.md) [Rafiq Tarar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafiq_Tarar) resigned and General Musharraf became president. Elections were held in October 2002 and [Mir Zafarullah Khan Jamali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarullah_Khan_Jamali) became [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md). Jamali's premiership was followed by [Chaudhry Shujaat Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhry_Shujaat_Hussain) and [Shaukat Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaukat_Aziz). While the government was supposed to be run by an elected prime minister, there was a common understanding that important decisions were made by the President General Musharraf.

General Pervez Musharraf pointed out it as an emergency, not Martial Law. The Constitution, Parliament and Provincial Assemblies were suspended and Musharraf issued "Proclamation of Emergency" on 14 October 1999.

On 3 November 2007, President General Musharraf declared the state of emergency in the country which is claimed to be equivalent to the state of martial law as the constitution of Pakistan of 1973 was suspended, and the Chief Justices of the Supreme Court were fired.

On 12 November 2007, Musharraf issued some amendments in the Military Act, which gave the armed forces some additional powers.

#### Philippines

Main article: [Martial law in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_the_Philippines)

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_the_Philippines), [President](271126.md) [José P. Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_P._Laurel) placed the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Philippine_Republic) (then a [client state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state) of [Imperial Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan)) under martial law via Proclamation No. 29, dated 21 September 1944 and enforced the following day at 09\:00 [PST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Standard_Time). Proclamation No. 30 was issued on 23 September, declaring the existence of a [state of war](33158.md) between the [Philippines](23440.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md), effective 10\:00 that day.

The country was under martial law again from 1972 to 1981 under President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos). [Proclamation No. 1081](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._1081) ("Proclaiming a State of Martial Law in the Philippines") was signed on 21 September 1972 and came into force on 23 September. The official reason behind the declaration was to suppress increasing civil strife and the threat of a [communist takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_rebellion_in_the_Philippines), particularly after a series of bombings (including the [Plaza Miranda bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Miranda_bombing)) and an assassination attempt on [Defense Minister](36166520.md) [Juan Ponce Enrile](3322486.md) in [Mandaluyong](359686.md).

The policy of martial law was initially well received, but it eventually proved unpopular as the military's [human rights](4513322.md) abuses (e.g. use of [torture](47702.md) in intelligence gathering, [forced disappearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_disappearance)), along with the decadence and excess of the Marcos family and their allies, had emerged. Coupled with economic downturns, these factors fermented dissent in various sectors (e.g. the urban [middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class)) that crystallised with the [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Benigno_Aquino_Jr.) of jailed oppositionist [Senator](299521.md) [Benigno Aquino Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_Jr.) in 1983, and widespread fraud in the [1986 snap elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Philippine_presidential_election). These eventually led to the [1986 People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_People_Power_Revolution) that ousted Marcos and forced him into exile in [Hawaii](13270.md) where he died in 1989; his rival presidential candidate and Aquino's widow, [Corazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino), was installed as his successor.

During this 9-year period, curfews were implemented as a safety measure. Majority of radio and television networks were suspended. Journalists who were accused of speaking against the government were taken as political prisoners, some of them to be physically abused and tortured by the authorities.

Others have stated that the implementation of Martial Law was taken advantage by the Marcos regime. Billion pesos worth of property and ill-gotten wealth was said to be acquired by Marcos' consort, [First Lady](5628451.md) [Imelda Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos). This alleged money laundering issue was brought back recently, particularly in the *[PiliPinas Debates 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PiliPinas_Debates_2016)* for the recently held [Philippine Presidential Elections on May 9, 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_presidential_election). [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md), Ferdinand Marcos's son, ran for the vice presidency and lost.

There were rumours that President [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) was planning to impose martial law to end military *[coup d'etat](25914154.md)* plots, general civilian dissatisfaction, and criticism of her legitimacy arising from the dubious results of the [2004 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Philippine_presidential_election). Instead, a [State of National Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_state_of_emergency_in_the_Philippines) was imposed in 2006 from 24 February to 3 March, in order to quash a coup attempt and quell protesters.

On 4 December 2009, President Arroyo officially placed the [Province](165100.md) of [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao) under a state of martial law through Proclamation No. 1959.[^cite_note-gmanews.tv-53] As with the last imposition, the declaration suspended the [writ of habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writ_of_habeas_corpus) in the province.[^cite_note-54] The announcement came days after hundreds of government troops were sent to the province to raid the armories of the powerful Ampatuan clan. The Ampatuans were implicated in the [massacre of 58 persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_Massacre), including women from the rival [Mangudadatu clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esmael_Mangudadatu), human rights lawyers, and 31 media workers. Cited as one of the bloodiest incidents of [political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_violence) in Philippine history, the massacre was condemned worldwide as the worst loss of life of media professionals in one day.[^cite_note-gmanews.tv-53]

On 23 May 2017, President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) declared martial law throughout the main southern island of [Mindanao](20546.md), through [Proclamation No. 216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._216), due to the [attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi_crisis) of [Maute Group](50650448.md) in [Marawi City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi_City), [Lanao del Sur](297370.md). It was announced in a briefing in Moscow by Secretary [Ernesto Abella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Abella),[^cite_note-news.abs-cbn.com-55] and was in effect until December 2019.

#### South Korea

South Korea's presidents are able to impose either emergency or security martial law to maintain public order during national crises through Article 77 of the Korean Constitution.[^cite_note-\:0-57]

In October 1946, the [United States Army Military Government in Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Military_Government_in_Korea) declared martial law as a result of the [Daegu Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu_Riot).[^cite_note-champyungan-58] On 17 November 1948, president [Syngman Rhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syngman_Rhee) declared martial law in order to quell the [Jeju uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeju_uprising).[^cite_note-jejuweekly20100331-59] On 19 April 1960, Rhee declared martial law again in order to suppress the [April Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Revolution). The following day, Minister of National Defense [Song Yo-chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Yo-chan) declared martial law, forcing Rhee to resign from office.[^cite_note-Hankyoreh20110418-60] Martial law had been declared 10 times during Rhee's tenure.[^cite_note-\:0-57]

President [Park Chung Hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee) declared martial law in 1961, 1964, and 1972, and 1979. The reasons ranged from his military coup, suppressing protests, and consolidating his authority.[^cite_note-\:0-57]

Following the [1979 South Korean coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_South_Korean_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), General [Chun Doo-hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chun_Doo-hwan) staged a military coup and [declared martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_d'%C3%A9tat_of_May_Seventeenth) on 17 May 1980, forcing president [Choi Kyu-hah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choi_Kyu-hah) to resign and starting Chun's military dictatorship which lasted until 1981.[^cite_note-61]

On 3 December 2024, president [Yoon Suk Yeol](62877952.md) attempted a self-coup by [declaring martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_South_Korean_martial_law).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] However a few hours later, despite attempts by the [Republic of Korea Army Special Warfare Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea_Army_Special_Warfare_Command) to prevent lawmakers from entering the [National Assembly](601423.md), 190 lawmakers made it in and voted unanimously to lift the martial law.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Yoon then proceeded to lift it and apologized, roughly six hours after declaring it.[^cite_note-67] This declaration of martial law ultimately led to Yoon's [impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_of_Yoon_Suk_Yeol) two weeks later on 14 December. The Constitutional Court unanimously upheld Yoon's removal from office on 4 April 2025.

#### Syria

The martial law regime between the [1963 Syrian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and 2011 is the longest ranging period of active martial law.[^cite_note-reuters20110421-41] Similar to other countries, martial law in Syria was established as a response to the declaration of a state of emergency.[^cite_note-68] When on 8 March 1963, the Baath Party seized power, the prime minister of Syria, acting as the martial law governor, was granted extraordinary powers through his declaration of a state of emergency.[^cite_note-hrw2007-69] Syrian laws enabled the martial law governor to place many restrictions on freedoms of individuals, such as with respect to "meetings, residence, travel and passage in specific places or at particular times; to preventatively arrest anyone suspected of endangering public security and order; to authorize investigation of persons and places; and to delegate any person to perform any of these tasks".[^cite_note-hrw2007-69][^cite_note-70] However, the state of emergency declaration in Syria remained intact for nearly 50 consecutive years, prompting intervention and commentary from the international community.[^cite_note-hrw2007-69]

International bodies declared such an extended state of emergency as against international law. Specifically, it was held to be in violation of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (hereinafter "ICCPR"), which Syria is a party to.[^cite_note-hrw2007-69] Article 4 of the ICCPR "limits the application of emergency laws to a time of 'public emergency which threatens the life of the nation and the existence of which is officially proclaimed.' It further stipulates that the state parties to the ICCPR may derogate from their obligations under the treaty only 'to the extent strictly required by the exigencies of the situation, provided that such measures are not inconsistent with their other obligations under international law.'"[^cite_note-ohchr-71]

In 2000, Syria responded to the allegations from the ICCPR, and countered that it was in compliance with the ICCPR in a report to the United Nations Human Rights Committee.[^cite_note-un2009-72] Syria justified this ongoing declaration of emergency through their concerns of ongoing threats of war by Israel.[^cite_note-hrw2007-69]

&#x20;On 28 July 2005, the United Nations responded:&#x20;

> "Noting with concern that the state of emergency declared some forty years ago is still in force and provides for many derogations in law or practice from the rights guaranteed under articles 9 14, 19, and 22, among others, of the Covenant, without any convincing explanation being given as to the relevance of these derogations to the conflict with Israel and as to the necessity of these derogations to meet the exigencies of the situation claimed to have been created by the conflict."[^cite_note-un2009-72][^cite_note-ohchr-71]&#x20;

In further response, Syria reiterated their position that the ongoing emergency declaration was due to a continued threat of war with Israel.[^cite_note-un2009-72][^cite_note-hrw2007-69] Despite ongoing dialogue over a period of years between Syria, the ICCPR, and the United Nations, the declaration remained in effect for the next six years from the 2005 statement made by the United Nations advising of the invalidity of such an extensive state of emergency declaration.[^cite_note-un2009-72][^cite_note-ohchr-71][^cite_note-hrw2007-69]

Ultimately, after 48 years, in April 2011, President Bashar al-Assad ended Syria's state of emergency, thereby signaling the end of the longest martial law ruling in history.[^cite_note-routledge2016-73] This came as a response to [protests](39750126.md) demanding freedom from the historically long police rule over Syria.[^cite_note-routledge2016-73]

#### Thailand

Martial law in [Thailand](30128.md) derives statutory authority from the Act promulgated by King [Vajiravudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajiravudh) following the abortive [Palace Revolt of 1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_Revolt_of_1912), entitled "Martial Law, B.E. 2457 (1914)". Many coups have been attempted or succeeded since then, but the Act governing martial law, amended in 1942, 1944, 1959 and 1972, has remained essentially the same.[^cite_note-75] On 19 September 2006, the [Royal Thai Armed Forces](30136.md) declared martial law following a bloodless [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Thai_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) in the [Thai](30128.md) capital of [Bangkok](56615.md), declared while Prime Minister Shinawatra was in [New York City](645042.md) to address the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md). [General Sonthi Boonyaratglin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Sonthi_Boonyaratglin) took the control of the government, and soon after handed the premiership to ex-Army Chief [General Surayud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Surayud). Sonthi himself is [Chief of the Administrative Reform Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Administrative_Reform_Council). At 3 am, on 20 May 2014, following seven months of civil and political unrest, Army Commander-in-Chief Gen. [Prayut Chan-ocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayut_Chan-ocha), declared martial law nationwide.[^cite_note-76]

The nationwide martial law, which was imposed just prior to [a coup staged by the army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Thai_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), was lifted on 1 April 2015, through an approval by King [Bhumibol Adulyadej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumibol_Adulyadej). However, it was replaced by a new security order;[^cite_note-77] and the military retained sweeping powers.[^cite_note-78]

In [the country's Muslim-dominated south](1453076.md), martial law has been enforced amid [ongoing conflict involving Islamic separatists](2326869.md). On 5 January 2004, the national government declared such in [the provinces](204637.md) of [Pattani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattani_province), [Yala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yala_province), and [Narathiwat](241795.md);[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] and on 3 November 2005, the same was imposed in two [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Thailand) of [Songkhla](244844.md).[^cite_note-81] Since 2025, there are plans to lift it in four districts of the first three [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia%E2%80%93Thailand_border) provinces;[^cite_note-82] but these have been postponed due to increasing violent attacks.[^cite_note-83]

Amid [worsening disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cambodia%E2%80%93Thailand_border_conflict) with neighboring [Cambodia](334751.md), on 25 July 2025, a day after [the fighting began](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cambodia%E2%80%93Thailand_clashes) near [the border](65369821.md), the country's Border Defence Command declared martial law in the districts bordering Cambodia—seven in [Chanthaburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanthaburi_province) and one in [Trat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trat_province).[^cite_note-84]

#### Turkey

Main article: [Martial law and state of emergency in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_and_state_of_emergency_in_Turkey)

Since the foundation of the [Republic of Turkey](11125639.md) in 1923, the [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md) conducted three [coups d'état](25914154.md) and declared martial law. The first instance was established following the [1960 Turkish coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), which toppled down the [Democrat Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Party_\(Turkey,_1946%E2%80%931961\)) government and executed its leaders. The second was established after the [1971 Turkish military memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Turkish_military_memorandum) for a short period of time to impose reforms to confront escalated domestic violence, which proved unsuccessful. As a result of conflicts between [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics) and [far-right](221220.md) groups in Turkey growing, martial law was established for the third time in 1978, followed by the [1980 Turkish coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) that was kept in place until 1983. The martial law between 1978 and 1983 was replaced by a [state of emergency](298608.md) in a limited number of provinces that lasted until November 2002. The [Peace at Home Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_at_Home_Council)'s official statement in a broadcast on [TRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRT_1) during the [2016 coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Turkish_coup_attempt) included a declaration of martial law.[^cite_note-85]

#### Finland

The Preparedness Act (SDK 1552/2011, [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language): *valmiuslaki*) is a law in Finnish legislation, enacted in accordance with the constitutional procedure. The purpose of the Act is to give the authorities sufficient powers in times of war and other exceptional circumstances. During a state of defence (war), there is also the Defence Status Act, the provisions of which override the Preparedness Act. Together, the two laws form the Emergency Preparedness Act. The current Emergency Preparedness Act and its predecessor of the same name (1080/1991) were designed to replace the emergency provisions previously scattered over several different acts.

#### Ireland

In 1916, during the [Easter Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Rising), [Lord Wimborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Guest,_1st_Viscount_Wimborne) the [Lord Lieutenant of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland), declared martial law to maintain order in the streets of [Dublin](8504.md). This was later extended both in duration and geographical reach to the whole of the country with the consent of the [United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) government. Much of Ireland was declared under martial law by the British authorities during the [Irish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_War_of_Independence). A large portion of Ireland was also under de facto martial law during the [Irish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Civil_War).

In late July 1921 Lord Cave ([House of Lords](13658.md)) ruled on an appeal that: "...the military court, the validity of whose sentence was called into question, was a body possessing no statutory or common law authority...". The [Court of Chancery (Ireland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Chancery_\(Ireland\)) also ruled that the [Restoration of Order in Ireland Act 1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_of_Order_in_Ireland_Act_1920) had superseded the power to declare martial law. Any sentences by military tribunals that were not in accordance with that Act were declared void.[^cite_note-87]

The current [Irish Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ireland) allows for martial law if the government declares a state of emergency; however capital punishment is prohibited in all circumstances, including a state of emergency.

#### Israel

Military administrative government was in effect from 1949 to 1966 over some geographical areas of [Israel](9282173.md) having large [Arab](492331.md) populations, primarily the [Negev](142273.md), [Galilee](12639.md), and the [Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_\(Israel\)). The residents of these areas were subject to martial law.[^cite_note-Feron-88][^cite_note-Kodmani-Darwish-89] The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) enforced strict residency rules. Any Arab not registered in a census taken during November 1948 was deported.[^cite_note-90] Permits from the military governor had to be procured to travel more than a given distance from a person's registered place of residence, and [curfew](297681.md), [administrative detentions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_detention), and expulsions were common.[^cite_note-Feron-88] Although the military administration was officially for geographical areas, and not people, its restrictions were seldom enforced on the Jewish residents of these areas. In the early 1950s, martial law ceased to be in effect for those Arab citizens living in predominantly Jewish cities of [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa), [Ramla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramla), and [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod), constituting a total of approximately 15% of the Arab population of Israel. But military rule remained in place on the remaining Arab population elsewhere within Israel until 1966.[^cite_note-91]

This period is remembered for its extreme crackdown on political rights, as well as unaccountable military brutality. Most political and civil organization was prohibited. Flying of the [Palestinian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Palestine), as well as other expressions of Palestinian patriotism were prohibited. Furthermore, despite theoretical guarantee of full political rights, military government personnel frequently made threats against Arab citizens if they did not vote in elections for the candidates favored by the authorities.[^cite_note-92] Perhaps the most commemorated incidence of military brutality in this time period was the [Kafr Qasim massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Qasim_massacre) in 1956, in which the [Israel Border Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Border_Police) killed 48 people (19 men, 6 women and 23 children aged 8–17) as they were returning home from work in the evening. The Israeli army had ordered that all Arab villages in the proximity of the [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)) be placed under curfew. However, this order came into effect before the residents of these localities, including residents of [Kafr Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Qasim), were notified.

Following the 1967 war, in which the Israeli army occupied the [West Bank](33209.md), [Gaza Strip](12047.md), the [Golan Heights](7515865.md) in Syria, and the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) in Egypt, martial law over the [Palestinian](23267.md) population as well as the Jordanian, Syrian, and Egyptian populations in these areas was put in place. In 1993, the [Oslo I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_I_Accord) agreements facilitated limited self-rule for Palestinians under the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority). Officially, only parts of [Area C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_C_\(West_Bank\)) in the West Bank are under martial law.[^cite_note-93]

During the [2006 Lebanon war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_war), martial law was declared by Defense Minister [Amir Peretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Peretz) over the north of the country. The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) were granted the authority to issue instructions to civilians, and to close down offices, schools, camps and factories in cities considered under threat of attack, as well as to impose curfews on cities in the north.[^cite_note-Jpost-94]

Instructions of the [Home Front Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Front_Command) are obligatory under martial law, rather than merely recommended.[^cite_note-Jpost-94] The order signed by Peretz was in effect for 48 hours[^cite_note-Jpost-94] and was extended by the [Cabinet](5006115.md) and the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee over the war's duration.

#### Poland

Main articles: [Martial law in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Poland) and [Polish People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic)



Polish [ZOMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZOMO) squads with [police batons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_\(law_enforcement\)) preparing to violently disperse protesters during [martial law in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Poland), 1981–1983. The [sarcastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcasm) caption reads "outstretched hands of understanding" or "outstretched hands for agreement", with batons ironically symbolizing hands. 91 protesters died at the hands of the ZOMO and the [Secret Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Security_\(Poland\)) (SB).

Martial law was introduced in [Polish People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic) on 13 December 1981, by General [Wojciech Jaruzelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Jaruzelski) to prevent the [extraparliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra-parliamentary_opposition) opposition from gaining popularity and political power in the country. Thousands of people linked to the [Solidarity Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_Movement), including [Lech Wałęsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Wa%C5%82%C4%99sa), were arbitrarily arrested and detained. Approximately 91 deaths are attributed to the martial law, including 9 miners shot by the police force during the pacification of striking [Wujek Coal Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wujek_Coal_Mine). Curfews, censorship and food rationing were in place. A nationwide travel ban was imposed. The martial law was eventually lifted on 22 July 1983. Contemporary Polish society is divided in opinion on the necessity of introducing martial law in 1981. It is viewed by some as a lesser evil that was necessary to stop a potential [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) military intervention as the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact), which Poland signed in 1955, enabled other [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries to intervene if they believed that communism was in danger.

#### Switzerland

There are no provisions for martial law as such in [Switzerland](26748.md). Under the *Army Law* of 1995,[^cite_note-95] the [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Army) can be called upon by [cantonal](180752.md) (state) authorities for assistance (*Assistenzdienst*). This regularly happens in the case of natural disasters or special protection requirements (e.g., for the [World Economic Forum](172185.md) in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos)). This assistance generally requires parliamentary authorization, though, and takes place in the regular legal framework and under the civilian leadership of the cantonal authorities. On the other hand, the federal authorities are authorized to use the Army to enforce law and order when the Cantons no longer can or want to do so (*Ordnungsdienst*). With this came many significant points of reference. This power largely fell into disuse after [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-96]

#### Ukraine

Main article: [Martial law in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Ukraine)



2018 martial law in parts of Ukraine

The restrictions from martial law were defined in a 2015 law "On the Legal Regime of Martial Law". The [President](20688479.md) decides on the declaration of martial law and then [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) must approve it.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Martial law was first declared in Ukraine in 2018 and as a response to Russian hostilities.[^cite_note-americanbar-99] On 26 November 2018, lawmakers in the Verkhovna Rada overwhelmingly backed President [Petro Poroshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko)'s imposition of martial law along Ukraine's coastal regions and those bordering the [Russian Federation](25391.md) and [Transnistria](376581.md), an unrecognized breakaway state of [Moldova](19260.md) which has [Russian troops stationed in its territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_military_presence_in_Transnistria), in response to the [firing upon and seizure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Kerch_Strait_incident) of Ukrainian naval ships by Russia near the [Crimean Peninsula](163045.md) a day earlier. A total of 276 lawmakers in [Kyiv](585629.md) backed the measure, which took effect on 28 November 2018 and automatically expired in 30 days.[^cite_note-100]

This period of martial law was both intended to be, and ultimately was, limited in scope.[^cite_note-americanbar-99] Then-president Poroshenko proposed a 60-day martial law period, but ultimately a 30-day period of martial law was signed into effect.[^cite_note-americanbar-99] This period of martial law came to an end at its scheduled 30-day mark.[^cite_note-americanbar-99] This declaration was limited to specific areas of Ukraine, including territories along the Russia-Ukraine border, the Moldova-Ukraine border, the coasts of the Black Sea, the Sea of Azov, and the Azov – Kerch international waters.[^cite_note-americanbar-99]

On 24 February 2022, President [Volodymyr Zelensky](59210523.md) declared martial law in response to the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-101] Then, on 15 March, the Parliament of Ukraine adopted the Law of Ukraine "On Organizing Labor Relations under Martial Law" which came into effect on 24 March 2022, and "clarified relevant restrictions of the constitutional right and freedoms and set out special rules applicable to labor relations to replace the 'normal' rules of the Labour Code of Ukraine."[^cite_note-americanbar-99] Article 4(7) of the Law of Ukraine holds that the "General Staff of the Armed Forces of Ukraine shall direct, coordinate and control the activities of regional military administrations on defense, public safety, and order, and implement measures of martial law. The Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine shall direct, coordinate and control the regional military administrations regarding other issues."[^cite_note-americanbar-99]

As of November 2023, there have been ten extensions to the Ukrainian declaration of martial law.[^cite_note-rferl-102] This has led to the [2023 legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Ukrainian_parliamentary_election) and [2024 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Ukrainian_presidential_election) being delayed, due to elections not being allowed to be held in times of martial law.[^cite_note-rferl-102]

#### Yugoslavia

During the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars) in 1991, a "State of Direct War Threat" was declared. Although forces from the whole [SFRY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SFRY) were included in this conflict, martial law was never announced, but after secession, [Croatia](5573.md) and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) declared martial law. On 23 March 1999, a "State of Direct War Threat" was declared in [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), following the possibility of [NATO](21133.md) air-strikes. The day after strikes began, martial law was declared, which lasted until June 1999, although strikes ended on 10 June, following [Kumanovo Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumanovo_Treaty).

### Oceania

#### Australia

The [Black War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_War) was a period of violent conflict between [British colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1820_Settlers) and [Aboriginal Australians](2912594.md) in [Tasmania](29944.md) from the mid-1820s to 1832. With an escalation of violence in the late 1820s, Lieutenant-Governor [George Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Arthur) declared martial law in November 1828—effectively providing legal immunity for killing Aboriginal people.[^cite_note-103] It would remain in force for more than three years, the longest period of martial law in the history of the British colonies on the Australian continent. As of 2026, martial law has never been declared since [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) in 1901.
