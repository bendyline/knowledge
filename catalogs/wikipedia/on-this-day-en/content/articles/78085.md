**Sarnia** is a city in [Lambton County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambton_County), [Ontario](22218.md), Canada. It had a [2021 population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_2021_Census) of 72,047,[^cite_note-census-2021-city-4] and is the largest city on [Lake Huron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Huron). Sarnia is located on the eastern bank of the junction between the Upper and Lower [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes), where Lake Huron flows into the [St. Clair River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Clair_River) in the [Southwestern Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Ontario) region, which forms the [Canada–United States border](630260.md), directly across from [Port Huron, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Huron,_Michigan).

The site's natural harbour first attracted the French explorer [La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9-Robert_Cavelier,_Sieur_de_La_Salle). He named the site "The Rapids" on 23 August 1679, when he had horses and men pull his 45-ton barque *Le Griffon* north against the nearly four-knot current[^cite_note-7] of the St. Clair River.[^cite_note-Griffon-8] This was the first time that a vessel other than a canoe or other oar-powered vessel had sailed into Lake Huron,[^cite_note-9] and La Salle's voyage was germinal in the development of commercial shipping on the Great Lakes.[^cite_note-10] Located in the natural harbour, the Sarnia port remains an important centre for [lake freighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_freighter) and oceangoing ships carrying cargoes of grain and petroleum products.[^cite_note-11] The natural port and the salt caverns that exist in the surrounding areas,[^cite_note-12] together with the oil discovered in nearby [Oil Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_Springs,_Ontario) in 1858,[^cite_note-13] led to the dramatic growth of the petroleum industry in this area. Because Oil Springs was [the first place in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_petroleum_industry_in_Canada) and North America to drill commercially for oil, the knowledge that was acquired there resulted in oil drillers from Sarnia travelling the world teaching other enterprises and nations how to drill for oil.[^cite_note-14]

The complex of refining and [chemical companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_companies) is called "[Chemical Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_the_chemical_industry_in_Sarnia)" and located south of downtown Sarnia.[^cite_note-15] In 2011, the city had the highest level of [particulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter) [air pollution](10934212.md) of any Canadian city, but it has since dropped to rank 30th in this hazard.[^cite_note-16] About 60 percent of the particulate matter comes from industries and polluters in the neighbouring United States.[^cite_note-17]

Lake Huron is cooler than the air in summer and warmer than the air in winter; therefore, it moderates Sarnia's [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate), making temperature extremes of hot and cold less evident.[^cite_note-climate-6] In the winter, Sarnia occasionally experiences [lake-effect snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake-effect_snow) from Arctic air blowing across the warmer waters of Lake Huron and condensing to form [snow squalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_squall) over land.[^cite_note-snow-18]

## History

### Name

The word "Sarnia" is [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for [Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey), a British [Channel Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands).[^cite_note-19] In 1829 [Sir John Colborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Colborne,_1st_Baron_Seaton), a former governor of Guernsey, was appointed Lieutenant Governor of Upper Canada.[^cite_note-biographi2000-20] In this capacity, he visited two small settlements in 1835 that had been laid out on the shores of Lake Huron. One of these, named "The Rapids", consisted then of 44 taxpayers, nine frame houses, four log houses, two brick dwellings, two taverns and three stores.[^cite_note-sarnia2005-21][^cite_note-22] The villagers wanted to change its name but were unable to agree on an alternative. The English settlers favoured the name "Buenos Aires", and the ethnic Scottish favoured "New Glasgow".[^cite_note-Colborne-23]

Sir John Colborne suggested Port Sarnia. On 4 January 1836, the name was formally adopted by a vote of 26 to 16, and Colborne also named the nearby village Moore after British military hero Sir John Moore.[^cite_note-Colborne-23][^cite_note-sarnia-24] Sarnia adopted the nickname "The Imperial City" on 7 May 1914 because of the visit of Canada's [Governor General](38152.md), [the Duke of Connaught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Arthur,_Duke_of_Connaught_and_Strathearn), and his daughter [Princess Patricia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Patricia_of_Connaught).[^cite_note-Sarnia_City-25][^cite_note-26]

### Early history



Sarnia from space, 2018

Ethnic French colonists, who came from [Detroit](8687.md), were the first European colonizers of what became Sarnia in about 1807–1810; their role is marked by a historic plaque installed by the Ontario Heritage Society. They were fur traders with the Huron and [Three Fires Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Three_Fires). At this time, the French Jesuits also established a mission near the Huron village on the river's east bank. Later, the men established farms, attracted other settlers, and stimulated growth in the area.[^cite_note-27]

The township was surveyed in 1829, and in the early 1830s, a wave of Scottish immigrants settled in the area. They became dominant as English speakers and, for decades, claimed to have founded the city.

Port Sarnia expanded throughout the 19th century; on 19 June 1856, Parliament passed *An Act to Incorporate the Town of Sarnia,* and the name Port Sarnia was officially changed to Sarnia, effective 1 January 1857. The Act mentioned 1,000 inhabitants in three wards.[^cite_note-28] The important lumber industry was based on the wealth of virgin timber in the area, at a time of development around the Great Lakes. Lumber was especially in demand in the booming US cities of [Chicago](6886.md) and [Detroit](8687.md).

The discovery of oil in nearby Oil Springs in 1858 by [James Miller Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Miller_Williams), and the arrival of the [Great Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Western_Railway_\(Ontario\)) in 1858 and the [Grand Trunk Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Railway) in 1859, all stimulated Sarnia's growth.[^cite_note-29] The rail lines were later linked directly to the United States by the opening of the [St. Clair Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Clair_Tunnel) under the St. Clair River at Sarnia in 1890 by the Grand Trunk Railway. This was the first railroad tunnel ever constructed under a river.[^cite_note-30] The tunnel was an engineering marvel in its day, achieved through the development of original techniques for excavating in a compressed air environment.

In 1860 the Prince of Wales met the Chief of the Ojibways of Garden River, with 75 indigenous leaders, at Sarnia and gave them 1860 Queen Victoria Peace medals with the Prince of Wales logo engraved on the obverse.[^cite_note-31]

### 20th century to present

[Canada Steamship Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Steamship_Lines) formed in 1913 from many previous companies that plied the waters of the St. Clair River. One of these companies was Northwest Transportation Company of Sarnia, founded in 1870.[^cite_note-32] By 20 April 1914, when Parliament passed *An Act to Incorporate the City of Sarnia*, the population had grown to 10,985 in six wards.[^cite_note-33] Sarnia officially became a city as of 7 May 1914.[^cite_note-Sarnia_City-25]



Sarnia's grain elevator



Framed by the [Blue Water Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Water_Bridge), two lake freighters take on cargo in Sarnia Harbour.

Sarnia's grain elevator, which in the early 21st century is the 15th-largest operating in Canada,[^cite_note-34] was built in 1927 after the dredging of Sarnia Harbour to allow access to larger ships.[^cite_note-35] Two years later, grain shipments had become an important part of Sarnia's economy.[^cite_note-36]

The grain elevator rises above the harbour, and next to it is the slip for the numerous bulk carriers and other ships that are part of the contemporary shipping industry. They include vessels from all over the world. The waterway between Detroit and Sarnia is one of the world's busiest, as indicated by the average of 78,943,900 t (77,697,100 long tons; 87,020,800 short tons) of shipping that annually travelled the river going in both directions during the period 1993–2002.[^cite_note-37] Lake freighters and oceangoing ships, which are known as "salties",[^cite_note-38] pass up and down the river at the rate of about one every seven minutes during the shipping season.[^cite_note-39]

The [Paul M. Tellier Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Clair_Tunnel), which was named after [the retired president of CN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tellier) in 2004, was bored and began operation in 1995. It accommodates [double-stacked rail cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-stack_rail_transport) and is located next to the original tunnel, which has been sealed.[^cite_note-40]

A petroleum industry was established in the Sarnia area in 1858, and in 1942, [Polymer Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer_Corporation) manufactured synthetic rubber there during [World War II](32927.md), enhancing Sarnia's notability as a petrochemical centre.[^cite_note-41] During the [Cold War](325329.md), the United States Government included Sarnia on its list of possible targets for a Soviet nuclear strike because of its petrochemical industry.[^cite_note-42]

On 1 January 1991, Sarnia and the neighbouring town of Clearwater (formerly Sarnia Township) were amalgamated as the new city of Sarnia-Clearwater. The amalgamation was initially slated to include the village of [Point Edward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Edward,_Ontario), although that village's residents resisted. They were eventually permitted to remain independent of the city. On 1 January 1992, the city reverted to the name Sarnia.[^cite_note-sarnia2005-21]

Sarnia's population continued to grow from 1961 to 1991, with a 1991 population of 74,376. In 2001, the population had declined by approximately 3,000. Since 2001, Sarnia's population has been growing slowly, with a 2011 population count of 72,366.[^cite_note-2011Census_data-43] An April 2010 report "Sarnia-Lambton's Labour Market" states: "Large petrochemical companies are the community's main economic drivers. Over the recent past, several plants have shut down,\[sic] and of those still in operation, increased automation and outsourcing has led to significantly fewer workers."[^cite_note-44]

These shutdowns and the resulting loss of jobs, and therefore of population as workers search for employment elsewhere, will contribute to a general decline as forecast by an August 2011 study. It projects a 17% decline in population over the next twenty-five years.[^cite_note-45] The Monteith-Brown study cited outlines a plan for restructuring the city based on hybrid zoning areas, which will bring work opportunities closer to the neighbourhoods where people live. The City of Sarnia and Lambton County are also implementing an economic development plan with an emphasis on bio-industries and renewable energy.[^cite_note-46]

In 2020, Sarnia began to experience a "soaring murder rate".[^cite_note-Hunter-47] Sarnia had one homicide from 2016 to 2019 and eight homicides from 2020 to 2022.[^cite_note-48] The *Toronto Sun* reported that the increased murder rate was drug-related, with local youth unable to find opportunities for themselves in the city.[^cite_note-Hunter-47]

Sarnia is located on the eastern shore of Lake Huron at its extreme southern point, where it flows into the St. Clair River. Most of the surrounding area is flat, and the elevation ranges from 169 to 281 m (554 to 922 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-51] The soil mainly comprises clay.[^cite_note-52] Despite this high percentage of clay, the soil is remarkably rich for cultivation.

### Neighbourhoods

Wiltshire Park, Woodland, Oak Acres, Wees Beach, Oakwood Corners, Woodrow Shores, and Blackwell are part of the North End of Sarnia, which begins immediately north of [Ontario Highway 402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Highway_402) and terminates at the shore of Lake Huron. Blackwell, Bright's Grove, and the western part of Huron Heights are in the northeast part along the shores of Lake Huron. Coronation Park, Fourth Line Heritage Park, College Park, Lucasville, Bunyan, Froomfield, The Tree Streets, Mitton Village, and Sherwood Village are some of the neighbourhoods south of the highway.[^cite_note-53]

The village of Blue Water was built to house workers and their families in Chemical Valley during the construction of Polymer Corporation; at one point, it had nearly 3,000 residents. In 1961, all the residents were relocated, mostly to the North End, to make way for the expansion of the chemical industry. The village was demolished, and all that remains is a historical marker at the corner of Vidal Street and Huron Boulevard. This neighbourhood was largely forgotten until historian Lorraine Williams wrote two books about it. She was instrumental in gaining approval for the historical plaque.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

### Climate

Sarnia has a humid continental climate ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfb*), verging on the hot summer subtype *Dfa*.[^cite_note-56] Winters are cold with a few short-lasting [Arctic air masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_front) that dip far enough south and bring with them daily high temperatures below −10 °C (14 °F).[^cite_note-climate-6] Sarnia, while not quite located in the southwestern Ontario snowbelt, sometimes receives large quantities of [lake-effect snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake-effect_snow). Sarnia averages 112.0 cm (44.1 in) of snow per year, while London, inland and to the east, averages 194.3 cm (76.5 in).

The moderating effect of Lake Huron which Sarnia has a long shoreline on its north side, and to a lesser extent other nearby [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) ensure seasonal lag similar to other lakeside locations over much of Southern Ontario. This gives Sarnia a noticeably milder period following Summer and delaying onset of the first frost in the Fall.[^cite_note-57] Conversely, cooler average daytime temperatures tend to prevail for longer after winter breaks, through the late Spring and early Summer as compared to further inland. Daily lows of less than −10 °C (14 °F) occur an average of 29 days a year, and less than −20 °C (−4 °F), only average two days a year. Summers are warm with typically humid conditions. [Humidex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidex) readings and [Dew Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dew_Point) can be very high at times from late May to late September. Sarnia has the second greatest number of high humidex days at or above 35 °C (95 °F) (with 23.16 days on average per year) and humidex days at or above 30 °C (86 °F) (with 61.20 days on average per year) in Canada, ranking after [Windsor, Ontario](33596.md).[^cite_note-58] [Thunderstorms](70807.md) are frequent and can occasionally become severe from April to September.[^cite_note-59] Destructive weather is very rare in the area but has occurred, such as the [tornado event of 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Sarnia_tornado).

### Language

In 2021, 87.5% of Sarnians called English their mother tongue, 2.2% listed French, and 3.4% said another language was their mother tongue. 1.1% listed both English and a non-official language as their mother tongue.

### Religion

In 2021, 56.8% of residents were [Christian](18337522.md), down from 69.5% in 2011.[^cite_note-95] 25.2% of the population was [Catholic](606848.md), 21.1% were [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), and 6.6% were Christians of unspecified denomination. All other Christian denominations/Christian-related traditions comprised 3.9% of the population. 39.5% of residents were nonreligious or secular, up from 27.9% in 2011. All other religions (or spiritual beliefs) comprised 3.6% of residents. The largest non-Christian religions were [Islam](6037917.md) (1.2%) and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) (1.1%).

## Economy

See also: [Environmental impact of the chemical industry in Sarnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_the_chemical_industry_in_Sarnia)



Petrochemical industry of Sarnia's Chemical Valley

In its March 2011 Labour Market Report, the Sarnia-Lambton Workforce Development Board states that: "Even though employment in both the petrochemical and agricultural industries has declined significantly in recent years, these two industries remain central drivers of the Sarnia Lambton economy."[^cite_note-96]

When World War II threatened [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics) sources of natural [latex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latex) for [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_rubber), Sarnia was selected as the site to spearhead the development of synthetic petroleum-based rubbers for [war materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materiel). Polymer Corporation was built by [Dow Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Chemical_Company) at the request of the Government of Canada.[^cite_note-97] Large pipelines bring [Alberta](717.md) oil to Sarnia, where [oil refining](195137.md) and petrochemical production have become mainstays of the city's economy.[^cite_note-98] [Shell Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Canada), [Imperial Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Oil), and [Suncor Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suncor_Energy) ([Sunoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunoco)) operate refineries in Sarnia.[^cite_note-99] Large salt beds found under the city became a source of [chlorine](5667.md) and other significant ingredients which contributed to the success of Chemical Valley.[^cite_note-100] Chemical companies operating in Sarnia include [NOVA Chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Chemicals), [Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer) ([Lanxess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanxess) and H.C. Starck), [Cabot Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabot_Corporation), and [Ethyl Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethyl_Corporation).[^cite_note-101]

Dow Chemical ceased operations at its Sarnia site in 2009, with the plants after that decommissioned and the land sold to neighbouring [TransAlta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransAlta) Energy Corporation.[^cite_note-102] TransAlta produces power and steam for industry, and is the largest natural gas co-generation plant in Canada. It has created the Bluewater Energy Park on the former Dow site. Dow returned to Sarnia in 2019, when it took ownership of a former Dupont production site on Albert Street in Corunna that modifies polyethylene and polypropylene.[^cite_note-103]

Lanxess produces more than 150,000 t (150,000 long tons; 170,000 short tons) of [butyl rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butyl_rubber) annually at its Sarnia location and is the sole producer of regulatory-approved, food-grade butyl rubber, used in the manufacture of [chewing gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chewing_gum).[^cite_note-104] Within the boundaries of its Sarnia plant Lanxess has also created the Bio-industrial Park Sarnia.[^cite_note-105]

Chemical Valley and the surrounding area are home to 62 facilities and refineries.[^cite_note-106] These industrial complexes are the heart of Sarnia's infrastructure and economy. They directly employ nearly 8,000 and contribute to almost 45,000 additional jobs in the area.[^cite_note-107] In 1971, the Canadian government deemed this area so important to the economic development of the country that it printed an image of a Sarnia Oil Refinery on the reverse of the [Canadian \$10 note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_ten-dollar_note).[^cite_note-108] The huge industrial area is the cause of significant air and water pollution. The Canada Wide Daily Standard for airborne particulate matter and ozone pollution, regulation PM2.5, is 30 micrograms per cubic metre.[^cite_note-109] Forty-five percent of this particulate air pollution in Sarnia comes from Chemical Valley,[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] and the rest drifts over the St. Clair River from the neighbouring United States in the form of what is known as "Transboundary Air Pollution".[^cite_note-113]

Sarnia is the location of [Enbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enbridge)'s [Sarnia Photovoltaic Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia_Photovoltaic_Power_Plant). The facility underwent a full commercial operation in December 2009, with 20 [MW](21347693.md#Megawatt) of power. As of September 2010, the plant was the largest [photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaics) (PV) [solar power](13690575.md) generation facility in the world, putting out 97 MW.[^cite_note-114]

The 32 ha (80-acre) Western University Research Park, Sarnia-Lambton Campus was established in 2003 by the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario) as a joint initiative with the County of Lambton and the City of Sarnia.[^cite_note-115] The park is also the location of the Bioindustrial Innovation Centre, Canada's centre for the commercialization of industrial biotechnology.[^cite_note-116]

In 2015, [BioAmber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioAmber_Inc.) opened a \$141 million plant that manufactures 30,000 t (30,000 long tons; 33,000 short tons) of [succinic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succinic_acid) per year, a chemical used to make plastics, lubricants, paint, cosmetics, food additives, and other products. BioAmber plans to construct a second site and may build it in Sarnia.[^cite_note-117] Solutions4CO2 is developing a 4,645 m<sup>2</sup> (50,000 sq ft) demonstration facility at Bluewater Energy Park. This company captures waste gas/water streams to process into value-added co-products, pharmaceutical drugs, and biofuels.[^cite_note-118] PlantForm Corporation, a Canadian biotech startup company producing ultra-low-cost therapeutic antibody drugs, opened an office at the Western University Research Park in 2011.[^cite_note-119] At the same Park, from the summer of 2012 to the summer of 2016, KmX Corporation operated a pilot plant to produce membranes that filter wastewater from industrial processes. KmX production in Sarnia has since moved to Ottawa and Edmonton.[^cite_note-120]

### Retail and hospitality

Sarnia has one large mall, [Lambton Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambton_Mall), with 72 stores.[^cite_note-121] The mall, along with several smaller shopping centres, stores, and hotels, is the primary shopping area.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

## Arts and culture

### Music, theatre, and arts

The [International Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Symphony_Orchestra) plays at the Imperial Theatre for an annual season lasting from September to April. In addition to symphonic concerts, the Imperial Theatre offers year-round dramatic productions;[^cite_note-125] Former [Max Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Webster) frontman [Kim Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Mitchell) has returned to his hometown on occasion to play a concert, including his visit in 2008 for Sarnia's popular Ribfest. In this competition, local amateur chefs share their recipes for barbecued ribs and compete against each other.[^cite_note-126] Canadian composer and music educator [Raymond Murray Schafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Murray_Schafer) was born in Sarnia and developed his radical [schizophonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophonia) techniques there.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

The Sarnia Bayfest (which was preceded by the "Festival by the Bay") was an annual concert festival that featured big-name [rock](25423.md) and [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music) bands. Musicians and groups such as [Aerosmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosmith), [Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_\(band\)), [Keith Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Urban), [Jon Bon Jovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Bon_Jovi) and [Rascal Flatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rascal_Flatts) have played at the event.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] Financial problems caused the event's cancellation in 2013. In the summer of 2017, a new festival called Bluewater Borderfest enjoyed a successful inaugural event.[^cite_note-131]

Besides the single museum in Sarnia proper, six other local museums document Sarnia's history, including its legacy as the home of the North American oil industry.[^cite_note-132] Gallery Lambton offers 12 annual art exhibitions.[^cite_note-133] In 2012 the Judith and Norman Alex Art Gallery opened. It is an international Category A art gallery,[^cite_note-134] featuring exhibitions of Canadian art history, including paintings from the [Group of Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Seven_\(artists\)).[^cite_note-135]

In 2015, the [South Western International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Western_International_Film_Festival) was launched at the city's Imperial Theatre.[^cite_note-136]

During the Christmas season, the city of Sarnia presents the annual "Celebration of Lights" in Centennial Park. The event was created in 1984 by Wills Rawana and a committee funded by the retail chain [Hudson's Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_\(retailer\)), and the national telecommunications company [Telus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telus_Communications).[^cite_note-137] From modest beginnings, the event has garnered numerous awards as it has grown, including second place in the 2002 Canadian Government's Canada WinterLights competition. The celebration was incorporated in its national prizewinning year and is now run by a voluntary Board of Directors.[^cite_note-138]

## Attractions



Canatara Park



Germain Park, [Canadair Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadair_Sabre), in [Golden Hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Hawks) paint scheme

There are more than 100 parks in Sarnia,[^cite_note-Parks_and_Natural_Areas-139] the largest being Canatara Park, which covers more than 81 ha (200 acres) along the shore of Lake Huron.[^cite_note-Parks_and_Natural_Areas-139] Canatara is an [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe) word that means *Blue Water*. The park was opened in 1933.[^cite_note-140] Within the park is Lake Chipican, a haven for many different species of birds on their migration routes.[^cite_note-Canatara_Park_Open-141] Most years, birdwatchers recognize around 150 species. The park also maintains a Children's Animal Farm as part of Sarnia's commitment to wildlife.[^cite_note-142] The annual "Christmas on the Farm" weekend event held at the Farm in early December is a popular community event enjoyed by families.[^cite_note-Hagan_2009-143] Canatara Park is one of the first parks in southern Ontario to feature an outdoor fitness equipment installation.[^cite_note-144]

The largest recreational park in Sarnia is Germain Park, which incorporates five baseball diamonds, four soccer fields, an outdoor pool, and the Community Gardens.[^cite_note-Parks_and_Natural_Areas-139] As a memorial to Canadian aviators who died in World War II, one of the remaining [Canadair Sabres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadair_Sabre) in Canada is on display in the park.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

Centennial Park was opened on 1 July [Dominion Day holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Day) in 1967 as part of Canada's centenary celebrations.[^cite_note-147] The City of Sarnia decided in 2013 to close much of Centennial Park, after the discovery of toxic levels of lead and asbestos in the soil.[^cite_note-Centennial_Park-148] After years of remediation, the park was reopened in 2017.[^cite_note-149]

Howard Watson Trail is a former railway line that passes through a combination of urban and rural areas. This linear park is managed by a volunteer committee and spans 16 km (9.9 mi) through wooded areas and alongside ponds. Benches are available along the path as well as washroom facilities. The path is open year-round: bicycling, running, and dog walking are popular activities in the summer. Snowshoeing and cross-country skiing can be enjoyed on snowy days. Access to Lake Huron is available at Blackwell Side Road.

Sarnia connects to the Great Lakes [Waterfront Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfront_Trail), which stretches over 2,100 km (1,300 mi) along the Canadian shores of Lake Ontario, Lake Erie, Lake St. Clair, Lake Huron and the Niagara, Detroit, and St. Lawrence rivers. The Great Lakes Waterfront Trail connects 114 communities and hundreds of parks and natural areas, including wetlands, forests, and beaches.

Sarnia has one museum within its city limits, known as "Stones 'N Bones", which houses over 6,000 exhibits. The collection includes rocks, artifacts, fossils, and bones from around the world.[^cite_note-150]

The former Discovery House Museum has been converted into a [hospice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospice). This historic house, built between 1869 and 1875, is recognized as a testament to [Victorian Era construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture).[^cite_note-151]

The city's sandy freshwater beaches are a popular tourist attraction, while the sheltered harbour houses marinas for recreational [sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing). Since 1925, the 400 km (250 mi) [Mackinac race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Huron_to_Mackinac_Boat_Race) from Sarnia/Port Huron to [Mackinac Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackinac_Island) at the north end of the lake has been the highlight of the sailing season, drawing more than 3,000 sailors each year.[^cite_note-152]

Sarnia's fresh-cut potato fries are another popular tourist attraction. Thousands of visitors visit the chip trucks parked under the Blue Water Bridge annually. In 2012, during construction along the waterfront, Sarnia officials created a special detour to enable visitors to reach the chip trucks.[^cite_note-153] Realizing the popularity of Sarnia's chip trucks, the Ontario Medical Association includes them in a campaign to have fries and other junk food labelled for being dangerous in the same manner as cigarettes.[^cite_note-154]

[Mike Ceresia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Ceresia) is a Sarnia native. He won four IRF World Racquetball Championships and multiple silver medals between 1988 and 2002.[^cite_note-160]

The Sarnia-born world champion curler [Steve Bice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bice) played as alternate for the [Glenn Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Howard) rink in the [2007 Tim Hortons Brier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Tim_Hortons_Brier) and [2007 Ford World Men's Curling Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Ford_World_Men's_Curling_Championship), winning both times.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

## Government



City Hall and downtown

[Sarnia City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia_City_Council) consists of nine elected members: the Mayor, four city members, and four county members. The Mayor and all Council members are elected to four-year terms. The four Lambton County Council members serve both County and City Council.[^cite_note-163]

The current mayor, [Mike Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Bradley_\(politician\)), has held the position since December 1988 and is currently the second longest-serving mayor in the province of [Ontario](22218.md) behind [Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton,_Ontario)'s [Gord Krantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Krantz). Past mayors of the city have included [Andy Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Brandt), [Marceil Saddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marceil_Saddy), [Paul Blundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Blundy), and [Thomas George Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_George_Johnston).[^cite_note-164]

At the [provincial](2670174.md) level, Sarnia is located within the [Sarnia—Lambton provincial electoral district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia%E2%80%94Lambton_\(provincial_electoral_district\)), represented in 2013 by [Bob Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Bailey_\(politician\)), a member of the [Progressive Conservative Party of Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Ontario).[^cite_note-165] At the federal level, Sarnia is located within the [Sarnia—Lambton federal electoral district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia%E2%80%94Lambton_\(federal_electoral_district\)), which in 2026 is represented by Liberal [Marilyn Gladu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Gladu).[^cite_note-166]

Over the past 50 years, Sarnia's voters have been moderate. The party affiliation of its Members of Parliament, both provincial and federal, has swung back and forth largely between the Liberal and Progressive Conservative parties (a [New Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_New_Democratic_Party) was elected in their [1990 provincial wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Ontario_general_election)).[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

The Blue Water Bridge links Sarnia and its neighbouring village of Point Edward to the city of Port Huron in the United States. It spans the St. Clair River, which connects Lake Huron to [Lake St. Clair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Saint_Clair_\(North_America\)). The bridge's original three-lane span opened in 1938, was twinned on 22 July 1997,[^cite_note-171] making the bridge the fourth-busiest border crossing in Ontario.[^cite_note-172]

The Blue Water Bridge border crossing makes use of both the [NEXUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXUS) and the [Free and Secure Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_Secure_Trade) (FAST) program. Linking [Highway 402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Highway_402) with the American [Interstate 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_94_in_Michigan) (I-94) and [I-69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_69_in_Michigan), the bridge forms part of the [NAFTA Superhighway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAFTA_Superhighway). It is one of the most important gateways on the north–south truck routes.[^cite_note-173]

Public transportation within the City of Sarnia, including conventional bus transit, transportation of people with disabilities, transportation support for major events, and charter services, is provided by [Sarnia Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia_Transit).[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] From the city's local [Sarnia Chris Hadfield Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia_Chris_Hadfield_Airport), [Jazz Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_\(airline\)) use to operate services to and from [Toronto Pearson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Pearson_International_Airport) on behalf of [Air Canada Express](31110676.md), Air Canada plans to launch a motorcoach service leaving twice daily for Toronto airport in June 2026.[^cite_note-176] For rail travel, Sarnia is one of the two western [termini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_station), along with Windsor, of the [Via Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Rail) [Quebec City – Windsor Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City_%E2%80%93_Windsor_Corridor). It has service departing [Sarnia station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia_railway_station) in the morning and returning in the evening.[^cite_note-177]

### Health care

Sarnia is served by Bluewater Health, a hospital with 188 acute care beds, 70 complex continuing care beds and 27 rehabilitation beds.[^cite_note-178] The hospital opened in 2010, following the amalgamation of several smaller facilities and the destruction of the old hospital on Mitton Street.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-theobserver1-180]

## Education



Sarnia Education Centre of the [Lambton Kent District School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambton_Kent_District_School_Board)

The [Lambton Kent District School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambton_Kent_District_School_Board) is responsible for the 13 elementary and three secondary public schools (Northern Collegiate Institute and Vocational School, Alexander MacKenzie Secondary School, and Great Lakes Secondary School) located within Sarnia's boundaries.[^cite_note-181]

The [St. Clair Catholic District School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Clair_Catholic_District_School_Board) is responsible for the city's seven elementary and only secondary Catholic, [St. Patrick's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_Catholic_High_School). In 2014, St. Patrick's and St. Christopher's merged under the St. Patrick's name on St. Christopher's North Sarnia site.[^cite_note-182]

The *[Conseil scolaire catholique Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_scolaire_catholique_Providence)* (CSC Providence) represents the two French Catholic schools in the city, Saint-François-Xavier and Saint-Thomas-d'Aquin. In comparison, the *[Conseil scolaire Viamonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_scolaire_Viamonde)* operates two French public schools, the elementary École Les Rapides and the secondary École Secondaire Franco-Jeunesse. There are also two independent Christian elementary schools in Sarnia—Sarnia Christian School and Temple Christian Academy.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

[Lambton College](1413107.md), which offers two and three-year programs and diplomas, is one of Ontario's 21 colleges of applied arts and technology. It has a full-time enrolment of 3,500 and a part-time enrolment of about 8,000.[^cite_note-185] It is the city's only post-secondary school.[^cite_note-186]
