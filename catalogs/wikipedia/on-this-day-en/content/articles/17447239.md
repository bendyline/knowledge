[Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) at an official greeting ceremony in Norway with [Harald V of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_V_of_Norway)

**Norway–Russia relations** are the [bilateral relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateral_relations) between the [Kingdom of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Norway) and the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation). Diplomatic relations between the two nations were established on 30 October 1905 – four days after the establishment of Norway's independence.[^cite_note-1] Russia has an [embassy in Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Russia_in_Oslo), as well as consulates in [Barentsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barentsburg) and [Kirkenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkenes); likewise, Norway has an [embassy in Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Norway_in_Moscow), as well as consulates in [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk) and [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md). The countries border each other along a 195.7 km (121.6 mi) long [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway%E2%80%93Russia_border). Norway is on Russia's [Unfriendly Countries List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfriendly_Countries_List).

## Timeline

### Early history

In the 10th century, Norwegian [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) had sporadic contact with the inhabitants of *[Bjarmaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjarmaland)*, a region that today corresponds to the shores of the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea) in Russia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2] In the 13th century, the Norwegians conducted trading expeditions to the [Northern Dvina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Dvina) and they would sometimes reach the Russian interior.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2] A 1267 law code refers to the Norwegians' commercial expeditions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2]

Conflict between the Norwegians and the Russian city-state of [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Republic) over competing interests led to the signing of the [Treaty of Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Novgorod_\(1326\)) in 1326, which reaffirmed the [status quo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo) and maintained free trade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2] Although a border was not formally delineated, a buffer zone was created and some regions were placed under joint administration.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2]

The Norwegians built the fortress of [Vardøhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vard%C3%B8hus) in 1300 near the modern border while the main economic center on the Russian side was the [Solovetsky Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solovetsky_Monastery).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2] The Norwegians may have settled the [Kola Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_Peninsula) and they would continue to claim control over the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2] The Russian word *murmansky*, which refers to the [northern coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murman_Coast) in the region, is derived from an old name for the Norwegians, *nordmann*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2] The first Russian town in the region was founded in 1583 near the current site of [Arkhangelsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkhangelsk) and had a Norwegian guest house.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotilaine20041067-2]

### 1537–1814

Denmark handled the foreign relations of Norway during this period. Denmark and Russia were in general allies against their mutual enemy Sweden.

### 1814–1905

Sweden handled the foreign relations of Norway during this period. The [Norway–Russia border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway%E2%80%93Russia_border) was defined in 1826.

### 1917–1991

Further information: [Norway–Soviet Union relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway%E2%80%93Soviet_Union_relations)

### After 1991

On 27 April 2010, Norway and Russia officially resolved the [territorial dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Norway_border) in the Barents Sea.[^cite_note-3]

In May 2012, [Akhmed Zakayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhmed_Zakayev), Prime Minister of the [Ichkerian government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen_Republic_of_Ichkeria), attended the [Oslo Freedom Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Freedom_Forum), leading to formal complaints by Russia.[^cite_note-4]

In July 2020, Norway expelled a Russian diplomat on suspicion of espionage.[^cite_note-5]

After [Russia invaded Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in February 2022, Norway imposed sanctions on Russia.[^cite_note-6] Later on 6 April 2022, Norway [declared three Russian diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) *persona non grata*, and expelled them from the country.[^cite_note-7] Russia expelled three Norwegian diplomats later that month.[^cite_note-8] Except for fishing boats who may still dock in [Båtsfjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A5tsfjord_\(village\)), Kirkenes and [Tromsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troms%C3%B8), all Russian ships were also banned by Norway from entering Norwegian ports.[^cite_note-9] The border between the two countries remains open, but crossing is only allowed via [Borisoglebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borisoglebsky,_Murmansk_Oblast)/[Storskog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storskog).[^cite_note-10]

In October 2022, the [Norwegian Police Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Police_Security_Service) arrested [Mikhail Mikushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Mikushin), a Russian national posing as a Brazilian with the name of José Assis Giammaria, and charged him with gathering intelligence on state secrets on behalf of Russia.[^cite_note-11] Mikushin was exchanged back to Russia during [a prisoner exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ankara_prisoner_exchange) in August 2024.[^cite_note-12]

In April 2023 Norway expelled 15 Russian diplomats on suspicion of spying.[^cite_note-13] Russia promptly responded by expelling 10 Norwegian diplomats and in August added Norway to its [list of unfriendly countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfriendly_Countries_List).[^cite_note-14]

In September 2026, the Norwegian government acted on a Norwegian court's order, that resulted in Russian ship [Professor Molchanov](5343184.md) being arrested at [Svalbard](27441.md), Norway;[^cite_note-15] the Ukrainian company [Naftogaz](3614879.md) is owed money by Russia. On 3 September, Norway's ambassador was called in to Russia's foreign ministry; he explained that an [arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration) from 2023 in Hague, is the basis of the situation; furthermore he said the parties are one private party in addition to Russia.[^cite_note-16]

## Strains in bilateral relations

The environmentally harmful emissions from the [Norilsk Nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMC_Norilsk_Nickel) plant outside [Nikel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikel) in the [Murmansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Oblast)[^cite_note-17] have been for decades an unresolved issue in then Norwegian–Soviet, and now Norwegian–Russian relations.

Formerly there was a [territorial dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_dispute) over the [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea), but on 27 April 2010, Norway and Russia officially resolved the [territorial debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Norway_border).[^cite_note-18]

A 2017 Kremlin maritime [threat assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threat_assessment) which was sent to President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) highlighted Norway as a perceived threat and therefore a potential cause of [naval conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_conflict).[^cite_note-19]

In December 2017, Frode Berg, a Norwegian citizen, was arrested in Russia on allegations of having operated a [spy ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_ring) in the country since 2015, and was detained at [Lefortovo Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefortovo_Prison).[^cite_note-FP-20] In 2017, hackers believed to be Russians targeted the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Norway\)).[^cite_note-FP-20]

There has long been tension over the [GLOBUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLOBUS) radar installation in [Vardø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vard%C3%B8_\(town\)), which Russian officials believe to be part of a United States missile defense system. Two mock airstrikes involving Russian fighter jets and bombers were executed against the town in 2017 and 2018, each time pulling short of violating Norwegian airspace, and in 2019 a [Bal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_missile) coastal missile system was deployed 70 km from the radar system, just 35 km from the Norwegian-Russian land border.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Norway is strongly against the [ Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), and is among the leading countries in terms of military, humanitary and economic support to Ukraine.[^cite_note-25] Norway has instigated a long series of economic sanctions against Russia, including adhering to most of the sanctions from the European Union against Russia.[^cite_note-26]

## In popular culture

The 2015 fictional [political thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_thriller) [TV series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_series) *[Occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied)* is based on a hypothetical strain in relations between the two countries after Norway [ceases fossil fuel production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out) in response to a climate crisis. It is available on [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) in many countries.

In response to the TV series, the Vyacheslav Pavlovsky, the [Russian ambassador to Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Russia_in_Oslo), told [Russian News Agency TASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_News_Agency_TASS),

> It is certainly a shame that, in the year of the 70th anniversary of the victory in [World War II](32927.md), the authors have seemingly forgotten the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Army)'s heroic contribution to the [liberation of northern Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Finnmark) from [Nazi occupiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Norway), decided, in the worst traditions of the [Cold War](325329.md), to scare Norwegian spectators with the nonexistent threat from the east.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

The Russian embassy had been informed in an early stage of the work on the series.[^cite_note-29]
