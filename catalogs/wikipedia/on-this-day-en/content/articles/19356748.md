# Energy in South Korea



[Yongpyeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongpyeong) wind farm

[South Korea](27019.md) is a major energy importer, importing nearly all of its oil needs and ranking as the third-largest importer of [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) in the world in 2024.[^cite_note-1] [Electricity generation](9540.md) in the country mainly comes from conventional thermal power, which accounts for more than two thirds of production, and from [nuclear power](22153.md).[^cite_note-eia-2]

Energy producers were dominated by government enterprises, although privately operated coal mines and oil refineries also existed. The [National Assembly](601423.md) enacted a broad electricity sector restructuring program in 2000, but the restructuring process was halted amid political controversy in 2004 and remains a topic of intense political debate.[^cite_note-Pittman-3]

South Korea has no proven oil reserves.[^cite_note-eia-2] Exploration until the 1980s in the Yellow Sea and on the continental shelf between Korea and Japan did not find any offshore oil. The [Donghae-1 gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghae-1_gas_field) produced natural gas between 2004 and 2021, and natural gas exploration off the east coast was started in 2024.[^cite_note-4]

Coal supply in the country is insufficient and of low quality. The potential for hydroelectric power is limited because of high seasonal variations in the weather and the concentration of most of the rainfall in the summer. As of 2017, South Korean President [Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Jae-in) has vowed to end the country’s reliance on coal and also said the nation would move away from nuclear energy. He has taken a major step in that direction in June, saying his country would not try to extend the life of its nuclear plants, would close existing coal-fired plants, and would not build any new coal plants.[^cite_note-5]

In recent years, South Korea has set a new direction for its energy sector, with significant decarbonization goals, aiming to raise the share of electricity from renewable sources from 6% in 2019 to 35% by 2030.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Electric power



Electricity generation in South Korea - terawatt hours



[Siheung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siheung), [Gyeonggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeonggi)

### History

The [Korea Electric Power Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Electric_Power_Corporation) (KEPCO) provided electricity in the country. When KEPCO's predecessor, KECO, was founded in 1961, annual power production was 1,770 GWh. Production reached 73,992 GWh in 1987. In that year, residential customers used 17.9% of total production, public and service businesses used 16.2%, and the industrial sector used 65.9%. Sources of power generation were primarily nuclear power, coal, oil, and liquefied natural gas. Of the 54,885 GWh of electricity generated in 1985, 22% came from nuclear plants then in operation, 74% from non-nuclear thermal plants (oil and coal), and 4% from hydroelectric sites. It was predicted in 1988 that the generation structure by the year 2000 would be 10.2% hydroelectric, 12.2% oil, 22.9% coal, 10.2% LNG, and 44.5% nuclear.

## Sources

### Thermal

- [KEPCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Electric_Power_Corporation) (한국전력공사) controls 5 regional gencos who sell via [KPX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Power_Exchange) to the grid:

- [Korea East-West Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_East-West_Power) (한국동서발전㈜)

- [Korea Midland Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Midland_Power) (한국중부발전㈜)

- [Korea South-Eastern Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_South-Eastern_Power?action=edit\&redlink=1) (한국남동발전㈜)

- [Korea Southern Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Southern_Power?action=edit\&redlink=1) (한국남부발전㈜)

- [Korea Western Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Western_Power) (한국서부발전㈜)

[KOGAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOGAS) (한국가스공사) acts as importer of LNG for the power generators.

### Cogeneration and steam-heating

- [Korea District Heating Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_District_Heating_Corporation) (KDHC, 한국지역난방공사㈜) supplies steam and CHP to the Seoul area and Daegu. GS Power and SH Corp are local providers. KDHC is the world's largest [district heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_heating) company.

### Nuclear Power



[Kori Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kori_Nuclear_Power_Plant) in [Busan](68115.md)

Main article: [Nuclear power in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_South_Korea)

See also: [Anti-nuclear movement in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-nuclear_movement_in_South_Korea)

South Korea placed a heavy emphasis on [nuclear power](22153.md) generation. The country's first nuclear power plant, the [Kori Number One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kori_Nuclear_Power_Plant) located near Pusan, which opened in 1977. Eight plants operated in 1987, with yearly nuclear power generation at an estimated 39,314 gigawatt-hours, or 53.3% of total electric power output.[^cite_note-12]



For the first time on record, fossil fuels accounted for less than half (49.5%, 21.8 TWh) of South Korea’s total electricity generation.

### Storage

In December 2017, [Hyundai Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Electric) announced a plan to build a 150MW grid storage battery near [Ulsan](323111.md) for [Korea Zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Zinc).[^cite_note-13] A battery system at several substations, with a combined power of 978 MW and energy capacity of 889 MWh, was finished in 2024.[^cite_note-14]
