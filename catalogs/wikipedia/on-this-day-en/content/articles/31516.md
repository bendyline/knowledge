# Tibet

This article is about the historical ethno-cultural region of Tibet. For the current Chinese administrative division, see [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region). For the country that existed from 1912 to 1951, see [Tibet (1912–1951)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_\(1912%E2%80%931951\)).



Greater Tibet regions and claims; click image and go to 'More details' to understand more about the various colours

**Tibet** ([/tɪˈbɛt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d2/En-us-Tibet.ogg/En-us-Tibet.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d2/En-us-Tibet.ogg/En-us-Tibet.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a region in the western part of [East Asia](19605700.md), covering much of the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau). It is the homeland of the [Tibetans](249458.md). Other ethnic groups also reside on the plateau, including [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols), the [Lhoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhoba_people), [Monpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monpa_people), [Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_people),[^cite_note-1] [Sherpa](224737.md), and since the 20th century, [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) and [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) peoples.[^cite_note-2] Tibet is the highest region on Earth, with an average elevation of 4,380 m (14,000 ft).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Lying within the [Himalayas](37966.md), the highest point in Tibet is [Mount Everest](42179.md) – the highest peak on Earth, standing 8,848 m (29,000 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-5] Today, the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) governs most of the historical area as the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) and [autonomous prefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_prefecture) within [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu), Sichuan, [Qinghai](173818.md), and Yunnan provinces.&#x20;

In the 7th century, the central Tibetan [Yarlung dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_dynasty) expanded into surrounding areas such as [Zhangzhung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhung) and [Amdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo),[^cite_note-6] and by the 9th century the [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire) stretched from the [Tarim Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarim_Basin) and [Pamirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamirs) in the west to [Yunnan](166410.md) and [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) in the southeast, ruling over various non-Tibetan peoples. Following its dissolution, the region was divided among a number of principalities and tribal groups. Central Tibet and peripheral areas often fell under Chinese or [Mongol patronage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_under_Yuan_rule) between the 13th and the 18th centuries, when the [Gelug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelug) school of [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) rose to prominence. The [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) established its [rule in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_under_Qing_rule) after [expelling Mongol forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_expedition_to_Tibet_\(1720\)) and incorporated many areas into [Sichuan](65185.md) and other administrative regions.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]&#x20;

Following the [Xinhai Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution) in 1912, Qing soldiers were disarmed and escorted out of Tibet,[^cite_note-10] but it was constitutionally claimed by the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) as the [Tibet Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Area_\(administrative_division\)). The [13th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Dalai_Lama) [declared independence](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Independence_of_Tibet) in 1913, although it was neither recognised by the [Chinese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiyang_government) nor any foreign power.[^cite_note-11] Central Tibet operated autonomously until 1951 when, following the [Battle of Chamdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chamdo), it was [annexed by the People's Republic of China](14615536.md) after the [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama) ratified the [Seventeen Point Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeen_Point_Agreement). [His government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Phodrang) was abolished after the failure of the [1959 Tibetan uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Tibetan_uprising).[^cite_note-12]&#x20;

The [Tibetan independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_independence_movement)[^cite_note-lee-13] is principally led by the [Tibetan diaspora](23417648.md).[^cite_note-14] Various human rights organizations have reported restrictions on civil liberties, including limitations on religious practices, freedom of expression, and assembly, with the Chinese government maintaining strict control over information.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Reports on the extent of these restrictions vary, and claims of cultural suppression, including the [sinicization of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinicization_of_Tibet), are a subject of debate among scholars and international observers.&#x20;

The dominant [religion in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Tibet) is [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism); other religions include [Bön](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon), an [indigenous religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_religion) similar to Tibetan Buddhism;[^cite_note-17] [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Muslims); and [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Tibet). Tibetan Buddhism is a primary influence on the [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_art), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Tibet), and [festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_festivals) of the region. [Tibetan architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_architecture) reflects [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_architecture) and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_India) influences. [Staple foods in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_cuisine) are roasted barley, [yak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak) meat, and [butter tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_tea). With the growth of tourism in recent years, the service sector has become the largest sector in Tibet, accounting for 50.1% of the local GDP in 2020.[^cite_note-18] In 2025, around 70 million tourists visited Tibet.[^cite_note-19]&#x20;

Traditionally, the word *Tibet* referred only to central Tibet (roughly [Ü-Tsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9C-Tsang) geographically) in scholarly literature. The relatively recent usage of *Tibet* to include [Amdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo) and [Kham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kham) that historically were often not ruled or treated as part of the same [polity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity) as central Tibet has led to confusion and diplomatic difficulties.[^cite_note-\:1-20][^cite_note-\:2-21]&#x20;

## Name and languages



Location of the East Tibetian regions of Amdo & Kham

[^cite_note-22]

Main articles: [Etymology of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Tibet) and [Tibetic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetic_languages)

The [endonymic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym_and_exonym) term for Tibet is *Bod*(*Bö*) ([Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_script): བོད, IPA: [\[pʰøː˩˧˨\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tibetan)).[^cite_note-yangang-23] Some scholars believe it is derived from the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) *Bhauṭṭa*, IPA: [\[bʰɐu̯ʈːɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Sanskrit)[^cite_note-24] and referenced in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) in [*Geography* (Ptolemy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_\(Ptolemy\)) and *[Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea)*.[^cite_note-25] The first known English word *Tibet* dates back to 1827 CE[^cite_note-26] and may have been derived from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Mongolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolic_languages), or [Turkic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages).[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

Before the 1990s, *Tibet* in English scholarly literature almost always referred only to central Tibet (roughly [Ü-Tsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9C-Tsang) geographically). The currently promoted definition of *Tibet* with its broader inclusion of cultural or ethnographic Tibetan areas, also known as "Greater Tibet", often leads to historical misunderstandings and confusion, because [Amdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo) and [Kham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kham) were often not treated as part of the same [polity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity) as central Tibet even by the [government in Lhasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Phodrang). The push by [Tibetan exiles](54751.md) for a "Greater Tibet" has made negotiations with China more difficult for the [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama) because their demands cover areas with non-Tibetan populations and histories.[^cite_note-\:1-20][^cite_note-\:2-21]&#x20;



Ethnolinguistic map of Tibet (1967)



Tibetan family in [Kham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kham) attending a horse festival

Linguists generally classify Tibetic languages under the [Tibeto-Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages) branch of the [Sino-Tibetan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_languages) family.[^cite_note-28] They come in numerous dialects which are generally not mutually intelligible. Tibetic languages are spoken across the Tibetan plateau, [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), parts of [Nepal](171166.md), and northern India such as [Sikkim](140372.md). In general, dialects of central Tibet (including the [prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestige_\(sociolinguistics\)) [Lhasa variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa_Tibetan)), [Kham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khams_Tibetan), [Amdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo_Tibetan) and some smaller nearby areas are considered Tibetan dialects. Other forms, particularly [Dzongkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzongkha), [Sikkimese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikkimese_language), [Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherpa_language), and [Ladakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakhi_language), are considered by their speakers to be separate languages largely for political reasons. However, if the latter group is included in the calculation, then 'greater Tibetan' is spoken by approximately 6 million people[^cite_note-29] in the region and about 150,000 [speakers in exile](23417648.md).[^cite_note-30]&#x20;

Although spoken Tibetan varies according to the region, the written language based on [Classical Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Tibetan) is consistent throughout. This is probably due to the long-standing influence of the Tibetan empire, whose rule encompassed and at times extended beyond the present Tibetan linguistic area, from [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md) in the west to [Yunnan](166410.md) and [Sichuan](65185.md) in the east and from north of [Qinghai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai_Lake) to as far south as Bhutan. The [Tibetan script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_script), which is used for [Ladakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakhi_language) and [Dzongkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzongkha_language) as well, is derived from the ancient Indian [Brahmi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script).[^cite_note-31]&#x20;

Starting in 2001, the local [deaf sign languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf_sign_language) of Tibet were standardized, and [Tibetan Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Sign_Language) is now being promoted across the country.&#x20;

### Early history

Main articles: [Neolithic Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Tibet), [Zhangzhung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhung), and [Pre-Imperial Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Imperial_Tibet)



[Rishabhanatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishabhanatha), the first [Tirthankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankara) of [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism), is considered to have attained [nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana) near [Mount Kailash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kailash) in Tibet in Jain tradition.

[^cite_note-32]



King [Songtsen Gampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songts%C3%A4n_Gampo)

Humans inhabited the Tibetan Plateau at least 21,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Zhao-33] This population was largely replaced around 3,000 [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Present) by [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) immigrants from northern China, but there is a partial genetic continuity between the Paleolithic inhabitants and contemporary Tibetan populations.[^cite_note-Zhao-33]&#x20;

The earliest Tibetan historical texts identify the [Zhang Zhung culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Zhung_culture) as a people who migrated from the Amdo region into what is now the region of [Guge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guge) in western Tibet.[^cite_note-Norbu-34] Zhang Zhung is considered to be the original home of the [Bön](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6n) religion.[^cite_note-Hoffman-35] By the 1st century BCE, a neighboring kingdom arose in the [Yarlung valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_Valley), and the Yarlung king, [Drigum Tsenpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drigum_Tsenpo), attempted to remove the influence of the Zhang Zhung by expelling the Zhang's Bön priests from Yarlung.[^cite_note-Karmay-36] He was assassinated and Zhang Zhung continued its dominance of the region until it was annexed by Songtsen Gampo in the 7th century. Prior to [Songtsen Gampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songtsen_Gampo), the kings of Tibet were more mythological than factual, and there is insufficient evidence of their existence.[^cite_note-37]&#x20;

### Tibetan Empire

Main article: [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire)



Boundary of the Ancient Tibetan Empire in 820 CE from [The Historical Atlas of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Historical_Atlas_of_China)

[^cite_note-38]

[^cite_note-39]

In the 6th century, the king, [Namri Songtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namri_Songtsen), began to annex neighboring tribes by force. He was later murdered and his son [Songtsen Gampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songtsen_Gampo) succeeded him and quelled rebellions in various regions. Songtsen Gampo inherited his father's will and successively conquered and annexed other countries such as "Songbo" in the [Yushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yushu_City,_Qinghai) area of [Qinghai](173818.md), [Sumpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumpa) in the west, "[Kangguo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Queendom)" (called "Dongnwu Kingdom" in the [Old Book of Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Book_of_Tang)) in Qianduo (now [Chamdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamdo)), "Fuguo (Chinese\:附国)" in [Ganzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garz%C3%AA_County), "Fanlu (Chinese\:蕃绿)" in [Litang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litang_County), and [Tuyuhun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuyuhun) in Qinghai. Songtsen Gampo also led a large army to attack [Zhangzhung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhung) in 642. It took him three years to conquer Zhangzhung and sent [Khyungpo Pungse Sutse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyungpo_Pungse_Sutse) as the governor of Zhangzhung. Zhangzhung then became a vassal state of the Tibetan Empire.&#x20;

Songtsen Gampo also brought in many reforms, and Tibetan power spread rapidly, creating a large and powerful empire. It is traditionally considered that his first wife was the Princess of Nepal, [Bhrikuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhrikuti), and that she played a great role in the establishment of Buddhism in Tibet. In 640, he married [Princess Wencheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Wencheng), the niece of the Chinese emperor [Taizong of Tang China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong_of_Tang).[^cite_note-40]&#x20;

Under the next few Tibetan kings, Buddhism became established as the state religion and Tibetan power increased even further over large areas of [Central Asia](6742.md), while major inroads were made into Chinese territory, even reaching the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty)'s capital [Chang'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang%27an) (modern [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi%27an)) in late 763.[^cite_note-41] However, the Tibetan occupation of Chang'an only lasted for fifteen days, after which they were defeated by Tang and its ally, the Turkic [Uyghur Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_Khaganate).&#x20;



[Miran fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miran_fort)

The [Kingdom of Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nanzhao) (in [Yunnan](166410.md) and neighbouring regions) remained under Tibetan control from 750 to 794, when they turned on their Tibetan overlords and helped the Chinese inflict a serious defeat on the Tibetans.[^cite_note-42]&#x20;

In 747, the hold of Tibet was loosened by the campaign of general [Gao Xianzhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao_Xianzhi), who tried to re-open the direct communications between Central Asia and [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir). By 750, the Tibetans had lost almost all of their central Asian possessions to the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty). However, after Gao Xianzhi's defeat by the [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) and [Qarluqs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karluks) at the [Battle of Talas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Talas) (751) and the subsequent [civil war](7085.md) known as the [An Lushan Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Lushan_Rebellion) (755), Chinese influence decreased rapidly and Tibetan influence resumed.&#x20;

At its height in the 780s to 790s, the Tibetan Empire reached its highest glory when it ruled and controlled a territory stretching from modern-day Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Burma, China, India, Nepal, Pakistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan.&#x20;

In 821/822 CE, Tibet and China signed a peace treaty. A bilingual account of this treaty, including details of the borders between the two countries, is inscribed on a [stone pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang%E2%80%93Tibet_Treaty_Inscription) which stands outside the [Jokhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jokhang) temple in Lhasa.[^cite_note-43] Tibet continued as a Central Asian empire until the mid-9th century, when a civil war over succession led to the collapse of imperial Tibet. The period that followed is known traditionally as the *[Era of Fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_of_Fragmentation)*, when political control over Tibet became divided between regional warlords and tribes with no dominant centralized authority. An [Islamic invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Expedition_of_Islamic_Bengal) from Bengal took place in 1206.&#x20;

### Yuan dynasty

Main articles: [Mongol conquest of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_Tibet) and [Tibet under Yuan rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_under_Yuan_rule)

The Mongol [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), through the [Bureau of Buddhist and Tibetan Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Buddhist_and_Tibetan_Affairs), or Xuanzheng Yuan, ruled Tibet through a top-level administrative department. One of the department's purposes was to select a *[dpon-chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dpon-chen)* ("great administrator"), usually appointed by the lama and confirmed by the Mongol emperor in Beijing.[^cite_note-China's_Tibet_Policy-44] The [Sakya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya_\(tribe\)) lama retained a degree of autonomy, acting as the political authority of the region, while the *dpon-chen* held administrative and military power. Mongol rule of Tibet remained separate from the main provinces of China, but the region existed [under the administration of the Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_under_Yuan_rule). If the Sakya lama ever came into conflict with the *dpon-chen*, the *dpon-chen* had the authority to send Chinese troops into the region.[^cite_note-China's_Tibet_Policy-44]&#x20;

Tibet retained nominal power over religious and regional political affairs, while the Mongols managed a structural and administrative[^cite_note-45] rule over the region, reinforced by the rare military intervention. This existed as a "[diarchic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diarchy) structure" under the Yuan emperor, with power primarily in favor of the Mongols.[^cite_note-China's_Tibet_Policy-44] Mongolian prince [Khuden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuden) gained temporal power in Tibet in the 1240s and sponsored [Sakya Pandita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya_Pandita), whose seat became the capital of Tibet. [Drogön Chögyal Phagpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drog%C3%B6n_Ch%C3%B6gyal_Phagpa), Sakya Pandita's nephew became [Imperial Preceptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Preceptor) of [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan), founder of the Yuan dynasty.&#x20;

Yuan control over the region ended with the Ming overthrow of the Yuan and [Tai Situ Changchub Gyaltsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Situ_Changchub_Gyaltsen)'s revolt against the Mongols.[^cite_note-Rossabi194-46] Following the uprising, Tai Situ Changchub Gyaltsen founded the [Phagmodrupa dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagmodrupa_dynasty), and sought to reduce Yuan influences over Tibetan culture and politics.[^cite_note-47]&#x20;

### Phagmodrupa, Rinpungpa and Tsangpa dynasties

Main articles: [Phagmodrupa dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagmodrupa_dynasty), [Rinpungpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinpungpa), and [Tsangpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsangpa)

Further information: [Sino-Tibetan relations during the Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_relations_during_the_Ming_dynasty)



[Gyantse Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyantse_Fortress)

Between 1346 and 1354, Tai Situ Changchub Gyaltsen toppled the Sakya and founded the Phagmodrupa dynasty. The following 80 years saw the founding of the [Gelug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelug) school (also known as Yellow Hats) by the disciples of [Je Tsongkhapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je_Tsongkhapa), and the founding of the important [Ganden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Monastery), [Drepung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drepung_Monastery) and [Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sera_Monastery) monasteries near Lhasa. However, internal strife within the dynasty and the strong localism of the various fiefs and political-religious factions led to a long series of internal conflicts. The minister family [Rinpungpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinpungpa), based in [Tsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9C-Tsang) (West Central Tibet), dominated politics after 1435. In 1565 they were overthrown by the [Tsangpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsangpa) dynasty of [Shigatse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigatse) which expanded its power in different directions of Tibet in the following decades and favoured the [Karma Kagyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma_Kagyu) sect.&#x20;

### Rise of Ganden Phodrang and Buddhist Gelug school

Main article: [Ganden Phodrang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Phodrang)

In 1578, [Altan Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altan_Khan) of the [Tümed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCmed) Mongols gave [Sonam Gyatso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Dalai_Lama), a high lama of the Gelugpa school, the name *[Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama)*, *Dalai* being the Mongolian translation of the Tibetan name *Gyatso* "Ocean".[^cite_note-48]&#x20;

The [5th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Dalai_Lama) (1617–1682) is known for unifying the Tibetan heartland under the control of the [Gelug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelug) school of [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism), after defeating the rival [Kagyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagyu) and [Jonang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonang) sects and the secular ruler, the [Tsangpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsangpa) prince, in a prolonged civil war. His efforts were successful in part because of aid from [Güshi Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCshi_Khan), the [Oirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oirats) leader of the [Khoshut Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoshut_Khanate). With Güshi Khan as a largely uninvolved overlord, the 5th Dalai Lama and his intimates established a civil administration which is referred to by historians as the *Lhasa state*. This Tibetan regime or government is also referred to as the [Ganden Phodrang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Phodrang).&#x20;

### Qing dynasty

Main articles: [Chinese expedition to Tibet (1720)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_expedition_to_Tibet_\(1720\)) and [Tibet under Qing rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_under_Qing_rule)



[Potala Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potala_Palace)

[Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) rule in Tibet began with their [1720 expedition to the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_expedition_to_Tibet_\(1720\)) when they expelled the invading [Dzungars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar_Khanate). [Amdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo) came under Qing control in 1724, and eastern [Kham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kham) was incorporated into neighbouring Chinese provinces in 1728.[^cite_note-Wang_162-6-49] Meanwhile, the Qing government sent resident commissioners called *[Ambans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amban)* to Lhasa. In 1750, the Ambans and the majority of the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) and [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchus) living in Lhasa were killed in [a riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa_riot_of_1750), and Qing troops arrived quickly and suppressed the rebels in the next year. Like the preceding Yuan dynasty, the Manchus of the Qing dynasty exerted military and administrative control of the region, while granting it a degree of political autonomy. The Qing commander publicly executed a number of supporters of the rebels and, as in 1723 and 1728, made changes in the political structure and drew up a formal organization plan. The Qing now restored the Dalai Lama as ruler, leading the governing council called *[Kashag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashag)*,[^cite_note-50] but elevated the role of *Ambans* to include more direct involvement in Tibetan internal affairs. At the same time, the Qing took steps to counterbalance the power of the aristocracy by adding officials recruited from the clergy to key posts.[^cite_note-51]&#x20;

For several decades, peace reigned in Tibet, but in 1792, the Qing [Qianlong Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianlong_Emperor) sent [a large Chinese army into Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Nepalese_War) to push the invading [Nepalese](171166.md) out. This prompted yet another Qing reorganization of the Tibetan government, this time through a written plan called the "Twenty-Nine Regulations for Better Government in Tibet". Qing military garrisons staffed with Qing troops were now also established near the Nepalese border.[^cite_note-52] Tibet was dominated by the Manchus in various stages in the 18th century, and the years immediately following the 1792 regulations were the peak of the Qing imperial commissioners' authority; but there was no attempt to make Tibet a Chinese province.[^cite_note-53]&#x20;

In 1834, the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire) invaded and annexed [Ladakh](303611.md), a culturally Tibetan region that was an independent kingdom at the time. Seven years later, a Sikh army led by [General Zorawar Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Zorawar_Singh) invaded western Tibet from Ladakh, starting the [Sino-Sikh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Sikh_War). A Qing-Tibetan army repelled the invaders but was in turn defeated when it chased the Sikhs into Ladakh. The war ended with the signing of the [Treaty of Chushul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Chushul) between the Chinese and Sikh empires.[^cite_note-54]&#x20;



[Putuo Zongcheng Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putuo_Zongcheng_Temple), a Buddhist temple complex in [Chengde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengde), Hebei, built between 1767 and 1771. The temple was modeled after the [Potala Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potala_Palace).

As the Qing dynasty weakened, its authority over Tibet also gradually declined, and by the mid-19th century, its influence was minuscule. Qing authority over Tibet had become more symbolic than real by the late 19th century,[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] although in the 1860s, the Tibetans still chose for reasons of their own to emphasize the empire's symbolic authority and make it seem substantial.[^cite_note-59]&#x20;

In 1774, a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people) [nobleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerage_of_Scotland), [George Bogle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bogle_\(diplomat\)), travelled to [Shigatse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigatse) to investigate prospects of trade for the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company). His efforts, while largely unsuccessful, established permanent contact between Tibet and the [Western world](21208200.md).[^cite_note-60] However, in the 19th century, tensions between foreign powers and Tibet increased. The [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) was expanding its [territories in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) into the [Himalayas](37966.md), while the [Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Afghanistan) and the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) were both doing likewise in [Central Asia](6742.md).[^cite_note-61]&#x20;

In 1904, a [British expedition to Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_expedition_to_Tibet), spurred in part by a fear that [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) was extending its power into Tibet as part of [the Great Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Game), was launched. Although the expedition initially set out with the stated purpose of resolving border disputes between Tibet and [Sikkim](140372.md), it quickly turned into a military invasion. The British expeditionary force, consisting of [mostly Indian troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army), quickly invaded and captured Lhasa, with the [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Dalai_Lama) fleeing to the countryside.[^cite_note-smith154-6-62] Afterwards, the leader of the expedition, [Sir Francis Younghusband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Younghusband), negotiated the [Convention Between Great Britain and Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lhasa) with the Tibetans, which guaranteed the British great economic influence but ensured the region [remained under Chinese control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_under_Qing_rule). The Qing imperial resident, known as the [Amban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amban), publicly repudiated the treaty, while the British government, eager for friendly relations with China, negotiated a new treaty two years later known as the [Convention Between Great Britain and China Respecting Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_Between_Great_Britain_and_China_Respecting_Tibet). The British agreed not to annex or interfere in Tibet in return for an indemnity from the Chinese government, while China agreed not to permit any other foreign state to interfere with the territory or internal administration of Tibet.[^cite_note-smith154-6-62]&#x20;

In 1910, the Qing government sent [a military expedition of its own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_expedition_to_Tibet_\(1910\)) under [Zhao Erfeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Erfeng) to establish direct Manchu-Chinese rule and, in an imperial edict, deposed the Dalai Lama, who fled to British India. Zhao Erfeng defeated the Tibetan military conclusively and expelled the Dalai Lama's forces from the province. His actions were unpopular, and there was much animosity against him for his mistreatment of civilians and disregard for local culture.[^cite_note-63]&#x20;

### Post-Qing period

Main article: [Tibet (1912–1951)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_\(1912%E2%80%931951\))



Edmund Geer during the [1938–1939 German expedition to Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938%E2%80%931939_German_expedition_to_Tibet)



[Rogyapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogyapas), an [outcast group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_classes_of_Tibet), early 20th century. Their hereditary occupation included disposal of corpses and leather work.

After the [Xinhai Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution) (1911–1912) toppled the Qing dynasty and the last Qing troops were escorted out of Tibet, the new [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%9349\)) apologized for the actions of the Qing and offered to restore the Dalai Lama's title.[^cite_note-64] The Dalai Lama refused any Chinese title and declared himself ruler of an [independent Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_\(1912%E2%80%9351\)).[^cite_note-shakya5-65] In 1913, Tibet and [Outer Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogd_Khanate_of_Mongolia) concluded [a treaty of mutual recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_friendship_and_alliance_between_the_Government_of_Mongolia_and_Tibet).[^cite_note-66] The ROC continued to view the former Qing territory as its own, including Tibet.[^cite_note-\:Laikwan2-67]<sup>: 69 </sup> For the next 36 years, the 13th Dalai Lama and the [regents who succeeded him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_in_Tibet) governed Tibet. During this time, Tibet fought Chinese warlords for control of the ethnically Tibetan areas in [Xikang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xikang) and [Qinghai](173818.md) (parts of Kham and Amdo) along the upper reaches of the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River).[^cite_note-Wang_150-68] In 1914, the Tibetan government signed the [Simla Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simla_Convention) with Britain, which recognized Chinese suzerainty over Tibet in return for a border settlement. China refused to sign the convention.[^cite_note-69] Tibet continued to lack clear boundaries or international recognition of its status.[^cite_note-\:Laikwan2-67]<sup>: 69 </sup>&#x20;

When in the 1930s and 1940s the regents displayed negligence in affairs, the Kuomintang Government of the Republic of China took advantage of this to expand its reach into the territory.[^cite_note-70] On December 20, 1941, Kuomintang leader [Chiang Kai-Shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) noted in his diary that Tibet would be among the territories which he would demand as restitution for China following the conclusion of World War II.[^cite_note-71]&#x20;

### Since 1950

Main article: [History of Tibet (1950–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tibet_\(1950%E2%80%93present\))



A poster saying "Thank you India. 50 years in Exile." [Manali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manali,_Himachal_Pradesh), 2010.

Emerging with control over most of [mainland China](55079.md) after the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) [annexed Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Tibet_by_China) in 1950 and negotiated the [Seventeen Point Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeen_Point_Agreement) with the newly enthroned [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama)'s government, affirming the People's Republic of China's sovereignty but granting the area autonomy. Subsequently, on his journey into exile, the 14th Dalai Lama completely repudiated the agreement, which he has repeated on many occasions.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] According to the [CIA](5183633.md), the Chinese used the Dalai Lama to gain control of the military's training and actions.[^cite_note-74]&#x20;

The Dalai Lama had a strong following as many people from Tibet looked at him not just as their political leader, but as their spiritual leader.[^cite_note-75] After the Dalai Lama's government fled to [Dharamsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharamsala,_Himachal_Pradesh), India, during the [1959 Tibetan Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Tibetan_Rebellion), it established a [rival government-in-exile](54751.md). Afterwards, the [Central People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_People%27s_Government) in Beijing renounced the agreement and began implementation of the halted social and political reforms.[^cite_note-76] During the [Great Leap Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Leap_Forward), over 200,000 Tibetans may have died[^cite_note-77] and approximately 6,000 monasteries were destroyed during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution)—destroying the vast majority of historic Tibetan architecture.[^cite_note-Kevin-78]&#x20;

In 1980, General Secretary and reformist [Hu Yaobang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Yaobang) visited Tibet and ushered in a period of social, political, and [economic liberalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization).[^cite_note-Bank-79] At the end of the decade, however, before the [Tiananmen Square protests of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989), monks in the [Drepung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drepung_Monastery) and [Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sera_Monastery) monasteries started protesting for independence. The government halted reforms and started an anti-[separatist](286527.md) campaign.[^cite_note-Bank-79] Human rights organisations have been critical of the Beijing and Lhasa governments' approach to [human rights in the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Tibet) when cracking down on separatist convulsions that have occurred around monasteries and cities, most recently in the [2008 Tibetan unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Tibetan_unrest).&#x20;

The central region of Tibet is now an [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_divisions_of_China) within China, the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region). The Tibet Autonomous Region is a province-level entity of the People's Republic of China. It is governed by a People's Government, led by a chairman. In practice, however, the chairman is subordinate to the branch secretary of the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP). In 2010, it was reported that, as a matter of convention, the chairman had almost always been an ethnic Tibetan, while the party secretary had always been ethnically non-Tibetan.[^cite_note-80]&#x20;

### Mountains and rivers

Tibet has some of the world's tallest mountains, with several of them making the top ten list. [Mount Everest](42179.md), located on the border with [Nepal](171166.md), is, at 8,848.86 metres (29,032 ft), the [highest mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest_mountains) on earth. Several major rivers have their source in the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau) (mostly in present-day Qinghai Province). These include the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River), [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River), [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong), [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges), [Salween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salween_River) and the [Yarlung Tsangpo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_Tsangpo_River) ([Brahmaputra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River)).[^cite_note-84] The [Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_Tsangpo_Grand_Canyon), along the [Yarlung Tsangpo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_Tsangpo_River), is among the deepest and longest canyons in the world.&#x20;

Tibet has been called the "Water Tower" of Asia, and China is investing heavily in water projects in Tibet.[^cite_note-85]&#x20;

The Indus and Brahmaputra rivers originate from the vicinities of Lake [Mapam Yumco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapam_Yumco) in Western Tibet, near [Mount Kailash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kailash). The mountain is a holy pilgrimage site for both [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) and Tibetans. The Hindus consider the mountain to be the abode of [Lord Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Shiva). The Tibetan name for Mount Kailash is Khang Rinpoche. Tibet has numerous high-altitude lakes referred to in Tibetan as *tso* or *co*. These include [Qinghai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai_Lake), [Lake Manasarovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Manasarovar), [Namtso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namtso), [Pangong Tso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangong_Tso), [Yamdrok Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamdrok_Lake), [Siling Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siling_Co), [Lhamo La-tso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhamo_La-tso), [Lumajangdong Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumajangdong_Co), [Lake Puma Yumco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Puma_Yumco), [Lake Paiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Paiku), [Como Chamling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como_Chamling), [Lake Rakshastal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Rakshastal), [Dagze Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagze_Co) and [Dong Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Co). The Qinghai Lake (Koko Nor) is the largest lake in the People's Republic of China.&#x20;

### Climate

The climate is severely dry nine months of the year, and average annual snowfall is only 46 cm (18 inches), due to the [rain shadow effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow). Western passes receive small amounts of fresh snow each year but remain traversible all year round. Low temperatures are prevalent throughout these western regions, where bleak desolation is unrelieved by any vegetation bigger than a low bush, and where the wind sweeps unchecked across vast expanses of arid plain. The Indian [monsoon](57630.md) exerts some influence on eastern Tibet. Northern Tibet is subject to high temperatures in the summer and intense cold in the winter.&#x20;

### Wildlife

*[Sus scrofa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sus_scrofa)* expanded from its origin in southeast Asia into the Plateau, acquiring and [fixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixation_\(population_genetics\)) adaptive alleles for the high-altitude environment.[^cite_note-Frantz-et-al-2016-91] The forests of Tibet are home to [black bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_black_bear), [red pandas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_panda), [musk deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk_deer), [barking deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntjac), and [squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel). Monkeys such as [rhesus macaques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhesus_macaque) and [langurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colobinae) live in the warmer forest zones. [Tibetan antelopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_antelope), [gazelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa_\(antelope\)), and [kiangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiang) graze on the grasslands of the Tibetan plateau. [Wild yak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_yak) inhabit alpine tundra in the northern and western regions of Tibet.[^cite_note-92] There are more than 500 bird species in Tibet. Because of the high altitude and harsh climate, Tibet has few insects.[^cite_note-\:0-93]&#x20;

[Snow leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_leopard) are hunted for their fur and the eggs of [black-necked cranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-necked_crane) have been collected as a delicacy food.&#x20;

### Regions



[Basum Tso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basum_Tso) in [Gongbo'gyamda County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongbo%27gyamda_County), eastern Tibet

Cultural Tibet consists of several regions. These include Amdo (*A mdo*) in the northeast, which is administratively part of the provinces of Qinghai, Gansu, and Sichuan. Kham (*Khams*) in the southeast encompasses parts of western Sichuan, northern [Yunnan](166410.md), southern Qinghai, and the eastern part of the Tibet Autonomous Region. [Ü-Tsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9C-Tsang) (*dBus gTsang*) (Ü in the center, Tsang in the center-west, and Ngari (*mNga' ris*) in the far west) covered the central and western portion of Tibet Autonomous Region.[^cite_note-94]&#x20;

Tibetan cultural influences extend to the neighboring states of [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), Nepal, regions of India such as [Sikkim](140372.md), [Ladakh](303611.md), [Lahaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaul), and [Spiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiti), Northern Pakistan [Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltistan) or [Balti-yul](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Balti-yul\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in addition to designated Tibetan [autonomous areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_area) in adjacent Chinese provinces.&#x20;

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Tibet)

| --- | --- |



The Tibetan [yak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak) is an integral part of Tibetan life.

The Tibetan economy is dominated by [subsistence agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture). Due to limited arable land, the primary occupation of the Tibetan Plateau is raising livestock, such as [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_sheep), cattle, [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_goat), [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel), [yaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak), [dzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzo), and [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse).&#x20;

The main crops grown are [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), wheat, [buckwheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckwheat), [rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye), potatoes, and assorted fruits and vegetables. Tibet is ranked the lowest among China's 31 provinces[^cite_note-95] on the Human Development Index according to UN Development Programme data.[^cite_note-96] In recent years, due to increased interest in Tibetan Buddhism, tourism has become an increasingly important sector, and is actively promoted by the authorities.[^cite_note-97] Tourism brings in the most income from the sale of handicrafts. These include Tibetan hats, jewelry (silver and gold), wooden items, clothing, quilts, fabrics, [Tibetan rugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_rug) and carpets. The Central People's Government exempts Tibet from all taxation and provides 90% of Tibet's government expenditures.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] However, most of this investment goes to pay migrant workers who do not settle in Tibet and send much of their income home to other provinces.[^cite_note-102]&#x20;



Pastoral [nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) constitute about 40% of the ethnic [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) population.

[^cite_note-103]

Forty percent of the rural cash income in the Tibet Autonomous Region is derived from the harvesting of the fungus *[Ophiocordyceps sinensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophiocordyceps_sinensis)* (formerly *Cordyceps sinensis*); contributing at least 1.8 billion yuan, (US\$225 million) to the region's GDP.[^cite_note-104]&#x20;



[Tromzikhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tromzikhang) market in Lhasa

The [Qingzang railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzang_railway) linking the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) to [Qinghai Province](173818.md) was opened in 2006, but it was controversial.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]&#x20;

In January 2007, the Chinese government issued a report outlining the discovery of a large mineral deposit under the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau).[^cite_note-Tibet12345-108] The deposit has an estimated value of \$128 billion and may double Chinese reserves of zinc, copper, and lead. The Chinese government sees this as a way to alleviate the nation's dependence on foreign mineral imports for its growing economy. However, critics worry that mining these vast resources will harm Tibet's fragile ecosystem and undermine Tibetan culture.[^cite_note-Tibet12345-108]&#x20;

On January 15, 2009, China announced the construction of Tibet's first expressway, the [Lhasa Airport Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa_Airport_Expressway), a 37.9 km (23.5 mi) stretch of [controlled-access highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway) in southwestern Lhasa. The project will cost 1.55 billion [yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_yuan) (US\$227 million).[^cite_note-109]&#x20;

From January 18–20, 2010, a national conference on Tibet and areas inhabited by Tibetans in Sichuan, Yunnan, Gansu and Qinghai was held in China and a plan to improve development of the areas was announced. The conference was attended by General secretary [Hu Jintao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Jintao), [Wu Bangguo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Bangguo), [Wen Jiabao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wen_Jiabao), [Jia Qinglin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jia_Qinglin), [Li Changchun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Changchun), [Xi Jinping](2017814.md), [Li Keqiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Keqiang), [He Guoqiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Guoqiang) and [Zhou Yongkang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Yongkang), all members of [Politburo Standing Committee of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politburo_Standing_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party). The plan called for improvement of rural Tibetan income to national standards by 2020 and free education for all rural Tibetan children. China has invested 310 billion yuan (about 45.6 billion U.S. dollars) in Tibet since 2001.[^cite_note-110]&#x20;

### Development zone

The State Council approved Tibet [Lhasa Economic and Technological Development Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa_Economic_and_Technological_Development_Zone) as a state-level development zone in 2001. It is located in the western suburbs of Lhasa, the capital of the Tibet Autonomous Region. It is 50 kilometres (31 miles) away from the [Gonggar Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonggar_Airport), and 2 km (1.2 mi) away from Lhasa Railway Station and 2 km (1.2 mi) away from 318 national highway.&#x20;

The zone has a planned area of 5.46 km<sup>2</sup> (2.11 sq mi) and is divided into two zones. Zone A developed a land area of 2.51 km<sup>2</sup> (0.97 sq mi) for construction purposes. It is a flat zone, and has the natural conditions for good drainage.[^cite_note-111]&#x20;

## Demographics

See also: [History of Tibet (1950–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tibet_\(1950%E2%80%93present\)) and [Demographics of Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region#Demographics)

Historically, the population of Tibet consisted of primarily ethnic [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) and some other ethnic groups. According to tradition the original ancestors of the Tibetan people, as represented by the six red bands in the [Tibetan flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_flag), are: the Se, Mu, Dong, Tong, Dru and Ra. Other traditional ethnic groups with significant population or with the majority of the ethnic group residing in Tibet (excluding a [disputed area with India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_War)) include [Bai people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_people), [Blang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blang_people), [Bonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonans), [Dongxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongxiang_people), [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), [Hui people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Lhoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhoba), [Lisu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people), [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols), [Monguor (Tu people)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monguor_people), [Menba (Monpa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monpa_people), [Mosuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosuo), [Nakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhi), [Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_people), [Nu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_people), [Pumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumi_people), [Salar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_people), and [Yi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people).&#x20;

The proportion of the non-Tibetan population in Tibet is disputed. On the one hand, the Central Tibetan Administration of the Dalai Lama accuses China of actively swamping Tibet with [migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_settlements_in_Tibet) in order to alter Tibet's demographic makeup.[^cite_note-fifth-112] On the other hand, according to the [2010 Chinese census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Population_Census_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China) ethnic Tibetans comprise 90% of a total population of 3 million in the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region).[^cite_note-113]&#x20;

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Tibet)

#### Buddhism

Main article: [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism)

Religion is extremely important to the Tibetans and has a strong influence over all aspects of their lives. [Bön](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6n) is the indigenous religion of Tibet, but has been almost eclipsed by Tibetan Buddhism, a distinctive form of [Mahayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana) and [Vajrayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana), which was introduced into Tibet from the Sanskrit Buddhist tradition of northern India.[^cite_note-114] Tibetan Buddhism is practiced not only in Tibet but also in [Mongolia](19271.md), parts of northern India, the [Buryat Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_Republic), the [Tuva Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuva_Republic), and in the [Republic of Kalmykia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Kalmykia) and some other parts of China. During China's [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), nearly all Tibet's [monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Tibetan_monasteries) were ransacked and destroyed by the [Red Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Guards_\(China\)).[^cite_note-Tibetan_monks:_A_controlled_life-115][^cite_note-116] A few monasteries have begun to rebuild since the 1980s (with limited support from the Chinese government) and greater religious freedom has been granted – although it is still limited. Monks returned to monasteries across Tibet and monastic education resumed even though the number of monks imposed is strictly limited.[^cite_note-Tibetan_monks:_A_controlled_life-115][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] Before the 1950s, between 10 and 20% of males in Tibet were monks.[^cite_note-119]&#x20;

Tibetan Buddhism has five main traditions (the suffix *pa* is comparable to "er" in English):&#x20;

- **[Gelug(pa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelug)**, *Way of Virtue*, also known casually as *Yellow Hat*, whose spiritual head is the [Ganden Tripa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Tripa) and whose temporal head is the [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama). Successive Dalai Lamas ruled Tibet from the mid-17th to mid-20th centuries. This order was founded in the 14th to 15th centuries by [Je Tsongkhapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je_Tsongkhapa), based on the foundations of the [Kadampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadampa) tradition. Tsongkhapa was renowned for both his scholasticism and his virtue. The Dalai Lama belongs to the Gelugpa school, and is regarded as the embodiment of the Bodhisattva of Compassion.

  [^cite_note-120]

- **[Kagyu(pa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagyu)**, *Oral Lineage*. This contains one major subsect and one minor subsect. The first, the Dagpo Kagyu, encompasses those Kagyu schools that trace back to [Gampopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gampopa). In turn, the Dagpo Kagyu consists of four major sub-sects: the [Karma Kagyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma_Kagyu), headed by a [Karmapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmapa), the Tsalpa Kagyu, the Barom Kagyu, and Pagtru Kagyu. The once-obscure [Shangpa Kagyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangpa_Kagyu), which was famously represented by the 20th-century teacher [Kalu Rinpoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalu_Rinpoche), traces its history back to the Indian master Niguma, sister of Kagyu lineage holder [Naropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naropa). This is an oral tradition which is very much concerned with the experiential dimension of meditation. Its most famous exponent was Milarepa, an 11th-century mystic.

- **[Nyingma(pa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyingma)**, *The Ancient Ones*. This is the oldest, the original order founded by [Padmasambhava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmasambhava).

- **[Sakya(pa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya_\(Tibetan_Buddhist_school\))**, *Grey Earth*, headed by the [Sakya Trizin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya_Trizin), founded by Khon Konchog Gyalpo, a disciple of the great translator Drokmi Lotsawa. [Sakya Pandita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya_Pandita) 1182–1251 CE was the great-grandson of Khon Konchog Gyalpo. This school emphasizes scholarship.

- **[Jonang(pa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonang)** Its origins in Tibet can be traced to early 12th century master [Yumo Mikyo Dorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yumo_Mikyo_Dorje), but became much wider known with the help of [Dolpopa Sherab Gyaltsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolpopa_Sherab_Gyaltsen), a monk originally trained in the [Sakya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya_\(Tibetan_Buddhist_school\)) school. the Jonang school was widely thought to have become extinct in the late 17th century at the hands of the [5th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Dalai_Lama), who forcibly annexed the Jonang monasteries to his [Gelug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelug) school, declaring them heretical. Thus, [Tibetologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetology) were astonished when fieldwork turned up several active Jonangpa monasteries, including the main monastery, Tsangwa, located in Zamtang County, Sichuan. Almost 40 monasteries, comprising about 5000 monks, have subsequently been found, including some in the [Amdo Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo_Tibetan) and [rGyalgrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_people) areas of [Qinghai](173818.md), Sichuan and Tibet. One of the primary supporters of the Jonang lineage in exile has been the [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama) of the Gelugpa lineage. The Jonang tradition has recently officially registered with the [Tibetan Government](54751.md) in exile to be recognized as the fifth living Buddhist tradition of [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism). The 14th Dalai Lama assigned [Jebtsundamba Khutuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebtsundamba_Khutuktu) of Mongolia (who is considered to be an incarnation of Taranatha) as the leader of the Jonang tradition.

The Chinese government continued to pursue a strategy of forced assimilation and suppression of Tibetan Buddhism, as demonstrated by the laws designed to control the next reincarnation of the Dalai Lama and those of other Tibetan eminent lamas. Monks and nuns who refused to denounce the Dalai Lama have been expelled from their monasteries, imprisoned, and tortured.[^cite_note-121]&#x20;

It was reported in June 2021 that amidst the [2020–2022 China–India skirmishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932022_China%E2%80%93India_skirmishes), the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) had been forming a new unit for Tibetans who would be taken to Buddhist monks for religious blessings after completing their training.[^cite_note-122]&#x20;

#### Christianity

See also: [Catholic Church in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Tibet)

The first Christians documented to have reached Tibet were the [Nestorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestorian_Christians), of whom various remains and inscriptions have been found in Tibet. They were also present at the imperial camp of [Möngke Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ngke_Khan) at Shira Ordo, where they debated in 1256 with [Karma Pakshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma_Pakshi) (1204/6-83), head of the [Karma Kagyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma_Kagyu) order.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] Desideri, who reached Lhasa in 1716, encountered Armenian and Russian merchants.[^cite_note-125]&#x20;

Roman Catholic [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) and [Capuchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin) arrived from Europe in the 17th and 18th centuries. Portuguese missionaries Jesuit Father [António de Andrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Andrade) and Brother Manuel Marques first reached the kingdom of [Gelu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelu,_Nepal) in western Tibet in 1624 and were welcomed by the royal family, who allowed them to build a church later on.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] By 1627, there were about a hundred local converts in the Guge kingdom.[^cite_note-128] Later on, Christianity was introduced to [Rudok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudok), [Ladakh](303611.md) and Tsang and was welcomed by the ruler of the [Tsang kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9C-Tsang), where Andrade and his fellows established a Jesuit outpost at Shigatse in 1626.[^cite_note-129]&#x20;

In 1661 another Jesuit, [Johann Grueber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Grueber), crossed Tibet from [Sining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sining) to Lhasa (where he spent a month), before heading on to Nepal.[^cite_note-130] He was followed by others who actually built a church in Lhasa. These included the Jesuit Father [Ippolito Desideri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ippolito_Desideri), 1716–1721, who gained a deep knowledge of Tibetan culture, language and Buddhism, and various Capuchins in 1707–1711, 1716–1733 and 1741–1745,[^cite_note-Stein_1972,_p._85-131] Christianity was used by some Tibetan monarchs and their courts and the [Karmapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmapa) sect lamas to counterbalance the influence of the [Gelugpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelugpa) sect in the 17th century until in 1745 when all the missionaries were expelled at the lama's insistence.[^cite_note-pacificrim.usfca.edu-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]&#x20;

In 1877, the [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) [James Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=James_Cameron_\(China_Inland_Mission\)\&action=edit\&redlink=1) from the [China Inland Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Inland_Mission) walked from [Chongqing](171752.md) to [Batang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batang_County) in [Garzê Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garz%C3%AA_Tibetan_Autonomous_Prefecture), Sichuan province, and "brought the Gospel to the Tibetan people." Beginning in the 20th century, in [Dêqên Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%AAq%C3%AAn_Tibetan_Autonomous_Prefecture) in Yunnan, a large number of Lisu people and some Yi and Nu people converted to Christianity. Famous earlier missionaries include [James O. Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_O._Fraser), [Alfred James Broomhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_James_Broomhall) and [Isobel Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isobel_Kuhn) of the China Inland Mission, among others who were active in this area.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]&#x20;

[Proselytising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proselytising) has been illegal in China since 1949. But as of 2013, many Christian missionaries were reported to be active in Tibet with the tacit approval of Chinese authorities, who view the missionaries as a counterforce to Tibetan Buddhism or as a boon to the local economy.[^cite_note-140]&#x20;

#### Islam

Main article: [Islam in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Tibet)



The [Lhasa Great Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa_Great_Mosque)

Muslims have been living in Tibet since as early as the 8th or 9th century. In Tibetan cities, there are small communities of [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Muslim), known as Kachee (Kache), who trace their origin to immigrants from three main regions: [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) (Kachee Yul in ancient Tibetan), Ladakh and the Central Asian Turkic countries. Islamic influence in Tibet also came from Persia. A Muslim Sufi [Syed Ali Hamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hamadani) preached to the people of [Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltistan), then known as little Tibet. After 1959, a group of Tibetan Muslims made a case for Indian nationality based on their historic roots to Kashmir and the Indian government declared all Tibetan Muslims Indian citizens later on that year.[^cite_note-141] Other Muslim ethnic groups who have long inhabited Tibet include [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Salar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_people), [Dongxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongxiang_people) and [Bonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonans). There is also a well established Chinese Muslim community (gya kachee), which traces its ancestry back to the [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) ethnic group of China.&#x20;

### Tibetan art

Main article: [Tibetan art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_art)

Tibetan representations of art are intrinsically bound with [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) and commonly depict [deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deities) or variations of [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) in various forms from bronze Buddhist statues and shrines, to highly colorful [thangka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thangka) paintings and [mandalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandala). Thangkas are Tibet's traditional cloth paintings. Rendered on cotton cloth with a thin rod at the top, they portray Buddhist deities or themes in color and detail.[^cite_note-\:0-93]&#x20;

### Architecture

Main article: [Tibetan culture § Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_culture#Architecture)

Tibetan architecture contains Chinese and Indian influences, and reflects a deeply [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) approach. The [Buddhist wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmacakra), along with two dragons, can be seen on nearly every [Gompa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gompa) in Tibet. The design of the Tibetan [Chörtens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%B6rten) can vary, from roundish walls in [Kham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kham) to squarish, four-sided walls in [Ladakh](303611.md).&#x20;

The most distinctive feature of Tibetan architecture is that many of the houses and monasteries are built on elevated, sunny sites facing the south, and are often made out of a mixture of rocks, wood, cement and earth. Little fuel is available for heat or lighting, so flat roofs are built to conserve heat, and multiple windows are constructed to let in sunlight. Walls are usually sloped inwards at 10 degrees as a precaution against the frequent earthquakes in this mountainous area.&#x20;

Standing at 117 metres (384 feet) in height and 360 metres (1,180 feet) in width, the [Potala Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potala_Palace) is the most important example of Tibetan architecture. Formerly the residence of the [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama), it contains over one thousand rooms within thirteen stories, and houses portraits of the past Dalai Lamas and statues of the Buddha. It is divided between the outer White Palace, which serves as the administrative quarters, and the inner Red Quarters, which houses the assembly hall of the Lamas, chapels, 10,000 shrines, and a vast library of Buddhist scriptures. The Potala Palace is a [World Heritage Site](44940.md), as is [Norbulingka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbulingka), the former summer residence of the Dalai Lama.&#x20;

### Music

Main article: [Music of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Tibet)

The music of Tibet reflects the cultural heritage of the trans-Himalayan region, centered in Tibet but also known wherever ethnic [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) groups are found in India, [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), Nepal and further abroad. First and foremost Tibetan music is [religious music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_music), reflecting the profound influence of [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) on the culture.&#x20;

Tibetan music often involves [chanting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanting) in [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Tibetan) or [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), as an integral part of the religion. These chants are complex, often recitations of [sacred texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_text) or in celebration of various festivals. [Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang) chanting, performed without metrical timing, is accompanied by resonant drums and low, sustained syllables. Other styles include those unique to the various schools of Tibetan Buddhism, such as the classical music of the popular [Gelugpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelugpa) school, and the romantic music of the [Nyingmapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyingmapa), [Sakyapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya_\(Tibetan_Buddhist_school\)) and [Kagyupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagyupa) schools.[^cite_note-142]&#x20;

[Nangma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangma) dance music is especially popular in the [karaoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaoke) bars of the urban center of Tibet, [Lhasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa_\(prefecture-level_city\)). Another form of popular music is the classical [gar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gar_\(music\)) style, which is performed at rituals and ceremonies. Lu are a type of songs that feature glottal vibrations and high pitches. There are also epic bards who sing of [Gesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesar), who is a hero to ethnic Tibetans.&#x20;

### Festivals

Main article: [Tibetan festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_festivals)



The [Monlam Prayer Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monlam_Prayer_Festival)

Tibet has various festivals, many for worshipping the Buddha,[^cite_note-143] that take place throughout the year. [Losar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losar) is the Tibetan New Year Festival. Preparations for the festive event are manifested by special offerings to family shrine deities, painted doors with religious symbols, and other painstaking jobs done to prepare for the event. Tibetans eat *[Guthuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guthuk)* (barley noodle soup with filling) on New Year's Eve with their families. The [Monlam Prayer Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monlam_Prayer_Festival) follows it in the first month of the [Tibetan calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_calendar), falling between the fourth and the eleventh days of the first Tibetan month. It involves dancing and participating in sports events, as well as sharing picnics. The event was established in 1049 by Tsong Khapa, the founder of the Dalai Lama and the Panchen Lama's order.&#x20;

### Cuisine

Main article: [Tibetan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_cuisine)

See also: [List of Tibetan dishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Tibetan_dishes)



[Thukpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thukpa) with Momo – Tibetan Style

The most important crop in Tibet is [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), and dough made from barley flour—called [tsampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsampa)—is the [staple food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staple_food) of Tibet. This is either rolled into noodles or made into steamed dumplings called [momos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momo_\(food\)). Meat dishes are likely to be [yak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak), goat, or [mutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_\(food\)), often dried, or cooked into a spicy [stew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew) with potatoes. [Mustard seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_seed) is cultivated in Tibet, and therefore features heavily in its cuisine. Yak [yogurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt), butter and cheese are frequently eaten, and well-prepared yogurt is considered something of a prestige item. [Butter tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_tea) is a very popular drink.&#x20;
