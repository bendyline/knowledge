**Newtownards** ([/ˌnjuːtənˈɑːrdz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) referred to simply as **Ards**, is a town in [County Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Down), Northern Ireland. It lies at the most northern tip of [Strangford Lough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangford_Lough), 10 miles (16 km) east of [Belfast](5046.md), on the [Ards Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ards_Peninsula). It is in the [civil parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_parishes_in_Ireland) of [Newtownards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtownards_\(civil_parish\)) and the historic [baronies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barony_\(Ireland\)) of [Ards Lower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ards_Lower) and [Castlereagh Lower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlereagh_Lower).[^cite_note-4] Newtownards is in the [Ards and North Down Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ards_and_North_Down_Borough_Council). The population was 29,677 in the [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Kingdom_census).[^cite_note-2021_pop-5]

## History

### Irish settlement

In 540 AD, [St. Finian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnian_of_Moville) founded [Movilla Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movilla_Abbey), a [monastery](45856.md), on a hill overlooking [Strangford Lough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangford_Lough) about a mile northeast of present-day Newtownards town centre. "Movilla" (*Magh Bhile*) means "the plain of the sacred tree" in [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language), which suggests that the land had previously been a sacred [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_paganism) site.[^cite_note-6] It became a significant Christian settlement – a centre for worship, study, mission and commercial trade, well known throughout Ireland. It was sacked by the [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) sometime after AD 824, though survived for a thousand years as a monastic settlement (becoming part of the [Augustinian](144980.md) Order in 1135), until the [dissolution of the monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries) in 1542.[^cite_note-7]

The [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans_in_Ireland) conquered east Ulster in the 1170s, founding the [Earldom of Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earldom_of_Ulster). Around 1226, they established a new town around Movilla, which became known as the "New Town of Blathewic", after the Irish territory of [Uí Blathmhaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blathewyc).[^cite_note-8] A [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) priory was built in 1244 by [Walter de Burgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_de_Burgh,_1st_Earl_of_Ulster)[^cite_note-9] and was also dissolved in 1542.[^cite_note-Thompson09-10]

In 1572, both monasteries were burned by the [Clannaboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clannaboy) [O'Neills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Neill_dynasty#O'Neills_of_Clanaboy) under Sir [Brian McPhelim O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_McPhelim_O'Neill) to deny buildings to the English, who were attempting to [colonize the Ards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_of_Ireland#East_Ulster).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-Thompson09-10] After this the urban settlement at Movilla disappeared and the area around it became known as "Ballylisnevin" ("the town of Nevin's fort").[^cite_note-12]

### The Scottish town



[Market House, Newtownards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_House,_Newtownards)

In 1605 (prior to the official [Plantation of Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_of_Ulster) in 1610), [Hugh Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Montgomery,_1st_Viscount_of_the_Great_Ardes) was granted the lands and set about rebuilding what was by then known as *Newtown*, later expanded to *Newtownards*. Official records show the town was established in 1606. Montgomery built a residence in the ruins of the old priory, the tower of which remains. Scottish Protestant settlers, particularly from [Ayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayr), and to a lesser extent [Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_North_Ayrshire), in [Ayrshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrshire), arrived in large numbers and the town grew quickly.[^cite_note-13]

Due to the shallow mud of Strangford Lough, Newtown never developed as a port, with goods instead transported from the nearby town of [Donaghadee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaghadee) on the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea) coast of the Ards Peninsula. Instead, it became a market town, with the [Market House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_House,_Newtownards) in Conway Square constructed in 1771.[^cite_note-14]

### United Irishmen rebellion

North Down and the Ards were briefly held by [United Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_United_Irishmen) insurgents in the [Irish Rebellion of 1798](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rebellion_of_1798). On the morning of 9 June, "Pike Sunday", United Irishmen, mainly from [Bangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down), [Donaghadee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaghadee), [Greyabbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyabbey) and [Ballywalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballywalter), under the command of the Presbyterian licentiate (later American diplomat) [David Bailie Warden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bailie_Warden), marched on the town. They were driven off with [musket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musket) fire from the Market House, but the garrison, consisting of troops from the [York Fencible Regiment of Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fencibles#Irish_Rebellion_of_1798) subsequently withdrew, allowing the rebels to establish a French revolutionary-style [Committee of Public Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Public_Safety). The "Republic" in Newtownards did not survive the rout two days later of [the main rebel force at Ballynahinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ballynahinch).[^cite_note-15]

### The Great Famine

During the [Great Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_\(Ireland\)), which resulted from the dependence of small tenants and cottiers on a blighted potato crop, the largest local landowner, [Lord Londonderry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Vane,_3rd_Marquess_of_Londonderry), rejected the call for rent reductions on grounds of "personal inconvenience". By 1847 the 800 inhabitants of the town were witness to "emaciated and half-famished souls" queuing at soup kitchens and overflowing the newly built [workhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workhouse). Despite Lord Londonderry's objection, several public works programs for famine relief were initiated beginning with the upgrading of the road to Donaghadee. In general, with tenant holdings not as acutely subdivided as in western districts of Ireland, and with the availability of weaving and other employments, the town was saved from the worst.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-McCavery-17]

### Victorian growth

The early 19th century saw the reclamation of the marshlands south of the town. At the same time, its growth was accelerated by integration into the Belfast and Lagan Valley industrial region and market. The [Belfast and County Down Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast_and_County_Down_Railway) connected Newtownards to [Belfast](5046.md), via [Comber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comber) and [Dundonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundonald,_County_Down), in 1850 (closed in 1950), and to Donaghadee in 1861. By the same year, the town's population had risen to 9,500. On 12 July 1867, despite the [Party Processions Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Processions_Act), the [Orange Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Institution) paraded from Bangor to Newtownards. The parade, in which upwards of 30,000 took part, led to the short-term imprisonment of its principal organiser [William Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Johnston_\(Irish_politician\)), and to his subsequent election as an [MP](3776360.md) for Belfast.[^cite_note-18]

As the nineteenth century progressed the economy became increasingly tied to the growth of Belfast. From mid-20th century it increasingly became a [commuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter) town. Newtownards' population reached 13,100 in 1961 and had doubled to 28,000 by 2011.[^cite_note-19]

### The Troubles

During [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles), Newtownards was the scene of a [car bomb](155282.md) attack on 5 July 1993, when Roma's Bar in Regent Street was targeted. The pub was destroyed, but has since been rebuilt. The attack was carried out by the [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army) (PIRA) with a 700 kg (1,500 lb) device.[^cite_note-20] There were no fatalities. Police said the 10-minute warning, telephoned to a local radio station, was "totally inadequate." The warning said the bomb contained 1,500 pounds of explosives.[^cite_note-21]

### Recent times

On 1 November 2021, a bus in the town was hijacked and set on fire by two masked assailants allegedly protesting the [Northern Ireland Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Protocol).[^cite_note-22]

## Places of interest



[Scrabo Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrabo_Tower) (with Newtownards in the background)

### Scrabo Tower

Main article: [Scrabo Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrabo_Tower)

The town of Newtownards is overlooked by the 100-foot (30 m) high [Scrabo Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrabo_Tower). The tower is 41 metres high, and was erected on Scrabo Hill as a memorial to [Charles Stewart, 3rd Marquess of Londonderry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stewart,_3rd_Marquess_of_Londonderry) in 1857. Those loyal to the Stewart family suggested the inspiration lay in the gratitude of his tenantry for his solicitude during the famine. Given the popular criticism the Marquess in those years, this seems doubtful. In 1847 he and his wife made contributions of £20 and £10 to their local relief committees. The following year they expended £15,000 renovating their home in [Mount Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Stewart).[^cite_note-23] Only 450 subscribers were connected to the estate on which there were 1,200 tenants farmers and many associated employees. Two-thirds of the cost was met by 98 subscribers (on a list headed by Emperor [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) of France), most of whom were fellow gentry.[^cite_note-24]

The Scottish baronial-style tower is open to the public and houses a historical and local environment exhibition. The basalt-topped sandstone hill at Scrabo is one of the dominant features of north [Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Down). The tower now stands in Scrabo Country Park with its woodland walks and parkland through Killynether Wood.[^cite_note-25]

### Movilla Abbey

The ancient ruins of [Movilla Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movilla_Abbey), monastic settlement are situated within the grounds of Movilla Cemetery. Nothing visible remains today of [Finnian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnian_of_Movilla)'s original Celtic Abbey, but the 15th Century Augustinian ruins still stand, and are worth seeing. They are a part of the St Patrick's Trail Tourist Route.[^cite_note-26]

### Somme Heritage Centre

The [Somme Heritage Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somme_Heritage_Centre), which is situated a little north of the town, is the Somme Association's flagship project. Situated adjacent to the [Clandeboye Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clandeboye_Estate) outside Newtownards, the centre is a unique visitor attraction of international significance showing the reality of the [Great War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) and its effects on the community at home. The centre commemorates the involvement of the [36th (Ulster)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36th_\(Ulster\)_Division) and [16th (Irish)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_\(Irish\)_Division) divisions in the [Battle of the Somme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme), the [10th (Irish) Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_\(Irish\)_Division) in [Gallipoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli), [Salonika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salonika) and [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), and provides displays and information on the entire Irish contribution to the First World War.[^cite_note-27]

### Mount Stewart

On the east shore of Strangford Lough, a few miles outside Newtownards and near [Greyabbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyabbey), stands [Mount Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Stewart), an 18th-century house and garden – the home of the [Londonderry family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquess_of_Londonderry). The house and its contents reflect the history of the Londonderrys who played a leading role in British social and political life. The ninety-eight acre garden at [Mount Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Stewart) has been proposed as a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-28]

## Sport

Rugby

[Ards Rugby Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ards_RFC) plays at Lansdowne Road, south of the town along the main Comber road.[^cite_note-38]

Cricket

Ards and Donaghadee Cricket Club currently plays its home games at Londonderry Park, which is on Portaferry Road.[^cite_note-39]

Football

There are two local football teams: [Ards F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ards_F.C.), who play in the [NIFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Football_League)'s Danske Bank Premiership, and [Ards Rangers F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ards_Rangers_F.C.), who play in the [Northern Amateur Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Amateur_Football_League).[^cite_note-40]

Ards motor racing Circuit

Main article: [Ards Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ards_Circuit)

The Ards Circuit through Newtownards was a motorsport [street circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_circuit) used for [RAC Tourist Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAC_Tourist_Trophy) sports car races from 1928 until 1936. At the time it was Northern Ireland's premier sporting event, regularly attracting crowds in excess of a quarter of a million people.[^cite_note-41]

On 5 September 1936, in appallingly wet conditions, local driver Jack Chambers lost control of his [Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riley_\(motor-car\)) approaching the Strangford Arms in Newtownards at the Newtownards rail bridge and crashed into the crowd, killing eight spectators. This tragedy brought an end to nine years of racing over the Ards street circuit.[^cite_note-BBC_Article-42]

## Loyal orders and parades

### Apprentice Boys



Newtownards Orange Hall

[Apprentice Boys of Derry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apprentice_Boys_of_Derry) (Newtownards Branch Club) is the main branch club in the town, originally inaugurated in December 1905. The other branch, the No Surrender Club (Newtownards Branch) was formed in May 1948.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] They parades locally before traveling to the main commemorations in [Derry](9055.md), as well as the [Easter Monday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Monday) parade.[^cite_note-45]

### Orange Order

Newtownards makes of 2 of the 4 districts within the North Down Combine. Newtownards District LOL No. 4, established in 1831, oversee 12 private Orange Lodges in the town. Upper Ards District L.O.L. 11, formed in 1861, oversee 15 private Orange Lodges in around the Ards Peninsula, including [Donaghadee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaghadee), [Carrowdore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrowdore), [Ballywalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballywalter) and [Portavogie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portavogie).[^cite_note-46] The first Orange Lodge in Newtownards was established in 1798 [^cite_note-47]

Orange Lodges in Newtownards include [King George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI) Memorial LOL 1973, Ex-Servicemen’s LOL 1952, Loughries True Blues LOL 1948, Scarlet Crown LOL 1919, Mill Street Heroes LOL 1908, Reformation Truth Defenders LOL 1521, Greenwell Street LOL 1363, Boyne LOL 1054, Temple of Loyalty LOL 481, Rising Sons of William LOL 240, Apprentice Boys LOL 128 and Ulster’s Chosen Few LOL 111.[^cite_note-48]

There are two women's lodges, Sister S. Baird Memorial WLOL 164 and Ulster’s Pride WLOL 110.[^cite_note-49]

There are two junior lodges, Star of Ards JOWAI 47 for girls, and Newtownards District JLOL 131 for boys, reformed in 2025.[^cite_note-50]

### Royal Black Institution

Newtownards Royal Black District Chapter No. 11, instituted in 1911 is a local branch of the [Royal Black Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Black_Institution). They oversee 13 preceptories across North Down and the Ards Peninsula.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

Ards Chosen Few RBP 290, established in 1899, is the town's preceptory under Chapter No. 11.[^cite_note-53]

### Parades

The [Twelfth of July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Twelfth) is held in Newtownards (District No. 4) every 4 years, rotation with other districts under the North Down Combine.[^cite_note-54] Newtownards Protestant Boys hold an annual band competition and parade the Saturday after The Twelfth. Over 40 marching bands typically participate in the parade.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

The Royal Black Institution's "Last Saturday in August" parade is held in Newtownards in rotation with other Chapters.[^cite_note-57]

The [Battle of the Somme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme) Remembrance Parade takes place annually on the 1st of July.[^cite_note-58]

#### Marching bands

[Marching bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_band) from Newtownards include Newtownards Protestant Boys, Newtownards Sons Of Ulster Auld Boys [Flute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flute) Band and Newtownards Melody Flute Band, North Down First Flute Band, William Strain & William Lightbody Memorial Flute Band and Newtownards Ex-Servicemen Flute Band.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] They participate in [Orange walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_walk) and the Royal Black Institution parades.[^cite_note-62]
