A series of demonstrations took place in [Jantar Mantar, New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_New_Delhi), India, between 6 June and 25 July 2026. They were initiated by the [Cockroach Janta Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockroach_Janta_Party) (CJP), an Indian youth-based [satirical political movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_satire), along with left-wing student organisations[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] including the [All India Students Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Students_Federation), [All India Students Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Students_Association), and [Students' Federation of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students'_Federation_of_India).[^cite_note-bbc2-19][^cite_note-ap-20] Held primarily at [Jantar Mantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_New_Delhi) in [New Delhi](51585.md), the protesters have demanded the resignation of Union Education Minister [Dharmendra Pradhan](7119332.md) over irregularities in several of that year's national examinations — the [2026 NEET paper leak](83200188.md) and the [CBSE On-Screen Marking irregularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_CBSE_On-Screen_Marking_controversy) — along with broader reforms in the conduct of public examinations.[^cite_note-etv-21][^cite_note-22] The CJP, along with the [Congress party](149333.md), also sought compensation for the families of students who died by suicide due to the examination turmoil.[^cite_note-AhmedReuters-23] The agitation drew national attention after activist [Sonam Wangchuk](52221506.md) began an indefinite [hunger strike](222292.md) at the protest site on 28 June.[^cite_note-bbc3-24]

The CJP had been created in May 2026 by digital communications strategist Abhijeet Dipke as a political satire, alluding to a statement by [Surya Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surya_Kant), the [Chief Justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India), who compared unemployed youth and social media activists to "cockroaches" and "parasites".[^cite_note-bbc-25] Having gained widespread online popularity, the movement transitioned into offline demonstrations, which were initially permitted by the [Delhi Police](8808263.md).

On 28 June 2026, [Ladakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakhis) environmentalist and education activist [Sonam Wangchuk](52221506.md) joined the demonstration at [Jantar Mantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar). He started an indefinite [hunger strike](222292.md) and demanded the resignation of [Dharmendra Pradhan](7119332.md).[^cite_note-TP1-14]

In mid-July 2026, following a leadership change in the Delhi Police, police removed barricades at the protest site and transferred Wangchuk to a hospital, a move organisers claimed was done against his will.[^cite_note-26] This action, along with an incident where a woman who chanted [Hindutva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindutva) slogans and threw ink at Dipke,[^cite_note-27] drew increased media attention to the agitation.[^cite_note-Sonam_Wangchuk_to_Express-28][^cite_note-Atmosphere_of_Intimidation-29]

On 20 July, thousands of protesters, comprising primarily students and [Gen Z](4015968.md) congregants, gathered near Jantar Mantar, defying orders from the Delhi Police to disperse, and began to [march toward Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansad_Chalo_march).[^cite_note-AJ-30] Clashes ensued, with police using baton charges and tear gas against protesters in an attempt to prevent them from reaching the Parliament, where the monsoon session was underway.[^cite_note-31] International media outlets, including [France 24](1263244.md) and [CNN](62028.md), described the protests as the largest demonstrations against the country's government in several years, while the Delhi Police stated their actions were necessary to maintain public order.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-BBCKP-34] Following the police response, CJP leaders announced the movement would not organise further street marches in the capital, though the sit-in at Jantar Mantar and the campaign for Pradhan's resignation would continue.[^cite_note-ajhalt-35]

Following the crackdown, the Delhi Police dismantled the protesters' tents at the designated site, though demonstrators quickly began rebuilding their camps amidst rain and rubble.[^cite_note-36]

On 23 July 2026, Wangchuk ended his hunger strike after the government assured him that no legal action would be taken against protesters rallying at Jantar Mantar site, a discussion would be held on preventing paper leaks in future and compensation would be given to students who committed suicide.[^cite_note-Lukiv-37][^cite_note-38] Pradhan resigned on 25 July 2026.[^cite_note-resign-4] The protests were called off later in the day after the government agreed to the CJP's demands.[^cite_note-Hindu25Jul-39]

## Background

### Formation of the Cockroach Janta Party



Logo of the Cockroach Janta Party

In May 2026, [Surya Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surya_Kant), the [Chief Justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India), drew criticism for remarks made during a [Supreme Court](395852.md) hearing in which he compared the unemployed youth to "cockroaches" and "parasites" who attack institutions through social media activism and litigation.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-\:1-41]

> There are already parasites of society who attack the system, and you want to join hands with them? There are youngsters like cockroaches; they don't get any employment, and they don't have any place in a profession. Some of them become media, some of them become social media, some of them become [RTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Information_Act,_2005) activists, some of them become other activists, and they start attacking everyone \[...] And you people file contempt petitions.[^cite_note-ANtnie-42]

The remarks drew widespread public criticism. On 16 May 2026, digital communications strategist Abhijeet Dipke founded the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP; Cockroach People's Party). Its name is a direct reference to Kant's remarks.[^cite_note-43] The movement rapidly gained a large social media following.[^cite_note-bbc-25] Kant later clarified that his criticism was directed at individuals who had entered the legal, media, and activist professions using fraudulent academic credentials, and that he respected the youth.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### Scandals

Main articles: [2026 NEET controversy](83200188.md) and [2026 CBSE On-Screen Marking controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_CBSE_On-Screen_Marking_controversy)

The protests followed two examination-related scandals in 2026: a [paper leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_leak_in_India) affecting the [NEET-UG](33246416.md) medical entrance examination and irregularities in the [Central Board of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education)'s on-screen marking and revaluation process, which left thousands of students awaiting clarity on their final results.[^cite_note-printlive-47]

## Protests

### First protest at Jantar Mantar

The CJP held its first street protest at [Jantar Mantar, New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_New_Delhi) on 6 June 2026, to demand the resignation of [Dharmendra Pradhan](7119332.md), the [union minister of education](49127628.md);[^cite_note-AJZ-52] it was the first major demonstration at Jantar Mantar since the [2023 Indian wrestlers' protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Indian_wrestlers'_protest).[^cite_note-WR-9] The [Delhi Police](8808263.md) granted permission for the protest until 5 P.M. in what they described as a one-time exception.[^cite_note-printlive-47] The police deployed around 5,000 personnel in the [New Delhi district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi_district) and strengthened their presence at several locations, including [Indira Gandhi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_International_Airport), ahead of the protest.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] Organisers asked participants to protest peacefully and encouraged them to bring the [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_India) and a book, which they said symbolised the [right to education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_education) and equal opportunity.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-JZ2-118]

Dipke, who arrived in New Delhi from the [United States](3434750.md) shortly before the protest, was joined at the site by Indian engineer, activist, and education reformer [Sonam Wangchuk](52221506.md).[^cite_note-printlive-47] Hundreds of protesters gathered at the site,[^cite_note-JZ2-118][^cite_note-MPI-119] including students, parents, doctors, lawyers, and teachers, among others,[^cite_note-WR-9] with some holding placards and wearing cockroach masks.[^cite_note-JZ2-118][^cite_note-MPI-119] The [Communist Party of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_India) affiliated [All India Youth Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Youth_Federation) (AIYF) and [All India Students' Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Students'_Federation) (AISF) alleged that people affiliated with the [Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtriya_Swayamsevak_Sangh) (RSS), a [Hindutva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindutva) paramilitary organisation, attempted to disrupt the protest by harassing and assaulting some protesters. [^cite_note-WR-9] The CJP announced that it would launch a nationwide agitation if Pradhan did not resign within seven days.[^cite_note-NIE-120]

### National campaign

On 11 June 2026, the CJP launched a nationwide campaign seeking reforms in the education system, beginning at [Savitribai Phule Pune University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitribai_Phule_Pune_University) in [Pune](164634.md). Dipke released what he called an exam manifesto containing five demands, including compensation of [₹](490783.md)10,000 for students affected by a paper leak, postponement of an examination, or delay in the declaration of results. He said the movement would travel to cities including [Jaipur](166537.md), [Lucknow](392095.md), [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar), and [Bengaluru](44275267.md) before returning to Jantar Mantar on 20 June 2026.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-ap-20]

### Second protest at Jantar Mantar

The CJP held its second protest at Jantar Mantar on 20 June 2026. Hundreds of protesters, mostly students, gathered, holding placards, singing the [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jana_Gana_Mana), and banging metal plates with spoons as a symbol of protest;[^cite_note-DRD-122][^cite_note-THT-123] many had travelled from across the nation to take part.[^cite_note-DRD-122] Ahead of the protest, Dipke alleged on social media that the authorities may allow "goons" to disrupt the protest and blame the CJP, posting photos of lorries parked at Jantar Mantar which he claimed were loaded with stones.[^cite_note-\:62-124]

According to the Delhi Police, the protest, which began at 1 P.M., had permission only until 5 P.M. Dipke refused to end the demonstration, stating that he would not leave Jantar Mantar until Pradhan resigned, and urged supporters to join the protest that evening.[^cite_note-TT-125][^cite_note-YSHR-126] The Delhi police employed several pressure tactics to persuade the protesters to leave the barricaded site, including switching off the lights and briefly cutting off access to food and drinking water.[^cite_note-DRD-122][^cite_note-YSHR-126] One of the volunteers, Mohammad Junaid, who served food and water to the protestors and was thanked by Dipke himself, alleged in his petition to the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) that he was picked up from Delhi by police officials, kept in a vehicle for about six hours, threatened, intimidated and was subsequently dropped off at a far-off location in [Dehradun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehradun).[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-HT-28072026-129] He also alleged his family being harassed by [Uttar Pradesh Police](14570210.md).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-HT-28072026-129]

Pradhan referred to the protesters as a "B-team of terrorists". In response, Dipke stated, "Sir, we are not terrorists. We do not need a certificate of patriotism from people like you, who are responsible for the deaths of innocent students", in reference to the students who had died by suicide amidst the [2026 NEET controversy](83200188.md).[^cite_note-131] At the protest, Wangchuk announced that he would begin an indefinite hunger strike from 27 June if Pradhan did not resign.[^cite_note-TT-125]

### Hunger strikes

#### Hunger strike of Sonam Wangchuk and AISA leaders

Sonam Wangchuk became a prominent figure in the protests. On 28 June, he began an indefinite hunger strike at Jantar Mantar along with six students from the [All India Students Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Students_Association) (AISA) including their National President [Neha Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neha_Bora) and an independent student activist named Hamad.[^cite_note-bbc3-24]

By 17 July, the nineteenth day of the strike, Wangchuk had lost 9.1 kilograms of body weight. A doctor attending him said he had begun losing muscle mass, and video footage showed him unable to stand or walk unaided. Politicians and entertainers, including the signatories of a statement with more than 1,800 signatures, appealed to him to end the fast. A petition filed in the [Delhi High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_High_Court) asked the government to admit him to a hospital to prevent his death; the government told the court that it would intervene if his health deteriorated.[^cite_note-bbc3-24]

On 18 July, Delhi Police removed Wangchuk from the protest site and admitted him to [Safdarjung Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safdarjung_Hospital), attached to [Vardhman Mahavir Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vardhman_Mahavir_Medical_College). Police stated that the action was taken on medical advice and in compliance with the directions of the Delhi High Court. Protest organisers criticised the police action, alleging that Wangchuk had been taken from the site forcibly and without prior notice.[^cite_note-et22-132][^cite_note-133]

Dipke warned that suppressing the protest would spark demonstrations across Delhi. On 20 July, Wangchuk announced in a handwritten statement issued from the Safdarjung Hospital, that he would continue the fast until the "youth leaders are allowed to meet the parliamentarians at the Sansad Bhawan or I am allowed to meet them here at the hospital".[^cite_note-134] Three of the AISA hunger strikers who had begun fasting alongside Wangchuk on 28 June ended their strike that same day, though Wangchuk continued his own fast from hospital.[^cite_note-npr2026-76]

Wangchuk broke his fast after 26 days in presence of J.P. Nadda and Jitendra Singh Rana at the Medanta Hospital, where he had been shifted to on 21 July,[^cite_note-\:0-85] after the government gave written assurances to take back cases against non-violent protesters and give compensation to suicide victims.[^cite_note-\:2-107] [Prime Minister](24452.md) [Narendra Modi](444222.md) moved to address the controversy with a proposed law providing for fast-track courts and stricter punishment for those involved in paper leaks. Dipke announced that the protest will however continue until the resignation of Dharmendra Pradhan.[^cite_note-135]

#### Hunger strike of Abhijeet Dipke and left-affiliated organisations

On 18 July, Dipke announced that he had begun an indefinite hunger strike. The announcement came after the office of the New Delhi [deputy commissioner of police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Commissioner_of_Police) said Wangchuk had been shifted to hospital for essential medical care on the orders of the Delhi High Court and on expert medical advice. Dipke alleged that he had been beaten and detained by Delhi Police during what he described as a crackdown on protesters at Jantar Mantar while Wangchuk was being taken to hospital. He said that the march planned for 20 July would proceed as scheduled.[^cite_note-Hunger_Strike-62][^cite_note-July_20_March-63]

On the same day, [Students Federation of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_Federation_of_India) General Secretary [Srijan Bhattacharyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srijan_Bhattacharyya) announced that SFI President Adarsh M. Saji and SFI Joint Secretary [Aishe Ghosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aishe_Ghosh) will join the indefinite hunger strike. Three AISA activists, Neha Bora, Ameen Amitoj and Manish Kumar and independent student Hamad continued their strike on their 21st day and so on, though according to *[NPR](29697232.md)*, three of the AISA fasters ended their strike on 20 July.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-npr2026-76] Dipke also ended his hunger strike on Sonam Wangchuk's request, as he appealed to Dipke to participate in the Sansad Chalo March.[^cite_note-137]

### Sansad Chalo March

Main article: [Sansad Chalo March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansad_Chalo_March)

On 20 July, the first day of the monsoon session of the [Parliament of India](410238.md), CJP launched its 'Chalo Sansad' march. CJP asked the protesters to bring the national flag and a copy of the Constitution or photographs of [B. R. Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar), [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) or [Bhagat Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagat_Singh). It also urged them to avoid political or organisational flags and to protest peacefully.[^cite_note-138] The march became more important after the treatment of Wangchuk, who went on a 20-day hunger strike to support the CJP at Jantar Mantar. On Saturday, he was taken to the hospital by force. This upset CJP supporters and brought more attention to the protest movement.[^cite_note-139]

Delhi Police tightened security across New Delhi district ahead of the march and imposed prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the [Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Nagarik_Suraksha_Sanhita) (BNSS). A day before the march, Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP) Sachin Sharma issued a public advisory on [X](9988187.md) stating that Delhi Police had not granted permission for the march.[^cite_note-140] [Delhi Metro Rail Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Metro_Rail_Corporation) (DMRC) closed five metro stations – [Udyog Bhawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udyog_Bhawan_metro_station), [Central Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Secretariat_metro_station), [Patel Chowk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patel_Chowk_metro_station), [Janpath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janpath_metro_station) and one of India's biggest interchange stations, [Rajiv Chowk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Chowk_metro_station) ahead of CJP's march to Parliament.[^cite_note-141] Protests broke out inside metro stations as the government sealed the exit points at stations which were near the protest site.[^cite_note-AJ-30] Internet services were also shut down in the vicinity of the Parliament.[^cite_note-Tear_Gas-142][^cite_note-Bhandari_&_Priya-143]

Security forces used baton charges and tear gas against the protesters who were marching towards the Parliament by breaking [police barricades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barricade).[^cite_note-Tear_Gas-142][^cite_note-Bhandari_&_Priya-143] Near Constitution Club, tear gas affected commuters at Central Secretariat metro station and a *The Wire* reporter covering the protest.[^cite_note-144]

Students and eyewitnesses alleged that authorities used excessive force, including beating demonstrators,[^cite_note-auto-145][^cite_note-AJ-30] dragging students along the streets,[^cite_note-146] and detaining students and children in police buses.[^cite_note-147] Security forces took firing positions with machine guns aimed at the Parliament gates.[^cite_note-\:62-124][^cite_note-148] *The South First* reported that visuals showed protesters sustaining injuries to their head and upper body.[^cite_note-\:62-124] [Gurdwara Bangla Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Bangla_Sahib) became a refuge for several such students and protesters fleeing batons and teargas.[^cite_note-bangla1-68][^cite_note-bangla2-69]

The police action drew widespread criticism from public figures and several opposition leaders, including [Shashi Tharoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor), [Rahul Gandhi](305010.md), and [Arvind Kejriwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvind_Kejriwal). Tharoor questioned the use of force, asking, "When a non-violent protest is taking place, what is the reason for a lathi charge?".[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-auto-145]

Delhi Police rejected the allegations, claiming that it had engaged in maintaining public order in a professional manner and denied reports of any violence or detentions during the protest.[^cite_note-150]

### Post-march strategic shift

After the 20 July crackdown, CJP leaders said the movement would not organise further marches through central Delhi, citing the risk of renewed police violence against participants, while affirming that the sit-in at Jantar Mantar and the demand for Pradhan's resignation would continue.[^cite_note-ajhalt-35] Dipke linked the decision to reports that around 150 protesters had been hurt in the 20 July march, while CJP spokesperson Ashutosh Ranka said the movement's core demand for Pradhan's dismissal remained unchanged.[^cite_note-ajhalt-35] NPR reported that although marches had been suspended, protesters continued to camp at Jantar Mantar and said they would remain until their demands were met.[^cite_note-npr2026-76] A profile of the movement published by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* on 22 July quotes Dipke describing Modi as behaving like "a dictator" in the aftermath of the crackdown, a characterisation the Bharatiya Janata Party has not directly responded to.[^cite_note-time2026-151] By 23 July, the dispute over Pradhan's resignation had also stalled proceedings in Parliament, where the opposition and the government remained deadlocked over the terms of a debate on the paper leak.[^cite_note-rsadjourn-103][^cite_note-onmanoramaneet-104]

### Parliamentary deadlock over a NEET debate

The protests coincided with repeated disruptions to the [Monsoon session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_session) of Parliament. Opposition members pressed for Pradhan's resignation as a precondition for any discussion of the alleged NEET-UG paper leak, while the government maintained that it was willing to hold a debate without preconditions.[^cite_note-rsadjourn-103] On 23 July, the fourth consecutive day of disruptions since the session began, both the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha were adjourned multiple times after opposition members raised slogans demanding Pradhan's dismissal.[^cite_note-onmanoramaneet-104] Parliamentary Affairs Minister [Kiren Rijiju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiren_Rijiju) said the government had approached the Congress and other opposition parties to hold the debate and that members could speak for as long as they wished, but accused the opposition of "trying to run away" from the discussion by attaching preconditions.[^cite_note-onmanoramaneet-104] Leader of the Opposition in the Rajya Sabha [Mallikarjun Kharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallikarjun_Kharge) responded that the Education Minister should resign before any debate could take place.[^cite_note-rsadjourn-103]

Rijiju also referred to remarks made earlier the same day by Prime Minister Modi, who announced fast-track courts to deliver punishment for those found responsible in paper-leak cases[^cite_note-rsadjourn-103]

### Delhi Police response

The handling of the protests by Delhi Police became a subject of public and legal scrutiny. Police stated that permission for the demonstration on 20 June 2026 had been granted only until 5 p.m., but organisers continued the sit-in beyond the permitted time, saying they would remain until their demands were addressed.[^cite_note-152]

On 20 July, Delhi Police used [lathis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathi_\(stick\)) to disperse hundreds of protesters marching from Mandi House Metro Station to Jantar Mantar, intending to reach the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) protest site, as reported by an eyewitness to PTI.[^cite_note-153] Allegations emerged regarding the deployment of pellet gun by security personnel, Delhi Police denied these claims, describing them as "completely false and misleading".[^cite_note-154] A medical report from the Safdargunj Hospital however confirmed that one of the protesters had suffered wounds from a pellet gun.[^cite_note-155] A general diary entry at the Parliament Street police station recorded the use of two rounds of pellets on 20 July by the [Rapid Action Force](3356632.md) (RAF) according to media reports.[^cite_note-156]

One of the protesters alleged that she was shot at with live bullets near Jantar Mantar. A medical report by [Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Ram_Manohar_Lohia_Hospital) stated that she suffered trauma to her right ear due to a gunshot. The Delhi Police however stated that she suffered injury from a blunt weapon and not a firearm according to her medical legal report.[^cite_note-157]

In July 2026, a [public interest litigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_interest_litigation) was filed before the Delhi High Court alleging that Delhi Police had subjected protesters to continuous photography, videography, and surveillance, creating what the petition called an "atmosphere of intimidation" and infringing their rights to privacy and peaceful assembly. The matter was admitted for hearing.[^cite_note-Atmosphere_of_Intimidation-29] Furthermore, police personnel from the [Delhi Police](8808263.md) were observed operating in civilian clothing during the crackdowns.[^cite_note-158]

On 22 July, the [Delhi High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_High_Court) sought a response from the central government and the Delhi Police to [PILs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_interest_litigation_in_India) alleging excessive use of force against the protesters. [Additional Solicitor General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_Solicitor_General_of_India) S.V. Raju submitted that the section of the protests had turned violent with some of the protesters engaging in stone-pelting. Lawyers arguing on behalf of the petitioners termed the police action as unwarranted and brutal.[^cite_note-159]

CCTV footage from the protest sites showed protesters turning violent during the demonstration, where a section of the crowd attacked security personnel, breaking through barricades, pelting stones, using pepper spray, overturning vehicles, damaging police vehicles and assaulting officers sitting inside them.[^cite_note-160] The video showed protesters damaging public property and violating prohibitory orders imposed in the area. Delhi Police alleged that 2,873 men with criminal antecedents were present at the protest sites between 20–25 July,[^cite_note-161] including criminals involved in heinous crimes such as sexual assault, murder and crimes involving minors (POSCO).[^cite_note-162]

## Aftermath

Two days after the protests had been paused, the CJP and the All India Students Association asserted that the government had not fulfilled its agreement to refrain from punishing protesters, and floated the possibility of restarting protests.[^cite_note-EllisPetersenGuardian-163] The CJP reckoned that more than 100 students had been arrested.[^cite_note-EllisPetersenGuardian-163]

## Reactions

Opposition leaders supported the protesters and Wangchuk's hunger strike. [Samajwadi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samajwadi_Party) president [Akhilesh Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhilesh_Yadav) urged Wangchuk to end his fast, calling his life "invaluable" while supporting the demand for greater accountability in the education system.[^cite_note-aljazeera-164]

[Rahul Gandhi](305010.md) characterised Modi as India's most "anti-youth Prime Minister", also saying "Tear-gassing students and lathi-charging them is not democratic. They have legitimate demands. The Prime Minister should accept them and act." The [Congress party](149333.md) condemned police action against protesters marching to Parliament on the opening day of the monsoon session. It also said it had raised concerns about paper leaks before the current agitation led by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP).[^cite_note-165]

On 28 June, [NCP (SP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCPSP) national spokesperson [Anish Gawande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anish_Gawande) joined the protest at [Jantar Mantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_New_Delhi) and reiterated the demand for Pradhan's resignation. He said the [Trinamool Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinamool_Congress), the [Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravida_Munnetra_Kazhagam), and the NCP (SP) had supported the agitation.[^cite_note-166]

On 16 July, former Delhi Chief Minister Arvind Kejriwal joined the protest at Jantar Mantar, seek for Pradhan's removal, and suggested Wangchuk as education minister.[^cite_note-toikejriwal2-167]

On 16 July, AIMIM president and Hyderabad MP [Asaduddin Owaisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asaduddin_Owaisi) urged the Union government to seriously consider Wangchuk's demands and expressed concern over his deteriorating health. Following the police action during the 20 July march, Owaisi criticised the government for failing to engage with the students earlier and described the situation as "complete mismanagement", comparing the response with the handling of the anti-CAA and farmers' protests.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169]

During his hunger strike, Wangchuk said the movement had "no political colour" and described its demands as an appeal for accountability and reform in the education system, not just the resignation of the education minister.[^cite_note-Sonam_Wangchuk_to_Express-28]

In [Kerala](4349459.md), solidarity demonstrations were held in several cities, including [Thrissur](179902.md), [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi), [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode) and [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram), where students, activists, and civil society groups expressed support for the Jantar Mantar protests.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]Student organisations in Kerala, including SFI and [Kerala Students' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Students'_Union), organised solidarity protests, with KSU also observing a statewide educational [bandh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandh).[^cite_note-ONM1-6]

On 26 July 2026, the Students Federation of India – United Kingdom organised a demonstration at the [Trafalgar Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafalgar_Square) in London, to celebrate the victory i.e. resignation of Dharmendra Pradhan, in solidarity with the protestors of India. It was attended by the activist, [Greta Thunberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Thunberg), as well.[^cite_note-181] Addressing to the crowd, she gave a short speech:

> "The Indian student protest shows what we can achieve when people come together and resist. The protests show the true meaning of people power. The Indian student struggle for justice and broader struggle for democracy and liberation is also our struggle," she said. She closed with the words "Power to the people."[^cite_note-182]

### Film fraternity of India

The protest was supported by actors such as [Prakash Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakash_Raj), [Naseeruddin Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naseeruddin_Shah), [Ratna Pathak Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratna_Pathak_Shah), [Sonakshi Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonakshi_Sinha) and [Seema Pahwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seema_Pahwa).[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185][^cite_note-EntertainmentIE-186] However, overall solidarity from [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood) was signally absent, especially from actors who had previously supported political causes in other countries.[^cite_note-DhillonGuardian-187][^cite_note-EntertainmentIE-186] Amrit Dhillon wrote in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* that Bollywood was widely understood to have been captured by the [Modi government](52507144.md) in recent years.[^cite_note-DhillonGuardian-187]

Actors from the [Malayalam film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam_cinema) — including [Tovino Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tovino_Thomas), [Parvathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parvathy_Thiruvothu), [Mammootty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammootty) and [Ranjini Haridas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjini_Haridas) — extended support to the student protests.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191]

### Hip-hop community of India

[Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_hip-hop) and [Desi hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desi_hip-hop) as an art form also played a crucial role in the protests.[^cite_note-192] Songs like "[Azadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gully_Boy_\(soundtrack\))", by Indian rapper [Divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_\(rapper\)) and producer Dub Sharma, and "Boom Shaka" by rappers [KR\$NA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krsna_\(rapper\)) and Dhanda Nyoliwala,[^cite_note-193] were used extensively to promote the cause, while members of the hip-hop collective Zuban-e-Lucknow, came together to perform a rap cypher, whose message was centred on accountability and education reforms.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

Indian rappers like [Hanumankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanumankind), Chaar Diwaari, Encore ABJ of [Seedhe Maut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedhe_Maut), Raga, Karma and [KR\$NA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krsna_\(rapper\)) joined the Delhi protests.[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-Krsna-198][^cite_note-199] Meanwhile, [Emiway Bantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiway_Bantai) and [Raftaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raftaar), among others attended Mumbai solidarity protests.[^cite_note-200][^cite_note-Krsna-198] [Vedan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedan_\(rapper\)) was among the 100 protesters booked for protesting in [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi) in solidarity with the CJP.[^cite_note-201] [Arivu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arivu) was also detained while leading a surprise protest outside the [Tamil Nadu](29918.md) Secretariat.[^cite_note-202]
