The **secretary of state for transport**, also referred to as the **transport secretary**, is a [secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_\(United_Kingdom\)) in the [Government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md), with overall responsibility for the policies of the [Department for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Transport).[^cite_note-3] The incumbent is a member of the [Cabinet of the United Kingdom](162790.md).

The office holder works alongside the other [transport ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Transport#Ministers). The corresponding [shadow minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_minister) is the [shadow secretary of state for transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Secretary_of_State_for_Transport), and the secretary of state is also scrutinised by the [Transport Select Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Select_Committee).[^cite_note-4]

The position of secretary of state for transport is held by [Heidi Alexander](27306797.md), who was appointed by [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) following the resignation of [Louise Haigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Haigh).

## History

The Ministry of Transport absorbed the [Ministry of Shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Shipping) and was renamed the Ministry of War Transport in 1941, but resumed its previous name at the end of the war.[^cite_note-5]

The Ministry of Civil Aviation was created by [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) in 1944 to look at peaceful ways of using [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) and to find something for the aircraft factories to do after the war.[^cite_note-6] The new Conservative government in 1951 appointed the same minister to both Transport and Civil Aviation, finally amalgamating the ministries on 1 October 1953.[^cite_note-7]

The ministry was renamed back to the Ministry of Transport on 14 October 1959, when a separate [Ministry of Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Aviation) was formed.

Transport responsibilities were subsumed by the Department for the Environment, headed by the [Secretary of State for the Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Environment) from 15 October 1970 to 10 September 1976.

The [Department for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Transport) was recreated as a separate department by [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan) in 1976.[^cite_note-8] The position was also incorporated as a [corporation sole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporation_sole) by the **Secretary of State for Transport Order 1976** ([SI 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_1976)/1775).[^cite_note-9]

The super-department [Department of the Environment, Transport and the Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Environment,_Transport_and_the_Regions) was created in 1997 for [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom) [John Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Prescott).

In 2001, the [Department of the Environment, Transport and the Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Environment,_Transport_and_the_Regions) was widely considered unwieldy and so was broken up,[^cite_note-10] with the Transport functions now combined with Local Government and the Regions in the **DTLR** (**Department for Transport, Local Government and the Regions**).
