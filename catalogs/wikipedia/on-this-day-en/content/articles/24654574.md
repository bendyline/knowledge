# White liquor

For home-made high-proof distilled spirits also known as white liquor, see [moonshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonshine).

**White liquor** is a strongly [alkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline) solution composed mainly of [sodium hydroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_hydroxide) and [sodium sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_sulfide). It is used in the first stage of the [Kraft process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraft_process) in which [lignin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignin) and [hemicellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicellulose) are separated from [cellulose fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose_fiber) for the production of [pulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_\(paper\)).[^cite_note-pulping-1] The white liquor breaks the chemical bonds between lignin and cellulose substructures in wood materials and further degrades the lignin itself.[^cite_note-Ullmann-2]

Sulfide is the active component in delignification.[^cite_note-pulping-1] White liquor also contains minor amounts of [sodium carbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_carbonate), [sodium sulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_sulfate), [sodium thiosulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_thiosulfate), [sodium chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_chloride), [sodium bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_bicarbonate), and [calcium carbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_carbonate). Because the components of White Liquor are not consumed by the Kraft process they can be recovered from Black Liquor, the biproduct of pulp digestion. To do so, [black liquor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_liquor) is converted to smelt, which is extracted with water to give [green liquor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_liquor). Addition of lime converts green liquor to white liquor, ready for reuse.[^cite_note-Ullmann-2]

The chemical characteristics of the white liquor, deriving from its properties, are described by the parameters *total alkali*, *active alkali*, *effective alkali*, *sulfidity*, *causticity*, and *reduction*.

| Parameter | Defining analytical value |
| --- | --- |
| Total alkali | All Na<sup>+</sup> compounds |
| Active alkali (AA) | NaOH + Na<sub>2</sub>S |
| Effective alkali (EA) | NaOH + ½ Na<sub>2</sub>S |
| Sulfidity | Na<sub>2</sub>S / (Na2S + NaOH) |
| Causticity | NaOH / (NaOH + Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub>) |
| Reduction | Na<sub>2</sub>S / (Na<sub>2</sub>S + Na<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>) |
