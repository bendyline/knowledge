The **General Directorate for National Security** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): المديرية العامة للأمن الوطني, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Aālmdyryh al-ʻĀmmah lil-amn al-Waṭanī*; [Standard Moroccan Tamazight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Moroccan_Tamazight_language): ⵜⴰⵎⵀⵍⴰ ⵜⴰⵎⴰⵜⴰⵢⵜ ⵏ ⵜⵏⴼⵔⵓⵜ ⵜⴰⵏⴰⵎⵓⵔⵜ, romanized: *tamhla tamatayt n tnfrut tanamurt*; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Direction Générale de la Sûreté Nationale*, **DGSN**) is the national [police force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_force) of the [Kingdom of Morocco](19291.md). It is responsible for maintaining [public order](3905736.md), enforcing the law, and ensuring public security.

The DGSN was established on 16 May 1956 by King [Mohammed V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_V_of_Morocco).[^cite_note-ref1-1] It operates alongside other security forces, notably the [Auxiliary Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_Forces) and the [Royal Gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Moroccan_Gendarmerie).

## History

Before the 20th century, a regionalized [Shurta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shurta) (police) enforced [Sharia law](28840.md) and ensured security across the country.[^cite_note-\:02-4] During the [Almohad caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate), the caliph also took on the role of [ṣāḥib al-shurṭa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%B9%A2%C4%81%E1%B8%A5ib_al-shur%E1%B9%ADa) (head of police).[^cite_note-\:02-4][^cite_note-5]

There were regional and tribal [Muhtasibs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhtasib) (equivalent to a police commissioner) who were appointed by the [Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha).[^cite_note-\:0-6] The Muhtasib had the power to issue fines for minor offenses.[^cite_note-7] Criminals were tried in front of a [Qadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadi), the judge of a Sharia court. The Qadi issued judgements in criminal, civil and commercial cases.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

On July 20, 1901, Morocco formed its first national border patrol.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The patrol was in charge of ensuring the security of the border with [French Algeria](292341.md), under the control of a commissioner in [Oujda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oujda).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

In 1906, the [Algeciras Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras_Conference) led to various police reforms in Morocco.[^cite_note-12] The reforms established a national police force that would fall under the Sultan's sovereign authority. The police force's recruitment would be overseen by the [Makhzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhzen) and commanded by [Caïds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaid). Additionally, the police force would be stationed in the eight ports that were open to international trade.[^cite_note-\:4-13][^cite_note-14]

In 1912, the General Police Service was created after the installement of the [French Protectorate in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_in_Morocco).[^cite_note-\:1-15] In 1913, a forensic identification unit was formed.[^cite_note-\:1-15][^cite_note-\:2-16] A general forensic identification service was formed in November 1931.[^cite_note-\:2-16]

On May 16, 1956, a year after Morocco regained independence, the DGSN was formed.[^cite_note-17] The Royal Institute of Police in Kenitra was inaugurated in 1978.[^cite_note-\:3-18] The Scientific and Technical Police Laboratory in Casablanca was created in 1991.[^cite_note-\:3-18]

On 8 September 2023, [an earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Marrakesh-Safi_earthquake) with a magnitude of 6.8 [M<sub>w</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) hit the [Marrakesh-Safi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh-Safi) region of Morocco.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] DGSN along with [DGST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Directorate_for_Territorial_Surveillance_\(Morocco\)) has announced it will contribute MAD 50 million to Special Fund for Managing Earthquake Effects.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] They set up two mobile bakeries near Marrakech, to provide bread to those who were affected by the earthquake.[^cite_note-24]

## Organization

In 2007, the Sûreté Nationale had approximately 46,000 personnel. A decade later, in 2017, the number of personnel had increased to 70,000.

As of 2004, the Sûreté Nationale operated the following specialist divisions:[^cite_note-DerdMorocco-25]

- The Border Police: responsible for border control and surveillance

- Mobile Intervention Corps: tasked with rapid intervention in major emergencies

- National Brigade: primarily responsible for investigation of serious crimes, including [terrorism](30636.md), organized and [white-collar crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-collar_crime).
