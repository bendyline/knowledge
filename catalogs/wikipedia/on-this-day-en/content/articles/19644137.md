# Mobile phone

For the modern mobile phone, see [Smartphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone).

"Cell Phone" and "Handphone" redirect here. For the films, see [*Cell Phone* (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_Phone_\(film\)) and [*Handphone* (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handphone_\(film\)).

A **mobile phone** or **cell phone** is a portable wireless [telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone) that allows users to make and receive [calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_call) over a [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) link while moving within a designated telephone service area, unlike fixed-location phones ([landline phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landline_phones)). This radio frequency link connects to the switching systems of a [mobile phone operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_operator), providing access to the [public switched telephone network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_switched_telephone_network) (PSTN). Modern mobile telephony relies on a [cellular network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_network) architecture, which is why mobile phones are often referred to as 'cell phones' in North America.

Beyond traditional [voice communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_call), digital mobile phones have evolved to support a wide range of additional services. These include [text messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_messaging), [ad-hoc networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone_ad-hoc_network),[^cite_note-Forbes_teens-1] [multimedia messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia_Messaging_Service), [email](9738.md), and [internet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_access) (via [LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTE_\(telecommunication\)), [5G NR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G_NR), or [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi)), as well as short-range wireless technologies like [Bluetooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluetooth), [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_Data_Association), and [ultra-wideband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-wideband) (UWB).

Mobile phones also support a variety of [multimedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia) capabilities, such as [digital photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_photography), [video recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_recording), and [gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_gaming). In addition, they enable multimedia playback and [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media), including video content, as well as [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_radio) and [television streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_television). Furthermore, mobile phones offer [satellite-based](45207.md) services, such as [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_navigation) and [messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_phone#Integration_into_conventional_mobile_phones), as well as business applications and [payment solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_payment) (via [scanning QR codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QR_code_payment) or [near-field communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-field_communication) (NFC)). Mobile phones offering only basic features are often referred to as [feature phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_phone) ([slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slang): *dumbphones*), while those with advanced computing power are known as [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone).[^cite_note-Srivastava-2]

The first handheld mobile phone was demonstrated by [Martin Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Cooper_\(inventor\)) of [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola) in [New York City](645042.md) on 3 April 1973, using a handset weighing c. 2 kilograms (4.4 lbs).[^cite_note-Inventor-3] In 1979, [Nippon Telegraph and Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Telegraph_and_Telephone) (NTT) launched the world's first cellular network in Japan.[^cite_note-4] In 1983, the [DynaTAC 8000x](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola_DynaTAC) was the first commercially available handheld mobile phone. From 1993 to 2024, worldwide mobile phone subscriptions grew to over 9.1 billion; enough to provide one for every person on Earth.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-ITU-6] In 2024, the top smartphone manufacturers worldwide were [Samsung](46445426.md), [Apple](856.md) and [Xiaomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaomi); smartphone sales represented about 50 percent of total mobile phone sales.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] For feature phones as of 2016, the top-selling brands were Samsung, [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMD_Global) and [Alcatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcatel_Mobile).[^cite_note-9]

Mobile phones are considered an important human invention as they have been one of the most widely used and sold pieces of [consumer technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_electronics).[^cite_note-Harris-2019-10] The growth in popularity has been rapid in some places; for example, in the UK, the total number of mobile phones overtook the number of houses in 1999.[^cite_note-11] Today, mobile phones are globally ubiquitous,[^cite_note-Gupta-2011-12] and in almost half the world's countries, over 90% of the world population, or approximately 7.2 billion people, owns at least one.[^cite_note-13]

## Name

"Mobile phone" is the most common English-language term, while the term "cell phone" is in more common use in North America[^cite_note-14] – both are in essence shorter versions of "*mobile telephone*" and "*cellular telephone*", respectively. Often in colloquial terms it is referred to as simply **phone**, **mobile** or **cell**. A number of alternative words have also been used to describe a mobile phone, most of which have fallen out of use, including: "*mobile handset*", "*wireless phone*", "*mobile terminal*", "*cellular device*", "*hand phone*", and "*pocket phone*".

## History

Main article: [History of mobile phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mobile_phones)

See also: [List of wireless network technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wireless_network_technologies)



[Martin Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Cooper_\(inventor\)) of Motorola, shown here in a 2007 reenactment, made the first publicized handheld mobile phone call on a prototype DynaTAC model on 3 April 1973.

A handheld mobile radio telephone service was envisioned in the early stages of radio engineering. In 1917, [Finnish](10577.md) inventor [Eric Tigerstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Tigerstedt) filed a patent for a "pocket-size folding telephone with a very thin carbon microphone". Early predecessors of cellular phones included [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal) radio communications from ships and trains. The race to create truly portable telephone devices began after World War II, with developments taking place in many countries. The advances in [mobile telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_telephony) have been traced in successive "generations", starting with the early zeroth-generation ([0G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_radio_telephone)) services, such as [Bell System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_System)'s [Mobile Telephone Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Telephone_Service) and its successor, the [Improved Mobile Telephone Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improved_Mobile_Telephone_Service). These 0G systems were not [cellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_network), supported a few simultaneous calls, and were very expensive.



The Motorola [DynaTAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DynaTAC) 8000X. In 1983, it became the first commercially available handheld cellular mobile phone.

Mobile phone technology has progressed significantly since its origins, evolving from large car-mounted systems to compact, handheld devices.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Early mobile phones required vehicle installation due to their size and power needs.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] A major breakthrough came in 1973, when the first handheld cellular mobile phone was demonstrated by [John F. Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Mitchell)[^cite_note-John_F._Mitchell_Biography-19][^cite_note-Who_invented_the_cell_phone-20] and [Martin Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Cooper_\(inventor\)) of [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola_Mobility), using a handset weighing 2 kilograms (4.4 lb).[^cite_note-Inventor-3][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Cooper made the first ever call on a cell phone when he called [Joel S. Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_S._Engel), a rival of his who worked for [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T), saying, "I'm calling you on a cell phone, but a real cell phone, a personal, handheld, portable cell phone."[^cite_note-23]

The first commercial automated cellular network ([1G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1G)) [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal) was launched in Japan by [Nippon Telegraph and Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Telegraph_and_Telephone) in 1979. This was followed in 1981 by the simultaneous launch of the [Nordic Mobile Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Mobile_Telephone) (NMT) system in Denmark, Finland, Norway, and Sweden.[^cite_note-24] Several other countries then followed in the early to mid-1980s. These first-generation ([1G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1G)) systems could support far more simultaneous calls but still used [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_electronics) cellular technology. In 1983, the [DynaTAC 8000x](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola_DynaTAC) was the first commercially available handheld mobile phone.

In 1991, the second-generation ([2G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2G)) digital cellular technology was launched in Finland by [Radiolinja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiolinja) on the [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) standard. This sparked competition in the sector as the new operators challenged the incumbent 1G network operators. The GSM standard is a European initiative expressed at the [CEPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Conference_of_Postal_and_Telecommunications_Administrations) ("Conférence Européenne des Postes et Telecommunications", European Postal and Telecommunications conference). The Franco-German R\&D cooperation demonstrated the technical feasibility, and in 1987, a Memorandum of Understanding was signed between 13 European countries that agreed to launch a commercial service by 1991. The first version of the GSM standard had 6,000 pages. The [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) and [RSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) awarded [Thomas Haug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Haug) and [Philippe Dupuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Dupuis_\(engineer\)) the 2018 [James Clerk Maxwell medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE/RSE_James_Clerk_Maxwell_Medal) for their contributions to the first digital mobile telephone standard.[^cite_note-25] In 2018, the GSM was used by over 5 billion people in over 220 countries. The GSM (2G) has evolved into 3G, 4G and 5G. The standardization body for GSM started at the CEPT Working Group GSM (Group Special Mobile) in 1982 under the umbrella of CEPT. In 1988, [ETSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETSI) was established, and all CEPT standardization activities were transferred to ETSI. Working Group GSM became Technical Committee GSM. In 1991, it became Technical Committee SMG (Special Mobile Group) when ETSI tasked the committee with UMTS (3G). In addition to transmitting voice over digital signals, the 2G network introduced data services for mobile, starting with [SMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS) text messages, then expanding to [Multimedia Messaging Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia_Messaging_Service) (MMS), and [mobile internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_web) with a theoretical maximum transfer speed of 384 kbit/s (48 kB/s).&#x20;



Dupuis and Haug during a GSM meeting in Belgium, April 1992



[Personal Handy-phone System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Handy-phone_System) mobiles and modems, 1997–2003

In 2001, the third-generation ([3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G)) was launched in Japan by [NTT DoCoMo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTT_DoCoMo) on the [WCDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCDMA) standard.[^cite_note-26] This was followed by 3.5G or 3G+ enhancements based on the [high-speed packet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-Speed_Packet_Access) (HSPA) family, allowing [UMTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMTS) networks to have higher data transfer speeds and capacity. 3G is able to provide [mobile broadband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_802.20) access of several [Mbit/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbps) to smartphones and [mobile modems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_modem) in laptop computers. This ensures it can be applied to mobile Internet access, [VoIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_over_IP), video calls, and sending large e-mail messages, as well as watching videos, typically in [standard-definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-definition_television) quality.

By 2009, it had become clear that, at some point, 3G networks would be overwhelmed by the growth of bandwidth-intensive applications, such as [streaming media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media).[^cite_note-27] Consequently, the industry began looking to data-optimized fourth-generation ([4G](486547.md)) technologies, with the promise of speed improvements up to tenfold over existing 3G technologies. The first publicly available [LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTE_\(telecommunication\)) service was launched in Scandinavia by [TeliaSonera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeliaSonera) in 2009. In the 2010s, 4G technology has found diverse applications across various sectors, showcasing its versatility in delivering high-speed wireless communication, such as mobile broadband, the [internet of things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_things) (IoT), [fixed wireless access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_wireless_access), and multimedia streaming (including music, video, [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_radio), and [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_television)).

Deployment of fifth-generation ([5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G)) cellular networks commenced worldwide in 2019. The term "5G" was originally used in research papers and projects to denote the next major phase in mobile telecommunication standards beyond the [4G](486547.md)/[IMT-Advanced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMT-Advanced) standards. The [3GPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3GPP) defines 5G as any system that adheres to the [5G NR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G_NR) (5G New Radio) standard. 5G can be implemented in low-band, mid-band or high-band millimeter-wave, with download speeds that can achieve [gigabit-per-second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigabit_per_second) (Gbit/s) range, aiming for a network latency of 1 ms. This near-real-time responsiveness and improved overall data performance are crucial for applications like [online gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_game), [augmented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_reality) and [virtual reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality), [autonomous vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_vehicles), IoT, and critical communication services.

### Smartphone

Main article: [Smartphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone)

Smartphones are defined by their advanced computing capabilities, which include internet connectivity and access to a wide range of applications. The [International Telecommunication Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union) measures those with Internet connection, which it calls *Active Mobile-Broadband subscriptions* (which includes tablets, etc.). In developed countries, smartphones have largely replaced earlier mobile technologies, while in developing regions, they account for around 50% of all mobile phone usage.

### Feature phone

Main article: [Feature phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_phone)

Feature phone is a term typically used as a [retronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retronym) to describe mobile phones which are limited in capabilities in contrast to a modern smartphone. Feature phones typically provide [voice calling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_calling) and [text messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_messaging) functionality, in addition to basic [multimedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia) and [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) capabilities, and other services offered by the user's [wireless service provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_service_provider). A feature phone has additional functions over and above a basic mobile phone, which is only capable of voice calling and text messaging.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-forbes-twoweeks-30] Feature phones and basic mobile phones tend to use a proprietary, custom-designed [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) and [user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_interface). By contrast, smartphones generally use a [mobile operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_operating_system) that often shares common traits across devices.

## Infrastructure

Main articles: [Cellular network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_network) and [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi)



Cellular networks work by only reusing radio frequencies (in this example frequencies f1–f4) in non adjacent cells to avoid interference

The critical advantage that modern cellular networks have over predecessor systems is the concept of [frequency reuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_reuse) allowing many simultaneous telephone conversations in a given service area. This allows efficient use of the limited [radio spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_spectrum) allocated to mobile services, and lets thousands of subscribers converse at the same time within a given geographic area.

Former systems would cover a service area with one or two powerful [base stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_station) with a range of up to tens of kilometers' (miles), using only a few sets of radio channels (frequencies). Once these few channels were in use by customers, no further customers could be served until another user vacated a channel. It would be impractical to give every customer a unique channel since there would not be enough [bandwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandwidth_\(computing\)) allocated to the mobile service. As well, technical limitations such as antenna efficiency and receiver design limit the range of frequencies a customer unit could use.

A [cellular network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_network) mobile phone system gets its name from dividing the service area into many small cells, each with a base station with (for example) a useful range on the order of a kilometer (mile). These systems have dozens or hundreds of possible channels allocated to them. When a subscriber is using a given channel for a telephone connection, that frequency is unavailable for other customers in the local cell and in the adjacent cells. However, cells further away can re-use that channel without interference as the subscriber's handset is too far away to be detected. The transmitter power of each base station is coordinated to efficiently service its own cell, but not to interfere with the cells further away.

Automation embedded in the customer's handset and in the base stations control all phases of the call, from detecting the presence of a handset in a service area, temporary assignment of a channel to a handset making a call, interface with the land-line side of the network to connect to other subscribers, and collection of billing information for the service. The automation systems can control the "hand off" of a customer handset moving between one cell and another so that a call in progress continues without interruption, changing channels if required. In the earliest mobile phone systems by contrast, all control was done manually; the customer would search for an unoccupied channel and speak to a mobile operator to request connection of a call to a landline number or another mobile. At the termination of the call the mobile operator would manually record the billing information.

Mobile phones communicate with cell towers that are placed to give coverage across a telephone service area, which is divided up into 'cells'. Each cell uses a different set of frequencies from neighboring cells, and will typically be covered by three towers placed at different locations. The cell towers are usually interconnected to each other and the phone network and the internet by wired connections. Due to bandwidth limitations each cell will have a maximum number of cell phones it can handle at once. The cells are therefore sized depending on the expected usage density, and may be much smaller in cities. In that case much lower transmitter powers are used to avoid broadcasting beyond the cell.

In order to handle the high traffic, multiple towers can be set up in the same area (using different frequencies). This can be done permanently or temporarily such as at special events or in disasters. Cell phone companies will bring a truck with equipment to host the abnormally high traffic.

Capacity was further increased when phone companies implemented digital networks. With digital, one frequency can host multiple simultaneous calls.

Additionally, short-range [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) infrastructure is often used by smartphones as much as possible as it offloads traffic from cell networks on to local area networks.

## Hardware

Main article: [Mobile phone features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_features)

The common components found on all mobile phones are:

- A [central processing unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit) (CPU), the processor of phones. The CPU is a [microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor) fabricated on a [metal–oxide–semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal%E2%80%93oxide%E2%80%93semiconductor) (MOS) [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) (IC) chip.

- A [battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_\(electricity\)), providing the power source for the phone functions. A modern handset typically uses a [lithium-ion battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) (LIB), whereas older handsets used [nickel–metal hydride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel%E2%80%93metal_hydride) (Ni–MH) batteries.

- An input mechanism to allow the user to interact with the phone. These are a [keypad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keypad) for feature phones, and [touch screens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_screens) for most smartphones (typically with [capacitive sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitive_sensing)).

- A [display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_device) which echoes the user's typing, and displays text messages, contacts, and more. The display is typically either a [liquid-crystal display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-crystal_display) (LCD) or [organic light-emitting diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_light-emitting_diode) (OLED) display.

- [Speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudspeaker) for sound.

- [Subscriber identity module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscriber_identity_module) (SIM) cards and removable user identity module (R-UIM) cards.

- A hardware [notification LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notification_LED) on some phones

Low-end mobile phones are often referred to as [feature phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_phone) and offer basic telephony. Handsets with more advanced computing ability through the use of native software applications are known as smartphones. The first [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) phones and many feature phones had [NOR flash memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOR_flash_memory), from which processor instructions could be executed directly in an execute in place architecture and allowed for short boot times. With smartphones, [NAND flash memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAND_flash_memory) was adopted as it has larger storage capacities and lower costs, but causes longer boot times because instructions cannot be executed from it directly, and must be copied to RAM memory first before execution.[^cite_note-31]

### Central processing unit

Mobile phones have [central processing units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit) (CPUs), similar to those in computers, but optimised to operate in low power environments. Mobile processors commonly use heterogeneous multicore architectures, combining cores with different performance and power characteristics so that computational workloads can be distributed according to their processing requirements and energy constraints.[^cite_note-32]

Mobile CPU performance depends not only on the clock rate (generally given in multiples of [hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz))[^cite_note-33] but also the [memory hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_hierarchy) also greatly affects overall performance. Because of these problems, the performance of mobile phone CPUs is often more appropriately given by scores derived from various standardized tests to measure the real effective performance in commonly used applications.

### Display

Main article: [Display device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_device)

One of the main characteristics of phones is the [screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_device). Depending on the device's type and design, the screen fills most or nearly all of the space on a device's front surface. Many smartphone displays have an [aspect ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_aspect_ratio) of [16\:9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16\:9), but taller aspect ratios became more common in 2017.

Screen sizes are often measured in diagonal [inches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch) or [millimeters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millimeter); feature phones generally have screen sizes below 90 millimetres (3.5 in). Phones with screens larger than 130 millimetres (5.2 in) are often called "[phablets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phablet)." Smartphones with screens over 115 millimetres (4.5 in) in size are commonly difficult to use with only a single hand, since most thumbs cannot reach the entire screen surface; they may need to be shifted around in the hand, held in one hand and manipulated by the other, or used in place with both hands. Due to design advances, some modern smartphones with large screen sizes and "edge-to-edge" designs have compact builds that improve their ergonomics, while the shift to taller aspect ratios have resulted in phones that have larger screen sizes whilst maintaining the ergonomics associated with smaller 16\:9 displays.[^cite_note-phonearena-s7edgenotphablet-34][^cite_note-verge-phabletpytha-35][^cite_note-verge-s8aspectratio-36]

[Liquid-crystal displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-crystal_display) are the most common; others are [IPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPS_panel), [LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LED), [OLED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OLED), and [AMOLED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMOLED) displays. Some displays are integrated with pressure-sensitive digitizers, such as those developed by [Wacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wacom_\(company\)) and [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung),[^cite_note-37] and Apple's "[3D Touch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_Touch)" system.

### Sound

In sound, smartphones and feature phones vary little. Some audio-quality enhancing features, such as [Voice over LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_over_LTE) and [HD Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_Voice), have appeared and are often available on newer smartphones. Sound quality can remain a problem due to the design of the phone, the quality of the cellular network and compression algorithms used in [long-distance calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_call).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Audio quality can be improved using a [VoIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VoIP) application over [WiFi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WiFi).[^cite_note-40] Cellphones have small speakers so that the user can use a [speakerphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speakerphone) feature and talk to a person on the phone without holding it to their ear. The small speakers can also be used to listen to digital audio files of music or speech or watch videos with an audio component, without holding the phone close to the ear.

### Battery

The typical lifespan of a mobile phone battery is approximately two to three years, although this varies based on usage patterns, environmental conditions, and overall care. Most modern mobile phones use [lithium-ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) (Li-ion) batteries, which are designed to endure between 500 and 2,500 charge cycles. The exact number of cycles depends on factors such as charging habits, operating temperature, and battery management systems.[^cite_note-41]

Li-ion batteries gradually degrade over time due to chemical aging, leading to reduced capacity and performance, often noticeable after one or two years of regular use. Unlike older battery types, such as [nickel-metal hydride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel%E2%80%93metal_hydride_battery) (Ni-MH), Li-ion batteries do not need to be fully discharged to maintain their longevity. In fact, they perform best when kept between 30% and 80% of their full charge.[^cite_note-42] While practices such as avoiding excessive heat and minimizing overcharging can help preserve battery health, many modern devices include built-in safeguards.[^cite_note-43] These safeguards, typically managed by the phone's internal battery management system (BMS), prevent overcharging by cutting off power once the battery reaches full capacity. Additionally, most contemporary chargers and devices are designed to regulate charging to minimize stress on the battery. Therefore, while good charging habits can positively impact battery longevity, most users benefit from these integrated protections, making battery maintenance less of a concern in day-to-day use.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

Future mobile phone batteries are expected to utilize advanced technologies such as [silicon-carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon-carbon_battery) (Si/C) batteries and [solid-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_battery) batteries, which promise to offer higher energy densities, longer lifespans, and improved safety compared to current lithium-ion batteries.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

### SIM card

Main articles: [SIM card](197489.md) and [Removable User Identity Module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removable_User_Identity_Module)



Typical mobile phone mini-[SIM card](197489.md)

Mobile phones require a small [microchip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) called a Subscriber Identity Module or [SIM card](197489.md), in order to function. The SIM card is approximately the size of a small postage stamp and is usually placed underneath the battery in the rear of the unit. The SIM securely stores the [service-subscriber key (IMSI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mobile_Subscriber_Identity) and the [K<sub>i</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentication_Center) used to identify and authenticate the user of the mobile phone. The SIM card allows users to change phones by simply removing the SIM card from one mobile phone and inserting it into another mobile phone or broadband telephony device, provided that this is not prevented by a [SIM lock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM_lock). The first SIM card was made in 1991 by Munich smart card maker [Giesecke & Devrient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giesecke_&_Devrient) for the Finnish wireless network operator [Radiolinja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiolinja).

A hybrid mobile phone can hold up to four SIM cards, with a phone having a different [device identifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMEI) for each SIM Card. SIM and [R-UIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-UIM) cards may be mixed together to allow both [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) and [CDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDMA) networks to be accessed. From 2010 onwards, such phones became popular in emerging markets,[^cite_note-49] and this was attributed to the desire to obtain the lowest calling costs.

When the removal of a SIM card is detected by the operating system, it may deny further operation until a reboot.[^cite_note-50]

History

From 1983 to 1998, [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola) was market leader in mobile phones. ## Use

See also: [Smartphone § Use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone#Use)



Mobile phone subscribers per 100 inhabitants. 2014 figure is estimated.

Mobile phones are used for a variety of purposes, such as keeping in touch with family members, for conducting business, and in order to have access to a telephone in the event of an emergency. Some people carry more than one mobile phone for different purposes, such as for business and personal use. Multiple SIM cards may be used to take advantage of the benefits of different calling plans. For example, a particular plan might provide for cheaper local calls, long-distance calls, international calls, or roaming.

The mobile phone has been used in a variety of diverse contexts in society. For example:

- A study by [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola_Mobility) found that one in ten mobile phone subscribers have a second phone that is often kept secret from other family members. These phones may be used to engage in such activities as extramarital affairs or clandestine business dealings.

  [^cite_note-62]

- Some organizations assist victims of domestic violence by providing mobile phones for use in emergencies. These are often refurbished phones.

  [^cite_note-63]

- The advent of widespread text-messaging has resulted in the [cell phone novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_phone_novel), the first literary genre to emerge from the cellular age, via [text messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_messaging) to a website that collects the novels as a whole.

  [^cite_note-64]

- [Mobile telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_telephony) also facilitates activism and [citizen journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_journalism).

- The [United Nations](31769.md) reported that mobile phones have spread faster than any other form of technology and can improve the livelihood of the poorest people in developing countries, by providing access to information in places where [landlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landline) or the Internet are not available, especially in the [least developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries). Use of mobile phones also spawns a wealth of micro-enterprises, by providing such work as selling airtime on the streets and repairing or refurbishing handsets.

  [^cite_note-65]

- In [Mali](19127.md) and other African countries, people used to travel from village to village to let friends and relatives know about weddings, births, and other events. This can now be avoided in areas with mobile phone coverage, which are usually more extensive than areas with just land-line penetration.

- The TV industry has recently started using mobile phones to drive live TV viewing through mobile apps, advertising, [social TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_TV), and [mobile TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_TV).

- In some parts of the world, mobile phone sharing is common. Cell phone sharing is prevalent in urban India, as families and groups of friends often share one or more mobile phones among their members. There are obvious economic benefits, but often familial customs and traditional gender roles play a part.

### Content distribution

In 1998, one of the first examples of [distributing and selling media content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_distribution) through the mobile phone was the sale of [ringtones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringtone) by [Radiolinja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiolinja) in Finland. Soon afterwards, other media content appeared, such as news, video games, jokes, horoscopes, TV content and advertising. Most early content for mobile phones tended to be copies of [legacy media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy_media), such as banner advertisements or TV news highlight video clips. Recently, unique content for mobile phones has been emerging, from ringtones and [ringback tones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringback_tones) to [mobisodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobisodes), video content that has been produced exclusively for mobile phones.

### Mobile banking and payment



Mobile payment system

Main articles: [Mobile banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_banking) and [Mobile payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_payment)

See also: [Branchless banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branchless_banking) and [Contactless payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contactless_payment)

In many countries, mobile phones are used to provide [mobile banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_banking) services, which may include the ability to transfer cash payments by secure SMS text message. Kenya's [M-PESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-PESA) mobile banking service, for example, allows customers of the mobile phone operator [Safaricom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safaricom) to hold cash balances which are recorded on their SIM cards. Cash can be deposited or withdrawn from M-PESA accounts at Safaricom retail outlets located throughout the country and can be transferred electronically from person to person and used to pay bills to companies.

[Branchless banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branchless_banking) has also been successful in South Africa and the [Philippines](23440.md). A pilot project in [Bali](4147.md) was launched in 2011 by the [International Finance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Finance_Corporation) and an [Indonesian](14579.md) bank, [Bank Mandiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Mandiri).[^cite_note-69]

Mobile payments were first trialled in Finland in 1998 when two Coca-Cola vending machines in [Espoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espoo) were enabled to work with SMS payments. Eventually, the idea spread and in 1999, the Philippines launched the country's first commercial mobile payments systems with mobile operators [Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Telecom) and [Smart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Communications).

Some mobile phones can make [mobile payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_payment) via direct mobile billing schemes, or through [contactless payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contactless_payment) if the phone and the [point of sale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_sale) support [near field communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_field_communication) (NFC).[^cite_note-70] Enabling contactless payments through NFC-equipped mobile phones requires the co-operation of manufacturers, network operators, and retail merchants.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

### Mobile tracking

See also: [Cellphone surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellphone_surveillance) and [Mobile phone tracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_tracking)

Mobile phones are commonly used to collect location data. While the phone is turned on, the geographical location of a mobile phone can be determined easily (whether it is being used or not) using a technique known as [multilateration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateration) to calculate the differences in time for a signal to travel from the mobile phone to each of several [cell towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_towers) near the owner of the phone.[^cite_note-bbc-phone-locate-73][^cite_note-foxnews-phone-locate-74]

The movements of a mobile phone user can be tracked by their service provider and, if desired, by law enforcement agencies and their governments. Both the [SIM card](197489.md) and the handset can be tracked.[^cite_note-bbc-phone-locate-73]

China has proposed using this technology to track the commuting patterns of Beijing city residents.[^cite_note-75] In the UK and US, law enforcement and intelligence services use mobile phones to perform surveillance operations.[^cite_note-roving-bugs-76]

Hackers have been able to track a phone's location, read messages, and record calls, through obtaining a subscriber's phone number.[^cite_note-77]

### Theft

According to the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission), one out of three robberies involve the theft of a cellular phone. Police data in San Francisco show that half of all robberies in 2012 were thefts of cellular phones. An [online petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_petition) on [Change.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change.org), called *Secure our Smartphones*, urged smartphone manufacturers to install [kill switches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_switch) in their devices to make them unusable if stolen. The petition is part of a joint effort by New York Attorney General [Eric Schneiderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Schneiderman) and San Francisco District Attorney [George Gascón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gasc%C3%B3n) and was directed to the [CEOs](52234.md) of the major smartphone manufacturers and telecommunication carriers.[^cite_note-82] On 10 June 2013, Apple announced that it would install a "[kill switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone_kill_switch)" on its [next iPhone operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_7), due to debut in October 2013.[^cite_note-83]

All mobile phones have a unique identifier called [IMEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mobile_Station_Equipment_Identity). Anyone can report their phone as lost or stolen with their Telecom Carrier, and the IMEI would be blacklisted with a central registry.[^cite_note-84] Telecom carriers, depending upon local regulation can or must implement blocking of blacklisted phones in their network. There are, however, a number of ways to circumvent a blacklist. One method is to send the phone to a country where the telecom carriers are not required to implement the blacklisting and sell it there,[^cite_note-85] another involves altering the phone's IMEI number.[^cite_note-86] Even so, mobile phones typically have less value on the second-hand market if the phones original IMEI is blacklisted.

### Conflict minerals

Main article: [Conflict minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_minerals)

Demand for metals used in mobile phones and other electronics fuelled the [Second Congo War](877492.md), which claimed almost 5.5 million lives.[^cite_note-87] In a 2012 news story, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported: "In unsafe mines deep underground in eastern Congo, [children are working](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_labour) to extract minerals essential for the electronics industry. The profits from the minerals finance the bloodiest conflict since the second world war; the war has lasted nearly 20 years and has recently flared up again. For the last 15 years, the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) has been a major source of natural resources for the mobile phone industry."[^cite_note-88] The company [Fairphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairphone) has worked to develop a mobile phone that does not contain [conflict minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_resource).

### Kosher phones

Due to concerns by the [Orthodox Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Jewish) rabbinate in Britain that texting by youths could waste time and lead to "immodest" communication, the rabbinate recommended that phones with text-messaging capability not be used by children; to address this, they gave their official approval to a brand of "Kosher" phones with no texting capabilities. Although these phones are intended to prevent [immodesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immodesty#Judaism), some vendors report good sales to adults who prefer the simplicity of the devices; other Orthodox Jews question the need for them.[^cite_note-89]

In Israel, similar phones to kosher phones with restricted features exist to observe the [sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabbat); under Orthodox Judaism, the use of any electrical device is generally prohibited during this time, other than to save lives, or reduce the risk of death or similar needs. Such phones are approved for use by essential workers, such as health, security, and public service workers.[^cite_note-kosher-90]

### Restrictions

Restrictions on the use of mobile phones are applied in a number of different contexts, often with the goal of health, safety, security or proper functioning of an establishment, or as a matter of etiquette. Such contexts include:

#### While driving

Main articles: [Mobile phones and driving safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phones_and_driving_safety) and [Texting while driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texting_while_driving)



A driver using two handheld mobile phones at once



A sign in the US restricting cell phone use to certain times of day (no cell phone use between 7\:30–9\:00 am and 2\:00–4\:15 pm)

Mobile phone use while driving, including talking on the phone, texting, or operating other phone features, is common but controversial. It is widely considered dangerous due to [distracted driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distracted_driving). Being distracted while operating a motor vehicle has been shown to increase the risk of accidents. In September 2010, the US [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration](989530.md) (NHTSA) reported that 995 people were killed by [drivers distracted by cell phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restrictions_on_cell_phone_use_by_U.S._drivers). In March 2011, a US insurance company, [State Farm Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Farm_Insurance), announced the results of a study which showed 19% of drivers surveyed accessed the Internet on a smartphone while driving.[^cite_note-post-91] Many jurisdictions prohibit the use of mobile phones while driving. In Egypt, Israel, Japan, Portugal, and Singapore, both handheld and hands-free use of a mobile phone (which uses a [speakerphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speakerphone)) is banned. In other countries, including the UK and France and in many [US states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restrictions_on_cell_phone_use_by_US_drivers), only handheld phone use is banned while hands-free use is permitted.

A 2011 study reported that over 90% of college students surveyed text (initiate, reply or read) while driving.[^cite_note-92] The scientific literature on the dangers of driving while sending a text message from a mobile phone, or *[texting while driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texting_while_driving)*, is limited. A simulation study at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah) found a sixfold increase in distraction-related accidents when texting.[^cite_note-93]

Due to the increasing complexity of mobile phones, they are often more like mobile computers in their available uses. This has introduced additional difficulties for law enforcement officials when attempting to distinguish one usage from another in drivers using their devices. This is more apparent in countries which ban both handheld and hands-free usage, rather than those which ban handheld use only, as officials cannot easily tell which function of the mobile phone is being used simply by looking at the driver. This can lead to drivers being stopped for using their device illegally for a phone call when, in fact, they were using the device legally, for example, when using the phone's incorporated controls for car stereo, [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS) or [satnav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satnav).

A 2010 study reviewed the incidence of mobile phone use while [cycling](5931.md) and its effects on behaviour and safety.[^cite_note-94] In 2013, a national survey in the US reported the number of drivers who reported using their cellphones to access the Internet while driving had risen to nearly one of four.[^cite_note-95] A study conducted by the University of Vienna examined approaches for reducing inappropriate and problematic use of mobile phones, such as using mobile phones while driving.[^cite_note-96]

Accidents involving a driver being distracted by talking on a mobile phone have begun to be prosecuted as negligence similar to speeding. In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), from 27 February 2007, motorists who are caught using a hand-held mobile phone while driving will have three penalty points added to their license in addition to the fine of £60.[^cite_note-BBC-97] This increase was introduced to try to stem the increase in drivers ignoring the law.[^cite_note-BBC2-98] [Japan](15573.md) prohibits all mobile phone use while driving, including use of hands-free devices. New Zealand has banned hand-held cell phone use since 1 November 2009. Many states in the United States have banned texting on cell phones while driving. Illinois became the 17th American state to enforce this law.[^cite_note-CNN-99] As of July 2010, 30 states had banned texting while driving, with Kentucky becoming the most recent addition on 15 July.[^cite_note-Courier-Journal-100]

[Public Health Law Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health_law) maintains a list of distracted driving laws in the United States. This database of laws provides a comprehensive view of the provisions of laws that restrict the use of mobile communication devices while driving for all 50 states and the District of Columbia between 1992 when first law was passed, through 1 December 2010. The dataset contains information on 22 dichotomous, continuous or [categorical variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_variable) including, for example, activities regulated (e.g., texting versus talking, hands-free versus handheld), targeted populations, and exemptions.[^cite_note-101]

#### On aircraft

This section is an excerpt from [Mobile phones on aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phones_on_aircraft).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mobile_phones_on_aircraft\&action=edit)]

In the U.S., the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) (FCC) regulations prohibit the use of [mobile phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phones) aboard aircraft in flight.[^cite_note-Mobile_phones_on_aircraft_FCC_Cellban01-102] Contrary to popular misconception, the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) does not actually prohibit the use of personal electronic devices (including cell phones) on aircraft. Paragraph (b)(5) of 14 CFR 91.21 permits airlines to determine if devices can be used in flight, allowing use of "any other portable electronic device that the operator of the aircraft has determined will not cause interference with the navigation or communication system of the aircraft on which it is to be used."[^cite_note-Mobile_phones_on_aircraft_FAR9121-103]

In Europe, regulations and technology have allowed the limited introduction of the use of passenger mobile phones on some commercial flights, and elsewhere in the world many [airlines](1942.md) are moving towards allowing mobile phone use in flight.[^cite_note-Mobile_phones_on_aircraft_OnAirAirfax-104] Many airlines still do not allow the use of mobile phones on aircraft.[^cite_note-Mobile_phones_on_aircraft_ABCNews2007-105] Those that do often ban the use of mobile phones during take-off and landing.

Many passengers are pressing airlines and their governments to allow and deregulate mobile phone use, while some airlines, under the pressure of competition, are also pushing for deregulation or seeking new technology which could solve the present problems.[^cite_note-Mobile_phones_on_aircraft_CNN-106] Official aviation agencies and safety boards are resisting any relaxation of the present safety rules unless and until it can be conclusively shown that it would be safe to do so. There are both technical and social factors which make the issues more complex than a simple discussion of safety versus hazard.[^cite_note-Mobile_phones_on_aircraft_USATodayCingular-107]

See also: [Airplane mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane_mode)

#### While walking

Main article: [Smartphones and pedestrian safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphones_and_pedestrian_safety)



A pedestrian looking down at a smartphone while crossing a road

Between 2011 and 2019, an estimated 30,000 walking injuries occurred in the US related to using a cellphone, leading to some jurisdictions attempting to ban pedestrians from using their cellphones.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] Other countries, such as China and the Netherlands, have introduced special lanes for smartphone users to help direct and manage them.[^cite_note-G14-111][^cite_note-DT15-112]

#### In prisons

This section is an excerpt from [Mobile phones in prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phones_in_prison).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mobile_phones_in_prison\&action=edit)]

In most jurisdictions, [prison](19008450.md) [inmates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inmate) are forbidden from possessing mobile phones due to their ability to communicate with the outside world and other security issues. Mobile phones are one of the most [smuggled](28645.md) items into prisons. They provide inmates the ability to make and receive unauthorized phone calls, send [email](9738.md) and [text messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_messaging), use [social media](5897742.md), and follow news pertaining to their case, among other forbidden uses.[^cite_note-Mobile_phones_in_prison_CBS-113][^cite_note-114]

#### In hospitals

As of 2007, some hospitals had banned mobile devices due to a [common misconception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_common_misconceptions) that their use would create significant [electromagnetic interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_interference).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

#### Health effects

Main article: [Mobile phone radiation and health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_radiation_and_health)

See also: [Nomophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomophobia) and [Mobile phone overuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_overuse)

[Screen time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_time), the amount of time using a device with a screen, has become an issue for mobile phones since the adaptation of smartphones.[^cite_note-117] Research is being conducted to show the correlation between screen time and the mental and physical harm in child development.[^cite_note-Stiglic_e023191-118] To prevent harm, some parents and even governments have placed restrictions on its usage.[^cite_note-Locker-2015-119][^cite_note-Radesky-2016-120]

There have been rumors that mobile phone use can cause cancer, but this is a myth.[^cite_note-cruk-121][^cite_note-122]

While there are rumors of mobile phones causing cancer, there was a study conducted by International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) that stated the there could be an increase risk of brain tumors with the use of smartphones, this is not confirmed. They also stated that with the lack of data for the research and the usage periods of 15 years will warrant further research for smartphones and the cause of brain tumors.[^cite_note-123]

#### Educational impact

See also: [Mobile phone use in schools](49478366.md)

A study by the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) found that banning mobile phones in schools could increase pupils' academic performance, providing benefits equal to one extra week of schooling per year.[^cite_note-124]

## Culture and popularity

See also: [SMS language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_language) and [Xelibri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xelibri)



A young woman speaking on a mobile phone in [Copenhagen](5166.md), 2025

Mobile phones are considered an important human invention as it has been one of the most widely used and sold pieces of consumer technology.[^cite_note-Harris-2019-10][^cite_note-Gupta-2011-12] They have also become culturally symbolic. In [Japanese mobile phone culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_mobile_phone_culture) for example, mobile phones are often decorated with charms. They have also become fashion symbols at times.[^cite_note-125] The [Motorola Razr V3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola_Razr_V3) and [LG Chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Chocolate) are two examples of devices that were popular for being fashionable while not necessarily focusing on the original purpose of mobile phones, i.e. a device to provide mobile telephony.[^cite_note-126]

Some have also suggested that mobile phones or smartphones are a [status symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_symbol).[^cite_note-127] For example, a research paper suggested that owning specifically an [Apple iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_iPhone) was seen to be a status symbol.[^cite_note-128]

[Text messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_messaging), which are performed on mobile phones, has also led to the creation of '[SMS language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_language)'. It also led to the growing popularity of [emojis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emoji).[^cite_note-129]
