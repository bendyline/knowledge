The **United States Armed Forces** are the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) forces of the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-14] [United States federal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Code) establishes six armed forces: the [Army](32087.md), [Marine Corps](17349325.md), [Navy](20518076.md), [Air Force](32090.md), [Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force), and [Coast Guard](32223.md), each assigned specific roles and operational domains.[^cite_note-auto1-15][^cite_note-auto5-16][^cite_note-17] With the exception of the Coast Guard, which operates under the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) in peacetime, the services are organized under the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense) (DoD).

Established during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), the Army and the Navy, and later the other services, have played a decisive role in [the country's history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States). They contributed to early national consolidation through conflicts such as the [Barbary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Wars) and the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), shaped the [country's territorial evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_the_United_States), and were involved in global conflicts including [World War I](4764461.md), [World War II](32927.md), the [Cold War](325329.md), and the [war on terror](13425800.md).[^cite_note-18] The [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947) reorganized the military establishment by creating the DoD, the Air Force, and the [National Security Council](44026.md); in 1949, an amendment to the act merged the cabinet-level departments of the Army, Navy, and Air Force into the DoD.[^cite_note-19] formalizing a unified defense structure under civilian control. The Space Force was established in 2019 as the newest branch.

The [president of the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_U.S.) serves as [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief_of_the_United_States) and exercises authority over the armed forces through the DoD and, for the Coast Guard in peacetime, DHS. Since 1973, the United States has maintained an all-volunteer force, although the [Selective Service System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_Service_System) remains authorized to [conscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_the_United_States) most male citizens and residents aged 18 and 25 to register. All six services are also among the eight [uniformed services of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformed_services_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-20] The armed forces consist of active-duty personnel, Reserve components, and the [National Guard](37988.md).

The U.S. Armed Forces are widely considered the world's most powerful[^cite_note-CreditSuisse2015-21][^cite_note-22] and most advanced[^cite_note-23] military. The [military expenditure of the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_the_United_States) was US \$957 billion in 2025, the highest in the world, accounting for [33% of the world's military expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures).***[^cite_note-SIPRI-2020-24]*** The personnel size of the six armed forces together ranks them among the world's [largest state armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_military_and_paramilitary_personnel). The U.S. Armed Forces has significant capabilities in both defense and [power projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) due to its large budget, resulting in advanced and powerful technologies which enable widespread deployment of the force globally, including around 800 military bases around the world.[^cite_note-25]

## History

Main articles: [Military history of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_States), [History of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Army), [Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Marine_Corps), [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Navy), [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Air_Force), [Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Space_Force), and [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Coast_Guard)

The history of the U.S. Armed Forces dates back to 14 June 1775, with the creation of the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army), even before the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) marked the establishment of the United States.[^cite_note-\:4-26] The [Continental Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Navy), established on 13 October 1775, and [Continental Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Marines), established on 10 November 1775, were created in close succession by the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress) in order to defend the new nation against the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War).[^cite_note-\:5-27][^cite_note-28]

These forces demobilized in 1784 after the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)) ended the Revolutionary War. The [Congress of the Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Confederation) created the current [United States Army](32087.md) on 3 June 1784.[^cite_note-\:4-26] The [United States Congress](31756.md) created the current [United States Navy](20518076.md) on 27 March 1794 and the current [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) on 11 July 1798.[^cite_note-\:5-27][^cite_note-29] All three services trace their origins to their respective Continental predecessors.

The 1787 adoption of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution) gave [Congress](31756.md) the power to "raise and support armies," to "provide and maintain a navy", and to "make rules for the government and regulation of the land and naval forces", as well as the power to [declare war](455614.md). The president of the United States is the United States Armed Forces' [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief).[^cite_note-30]

The [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) traces its origin to the formation of the [Revenue Cutter Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_Cutter_Service) on 4 August 1790, which merged with the [United States Life-Saving Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Life-Saving_Service) on 28 January 1915 to establish the Coast Guard.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

The [United States Air Force](32090.md) was established as an independent service on 18 September 1947; it traces its origin to the formation of the [Aeronautical Division, U.S. Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_Division,_U.S._Signal_Corps), which was formed 1 August 1907 and was part of the [Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) before being recognized as an independent service in the National Security Act of 1947.[^cite_note-33]

The [United States Public Health Service Commissioned Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Public_Health_Service_Commissioned_Corps) was formerly considered to be a branch of the United States Armed Forces from 29 July 1945 until 3 July 1952, and is now one of the eight uniformed services of the United States.[^cite_note-34] Should it be called into active duty again, it would constitute a seventh branch of the Armed Forces.[^cite_note-crs-35]

The [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) was established as an independent service on 20 December 2019. It is the sixth branch of the U.S. military and the first new branch in 72 years.[^cite_note-36] The origin of the Space Force can be traced back to the [Air Force Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Space_Command), which was formed 1 September 1982 and was a [major command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_major_commands_of_the_United_States_Air_Force) of the United States Air Force.[^cite_note-37]

The U.S. Congressional Research Office annually publishes a *[List of Notable Deployments of U.S. Military Forces Overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Notable_Deployments_of_U.S._Military_Forces_Overseas)* since 1798.[^cite_note-38]

## Structure

Presidential command over the U.S. Armed Forces is established in [Article II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_Two_of_the_United_States_Constitution) in the Constitution whereby the president is named as the "[Commander in Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief_of_the_United_States) of the Army and Navy of the United States, and of the Militia of the several States, when called into the actual Service of the United States."[^cite_note-39]

The United States Armed Forces are split between two cabinet departments, with the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) serving as the primary cabinet department for military affairs and the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) responsible for administering the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md).[^cite_note-40]

The military chain of command flows from the President of the United States to the secretary of defense (for services under the Defense Department) or secretary of homeland security (for services under the Department of Homeland Security), ensuring [civilian control of the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military).

Within the Department of Defense, the military departments ([Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army), [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), and [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force)) are civilian led entities that oversee the coequal military service branches organized within each department. The military departments and services are responsible for organizing, training, and equipping forces, with the actual chain of command flowing through the [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command).[^cite_note-41]

### Joint Chiefs of Staff



The Joint Chiefs of Staff in January 2026

The [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff), although outside the operational chain of command, is the senior-most military body of the Department of Defense. It is led by the [Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md), who is the principal advisor to the president and secretary of defense on military matters. Their deputy is the [Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff). Both the Chairman and Vice Chairman are barred from exercising military command over the Joint Chiefs of Staff or any of the armed forces with the exception of the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff#Joint_Staff).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

Other members include the [chief of staff of the Army](485507.md), [commandant of the Marine Corps](7708.md), [chief of naval operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_naval_operations), [chief of staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force), [chief of space operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Space_Operations), and the [chief of the National Guard Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_National_Guard_Bureau).[^cite_note-45]

The [commandant of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant_of_the_Coast_Guard) is not an official member of the Joint Chiefs, but sometimes attends meetings as one of the military service chiefs.

The [senior enlisted advisor to the chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Enlisted_Advisor_to_the_Chairman) is the most senior non-commissioned officer (NCO) in the United States Armed Forces and is a unique enlisted rank which only one individual may hold it. Is the primary enlisted advisor to the chairman, and serves at the pleasure of the secretary of defense.[^cite_note-46]

The president of the United States, the [secretary of defense](43998.md), the [secretary of homeland security](212926.md) and [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md) are members of the [United States National Security Council](44026.md), which advises the president on national security, military, and foreign policy matters.[^cite_note-47]

The [national security advisor](44028.md), the [homeland security advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Advisor), and the [deputy national security advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_National_Security_Advisor_\(United_States\)) may also be members of the United States Armed Forces.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

The [National Security Council Deputies Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_Deputies_Committee) also includes the [deputy secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Defense), [deputy secretary of homeland security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Homeland_Security), and [vice chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff).[^cite_note-50]

Military leadership, including the secretary of defense, the secretary of Homeland Security, and chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff also sit on the [National Space Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Space_Council).[^cite_note-51]

### Unified combatant commands

Main article: [Unified combatant command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command)

Unified combatant commands are joint military commands consisting of forces from multiple military departments, with their chain of command flowing from the president, to the secretary of defense, to the commanders of the combatant commands.

Each service organizes, trains, and equips forces that are then presented to the unified combatant commands through service component commands. Special Operations Command and Cyber Command also present theater special operations commands or joint force headquarters – cyber to other combatant commanders.

Army components are typically dual-hatted as the [joint force land component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Force_Land_Component_Commander), Navy components are typically dual-hatted as the [joint force maritime component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Force_Maritime_Component_Commander), and Air Force components are typically dual-hatted as the [joint force air component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Force_Air_Component_Commander), with the theater special operations command dual-hatted as the joint force special operations component, and Space Force component typically dual-hatted as the joint force space component.[^cite_note-52]

### Combat support agencies

Main article: [combat support agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_support_agency)

[Combat support agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_support_agency) are Department of Defense agencies with combat support missions that service operating forces planning or conducting military operations. This includes support during conflict or in the conduct of other military activities related to countering threats to U.S. national security. This mission is focused on providing support to echelons at the CCMD level and below and may not encompass the full scope of the CSA's mission.[^cite_note-54]

## Service branches

The United States Armed Forces is composed of six coequal military service branches. Five of the branches, the [United States Army](32087.md), [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md), [United States Navy](20518076.md), [United States Air Force](32090.md), and [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force), are part of the Department of Defense.

The [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) is normally under the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md), but may be transferred to the Department of Defense's Department of the Navy (which is the civilian entity that oversees the coequal U.S. Marine Corps and U.S. Navy) at the direction of the President or Congress.

With the exception of the Coast Guard, the military services only organize, train, and equip forces. The unified combatant commands are responsible for operational control of non-service retained forces.

Each of the different military services is assigned a role and domain. The U.S. Army conducts land operations, while the U.S. Navy and U.S. Marine Corps conduct maritime operations, with the Marine Corps specializing in amphibious and maritime littoral operations in support of the Navy. The U.S. Air Force conducts air operations, while the U.S. Space Force conducts space operations.

The U.S. Coast Guard is unique in that it is a military branch specializing in maritime operations and also a law enforcement agency.[^cite_note-auto1-15][^cite_note-auto5-16]

### U.S. Army

Main article: [United States Army](32087.md)



[1st Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Armored_Division_\(United_States\)) infantry, an [M2 Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_Bradley) armored vehicle, and [AH-64 Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-64_Apache) attack helicopters as part of [Combined Joint Task Force – Operation Inherent Resolve](44618350.md) in Syria

The [United States Army](32087.md) (USA) is the United States Armed Forces' land force and is the largest and oldest service. Originally established in 1775 as the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army), it consists of one million soldiers across the Regular Army, [Army Reserve](1187028.md), and [Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_National_Guard).[^cite_note-auto1-15] The Army serves as the Armed Forces principal land service, responsible for conducting [land warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare) operations.[^cite_note-64]

The U.S. Army is organized under the [Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army), which is a military department under the leadership of the [secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Army) and [under secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Under_Secretary_of_the_Army).

The U.S. Army itself is led by the [chief of staff of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_staff_of_the_United_States_Army) and [vice chief of staff of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_chief_of_staff_of_the_United_States_Army), both [generals](2965165.md) who are advised by the [sergeant major of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_major_of_the_Army).[^cite_note-65]

The Army's primary responsibility is to conduct prompt and sustained land combat as part of the joint force. Army landpower focuses on destroying an enemy's armed forces, occupying its territory, and breaking the will of an adversary.[^cite_note-auto-66]

The five core competencies of the Army are:

- Prompt and sustained land combat

- Combined arms operations:&#x20;

  - Combined arms maneuver and wide area security

  - Armored and mechanized operations

  - Airborne and air assault operations

- Special operations

- Set and sustain the theater for the joint force

- Integrate national, multinational, and joint power on land

  [^cite_note-auto-66]



[82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division) paratroopers parachuting from a [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force) [C-17 Globemaster III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17_Globemaster_III) transport plane

The thirteen specified functions of the Army are:

1. Conduct prompt and sustained [combined arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_arms) combat operations on land in all environments and types of terrain, to include complex urban environments, in order to defeat enemy ground forces, and seize, occupy, and defend land areas.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

2. Conduct [air](146640.md) and [missile defense](1099196.md) to support joint campaigns and assist in achieving [air superiority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority). This is conducted by the Army's [Air Defense Artillery Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Defense_Artillery_Branch), specifically by the [10th Army Air and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Army_Air_and_Missile_Defense_Command), [32nd Army Air and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Army_Air_and_Missile_Defense_Command), [94th Army Air and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_Army_Air_and_Missile_Defense_Command), [263rd Army Air and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/263rd_Army_Air_and_Missile_Defense_Command), and [Army Space and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Space_and_Missile_Defense_Command).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

3. Conduct [airborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_forces) and [air assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_assault), and [amphibious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) operations. The Army has primary responsibility for the development of airborne doctrine, tactics, techniques, and procedures. Army airborne and air assault operations are conducted by the [XVIII Airborne Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVIII_Airborne_Corps), [11th Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airborne_Division), [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division), [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division), and the [173rd Airborne Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/173rd_Airborne_Brigade).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

4. Conduct [civil affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_affairs) operations. Civil affairs operations are conducted by the [United States Army Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Operations_Command), predominantly under [United States Army Civil Affairs and Psychological Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Civil_Affairs_and_Psychological_Operations_Command) and the [95th Civil Affairs Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Civil_Affairs_Brigade).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

5. Conduct [riverine operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverine_warfare).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

6. [Occupy](816214.md) territories abroad and provide for the initial establishment of a [military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_government) pending transfer of this responsibility to other authorities.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

7. [Interdict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdiction) enemy [sea power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_power), [space power](956982.md), [air power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airpower), and [communications](82272.md) through operations on and from the land.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

8. Provide logistics to joint operations and campaigns, including joint over-the-shore and intra-theater transport of time-sensitive, mission-critical personnel and materiel. This is primarily conducted through the [Army Logistics Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Logistics_Branch), including the [Quartermaster Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Quartermaster_Corps), [Ordnance Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Ordnance_Corps), [Transportation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Corps), and through [Army Materiel Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Materiel_Command)'s [Military Surface Deployment and Distribution Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Surface_Deployment_and_Distribution_Command).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

9. Provide support to [space operations](956982.md) to enhance joint campaigns, in coordination with the other military services (primarily the [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force)), combatant commands (primarily [United States Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Command)), and other U.S. government departments and agencies. Army space operations are conducted by [Army Space and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Space_and_Missile_Defense_Command).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

10. Conduct authorized [civil works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_works) programs, to include projects for the improvement of [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation), [flood control](20913655.md), [beach erosion control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion), and other water resource developments in the United States, its territories, and its possessions, and conduct other civil activities prescribed by law. These are conducted by the [Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers).

    [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

11. Provide intra-theater [aeromedical evacuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeromedical_evacuation). These missions are flown by the [Army Medical Service Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Medical_Department_\(United_States\)) and [Army Aviation Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Aviation_Branch).

    [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

12. Conduct [reconnaissance](146911.md), [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance), and [target acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_acquisition).

13. Operate [land lines of communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_communication). This is primarily conducted through the [Transportation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Corps) and [Military Surface Deployment and Distribution Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Surface_Deployment_and_Distribution_Command).

    [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

#### Infantry

Main article: [Infantry Branch (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Branch_\(United_States\))



U.S. Army [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Rangers) with the [75th Ranger Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Ranger_Regiment) prepare for extraction on a [MH-47 Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-47_Chinook)

The Infantry Branch forms the core of the service's land combat power. U.S. Army infantry are generally equipped with the [M4 carbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_carbine) and [M249 light machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M249_light_machine_gun), which will be replaced by the [XM7 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM7_rifle) and [XM250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM250).[^cite_note-68]

Infantry is a core part of the Army's [Brigade Combat Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_Combat_Team). The most numerous variant, the Infantry Brigade Combat Team, comprises [light infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_infantry) battalions who fight on foot.

Infantry Brigade Combat Teams of the [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division) are [air assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_assault) capable, with infantry soldiers being transported by U.S. Army Aviation [UH-60 Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Black_Hawk) and [CH-47 Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-47_Chinook) helicopters.[^cite_note-69]

Infantry Brigade Combat Teams of the [11th Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airborne_Division), [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division), and [173rd Airborne Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/173rd_Airborne_Brigade) are capable of [airborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_forces) operations, in cooperation with the U.S. Air Force's transport aircraft.[^cite_note-70]

Finally, Infantry Brigade Combat Teams assigned to the [10th Mountain Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Mountain_Division) specialize in [mountain warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_warfare).[^cite_note-71] Standard Infantry Brigade Combat Teams are assigned to the [25th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), which offers additional training in [jungle warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_warfare).[^cite_note-72]

Armored Brigade Combat Teams comprise [mechanized infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanized_infantry) battalions mounted in the [M2 Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_Bradley) [infantry fighting vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle).Divisions with Armored Brigade Combat Teams include the [1st Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [3rd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [4th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [1st Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), and [1st Cavalry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Cavalry_Division_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-auto14-73]

Stryker Brigade Combat Teams are centered around Stryker infantry battalions operating out of the [Stryker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryker).Divisions with Stryker Brigade Combat Teams include the [2nd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [4th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [11th Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airborne_Division), [2nd Cavalry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Cavalry_Regiment_\(United_States\)), and [3rd Cavalry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Cavalry_Regiment_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-auto14-73]

[United States Army Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Rangers) with the [75th Ranger Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Ranger_Regiment) are an elite [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations) infantry force in the [United States Army Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Operations_Command), specializing in air assault and airborne infiltration methods. The three primary missions of the 75th Ranger Regiment are [special operations raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action_\(military\)), forcible entry operations, such as an airfield seizure to enable the Air Force to bring in more forces, and [special reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_reconnaissance).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] As a special operations force, Army Rangers are generally better equipped than standard infantry, utilizing the [FN SCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_SCAR) rifle.[^cite_note-76]

#### Army Special Forces

Main article: [United States Army Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces)



Soldiers from the [5th Special Forces Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Special_Forces_Group) conduct sensitive site exploitation training

[Army Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces), commonly known as Green Berets after their iconic headgear, are among the most elite soldiers in the Army. Special Forces conduct:[^cite_note-Special_Forces-77]

- [counterinsurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterinsurgency) distinguishing between civilians and enemy combatants while assisting with the stabilization, defense, and training of developing countries facing insurgent threats.

- [direct action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action_\(military\)) seizing, capturing, recovering, or destroying enemy material; or utilizing quick strikes to recover personnel.

- [foreign internal defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_internal_defense) training and equipping foreign allied military forces to defend against insurgency, subversion, terrorism, and other security threats.

- [special reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_reconnaissance) executing surveillance in hostile, denied, or diplomatically or politically sensitive environments to collect or verify information of strategic significance.

- [unconventional warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_warfare_\(United_States\)) enabling a resistance movement or insurgency to coerce, disrupt, or overthrow a government or occupying power by operating through or with an underground force in a denied area.

Army Special Forces are trained in [military free-fall parachuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-altitude_military_parachuting) and [combat diver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_diver) skillsets.[^cite_note-Special_Forces-77] They are considered the most versatile special operations force in the entire world, operating as a multi-purpose force since 1952.[^cite_note-78]

#### Armor and Cavalry

Main article: [Armor Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armor_Branch)



[1st Cavalry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Cavalry_Division_\(United_States\)) [M1 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_Abrams) tanks during a training exercise in Germany

The Armor Branch traces its history back to the [United States Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cavalry) and are responsible for tank and cavalry reconnaissance operations.[^cite_note-79]

The U.S. Army fields the [M1 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_Abrams) [main battle tank](26595097.md) in Armored Battalions as part of Armored Brigade Combat Teams across the [1st Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [1st Cavalry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Cavalry_Division_\(United_States\)), [1st Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [3rd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), and the [4th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)).Each Armored Brigade Combat Team also possesses a cavalry squadron equipped with [M2 Bradleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_Bradley) for scouting and security.

Stryker Brigade Combat Teams from the [2nd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [4th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [11th Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airborne_Division), [2nd Cavalry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Cavalry_Regiment_\(United_States\)), and [3rd Cavalry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Cavalry_Regiment_\(United_States\)) have a cavalry squadron equipped with [Strykers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryker).

Infantry Brigade Combat Teams from the [10th Mountain Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Mountain_Division), [11th Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airborne_Division), [25th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division), [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division), and [173rd Airborne Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/173rd_Airborne_Brigade) have a cavalry squadron equipped with the [Joint Light Tactical Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Light_Tactical_Vehicle).[^cite_note-80]

#### Field Artillery

Main article: [Field Artillery Branch (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Artillery_Branch_\(United_States\))



[M142 HIMARS](147011.md) from the [5th Battalion, 3rd Field Artillery Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Field_Artillery_Regiment_\(United_States\))

The Field Artillery's mission is to destroy, suppress or neutralize the enemy by cannon, rocket or missile fire.[^cite_note-81]

Rocket systems include the [M142 HIMARS](147011.md) and [M270 multiple launch rocket system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_multiple_launch_rocket_system), which are corps-level assets found in field artillery brigades.

Towed artillery includes the [M119 howitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M119_howitzer) in infantry brigade combat teams and the [M777 howitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M777_howitzer) found in both infantry and Stryker brigade combat teams.

The [M109 self-propelled howitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M109_howitzer) is utilized in armored brigade combat teams.[^cite_note-82]

During the Cold War, Army field artillery was responsible for the service's ballistic missile programs, including the [PGM-11 Redstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGM-11_Redstone), which was the first large ballistic missile in the U.S. arsenal, the [MGM-31 Pershing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM-31_Pershing), and the [Pershing II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pershing_II).[^cite_note-83]

In 2023, the Army is intending to field the [Long-Range Hypersonic Weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-Range_Hypersonic_Weapon) and has reestablished larger artillery formations like the [56th Artillery Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/56th_Artillery_Command).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

#### Air Defense Artillery

Main article: [Air Defense Artillery Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Defense_Artillery_Branch)



An [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) missile battery in [Alaska](624.md) operated by the [11th Air Defense Artillery Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Air_Defense_Artillery_Brigade_\(United_States\))

The Air Defense Artillery is responsible for defending geopolitical assets and providing maneuver forces with the freedom to move on the battlefield by deterring the enemy and destroying aerial threats, missile attacks, and surveillance platforms.[^cite_note-auto9-87]

Weapons employed by Air Defense Artillery include the [FIM-92 Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger) [man-portable air-defense system](1389714.md), [AN/TWQ-1 Avenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/TWQ-1_Avenger) for [short range air defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_range_air_defense), and the [counter rocket, artillery, and mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_rocket,_artillery,_and_mortar) 20mm gun system.

The [Iron Dome](18342427.md) provides air defense against rockets, artillery, mortars, missiles, and unmanned aerial vehicles. The [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) is capable of defeating a wide range of threats including aircraft, helicopters, UAVs, ballistic and cruise missiles, and Weapons of Mass Destruction.[^cite_note-auto9-87]

The [Terminal High Altitude Area Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_High_Altitude_Area_Defense) protects strategic critical assets by conducting long-range endo-and-exo-atmospheric engagements of ballistic missiles using the world's largest air-transportable X-band radar.

The [Ground-Based Midcourse Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-Based_Midcourse_Defense) is an [anti-ballistic missile](1791.md) system operated by [Army Space and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Space_and_Missile_Defense_Command) to defend the United States homeland against an intercontinental ballistic missile attack.[^cite_note-auto9-87]

Major Air Defense Artillery units include the [32nd Army Air and Missile Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Army_Air_and_Missile_Defense_Command) and Army Space and Missile Defense Command's [100th Missile Defense Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100th_Missile_Defense_Brigade).[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

Air Defense Artillery has an extremely close relationship with the Air Force through its Air and Missile Defense Commands and the Space Force through Army Space and Missile Defense Command, given their shared missile defense and space roles.

In 1962, Air Defense Artillery achieved the first intercept of a ballistic missile with a nuclear-tipped [Nike Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Zeus) and operated the Nike Zeus as an [anti-satellite weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_weapon) after completing a successful intercept in 1963.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

#### Army Aviation

Main article: [United States Army Aviation Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Aviation_Branch)



Formation of [UH-60 Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Blackhawk) with the [1st Cavalry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Cavalry_Division_\(United_States\))

Army Aviation, distinct from the U.S. Air Force and its predecessors, began as part of the field artillery in 1942. Small spotter planes were used to spot for artillery and naval bombardment, as well as to perform observation.

These few aircraft formed the core of Army Aviation once the U.S. Air Force gained independence. In 1983, the Army created the Aviation Branch, for the first time since the Air Force's independence consolidating aviation under a single organization.[^cite_note-92]

The mission of Army Aviation is to find, fix and destroy any enemy through fire and maneuver and to provide combat support and combat service support in coordinated operations as an integral member of the combined arms team.[^cite_note-93]

Major aircraft include the [AH-64 Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-64_Apache), which serves as the Army's attack helicopter, the [UH-60 Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Black_Hawk), and the [CH-47 Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-47_Chinook) for troop and cargo transport. Army Aviation also flies the [MQ-1C Gray Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-1C_Gray_Eagle) drone.[^cite_note-94]

A specialized unit within Army Aviation, the [160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment (Airborne)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/160th_Special_Operations_Aviation_Regiment_\(Airborne\)) serves as a special operations unit and operates modified variants of the MH-60 Black Hawk, MH-47 Chinook, and the [MH-6 Little Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-6_Little_Bird).[^cite_note-95]

### U.S. Marine Corps

Main article: [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md)



[24th Marine Expeditionary Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24th_Marine_Expeditionary_Unit) Marines during a simulated amphibious assault operation

The [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) (USMC) serves as the U.S. Armed Forces' naval land force, responsible for executing [amphibious warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) and operating in the maritime littorals in support of the U.S. Navy. Originally established in 1775 as the [Continental Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Marines), the Marine Corps consists of the Regular Marine Corps and the [Marine Corps Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Reserve).[^cite_note-auto3-101]

The Marine Corps maintains a very close relationship with the U.S. Navy, its sister service in the Department of the Navy. Although the Marine Corps has previously operated as an independent land force alongside the Army, its primary purpose is to serve as part of a unified naval service alongside the Navy in the maritime domain.[^cite_note-auto4-102]

The U.S. Marine Corps is organized under the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), which is a military department under the leadership of the [secretary of the Navy](44001.md) and the [under secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Under_Secretary_of_the_Navy).

The U.S. Marine Corps itself is led by the [commandant of the Marine Corps](7708.md) and the [assistant commandant of the Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Commandant_of_the_Marine_Corps), both [generals](2965165.md) who are advised by the [sergeant major of the Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_Major_of_the_Marine_Corps).[^cite_note-auto3-101]

The Marine Corps statutory mission is outlined in [10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 5063](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/5063) and as originally introduced under the [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947), with its three primary areas of responsibility including:

- Seizure or defense of advanced naval bases and other land operations to support [naval campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare);

- Development of tactics, technique, and equipment used by amphibious landing forces in coordination with the [Army](32087.md) and [Air Force](32090.md); and

- Such other duties as the [president](24113.md) or [Department of Defense](7279897.md) may direct.



A [15th Marine Expeditionary Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Marine_Expeditionary_Unit) [AH-1Z Viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-1Z_Viper) flying over the [USS *Anchorage* (LPD-23)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Anchorage_\(LPD-23\))

The seven specified functions of the Marine Corps are:

1. Seize and defend advanced [naval bases](291782.md) or lodgments to facilitate subsequent joint operations.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

2. Provide [close air support](600792.md) for ground forces.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

3. Conduct [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare) and [air operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare) essential to the prosecution of a [naval campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare) or as directed.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

4. Conduct complex [expeditionary operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_warfare) in the urban littorals and other challenging environments.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

5. Conduct [amphibious operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare), including engagement, [crisis response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_response), and [power projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) operations to assure access. The Marine Corps has primary responsibility for the development of amphibious doctrine, tactics, techniques, and equipment.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

6. Conduct [security and stability operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stability_and_support_operations) and assist with the initial establishment of a [military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_government) pending transfer of responsibility to other authorities.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

7. Provide security detachments and units for service on armed vessels of the Navy, provide protection of naval property at naval stations and bases, provide security at designated U.S. [embassies and consulates](8970.md), and perform other such duties as the president or secretary of defense may direct. These additional duties may not detract from or interfere with the operations for which the Marine Corps is primarily organized.

[Marine Corps Security Force Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Security_Force_Regiment) is responsible for supporting the Navy with Marine security operations, while the [Marine Security Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Security_Guard) protects embassies and consulates of the [United States Department of State](31975.md).[^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

#### Fleet Marine Force

Main article: [Fleet Marine Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Marine_Force)



Four Marine Corps [F-35B Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35B_Lightning_II) stealth fighters flying over the [USS *America* (LHA-6)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_America_\(LHA-6\))

The combat power of the Marine Corps is centralized in the [Fleet Marine Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Marine_Force) (FMF), which itself is organized into [Fleet Marine Force Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Marine_Force_Atlantic), to support the U.S. Navy's [Fleet Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fleet_Forces_Command), and [Fleet Marine Force Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Marine_Force_Pacific), which supports the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md)'s [U.S. Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Fleet).[^cite_note-auto4-102]

The basic Marine Corps unit for conducting operations is the [Marine Air-Ground Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Air-Ground_Task_Force) (MAGTF), which combines Marine Corps and Navy land, air, sea, and cyberspace capabilities into a single command. There are three size variants of a Marine Air-Ground Task Force, but each consists of a [command element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_element_\(United_States_Marine_Corps\)), [ground combat element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_combat_element), [aviation combat element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_combat_element), and [logistics combat element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics_combat_element).[^cite_note-auto7-103]

A Marine [ground combat element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_combat_element) (GCE) is centered around Marine infantry, typically armed with a [M27 Infantry Automatic Rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M27_Infantry_Automatic_Rifle).[^cite_note-104]

Unlike the Army, the Marine Corps does not train its own [combat medics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_medic), relying on the Navy to provide [hospital corpsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_corpsman).[^cite_note-105]

These infantry units are supported by Marine Corps [combat engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_engineer), who conduct engineer reconnaissance, obstacle system emplacement, and breaching operations; and [Marine Corps Force Reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Force_Reconnaissance) teams.

While the Marine Corps no longer operates its own tanks, opting to request support from the Army if needed, it maintains Light Armored Reconnaissance Battalions which operate the [LAV-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAV-25) amphibious armored reconnaissance vehicle.

Assault Amphibian Battalions operate the [Assault Amphibious Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_Amphibious_Vehicle) and [Amphibious Combat Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_Combat_Vehicle), which enable the ground combat element to conduct amphibious landing operations.[^cite_note-106]

Marine Corps artillery operates the [M777 howitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M777_howitzer) and the [M142 HIMARS](147011.md), both supporting the ground combat element and the Navy at sea by striking enemy ships.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]



[MV-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV-22_Osprey) tiltrotor aircraft from [VMX-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMX-22) taking off from the [USS *Wasp* (LHD-1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wasp_\(LHD-1\))

The Marine [aviation combat element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_combat_element) (ACE) is the operational arm of [Marine Corps Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Aviation), working to support the ground combat element. The [F-35B Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35B_Lightning_II) and [AV-8B Harrier II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AV-8B_Harrier_II) are flown off Navy amphibious assault ships, while the [F-35C Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35C_Lightning_II) and [F/A-18 Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18_Hornet) are flown off Navy carriers by Marine Corps pilots.

The Marine Corps also operates the [KC-130J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-130J) to serve as a tanker and tactical airlift platform.

The [UH-1Y Venom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-1Y_Venom) helicopter provides the Marine Corps with light transport and attack, while the [AH-1Z Viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-1Z_Viper) is a dedicated attack helicopter.

Medium-lift squadrons fly the [MV-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV-22_Osprey), while heavy-lift squadrons use the [CH-53K King Stallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-53K_King_Stallion).

The Marine Corps has also begun flying unmanned aerial vehicles, such as the [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md) and [MQ-8 Fire Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-8_Fire_Scout).[^cite_note-109] Notably, the aviation combat element also includes Low-Altitude Air Defense Battalions, which employ the [FIM-92 Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger) surface-to-air missile.[^cite_note-110]

The smallest MAGTF is the [Marine Expeditionary Unit](160354.md) (MEU), which is typically forward deployed on a Navy ship. Commanded by a [colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(United_States\)), a Marine Expeditionary Unit consist of 2,200 marines split across a battalion landing team (ground combat element), a composite helicopter squadron (aviation combat element), and a combat logistics element (logistics element). Marine Expeditionary Units are supplied for 15 days.[^cite_note-auto7-103] Forward deployed Marine Expeditionary Units are often embarked on Navy amphibious assault ships as part of an [amphibious ready group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_ready_group).[^cite_note-111]

The mid-sized MAGTF is the [Marine Expeditionary Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Expeditionary_Brigade) (MEB), which is organized for specific missions. Commanded by a [brigadier general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_general_\(United_States\)), Marine Expeditionary Brigades consist of 4,000 to 16,000 marines across a Regimental-sized ground combat element, a Marine Aircraft Group, and a Combat Logistics Regiment. Marine Expeditionary Brigades are supplied for 30 days and offer increased firepower and airpower over the Marine Expeditionary Unit.[^cite_note-auto7-103]

The largest MAGTF is the [Marine Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Expeditionary_Force) (MEF), which is the primary warfighting force for larger operations. A Marine Expeditionary Force is commanded by a [lieutenant general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_general_\(United_States\)) and consists of 46,000 to 90,000 marines. Currently there are only three Marine Expeditionary Forces, each with its own Marine Division, Marine Aircraft Wing, Marine Logistics Group, and Marine Expeditionary Force Information Group. Marine Expeditionary Forces are supplied for 60 days.[^cite_note-auto7-103] Certain elements are held at the Marine Expeditionary Force level, such as [Force Reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Force_Reconnaissance).

The MEF Information Group (MIG) also provides a number of functions such as the intelligence battalion; [radio battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Battalion) which conducts signals intelligence, electronic warfare, and cyberspace operations; communications battalion; the MEF support battalion; and the [Air Naval Gunfire Liaison Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Naval_Gunfire_Liaison_Company).[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]



[Marine raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Raiders) with the [1st Marine Raider Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Marine_Raider_Battalion_\(MARSOC\)) conducting a [visit, board, search, and seizure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visit,_board,_search,_and_seizure) operation

While not a MAGTF, the Marine Corps has begun to reorganize some of its regiments into Marine Littoral Regiments (MRL), which are similar in size to a Marine Expeditionary Unit. A Marine Littoral Regiment is a self-deployable force, designed to be naval in nature and operate in the littoral region. A Marine Littoral Regiment consists of a littoral combat team, a littoral anti-air battalion, and a combat logistics battalion. Notably, the Marine Littoral Regiment has no aviation combat element, unlike a Marine Expeditionary Unit.[^cite_note-114]

Another Marine Corps element that does not function as part of the MAGTF is the [Marine Raider Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Raider_Regiment), functioning under [United States Marine Forces Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Forces_Special_Operations_Command). [Marine Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Raiders) specialize in direct action, unconventional warfare, maritime interdiction, special reconnaissance, foreign internal defense, counterterrorism, and counterinsurgency missions.[^cite_note-115]

### U.S. Navy

Main article: [United States Navy](20518076.md)



[USS *John C. Stennis* (CVN-74)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_John_C._Stennis) and [USS *Ronald Reagan* (CVN-76)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Ronald_Reagan) conducting dual carrier strike group operations as part of the [United States Seventh Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Seventh_Fleet)

The United States Navy (USN) is the United States Armed Forces' maritime force. Originally established in 1775 as the [Continental Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Navy), the U.S. Navy consists of the Regular Navy and the [Navy Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_Reserve). The Navy is the United States' principal maritime service, responsible for [maritime warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare) operations.[^cite_note-auto6-120]

The U.S. Navy is organized under the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), which is a military department under the leadership of the [secretary of the Navy](44001.md) and the [under secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Under_Secretary_of_the_Navy).

The U.S. Navy itself is led by the [chief of naval operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_naval_operations) and the [vice chief of naval operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_chief_of_naval_operations), both [admirals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(United_States\)) who are advised by the [master chief petty officer of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_chief_petty_officer_of_the_Navy).[^cite_note-auto6-120]

The five enduring functions of the Navy are:[^cite_note-121]

- [Sea control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_control)

- [Power projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection)

- [Deterrence](735661.md)

- [Maritime security](8111405.md)

- [Sealift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sealift)



The [USS *Vicksburg* (CG-69)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Vicksburg_\(CG-69\)), [USS *Roosevelt* (DDG-80)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Roosevelt_\(DDG-80\)), [USS *Carney* (DDG-64)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Carney), and [USS *The Sullivans* (DDG-68)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_The_Sullivans_\(DDG-68\)) launching a coordinated missile volley

The nine specified tasks of the Navy are:

1. Conduct offensive and defensive operations associated with the maritime domain including achieving and maintaining [sea control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_control), to include [subsurface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_warfare), [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_warfare), [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare), [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare), [space](956982.md), and [cyberspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

2. Provide [power projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) through sea-based global strike, to include [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warfare) and [conventional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_warfare) capabilities; [interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdiction) and interception capabilities; maritime and littoral fires, to include [naval surface fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_gunfire_support); and [close air support](600792.md) for ground forces.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

3. Conduct [ballistic missile defense](1099196.md). This is executed through the Navy's [Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System](249526.md).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

4. Conduct ocean, hydro, and river survey and reconstruction.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

5. Conduct [riverine operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverine_warfare). This is conducted by the [Maritime Expeditionary Security Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Expeditionary_Security_Force) and [Naval Special Warfare Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Special_Warfare_Command).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

6. Establish, maintain, and defend sea bases in support of naval, amphibious, land, air, or other joint operations as directed.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

7. Provide [naval expeditionary logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_logistics) to enhance the deployment, sustainment, and redeployment of naval forces and other forces operating within the maritime domain, to include joint sea bases, and provide [sea transport](28769.md) for the Armed Forces other than which is organic to the individual military services, [United States Special Operations Command](45204749.md), and [United States Cyber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cyber_Command). This is conducted by the [Navy Expeditionary Logistics Support Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Expeditionary_Logistics_Support_Group_\(United_States\)) and [Military Sealift Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Sealift_Command).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

8. Provide support for joint [space operations](956982.md) to enhance naval operations, in coordination with the other military services (primarily the [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force)), combatant commands (primarily [United States Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Command)), and other U.S. government departments and agencies. U.S. Navy space operations are conducted by [Navy Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Space_Command).

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

9. Conduct [nuclear operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warfare) in support of strategic deterrence, to include providing and maintaining nuclear surety and capabilities.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

#### Naval Surface Forces

Main article: [Naval Surface Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Surface_Forces)



The stealth destroyer [USS *Zumwalt* (DDG-1000)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Zumwalt) alongside the [USS *Independence* (LCS-2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Independence_\(LCS-2\))

The Naval Surface Forces (NAVSURFOR) is the backbone of the U.S. Navy's combat power, conducting [surface warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_warfare) operations and operating its fleet of combat surface ships.[^cite_note-122]

The Naval Surface Forces operates eleven nuclear-powered [aircraft carriers](2219.md) (CVN), split between the [*Nimitz*-class](181160.md) and the newer [*Gerald R. Ford*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier). Aircraft carriers are the centerpiece of the U.S. Navy's combat power, forming the nucleus of its eleven [carrier strike groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_strike_group) (CSG). Each aircraft carrier has an embarked [carrier air wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_air_wing) from the Naval Air Forces.[^cite_note-123]

The Naval Surface Forces also operate 116 [surface combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_combatant). These include the [*Ticonderoga*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticonderoga-class_cruiser) [guided-missile cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided-missile_cruiser), [*Arleigh Burke-class*](197341.md) [guided-missile destroyers](232005.md) (DDG) and [*Zumwalt-class*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer) [stealth](1958969.md) [guided-missile destroyers](232005.md).

Cruisers and destroyers often operate as part of larger formations, where they serve as escorts for [anti-aircraft](146640.md) and [anti-submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) operations. However, they also are able to conduct [sea control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_control) and striker operations ashore with their [tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) cruise missiles.

The Navy also operates a complement of smaller [*Freedom*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom-class_littoral_combat_ship) and [*Independence*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence-class_littoral_combat_ship) [littoral combat ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship) (LCS) that can be modularly reconfigured for specific mission sets.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125]

Having lacked a [frigate](11464.md) since the [*Oliver Hazard Perry*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hazard_Perry-class_frigate) was decommissioned, the Navy is in the process of acquiring the new [*Constellation*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation-class_frigate) [guided-missile frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided-missile_frigate) (FFG).[^cite_note-126]

Finally, the Naval Surface Forces operate 31 [amphibious warfare ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare_ship) to support the [Fleet Marine Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Marine_Force) and its embarked [Marine Air-Ground Task Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Air-Ground_Task_Force) as part of an [amphibious ready group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_ready_group) or [expeditionary strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_strike_group). This includes the [*America-class*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America-class_amphibious_assault_ship) [landing helicopter assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_helicopter_assault) (LHA) ships which can carry U.S. Marine Corps [F-35B Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35B_Lightning_II) fighters and helicopters; and [*Wasp-class*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasp-class_amphibious_assault_ship) [landing helicopter dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_helicopter_dock) (LHD) ships, which can carry both F-35B fighters, helicopters, and landing craft. These are in addition to the [San Antonio-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio-class_amphibious_transport_dock) [amphibious transport docks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_transport_dock) (LPD), and the [Whidbey Island-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whidbey_Island-class_dock_landing_ship) and [*Harpers Ferry*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpers_Ferry-class_dock_landing_ship) [dock landing ships](641435.md).[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

#### Naval Submarine Forces

Main article: [Naval Submarine Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Submarine_Forces)



The [USS *Albuquerque* (SSN-706)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Albuquerque_\(SSN-706\)) attack submarine underway in the Atlantic Ocean

The Naval Submarine Forces (NAVSUBFOR) is often referred to as the "silent service", consisting of 68 commissioned submarines.[^cite_note-129]

[*Los Angeles*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles-class_submarine), [*Seawolf*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawolf-class_submarine), and [*Virginia*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine) nuclear-powered [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine) are capable of performing [sea control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_control) missions by destroying enemy submarines and surface ships, conducting surveillance and reconnaissance, performing irregular warfare, covert troop insertion, mine and anti-mine operations, and land attack missions with [tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) cruise missiles.[^cite_note-130]

[*Ohio*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine) nuclear-powered [ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine) (SSBN) have the sole mission of being launch platforms for the nuclear [submarine-launched ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine-launched_ballistic_missile) (SLBM). Each carries 20 [UGM-133 Trident II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UGM-133_Trident_II) SLBMs.

The Navy is currently in the process of procuring the [*Columbia*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia-class_submarine) SSBNs to replace the *Ohio*-class.[^cite_note-131] Some *Ohio*-class submarines have been converted to [cruise-missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise-missile_submarine) (SSGN), capable of carrying 154 [tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) cruise missiles and deploying 66 special operations forces personnel, such as [Navy SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_SEALs).[^cite_note-132]

#### Naval Air Forces

Main article: [Naval Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Forces)



An [F/A-18F Super Hornet](845012.md) from [VX-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VX-23) flies over the [USS *Gerald R. Ford* (CVN-78)](4050542.md), the world's largest aircraft carrier, and the largest warship ever constructed

The Naval Air Forces (NAVAIRFOR) is the Navy's [naval aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_aviation) arm, centered around the [carrier air wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_air_wing). The core of the carrier air wing are the Naval Air Forces strike fighter squadrons (VFA), which fly the [F-35C Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35C_Lightning_II) stealth fighter and the [F/A-18E/F Super Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18E/F_Super_Hornet).

A variant of the F/A-18, the [EA-18G Growler](833647.md) is an [electronic-warfare aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic-warfare_aircraft) flown by electronic attack squadrons (VAQ) off of carriers or land bases.

The [E-2 Hawkeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-2_Hawkeye) provides carriers with airborne early warning and command-and-control, while the [C-2A Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-2A_Greyhound) keeps carriers supplied.

The [CMV-22B Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMV-22B_Osprey) is currently in the process of replacing the C-2 Greyhound for carrier resupply.[^cite_note-auto8-133]

The Naval Air Forces also operate the [MH-60 Seahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-60_Seahawk) for [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare), [anti-ship warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ship_warfare), and search and rescue operations.

The [MH-53E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-53E) is primarily used for anti-mine warfare but can also be used for assault support.[^cite_note-auto8-133]

Although primarily centered on carriers, the Naval Air Forces do operate a small number of land-based aircraft. These include the [P-3C Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-3C_Orion) and [P-8A Poseidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-8A_Poseidon), which conduct [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) operations and serve as maritime patrol aircraft, alongside the unmanned [MQ-4C Triton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-4C_Triton).

The [E-6 Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-6_Mercury) is also flown by the Navy to communicate instructions to U.S. strategic forces.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-auto8-133]

### U.S. Air Force

Main article: [United States Air Force](32090.md)



A [B-2A Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-2A_Spirit) stealth bomber from the [509th Bomb Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/509th_Bomb_Wing) escorted by two [F-22A Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-22A_Raptor) stealth fighters from the [3rd Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Wing)

The [United States Air Force](32090.md) (USAF) is the United States Armed Forces' air force originally established in 1947 when it gained independence from the U.S. Army, it traces its history back through the [United States Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces), [United States Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Corps), [United States Army Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Service), the [Division of Military Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Military_Aeronautics), [Aviation Section, U.S. Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Section,_U.S._Signal_Corps), to the birth of [Aeronautical Division, U.S. Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_Division,_U.S._Signal_Corps) on 1 August 1907.

The U.S. Air Force serves as the principal air service, responsible for [aerial warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare) operations. The U.S. Air Force is composed of the Regular Air Force, [Air Force Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Reserve), and [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard).[^cite_note-136]

The U.S. Air Force is organized under the [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force), which is a military department under the leadership of the [secretary of the Air Force](305210.md) and [under secretary of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Under_Secretary_of_the_Air_Force).

The U.S. Air Force itself is led by the [chief of staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force) and [vice chief of staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force), both [generals](2965165.md) who are advised by the [chief master sergeant of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_master_sergeant_of_the_Air_Force).

The five core missions of the Air Force are:[^cite_note-137]

- [Air superiority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare)

- [Global integrated intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance](650971.md)

- [Rapid global mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlift)

- [Global strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing)

- [Command and control](7092305.md)



Air Mobility Command [C-17 Globemasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17_Globemaster) performing low-level flight training

The eight specified functions of the Air Force are:[^cite_note-138]

1. Conduct [nuclear operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warfare) in support of strategic deterrence, to include providing and maintaining nuclear surety and capabilities.

2. Conduct offensive and defensive operations, to include appropriate [air](146640.md) and [missile defense](1099196.md), to gain and maintain [air superiority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority) and [air supremacy](537478.md) as required, to enable the conduct of operations by U.S. and allied land, sea, air, space, and special operations forces.

3. Conduct global precision attack, to include [strategic attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing), [interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interdiction), [close air support](600792.md), and prompt global strike.

4. Provide timely, globally integrated [intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence,_surveillance,_and_reconnaissance) capability and capacity from forward deployed locations and globally distributed centers to support world-wide operations.

5. Provide rapid global mobility to employ and sustain organic air and space forces and other military service and U. S. Special Operations Command forces, as directed, to include [airlift forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft) for [airborne operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_forces), air logistical support, [tanker forces](237949.md) for in-flight refueling, and assets for [aeromedical evacuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeromedical_evacuation).

6. Provide agile combat support to enhance the air and space campaign and the deployment, employment, sustainment, and redeployment of air and space forces and other forces operating within the air and space domains, to include joint air and space bases, and for the Armed Forces other than which is organic to the individual military services and U.S. Special Operations Command in coordination with the other military services, combatant commands, and U.S. Government departments and agencies.

7. Conduct global personnel recovery operations including theater-wide combat and civil search and rescue in coordination with the other military services, combatant commands, and DoD components.

8. Conduct globally integrated command and control for air and space operations.

#### Combat Air Force

Main articles: [Air Combat Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Combat_Command), [Air Force Global Strike Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Global_Strike_Command), and [Air Force Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Special_Operations_Command)



Two [F-22A Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-22A_Raptor) from the [90th Fighter Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90th_Fighter_Squadron)

The Combat Air Force (CAF) comprises the majority of the Air Force's combat power, consisting of its [fighter](10929.md), [bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber), [intercontinental ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile), and [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations) forces.[^cite_note-139]

The Air Force's fighter forces are led by [Air Combat Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Combat_Command)'s [Fifteenth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifteenth_Air_Force), with other fighter units under [Pacific Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Air_Forces) and [United States Air Forces in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Forces_in_Europe). Air Force fighters are predominantly used to achieve air superiority and strike enemy ground and naval forces.

The Air Force operates an expanding force of [fifth-generation fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth-generation_fighter). The [F-22A Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-22A_Raptor) stealth fighter is designed to replace the F-15C in [air superiority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority) operations carrying two [AIM-9 Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) and six [AIM-120 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM) missiles. It also has a significant air-to-ground mission, carrying two [GBU-32 Joint Direct Attack Munition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Direct_Attack_Munition) bombs, in addition to two AIM-9 and two AIM-120 missiles.[^cite_note-140] Ultimately, the F-22 is intended to be replaced by the [sixth-generation fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth-generation_fighter), [Next Generation Air Dominance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Air_Dominance) program.[^cite_note-141] The F-22 is complemented by the more numerous [F-35A Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35A_Lightning_II) multi-role stealth fighters, which are in the process of replacing the F-16C fighters and A-10 attack aircraft in air superiority and ground attack roles, to include the [nuclear strike mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warfare).[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]

The Air Force still operates an extremely sizable force of [fourth-generation fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-generation_fighter). The [F-15C Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15C_Eagle) is a dedicated air superiority fighter, while the [F-15E Strike Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15E_Strike_Eagle) has been modified to be a dual-role [strike fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_fighter), carrying both conventional and nuclear weapons.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] The F-15C Eagle and F-15E Strike Eagle are both being replaced by the [F-15EX Eagle II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15EX_Eagle_II), which is significantly more advanced.[^cite_note-146]

The [F-16C Fighting Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16C_Fighting_Falcon) is a multirole fighter that has served as the primary Air Force fighter for decades, including as a dual-capable tactical nuclear strike fighter.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

The [A-10C Thunderbolt II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-10C_Thunderbolt_II) [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft) has been the first Air Force fighter specifically designed for [close air support](600792.md) missions, operating against enemy ground forces and light naval ships with its [GAU-8 Avenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAU-8_Avenger) gatling cannon and array of air-to-ground munitions.[^cite_note-149]



A [B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md) of the [2nd Bomb Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Bomb_Wing) and a [B-2A Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-2A_Spirit) of the [509th Bomb Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/509th_Bomb_Wing) flying in formation

The Air Force's bomber forces are organized under [Air Force Global Strike Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Global_Strike_Command)'s [Eighth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Air_Force), executing long-range strike operations. The [B-2A Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-2A_Spirit) stealth bomber is capable of conducting both conventional and nuclear strike operations flying through air defenses.[^cite_note-auto12-150]

The [B-1B Lancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-1B_Lancer), in contrast, is a supersonic bomber that carries only conventional munitions and serves as the backbone of the bomber force.[^cite_note-auto12-150] Both the B-2A Spirit and the B-1B Lancer are being replaced by the [B-21 Raider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-21_Raider) stealth bomber, which can be equipped with both conventional and nuclear munitions.[^cite_note-151]

The [B-52H Stratofortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-52H_Stratofortress) is a long-range, [heavy bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_bomber) that the Air Force has flown since the 1950s and operates a variety of conventional and nuclear munitions, including the [AGM-86 air-launched cruise missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-86_ALCM).[^cite_note-152]

The Air Force's intercontinental ballistic missile forces are organized under Air Force Global Strike Command's [Twentieth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twentieth_Air_Force), serving as the land component of the [nuclear triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_triad).

The [LGM-30G Minuteman III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGM-30G_Minuteman_III) serves as the only ballistic missile operated by the Air Force, with 400 stationed in hardened silos.[^cite_note-153] The LGM-30G will be replaced by the [LGM-35A Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGM-35A_Sentinel) intercontinental ballistic missile.[^cite_note-154]



A formation of [CV-22 Ospreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CV-22_Osprey) from the [353rd Special Operations Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/353rd_Special_Operations_Group)

The Air Force's special operations forces are organized under [Air Force Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Special_Operations_Command), consisting of both special operations aviation and Air Force special tactics airmen on the ground. The [AC-130J Ghostrider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC-130J_Ghostrider) [gunships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunship) have the primary mission of [close air support](600792.md) and [air interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interdiction), using cannons and precision guided munitions.[^cite_note-155]

The [MC-130J Commando II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MC-130J_Commando_II), also a variant of the C-130 Hercules, fly exfiltration and resupply operations for special operations forces, alongside conducting air-to-air refueling for helicopters.[^cite_note-156]

The [CV-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CV-22_Osprey) is a tiltrotor aircraft used for the infiltration and exfiltration of special operations forces.[^cite_note-157]

While not under Air Force Special Operations Command, rescue operations are supported by the [HC-130J Combat King II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC-130J_Combat_King_II) and [HH-60W Jolly Green II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HH-60W_Jolly_Green_II) combat rescue aircraft.[^cite_note-158]

&#x20;The [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md) also serves as a remotely piloted intelligence and strike aircraft, serving under Air Force Special Operations Command and Air Combat Command.[^cite_note-159]&#x20;



Air Force [Pararescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pararescue) with a [HH-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HH-60) helicopter in the background

Air Force Special Tactics are the ground special warfare force of the U.S. Air Force, integrating air-ground operations. Special Tactics conduct four core missions.

Global access teams assess and open airfields, ranging from international airports to dirt strips, in permissive or hostile locations to facilitate the landing and operation of air forces. Precision strike teams are trained to direct aircraft and other forces to conduct kinetic and non-kinetic strikes, as well as humanitarian aid drops.Special Tactics teams also conduct personnel recovery missions, possessing significant medical and rescue experience.Finally, Special Operations Surgical Teams conduct surgery and medical operations in battlefield operations in support of special operations.[^cite_note-160]

The Air Force also operates a wide array of [reconnaissance aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft) under Air Combat Command's [Sixteenth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteenth_Air_Force), including the [RQ-4 Global Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RQ-4_Global_Hawk) drone, [U-2 Dragon Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-2_Dragon_Lady), and [RC-135 Rivet Joint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC-135_Rivet_Joint).

Air Force operations are typically supported by command and control aircraft, such as the [E-3 Sentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-3_Sentry) [airborne warning and control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_warning_and_control_system). The E-3 Sentry is in the process of being replaced by the [E-7A Wedgetail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-7A_Wedgetail).[^cite_note-161]

#### Mobility Air Force

Main article: [Air Mobility Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Mobility_Command)

The Mobility Air Force (MAF) is organized under Air Mobility Command and comprises the Air Force's [airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft), [air refueling](237949.md), and [aeromedical evacuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeromedical_evacuation) forces.[^cite_note-162]



A [KC-46A Pegasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-46A_Pegasus) refueling a [C-17A Globemaster III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17A_Globemaster_III)

The airlift forces operate three different major aircraft. The [C-5M Super Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-5M_Super_Galaxy) is the largest aircraft in the Air Force, serving as a strategic transport aircraft.[^cite_note-163]

The [C-17A Globemaster III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17A_Globemaster_III) is the airlift force's most flexible aircraft, conducting both strategic and tactical airlift operations. It is also capable of conducting airborne operations for the Army and aerial resupply through airdropping cargo.[^cite_note-164]

Finally, the [C-130J Super Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130J_Super_Hercules) is a tactical airlifter, conducting both cargo airlift and supporting Army airborne operations.[^cite_note-165]

Other major airlift platforms include the [VC-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VC-25), which serves as the personal plane of the [president of the United States](24113.md), better known as [Air Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_One).[^cite_note-166]

The Air Force also operates three major aerial refueling tankers. The [KC-46A Pegasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-46A_Pegasus) is its most modern tanker, replacing the aging [KC-10A Extenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-10A_Extender).[^cite_note-167] The remaining tanker is the [KC-135 Stratotanker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-135_Stratotanker), which has flown since the 1950s.[^cite_note-168] Tankers are also capable of conducting limited airlift operations.

### U.S. Space Force

Main article: [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force)



[Boeing X-37B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_X-37) spaceplane after deorbiting and landing

The [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) (USSF) is the United States Armed Forces' space force and is the newest military branch. Originally established in 2019, it traces its history through Air Force Space Command and the Western Development Division to 1954.

The United States Space Force is the principal space service, responsible for [space warfare](956982.md) operations.[^cite_note-176] The U.S. Space Force is composed of the Regular Space Force, not yet having organized a reserve component outside of the Air Force.

The U.S. Space Force is organized under the [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force), which is a military department under the leadership of the [secretary of the Air Force](305210.md) and [under secretary of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Under_Secretary_of_the_Air_Force).

The U.S. Space Force itself is led by the [chief of space operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_space_operations) and [vice chief of space operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_chief_of_space_operations), both [generals](2965165.md) who are advised by the [chief master sergeant of the Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_master_sergeant_of_the_Space_Force).[^cite_note-177]



[Delta IV Heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_IV_Heavy) launch for the [NROL-44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NROL-44) mission

The five core competencies of the Space Force are:[^cite_note-Spacepower-178]

- [Space security](1946540.md)

- [Combat](956982.md) [power projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection)

- [Space mobility and logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_logistics)

- [Information mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_warfare)

- [Space domain awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_domain_awareness)

The five specified functions of the Space Force are:

1. Provide freedom of operation for the United States in, from, and to space.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

2. Provide prompt and sustained space operations.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

3. Protect the interests of the United States in space.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

4. Deter aggression in, from, and to space.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

5. Conduct space operations.

   [^cite_note-DoDI_5100.01-67]

#### Orbital warfare and space electromagnetic warfare

Main articles: [Space Delta 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_3) and [Space Delta 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_9)

The Space Force's combat power is centered around Space Delta 3, which conducts [space electromagnetic warfare](82272.md) and Space Delta 9, which conducts [orbital warfare](956982.md).[^cite_note-179]

Combat power projection operations ensure freedom of action in space for the U.S. and its allies and denies an adversary freedom of action in space.

These are divided into offensive and defensive space operations.

Defensive operations preserve and protect U.S. and allied space capabilities, which are further sub-divided into active and passive actions.

Offensive operations target a U.S. adversary's space and counterspace capabilities, achieving space superiority.[^cite_note-Spacepower-178]

Orbital warfare forces conduct protect-and-defend operations and provide U.S. national decision authorities with response options to deter and, when necessary, defeat orbital threats.

The space electromagnetic warfare forces conduct offensive and defensive space control operations. Space Force cyber forces conduct defensive cyber operations to protect space assets.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

#### Cyber operations and Satellite Control Network

Main article: [Space Delta 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_6)



[Satellite Control Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Control_Network) antenna at [Kaena Point Space Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaena_Point_Space_Force_Station)

Although the U.S. Space Force is not a [cyber force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_force), it does conduct extensive [cyber operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare) under Space Delta 6.

The primary focus of Space Force cyber operations is defending U.S. Space Force networks and ensuring the operations of its spacecraft, which are controlled remotely from ground stations.[^cite_note-182] All space operations units have cyber squadrons assigned to defend them and incorporate offensive cyber operations.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-auto17-185]

Additionally, Space Delta 6 is responsible for managing the [Satellite Control Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Control_Network), a global network of antennas used to communicate with the service's spacecraft.

The [22nd Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Space_Operations_Squadron) is responsible for overall operations, with the [21st Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Space_Operations_Squadron) and [23rd Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Space_Operations_Squadron) managing the ground station sites at [Vandenberg Space Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandenberg_Space_Force_Base), [New Boston Space Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Boston_Space_Force_Station), [Kaena Point Space Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaena_Point_Space_Force_Station), Diego Garcia, Guam, Greenland, and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-auto17-185]

#### Space domain awareness

Main article: [Space Delta 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_2)



[15th Space Surveillance Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Space_Surveillance_Squadron) [GEODSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-based_Electro-Optical_Deep_Space_Surveillance) telescope at [White Sands Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range)

The Space Force's Space Delta 2 operates the [United States Space Surveillance Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Surveillance_Network), tracking 47,000 objects in space as of 2022.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187]

Space domain awareness encompasses the identification, characterization, and understanding of any factor associated with the space domain that could affect space operations.[^cite_note-Spacepower-178]

Space Delta 2 sensors include the [AN/FPS-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/FPS-85) phased array radar at [Eglin Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglin_Air_Force_Base) and the [Space Fence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Fence) radar at Kwajalein Atoll operated by the [20th Space Surveillance Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Space_Surveillance_Squadron); and a global network of three [Ground-based Electro-Optical Deep Space Surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-based_Electro-Optical_Deep_Space_Surveillance) telescopes operated by the [15th Space Surveillance Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Space_Surveillance_Squadron).[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190]

The [18th Space Defense Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Space_Defense_Squadron), collocated with the [Combined Space Operations Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Space_Operations_Center), executes command and control of the Space Surveillance Network and manages the [Space Catalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Catalog_Number) of all objects in Earth orbit.

The [19th Space Defense Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Space_Defense_Squadron) focuses on objects further in [cislunar space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cislunar_space), beyond [geosynchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geosynchronous_orbit), with a specific focus on supporting [NASA](18426568.md)'s [Artemis program](60758751.md) and other human spaceflight operations.[^cite_note-191]

#### Missile warning

Main article: [Space Delta 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_4)



Deployment of a [Defense Support Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Support_Program) missile warning spacecraft from the [Space Shuttle *Atlantis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Atlantis) on the [STS-44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-44) mission

The Space Force's Space Delta 4 uses orbital spacecraft and ground-based radars to conduct theater and strategic missile warnings for the United States and its international partners.[^cite_note-192]

This includes the network of [Upgraded Early Warning Radars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upgraded_Early_Warning_Radar) at [Beale Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beale_Air_Force_Base), [Clear Space Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_Space_Force_Station), [Cape Cod Space Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod_Space_Force_Station), [Pituffik Space Base](750735.md), and [RAF Fylingdales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Fylingdales), alongside the [AN/FPQ-16 PARCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/FPQ-16_PARCS) radar at [Cavalier Space Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalier_Space_Force_Station).[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194]

Currently, the Space Force is working with the [Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Defense_Agency) to acquire the [Long Range Discrimination Radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Range_Discrimination_Radar) at Clear Space Force Station in Alaska.These ground-based radars also contribute to the Space Surveillance Network.[^cite_note-195]

Space-based missile warning systems include the [Defense Support Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Support_Program) and [Space-Based Infrared System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-Based_Infrared_System) (SIBRS) spacecraft, which use infrared sensors to conduct missile defense and missile warning. SIBRS also has a battle space awareness and technical intelligence mission.[^cite_note-196]

The Defense Support Program spacecraft are also capable of detecting nuclear detonations, in addition to space and missile launches.[^cite_note-197]

#### Global Positioning System and military satellite communications

Main article: [Space Delta 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_8)



Members of the [4th Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Space_Operations_Squadron) Mobile Operations Flight conducting armed convoy operations

The Space Force's Space Delta 8 is the operator of the [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) and the military's array of communications spacecraft.[^cite_note-198]

The Global Positioning System is operated by the [2nd Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Space_Operations_Squadron), providing [positioning, navigation, and timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positioning,_navigation,_and_timing) information for civilian and military users across the entire world.[^cite_note-199]

The Space Force's GPS system has become an integral element of the global information infrastructure, being used in virtually all sectors of the economy, including agriculture, aviation, marine transportation, surveying and mapping, and transit navigation. Its timing signal is used to synchronize global communication systems, electrical power grids, and financial networks.[^cite_note-200]

The Global Positioning System also has a secondary mission of carrying [nuclear detonation detection sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_detonation_detection_system).[^cite_note-201]

Military satellite communication systems include the [Fleet Satellite Communications System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Satellite_Communications_System), [UHF Follow-On satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UHF_Follow-On_satellite), and [Mobile User Objective System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_User_Objective_System), operated by the [10th Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Space_Operations_Squadron) and inherited from the U.S. Navy.[^cite_note-202]

The payloads on the [Wideband Global SATCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wideband_Global_SATCOM) and [Defense Satellite Communications System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Satellite_Communications_System) are operated by the [53rd Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Space_Operations_Squadron), a role inherited from the U.S. Army.[^cite_note-203]

Finally, spacecraft operations for the Wideband Global SATCOM and Defense Satellite Communications System are conducted by the [4th Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Space_Operations_Squadron), in addition to the [Milstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milstar) and [Advanced Extremely High Frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Extremely_High_Frequency) satellites, which both additionally support command and control of strategic nuclear forces.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]

#### Space launch

Main articles: [Space Launch Delta 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_Delta_30) and [Space Launch Delta 45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_Delta_45)



Launch of the [Falcon Heavy](31406060.md) on the [STP-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Test_Program) mission

The Space Force's [space launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_launch) enterprise is organized under [Space Systems Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Systems_Command), with [Space Launch Delta 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_Delta_30) managing the [Western Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Range_\(USSF\)) from [Vandenberg Space Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandenberg_Space_Force_Base) and [Space Launch Delta 45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_Delta_45) managing the [Eastern Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Range) from [Cape Canaveral Space Force Station](306778.md).[^cite_note-auto16-206]

The Space Force does not just manage military space launches, but also supports [NASA](18426568.md) and commercial space launches.[^cite_note-auto16-206]

Major space launch vehicles flown or scheduled to fly off of Space Force launch ranges include [NASA](18426568.md)'s [Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System), [SpaceX](832774.md)'s [Starship](59118602.md), [Falcon Heavy](31406060.md), and [Falcon 9](2647515.md), and [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance)'s [Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_Centaur), which will replace the [Atlas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_V) and [Delta IV Heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_IV_Heavy).

Currently, vehicles for the [National Security Space Launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Space_Launch) program include the Vulcan, Atlas V, Falcon Heavy, and Falcon 9 rockets.[^cite_note-207]

An experimental [Air Force Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Research_Laboratory) vanguard program that the Space Force leads, [Rocket Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Cargo), is exploring using rockets to supplement naval and air transport to rapidly deliver supplies to forces across the Earth.[^cite_note-auto11-208] The [SpaceX Starship](59118602.md) rocket is one such system currently being explored.[^cite_note-209]

### U.S. Coast Guard

Main article: [United States Coast Guard](32223.md)



&#x20;[USCGC *Hamilton* (WMSL-753)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Hamilton_\(WMSL-753\)) sailing alongside the [USCGC *William Flores* (WPC-1103)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_William_Flores)

The [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) (USCG) is the United States Armed Forces' [maritime security](8111405.md), maritime [search and rescue](172599.md), and [maritime law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_law_enforcement) force. It was first established in 1790 as the [United States Revenue-Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Revenue_Cutter_Service) and consists of the Regular Coast Guard and the [Coast Guard Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Reserve).

Although it has always been one of the six military branches, the Coast Guard is organized under the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) under the leadership of the [secretary of Homeland Security](212926.md) and the [deputy secretary of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Homeland_Security).

During times of war, the U.S. Coast Guard can be transferred to the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy)[^cite_note-211]

The Coast Guard itself is led by the [commandant of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant_of_the_Coast_Guard) and [vice commandant of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Commandant_of_the_United_States_Coast_Guard), both [admirals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(United_States\)) advised by the [master chief petty officer of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_chief_petty_officer_of_the_Coast_Guard).[^cite_note-212]



A Coast Guard [Maritime Security Response team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Safety_and_Security_Team) executing a boarding action from an [MH-60 Jayhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-60_Jayhawk) helicopter

The Coast Guard has six major operational mission programs, through which it executes its 11 statutory missions:[^cite_note-auto2-213]

- Maritime law enforcement

- Maritime response

- Maritime prevention

- Maritime transport system management

- Maritime security operations

- Defense operations

[Maritime law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_law_enforcement) operations focus on protecting the United States maritime borders and assuring its maritime sovereignty.

The Coast Guard conducts operations to suppress violations of U.S. law at sea, including counter-illegal migration and transnational organized crime operations. Codified missions executed under the maritime law enforcement program include drug interdiction, migrant interdiction, living marine resources, and other law enforcement.[^cite_note-auto2-213]

Maritime response operations see the Coast Guard conducting search-and-rescue operations and rescuing mariners responding to maritime disasters. Codified missions include search and rescue and marine environmental protection (response activities).[^cite_note-auto2-213]



Coast Guard aviation [MH-60 Jayhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-60_Jayhawk) helicopter with a rescue swimmer

Maritime prevention operations prevent marine casualties and property losses, minimize security risks, and protect the marine environment.

The Coast Guard does so by developing and enforcing federal regulations, conducting safety and security inspections, and analyzing port security risk assessments. Codified missions include ports, waterways, and coastal security, marine safety, and marine environmental protection (protection activities).[^cite_note-auto2-213]

Maritime transport system management ensures a safe, secure, and environmentally sound waterways system. Codified missions include maintaining aids to navigation and ice operations.[^cite_note-auto2-213]

Maritime security operations include activities to detect, deter, prevent, and disrupt terrorist attacks, and other criminal acts in the U.S. maritime domain. This includes the execution of anti-terrorism, response, and select recovery operations.

This mission performs the operational element of the Coast Guard's Ports, Waterways, and Coastal Security mission and complements its Maritime Response and Prevention efforts. Codified missions include ports, waterways, and coastal security (response activities).[^cite_note-auto2-213]

Coast Guard Defense Operations deploy the Coast Guard globally under the Department of Defense's unified combatant commands, where it operates under the joint force maritime component commands. Codified missions include defense readiness.[^cite_note-auto2-213]

### Budget

Main article: [Military budget of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_the_United_States)

The United States manages the [world's largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures) [military budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_the_United_States), followed by the [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_China), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_India), United Kingdom, and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_Russia).[^cite_note-219]

The [James M. Inhofe National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_M._Inhofe_National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023) established the topline defense budget as \$857.9 billion, with the Department of Defense receiving \$816.7 billion and the [Department of Energy](62866.md)'s national security programs receiving \$30.3 billion, an 8% increase from Fiscal Year 2022.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221]

The [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force) budget, unlike the [Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army) or [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) has a sizable portion of "pass-through." This is money not controlled or used by the Air Force, but is instead passed to other Department of Defense agencies and can be up to 17% of the department's budget. This pass-through allocation gives the impression that the Air Force is the highest funded military department. It is actually the least funded.[^cite_note-222]

### Army modernization

Main article: [Army Futures Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Futures_Command)



Launch of [Precision Strike Missile](71176097.md) from a [M142 HIMARS](147011.md) at [Vandenberg Space Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandenberg_Space_Force_Base)

The Army's modernization efforts, led by [United States Army Transformation and Training Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Transformation_and_Training_Command), are centralized into six priorities. Each priority is led by a Cross Functional Team.

[Long Range Precision Fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Range_Precision_Fires) is the land service's top modernization priority, focusing on rebuilding its [Field Artillery Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Artillery_Branch_\(United_States\)) in response to longer range Russian and Chinese artillery systems.

The [Extended Range Cannon Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_Range_Cannon_Artillery) program is developing a cannon artillery piece that can accurately fire at targets 70 kilometers away, an increase from the 30 kilometer distance of current cannon artillery.

The [Precision Strike Missile](71176097.md) is a surface-to-surface guided missile intended to be fired from the current [M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_Multiple_Launch_Rocket_System) and [M142 HIMARS](147011.md), replacing their current missiles and doubling the rate of fire.

The Army is also working with the [Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Defense_Agency), U.S. Navy, and U.S. Air Force to develop a common hypersonic glide body, which the Army will employ as part of the mobile ground launched [Long-Range Hypersonic Weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-Range_Hypersonic_Weapon) program.

Finally, the Army is working to modify the U.S. Navy's [RIM-174 Standard ERAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM) and [UGM-109 Tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) land attack cruise missile for ground launch to provide the Army with mid-range artillery capability.[^cite_note-\:3-230]

The [Next Generation Combat Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Combat_Vehicle) program is developing a family of fighting vehicles for the [Armor Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armor_Branch) to increase firepower, speed, and survivability.

The [Optionally Manned Fighting Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optionally_Manned_Fighting_Vehicle) is intended to replace the [M2 Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_Bradley), while the [Armored Multi-Purpose Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_Multi-Purpose_Vehicle) will replace the [M113 armored personnel carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113_armored_personnel_carrier), which have been used since the Vietnam War.

The three variants of the Armored Multi-Purpose Vehicle are general purpose, mission command, and medical treatment. The [Mobile Protected Firepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Protected_Firepower) is designed to be a [light tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_tank) for [Infantry Brigade Combat Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Brigade_Combat_Team).

Finally, the [Robotic Combat Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotic_Combat_Vehicle) are intended to come in light, medium, and heavy variants that will serve as scouts and escorts for crewed combat vehicles.[^cite_note-\:3-230]



The [V-280 Valor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-280_Valor) flying in tiltrotor configuration

The [Future Vertical Lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Vertical_Lift) program is intended to replace the current helicopter fleet flown by the [Army Aviation Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Aviation_Branch).

The [Future Long-Range Assault Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Long-Range_Assault_Aircraft) is intended to replace the [UH-60 Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Black_Hawk) and the Marine Corps [UH-1Y Venom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-1Y_Venom), with the Army selecting the [Bell V-280 Valor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_V-280_Valor) [tiltrotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiltrotor) aircraft as the winner.[^cite_note-231]

The [Future Attack Reconnaissance Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Attack_Reconnaissance_Aircraft) is intended to replace the [AH-64 Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-64_Apache) in the attack and reconnaissance roles.[^cite_note-\:3-230]

The Army is working to modernize its [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_communication) networks for the [Army Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Signal_Corps), including developing a Unified Network consisting of an integrated tactical network, an integrated enterprise network, and unified network-enabling capabilities.

Other sub-efforts include developing a common operating environment, ensuring the network is interoperable with the other services and allied countries, and increasing the mobility and reducing the signature of its [command posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_post).

Efforts also include modernizing [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) technology to provide assured positioning, navigation, and timing, and working with the [United States Intelligence Community](501244.md) and commercial space companies to increase the Army's access to space-based [intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence,_surveillance,_and_reconnaissance) capabilities.[^cite_note-\:3-230]



Army Rangers armed with the [XM7 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM7_rifle) and [XM250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM250) light machine gun

Recognizing that the United States is unlikely to have uncontested [air superiority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority), the Army is undergoing a mass revitalization of its [air and missile defense enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Air_and_Missile_Defense) through the [Air Defense Artillery Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Defense_Artillery_Branch).

The first layer of defense is the Ballistic Low-Altitude Drone Engagement, which will be mounted on the [Common Remotely Operated Weapon Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Remotely_Operated_Weapon_Station) and is designed to engage small [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md).

The second layer is the Multi-Mission High Energy Laser, which will intercept small drones and munitions.

The third and fourth layers comprise the Maneuver Air Defense Technology and Next-Generation Fires Radar, which will be integrated into [short range air defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_range_air_defense) systems.

The fifth layer puts a High-Energy Laser Tactical Vehicle Demonstrator onto a [Medium Tactical Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_of_Medium_Tactical_Vehicles).

While the sixth layer encompasses the Low-Cost Extended-Range Air Defense to supplement the [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) missiles.[^cite_note-\:3-230]

Finally, the Army is looking to improve the equipment of its soldiers in the [Infantry Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Branch_\(United_States\)) with the [Next Generation Squad Weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Squad_Weapon), the [Integrated Visual Augmentation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Visual_Augmentation_System), and the Synthetic Training Environment.[^cite_note-\:3-230] In 2022, the Army selected the [SIG Sauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_Sauer)'s [XM7 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM7_rifle) and [XM250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM250) light machine gun to replace the [M4 carbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_carbine) and the [M249 light machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M249_light_machine_gun) through the Next Generation Squad Weapon program.[^cite_note-232]

### Marine Corps modernization

Main article: [Force Design 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Design_2030)



A [Naval Strike Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Strike_Missile) launcher on the [Joint Light Tactical Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Light_Tactical_Vehicle)

The Marine Corps modernization is being executed under the aegis of [Force Design 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Design_2030), which is intended to return the service to its naval and amphibious roots serving as a "stand-in" force within contested areas of the maritime littorals.[^cite_note-233]

As part of this effort, the Marine Corps has begun establishing naval-focused Marine Littoral Regiments, consisting of a Littoral Combat Team, Littoral Anti-Air Battalion, and a Combat Logistics Battalion. The Littoral Combat Team is organized around an infantry battalion with an anti-ship missile battery, focused on conducting sea denial operations in support of the Navy.[^cite_note-234]

The Marine Corps is in the process of acquiring the [Amphibious Combat Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_Combat_Vehicle), which is slated to replace the aging [Assault Amphibious Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_Amphibious_Vehicle). The Amphibious Combat Vehicle is intended to support the Marines during amphibious assaults and once they have reached shore.

The Marine Corps has also adopted the [Naval Strike Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Strike_Missile) which is fielded from a modified [Joint Light Tactical Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Light_Tactical_Vehicle) as part of the Marines' artillery battalions. The concept is that small mobile units of Marines would move around different islands and shorelines with these weapons to fire on adversary ships.[^cite_note-auto18-235]

Marine Corps aviation is also in the process of acquiring the [CH-53K King Stallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-53K_King_Stallion) helicopter for heavy lift, replacing the current [CH-53E Super Stallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-53E_Super_Stallion) in the role.

The Marine Corps is also looking to replace its [UH-1Y Venom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-1Y_Venom) helicopters through the [Future Vertical Lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Vertical_Lift) program and is in the process of acquiring a significant number of [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md), such as the [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md).[^cite_note-auto18-235]

### Navy modernization



Rendering of [USS *Constellation* (FFG-62)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constellation_\(FFG-62\))

Like the Marine Corps, the Navy is in the process of overhauling and modernizing its fleet with a renewed focus. While the Navy is continuing to purchase [*Arleigh Burke*-class destroyers](197341.md), it is embarking on the [DDG(X)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDG\(X\)) program of guided missile destroyers to replace them and the [*Ticonderoga*-class cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticonderoga-class_cruiser). The DDG(X) will include [directed energy weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_energy_weapon) and potentially hypersonic weapons.[^cite_note-236]

The [*Constellation*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation-class_frigate) will be the first frigates in the U.S. Navy since the [*Oliver Hazard Perry*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hazard_Perry-class_frigate) were retired. The *Constellation*-class frigates are based on the Italian [FREMM multipurpose frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FREMM_multipurpose_frigate) and will replace the [littoral combat ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship).[^cite_note-auto19-237]



Rendering of a [*Columbia*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia-class_submarine) ballistic missile submarine

The Navy is starting development on the [SSN(X)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSN\(X\)) attack submarines, intended to replace the [*Virginia*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine) and [*Seawolf*-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawolf-class_submarine).

The [*Columbia*-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia-class_submarine) will begin replacing the [*Ohio*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine) ballistic missile submarines. The acquisition of the *Columbia*-class submarines is the first priority of the Navy.[^cite_note-auto13-238][^cite_note-auto19-237]

With Naval Aviation, the service is continuing to procure additional [*Gerald R. Ford*-class aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier) and [F-35C Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35C_Lightning_II) stealth fighters.

Ultimately, the [F/A-XX program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-XX_program) is intended to produce a [sixth-generation fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth-generation_fighter) to replace the legacy [F/A-18E/F Super Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18E/F_Super_Hornet) as part of the Carrier Air Wing and is using the [Future Vertical Lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Vertical_Lift) program to replace its fleet of [SH-60 Seahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH-60_Seahawk) helicopters.[^cite_note-auto13-238][^cite_note-auto19-237]

The service is also investing heavily in unmanned platforms, such as [unmanned surface vehicles](7808557.md), using the [Ghost Fleet Overlord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Fleet_Overlord) to test the concept.

It is also fielding [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md), such as the land-based [MQ-4C Triton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-4C_Triton) for maritime patrol and the carrier-based [MQ-25A Stingray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-25A_Stingray) for [aerial refueling](237949.md), replacing the F/A-18F in the role.[^cite_note-auto19-237]

### Air Force modernization



[B-21 Raider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-21_Raider) at [United States Air Force Plant 42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Plant_42)



[F-15EX Eagle II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F-15EX_Eagle_II) from the [40th Flight Test Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_Flight_Test_Squadron)

The [B-21 Raider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-21_Raider) stealth bomber is the first new Air Force bomber since the [B-2A Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-2A_Spirit). The B-21 will replace the B-2 and the [B-1B Lancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-1B_Lancer), flying alongside the [B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md).[^cite_note-auto10-239] The development of the B-21 Raider was led by the [Department of the Air Force Rapid Capabilities Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Air_Force_Rapid_Capabilities_Office).

The service is also developing the [LGM-35 Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGM-35_Sentinel) intercontinental ballistic missile to replace the [LGM-30G Minuteman IIIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGM-30G_Minuteman_III).[^cite_note-240]

The U.S. Air Force is also in the process of developing the [Next Generation Air Dominance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Air_Dominance) program, which will produce a [sixth generation fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_generation_fighter) to replace the [F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-22_Raptor).

The service is also procuring the [fourth generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_generation_fighter) [F-15EX Eagle II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F-15EX_Eagle_II) to replace the aging [F-15C Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15C_Eagle) and [F-15E Strike Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15E_Strike_Eagle).

It is procuring the [T-7A Red Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-7A_Red_Hawk) trainer jet to replace the 1950s-era [T-38 Talon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-38_Talon).[^cite_note-auto10-239]

While not an aircraft, the Air Force is investing in developing the [AIM-260 Joint Advanced Tactical Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-260_JATM) to replace or supplement the [AIM-120 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM) for its fighter forces.

It is also procuring the [AGM-181 Long Range Stand Off Weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-181_LRSO) to replace the [AGM-86 ALCM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-86_ALCM) as a nuclear air-launched cruise missile for the B-21 Raider and the B-52 Stratofortress.

The air service is investing in hypersonic weapons, with the [AGM-183 Air-Launched Rapid Response Weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-183_ARRW), the [Hypersonic Air-breathing Weapon Concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_Air-breathing_Weapon_Concept), and the [Hypersonic Attack Cruise Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_Attack_Cruise_Missile) in development.[^cite_note-auto10-239]

### Space Force modernization

Main article: [Space Warfighting Analysis Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Warfighting_Analysis_Center)



Concept for a Space Force [Rocket Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Cargo) program conducting humanitarian assistance and disaster relief operations

The U.S. Space Force is undergoing intensive modernization efforts. The [Deep Space Advanced Radar Capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Space_Advanced_Radar_Capability) (DARC) is intended to track objects in [geosynchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geosynchronous_orbit) with three sites, one in the United States, one in the Indo-Pacific, and one in Europe.[^cite_note-241]



[NASA](18426568.md)'s return to the Moon through the [Artemis program](60758751.md) is leading to a greater emphasis on cislunar domain awareness

Oracle, a spacecraft developed by the [Air Force Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Research_Laboratory) for the Space Force, will demonstrate technologies that the space service needs for cislunar domain awareness – tracking objects outside of geosynchronous orbit and between Earth and the Moon.

The spacecraft itself will launch to an area of gravitational stability between the Earth and the Moon to conduct operations, using a wide-field sensor and a more sensitive narrow-field sensor to discover and maintain custody of objects operating in this region.

Oracle will directly support [NASA](18426568.md)'s [Artemis program](60758751.md) as it returns to the Moon and track potentially hazardous near-Earth objects in support of [planetary defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_defense) operations.[^cite_note-242]

Also an Air Force Research Laboratory program for the Space Force, Arachne is the keystone experiment in the [Space Solar Power Incremental Demonstrations and Research Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Solar_Power_Incremental_Demonstrations_and_Research_Project), which aims to prove and mature essential technologies for a prototype [space-based solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_solar_power) transmission system capable of powering a [forward operating base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_operating_base).

Arachne will specifically demonstrate and mature technologies related to more efficient energy generation, radio frequency forming, and radio frequency beaming.

Current forward operation bases rely on significant logistics convoys to transport fuel for power – space-based solar power would move these supply lines to space, where they cannot be easily attacked.

Space Force provided space-based solar power may transition to civilian use in the same vein as GPS.[^cite_note-243]

Other space-based power beaming demonstrations include the Space Power InfraRed Regulation and Analysis of Lifetime (SPIRRAL) and Space Power INcremental DepLoyable Experiment (SPINDLE) experiments.[^cite_note-244]



Concept for a [space-based solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_solar_power) spacecraft

The Navigation Technology Satellite-3 (NTS-3), building on the Space Force's [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) constellation, is an Air Force Research Laboratory spacecraft that will operate in geosynchronous orbit to test advanced techniques and technologies to detect and mitigate interference to positioning, navigation, and timing capabilities and increase system resiliency for military, civil, and commercial users. NTS-3 is a Vanguard program, described as aiming to deliver potentially game changing capabilities.[^cite_note-245]

The Space Force's [Rocket Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Cargo) program is another Air Force Research Laboratory Vanguard program, focused on leasing space launch services to quickly transport military materiel to ports across the globe.

If proven viable, the Space Force's [Space Systems Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Systems_Command) will be responsible for transitioning it to a program of record.

[United States Transportation Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Transportation_Command) would be the primary user of this capability, rapidly launching up to 100 tons of cargo anywhere in the world.[^cite_note-auto11-208]

The U.S. Armed Forces is the [world's third largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_active_troops) military by active personnel, after the Chinese [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) and the [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md), consisting of 1,359,685 service members in the regular armed forces with an additional 799,845 service members in the reserves as of 28 February 2019.[^cite_note-246]

While the United States Armed Forces is an all-volunteer military, conscription through the [Selective Service System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_Service_System) can be enacted at the president's request and Congress' approval, with all males ages 18 through 25 living in the United States required to register with the Selective Service.[^cite_note-247]

Although the constitutionality of registering only males for Selective Service was [challenged by federal district court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_for_Men_v._Selective_Service_System) in 2019, its legality was upheld by a federal appeals court in 2020.[^cite_note-McGill-248]

As in most militaries, members of the U.S. Armed Forces hold a rank, either that of [officer](36301328.md), [warrant officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_Officer_\(United_States\)) or [enlisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlisted_rank), to determine seniority and eligibility for promotion. Those who have served are known as [veterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veteran#United_States).

Rank names may be different between services, but they are matched to each other by their corresponding [pay grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._uniformed_services_pay_grades).[^cite_note-249]

Officers who hold the same rank or pay grade are distinguished by their date of rank to determine seniority. Officers who serve in certain positions of office of importance set by law, outrank all other officers on active duty of the same rank and pay grade, regardless of their date of rank.[^cite_note-senior_officers-250]

### Rank structure

Rank in the United States Armed Forces is split into three distinct categories: [officers](36301328.md), [warrant officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer_\(United_States\)), and [enlisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlisted_rank) personnel.

Officers are the leadership of the military, holding commissions from the president of the United States and confirmed to their rank by the [Senate](24909346.md).

Warrant officers hold a warrant from the secretaries of the military departments, serving as specialists in certain military technologies and capabilities. Upon promotion to chief warrant officer 2, they gain a commission from the president of the United States.

Enlisted personnel constitute the majority of the armed forces, serving as specialists and tactical-level leaders until they become senior non-commissioned officers or senior petty officers.

Military ranks across the services can be compared by U.S. Uniformed Services pay grade or NATO rank code.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-251]

Officers represent the top 18% of the armed forces, serving in leadership and command roles.[^cite_note-bls.gov-255] Officers are divided into three categories:[^cite_note-ReferenceC-251]

- O-1 to O-3: [Company grade officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_grade_officers) in the Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force or [junior officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_officer) in the Navy and the Coast Guard.

- O-4 to O-6: [Field grade officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_grade_officers) in the Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force or [mid-grade officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-grade_officers) in the Navy and Coast Guard.

- O-7 to O-10: [General officers](148537.md) in the Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force or [flag officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_and_general_officer_flags_of_United_States_military) in the Navy and Coast Guard.

Officers are typically commissioned as second lieutenants or ensigns with a bachelor's degree after several years of training and education or [directly commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_commission_officer) from civilian life into a specific specialty, such as a medical professional, lawyer, chaplain, or cyber specialist.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257]

- The United States Military Academy commissions officers into the United States Army.

  The [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) commissions officers into the [United States Army](32087.md).

- The United States Naval Academy commissions officers into the United States Marine Corps and United States Navy.

  The [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) commissions officers into the [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) and [United States Navy](20518076.md).

- The United States Air Force Academy commissions officers into the United States Air Force and United States Space Force.

  The [United States Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Academy) commissions officers into the [United States Air Force](32090.md) and [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force).

- The United States Coast Guard Academy commissions officers into the United States Coast Guard.

  The [United States Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy) commissions officers into the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md).

Officers are commissioned through the [United States service academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_service_academies), [Reserve Officer Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officer_Training_Corps) programs, and the [Officer Candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_Candidate_School) and [Officer Training Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_Training_School).

During a time of war, officers may be promoted to [five-star ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-star_rank), with [general of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_of_the_Army_\(United_States\)), [fleet admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_admiral_\(United_States\)), and [general of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_of_the_Air_Force) the only five-star ranks currently authorized.[^cite_note-258]

[Warrant officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer_\(United_States\)) are specialists, accounting for only 8% of the officer corps.[^cite_note-bls.gov-255] Warrant officers hold warrants from their service secretary and are specialists and experts in certain military technologies or capabilities.

The lowest-ranking warrant officers serve under a warrant, but they receive commissions from the president upon promotion to chief warrant officer 2.

They derive their authority from the same source as commissioned officers but remain specialists, in contrast to commissioned officers, who are generalists.

There are no warrant officers in the Air Force or Space Force.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-251]

Warrant officers are typically non-commissioned officers before being selected, with the exception of Army Aviation where any enlisted grade can apply for a warrant.

Army warrant officers attend the [Army Warrant Officer Candidate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_Officer_Candidate_School_\(United_States_Army\)).[^cite_note-261]

Air Force basic trainees in a base defense exercise at [Air Force Basic Military Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Basic_Military_Training)

Enlisted personnel comprise 82% of the armed forces, serving as specialists and tactical leaders.[^cite_note-bls.gov-255] Enlisted personnel are divided into three categories:

- E-1 to E-3/4: Junior enlisted personnel are usually in initial training or at their first assignment. E-1 to E-3 in the Marine Corps, Navy, and Coast Guard, and E-1 to E-4 in the Army, Air Force, and Space Force. In the Army, specialists (E-4) are considered to be junior enlisted, while corporals (E-4) are non-commissioned officers.

- E-4/5 to E-6: [Non-commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer) in the Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force and [petty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty_officer) in the Navy and Coast Guard. In the Air Force and Space Force, E-5 is the first non-commissioned officer rank. Non-commissioned officers and petty officers are responsible for tactical leadership.

- E-7 to E-9: [Senior non-commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_non-commissioned_officers) in the Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force and [chief petty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_petty_officer) in the Navy and Coast Guard. Serve as senior enlisted advisors to officers.

The rank of [senior enlisted advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_enlisted_advisor) is the highest rank in each service, serving as the primary advisor to its service secretary and service chief on enlisted matters.

Prior to entering service, enlisted personnel must complete their service's [basic training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training).

In the Army, after completing [Basic Combat Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Basic_Training), recruits then go to [advanced individual training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_individual_training) for their [military occupational specialty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_occupation_code).

Upon completion of [Marine Corps Recruit Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Recruit_Training), Infantry Marines attend the [School of Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_School_of_Infantry). Non-infantry Marines complete Marine Combat Training before advancing to technical schools for their [Military Occupational Specialty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Marine_Corps_MOS).

In the Navy, after completing [Recruit Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_Training_Command,_Great_Lakes,_Illinois), sailors advance to their "A" schools to complete training for their [rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_ratings).

In the Air Force and Space Force, recruits complete combined [Basic Military Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Basic_Military_Training) before going to technical training for their [Air Force Specialty Codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Specialty_Code).

In the Coast Guard, after completing [Recruit Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Training_Center_Cape_May), sailors advance to their "A" schools to complete training for their [rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Coast_Guard_ratings).

### Women in the armed forces



The existing four-star women in the United States Armed Forces in March 2023 during [Women's History Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_History_Month). From left to right: Admiral [Linda L. Fagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_L._Fagan), General [Jacqueline Van Ovost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Van_Ovost), General [Laura J. Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_J._Richardson) and Admiral [Lisa Franchetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Franchetti).



Two female Marines of the [2nd Marine Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Marine_Regiment) patrolling in Afghanistan in 2010

Women such as [Deborah Sampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_Sampson) disguised themselves as men to join the military during the Revolutionary War and War of 1812. Some historians estimate that as many as 400 women disguised themselves as men to enlist during the Civil War.

The first woman doctor in the Army, [Mary Edwards Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Edwards_Walker), was commissioned in 1864. In 1901, the [United States Army Nurse Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Nurse_Corps) was established as a quasi-military auxiliary, followed by the [United States Navy Nurse Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_Nurse_Corps) in 1908.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 4–9 </sup>

Women were not accepted in the armed forces outside of medical roles until World War I, when they were allowed to enlist to perform clerical roles.

Women were accepted into the Naval Reserve Force in 1917, and the Marine Corps Reserves and Coast Guard in 1918.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 9–10 </sup>

The War Department forbid Army and National Guard posts from employing any women except as nurses,[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 13 </sup> but the need for telephone operators overseas during World War I became urgent and hundreds of "[Hello Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Girls)" were recruited.

These members of the Army Signal Corps wore military uniforms and took the Army oath, but were classified as civilian employees until 1977 when their military service was officially recognized.[^cite_note-hoffman-264]

After the [Armistice of 11 November 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_11_November_1918) was signed, enlisted women were demobilized and the nurse corps returned to peacetime strength. The Naval Reserve Act of 1916, which authorized the Navy to enlist "citizens", was changed in 1925 to specify "male citizens".[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 16–17 </sup>

During World War II, all branches of the U.S. military enlisted women. The [Woman's Army Auxiliary Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Army_Corps) (WAAC) was established by the Army in 1942 with auxiliary status, and converted to the [Women's Army Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Army_Corps) (WAC) in June 1943.[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 24–25 </sup>

Also formed during this time were the Women's Airforce Service Pilots ([WASPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_Airforce_Service_Pilots)), the Navy's Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Services ([WAVES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WAVES)), the Marine Corps Women's Reserve, and the Coast Guard Women's Reserve ([SPARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPARS)).[^cite_note-desimone-266]

Women experienced combat as nurses in the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) on 7 December 1941, before the U.S. officially entered the war.

In 1944, WACs arrived in the Pacific and in Normandy. During the war, 67 Army nurses and 16 [Navy nurses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_Nurse_Corps) were captured and spent three years as Japanese prisoners of war.

There were 350,000 American women who served during World War II, and 432 were killed in the line of service.[^cite_note-desimone-266] In total, they gained more than 1,500 medals, citations, and commendations.

After World War II, demobilization led to the vast majority of serving women being returned to civilian life. By 1946, the Coast Guard had demobilized all of its women members, while the other branches retained some.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 105 </sup>

Law 625, [The Women's Armed Services Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Armed_Services_Integration_Act) of 1948, was signed by President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman), allowing women to serve in the U.S. Armed Forces in fully integrated units during peace time, albeit with limits that did not apply to men.[^cite_note-267]

The intent of Congress was that women should be noncombatants only, but because of the difficulty in defining restrictions for the Army in the law, it was left up to the [service secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_structure_of_the_United_States_Department_of_Defense#Military_Departments) to comply with that intent, although the law did prohibit women from serving aboard ships and on aircraft that engaged in combat missions.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 118–120 </sup> The Army retained a separate corps for women (WAC), while the other services integrated women into their organizational structure.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 121 </sup>

In 1951, Executive Order 10240 was issued, authorizing the services to discharge women who became pregnant or had minor children in the home (including stepchildren, foster children, and siblings).[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 125 </sup>

During the [Korean War](16772.md) of 1950–1953, many women served in the [Mobile Army Surgical Hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Army_Surgical_Hospital). A recruiting project started in 1951 aimed to increase the number of women in the military from 40,000 to 112,000 by July 1952, but it only achieved 46,000.

Some of the reasons were the lack of public support for involvement in Korea; public disapproval of women in the military; fewer women in the right age group due to the low birthrate during the Depression; and the higher standards required for women enlistees.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 149–155 </sup>[^cite_note-268]

In the 1960s, recruiting and training focused on the attractiveness and femininity of women enlistees. Outside of the medical fields, women were mostly assigned to clerical, administrative, and protocol-related jobs. Women who were previously in technical positions were retrained for the few jobs now permitted for women.

Of the 61 non-combat occupational groups, only 36 were open to women by 1965.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 180–184 </sup> Beginning in 1965, efforts to increase the number of women in the armed forces accompanied concern about the expiration of the Selective Service Act and reduction in enlistment standards to ensure sufficient troops to support the Vietnam War.

Public Law 90-130, signed on 8 November 1967, removed the restrictions on female officers in the armed forces and in 1970, two women Army officers were promoted to brigadier general.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 187–203 </sup>

During the [Vietnam War](32611.md), 600 women served in the country as part of the Air Force, alongside 500 members of the WAC and more than 6,000 medical personnel and support staff.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 205–228 </sup>

The end of conscription in the early 1970s was a major driver of the expansion of the roles of women in the armed forces.

The number of enlisted and commissioned women in the military hit 110,000 by June 1977.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 246–250 </sup>

The [Army Ordnance Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Ordnance_Corps) began accepting female missile technicians in 1974.[^cite_note-269] Female crew members and officers were accepted into [Field Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Artillery_Branch_\(United_States\)) missile units.[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271]

The services opened up their [Reserve Officers' Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officers'_Training_Corps) (ROTC) programs to women, and in 1976, women were admitted to the service academies.[^cite_note-holm-263]<sup>: 268–270 </sup>

In 1974, the first six female naval aviators earned their wings as Navy pilots. The [Combat Exclusion Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_Exclusion_Policy) that prohibited women in combat placed limitations on the pilots' advancement,[^cite_note-272] but at least two retired as captains.[^cite_note-273]

The role of women in the U.S. Armed Forces received global media attention during the 1991 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), though their perception in media was skewed during this time period as little media attention was given to the situations where women faced combat.[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275]

In 1991, women were permitted to fly military aircraft. Since 1994, women have been permitted to serve on U.S. combat ships.[^cite_note-276]

In 2010, the ban on women serving on submarines was lifted.[^cite_note-277]



A female U.S. Air Force fighter pilot at [Osan Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osan_Air_Base) in South Korea in 2017

On 3 December 2015, U.S. defense secretary Ashton Carter announced that all military combat positions would become available to women.[^cite_note-278] This gave women access to the roughly 10% of military jobs which were previously closed to them.[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-\:1-280]

The various military services were given until January 2016 to provide plans on how they would enforce the policy change.[^cite_note-\:0-281] Many women believed this would allow them to improve their positions in the military, since combat experience can contribute to career advancement.[^cite_note-282]

[Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_the_United_States) registration for females was recommended by the [National Commission on Military, National, and Public Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Commission_on_Military,_National,_and_Public_Service) and has been proposed, but never implemented.[^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285]

No woman has ever become a [Navy SEAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs).[^cite_note-286][^cite_note-287]

In July 2021, the first woman graduated from the Naval Special Warfare (NSW) training program to become a Special Warfare Combatant craft Crewman (SWCC). The SWCC directly supports the SEALs and other special forces units, and are experts in covert insertion and extraction special operation tactics.[^cite_note-288][^cite_note-289]

Despite concerns of a gender gap, all personnel both men and women, at the same rank and time of service, are compensated the same across all branches.[^cite_note-290]

On 1 June 2022, ADM [Linda L. Fagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_L._Fagan) assumed command of the [U.S. Coast Guard](32223.md), becoming not only the first woman to serve as [Commandant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant_of_the_Coast_Guard) of the Coast Guard, but also the first woman in American history to serve as a service chief in the U.S. Military.[^cite_note-291]

A study conducted by the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation) suggests that women who make the military their careers experience improved rates of promotion.[^cite_note-292]

As per the DoD's report on [sexual assault](218407.md) within the U.S. Army for fiscal year 2019, 7,825 cases had been reported. This represented a 3% increase relative to the 2018 report.[^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294]

As of 2022, there are 228,966 women in the military, representing 17.5% of the total active duty force. Since 2021, the percentage of women on active duty service has increased slightly, by 0.3%. Since 2005, the population of active duty women has increased by 2.9%.[^cite_note-295][^cite_note-296]

### Restrictions on political activity

It has been a long-standing policy in the armed forces to limit the political activity by active duty members to voting and making personal campaign donations.

However, all other political activities, such as campaigning for a candidate (even outside military facilities), endorsing candidates, soliciting contributions, marching in a partisan parade or wearing the uniform to a partisan event, are prohibited.[^cite_note-297]

## Order of precedence



The Joint Armed Forces Color Guard at [Arlington National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_National_Cemetery)



Soldiers, marines, sailors, airmen, guardians, and coast guardsmen from their services ceremonial units stand in formation at [Arlington National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_National_Cemetery)

Under Department of Defense regulation, the various components of the U.S. Armed Forces have a set order of precedence that is based on founding dates.

This order is used for the display of service flags as well as the placement of soldiers, marines, sailors, airmen, guardians, and coast guardsmen in formations and parades.[^cite_note-298]

1. Cadets, [U.S. Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy)

2. Midshipmen, [U.S. Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy)

3. Cadets, [U.S. Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Academy)

4. Cadets, [U.S. Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy)

5. Midshipmen, [U.S. Merchant Marine Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Merchant_Marine_Academy)

6. United States Army

7. United States Marine Corps

8. United States Navy

9. United States Air Force

10. United States Space Force

11. United States Coast Guard

12. Army National Guard

13. United States Army Reserve

14. United States Marine Corps Reserve

15. United States Navy Reserve

16. Air National Guard

17. United States Air Force Reserve

18. United States Coast Guard Reserve

19. Other training and auxiliary organizations of the Army, Marine Corps, [Merchant Marine](307054.md), [Civil Air Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Air_Patrol), and [Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary), as in the preceding order

While the original founding date of a U.S. Navy was earlier than that of the Marine Corps,[^cite_note-precedence-299] the Marine Corps takes precedence due to previous inconsistencies in the Navy's birth date. The Marine Corps has recognized its observed birth date on a more consistent basis.

The [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress) is considered to have established the Navy on 13 October 1775 by authorizing the purchase of ships, but the ["Rules for the Regulation of the Navy of the United Colonies"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_Regulations#History) were not established until 27 November 1775,[^cite_note-300] and the Navy also lost funding and was temporarily discontinued in 1785.[^cite_note-preced2024-301]

The Marine Corps was established by an act of the Second Continental Congress on 10 November 1775. The Navy did not officially recognize 13 October 1775 as its birth date until 1972, when then–[chief of naval operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations) Admiral [Elmo Zumwalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmo_Zumwalt) authorized it to be observed as such.[^cite_note-precedence-299]

The Coast Guard is normally situated after the Space Force, but if it is moved to the Department of the Navy, then its place in the order of precedence would change to being situated after the Navy and before the Air Force.[^cite_note-precedence-299]
