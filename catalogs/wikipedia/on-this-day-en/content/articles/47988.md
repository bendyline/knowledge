The **Godavari** ([IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAST): *Godāvarī*, IPA: [\[ɡoːd̪ɑːʋɐɾiː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Sanskrit)) is [India](14533.md)'s second longest river after the [Ganges River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_River), and drains the third largest [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) in India, covering about 10% of the total geographical area.[^cite_note-5] Its [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_source) is in [Trimbakeshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimbakeshwar_Range), [Nashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik), [Maharashtra](20629.md).[^cite_note-6] It flows east for 1,465 kilometres (910 mi), draining the states of [Maharashtra](20629.md) (48.6%), [Telangana](990267.md) (18.8%), [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) (4.5%), [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) (10.9%), and [Odisha](250724.md) (5.7%). The river ultimately empties into the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) through an extensive network of distributaries.[^cite_note-7] Its 312,812 km<sup>2</sup> (120,777 sq mi) drainage basin is one of the largest in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), with only the [Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga) and [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus) rivers having a larger drainage basin.[^cite_note-8] In terms of length, catchment area and discharge, the Godavari is the largest in peninsular India, and had been dubbed as the **Dakshina Ganga** (Southern [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges)).[^cite_note-9]

In the past few decades, the river has been barricaded by several [barrages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrage_\(dam\)) and [dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dams), keeping a head of water (depth) which lowers evaporation. Its broad [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) houses 729 persons/km<sup>2</sup> – nearly twice the Indian average population density and has a substantial risk of flooding, which in lower parts would be exacerbated if the [global sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Sea_Level_Observing_System) level were to rise.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Course

[^cite_note-12]

The Godavari originates in the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats) of central India, near [Nashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik) in [Maharashtra](20629.md), 80 km (50 mi) from the [Arabian Sea](2747.md). It flows for 1,465 km (910 mi), first eastwards across the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau), then turns southeast, entering the [Eluru district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eluru_district) and [Alluri Sitharama Raju district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluri_Sitharama_Raju_district) of Andhra Pradesh, until it splits into two distributaries that widen into a large [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) at [Dhavaleshwaram Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowleswaram_Barrage), in [Rajamahendravaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajamahendravaram), and then flows into the Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-13]

The Godavari River has a coverage area of 312,812 km<sup>2</sup> (120,777 sq mi), which is nearly one-tenth of the area of India and is equivalent to the area of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) put together. The river basin is considered to be divided into 3 sections:&#x20;

- Upper (source to the confluence with [Manjira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjira_River)),

- Middle (between confluence of Manjira and [Pranhita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pranhita)) and

- Lower (Pranhita confluence to mouth).

These put together account for 24.2% of the total basin area.[^cite_note-14] The rivers annual average water inflows are nearly 110 billion cubic metres.[^cite_note-15] Nearly 50% of the water availability is being harnessed. The water allocation from the river among the riparian states are governed by the [Godavari Water Disputes Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari_Water_Disputes_Tribunal). The river has highest flood flows in India and experienced recorded flood of 3.6 million [cusecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_foot#Cubic_foot_per_second) in the year 1986 and annual flood of 1.0 million cusecs is normal.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Within Maharashtra

The river originates in [Maharashtra](20629.md) state and has an extensive course. The upper basin (origin to its confluence with [Manjira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjira_River)), of which lies entirely within the state, cumulatively draining an area as large as 152,199 km<sup>2</sup> (58,764 sq mi) – about half the area of Maharashtra.[^cite_note-18] Within [Nashik district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik_district), the river assumes a north-easterly course until it flows into the [Gangapur Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangapur_Dam), created by a dam of the same name. The reservoir along with the [Kashypi Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashypi_Dam), provides [potable water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potable_water) to Nashik, one of the largest cities located on its banks. The river, as it emerges through the dam, some 8 km (5.0 mi) upstream from Nashik, flows on a rocky bed, undulated by a series of chasms and rocky ledges, resulting in the formation of two significant waterfalls – the Gangapur and the Someshwar waterfalls. The latter, located at Someshwar, is more popularly known as the Dudhsagar Waterfall.[^cite_note-19] About 10 km (6.2 mi) east of Gangapur, the river passes the town of [Nashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik), where it collects its effluents in the form of the river Nandini on its right bank.

About 0.5 km (0.31 mi) south of Nashik, the river bends sharply to the east, washing the base of a high cliff formerly the site of a [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) fort, but which is now being eroded away by the action of floods. About 25 km (16 mi) below Nashik is the confluence of the Godavari and one of its tributaries, the [Darna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darna_River). The stream occupies, for nine months in the year, a small space in a wide and gravelly bed, the greyish banks being 4 to 6 m (13 to 20 ft) high, topped with a deep layer of black soil. A few kilometres after its meeting with the Darna, the Godavari swerves to the north-east, before the [Banganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banganga_River_\(Maharashtra\)), from the north-west, meets it on the left. The course of the main stream then tends more decidedly south. At Nandur-Madhmeshwar, the [Kadva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadva_River), a second large affluent, brings considerable increase to the waters of the Godavari. The river begins its southeasterly course characteristic of rivers of the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau). The river exits the [Niphad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niphad) Taluka of [Nashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik) and enters the [Kopargaon taluka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopargaon_taluka), [Ahmednagar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmednagar_district). Within Ahmednagar District the river quickly completes its short course, flowing alongside the town of [Kopargaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopargaon) and reaching [Puntamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puntamba). Beyond this, the river serves as a natural boundary between the following districts:

- [Ahmednagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmednagar_district) and [Aurangabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangabad_district,_Maharashtra): Along the boundary here, it receives its first major tributary the [Pravara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravara_River), draining the former district. The confluence is located at Pravarasangam. By virtue of a sub-tributary of Pravara – Mandohol, which originates in Pune District – the basin impinges the [Pune district](3123898.md). The river at Paithan has been impounded by the [Jayakwadi Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayakwadi_Dam) forming the NathSagar Reservoir. [Kalsubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalsubai) located in Godavari basin, is the highest peak in Maharashtra.

- [Beed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beed_district) and [Jalna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalna_district)

- Beed and [Parbhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parbhani_district): Located along here is its merger with [Sindphana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindphana_River), an important tributary

  [^cite_note-20]

  &#x20;which drains a considerably large area within Beed. The sub-tributary river [Bindusara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bindusara_River) forms a landmark at [Beed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beed).

The river beyond, near the village Sonpeth, flows into [Parbhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parbhani_district). In Parbhani district, the river flows through [Gangakhed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangakhed) taluka. As mentioned above, the Godavari is also called Dakshinganga so the city is called as Gangakhed (meaning a village on the bank of Ganga). As per Hindu rituals this place is considered quite important for after death peace to flow ashes into the river.

Its course is relatively non-significant except for receiving two smaller streams – Indrayani and Masuli – merging at its left and right banks, respectively. Within the last taluka of the district Parbhani, [Purna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purna), the river drains a major tributary of the same name: [Purna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purna_River_\(tributary_of_Godavari\)).

It then exits into the neighbouring district of [Nanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanded_district) where 10 km (6.2 mi) before reaching the town Nanded, it is impounded by the Vishnupuri Dam and thus with it, bringing Asia's largest lift irrigation projects to life. A little downstream from Nanded, the river receives Asna, a small stream, on its left bank. It then runs into the controversial [Babli project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babli_project) soon ends its course within [Maharashtra](20629.md), albeit temporarily, at its merger with a major tributary – [Manjira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjira_River).

The river after flowing into Telangana, re-emerges to run as a state boundary separating the [Mancherial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancherial_district), [Telangana](990267.md) from [Gadchiroli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadchiroli_district), Maharashtra. At the state border, it runs between [Sironcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sironcha) and Somnoor Sangam receiving one tributary at each of those nodal points – the [Pranhita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pranhita_River) and subsequently the [Indravati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indravati_River).

### Within Telangana



Road bridge over Godavari River at Bhadrachalam

Godavari enters into Telangana in Nizamabad district at Kandakurthy where Manjira, Haridra rivers joins Godavari and forms Triveni Sangamam. The river flows along the border between [Nirmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirmal_district) and [Mancherial districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancherial_district) in the north and [Nizamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamabad_district), [Jagtial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagtial_district), [Peddapalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peddapalli_district) districts to its south. About 12 km (7.5 mi) after entering [Telangana](990267.md) it merges with the back waters of the [Sriram Sagar Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriram_Sagar_Project). The river after emerging through the dam gates, enjoys a wide river bed, often splitting to encase sandy islands. The river receives a minor but significant tributary [Kadam river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadam_river). It then emerges at its eastern side to act as a state border with Maharashtra only to later enter into [Bhadradri Kothagudem district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadradri_Kothagudem_district). In this district, the river flows through an important Hindu pilgrimage town – [Bhadrachalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadrachalam).

The river further swells after receiving a minor tributary [Kinnerasani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinnerasani_River) and exits into [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md).

### Within Andhra Pradesh



The river at [Papi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papi_Hills) near [Rajamahendravaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajamahendravaram) in [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md).

Within the state of [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md), the river flows through hilly terrain of the [Eastern Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ghats) known as the [Papi hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papi_Hills) which explains the narrowing of its bed as it flows through a gorge for a few km, only to re-widen at [Polavaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polavaram,_West_Godavari_district). The deepest bed level of a submarine [plunge pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plunge_pool) in Godavari River, located 36 km upstream of Polavaram dam, is at 45 meters below the sea level.[^cite_note-21] Before crossing the Papi hills, it receives its last major tributary [Sabari River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabari_River) on its left bank. The river upon reaching the plains begins to widen out until it reaches [Rajamahendravaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajamahendravaram). [Arma Konda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arma_Konda) (1,680 m (5,510 ft)) is the highest peak in the Godavari river basin as well as in the Eastern Ghats.[^cite_note-Pletcher2013-22]

[Dowleswaram Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowleswaram_Barrage) was constructed across the river in Rajamahendravaram. At Rajamahendravaram, the Godavari splits into two large branches which are called Gautami (Gautami Godavari) and Vasishta Godavari and five smaller branches. Similarly, the Vasishta splits into two branches named Vasishta and Vainateya. These four branches which join the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) at different places, form a delta of length 170 km (110 mi) along the coast of the Bay of Bengal and is called the [Konaseema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konaseema) region. This delta along with the delta of the [Krishna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River) is called the *Rice Granary* of [South India](678583.md).[^cite_note-23]

### Within Puducherry

The Gautami which is the largest branch of the whole passes along [Yanam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanam) enclave of [Union territory of Puducherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory_of_Puducherry) and empties into sea at *Point Godavery*.[^cite_note-Godaverymouths-24] In fact, Yanam is bounded on south by Gautami branch and the [Coringa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coringa_River) originates at Yanam which merges into the sea near [Coringa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coringa,_East_Godavari_district) village in Andhra Pradesh.[^cite_note-25]

## Tributaries

The major left bank [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary)[^cite_note-26] include the [Purna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purna_River_\(tributary_of_Godavari\)), [Pranhita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pranhita), [Indravati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indravati), and [Sabari River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabari_River), covering nearly 59.7% of the total catchment area of the basin. The right bank tributaries [Pravara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravara_River), [Manjira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjira_River), and [Manair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manair_River) contribute 16.1% of the basin.

The Pranhita River is the largest tributary of the Godavari River, covering about 34% of its drainage basin.[^cite_note-27] Though the river proper flows only for 113 km (70 mi), by virtue of its extensive tributaries [Wardha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wardha_River), [Wainganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wainganga), [Penganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painganga_River), the sub-basin drains all of [Vidharba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidharba) region as well as the southern slopes of the [Satpura Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satpura_Range). Indravati is the 2nd largest tributary, known as the "lifeline" of the [Kalahandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahandi), [Nabarangapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabarangapur) of [Odisha](250724.md) and [Bastar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastar_district) of [Chhattisgarh](47734.md). Due to their enormous sub-basins both Indravati and Pranhita are considered rivers in their own right. Manjira is the longest tributary and holds the [Nizam Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_Sagar) reservoir. Purna is a prime river in the water scarce [Marathwada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathwada) region of [Maharashtra](20629.md).

### Seven mouths of Godavari

Before merging into the Bay of Bengal, the Godavari has seven mouths in total and is considered sacred by local Hindus.[^cite_note-GodavariReferenceBritish-29] As per their traditional belief, the holy waters of the Godavari are said to have been brought from the head of [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) by the *Rishi [Gautama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Maharishi)*, and the seven branches by which it is traditionally supposed to have reached the sea are said to have been made by seven great rishis known as [Sapta Rishis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapta_Rishis). Thus, they are named after these seven great rishis[^cite_note-sevenRishis-30] and are named as **Tulyabhāga** (Tulya or [Kaśyapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ka%C5%9Byapa)), **Ātreya** ([Atri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atri)), **Gautamī** ([Gautama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Maharishi)), **[Jamadagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamadagni)** (now replaced by **Vṛddhagautamī** i.e. Old Gautami), **Bhardvāja** ([Bharadvaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharadwaja)), **Kauśika** ([Visvamitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viswamitra)) and **Vaśișțha** ([Vasishtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasishtha)).[^cite_note-31] So bathing in these mouths are considered an act of great religious efficacy by native Hindus. These mouths are remembered by a Sanskrit sloka as follow:

> *_tulyātreyī bharadvāja gautamī vṛddhagautamī\
> kauśikīca vaśiṣṭhaaca tathā sāgaraṃ gataḥ_*\
> \
> (Godavari becomes) Tulya, Ātreyi, Bharadvāja, Gautamī, Vṛddhagautamī,\
> Kauśikī and Vaśiṣṭhaa and then passes into sea.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

Together they are referred as *Sapta Godavari* and the Godavari river before splitting is referred as *Akhanda Godavari*.[^cite_note-sevenRishis-30] However, there exists another eight mouth named as **Vainateyam**, which is not one of these traditional seven mouths and is supposed to have been created by a rishi of that name who stole a part of Vasisththa branch. Godavari was frequently referred as **Ganga** or **Ganges** by ancient Indian writings.[^cite_note-GodavariReferenceBritish-29] However, the original branches of *Kauśika*, *Bhardwaja* and *Jamadagni* does not exist any longer and the pilgrims bathe in the sea at the spots where they are supposed to have been. The traditional Bharadwāja mouth is in *Tirthālamondi* (now bordering Savithri Nagar of [Yanam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanam) and before a Hamlet of [Guttenadivi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guttenadeevi)) and the traditional Kauśika mouth is located at Rameswaram, a hamlet of Samathakurru village in Allavaram Mandal of Konaseema district. Traditional mouth of Jamadagni is not known and people instead take bath in the Vriddha Gautami branch at Kundaleswaram village in [Katrenikona Mandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katrenikona_Mandal) of Konaseema district. There is a local legend saying the *[Injaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injaram)* and *Patha (Old) Injaram* (now on the other bank of Gautami river within Island Polavalam mandal of [Konaseema district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konaseema_district)) were split by Godavari river. Thus the Godavari passing between these two now referred as Gautami and the old passage being referred as Vriddha Gautami. In early British records, the Injaram *Paragana* (district) was counted along with Muramalla village (now located on the other side of Gautami within Island Polavalam mandal) and said to have comprised 22 villages.[^cite_note-IngeramParagana-34]

Godavari Statue at Gangadwar, worshiped as origin of Godavari, Triambak



Goddess Godavari

The river is sacred to [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) and has several places on its banks that have been places of pilgrimage for thousands of years. Amongst the huge numbers of people who have bathed in her waters as a rite of cleansing are said to have been the deity [Baladeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balarama) 5000 years ago and the saint [Chaitanya Mahaprabhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitanya_Mahaprabhu) 500 years ago. Every twelve years, the [Pushkaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari_Maha_Pushkaram) fair is held on the banks of the river.[^cite_note-\:0-35]



Sculpture depicting govu vatsa and gowthama legend about birth of Godavari River

A legend has it that the sage Gautama lived in the Brahmagiri Hills at [Tryambakeshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryambakeshwar) with his wife [Ahalya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahalya). The couple lived the rest of their lives in the then village called *Govuru*, now known as [Kovvur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovvur) ("cow") since British rule. Ahalya lived in a nearby place called *Thagami* (now [Thogummi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thogummi)). The sage, as a reason for the practice of *annadanam* ("giving away food" to the needy), started cultivating rice crops and other crops. Once, the god [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha), on the wish of the sages, sent a miraculous cow *mayadhenu*, which resembled a normal cow. It entered the sage's abode and started spoiling the rice while he was meditating. Since cattle is sacred to Hindus and treated with respect, he put the *[darbha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusha_grass)* grass on the cow. But, to his surprise, it fell dead. Seeing what happened before their eyes, the sages and their wives cried out, "We thought that Gautama-maharishi is a righteous man, but he committed bovicide (killing of a cow or cattle)!". The sage wished to atone for this grievous sin. Therefore, he went to [Nashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik) and observed [tapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapas_\(Sanskrit\)) (penance) to propitiate Tryambakeshvara (a manifestation of the god Shiva), on the advice of the sages, praying for atonement and asking him to make the Ganges flow over the cow. Shiva was pleased with the sage and diverted the Ganges, which washed away the cow and gave rise to the Godavari River in Nashik. The water stream flowed past Kovvur and ultimately merged with the Bay of Bengal.

### Sapta Sāgara Yatra

In olden days a pilgrimage named as *sapta sāgara yātra* was made by those desirous of offspring along the banks of the holy waters from the seven mouths.[^cite_note-36] It starts with holy bathing at Tulyabhaga river at [Chollangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chollangi) village on Amavasya during Krishna Paksha of [Pushya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushya) month as per Hindu calendar. That day is locally referred as *Chollangi Amavasya*. That place where the river branch merges with sea is referred as *Tulya Sāgara Sangamam*.[^cite_note-37] Secondly, they take bath in [Coringa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coringa,_East_Godavari_district) village in the Coringa river which is considered as Atreya branch of Godavari and the holy bathing place is called as *Atreya Sāgara Sangamam*.[^cite_note-38] After bathing at different banks of the other branches the pilgrimage ends by bathing near [Narsapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narsapuram) or [Antarvedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarvedi).

There are 4 bridges spanning the river between [East Godavari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Godavari) and [West Godavari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Godavari) districts.

1. [Old Godavari Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Godavari_Bridge) (also known as Havelock bridge, and named after then Madras governor)

2. [Godavari Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari_Bridge) (also known as Rail-cum-road bridge and Kovvur-Rajahmundry Bridge)

3. [Godavari Arch Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari_Arch_Bridge) (also known as New railway bridge)

4. [Godavari Fourth Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari_Fourth_Bridge) (also known as new road bridge)

Details:

- Old Godavari Bridge. Construction of this bridge started in 1876, and was completed in 1897. It was constructed under the supervision of F.T. Granville Walton who had constructed the [Dufferin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malviya_Bridge) over the Ganges, and Granville Mills, both British engineers. Spanning over 3 km in length, it linked the East Godavari and West Godavari districts. The bridge has been a vital link enabling trains to run between [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) and [Howrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howrah). Trains continued over the bridge for a century until 1997, when train services over the bridge were suspended after the construction of two additional bridges.

- Godavari Bridge. Construction of this bridge started in 1970, and was completed in 1974. It serves as both a railway and a roadway between the East Godavari and West Godavari Districts.

- Godavari Arch Bridge. This bridge was completed in 1997, was built upstream of the earlier bridges.

- Godavari Fourth Bridge. This bridge is the newest. It was opened to public from Godavari Pushkaras 2015. This is a road connectivity bridge link supposed to ease traffic flow between Rajamahendravaram and Kovvur

## Dams



An upstream view of Jayakwadi Dam.



[Sriram Sagar Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriram_Sagar_Project).



[Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Arthur_Cotton_Barrage) at [Rajamahendravaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajamahendravaram) City.

The main Godavari River up to the confluence with Pranhita tributary is dammed fully to utilize the available water for irrigation. However, its main tributaries Pranhita, Indravati and Sabari which join in the lower reaches of the basin, carry three times more water compared to main Godavari. In 2015, the water surplus Godavari River is linked to the water deficit [Krishna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River) by commissioning the [Polavaram right bank canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polavaram_Project) with the help of Pattiseema lift scheme to augment water availability to the [Prakasam Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakasam_Barrage) located in Andhra Pradesh. More dams are constructed in the Godavari River basin than in any other river basin of India.[^cite_note-42] The following are the few dams located in the river basin:

- [Gangapur Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangapur_Dam): This is a large earth fill dam with gross water storage of 215.88 million cubic metres,

  [^cite_note-43]

  &#x20;and located 10 km (6.2 mi) upstream from [Nashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik) city. The reservoir known as the Gangapur Bandh Sagar provides drinking water to the Nashik city and also supplies water to the thermal power station situated downstream at [Eklahare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eklahare).

- [Jayakwadi Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayakwadi_Dam): Located near [Paithan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paithan), it is one of the largest earthen dams in India. This dam was built to address the dual problems of flooding along the banks, during monsoon months, and that of drought, rest of the year, in the [Marathwada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathwada) region. Two 'left' and 'right' canals provide the irrigation to fertile land up to [Nanded district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanded_district). This dam has contributed to industrial development of [Aurangabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangabad_district,_Maharashtra) and [Jalna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalna_District), [Maharashtra](20629.md).

  [^cite_note-JDR-44]

  &#x20;[Majalgaon Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majalgaon_Dam) is also constructed under Jayakwadi stage 2 to expand the irrigation potential further in [Parbhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parbhani_district), [Nanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanded_district) and [Beed districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beed_district).

- Vishnupuri barrage: Asia's Largest Lift Irrigation project, the Vishnupuri Prakalp

  [^cite_note-45]

  &#x20;has been constructed on the river at a distance of 5 km (3.1 mi) from the city [Nanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanded).

- [Ghatghar Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghatghar_Dam) was built for hydro power generation by diverting the water of Pravara tributary outside Godavari river basin to a west flowing river which joins Arabian sea.

- [Upper Vaitarna reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Vaitarna_Dam) was built across west flowing [Vaitarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaitarna) river merging some part of Godavari river catchment area. Godavari water impounded in this reservoir is diverted outside the river basin for [Mumbai](19189.md) city drinking water supply after generating hydro power.

- [Sriram Sagar Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriram_Sagar_Project): This is another multipurpose project on the Godavari River on the borders of Adilabad and Nizamabad District. It is near the town of Pochampadu, 60 km away from Nizamabad. It has been described by [The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindu) as a "lifeline for a large part of [Telangana](990267.md)".

  [^cite_note-46]

  &#x20;It serves the irrigation needs in [Karimnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimnagar), [Warangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warangal), [Adilabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adilabad), [Nalgonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalgonda), and [Khammam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khammam) districts and also generates power.

- [Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Arthur_Cotton_Barrage) was built by Sir [Arthur Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Cotton) in 1852. It got damaged in 1987 floods, and rebuilt as a barrage cum roadway soon after and named after him. The roadway connects [Rajamahendravaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajamahendravaram) in East Godavari and [Vijjeswaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijjeswaram) in West Godavari. The irrigation canals of this barrage also form part of [National Waterway 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Waterway_4).

## Geology and sediment transfer in the Godavari Drainage Basin



Generalized Geological Map of Godavari Drainage Basin

The primary and initial catchment of the Godavari drainage basin is largely represented by the basalt of the [Deccan Volcanic Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Traps) (\~50% of the total basin area). This is followed by the [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) [granites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and [gneisses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gneiss) of the eastern [Dharwar Craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharwar_craton), [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone), [shales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale) and [limestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) of the [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana) Supergroup, various sedimentary units of Cuddapah and Vindhyan basins, [charnockites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charnockite) and [khondalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khondalite) of the [Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic) [Eastern Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ghats) Mobile Belt and the sandstones of the Rajahmundry Formation.[^cite_note-Kulkarni_376–384-52] The Godavari River carries the largest sediment load among the peninsular rivers and the majority of the mass transfer in Godavari occurs during the monsoon.[^cite_note-53] Mineral magnetic studies of the Godavari River sediments suggest that the floodplains in the entire stretch of the river are characterized by a Deccan basalt source. The bed loads on the other hand are of sourced from local bedrock. Influx of Deccan source in the Godavari River up to the delta regions and possibly in the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) off the Godavari, therefore, can be related to the intensive [chemical weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_weathering) in the Deccan basalts.[^cite_note-Kulkarni_376–384-52] Abrupt increase in δ<sup>13</sup>C values and decrease in TOC content accompanied with a significant increase in ferrimagnetic mineral concentration in Bay of Bengal sediments from \~3.2 to 3.1 cal. ka BP reflected a shift of organic carbon and sediment source and a severe decline in vegetation coverage. Such phenomena indicate intensified deforestation and soil/rock erosion in the Deccan Plateau producing higher ferrimagnetic mineral inputs, which is in agreement with significant expansion of agricultural activities in the Deccan Chalcolithic cultural period.[^cite_note-54]

## Ecological concerns



Dried up Godavari exposing [flood basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_basalt) river bed as seen from the back of Changdev temple in [Puntamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puntamba)

The frequent drying up of the Godavari River in the drier months has been a matter of great concern. Indiscriminate damming along the river has been cited as an obvious reason. Within Maharashtra sugarcane irrigation has been blamed as one of the foremost causes.[^cite_note-55]

In 2013, the river was at its all-time low in the [Nizamabad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamabad_district) of Telangana. This had hit the growth of fish, making the life of fishermen miserable. The water-level was so low that people could easily walk into the middle of the river. Shortage in rainfall and closure of the controversial [Babli project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babli_project) gates in Maharashtra was thought to have affected the water flow in the river and water availability to the [Sriram Sagar Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriram_Sagar_Project) except during above 20% excess monsoon (i.e. one out of four years) years.[^cite_note-56]

A study has found that the delta is at a greater risk as the rate of sediment [aggradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggradation) (raising the level of the delta through sediment deposition) no longer exceeds relative [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).[^cite_note-57] It further states that the suspended sediment load at the delta has reduced from 150·2 million tons during 1970–1979 to 57·2 million tons by 2000–2006,[^cite_note-58] which translates into a three-fold decline in the past 4 decades. Impacts of this can be seen in destroyed villages like [Uppada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppada) in Godavari delta,[^cite_note-59] destruction of Mangrove forests and fragmentation of shoreline – possibly a fallout of dam construction.

Said to further epitomise the insensitivity towards Godavari, is the [Polavaram Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polavaram_Project) which is touted to be gigantic – both in terms of size and violations.[^cite_note-60] Deemed as being pointless and politically driven,[^cite_note-61] the project raises questions about environmental clearance, displacement of upstream human habitations,[^cite_note-62] loss of forest cover, technicalities in the dam design which are said to play down flood threats and unsafe embankments.

[High alkalinity water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residual_sodium_carbonate_index) is discharged from the ash dump areas of many coal fired power stations into the river which further increases the [alkalinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkalinity) of the river water whose water is naturally of high alkalinity since the river basin is draining vast area of [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt#Weathering) formations.[^cite_note-63] This problem aggravates during the lean flow months in entire river basin. Already the Godavari basin area in Telangana is suffering from high alkalinity and salinity water problem which is converting soils in to unproductive [sodic alkali soils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkali_soil).[^cite_note-64] The following are the few coal fired power stations located in the river basin:
