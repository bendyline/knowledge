A total [solar eclipse](5024887.md) occurred at the [Moon](19331.md)'s [descending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descending_node) of orbit on Wednesday, 12 August 2026,[^cite_note-August_12,_2026-1] with a [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitude_of_eclipse) of 1.0386. Totality occurred in a narrow path across Earth's surface, with the partial solar eclipse visible over a surrounding region thousands of kilometres wide. Occurring about 2.3 days after [perigee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (on 10 August 2026, at 11\:18 UTC), the Moon's apparent diameter was larger.[^cite_note-astropixels_20220106-2]

The total eclipse passed over northernmost [Siberia](29639.md), the Arctic, Greenland, Iceland, the Atlantic Ocean, northern Spain, a small part of northeastern Portugal, the Mediterranean Sea, and the [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands).[^cite_note-space_20240825-3] The points of greatest duration and greatest eclipse were just 45 km (28 mi) off the western coast of Iceland around 65°30' N and 25°25' W, where the totality lasted 2 minutes and 18.2 seconds.[^cite_note-nasa_20260812-4] The first part of the total eclipse path unusually passed from east to west from Russia to Greenland, passing about 100 km south of the North Pole.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] A partial eclipse covered more than 90% of the Sun in Ireland, Great Britain, and northern Italy, and to a lesser extent across much of the rest of Europe, North Africa, and northern North America.[^cite_note-nasa_20260605-7][^cite_note-8] The total eclipse passed over northern Spain from the Atlantic coast to the Mediterranean coast, as well as the Balearic Islands.[^cite_note-timeanddate_Buckle-9][^cite_note-mappr-10] The total eclipse was visible from the cities of [A Coruña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Coru%C3%B1a), [Valencia](63861.md), [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza), [Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma,_Majorca), and [Bilbao](68029.md), but both [Madrid](41188263.md) and [Barcelona](4443.md) were just outside the path of [totality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totality_\(eclipse\)).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-mappr-10]

This was the first total eclipse to be visible in Europe since [20 March 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_March_20,_2015), and the first one to cross over [Mainland Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe) since [11 August 1999](4557286.md).[^cite_note-27_years-12] It was the first total solar eclipse visible in Iceland since [30 June 1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_June_30,_1954), also [solar saros series 126 (descending node)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Saros_126), and the only one to occur in the 21st century; the next one visible over Iceland will be in 2196. It was also the first total solar eclipse visible from the territory of present-day Spain since [2 October 1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_October_2,_1959) in the Canary Islands, and the first from the Spanish mainland since [30 August 1905](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_August_30,_1905).[^cite_note-13] The next total eclipse visible in Spain will happen less than a year later on [2 August 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_August_2,_2027).[^cite_note-usatoday_73275302007-14]

## Observations



Animated path

The eclipse path proceeded from North Siberia throughout the Arctic Region, Iceland, the eastern Atlantic to Spain, and the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-euractiv_20260812-15]

### Solar eclipse below the horizon

The considerable [eclipse gamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_\(eclipse\)) (more than 0.8) gave observers—where the totally eclipsed Sun was just below the horizon—an opportunity to observe the lunar shadow in the high atmosphere as well as shortened civil twilight and extended nautical twilight. The darkening of the twilight sky improved the chances of observing the inner [zodiacal light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zodiacal_light).[^cite_note-16]

### Bright planets and stars visible during totality

Far northern Russia was treated to a dawn eclipse. In the east of the [Taymyr Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taymyr_Peninsula) (north-east of [Krasnoyarsk Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoyarsk_Krai)) the maximum of total phase occurred on 13 August at 00\:00 [local time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+7) during the [midnight sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_sun).[^cite_note-timeanddate_20260812-17]

In Iceland, the eclipse was a mid-afternoon event occurring about 4 hours before sunset; it started in [Reykjavík](25798.md) at around 16\:47, with totality being reached at 17\:48.[^cite_note-18]

In Spain, the eclipse occurred about 1 hour before sunset. [Mercury](19694.md) and [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter), west of the eclipsed Sun, were therefore very low below it. [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus) was brilliant well up in the southwest, with [Spica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spica) to its east. [Arcturus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcturus) was high in the south, and the [Summer Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Triangle) was well up in the east. Lower in the south, [Antares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antares) was minutes away from transit.[^cite_note-archive_202404211105-19]

## News and tourism



People watching the eclipse in [Antwerp](32149462.md), Belgium



Queue for eclipse glasses, London, UK

Totality of the solar eclipse in Valencia, Spain, with large crowds of visitors gathered to witness the event.

Totality of the solar eclipse in [Valencia](63861.md), Spain, with large crowds of visitors gathered to witness the event.

The longest the eclipse lasted on land was in [Látrabjarg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1trabjarg), in the remote Westfjords of Iceland. Plans were made to ensure safety in the area, but many of the roads to Látrabjarg are very narrow and dangerous. There was talk of closing the area to car traffic and ferrying people by bus.[^cite_note-20] Work had started on repairing parts of the road to Látrabjarg, partly as routine maintenance but also because of the eclipse.[^cite_note-visir_20252699961-21]

All locations where the eclipse was likely to be better seen experienced a surge in hotel room prices.[^cite_note-theguardian_20260811_hotel-22]

[Roscosmos](406573.md) and [Yandex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yandex) Weather organized a live broadcast of the partial eclipse (up to 79% obscuration) in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) from the top of [Lakhta Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhta_Center), the tallest building in Russia and Europe. Astronaut Pyotr Dubrov also captured the eclipse from the [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS), at an altitude of approximately 400 kilometers.[^cite_note-23]

In Spain and Portugal, there was a surge of eclipse tourists moving towards locations that social media and [news reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News) identified as the best spots to see the eclipse. In Spain, 660 official viewing sites were selected by regional governments to handle the expected influx of more than 440,000 people. Authorities estimated between 1.5 million and 6 million people could be on the road during the day to head to viewing sites, which prompted the deployment of 24,000 police officers around the area of the eclipse.[^cite_note-theguardian_20260812_its-24][^cite_note-25] In Portugal, news outlets outlined the use of [eclipse glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_viewer),[^cite_note-rtp-26] pointed out a [Perseid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perseids) [meteor shower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor_shower) would happen after the eclipse,[^cite_note-euronews_20260812-27] and explored the history of the last time a total solar eclipse was seen in the country ([17 April 1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_April_17,_1912))[^cite_note-28] and the fact that the next one will only be seen in 118 years.[^cite_note-rtp-26][^cite_note-29]

[Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews) reported on the [hat-trick](411541.md) of solar eclipses that Spain will experience in the next three consecutive years.[^cite_note-euronews_20260810-30]

On 13 August, the [BBC](19344654.md) published an article about how to check for [eye damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photic_retinopathy) caused by the Sun during an eclipse, citing that 70 people were left with permanent damaged sight after the [1999 eclipse](4557286.md).[^cite_note-bbc_20260813-31]

## Research



GeoColor satellite imagery at 18\:10 UTC with [Tropical Storm Cristobal](65440738.md#Tropical_Storm_Cristobal) in the bottom left

[NASA](18426568.md) flew one of its [WB-57s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_B-57_Canberra#NASA's_High-altitude_atmospheric_research_WB-57s) to chase the eclipse's shadow over the [North Atlantic Ocean](698.md) near Iceland, allowing more time to conduct [coronal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_corona) research. The aircraft flew at an altitude of 50,000 feet (15.2 kilometres) to avoid cloud cover and reduce atmospheric effects, and its planned speed of 460 miles per hour (400 kn) aimed to extend its view of totality from 2 minutes and 18 seconds to nearly 3 minutes. On board the aircraft were four cameras to capture the corona in various wavelengths of visible and infrared light. Data captured aims to help researchers better understand the corona's temperature and the causes of [solar wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_wind).[^cite_note-skyandtelescope_20260807-32][^cite_note-nasa_20260727-33][^cite_note-nature_20260811-34]

## Eclipse season

See also: [Eclipse cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_cycle)

This eclipse was part of an [eclipse season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_season), a period, roughly every six months, when eclipses occur. Only two (or occasionally three) eclipse seasons occur each year, and each season lasts about 35 days and repeats just short of six months (173 days) later; thus two full eclipse seasons always occur each year. Either two or three eclipses happen each eclipse season. In the sequence below, each eclipse is separated by a fortnight.[^cite_note-earthsky_20260807-36]

### Saros 126

It contains annular eclipses from June 4, 1323 through April 4, 1810; hybrid eclipses from April 14, 1828 through May 6, 1864; and total eclipses from [May 17, 1882](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_May_17,_1882) through [August 23, 2044](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_August_23,_2044). The series ends at member 72 as a partial eclipse on May 3, 2459. Its eclipses are tabulated in three columns; every third eclipse in the same column is one [exeligmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeligmos) apart, so they all cast shadows over approximately the same parts of the Earth.&#x20;

All eclipses in this series occur at the Moon’s [descending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit.[^cite_note-42]
