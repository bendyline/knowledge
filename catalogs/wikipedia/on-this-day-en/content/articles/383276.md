The **Islamic Consultative Assembly of Iran** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): مجلس شورای اسلامی ایران, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Majles-e Shurâ-ye Eslâmi Iran*),[^cite_note-2] also called the **Iranian Parliament**, the **Iranian Majles** or **Majlis,** is the [unicameral](204304.md) national [legislative body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_body) of [Iran](14653.md). The parliament currently consists of 290 representatives, an increase from the previous 270 seats since the [18 February 2000 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Iranian_legislative_election).

## History

### Islamic Republic of Iran

Following the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) of 1979, the [Senate of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Iran) was abolished and effectively succeeded by the [Guardian Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_Council), maintaining the bicameral structure of the Iranian legislature. In the 1989 constitutional revision, the *National Consultative Assembly* was renamed the *Islamic Consultative Assembly*.

Since the Iranian Revolution, the Parliament of Iran has been led by six chairmen. [Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Hashemi_Rafsanjani) served as the inaugural chairman from 1980 to 1989. Subsequently, [Mehdi Karroubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Karroubi) held the position in two separate terms (1989–1992 and 2000–2004), followed by [Ali Akbar Nategh-Nouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Akbar_Nategh-Nouri) (1992–2000), [Gholam-Ali Haddad-Adel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gholam-Ali_Haddad-Adel) (2004–2008), [Ali Larijani](531848.md) (2008–2020), and, since 2020, [Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf](1421458.md).

Throughout its history, the parliament's character has evolved from being a "debating chamber for notables" to a "club for the shah's [placemen](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/placeman)" during the Pahlavi era. In the era of the Islamic Republic, it has shifted to being a body primarily influenced by members of the "propertied middle class."[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

### 2017 attack

Main article: [2017 Tehran attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Tehran_attacks)

On 7 June 2017, there were [shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Tehran_attacks) at the Iranian parliament and at the [shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Ruhollah_Khomeini) of Ayatollah [Khomeini](26232.md).[^cite_note-5] Gunmen opened fire at the Iranian Parliament and the mausoleum of religious leader Ayatollah Khomeini in Tehran. The attack on the mausoleum reportedly left 17 persons dead and more than 30 people injured. The parliament was attacked by four gunmen which left seven to eight people injured. Both attacks took place around the same time and appear to have been coordinated.

### 2026 shutdown and claimed coup d'état

In 2026, the parliament was shut down following the outbreak of the [Iran war](82533582.md). Ayatallah Nabavian, the national security commissioner, claimed that a coup d'état was in progress.[^cite_note-6] There were plans to reopen the parliament after 9 July,[^cite_note-7] but it remained shut down, as reported by an MP, due to the government wanting to prevent interference with negotiations with the United States over the [Islamabad Memorandum](83441543.md).[^cite_note-8]

After 137 days, it conducted a nightly session, and its members approved of a motion permitting future sessions to be held by teleconference.[^cite_note-9] They also suspended Iran's commitments to the Memorandum and called for revenge over [Ali Khamenei's assassination](82537558.md). The National Security Commission called for the deaths of American leaders, the development of nuclear weapons, and the expansion of the war. The parliament appointed Ebrahim Azizi as the new chief of the Commission, and Abas Moghtadae as deputy.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12].[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Functions

The Islamic Consultative Assembly holds the authority to legislate laws on all matters within the boundaries defined by the Constitution.[^cite_note-17] Nevertheless, it is restricted from enacting laws that contradict the fundamental principles of the official religion of the nation (Shia Islam) or the Constitution itself.[^cite_note-18]

Government bills are submitted to the Islamic Consultative Assembly only after obtaining the approval of the Council of Ministers.[^cite_note-19]

The Islamic Consultative Assembly possesses the prerogative to investigate and scrutinize all matters concerning the country.[^cite_note-20]

International treaties, protocols, contracts, and agreements necessitate approval from the Islamic Consultative Assembly.[^cite_note-21]

Sanctioning and obtaining national or international loans or grants by the government requires ratification from the Islamic Consultative Assembly.[^cite_note-22]

The President must secure a vote of confidence from the Assembly, through a Council of Ministers approval, upon forming the government and prior to conducting any other business.[^cite_note-23]

In the event that at least one-fourth of the total members of the Islamic Consultative Assembly raise a question to the President, or if any Assembly member poses a question to a minister regarding their duties, the President or the minister is obligated to attend the Assembly and address the query.[^cite_note-24]

All legislation endorsed by the Islamic Consultative Assembly must be submitted to the Guardian Council. Within a maximum of ten days from its receipt, the Guardian Council must review the legislation to ensure its compatibility with Islamic criteria and the Constitution. If any incompatibility is identified, the legislation is returned to the Assembly for further review. Otherwise, the legislation is deemed enforceable.[^cite_note-25]

The [Mellat Polling Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellat_Polling_Center) is an affiliated institution of the Research Center of the Islamic Consultative Assembly. It provides public opinion research to support evidence-based legislation and policymaking.[^cite_note-26]

## Eligibility

People need to sign up online and upload their university degree document. Candidates need to be 30 at least and 75 years maximum, have a master's degree or equal Level 3 Islamic seminary, and be Iranian born.[^cite_note-27]

Currently, there are 290 members of Parliament, elected for a four-year term. There are five [seats reserved for religious minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Parliament_religious_minority_reserved_seats) (1.7% of the total members), with two for the [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian-Iranians) and one each for the [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Iran), [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Jews) and [Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism).

The parliament can force the dismissal of cabinet ministers through no-confidence votes and can impeach the president for misconduct in office. Although the executive proposes most new laws, individual deputies of the parliament also may introduce legislation. Deputies also may propose amendments to bills being debated. The parliament also drafts legislation, ratifies international treaties, and approves the national budget.

All candidates running for election, and proposed legislation from the assembly must be approved by the Guardian Council. Candidates must pledge in writing that they are committed, in theory and in practice, to the [Iranian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran).

### Constituencies

Main article: [Electoral districts of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_districts_of_Iran)

The parliament currently has 207 constituencies, including the five [reserved seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_seats) for religious minorities. The remaining 202 constituencies are territorial, each covering one or more of Iran's 368 [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Iran).

## Leadership

Main article: [List of Speakers of the Parliament of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Speakers_of_the_Parliament_of_Iran)

Members of Parliament elect their speaker and deputy speakers during the first session of Parliament for a one-year term. Every year, almost always in May, elections for new speakers are held in which incumbents may be re-elected.

The current Speaker of Parliament is Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, with First Deputy Speaker [Hamid-Reza Haji Babaee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid-Reza_Haji_Babaee) and Second Deputy Speaker [Ali Nikzad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Nikzad).

## Hack

Prior to the [1 March legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iranian_legislative_election), on February 14, 2024, the Assembly servers were hacked, revealing monthly payments of 270 million [tomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_toman) to members. The following day, the voting system was hacked, and journalists were barred from entering.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

## Building



Exterior view of the parliament building

After 1979, the Parliament convened at the building that used to house the [Senate of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Iran). [A new building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baharestan_Palace) for the Assembly was constructed at [Baharestan Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baharestan_Square) in central Tehran, near the old Iranian Parliament building that had been used from 1906 to 1979. After several debates, the move was finally approved in 2004. The first session of the Parliament in the new building was held on 16 November 2004.

The old building is depicted on the [reverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse_and_reverse) of the Iranian 100 [rial](708656.md) banknote.[^cite_note-35]
