The **lieutenant governor of the Commonwealth of Virginia** is a [constitutional officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_officer) of the [Commonwealth of Virginia](32432.md). The lieutenant governor is elected every four years along with the [governor](253093.md) and [attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Virginia).

The office is currently held by [Ghazala Hashmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazala_Hashmi), who was elected in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Virginia_lieutenant_gubernatorial_election) and is the second woman and fourth person of color to hold this position. The governor and lieutenant governor are elected separately and thus may be of different political parties. The lieutenant governor serves as the president of the [Senate of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Virginia) and is first in the line of succession to the governorship; if the governor dies, resigns, or otherwise leaves office, the lieutenant governor becomes governor. In Virginia, the governor is not permitted to serve consecutive terms, but the lieutenant governor may do so, and has no term limit.

## History

Beginning in the 1630s, the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_the_United_Kingdom) appointed several officials to aide the [governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colonial_governors_of_Virginia) of the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia) in the execution of their duties, collectively known as the Governor's Council or the Council of State. One member of this body was designated as the governor's deputy, or lieutenant governor, and exercised the governor's authority when they were absent.[^cite_note-tarter1-1][^cite_note-encyclopedia1-2] The [Virginia Constitution of 1776](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Virginia#1776) abolished the council.[^cite_note-tarter1-1]

The [Virginia Constitution of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Virginia#1851) created the modern office of the lieutenant governor.[^cite_note-encyclopedia1-2] It provided for the popular election of the officer and designated them *ex officio* president of the [Virginia Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Senate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014142-3] In this capacity they replaced the Speaker of the Senate, which had been chosen by the body's own members as their presiding officer from 1776 until 1852. During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), Virginia had two different governments and accordingly different sets of lieutenant governors. From 1865 until 1870, the lieutenant governors were appointed by the commanding general of the [First Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Military_District). In 1870, Virginia was readmitted to the federal union and, from then on, the officers were chosen by popular election.[^cite_note-encyclopedia1-2] That year, the state adopted [a new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Virginia#1870) which gave the lieutenant governor the power to cast tie-breaking votes in the Senate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014142-3]

[Douglas Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Wilder), sworn-in in 1986, was Virginia's first black lieutenant governor.[^cite_note-4] [Winsome Sears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winsome_Sears), sworn in on January 15, 2022,[^cite_note-5] is the first woman to have held the office.[^cite_note-6]

## Election

Along with the governor and attorney general, the lieutenant governor is one of three popularly elected executive offices in the state of Virginia.[^cite_note-7] The lieutenant governor is elected on their own ticket separate from the governor. They serve without term limits.[^cite_note-about-8] If the governor-elect does not assume their office, the lieutenant governor-elect becomes governor.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014160-9]

## Powers, duties, and structure

Article V of the [Constitution of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Virginia) designates the lieutenant governor as the president of the Senate.[^cite_note-about-8] If absent, its president pro tempore serves as its presiding officer.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014121-10] The lieutenant governor is allowed to vote in the Senate only to break ties.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014142-3] While this power has been interpreted to apply to most generic legislation, it has been disputed by state officials as to whether the tie-breaking power applies to votes on matters such as constitutional amendments or on the Senate's concurrence with an executive appointment. Such questions are yet to be resolved by litigation in state courts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014143–144-11] The constitution additionally stipulates that the lieutenant governor succeeds the governor in their office should it become vacant due to death, disqualification, or resignation. State law empowers the lieutenant governor to serve on several state boards and commissions.[^cite_note-about-8] The lieutenant governor can be impeached and removed from office by the Virginia General Assembly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014138-12]

The lieutenant governor's office is located in the [Oliver Hill Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hill_Building) in [Richmond, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Their salary is set by legislation and cannot be altered during a given term to which they were elected.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinan2014142-3] As of 2021, the annual salary is \$36,321.[^cite_note-15] As the role is a part-time position, most incumbents—unless already wealthy or retired by the time of their tenure—have held additional occupations.[^cite_note-16]
