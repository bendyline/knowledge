**Shuja'iyya** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الشجاعية), also *Shejaiya*, *Shijaiyeh*, *Shujayya*, *Shuja'ia*, *Shuja'iya*, is the southern quarter of [Old City of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_of_Gaza), and the only quarter of the Old City located outside the historical city walls.[^cite_note-Sharon-1] It is one of the largest neighborhoods in Gaza, holding an estimated 92,000 residents in 2015,[^cite_note-Commission2015-2] and over 115,000 by 2020.[^cite_note-thousands-3] It is located east of Gaza's city center, and its nucleus is situated on a hill located across the main [Salah al-Din Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_al-Din_Road) that runs north–south throughout the [Gaza Strip](12047.md). Shuja-iyya is divided into four residential neighborhoods: [Turukman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turukman), Al-Jadida, East Turukman, and East al-Jadida,[^cite_note-thousands-3] and contains several ancient structures, mosques and tombs.[^cite_note-MET-4] The Commonwealth War Cemetery is located 2 kilometres (1.2 mi) north of the commercial center of the neighborhood.[^cite_note-Jacobs-5]

## History

Shuja'iyya dates from the [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid) period in Gaza, and is named after Shuja' al-Din Uthman al-Kurdi, an Ayyubid *[emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir)* ("commander") who died fighting against the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) in 1239.[^cite_note-Haldiman195-6][^cite_note-7] The district is the first extension of Gaza beyond its city walls,[^cite_note-Haldiman195-6] and was a mixed quarter, as opposed to other quarters of Gaza.[^cite_note-8] It was generally a commercial district, but also partially residential. During the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), the neighborhood's houses were poorly built and its roads were narrow and unpaved. However, it also boasted many extravagant mosques and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) sanctuaries.[^cite_note-Sharon30-9] The 14th-century [Ibn Uthman Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Uthman_Mosque) and [al-Zufurdimri Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zofor_Domri_Mosque) are located in Shuja'iyya,[^cite_note-Jacobs-5] The 15th-century [Mahkamah Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahkamah_Mosque) is also located there.

Being built outside the city's wall, Shuja'iyya had more potential to grow than the [al-Daraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Daraj), Zaytoun, and [al-Tuffah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tuffah) quarters. It eventually grew to become the largest neighborhood in Gaza. It was divided into separate northern and southern parts along ethnic lines. The southern part of the area is called al-Turkuman because of the concentration of [Turkoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Turkmen) clans who settled there during the reign of Ayyubid sultan [as-Salih Ayyub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Salih_Ayyub) between 1240 and 1249. The northern part was called al-Judaida or Shuja'iyya al-Akrad. It was populated by [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurd) originally from the [Mosul](200475.md) area.[^cite_note-Sharon30-9]

The [Ottoman](22278.md) census of 1525 shows a relatively equal population of Turks and Kurds, with 89 and 90 households, respectively. The [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) were the single largest ethnic group with 95 households, while the [Christians](18337522.md) had 82. In 1538, however, the Kurdish and Turkoman populations grew dramatically, especially the former. The Kurds had 278 households while the Turks had 181. In 1549, the Kurdish population grew sharply to 406 houses, while the Turkomans maintained a steady growth to 195 houses. The remnants of [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) military units resided in their own separate small community in Shuja'iyya. Their population consisted of 44 households in 1557 which dwindled to 66 persons in 1597.[^cite_note-10]

### Modern era

The Shuja'iya Primary School for Girls was founded in the neighborhood in 1967. In 2011 it had an enrollment of 1,326 students. On October 6, 1987, just before the outbreak of the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), Shuja'iyya was the site of an armed confrontation between the [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Jihad_Movement_in_Palestine) and the [Israeli Army](39237.md) (IDF). The clash resulted in the death of an IDF officer and four Islamic Jihad militants, and the day has been commemorated by the latter as the "Battle of Shuja'iyya." On the first anniversary of the operation, in 1988, the Islamic Jihad called for a general strike against [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-11]

The neighborhood is a long-term stronghold of [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-Harel-12] The rival [Fatah](11104.md)-aligned [Hilles clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilles_clan) carried influence in Shuja'iyya until Hamas police claimed that they were hiding suspects of a previous bombing in the city, consequently resulting in clashes between police forces and clan members, resulting in nine deaths and causing several men from the Hilles clan to leave the neighborhood for resettlement in the West Bank.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Shuja'iyya had been a frequent target in [Israel's 2008-09 airstrikes of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932009_Israel%E2%80%93Gaza_conflict), which killed several members of Hamas' security forces and destroyed the local police station.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] A coalition of Hamas and Islamic Jihad forces battled intensely with the IDF days after the airstrikes.[^cite_note-Harel-12] Hamas claimed to have lost three fighters, including a local commander after an Israeli airstrike and the Fatah-affiliated [al-Aqsa Martyrs Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Martyrs_Brigades) lost a fighter after ambushing undercover Israeli forces in the district.[^cite_note-17] A women and children's clinic was destroyed by an Israeli airstrike during the hostilities with no casualties reported.[^cite_note-18]

During the [2014 Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Gaza_war), Shuja'iyya was the location of the [Battle of Shuja'iyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shuja'iyya_\(2014\)), the fiercest battle of the war, and was heavily shelled by Israeli forces.[^cite_note-19] Between 65 and 120 Palestinians were killed in the fighting, including at least 17 children, 14 women and four elderly persons,[^cite_note-ReutYnet-20][^cite_note-21] as well as 13 Israeli soldiers.[^cite_note-americans-22] Israel stated the district was targeted because 8% of the rocket attacks by Palestinian forces against Israel originated in Shuja'iyya because the area was allegedly a source of tunnels crossing into Israel.[^cite_note-ReutYnet-20] After the 2014 war, an Israeli brigade gave soldiers who fought in Shuja'iyya before and after photos, showing the damage. IDF sources said the incident was under investigation.[^cite_note-haaretz_pics-23] Reconstruction of the quarter is complicated by UN priorities. Funding is allocated according to a ladder of importance, from small to large-scale damage, and many of the cement vouchers for families are sold on the black market.[^cite_note-24]

During the [Gaza war](74998519.md), Shuja'iyya has been the site of three military engagements. The first two battles took place [in December 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shuja'iyya_\(2023\)) and [June 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Shuja'iyya_incursion), with both ending in Israeli withdrawals. During the second battle, the neighborhood experienced more than a week of Israeli bombing, with footage showing the "immense destruction" of the neighborhood.[^cite_note-25] Between 60,000 and 80,000 people were estimated to have been displaced.[^cite_note-26] By November 2024, more than 80 percent of the neighborhood's population was reported to be displaced and only a "few intact buildings" remaining.[^cite_note-27] In April 2025, Israel began [a third invasion of Shuja'iyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Shuja'iyya_offensive), during which it began supporting the [Fatah](11104.md)-affiliated [Shuja'iyya Popular Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuja'iyya_Popular_Defense_Forces) against Hamas.

## Economy

Gaza's largest market, specializing mostly in clothes and household goods, is in Shuja'iyya; Midan Shuja'iyya ("Shuja'iyya Square") is located at the entrance of the neighborhood from the walled city quarters.[^cite_note-Jacobs-5] [Omar Mukhtar Street](20203548.md) starts in the district and ends in [Rimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimal).[^cite_note-28] The Municipality of Gaza is implementing a project to enhance Shuja'iyya. It aims to improve the environment and living conditions of its citizens, by widening, restoring, and repairing roads in the district. There is also a development of land located along Salah ad-Din Street, known as Park al-Bastat Shuja'iyya.[^cite_note-29]

## Landmarks



The [Ibn Marwan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Marwan_Mosque) in 2019

One of the most significant landmarks in this neighborhood is the mosque of "[Ahmed bin Othman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Uthman_Mosque)," commonly referred to by the locals as the Grand Mosque, situated at the heart of the residential and commercial district. Within this mosque lies the tomb of [Saif Al-Din Yalkhaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saif_Al-Din_Yalkhaja?action=edit\&redlink=1), a Mamluk under the rule of Sultan "[Al-Zahir Barquq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquq)." He served as the deputy of Gaza City in the year 849 Hijri and died in 850 Hijri, his grave is in the mosque.[^cite_note-الضوء-30][^cite_note-النجوم-31] The Grand Mosque has several foundational stones that reveal its history of demolition and restoration, dating back to the ninth century Hijri and continuing until the mid-century. Noteworthy are its intricate engravings and decorated stones, particularly those adorning its minaret.[^cite_note-32]

Another landmark in this neighborhood is the "[Mahkamah Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahkamah_Mosque)," which housed a school of significant importance, as indicated by an inscription above its north-closed door.

The neighborhood is also home to various mosques, including the "Al-Hawashi Mosque," the "Lady Ruqayya Mosque," the "[Ali bin Marwan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Marwan_Mosque)" along the Salah al-Din line, the "Sheikh Musafir Mosque," the "Reform Mosque," the "Tariq ibn Ziyad Mosque," the "[Qazmari Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zofor_Domri_Mosque)" (also known as the Zofor Domri Mosque), the "Daraqutni Mosque," the "Zu al-Nurayn Mosque," and the "Sayyid Ali Mosque." Adjacent to the Grand Mosque was the Shuja'iya Bath, which has since disappeared.

To the east of this neighborhood is the "Tunisian Cemetery" or the "Tiflisi Cemetery." Additionally, one of the graves in the neighborhood's cemeteries is said to belong to the famous [Samson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samson) the Mighty.[^cite_note-33]
