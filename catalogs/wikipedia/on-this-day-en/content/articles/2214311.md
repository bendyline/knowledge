**Kidal Region** ([Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language): ߞߌߘߊߟ ߘߌߣߋߖߊ tr. Kidal Dineja) the eighth administrative region of [Mali](19127.md), covering 151,450 square kilometres or 58,480 square miles. This area was formerly part of [Gao Region](2349862.md), but was created as a separate region in 1991. It is located in the north of the country. It was under the control of different armed movements such as the [Ansar Dine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_Dine) and MNLA until January 2013, when French forces liberated the region.[^cite_note-3] The Region's administrative capital is the town of [Kidal](1463133.md).

## Geography

The region is bordered on the west by [Taoudénit Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoud%C3%A9nit_Region), to the south by [Gao Region](2349862.md) and [Ménaka Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9naka_Region), to the east by [Niger](21373.md) and to the north by [Algeria](358.md).

Kidal has a desert climate with day-time temperatures that reach as high as 45 °C (113 °F).

In 2009 the region had a population of 67,638.[^cite_note-pop2009-4] In addition 30% of its population, mostly [Tuareg](103054.md) and [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people) were displaced to North Africa during the 1990s.

The largest towns of the region are Kidal, [Tessalit](2322222.md), and [Aguel'hoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguel'hoc).

## Transportation and economy

The primary trades of Kidal Region include livestock breeding, craft-making, and commerce. Commercial farming has been developed in some areas. The region is extremely isolated, with no paved highways or rivers for transportation.

## History

The region saw Tuareg Rebellions in 1963–1964 and in 1990–1991. The accords of Tamanresset followed on January 6, 1991, creating the Kidal Region by decree on August 8, 1991.

By the end of 2011, another group of [Tuareg rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_insurgency_in_northern_Mali), the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad), were active in the region.[^cite_note-5] The Movement declared Azawad to be an independent republic in April 2012, with the Kidal region as part of the new (but unrecognised) state.

## Culture

The region is peopled primarily by Tuaregs, a nomadic ethnicity of [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people) origin. Their writing is the [Tifinagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tifinagh) alphabet.

Though [nomadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) remains the lifestyle best adapted to the difficult environment of the region, several settlements have emerged at Kidal: [Aguel'hoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguel'hoc), [Tessalit](2322222.md), and [Tinzawatène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinzawat%C3%A8ne).

## Demographics

The Ménaka Region is the least populated region in Mali, with a population of 83,192 in 2022.[^cite_note-2022census-6]

Kidal is one of three Malian regions (the others being [Menaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9naka_Region) and [Tombouctou](1164276.md)) where the [Tuareg](103054.md) constitute the single largest ethnic group. In 2022, Tuareg people accounted for over 86% of the regional population. Minorities in Kidal include the [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people), [Arabs](2185.md) ([*Maure*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawagh_Arabs)), [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_people), [Senufo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senufo_people) (mostly [Minyanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyanka_language)), and Somono.[^cite_note-censusculture-7]

### Religion

The 2022 census found that 99.64% of the population in Kidal was [Muslim](19541.md), 0.29% was [Christian](18337522.md), 0.01 were adherents of [traditional faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_traditional_religions), and 0.04% practised other religions. [Islam](6037917.md) is the dominant religion among the Tuareg, who are almost entirely Muslim.[^cite_note-censusculture-7]
