**Jérémie** (French pronunciation: [\[ʒeʁemi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/ca/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Arthur_Crbz-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9mie.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Arthur_Crbz-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9mie.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/ca/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Arthur_Crbz-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9mie.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Arthur_Crbz-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9mie.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Haitian Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Creole_language): *Jeremi*) is a [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_communes_of_Haiti) and capital city of the [Grand'Anse department](492837.md) in [Haiti](13373.md). It had a population of about 134,317 at the 2015 census. It is relatively isolated from the rest of the country. The [Grande-Anse River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande-Anse_River) flows near the city.

Jérémie is called *the city of the poets* because of the numerous writers, poets, and historians born there.[^cite_note-3]

## History

During 1762, Creole General [Thomas-Alexandre Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas-Alexandre_Dumas) was born here. He is mostly known for fighting under Napoleon in Italy and Egypt and for being the father of writer [Alexandre Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Dumas).

In 1954 Jérémie was hit by Hurricane Hazel which destroyed much of the city and destroyed many neighboring inland villages. Hazel travelled north along the continental US east coast and went on to kill 39 Americans and 85 people in Canada.[^cite_note-4]

In 1964, during the [Jérémie Vespers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9mie_Vespers), the Haitian army and the *[Tonton Macoutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonton_Macoutes)* massacred 27 people in Jérémie.[^cite_note-5]

In the early 2000s, archaeologists uncovered an ancient synagogue of [Crypto-Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto-Judaism) in the city, the only one found on the [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola). Jérémie has historically been inhabited by many [mixed-race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulatto_Haitians) families of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Haiti) descent.[^cite_note-JTA-6]

In the aftermath of the 2010 [earthquake](10106.md), a food aid convoy transporting aid delivered to [Jérémie Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9mie_Airport) through Jérémie encountered a hijacking attempt by 20 men, on 30 January 2010.[^cite_note-7] Medical supplies have also been airlifted through the airport, due to the increase in medical needs from injured refugees arriving in the wake of the quake.[^cite_note-8]

In October 2016, Jérémie was almost completely destroyed by [Hurricane Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Matthew) as it went past Haiti.[^cite_note-BBCOct6-9]

Roughly 40 nautical miles (46 mi; 74 km) west of the city lies [Navassa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navassa_Island), which is claimed by Haiti and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-10]

## Facilities

The city has an airport, [Jérémie Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9mie_Airport). The area of the airport of Jérémie is known as "Numero Deux". [Mission Aviation Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Aviation_Fellowship) conducts charter flights into the airport. [Agape Flights](https://agapeflights.com/), based in Venice, Florida, provides weekly mail and cargo service for several missionaries in the area.

## Notable natives and residents

- [Thomas-Alexandre Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas-Alexandre_Dumas), a general of the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars), was born in Jérémie as the son of a French nobleman and his African slave, before independence. Dumas lived most of his life in France, where his father took him at age 18. He married and was the father of [Alexandre Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Dumas,_p%C3%A8re), who became a noted and prolific author in the nineteenth century.

- [Joseph Serge Miot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Serge_Miot), was born there. He was the [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) of Port-au-Prince, and was killed in the [2010 Haiti earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake).

- [Emile Roumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emile_Roumer) (1903–1988), a poet.

- [Modeste Testas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modeste_Testas) (1765–1870), formerly enslaved Ethiopian woman, whose statues stands in Bordeaux commemorating the [transatlantic slave trade](49005.md).
