**Arauca** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[aˈɾawka\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia) of Eastern [Colombia](5222.md) located in the extreme north of the [Orinoco Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco_River) of Colombia (the [Llanos Orientales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoqu%C3%ADa_Region)), bordering [Venezuela](32374.md). The southern boundary of Arauca is formed by the [Casanare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casanare_River) and [Meta Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_River), separating Arauca from the departments of [Casanare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casanare_Department) and [Vichada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichada_Department). To the west, Arauca borders the department of [Boyacá](185665.md). The [Caño Limón oilfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1o_Lim%C3%B3n_oilfield) located within Arauca account for almost a third of the Colombian [oil](23195.md) output. Its capital is the town of [Arauca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arauca,_Arauca).

## Etymology

The name Arauca is believed to derive from the name of an Indigenous people, who are thought to be related to the [Arawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawak) or [Arhuaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arhuaco) people.[^cite_note-4] Some have also speculated that the name Arauca is connected with the [Araucanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araucanian) or [Mapuche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche) Indians of [Chile](5489.md) and [Argentina](18951905.md).

## History

The first [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_conquistadors_in_Colombia) to set foot in the region of present-day Arauca was [Nikolaus Federmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaus_Federmann) in 1539. He was a soldier in the company of [Georg von Speyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_von_Speyer), who passed through the south of present-day Venezuela and the eastern part of what is today known as Colombia. In 1659, the Catholic missions were establishing new settlements in tribal Guahibo, U'wa, Aeric and Chirico tribal lands.

In the eighteenth century, being expelled from the Jesuits under Viceroy [Pedro Mesia de la Zerda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Mesia_de_la_Zerda?action=edit\&redlink=1), the Augustinian Recollect succeeded in their mission of evangelization. They founded five centers of the catechism: Solitude of Cravo, Cuiloto San Javier, San Jose del Ele, Lipa San Joaquin and San Fernando de Arauca.

In 1810, the Araucanian territory became part of the newly created province of Casanare and in 1819 Arauca was incorporated into the province of [Cundinamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cundinamarca_Province). By 1857, Arauca was made part of the Sovereign State of Boyacá, that later became the Boyacá department. In 1891, the Arauca police station was established with chief executive Pedro León Acosta. By decree 113 of January 20, 1955, the territory was elevated to the national quartermaster, and finally, with the Constitution of July 5, 1991, Arauca became a department. Arauca is the regional capital since 1911.

### Political and administrative evolution

- 1550: The [New Kingdom of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Granada) is officially established under the Audiencia

- 1810: With the division into new units, the Arauca territory becomes part of the province of Casanare

- 1818: Under [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_Colombia) [Fernando Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Serrano) and Santander, General Commander of the patriotic forces, Arauca becomes provisional capital of the Republic

- 1819: The province of Casanare became part of the Department of Cundinamarca

- 1831: With the dissolution of [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia), Arauca remains part of the province of Casanare

- 1886: With the creation of the departments, Arauca becomes part of the Department of Boyacá

- 1911: Creation of the Special Commissioner of Arauca; capital of Arauca

- 1955: The Araucanian territory acquired the status of National Quartermaster

- 1960: Boyacá Arauca is created

## Geography

Its territory covers an area of 23,818 km<sup>2</sup>, predominantly composed of plains.

The Colombian census does not collect data on race and ethnicity, but compiled population estimates of the five groupings are:

- [Indigenous Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Latino?action=edit\&redlink=1) (93.70%)

- [Afro-Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Colombian) (4.07%)

- [Native Tribal American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Tribal_American?action=edit\&redlink=1) or Tribal Indigenous (2.22%)

- [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (0.01%)

- [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian) (0.01%)

There are small numbers of descendants of European immigrants: the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_people), [German](152735.md), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), [Italian](385155.md), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), [British](31717.md), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), [Greek](42056.md) and [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) (i.e. [Lebanese](17771.md), [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian) and [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\))) nationalities.

Many [Venezuelan](32374.md) nationals live in the department; around 10-15 percent of the population is of Venezuelan origin.

## Indigenous population

See also: [Indigenous peoples of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Colombia)

The department's total indigenous population amounts to 3591 people. Across the department 26 *[resguardos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resguardo?action=edit\&redlink=1)* are located in an area of 128,167 hectares (316,710 acres). Six indigenous groups populate this region; [U'wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U'wa_people) with 1,124 members; [Betoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betoye_people?action=edit\&redlink=1) at 800, [Sikuani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikuani_people?action=edit\&redlink=1) number 782, 441 [Hitnü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitn%C3%BC_people?action=edit\&redlink=1) are registered, [Kuiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuiba_people?action=edit\&redlink=1) count up to 241, [Hitanü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitan%C3%BC_people?action=edit\&redlink=1) are listed at 110, the [Chiricoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiricoa_people?action=edit\&redlink=1) amount to 63 and thirty [Piapoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piapoco_people) are registered in Arauca. The predominant ethnic group in the department are the U'wa. They are located in the northeastern foothills of the [Eastern Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental_\(Colombia\)) of the Colombian [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes), until the [Sierra Nevada del Cocuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_del_Cocuy) in [Boyacá](185665.md). Its total population is estimated at 7231 people, belong to the [Chibcha language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibcha_language) family. U'wa means "intelligent people who can speak".

The Betoye with 800 members constitutes the second-largest ethnic group in the department. They inhabit the banks of the [Cravo Norte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cravo_Norte_River) and the municipality of Tame. The area of its territory is 702 hectares (1,730 acres). Their language is considered part of the Chibcha language family.[^cite_note-\:0-6] Although not retaining their original language, the people speak a mixture of Betoye with Spanish. Certain grammatical structures of the Betoye language persist in this mixture.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

The [Guahibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guahibo_people) are subdivided into tribes known as Sikuani, Kuiba, Chiricoa, Hitnü (iguanito) and Hitnü (Macaguane)[^cite_note-7], who speak [Arawakan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawakan_language).

## Economy

The main economical activity of Arauca is centered around the oil industry, with the [Caño Limón oilfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1o_Lim%C3%B3n_oilfield) as the most important source. The soils of the region have shown good conditions for growing [cacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theobroma_cacao), [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana), [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and fruit trees, as well as industrial crops such as [African palmtree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_palmtree?action=edit\&redlink=1), [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), soybeans and sesame. Livestock farming is another important factor of the department's economy.
