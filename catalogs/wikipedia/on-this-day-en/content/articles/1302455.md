The **Palestine national football team** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): منتخب فِلَسطِيْن لِكُرَّةُ الْقَدَم), governed by the [Palestinian Football Association](5531531.md), represents [Palestine](241405.md) in men's international [football](10568.md). The squad is under the global jurisdiction of [FIFA](11049.md), and is governed continentally by the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC).

A football federation in [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine_national_football_team) was founded in 1928. The national team was not recognized by FIFA until 1998, after the creation of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority). The same year, Palestine played their first FIFA-recognized match in a 3–1 defeat to [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) in a [friendly](977090.md). The team won the 2014 AFC Challenge Cup, thanks to a 1–0 win over the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_national_football_team) in the final. Their win in the competition qualified them to the [2015 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup), marking their first appearance in the competition. Palestine also qualified to the following edition of the Asian Cup in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup), their first through regular qualification. They have yet to qualify for the [World Cup](11370.md).

The Palestinian team is known by various nicknames: "Lions of [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan)" (Arabic: أُسُودُ كَنعَان), "the [Fedayoon](14068131.md)" (Arabic: الْفَدَائِيُّون), "the Knights" (Arabic: الْفُرْسَان). Their main colours are red and white. The team reached an all-time high position of 73rd in the [FIFA ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) in February 2018, after going on a 12-match unbeaten streak, from 29 March 2016 to 22 March 2018. Palestine's main venue is [Faisal Al-Husseini International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_Al-Husseini_International_Stadium) in [Al-Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ram), although they have been forced to play in neutral stadiums for home matches on numerous occasions.

## History

### 1928–1939: Mandate period

Under the British Mandate, Jewish sports leaders established the [Palestine Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Football_Association) (PFA) in 1928,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] which gained [FIFA](11049.md) affiliation in 1929.[^cite_note-4] While initial PFA participation included a small number of Arab clubs alongside Jewish teams, its membership became virtually all Jewish by the mid-1930s.[^cite_note-5] The [Mandatory Palestine national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine_national_football_team), which competed in the [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) and [1938 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), fielded exclusively Jewish players.[^cite_note-6] Following the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, the PFA became the [Israel Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Football_Association).&#x20;

Concurrently, the Arab Palestinian Sports Federation (APSF) was formed in 1931 due to concerns that the PFA did not represent Arab interests. The APSF fielded its own teams, playing its first match against the [American University of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_of_Beirut) that same year,[^cite_note-7] before its activities dissolved toward the end of the decade due to the [1936–1939 Arab revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine).[^cite_note-auto-8]

### 1953–1998: Regional participation and re-establishment

The first international match played by Palestine was an 8–1 defeat away to Egypt in 1953.[^cite_note-9] The last game saw Palestine lose to Libya 5–2, to finish bottom of the group.[^cite_note-10]

At the [1965 Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1965_Arab_Games), Palestine were grouped with [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_national_football_team), [Iraq](1039652.md), [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) and the [United Arab Republic](1022953.md). They finished second, thus advancing to the semi-finals for the first time. Palestine faced [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_national_football_team) and were beaten 2–1. In the third place play-off, Palestine met [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team), where they lost 4–2.

Palestine participated in the [1966 Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Arab_Cup) held in Iraq; they were drawn in Group B alongside Syria, Libya and North Yemen. Starting with a 0–0 draw to Libya, Palestine beat North Yemen 7–0 before losing 3–1 to Syria. They ended the tournament with four points and failed to qualify to the knock-out stages of the competition.[^cite_note-11]

The national team participated in the [1976 Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1976_Arab_Games), held in Damascus. Palestine started their campaign with two defeats to Morocco (3–0) and Saudi Arabia (3–1), before defeating Jordan (2–1). They lost to hosts Syria (2–0) in their fourth game of the tournament, drew against South Yemen (0–0), and finished with a win against Mauritania (1–0) to end the tournament in 6th place.[^cite_note-12] Palestine participated in the [1992 Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Arab_Cup) held in Syria; they were drawn in Group B alongside the hosts and Saudi Arabia but were eliminated from the group stage after one draw and one loss.[^cite_note-13]

### 1998–2014: International recognition

In May 1995, the PFA was granted the status of provisional member in FIFA. Palestine eventually gained FIFA membership on 8 June 1998 after numerous attempts since 1946.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-auto-8] Under Ricardo Carugati, Palestine played their first official matches in July 1998 against [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team), [Jordan](1090065.md) and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team) at the [1998 Arab Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Arab_Cup_qualification).[^cite_note-FIFA.com-15]

In the next year, Palestine took part in the [1999 Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1999_Arab_Games) held in Jordan. There, they won games against [Qatar](1111350.md) and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), drew with Libya and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team), while only losing to hosts [Jordan](1090065.md). Palestine finished in the third-place which is their best result to date. In their first ever [Asian Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification), Palestine was drawn into a five-team group with Jordan, Kazakhstan, [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_national_football_team), and Qatar. A single victory 2–0 against Pakistan proved to be not enough to qualify as they lost 1–0 to Qatar, 5–1 to Jordan before finishing the campaign with a 2–0 defeat to Kazakhstan.[^cite_note-16]

Managed by Mustafa Yacoub, Palestine drew into Group C of the [2002 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) along with [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_national_football_team), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_national_football_team) and Qatar. The team finished in second place. In the [2002 Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Arab_Cup), Palestine crashed out of the group stage. Under Nicola Hadwa Shahwan, the team were drawn alongside Kuwait, Qatar and Singapore. Palestine finished last with two points.

After a failed Asian Cup qualifying campaign, the PFA hired Austrian coach [Alfred Riedl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Riedl) to lead the team during the [2006 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)). Palestine was included in a group with Uzbekistan, Iraq and Chinese Taipei. They won both games against Chinese Taipei, drew 2–2 with Iraq, however this was insufficient for Palestine to advance beyond the second round as they lost both fixtures versus Uzbekistan 3–0 apart from a 3–0 defeat at the hands of Iraq. Palestine took part in the [inaugural AFC Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_AFC_Challenge_Cup) held in Bangladesh. They were drawn alongside the host team, [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia_national_football_team) and [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam_national_football_team). In the group stage, Palestine registered their biggest win ever, a 11–0 victory over Guam. Palestine advanced from [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_AFC_Challenge_Cup#Group_C) and met [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstan_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals. After playing a goal-lees first half, they were beaten with a last-gasp goal. In the summer of 2006, Palestine achieved its highest FIFA ranking at 115, placing them 16th in the Asian continent.

Managed by [Azmi Nassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azmi_Nassar), Palestine were drawn with [China national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team), Iraq and Singapore during the [2007 Asian Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification). Palestine finished in the bottom with 4 points. They achieved their only win against Singapore 1–0.[^cite_note-17]

In 2008, with the help of FIFA's goal program, the PFA built the [Faisal Al-Husseini International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_Al-Husseini_International_Stadium), and on 26 October 2008. Palestine held a match at their home for the first time since they became a member of FIFA, a 1–1 draw with Jordan ahead of a crowd of over 7,000.[^cite_note-18] Palestine entered the [first round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_first_round) of [2010 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) and were eliminated after only one match that finished 4–0 for Singapore. The second leg was not played as scheduled due to the Palestinian team being barred from traveling.[^cite_note-19] However, the FIFA refused to reschedule the match and Palestine forfeited the game 3–0.[^cite_note-20]

The [2010 AFC Challenge Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_AFC_Challenge_Cup_qualification) draw put Palestine in Group C, with Afghanistan (later withdrew), Kyrgyzstan, and Nepal. They started the run with a goalless draw against hosts Nepal. The final game finished in a 1–1 draw with Kyrgyzstan to miss the chance of qualification on goal difference.[^cite_note-21]

Palestine drew into group C with Jordan, Libya and Sudan for the [2011 Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2011_Arab_Games). They lost the opening match 4–1 to Jordan in [Doha](26214389.md). The team improved in the second match against Libya which finished 1–1. The last game against Sudan was won by Palestine 2–0 to clinch the second-place berth in the tournament.[^cite_note-22] At the semi-finals they lost 3–1 to Bahrain. The team completed their campaign with a 3–0 loss to Kuwait.

During the [2014 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)), Palestine were drawn with [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_national_football_team) in the [first round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_first_round). The first leg was won 2–0,[^cite_note-23] while the second leg ended in a draw 1–1.[^cite_note-24] On 3 July 2011, the first World Cup qualifying match played at home, Palestine took the lead with a long-range shot by [Houssam Wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houssam_Wadi). Palestine reached the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round) to face [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_national_football_team). The first match was lost 1–0 in [Buriram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buriram), while the second match resulted in Palestine's elimination.

The PFA chose not to renew [Moussa Bezaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Bezaz)'s contract after exiting the second round of the 2014 FIFA World Cup qualifying. Jamal Mahmoud, a former player and manager of [Al-Wehdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Wehdat_SC), was announced as the national team manager in November 2011, after two disastrous friendly matches, without a permanent manager saw defeats against [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team) and Iran 4–1 and 7–0, respectively. Palestine made history by playing and defeating their first [UEFA](32332.md) rival [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_national_football_team), on 2–0.[^cite_note-25] The match was later registered as unofficial for unknown reasons.

Mahmoud later led the team in the [2012 AFC Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_AFC_Challenge_Cup). In the run-up, Palestine finished group play at the [2012 AFC Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_AFC_Challenge_Cup) without conceding a goal after 2–0 wins over Nepal and [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_national_football_team) alongside a 0–0 draw against [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_national_football_team) on the way to a semifinal exit at the hands of eventual champions North Korea. The third place play-off ended in a 4–3 loss against the Philippines. During the 2014 AFC Challenge Cup qualification, Palestine drew into Group D with Bangladesh, Nepal and the [Northern Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands_national_football_team). They started with a hard win against Bangladesh 1–0. The next game ended in a 9–0 rout over the Northern Mariana Islands. The qualifiers finished in a goalless draw against Nepal to earn a second straight AFC Challenge Cup berth.

Palestine's starting line-up against the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_national_football_team) at the [2014 AFC Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AFC_Challenge_Cup) Final

The team ended up winning the 2014 AFC Challenge Cup by drawing only one of the 5 matches without conceding a goal. In the opening match, Palestine won against Kyrgyzstan with a last-gasp goal by [Abdelhamid Abuhabib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelhamid_Abuhabib).[^cite_note-26] In the next group game, they played against Myanmar and won 2–0.[^cite_note-27] The ended the first round with a 0–0 draw against hosts Maldives.[^cite_note-28] Palestine advanced as group winners to the semi-finals where they faced Afghanistan. The match ended in favor of the Palestinians with a 2–0 win.[^cite_note-29] At the final, Palestine clinched the win over the Philippines with a lone goal scored by [Ashraf Nu'man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashraf_Nu'man) through a free-kick.[^cite_note-30] The victory gave the Palestine team their first major trophy and guaranteed their participation in the next year's [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup). Following its 2014 AFC Challenge Cup win and qualification for the Asian Cup, [Jamal Mahmoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Mahmoud) resigned as national team manager citing differences with the Palestine Football Association.[^cite_note-31]

Palestine was placed in [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_D) along with [Japan](993546.md), Jordan and Iraq in the [2015 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup) having lost all three group matches. They started their campaign with a 0–4 defeat by Japan.[^cite_note-32] In the second game Palestine were beaten 1–5, with the opposing team ending the first half with a 3 goals lead. In the second half, Palestine scored their first ever Asian Cup goal by [Jaka Ihbeisheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaka_Ihbeisheh) six minutes before the end. However, at this point they were eliminated.[^cite_note-33] The participation ended with a 0–2 defeat to Iraq.[^cite_note-34] After a disastrous performance in the 2015 AFC Asian Cup, the PFA appointed Olympic team manager [Abdel Nasser Barakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Nasser_Barakat) as national team manager, who then decided not to pick several veteran players as the team moved to overhaul.

Palestine registered two 6–0 wins over Malaysia during [2018 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round). Those results, combined with a 0–0 draw at home to Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, placed Palestine close to qualification for the third round of Asian World Cup qualifiers. They were eliminated from the qualification following a 2–0 defeat to the UAE on 24 March 2016. They won their final FIFA World Cup qualification match at home 7–0 against [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste_national_football_team), five days later.[^cite_note-35]

Although eliminated from the World Cup, Palestine advanced to the third round of the [2019 AFC Asian Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_third_round). Drawn with Oman, the Maldives and Bhutan, Palestine came second in the group with five wins and one defeat, and qualified to the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) for the first time through regular qualification. Between February and March 2018, Palestine reached their best ever FIFA ranking of 73rd, thanks to an unprecedented 12-match unbeaten streak (between 29 March 2016 and 22 March 2018), winning eight and drawing four. In February 2018, Palestine achieved their best-ever FIFA ranking (73rd).[^cite_note-FIFA.com-15]

On 19 December 2017, [Julio César Baldivieso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_C%C3%A9sar_Baldivieso) was hired after Abdel Nasser Barakat was released.[^cite_note-36] The hiring was widely criticized by Palestinian fans.[^cite_note-37] On 22 April 2018, the PFA appointed [Noureddine Ould Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noureddine_Ould_Ali) as head coach to lead the team in the next period. Palestine was crowned champion of the [2018 Bangabandhu Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Bangabandhu_Cup) as an invited country to the tournament. In the road to the final, they topped their group with 6 points, defeating [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan_national_football_team) 2–0 before winning against Nepal 1–0 with a [Khaled Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Salem) header.[^cite_note-38] In the semi-final Palestine knocked out Bangladesh 2–0 with the goals coming in each half. At the final, Palestine played Tajikistan for the second time. The match ended 0–0 in regular time, to be decided in a penalty shoot-out which Palestine won 4–3.[^cite_note-39]



Palestinian defender [Abdullah Jaber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Jaber) (right) against Syria at the [2019 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup)

Drawn with Australia, Syria and Jordan, Palestine started their 2019 AFC Asian Cup campaign on 6 January 2019 with a 0–0 draw to Syria.[^cite_note-40] Five days later, Palestine faced title-holders Australia; a header by [Jamie Maclaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Maclaren) and a goal by [Awer Mabil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awer_Mabil) sent Palestine trailing 2–0 at half-time. Australia sealed the win with a 90th-minute header from [Apostolos Giannou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolos_Giannou). On 15 January 2019, Palestine headed into their last group-stage match needing all three points against Jordan, and hoping for a Syria win against Australia. In the 17th minute, Palestine came close to scoring when [Amer Shafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amer_Shafi) produced a fingertip save to keep out [Abdelatif Bahdari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelatif_Bahdari). In the second half, [Oday Dabbagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oday_Dabbagh) failed to score from two headers, and the match ended in a 0–0 draw.[^cite_note-41]

### 2019–2023: Decline and managerial instability

Between July and August 2019, Palestine participated in the [2019 WAFF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_WAFF_Championship); they were drawn with hosts [Iraq](1039652.md), [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team), Lebanon, and [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_national_football_team). Palestine ended the tournament with two wins, one draw and one defeat, and finished in second place in their group: however, this wasn't enough to reach the final.[^cite_note-42]

Palestine were drawn with Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Uzbekistan, Yemen at the [2022 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)). They kicked-off with a historic win over Uzbekistan 2–0 in [Al-Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ram).[^cite_note-43] Five days later, Palestine were defeated 2–1 to Singapore.[^cite_note-44] On 15 October 2019, Palestine drew 0–0 with Saudi Arabia, during which its decades-long boycott of play in the West Bank came to an end in protest against Israel's control over Palestinian territories.[^cite_note-45] Thereafter, the team lost again to Yemen 1–0.[^cite_note-46] This was later followed with another away loss to Uzbekistan, thus placing Palestine in the bottom and reducing the country's opportunity to reach the final round.

Manager Noureddine Ould Ali lost his job after 2022 World Cup qualification resumed in 2021 following a 5–0 loss to Saudi Arabia. In April 2021, Tunisian coach [Makram Daboub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makram_Daboub), who had previously worked as a goalkeeping coach under the leadership of Noureddine Ould Ali, who was dismissed, was assigned to supervise the national team on a temporary basis. Daboub led Palestine to victory over Singapore and Yemen. In June 2022, Palestine qualified for their third straight [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) finals, winning all three games against Mongolia, Yemen and Philippines.

### 2024–present: Revival and history made

On 23 January 2024, Palestine recorded their first ever [Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) victory and their first ever qualification to the Asian Cup knockout stage after a 3–0 victory over [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_national_football_team) in the [2023 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup).[^cite_note-47] They ended up losing 1–2 to the hosts [Qatar](1111350.md) in the quarterfinal despite taking the lead through [Oday Dabbagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oday_Dabbagh)'s goal in the 37th minute.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

On 7 June 2024, Palestine qualified to the [Third Round of FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round) for the first time after a 0–0 draw against [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team), securing second place in their [qualification group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round#Group_I) behind [Australia](4792249.md).[^cite_note-50] This result also saw them qualify for the [2027 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_AFC_Asian_Cup), their fourth consecutive qualification to the tournament.

Palestine then recorded their first ever qualification to the [Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Arab_Cup) [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Arab_Cup_knockout_stage) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Arab_Cup) but they lost the quarterfinal 2–1 against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) on 11 December 2025.

## Impact relating to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict

The [Palestinian Football Association](5531531.md) faces problems in training and playing due to the [Israeli conflict with the Palestinians](46216.md).[^cite_note-51]

Because of travel restrictions placed by [Israel](9282173.md) upon people in the West Bank and Gaza Strip and the difficulty in obtaining an exit visa from Israel, many players in the team are drawn from the [Palestinian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_diaspora), from as far away as [Chile](5489.md) and the United States.[^cite_note-52] Recently, Israel's refusal to issue exit visas has resulted in players, or in some case the entire team, being unable to represent the territories. In November 2006 all players based inside the West Bank and Gaza Strip were denied exit visas. The [AFC](715249.md) cancelled the match since both teams had been eliminated from the competition by virtue of their previous results against China and Iraq.[^cite_note-53]

In October 2007, the second leg of a crucial [2010 World Cup qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) between Palestine and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_national_football_team) was not played due to Palestine's inability to obtain exit visas. The [AFC](715249.md) and [FIFA](11049.md) decided not to reschedule the match despite protests from the PFA, and Singapore was awarded a 3–0 forfeit for their actions.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] In May 2008, the team was not allowed to travel to the [2008 AFC Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_AFC_Challenge_Cup).[^cite_note-57] After a 2011 World Cup qualifier against Thailand, two starters, Mohammed Samara and Majed Abusidu, were refused entry to the West Bank and therefore could not travel back with the team from Thailand.[^cite_note-58]

Striker [Ziyad Al-Kord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyad_Al-Kord) was prevented from travelling and had his house destroyed.[^cite_note-59] Tariq al Quto was killed by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md),[^cite_note-60] and during the [Gaza War (2008–2009)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%932009\)), three Palestinian footballers, [Ayman Alkurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Alkurd), Shadi Sbakh, and Wajeh Moshtahe, were among the casualties.[^cite_note-61]

[Mahmoud Sarsak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Sarsak) was [administratively detained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_detention) by Israel in July 2009 for being a member of the [Islamic Jihad Movement in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Jihad_Movement_in_Palestine); following a [hunger strike](222292.md) and international pressure on his behalf from [FIFA](11049.md) president [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter), he was released in July 2012.[^cite_note-62] In April 2012, Olympic Team goalkeeper Omar Abu Ruways was arrested by Israel in connection with an attack on [IDF](39237.md) soldiers.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

[Sameh Maraaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sameh_Maraaba) was arrested in April after returning with his team from training in [Qatar](8238258.md). The Shin Bet accused Maraaba of exploiting his status as a Palestinian football player to act as a courier for [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-jpost.com-65] Shin Bet released a statement in which it contended that Maraaba admitted that, before he left for Qatar, he met a senior Hamas operative in [Kalkilya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalkilya), asked him to meet an operative from Hamas' military wing in Qatar and received money, a cellphone and written messages which he brought back to Kalkilya.[^cite_note-jpost.com-65]

Palestinian facilities, such as the [Palestine Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Stadium), have been damaged in military conflicts.[^cite_note-66]

In addition, there have been reports of Israeli forces intentionally shooting Palestinian footballers in the knees and feet,[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] including on one occasion ten bullets in the feet of 19 year old footballer Jawhar Nasser Jawhar.[^cite_note-69]

In 2023, the team had to pull out of the [Merdeka Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Merdeka_Tournament) in Malaysia due to the [Gaza war](74998519.md). Later that year, all [2026 FIFA World Cup qualification](49459509.md) matches had to be held in neutral venues for the same reasons, though [Algeria](358.md) offered to host and was accepted by the PFA until FIFA and the AFC rejected the deal, stating that Palestine needed to play in AFC member venues, resulting in them going to [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), and other countries in the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world).[^cite_note-70]

In August 2025, the team relocated its base of operations to [Chile](5489.md) due to the [sizable Palestinian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinians_in_Chile) and distance from the Gaza war.[^cite_note-71]

## Competitive record

See also: [Palestine national football team results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_national_football_team_results)

As of 2 September 2021, the complete official match record of the Palestine national team comprises [229 matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_national_football_team_results): 66 wins, 57 draws and 106 losses.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] During these matches, the team scored 291 times and conceded 357 goals. Palestine's highest winning margin is 11 goals, which has been achieved against [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam_national_football_team) in 2006 (11–0). Their longest winning streak is 7 wins, and their unbeaten record is 12 consecutive official matches.
