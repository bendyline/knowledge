The **Holy See Press Office** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Sala Stampa Sanctae Sedis*; [Italian](14708.md): *Sala Stampa della Santa Sede*) is the [press office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_office) of the [Holy See](13393.md). It publishes the official news of the activities of the [Pope](23056.md) and of the various departments of the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia). All speeches, messages and documents, as well as the statements issued by the Director, are published in their entirety.&#x20;

The press office is located outside [Vatican City](32408.md), in [Via della Conciliazione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_della_Conciliazione).[^cite_note-1]&#x20;

## Role

The press office operates every day in Italian, although texts in other languages are also available.&#x20;

On 27 June 2015, [Pope Francis](1687680.md), through an [apostolic letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_letter#Letters_of_the_popes_in_modern_times) issued *[motu proprio](793396.md)* ("on his own initiative"), established the [Secretariat for Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_for_Communication) in the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia). The Press Office was incorporated into it, but at the same time belongs to the [Secretariat of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_of_State_\(Holy_See\)).[^cite_note-2] On 21 December 2015, Pope Francis appointed [Dr. Greg Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Burke_\(journalist\)), formerly the Communications Advisor for the Section for General Affairs of the Vatican's Secretariat of State (a key department in the Roman Curia), as Deputy Director of the Press Office.[^cite_note-3]&#x20;

Following Burke's appointment as director in 2016, Spanish journalist [Paloma García Ovejero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paloma_Garc%C3%ADa_Ovejero) took over as vice director, making her the first woman to hold that position.[^cite_note-ipppavab-4] It was also announced that both Burke and García Ovejero, both [laymen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laymen), would later begin their positions on 1 August 2016.[^cite_note-5] On 31 December 2018, both Burke and García Ovejero announced their resignations.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Pope Francis has therefore appointed Italian journalist Alessandro Gisotti, former deputy editor-in-chief of Vatican Radio, as interim director of the Holy See Press Office.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

In July 2019, Pope Francis named British-born Italian layman [Matteo Bruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Bruni) as Director.[^cite_note-auto-9] Bruni is the first non-journalist to serve in this position.[^cite_note-10] The same month, the Pope appointed Brazilian laywoman [Cristiane Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiane_Murray), who previously served as the [Vatican Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Radio) commentator for papal events and international trips for 25 years, as Vice Director.[^cite_note-auto-9][^cite_note-11] Murray was succeeded by Croatian nun and journalist [Nina Benedikta Krapić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Benedikta_Krapi%C4%87) on 1 March 2026.[^cite_note-press-12]&#x20;
