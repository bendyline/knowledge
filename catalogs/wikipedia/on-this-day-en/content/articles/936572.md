**Terrorism and mass attacks in Canada** includes [acts of terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_terrorism), as well as [mass shootings](31883778.md), [vehicle-ramming attacks](44318569.md), [mass stabbings](60784649.md), and other such acts committed in [Canada](5042916.md) that people may associate with terroristic tactics but have not been classified as terrorism by the Canadian legal system. (For example, the [2018 Toronto shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Toronto_shooting) was a mass shooting that law enforcement officials did not connect to terrorism.)[^cite_note-\:4-1]

The *[Criminal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Code_\(Canada\))* of Canada defines terrorist activity to include an "act or [omission](3577810.md) undertaken, in or outside Canada, for a political, religious or ideological purpose, that is intended to intimidate the public with regard to its security, including its economic security, or to compel a person, government or organization (whether in or outside Canada) to do or refrain from doing any act, and that intentionally causes one of a number of specific forms of serious harm."[^cite_note-\:4-1] As such, some of the terrorist acts listed here are related to external events and nationalities, while others, such as the [FLQ crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Crisis) in 1970, are related to internal tensions within the country.

Matters relevant to overall **counterterrorism in Canada**, as well as [national security](240468.md) within the federal government, fall under the jurisdiction of the [Minister of Public Safety and Emergency Preparedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Public_Safety_and_Emergency_Preparedness), who heads [Public Safety Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Safety_Canada) (PSC).[^cite_note-\:02-2] Two other ministers with particularly crucial roles with respect to counter-terrorism are the [Ministers of Foreign Affairs](270882.md) and [of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_National_Defence_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

The Canadian government uses the [National Terrorism Threat Level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Terrorism_Assessment_Centre#National_Terrorism_Threat_Level) (NTTL) to identify the probability of terrorism occurring in Canada. As of 22 May 2021, Canada's current threat level is "Medium," which means that a "violent act of terrorism could occur;" it has been at this level since October 2014.[^cite_note-\:4-1][^cite_note-\:3-4]

## Overview of legal framework and response

Overall issues of national security fall under the jurisdiction of the [Minister of Public Safety and Emergency Preparedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Public_Safety_and_Emergency_Preparedness), who heads [Public Safety Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Safety_Canada) (PSC).[^cite_note-\:02-2] While provincial and territorial governments are responsible for general law enforcement and criminal prosecutions, the [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md) (part of the PSC portfolio) is given primary responsibility, under the *[Security Offences Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Offences_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (SOA), for the investigation of offences involving national security.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Canadian federal [statutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute) establish a legal regime that prosecutes terrorists for their activities.[^cite_note-\:12-3][^cite_note-5] The [Public Prosecution Service of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Prosecution_Service_of_Canada) (PPSC), under the [Attorney General of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Canada), prosecutes offenses on behalf of the Canadian government, including those involving national security such as terrorist activities.[^cite_note-\:12-3] If a person carries out a terrorist attack or instructs someone else to carry out a terrorist attack, they are given a [life sentence](148681.md). Anyone who facilitates a terrorist attack is liable to imprisonment for no more than 10 years.[^cite_note-6]

The *[Criminal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Code_\(Canada\))* defines [terrorist activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_activity) to include an "act or omission undertaken, in or outside Canada, for a political, religious or ideological purpose, that is intended to intimidate the public with regard to its security, including its economic security, or to compel a person, government or organization (whether in or outside Canada) to do or refrain from doing any act, and that intentionally causes one of a number of specific forms of serious harm."[^cite_note-\:4-1]

The *[Combating Terrorism Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combating_Terrorism_Act)*, which came into force in July 2013, renewed parts of the Criminal Code by creating four new offences intended to prevent and deter individuals from leaving Canada for particular terrorism-related purposes.[^cite_note-\:4-1]

Canada's *[Anti-Terrorism Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-terrorism_Act,_2015)* (ATA) "provides measures for the Government of Canada to create a list of entities that: have knowingly carried out, attempted to carry out, participated in or facilitated a terrorist activity" or "knowingly acted on behalf of, at the direction of or in association with an entity that has knowingly carried out, attempted to carry out, participated in or facilitated a terrorist activity."[^cite_note-ListingProcess2-7] The Act specifically provides that "for the [Governor in Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_in_Council) to establish by regulation a list on which, on the recommendation of the Minister of Public Safety, any entity may be placed."[^cite_note-ListingProcess2-7] The ATA replaced the *Official Secrets Act* with the *[Security of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_of_Information_Act)* (SOIA), which focuses on "conduct harmful to, or likely to harm Canada." It also defined the concept of "**harm to Canadian interests**" to address a broad range of potential harms, including terrorist activity, interference with [critical infrastructure](521286.md), and the development of [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon_of_mass_destruction) in [contravention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravention) of international law.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

[Terrorist financing](4308598.md), on the other hand, is addressed through the *[Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceeds_of_Crime_\(Money_Laundering\)_and_Terrorist_Financing_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, as well as the *[Income Tax Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_taxes_in_Canada)* and the *Charities Registration (Security Information) Act*. In terms of the cross-[border](630260.md) movement of potential threats, assessing the admissibility of those applying for [temporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_residency_in_Canada) or [permanent residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_residency_in_Canada), or [refugee status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_in_Canada) is facilitated under the *[Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_and_Refugee_Protection_Act)* (IRPA).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

The *Department of Foreign Affairs and International Trade Act* provides the [Minister of Foreign Affairs](270882.md) with responsibility over all matters relating to the conduct of [Canada's external affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_Affairs_of_Canada), including countering [international terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_terrorism) and responding to terrorist incidents abroad.[^cite_note-\:12-3] Moreover, the Government of Canada lists [state supporters of terrorism](317645.md) under the *[State Immunity Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Immunity_Act_\(Canada\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, which (like the *JVTA*) revokes certain [legal immunities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_immunity) from listed countries, allowing [civil suits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_suit) to be filed against those countries in Canadian courts for loss or damage resulting from its involvement in terrorism anywhere in the world. In 2012, both [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Syria) and [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_iran) were listed.[^cite_note-\:4-1]

In terms of [emergency response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_response), leadership of the Public Safety Minister is granted by the *[Emergency Management Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Management_Act)*.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

### Redress

The *[Justice for Victims of Terrorism Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_for_Victims_of_Terrorism_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (*JVTA*) allows Canada's victims of terrorism to sue perpetrators and those that support them for loss or damage that occurred as a result of an act of terrorism committed in Canada or abroad.[^cite_note-\:0-8] Similar to the JVTA, amendments made to the *State Immunity Act*, allow for countries listed by the federal government as state supporters of terrorism to be [sued](113258.md) in Canadian courts for loss or damage resulting from its involvement in terrorism anywhere in the world.[^cite_note-\:4-1][^cite_note-\:0-8]

On 1 May 2006, the Government of Canada announced the launch of a full [public inquiry](826604.md) into the [Air India Flight 182 bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182) and its investigation. Initiated later in June, the inquiry was to look into the ways in which [Canadian law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Canada) restricted funding [terrorist groups](63578.md), how well [witness protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witness_protection) is provided in terrorist cases, if Canada needs to upgrade its [airport security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_security), and if issues of co-operation between the RCMP, CSIS, and other law enforcement agencies had been resolved. It was to also provide a forum wherein families of the victims could testify on the impact of the bombing and would not repeat any criminal trials.[^cite_note-9]

From 1 December 2011 to 31 July 2012, families of the passengers and crew who died on Air India Flight 182 on 23 June 1985 have been offered a one-time *[ex gratia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_gratia)* payment by Canadian government, as "a demonstration of solicitude and recognition for the administrative disdain families experienced in the years following" the tragedy. Payments have been made to eligible applicants for 275 victims.[^cite_note-\:1-10] Moreover, every June 23, some Canadians observe [National Day of Remembrance for Victims of Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Day_of_Remembrance_for_Victims_of_Terrorism) to honour the victims.

### Emergency response

In terms of emergency response, leadership of the Minister of Public Safety is granted by the *Emergency Management Act*. The chief means through which the Public Safety Minister facilitates their emergency response is through the [Government Operations Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Operations_Centre?action=edit\&redlink=1) (GOC). The GOC, on behalf of the federal government, supports response coordination across the government and others.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

In practice, the immediate response to terrorist incidents in Canada is to be led by local law enforcement and emergency management authorities. This involves the RCMP as being the first police responder in those provinces and territories where it provides local police services. Particular terrorist incidents may involve specific responses from designated agencies; for instance, under the *[National Defence Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_Act)* or through the [Crown Prerogative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_prerogative), the [Canadian Armed Forces](182792.md) can be called upon to respond directly to terrorist incidents in Canada. As such, the Minister of National Defence plays a key role in preparation for, and execution of, any deployment of Canadian Forces in response to terrorist activity domestically or abroad.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Immediately following the 2001 [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in the United States, the RCMP initiated [Project Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Shock) which sought to quickly collect and act on any information or intel related to possible threats posed by [Muslims in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Canada).[^cite_note-11] [Project Thread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Thread) was a police operation in 2003 that resulted in the arrest of 24 immigrants in the [Greater Toronto Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Toronto_Area) amidst allegations that they formed a threat to national security, and maintained "suspected ties to [al-Qaeda](1921.md)."[^cite_note-cbc-12][^cite_note-ctv-13]

## Counter-terrorism in Canada

Matters relevant to overall **counter-terrorism in Canada**, as well as [national security](240468.md) within the federal government, fall under the jurisdiction of the Minister of Public Safety and Emergency Preparedness, who heads Public Safety Canada (PSC).[^cite_note-\:02-2] Two other ministers with particularly crucial roles with respect to counter-terrorism are the Ministers of Foreign Affairs and [of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_National_Defence_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-\:12-3] Additionally, the [National Security Advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Advisor_\(Canada\)) (NSA) is responsible for providing information, advice, and recommendations on security and intelligence to the Prime Minister; co-ordinating the security and intelligence community; and overseeing intelligence assessment.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Claiming that the safety and security of [Canadians](19851291.md) to be its first priority, the [Government of Canada](616255.md) puts significant attention towards its [counterterrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) efforts.[^cite_note-\:22-14] *"*&#x42;uilding Resilience Against Terrorism" (2013)[^cite_note-\:12-3] is Canada's first counterterrorism strategy, which aims to counter [domestic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism) and international terrorism so to protect Canada, Canadians, and Canadian [interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_interest). The strategy "assesses the nature and scale of the threat, and "sets out basic principles and elements that underpin the Government's counter-terrorism activities."[^cite_note-\:22-14]

The Canadian government uses the [National Terrorism Threat Level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Terrorism_Assessment_Centre#National_Terrorism_Threat_Level) (NTTL) to identify the probability of terrorism occurring in Canada. As of 22 May 2021, Canada's current threat level is "Medium," which means that a "violent act of terrorism could occur;" it has been at this level since October 2014.[^cite_note-\:3-4][^cite_note-\:4-1] More specifically, this means that "[extremist groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremist_group) and individuals located in Canada and abroad, have both the intent AND capability to carry out an act of terrorism in Canada."[^cite_note-\:4-1]

#### Intelligence assessment

The [Canadian Security Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Security_Intelligence_Service), part of Public Safety Canada, is Canada's primary national [intelligence agency](81014.md). Among other things, CSIS compiles the information they collect themselves with information from other sources to provide [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) with intelligence assessments on terrorist threats. The National Security Advisor (NSA) is responsible for providing information, advice, and recommendations on security and intelligence to the Prime Minister; co-ordinating the security and intelligence community; and overseeing intelligence assessment.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

The [Integrated Terrorism Assessment Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Terrorism_Assessment_Centre) (ITAC) is a cooperative initiative, housed at CSIS, to facilitate [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_intelligence) information sharing and analysis within the Canadian [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_assessment) community and to [first responders](500273.md), such as [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police). Its assessments integrate intelligence from across departments and agencies as well as from external partners. Similarly, the RCMP's [Integrated National Security Enforcement Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_National_Security_Enforcement_Teams) (INSETs) bring together federal, provincial, and municipal police and intelligence resources "to collect, share, and analyze information in support of criminal investigations and threat assessments."[^cite_note-\:12-3]

The [Communications Security Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_Security_Establishment), administered under the [Department of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_National_Defence_\(Canada\)) (DND), is responsible for foreign [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence) (SIGINT) and protecting government [electronic communication networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_communication_network).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

The [Privy Council Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_Office_\(Canada\))'s Security and Intelligence Secretariat provides policy support to the NSA, the Prime Minister, and the [Clerk of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerk_of_the_Privy_Council_\(Canada\)), as well as coordinating committees on security and intelligence issues. The PCO's International Assessment Staff (PCO IAS) helps to coordinate "the efforts of the Canadian assessment community and provides PCO and other senior government clients with policy-neutral assessments of foreign developments and trends that may affect Canadian interests."[^cite_note-\:12-3]

#### Terrorist financing

Terrorist financing in Canada is addressed through the *Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act* (*PCMLTFA*), as well as the *Income Tax Act* and the *Charities Registration (Security Information) Act*, which are administered by the [Department of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Finance_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

In accordance with the *PCMLTFA*, the [Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Transactions_and_Reports_Analysis_Centre_of_Canada) works to prevent and deter terrorist financing. Likewise, the [Charities Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charities_Directorate_of_Canada) of the [Canada Revenue Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Revenue_Agency) reviews applications, conducts [audits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audit), and collects and analyzes multi-source intelligence in order to detect and address risks to Canada's [charitable sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_sector). These operations are in accordance with the *Income Tax Act*, the *Charities Registration (Security Information) Act*, and the *PCMLTFA*.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

The Finance Department also leads the Canadian effort in related international activities, particularly regarding the work of the [Financial Action Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force) (FATF).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

#### Border and transportation security

Measures taken in regards to cross-border movement of potential threats are facilitated under the *Immigration and Refugee Protection Act* (*IRPA*), primarily by the [Canada Border Services Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Border_Services_Agency) (CBSA) and [Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada](546737.md) (IRCC).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

The CBSA, which is a part of Public Safety Canada, has an "Immigration Security Screening" program, which can "detect the movement of potential subjects of interest as they apply for temporary or permanent residence, or refugee status," as per *IRPA.* CBSA also monitors the cross-border flow of currency, and has the authority to seize "unreported currency flows suspected of being the proceeds of crime or related to terrorist financing."[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Under *IRPA*, the Canadian government uses [security certificates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_certificate) to detain and [deport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deport) [foreign nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_national) and all other non-citizens living in Canada. The certificate can be issued towards a permanent resident or any other non-citizen perceived to be a threat to national security, as well as those suspected of violating human rights or of having membership within organized crime.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

The security of [transportation systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Canada) are primarily facilitated by [Transport Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Canada), which is the lead department for responding to transportation security incidents and for transportation-related [emergency preparedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_preparedness). This includes the security of [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_security), marine, rail, road, and [intermodal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_passenger_transport) transportation security systems. Transport Canada's responsibilities are granted through several key federal statutes, such as the *[Aeronautics Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics_Act)*, the *[Marine Transportation Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Transportation_Security_Act)*, the *[Railway Safety Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Safety_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, the *[International Bridges and Tunnels Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bridges_and_Tunnels_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1)* and the *[Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_of_Dangerous_Goods_Act,_1992)*.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Public Safety Canada and Transport Canada jointly oversee the Canadian [no-fly list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-fly_list), called the [Passenger Protect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_Protect) program, which identifies individuals who may pose a threat to aviation security and "reduces their ability to cause harm or threaten aviation by taking action, such as preventing them from boarding an aircraft." Additional security is provided through passenger and [baggage screening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baggage_screening), which is conducted by Transport Canada's [Canadian Air Transport Security Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Air_Transport_Security_Authority) (CATSA).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

#### International and extraterritorial security

The *Combating Terrorism Act*, which came into force in July 2013, was put in place with the intent to prevent and deter individuals from leaving Canada for particular terrorism-related purposes.[^cite_note-\:4-1]

The *Department of Foreign Affairs and International Trade Act* provides the Minister of Foreign Affairs with responsibility over all matters relating to the conduct of Canada's external affairs, including countering international terrorism and responding to terrorist incidents abroad.[^cite_note-\:12-3] As such, the Foreign Affairs Minister leads Canada's response to terrorist or security-related incidents outside of the country. [Global Affairs Canada](256258.md) is accordingly responsible for assessing social, economic, security, and political developments that "help define a global threat environment." Depending on the nature of the incident, the Canadian response can include the provision of [consular assistance](9587132.md) to Canadians overseas (including [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate)); financial or physical aid; or deployments of experts from Canada's national security community.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

[Extraterritorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritorial_jurisdiction) investigations of terrorist activity are investigated by the RCMP (part of Public Safety Canada) when committed against a [Canadian citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Citizen) or by a Canadian citizen abroad.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Various federal organizations, particularly the Canada Centre for Community Engagement and Prevention of Violence (Canada Centre; also part of Public Safety Canada), closely collaborate with partners in the [Five Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Eyes) ([United States](5199.md), [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations), [Australia](18254355.md), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93New_Zealand_relations)), the [Group of Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Seven) (G7), and the [European Union](16279404.md). The Canada Centre also actively participates in [multilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism) forums such as the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_and_the_United_Nations) and the [Global Counterterrorism Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Counterterrorism_Forum) (GCTF), as well as collaborating with the [Institute for Strategic Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Strategic_Dialogue), the [Hedayah Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedayah_Center?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the [Centre for Research and Evidence on Security Threats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Research_and_Evidence_on_Security_Threats?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CREST).[^cite_note-\:2-18]

The Canadian government also engages in the [Global Coalition against Daesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Global_Coalition_against_Daesh), a partnership of 79 countries that works towards defeating [Daesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daesh) through such activities as preventing the flow of foreign terrorist fighters across borders and countering the group's communications.[^cite_note-\:2-18]

#### Biosecurity

In terms of [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_warfare), [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_warfare), [radiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiological_warfare), and [nuclear warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warfare), the surveillance for diseases and events resulting from the use of [CBRNE agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBRN_defense) is the responsibility of [Public Health Agency of Canada](1170869.md), who is in charge of coordinating a public health response to such a terrorist incident. Monitoring services, hazard assessments, information, and advisories and decontamination strategies are also provided by [Health Canada](216516.md) for CBRNE events. PHAC also maintains the [National Emergency Stockpile System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Emergency_Stockpile_System), which contains [medical countermeasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_countermeasure) against CBRNE agents and disaster medical supplies for use in [mass-casualty incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass-casualty_incident).[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Moreover, all members of the Canadian Forces are trained in CBRNE defense. The [Canadian Joint Incident Response Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Joint_Incident_Response_Unit) is a unit under the direction of the [Canadian Special Operations Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Special_Operations_Forces_Command) that is tasked with supporting the federal government in preventing, controlling, and mitigating CBRN threats to Canada, Canadians, and Canadian interests.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

Health Canada is also mandated to provide services to support the overall security objectives for major international events (such as the 2010 [Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) or [G20 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_G20_Toronto_summit)), specifically in the areas of health and safety of federal government employees, surveillance and response to radiological nuclear threats, and support to first-responders in the event of a CBRNE event or disease outbreak.[^cite_note-\:12-3]

### Designated organizations

The government of Canada has banned more than 52 [terrorist organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizations_designated_as_terrorist_by_Canada).[^cite_note-19] These include [Al Qaeda](1921.md), the [Armed Islamic Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Islamic_Group), [Euskadi Ta Askatasuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskadi_Ta_Askatasuna) (ETA), [Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Tigers_of_Tamil_Eelam) (LTTE), the [International Sikh Youth Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Sikh_Youth_Federation), the [Palestinian Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Liberation_Front), the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine), the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine-General Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine-General_Command), [Hamas](13913.md), [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md), [Hezbollah](13919.md),[^cite_note-ADLPROFILE2-20] [Kahane Chai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahane_Chai), and the [Taliban](30635.md).[^cite_note-THREEORGS2-21] In 2019, [Combat 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_18) and [Blood & Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_&_Honour) were the first [neo-Nazi groups in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Nazism_in_Canada) to be banned by the government.[^cite_note-22]

In 2026, Canadian Security Intelligence Service declared Khalistani extremists a national security threat, highlighting that the group uses institutions to promote its "violent extremist agenda" in the country. The agency, in its 2025 Public Report, had stated that the involvement of Canada-based Khalistani extremist (CBKE) in violent activities "continues to pose a national security threat to Canada and to Canadian interests."[^cite_note-25]

## Research on extremism in Canada

The **Kanishka Project** was a CA\$10 million, five-year initiative of Public Safety Canada that provided funding to research on terrorism-related matters affecting Canada.[^cite_note-\:1-10][^cite_note-\:8-26][^cite_note-\:9-27]

Announced by the Government of Canada in June 2011, the Project was named after the [Boeing 747-237B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-237B) (*Emperor [Kanishka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanishka)*) plane that was bombed in the Air India Flight 182 attack of 1985.[^cite_note-\:8-26][^cite_note-\:9-27] It funded nearly 70 projects and contributed to hosting various events related to discussing counter-terrorism.[^cite_note-\:8-26] As part of the Project, \$3.7 million was invested with [Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Sciences_and_Humanities_Research_Council) (SSHRC) towards supporting research and related activities that addressed the issues identified by the Kanishka Project.[^cite_note-28]

The **Canadian Network for Research on Terrorism, Security and Society** (TSAS), located at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo), is an academic [research network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_network) purposed to form "[multidisciplinary research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidisciplinary_research) on terrorist radicalization" and "the coordinated interaction of academic researchers with government officials." It was created in 2012 with funding both the Kanishka Project and a grant from the SSHRC.[^cite_note-\:9-27][^cite_note-\:10-29] TSAS' co-directors are [Lorne Dawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorne_L._Dawson) and Veronica Kitchen.[^cite_note-\:10-29]

## Radicalization in Canada

The *[Constitution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada)*, through the *[Charter of Rights and Freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Charter_of_Rights_and_Freedoms)*, protects the [freedom of thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_thought), [belief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_belief), [opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_opinion), and [expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_expression_in_Canada) of Canadians. As a result, having radical ideals is not considered to be illegal in Canada.[^cite_note-\:2-18]

In terms of radicalization to violence, various federal and provincial/territorial government initiatives have been established, particularly under Public Safety Canada on the federal level. Particularly, the Canada Centre for Community Engagement and Prevention of Violence (Canada Centre) leads the Canadian government's efforts to "counter [radicalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicalization) to violence." It does not manage or advise on individual cases, but rather addresses the issue in terms of broad strategy.[^cite_note-30] The Centre was mandated in 2015; the [federal budget the following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Canadian_federal_budget) allocated \$35 million over five years to establish and support the Centre, in addition to \$10 million per year on-going.[^cite_note-\:2-18] The Canada Centre also leads the Canadian government's engagement and cooperation with the [Global Internet Forum to Counter Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Internet_Forum_to_Counter_Terrorism) (GIFCT), established by [Google](1092923.md), [Facebook](7529378.md), [Twitter](9988187.md), and [Microsoft](19001.md) in 2017.[^cite_note-\:2-18]

On 11 December 2018, the Canada Centre launched the "National Strategy on Countering Radicalization to Violence," which is meant to "explain\[] radicalization to violence and the destructive and harmful behaviours involved," as well as outlining the federal government's "approach and key priorities in countering and preventing radicalization to violence." Implementation of the National Strategy is supervised by the National Expert Committee on Countering Radicalization to Violence.[^cite_note-\:2-18]

The Community Resilience Fund (CRF) is a system, administered by the Canada Centre, for supporting "partnerships in countering radicalization to violence in Canada," providing funding to organizations towards engagement (e.g., research, cooperation, engaging communities, etc.) with the issue.[^cite_note-31] As of 2019‑2020 onward, the Fund has been promised \$7 million available each year for existing and new projects. Through CRF, the Canada Centre has supported the [Centre for the Prevention of Radicalization Leading to Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_the_Prevention_of_Radicalization_Leading_to_Violence?action=edit\&redlink=1), located in [Montréal](7954681.md), in conducting research on "better understanding risk and protective factors within families of individuals who radicalize to violence and also the role families and communities can play in mitigating radicalization to violence." Also through the Fund, the Canada Centre has supported "[multi-agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agency_coordination) intervention programs to build capacity to manage cases of individuals who are radicalizing to violence."[^cite_note-\:2-18]

- [Status of Women Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Women_Canada) — Strategy to Prevent and Address Gender-Based Violence

- [Edmonton Resiliency Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Resiliency_Project?action=edit\&redlink=1) — a "collaborative approach" delivered by the [Edmonton Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Police_Service), [City of Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Edmonton), and [Organization for the Prevention of Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_the_Prevention_of_Violence?action=edit\&redlink=1) "to prevention and intervention that draws on trusted community and organizational relationships to prevent violent extremism."

- [Calgary Re-Direct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Re-Direct?action=edit\&redlink=1) — a partnership between the [City of Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Calgary), Community and Neighbourhood Services, the [Calgary Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Police_Service), and others that "uses a multidisciplinary approach to intervention with youth and young adults who are vulnerable to radicalization to violence."

- [Social Polarizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Polarizations?action=edit\&redlink=1) — a team of mental-health professionals who specialize in interventions to counter radicalization to violence, based in a local Integrated Health and Social Services Centre in [Quebec](7954867.md).

- Centre for the Prevention of Radicalization Leading to Violence (CPRLV; often called the Montreal Centre)

### Extremists with Canadian connections abroad

The *Combating Terrorism Act*, which came into force in July 2013, was put in place with the intent to prevent and deter individuals from leaving Canada for particular terrorism-related purposes. In 2018, the Canadian government states that there has not been an increase, nor does it expect an increase, in the number of **Canadian Extremist Travellers** (**CETs**) who have returned to Canada. The total number of CETs identified by the government includes around 190 individuals who have a nexus to Canada, and near 60 who have returned.[^cite_note-\:4-1]

The following are some extremists around the world with Canadian connections.[^cite_note-\:4-1]

- Xristos Katsiroubas and Ali Medlej ([Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Canada_relations)) — two high school friends from [London, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Ontario) who went to [Morocco](19291.md) in 2011, and then onwards to [Mauritania](18778516.md), [Niger](21373.md), and [Libya](17633.md), where they are said to have trained under Algerian terrorist [Mokhtar Belmokhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokhtar_Belmokhtar). Both were killed after taking part in a terrorist attack in the [In Amenas hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Amenas_hostage_crisis) in January 2013, which killed 38 hostages at an Algerian gas plant.

- Andre Poulin ([Syria](7515849.md)) — a man from [Timmins, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timmins,_Ontario) who subscribed to violent extremist views in 2008. Arriving in Syria in 2012, he is considered to have been killed fighting at the al-Minakh airfield in August 2013.

- [Ali Mohamed Dirie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Dirie) (Syria) — a former member of the "[Toronto 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_18)" previously imprisoned for plotting terrorist attacks in Ontario. Within a year of his release, Dirie travelled to Syria and joined a terrorist group.

- Damian Clairmont or Mustafa al-Gharib (Syria) — a man from [Calgary, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary) who subscribed to violent extremist views. He is presumed to have been killed in infighting between terrorist groups in Syria.

- Mahad Ali Dhore ([Somalia](27358.md)) — a man who crossed into Somalia while visiting [Kenya](188171.md) to join an [al-Shabaab](8726463.md) training camp. He is considered to have been killed while helping conduct an April 2013 terrorist attack in [Mogadishu](60154.md).
