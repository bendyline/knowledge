**Gytheio** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Γύθειο [\[ˈʝiθio\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek)) or **Gythio**, also the ancient **Gythium** or **Gytheion** ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Γύθειον), is a town on the eastern shore of the [Mani Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_Peninsula) in the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese_\(region\)) of [southern Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Greece), in the historical and administrative region of [Laconia](18386.md). It is the largest and most important town in [Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_Peninsula). Gytheio is the seat of the municipality of [East Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Mani). Gytheio is significant in the history of Mani and the [Maniots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maniots).

Ancient Gytheio was the seaport of [Ancient Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta), approximately 40 kilometres (25 miles) north. Gytheio continued to be a major port until its destruction in the 4th century CE, possibly by an [earthquake](10106.md). Its strategic location made it a coveted possession for foreign powers into the 20th century.&#x20;

The small island of [Cranae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranae), where, according to myth, [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_\(mythology\)) and [Helen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy) spent their first night together before departing for [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy), lies off the coast of Gytheio.&#x20;

Gytheio was formerly a municipality in [Laconia](18386.md). Since a nationwide 2011 government reform it is part of the municipality [East Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Mani), of which it is a municipal unit.[^cite_note-Kallikratis-2] The municipal unit has an area of 197.313 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-stat01-3]

## Geography

Gytheio is located in the northeastern corner of [the Mani Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_Peninsula) and lies on the northwestern end of the [Laconian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laconian_Gulf). Gytheio was built on a hill called Koumaros or Laryssio in one of the most fertile areas in Mani, near the mouth of the [Gythium River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gythium_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is usually dry and has been nicknamed *Xerias* "dry river"; today, most of the Xerias is covered by Ermou Avenue.

Directly north and visible from the harbor is Profitis Ilias, the [ultra-prominent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-prominent_peak) peak of [Taygetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taygetus), the mountain range whose spine juts southward into the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and forms the Mani Peninsula.

On the ridgeline running south from Profitis Ilias sits the [Monastery of Panayia Yiatrissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery_of_Panayia_Yiatrissa) overlooking the valley toward Gytheio; the [E4 hiking path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E4_European_long_distance_path) connects the three, running south from Profitis Ilias, passing by the monastery, and leading to Gytheio.

Northeast of Gytheio is the delta of the [Evrotas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evrotas_River). Offshore are several small islands; the most important of these islands is [Cranae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranae), on which sits the Tzannetakis Tower (now the Historical and Cultural museum of Mani) and a lighthouse built of solid marble. Today Cranae is connected to the mainland by a causeway.

Approximately 5 km (3 mi) southwest is a passageway to the deeper Mani, historically guarded by [Castle Passavas](https://www.kastra.eu/castleen.php?kastro=passavas) (now in ruins), which towers over the site of ancient [Las](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_\(Greece\)). Further west is the historic city of [Areopoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areopoli) and the [Caves of Diros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alepotrypa_cave), which are important tourist attractions.

Gytheio is only 40 km (25 mi) southeast of [Sparti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparti), connected by [Greek National Road 39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_National_Road_39). The town center is situated around the port. Pine trees are situated in the west and rocky mountains in the north.

## History

The mythical founders of ancient Gythium were [Heracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles) and [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo),[^cite_note-7] who frequently appear on its coins or in other legends, and [Castor and Pollux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castor_and_Pollux):[^cite_note-Fermor-8] the former of these names may point to the influence of [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) traders from [Tyre](30796.md), who visited the Laconian shores at a very early period.[^cite_note-9] It is thought that Gytheio may have been the center of their purple dye trade because the Laconian Gulf had a plentiful source of [murex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murex). In classical times it was a community of the *[Perioeci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perioeci)* caste, politically dependent on Sparta, though doubtless with a municipal life of its own.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETod1911-10]

In 455 BC during the [First Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Peloponnesian_War) (460 – 445 BCE) between Sparta and [Athens](1216.md) Gytheium was burned by the Athenian admiral [Tolmides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolmides), who besieged the city with 50 ships and 4,000 [hoplites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoplites).[^cite_note-Fermor-8][^cite_note-11] It was rebuilt and likely became the shipyard for the Spartan fleet in the [Second Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Peloponnesian_War) (431 — 404 BCE). In 407 BC, [Alcibiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcibiades) landed there and saw the thirty [triremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trireme) the Spartans were building.[^cite_note-Fermor-8][^cite_note-12] In 370 BC, the [Thebans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes,_Greece) under the command of [Epaminondas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epaminondas) besieged the city successfully for three days after ravaging [Laconia](18386.md),[^cite_note-Fermor-8] but it was recaptured by the Spartans three days later.

In 219 BC, [Philip V of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Macedon) unsuccessfully attempted to capture the city.[^cite_note-Fermor-8] Under [Nabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabis_of_Sparta), Gythium became a major naval arsenal and port. During the [Roman-Spartan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman-Spartan_War), Gythium was captured after a lengthy siege. After the war finished, Gythium was made part of the [Union of Free Laconians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Free_Laconians) under [Achean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achean_League) protection.[^cite_note-Greenhalgh-Eliopoulos-13] Nabis recaptured Gythium three years and the Spartan fleet defeated the Achean fleet outside of Gythium. Gythium was liberated by a [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) fleet under the command of [Aulus Atilius Serranus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulus_Atilius_Serranus_\(consul_170_BC\)).

Subsequently, Gythium formed the most important of the [Union of Free Laconians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Free_Laconians), a group of twenty-four, later eighteen, communities leagued together to maintain their autonomy against Sparta and declared free by [Caesar Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar_Augustus).[^cite_note-14] The highest officer of the confederacy was the general, who was assisted by a treasurer (*rauias*), while the chief magistrates of the several communities bore the title of *[ephors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephors)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETod1911-10]

In Roman times Gythium remained a major port and it prospered as a member of the Union.<sup>[\[Roman Gythium\]](#cnote_Roman_Gythium)</sup>[^cite_note-Greenhalgh-Eliopoulos-13] As purple dye was popular in [Rome](25458.md), Gythium exported that as well as [porphyry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_\(geology\)) and rose antique marble.[^cite_note-Fermor-8] Evidence of the ancient Gythium prosperity can be found by the fact that the Romans built an ancient theatre which is well preserved today and is still used occasionally. The ancient theatre and the city's [acropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acropolis) (west of the theatre) were discovered by the archeologist Dimitris Skias in 1891. Some time in the 4th century AD, the city was destroyed.[^cite_note-Fermor-8] What happened to Gythium is not recorded but it is thought to have been either sacked by [Alaric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaric_I) and the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths), pillaged by the [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) or destroyed by the massive earthquake that struck the area in 375 AD.[^cite_note-Fermor-8]

After the earthquake, Gythium was abandoned. It remained a small village throughout the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) and [Ottoman](22278.md) times. Its importance grew when Tzannetos Grigorakis built his tower at [Cranae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranae) and more people came and settled at Gytheio.[^cite_note-Fermor-8] During the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence), refugees flooded into [Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_Peninsula) and made Gytheio a major town.[^cite_note-Saïtis46-47-15]

The modern Gytheio opened a port in the 1960s. Ferries sail from Gytheio to [Kythira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kythira) almost daily and also to [Crete](6591.md) twice a week. It is the [See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see) of the [Diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) of Gytheion and [Oitylo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oitylo), headed by a [Metropolitan bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of the [Orthodox](10186.md) [Church of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Greece). Gytheio is the largest and most important town in Mani. Most of the ruins of ancient Gythium are now submerged in the Laconian Gulf. Some walls' remains can be seen today on the sandy beach of Valtaki and in the shallow waters, where the well known [Dimitrios shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrios_shipwreck) lies stranded. It is also the capital of the municipality of Gytheio.

## Province



The former province of Gytheio within the Peloponnese.

The province of Gytheio ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Επαρχία Γυθείου) was one of the [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Greece) of the Laconia Prefecture. Its territory corresponded with that of the current municipal units Gytheio and [East Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Mani).[^cite_note-census91-16] It was abolished in 2006.
