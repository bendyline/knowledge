**Maryna Sergiivna Viazovska** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Марина Сергіївна Вязовська,[^cite_note-VNLU-2] pronounced [\[mɐˈrɪnɐ wʲɐˈzɔu̯sʲkɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian); born 2 December 1984[^cite_note-3]) is a Ukrainian mathematician known for her work in [sphere packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_packing). She is a full professor and Chair of [Number Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_Theory) at the Institute of Mathematics of the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Polytechnique_F%C3%A9d%C3%A9rale_de_Lausanne) in Switzerland.[^cite_note-\:0-4] She was awarded the [Fields Medal](10859.md) in 2022.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-qf-6]

## Education and career

Viazovska was born in [Kyiv](585629.md), the oldest of three sisters. Her father was a chemist who worked at the [Antonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov) aircraft factory, and her mother was an [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer).[^cite_note-qf-6] She attended a specialized secondary school for high-achieving students in science and technology, [Kyiv Natural Science Lyceum No. 145](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Natural_Science_Lyceum_No._145). An influential teacher there, Andrii Knyazyuk, had previously worked as a professional research mathematician before becoming a secondary school teacher.[^cite_note-mactutor-7] Viazovska competed in domestic mathematics Olympiads when she was at high school, placing 13th in a national competition where 12 students were selected to a training camp before a six-member team for the [International Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Olympiad) was chosen.[^cite_note-qf-6] As a student at [Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv), she competed at the [International Mathematics Competition for University Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematics_Competition_for_University_Students) in 2002, 2003, 2004, and 2005, and was one of the first-place winners in 2002 and 2005.[^cite_note-8] She co-authored her first research paper in 2005.[^cite_note-qf-6]

Viazovska earned a master's from the [University of Kaiserslautern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kaiserslautern) in 2007, [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) from the [Institute of Mathematics of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematics_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine) in 2010,[^cite_note-VNLU-2] and a doctorate ([Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat.)) from the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 2013. Her doctoral dissertation, *Modular Functions and Special Cycles*, concerns [analytic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_number_theory) and was supervised by [Don Zagier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Zagier) and [Werner Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_M%C3%BCller_\(mathematician\)).[^cite_note-9]

She was a postdoctoral researcher at the [Berlin Mathematical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Mathematical_School) and the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin)[^cite_note-quanta-10] and a Minerva Distinguished Visitor[^cite_note-11] at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). Since January 2018 she has held the Chair of Number Theory as a [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor) at the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Polytechnique_F%C3%A9d%C3%A9rale_de_Lausanne) (EPFL) in Switzerland after a short stint as [tenure-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_tenure) assistant professor.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

## Contributions

In 2016, Viazovska solved the [sphere-packing problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_packing) in [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension) 8.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Her dimension 8 solution quickly led to collaboration with others, and a solution in dimension 24.[^cite_note-newscientist-15][^cite_note-quanta-10] Previously, the problem had been solved only for three or fewer dimensions, and the proof of the three-dimensional version (the [Kepler conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler_conjecture)) involved long computer calculations. In contrast, Viazovska's proof for 8 and 24 dimensions has been described as "stunningly simple".[^cite_note-quanta-10]

A few years later, in 2018, Viazovska and her collaborators significantly extended[^cite_note-UniversalOptimality-16] their sphere packing results in dimensions 8 and 24 to address potential energy minimization.[^cite_note-17] In 2019, Viazovska and her team solved a mathematical equation that determines how an infinite number of points repelling each other are placed in 8- and 24-dimensional spaces.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-q19-19][^cite_note-siaz-20]

As well as for her work on sphere packing, Viazovska is also known for her research on [spherical designs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_design) with Bondarenko and Radchenko. With them she proved a conjecture of [Korevaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Korevaar) and Meyers on the existence of small designs in arbitrary dimensions. This result was one of the contributions for which her co-author Andriy Bondarenko won the Vasil A. Popov Prize for [approximation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_theory) in 2013.[^cite_note-21]

## Recognition

In 2016, Viazovska received the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize)[^cite_note-22] and, in 2017, the [Clay Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Research_Award) and the [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize) for her work on sphere packing and [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_form).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] In December 2017, she was awarded a 2018 [New Horizons Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Horizons_Prize_in_Mathematics).[^cite_note-25] She was an invited speaker at the 2018 [International Congress of Mathematicians](627842.md).[^cite_note-icm-26] For 2019 she was awarded the [Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Lyttle_Satter_Prize_in_Mathematics)[^cite_note-27] and the [Fermat Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_Prize).[^cite_note-28] She is one of the 2020 winners of the [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize).[^cite_note-29] In 2020, she also received the National Latsis Prize awarded by the [Latsis Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latsis_Foundation).[^cite_note-30] She was elected to the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2021.[^cite_note-31] She was appointed Senior Scholar at the [Clay Mathematics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Mathematics_Institute) in July 2022.[^cite_note-32]

She was awarded the [Fields Medal](10859.md) in July 2022, making her the second woman (after [Maryam Mirzakhani](6409655.md)), the second person born in the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) (after [Vladimir Drinfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Drinfeld)) and the first with a degree from a Ukrainian university to ever receive it.[^cite_note-Cohn_Viazovska-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] She was honored as one of the [BBC 100 Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Women_\(BBC\)) in December 2022.[^cite_note-36]

## Personal life

Viazovska met her husband, Daniil Evtushinsky, at an after-school physics group for schoolchildren. He is also a researcher at EPFL, in physics. They have two children, a son and a daughter.[^cite_note-qf-6][^cite_note-allnews-37]
