The **Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Peru** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores*, **MRE**) is the [government ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ministries_of_Peru) in charge of [foreign policy and international relations and cooperation of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Peru). It works in coordination with the country's [diplomatic network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_missions_of_Peru), accredited to different countries and [international organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organizations). In the same way, it also works with the [diplomatic body in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_in_Peru) accredited to the Peruvian State.

## History

On August 3, 1821, just six days after proclaiming [Peru's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence), [José de San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn) created three [Secretariats of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ministries_of_Peru) (this date is commemorated annually as *Diplomat's Day*):[^cite_note-1]

- The **Secretariat of State and Foreign Affairs** (*Secretaría de Estado y Relaciones Exteriores*), headed by [Juan García del Río](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Garc%C3%ADa_del_R%C3%ADo).

- The [Secretariat of War and the Navy](5629239.md), headed by Lieutenant Colonel [Bernardo de Monteagudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_de_Monteagudo)

- The [Secretariat of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_and_Finance_\(Peru\)), headed by Dr. [Hipólito Unanue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip%C3%B3lito_Unanue).

Two years later, Peru's [first Political Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Peru), promulgated on November 12, 1823, consolidated the existence of these three secretariats, giving them the name "ministry" (*ministerio*).

On March 21, 1824, [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) issued a decree in [Trujillo](476649.md) reducing the three ministries to a single one, which would become the **General Secretariat of Affairs of the Peruvian Republic** (*Secretaría General de los Negocios de la República Peruana*), under Colonel José Gabriel Pérez. However, shortly thereafter, the Governing Junta repealed the decree, reestablishing the three ministries.[^cite_note-2]

On June 1, 1826, [José de la Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_la_Mar), following Bolívar's orders, decreed the creation of six Secretariats of State: Foreign Affairs; [Justice and Ecclesiastical Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Peru\)); [Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Peru\)); [Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_and_Finance_\(Peru\)); [War](5629239.md), and [Navy](5629239.md). These six secretariats were to be distributed among four Ministries: **Foreign Affairs and Interior**; Justice and Ecclesiastical Affairs; Treasury; and War and Navy. At the end of that same year, Bolívar drafted his Lifetime Constitution, which was sworn in on December 9 and changed the names of the ministries to Secretariat Offices (*Secretarías de Despacho*).

After Bolívar left Peru, La Mar issued a new Constitution. Under this Constitution, only three Ministries of State were established: one of **Government and Foreign Affairs**; another of War and Navy; and another of Finance. This Constitution was replaced in 1834, and during the government of General [Luis José de Orbegoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Jos%C3%A9_de_Orbegoso), the administration of the three Ministries was to be consolidated into a General Secretariat. Later, during the brief administration of General [Felipe Salaverry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Salaverry), the three Ministries created by San Martín in 1821 were reinstated.

Once the [Peru–Bolivian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%E2%80%93Bolivian_Confederation) was established, [Andrés de Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_de_Santa_Cruz) ordered these three Ministries to be in charge of the Confederation's affairs. Following the [fall of the Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Peru%E2%80%93Bolivian_Confederation), the Constitution of 1839 was enacted, and a fourth Ministry was added: the Ministry of Public Instruction, Charity, and Ecclesiastical Affairs. In the following years, a General Ministry was once again created, but it was abolished in August 1844, reestablishing only two Ministries: the **Ministry of Government and Foreign Affairs**, and the Ministry of Finance.

In April 1845, during the government of Ramón Castilla, four ministries were reestablished: **Foreign Affairs**; Government; War and Navy; and Finance. By Decree No. 90 of July 31, 1846, Castilla created the **Peruvian Diplomatic Service** (*Servicio Diplomático del Perú*).[^cite_note-3] In June 1855, the Ministry was organized as the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Public Education**. The following year, in November 1856, the Constitution established that there would normally be five Ministries: Foreign Affairs; Government, Worship, and Public Works; Justice, Education, and Charity; War and Navy; and Finance and Commerce.

In March 1857, the Foreign Ministry was internally organized into two sections: the Overseas and Continental sections. In April 1878, during [Mariano Ignacio Prado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Ignacio_Prado)'s second administration, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs was reorganised. Under this arrangement, the Ministry was divided into two sections: the Diplomatic Section, and the Consulates, Chancery, and Accounting Sections, replacing the Overseas and Continental sections, respectively.

Following the aforementioned modifications, a series of measures were enacted from 1903 to 2006 regarding the internal organization of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

By Supreme Decree 025-2007-Re of April 20, 2007, the [Peruvian Antarctic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Ant%C3%A1rtico_Peruano) became part of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

## Organisation

According to the Organization and Functions Regulation, the Ministry has the following directorates:[^cite_note-4]

- Directorate-General of Foreign Policy Studies and Strategies

- Directorate-General of America

- Directorate-General of Sovereignty, Limits and Antarctic Affairs

- Directorate-General of Europe

- Directorate-General of Asia and Oceania

- Directorate-General of Africa, Middle East and Gulf countries

- Directorate-General for Multilateral and Global Affairs

- Directorate-General for Economic Affairs

- Directorate-General of Economic Promotion

- [Directorate-General of Peruvian Communities Abroad and Consular Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Stock_Exchange_Building)

- Directorate-General for Cultural Affairs

- Directorate-General of Treaties

- Directorate-General of Protocol and Ceremonial of the State

Entities administered by the ministry include:

- Peruvian Agency for International Cooperation (APCI)

- [Peruvian Antarctic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Ant%C3%A1rtico_Peruano) (IAP)
