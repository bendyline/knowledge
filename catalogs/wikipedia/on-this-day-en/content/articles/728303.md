The **Izu Islands** (伊豆諸島, *Izu Shotō*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[i.(d)zɯ, i.(d)zɯ ɕoꜜ.toː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-1]) are a group of [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic) islands stretching south and east from the [Izu Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_Peninsula) of [Honshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honsh%C5%AB), Japan.[^cite_note-2] Administratively, they form two towns and six villages, all part of [Tokyo Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolis). The largest is [Izu Ōshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_%C5%8Cshima), usually called simply Ōshima.

Although usually called the "[Seven Islands of Izu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Islands_of_Izu)" ([伊豆七島](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BC%8A%E8%B1%86%E4%B8%83%E5%B3%B6) in Japanese), there are in fact more than a dozen islands and islets. Nine among them are currently inhabited.

## Geography

The Izu islands stretch south-east from the [Izu Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_Peninsula) on [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) and cover an area of approximately 301.56 km<sup>2</sup> (116.43 sq mi). There are nine populated islands with a total population of 24,645 people (as of 2009) spread over 296.56 km<sup>2</sup> (114.50 sq mi). The largest of them is [Izu Oshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_%C5%8Cshima) (8,346 inhabitants, 91.06 km<sup>2</sup> (35.16 sq mi)), the smallest [Toshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To-shima,_Tokyo) (292 inhabitants, 4.12 km<sup>2</sup> (1.59 sq mi)). Of the inhabited islands, seven are traditionally referred to as the "Izu Seven": Oshima, Toshima, [Niijima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nii-jima), [Kozushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dzu-shima), [Miyakejima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyake-jima), [Hachijojima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachij%C5%8D-jima), and [Mikurajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikura-jima), though [Shikinejima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikine-jima) and [Aogashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aogashima) are sometimes included as well.

During the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period), Nii-jima, Miyake-jima, and Hachijō-jima served as places of exile for criminals.

The subtropical [Ogasawara Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogasawara_Islands), which are also administratively part of Tokyo, lie further to the south. They form a far-flung archipelago of over thirty (30) islands some 1,000 km (621 mi) due south of Tokyo.

## Administrative divisions

The Izu Islands are divided into two towns (Oshima and Hachijojima) and six villages (the remaining inhabited islands.) Three [subprefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprefectures_of_Japan) are formed above the municipalities as branch offices of the [metropolitan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government).

All the islands (more than a dozen in total) lie within the [Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji-Hakone-Izu_National_Park). The four southernmost islands are not administered under any town or village in Hachijō Subprefecture, but are [unincorporated areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area). [Torishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torishima_\(Izu_Islands\)) is now uninhabited but is an important bird refuge.

Deserted islands between Aogashima and Ogasawara Islands, namely Bayonaise Rocks (*Beyonēzu Retsugan*), [Smith Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Island_\(Japan\)) (*Sumisu-tō*), [Torishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torishima_\(Izu_Islands\)), and [Lot's Wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lot's_Wife_\(crag\)) (*Sōfu-iwa*) do not belong to any [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Japan), because both Hachijō Town and Aogashima Village claim administrative rights. They are directly controlled by [Hachijō Subprefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachij%C5%8D_Subprefecture) instead.

## Infrastructure



[Oshima Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshima_Airport).

The primary industries are fisheries, agriculture, and tourism. The most scenic spots on the islands are crowded with tourists during summers. Popular tourist activities include swimming, scuba diving, surfing, fishing, bird watching and trekking.

Transportation between the islands, by [cargo-passengers boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_ship), [jetfoils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetfoil), and [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft), is supported by harbours on all inhabited islands and five airports (small islands can be reached by helicopter).

There are 5 airports, 15 harbors, and 19 fishing ports. Flights from Tokyo take 30 minutes, while boats take 7–10 hours and jetfoils make the route in about two hours. Transportation on the islands is considered important to the quality of life, which is why about 215 km (134 mi) of paved main roads have been constructed to serve various kinds of vehicles.

There was no electricity on the islands before 1953, but by 1962 98% of the area was receiving electricity.

## Geology

The islands occupy the northern portion of the [Izu–Bonin–Mariana Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu%E2%80%93Bonin%E2%80%93Mariana_Arc) which extends to the [Izu Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_Peninsula) and [Mount Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fuji) on the [Honshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honsh%C5%AB) mainland which are northern extensions of the Izu [volcanic arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_arc). The Izu arc ends there at a tectonic [triple junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_junction).

Volcanic activity is frequent in the area.[^cite_note-6] 31 people were killed when the research vessel *Kaiyō Maru no 5* was destroyed during the 1953 eruption of [Myōjin-shō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My%C5%8Djin-sh%C5%8D). Volcanic activity, including the release of harmful gases, forced the evacuation of Miyake-jima in 2000. Residents were allowed to return permanently to the island in February 2005 but were required to carry [gas masks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_mask) in case of future volcanic emissions.

To handle the various types of natural disasters threatening the region, including tsunamis, storm, floods, and volcanism, Tokyo metropolitan government has developed prevention and safety measures, including hazard maps and evacuation guidance, radios, signs, and a transport system for emergency supplies.

## Ecology

A chain of volcanic islands, the Izu Archipelago are [oceanic islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_island) that formed relatively recently (within a few million years) without any previous connection to mainland Japan. In contrast to isolated Pacific islands, such as [Hawaii](13270.md) and the [Galápagos](171118.md), the Izu Islands are located near the mainland and have thus been frequently colonized by various species by overseas dispersal from the mainland or from adjacent islands. This make them interesting for the studies of [ecological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) and [evolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) processes.[^cite_note-Kuriyama-etal-7]

*[Campanula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanula)* (Bellflower) colonized the entire archipelago in a single event. Similarly, the *[Euhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euhadra)* snails, endemic to Japan, populated the islands in a single event and all individuals on inhabited islands possess an identical [haplotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplotype). The *[Apodemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apodemus)* mice, on the other hand, colonized the islands from the mainland in two independent events.[^cite_note-Kuriyama-etal-7]
