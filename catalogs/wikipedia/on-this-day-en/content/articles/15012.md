**Islamism** is a range of [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion) and [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics) ideological movements that believe that [Islam](6037917.md) should influence political systems.[^cite_note-1] Its proponents believe Islam is innately political, and that Islam as a political system is [superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_supremacism) to [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), [liberal democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy), [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism), and other alternatives in achieving a just, successful society.[^cite_note-2] The advocates of Islamism are usually affiliated with Islamic institutions or social mobilization movements,[^cite_note-3] emphasizing the implementation of *[sharia](28840.md)*,[^cite_note-eikmeier-2007-4] [pan-Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamic) political unity,[^cite_note-eikmeier-2007-4] and the creation of [Islamic states](2646438.md).[^cite_note-5]

In its original formulation, Islamism described an ideology seeking to revive Islam to its past assertiveness and glory,[^cite_note-Burgat-IMiNA-1997-6] purifying it of foreign elements, reasserting its role into "social and political as well as personal life";[^cite_note-Berman,_S_2003,_p._258-7] and in particular "reordering government and society in accordance with laws prescribed by Islam" (i.e., Sharia).[^cite_note-BYERS-2013-8][^cite_note-shepard-1996-40-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] According to at least one observer (author [Robin Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Wright_\(author\))), Islamist movements have "arguably altered the Middle East more than any trend since the modern states gained independence", redefining "politics and even borders".[^cite_note-WRIGHT-SHI-10-1015-12] Another sole author ([Graham E. Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_E._Fuller)) has argued for a broader notion of Islamism as a form of [identity politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics), involving "support for \[Muslim] identity, authenticity, broader regionalism, revivalism, \[and] revitalization of the community".[^cite_note-Fuller-Future-21-13]

Central and prominent figures in 20th-century Islamism include [Rashid Rida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Rida),[^cite_note-Zhongmin_2013_23–28-14] [Hassan al-Banna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_al-Banna) (founder of the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md)), [Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb), [Abul A'la Maududi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_A'la_Maududi),[^cite_note-Fuller-Future-120-15] [Ruhollah Khomeini](26232.md) (founder of the Islamic Republic of Iran), and [Hassan Al-Turabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Al-Turabi).[^cite_note-16] [Syrian](8862873.md) [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) [cleric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulama) Muhammad Rashid Riḍā, a fervent opponent of [Westernization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westernization), [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) and [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism), advocated Sunni internationalism through revolutionary restoration of a [pan-Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamism) [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) to politically unite the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Riḍā was a strong exponent of Islamic vanguardism, the belief that the [Muslim community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_community) should be guided by religious activists to steer the efforts for religious education and [Islamic revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_revival).[^cite_note-19] Riḍā's [Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement)-[Arabist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabist) synthesis and Islamist ideals greatly influenced his disciples like Hasan al-Banna,[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] an [Egyptian](31912046.md) schoolteacher who founded the Muslim Brotherhood movement, and [Hajji Amin al-Husayni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajj_Amin_al-Husayni), the [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism) [Grand Mufti of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mufti_of_Jerusalem).[^cite_note-22] Al-Banna and Maududi called for a "[reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformism)" strategy to re-Islamizing society through [grassroots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassroots) social and political activism.[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:24-23][^cite_note-Ham-24] Other Islamists (Al-Turabi) are proponents of a "[revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary)" strategy of [Islamizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization) society through exercise of state power,[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:24-23] or (Sayyid Qutb) for combining grassroots Islamization with armed revolution. The term has been applied to non-state reform movements, political parties, militias and revolutionary groups.[^cite_note-Nugent-Wapo-23-6-2014-25]

Islamists themselves prefer terms such as "Islamic movement",[^cite_note-26] or "Islamic activism" to "Islamism", objecting to the insinuation that Islamism is anything other than Islam renewed and revived.[^cite_note-ICG-27] Because of its connotations in Western mainstream media, critics say the term may lead to Islamophobia and stereotyping.[^cite_note-Poljarevic-def-28][^cite_note-Shepard-29]

Prominent Islamist groups and parties across the world include the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md), Turkey's [Justice and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_\(Turkey\)), [Hamas](13913.md), the Algerian [Islamic Salvation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Salvation_Front), the Malaysian [National Trust Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust_Party_\(Malaysia\)), [Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami) in [Bangladesh](21739288.md) and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami_\(Pakistan\)) and Bosnia's [Party of Democratic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Democratic_Action).[^cite_note-30] Following the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring), many [post-Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Islamism) currents became heavily involved in democratic politics,[^cite_note-WRIGHT-SHI-10-1015-12][^cite_note-foreignpolicy1-31] while others spawned "the most aggressive and ambitious Islamist [militia](20622.md)" to date, such as the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL).[^cite_note-WRIGHT-SHI-10-1015-12] ISIL has been rejected as blasphemous by the majority of Islamists.[^cite_note-32]

## Terminology

Originally the term *Islamism* was simply used to mean the religion of Islam, not an ideology or movement. It first appeared in the English language as *Islamismus* in 1696, and as *Islamism* in 1712.[^cite_note-OED-33] The term appears in the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) decision in *In Re Ross* (1891). By the turn of the twentieth century the shorter and purely Arabic term "Islam" had begun to displace it, and by 1938, when Orientalist scholars completed *The [Encyclopaedia of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopaedia_of_Islam)*, *Islamism* seems to have virtually disappeared from English usage.[^cite_note-34] The term remained "practically absent from the vocabulary" of scholars, writers or journalists until the [Iranian Islamic Revolution](347268.md) of 1978–79, which brought [Ayatollah Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatollah_Khomeini)'s concept of "Islamic government" to Iran.[^cite_note-35]

This new usage appeared without taking into consideration how the term *Islamist* (m. sing.: *Islami*, pl. nom/acc: *Islamiyyun*, gen. *Islamiyyin;* f. sing/pl: *Islamiyyah*) was already being used in traditional Arabic scholarship in a theological sense as in relating to the religion of Islam, not a political ideology. In heresiographical, theological and historical works, such as [al-Ash'ari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hasan_al-Ash'ari)'s well-known encyclopaedia *Maqālāt al-Islāmiyyīn* (*The Opinions of The Islamists*), an Islamist refers to any person who attributes himself to Islam without affirming nor negating that attribution. If used consistently, it is for impartiality, but if used in reference to a certain person or group in particular without others, it implies that the author is either unsure whether to affirm or negate their attribution to Islam, or trying to insinuate his disapproval of the attribution without controversy.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] In contrast, referring to a person as a [Muslim](19541.md) or a [Kafir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafir) implies an explicit affirmation or a negation of that person's attribution to Islam. To evade the problem resulting from the confusion between the Western and Arabic usage of the term Islamist, Arab journalists invented the term *Islamawi* (*Islamian*) instead of *Islami* (*Islamist*) in reference to the political movement, though this term is sometimes criticized as [grammatically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar) incorrect.[^cite_note-41]

### Definitions

Islamism has been defined as:

- "the belief that Islam should guide social and political as well as personal life" ([Sheri Berman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheri_Berman));

  [^cite_note-Berman,_S_2003,_p._258-7]

- the belief that Islam should influence political systems ([Cambridge English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Advanced_Learner's_Dictionary));

  [^cite_note-42]

- "the \[Islamic] ideology that guides society as a whole and that \[teaches] law must be in conformity with the [Islamic sharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Shari'a)", (W. E. Shepard);

  [^cite_note-shepard-1996-40-9]

- a combination of two pre-existing trends&#x20;

  - movements to revive the faith, weakened by "foreign influence, political opportunism, moral laxity, and the forgetting of sacred texts";

    [^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:4-43]

  - the more recent movement against imperialism/colonialism, morphed into a more simple anti-Westernism; formerly embraced by leftists and nationalists but whose supporters have turned to Islam.

    [^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:4-43]

- a form of "religionized politics" and an instance of [religious fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_fundamentalism) that imagines an Islamic community claiming global hegemony for its values ([Bassam Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassam_Tibi));

  [^cite_note-44]

- "political movement that favors reordering government and society in accordance with laws prescribed by Islam" ([Associated Press](18935732.md) stylebook);

  [^cite_note-BYERS-2013-8]

  [^cite_note-cair-5-4-2013-45]

- a political ideology which seeks to enforce Islamic precepts and norms as generally applicable rules for people's conduct; and whose adherents seek a state based on Islamic values and laws (sharia) and rejecting Western guiding principles, such as freedom of opinion, freedom of the press, artistic freedom and freedom of religion (Thomas Volk);

  [^cite_note-Volk-KAS-2015-1-46]

- a broad set of political ideologies that use and draw inspiration from Islamic symbols and traditions in pursuit of a sociopolitical objective—also called "political Islam" ([Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica));

    [^cite_note-Belt-2009-48]

- a movement so broad and flexible it reaches out to "everything to everyone" in Islam, making it "unsustainable" (Tarek Osman);

  - an alternative social provider to the poor masses;

  - an angry platform for the disillusioned young;

  - a loud trumpet-call announcing "a return to the pure religion" to those seeking an identity;

  - "\[...] and at the extremes, a violent vehicle for rejectionists and radicals.

    [^cite_note-Osman.p111-49]

  [^cite_note-Burgat-IMiNA-1997-6]

  [^cite_note-ICG-27]

### Relationship between Islam and Islamism

Further information: [Political aspects of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_aspects_of_Islam)



Islamists' public demonstration in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) for [sharia](28840.md), October 2009

Islamists simply believe that their movement is either a corrected version or a revival of [Islam](6037917.md), but others believe that Islamism is a modern deviation from Islam which should either be denounced or dismissed.

A writer for the [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group) maintains that "the conception of 'political Islam'" is a creation of Americans to explain the [Iranian Islamic Revolution](347268.md), ignoring the fact that (according to the writer) Islam is by definition political. In fact it is [quietist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_quietism)/non-political Islam, not Islamism, that requires explanation, which the author gives—calling it an historical fluke of the "short-lived era of the heyday of secular Arab nationalism between 1945 and 1970".[^cite_note-ICGUnderstandingIslam-52]

Hayri Abaza argues that the failure to distinguish Islam from Islamism leads many in the West to equate the two; they think that by supporting illiberal Islamic (Islamist) regimes, they are being respectful of Islam, to the detriment of those who seek to [separate religion from politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism).[^cite_note-53]

Another source distinguishes Islamist from Islam by emphasizing the fact that Islam "refers to a religion and [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_culture) in existence [over a millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Islam)", whereas Islamism "is a political/religious phenomenon linked to the great events of the 20th century". Islamists have, at least at times, defined themselves as "Islamiyyoun/Islamists" to differentiate themselves from "Muslimun/Muslims".[^cite_note-54] [Daniel Pipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Pipes) describes Islamism as a modern ideology that owes more to European utopian political ideologies and "isms" than to the traditional Islamic religion.[^cite_note-55]

According to Salman Sayyid, "Islamism is not a replacement of Islam akin to the way it could be argued that [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) and [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) are secularized substitutes for Christianity." Rather, it is "a constellation of political projects that seek to position Islam in the centre of any [social order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_order)".[^cite_note-56]

## Ideology

### Islamic revival

Further information: [Islamic revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_revival)



Islamist demonstrators carry signs reading "Islam will dominate the world" and "To hell with democracy" in [Maldives](19117.md), September 2014

The modern revival of Islamic devotion and the attraction to things Islamic can be traced to several events.

By the end of World War I, most Muslim states were seen to be dominated by the Christian-leaning Western states. Explanations offered were: that the claims of Islam were false and the Christian or post-Christian West had finally come up with another system that was superior; or Islam had failed through not being true to itself. The second explanation, preferred by Muslims, called for a redoubling of faith and devotion by the faithful to reverse this tide.[^cite_note-57]

During the [Cold War](325329.md) following [World War II](32927.md), the United States and the United Kingdom launched covert and overt campaigns to encourage and strengthen Islamic fundamentalists in the Middle East, North Africa, and southern Asia. These Islamic fundamentalists were seen as a hedge against potential expansion by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and as a counterweight against [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) and [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) movements that were seen as a threat to the interests of the Western nations.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

According to some estimates, between 1960s and 2016, the Saudis funnelled over US\$100 billion to spread [Wahhabi Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism).[^cite_note-61] According to [political scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) Alex Alexiev, the impetus for the [international propagation of Salafism and Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_propagation_of_the_Salafi_movement_and_Wahhabism) by Saudi Arabia was "the largest worldwide [propaganda campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_campaign) ever mounted"[^cite_note-62], [David A. Kaplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Kaplan) described it as "dwarfing the [Soviets' propaganda efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_measures) at the height of the Cold War".[^cite_note-63] In a 2018 interview with The Washington Post, [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md), the de facto ruler of Saudi Arabia, said that Saudi Arabia's International propagation of the Salafi movement and Wahhabism was "rooted in the Cold War, when allies asked Saudi Arabia to use its resources to prevent inroads in Muslim countries by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)."[^cite_note-64] In 2013, the [European Parliament](9581.md) identified Wahhabism as the main source of global terrorism.[^cite_note-65]

The connection between the lack of an Islamic spirit and the lack of victory was underscored by the disastrous defeat of Arab nationalist-led armies fighting Israel under the slogan "Land, Sea and Air" in the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md), compared to the (perceived) near-victory of the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War) six years later. In that war, the military's slogan was "God is Great".[^cite_note-66]

Along with the Yom Kippur War came the [Arab oil embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), where the (Muslim) Persian Gulf oil-producing states' dramatic decision to cut back on production and quadruple the price of oil made the terms oil, Arabs, and Islam synonymous with power throughout the world, and especially in the Muslim world's public imagination.[^cite_note-67] Many Muslims believe as Saudi Prince Saud al Faisal did that the hundreds of billions of dollars in wealth obtained from the Persian Gulf's huge oil deposits were nothing less than a gift from God to the Islamic faithful.[^cite_note-68]

As the [Islamic revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_revival) gained momentum, governments such as Egypt's, which had previously repressed (and were continuing to repress) Islamists, joined the bandwagon. They banned alcohol and flooded the airwaves with religious programming,[^cite_note-Mu-69] giving the movement even more exposure.

### Restoration of the Caliphate

See also: [Khilafat Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khilafat_Movement)



*[Al-Manār](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Man%C4%81r_\(magazine\))* magazine, the most popular 20th-century Islamic journal that called for the restoration of the Caliphate

The [abolition of the Ottoman Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Ottoman_Sultanate) by the [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) on 1 November 1922 ended the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), which had lasted since 1299. On 11 November 1922, at the [Conference of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne_Conference_of_1922%E2%80%931923), the sovereignty of the Grand National Assembly exercised by the [Government in Angora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Government) (now [Ankara](802.md)) over Turkey was recognized. The last sultan, [Mehmed VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_VI), departed the Ottoman capital, [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Constantinople) (now [Istanbul](3391396.md)), on 17 November 1922. The legal position was solidified with the signing of the [Treaty of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne) on 24 July 1923. In March 1924, [the Caliphate was abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Caliphate) legally by the Turkish National Assembly, marking the end of Ottoman influence. This shocked the Sunni clerical world, and many felt the need to present Islam not as a traditional religion but as an [innovative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bid%CA%BBah) socio-political ideology of a modern nation-state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERahnema2005101-70]

The reaction to new realities of the modern world gave birth to Islamist ideologues like [Rashid Rida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Rida) and [Abul A'la Maududi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_A'la_Maududi) and organizations such as the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) in Egypt and [Majlis-e-Ahrar-ul-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis-e-Ahrar-ul-Islam) in India. Rashid Rida, a prominent Syrian-born Salafi theologian based in [Egypt](8087628.md), was known as a revivalist of [Hadith studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith_studies) in Sunni seminaries and a pioneering theoretician of [Islamism](2646438.md) in the modern age.[^cite_note-71] During 1922–1923, Rida published a series of articles in the seminal *[Al-Manar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Man%C4%81r_\(magazine\))* magazine titled "*[The Caliphate or the Supreme Imamate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Caliphate_or_the_Supreme_Imamate_\(book\))*". In this highly influential treatise, Rida advocates the restoration of the Caliphate, guided by [Islamic jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faq%C4%ABh), and proposes gradualist measures of education, reform, and purification through the efforts of *[Salafiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafiyya)* reform movements across the globe.[^cite_note-Willis_2010_711–732-72]

Sayyid Rashid Rida had visited India in 1912 and was impressed by the [Deoband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Uloom_Deoband) and [Nadwatul Ulama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Uloom_Nadwatul_Ulama) seminaries.[^cite_note-73] These seminaries carried the legacy of [Sayyid Ahmad Shahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Ahmad_Shahid) and his pre-modern Islamic emirate.[^cite_note-74] In [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), the [Khilafat movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khilafat_movement) (1919–24) following [World War I](4764461.md) led by [Shaukat Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaukat_Ali_\(politician\)), Maulana [Mohammad Ali Jauhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Ali_Jauhar), [Hakim Ajmal Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakim_Ajmal_Khan) and [Maulana Azad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Azad) came to exemplify South Asian Muslims' aspirations for [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate).

### Anti-Westernization

Further information: [Anti-Western sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Western_sentiment)

Muslim alienation from Western ways, including its political ways.[^cite_note-75]

- The memory in Muslim societies of the many centuries of "cultural and institutional success" of Islamic civilization that have created an "intense resistance to an alternative 'civilizational order'", such as Western civilization.

  [^cite_note-76]

- The proximity of the core of the Muslim world to Europe and Christendom, where it first conquered and then was conquered. [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) in the eighth century, the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades), which began in the eleventh century, and, for centuries, the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) were all fields of war between Europe and Islam.

  [^cite_note-77]

In the words of [Bernard Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lewis):

> For almost a thousand years, from the first Moorish landing in Spain to the second Turkish siege of Vienna, Europe was under constant threat from Islam. In the early centuries it was a double threat—not only of invasion and conquest, but also of conversion and assimilation. All but the easternmost provinces of the Islamic realm had been taken from Christian rulers, and the vast majority of the first Muslims west of Iran and Arabia were converts from Christianity ... Their loss was sorely felt and it heightened the fear that a similar fate was in store for Europe.[^cite_note-78]

For Islamists, the primary threat of the West is cultural rather than political or economic. Cultural dependency robs one of faith and identity and thus destroys Islam and the Islamic community (*[ummah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah)*) far more effectively than political rule.[^cite_note-Haddad/Esposito1-79]

### Strength of identity politics

Islamism is described by Graham E. Fuller as part of [identity politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics), specifically the religiously oriented nationalism that emerged in the Third World in the 1970s: "[resurgent Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_nationalism) in India, [Religious Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) in Israel, [militant Buddhism in Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_the_Sri_Lankan_civil_war), resurgent [Sikh nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarnail_Singh_Bhindranwale) in the [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region), '[Liberation Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Theology)' of [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) in Latin America, and Islamism in the Muslim world."[^cite_note-80]

### Anti-communist stances

Further information: [Anti-communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism)

By the late 1960s, non-Soviet Muslim-majority countries had won their independence, and they tended to fall into one of the two Cold War blocs – with "Nasser's Egypt, Baathist Syria and Iraq, Muammar el-Qaddafi's Libya, Algeria under [Ahmed Ben Bella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Ben_Bella) and Houari Boumedienne, [Southern Yemen](718749.md), and Sukarno's Indonesia" aligned with Moscow.[^cite_note-81] Aware of the close attachment of the population to Islam, "school books of the 1960s in these countries" went out of their way to impress upon children that socialism was simply Islam properly understood.[^cite_note-82] [Olivier Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Roy_\(political_scientist\)) writes that the "failure of the 'Arab socialist' model ... left room for new protest ideologies to emerge in deconstructed societies ..."[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:52-83] Gilles Kepel notes that when a collapse in oil prices led to widespread violent and destructive rioting by the urban poor in Algeria in 1988, what might have appeared to be a natural opening for the left, was instead the beginning of major victories for the Islamist [Islamic Salvation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Salvation_Front) (FIS) party. The reason was that the corruption and economic malfunction of the policies of the [Third World socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World_socialism) ruling party (FNL) had "largely discredited" the "vocabulary of socialism".[^cite_note-84] In the [post-colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcolonialism) era, many Muslim-majority states such as Indonesia, Egypt, Syria, and Iraq were ruled by authoritarian regimes which were often continuously dominated by the same individuals or their cadres for decades. Simultaneously, the military played a significant part in government decisions in many of these states ([the outsized role played by the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_state_in_Turkey) could be seen also in democratic Turkey).[^cite_note-Wi-85]

The authoritarian regimes, backed by military support, took extra measures to silence leftist opposition forces, often with the help of foreign powers. Silencing leftist opposition deprived the masses of a channel to express their economic grievances and frustration toward the lack of democratic processes.[^cite_note-Wi-85] As a result, in the [post-Cold War era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93Cold_War_era), civil society-based Islamist movements such as the Muslim Brotherhood were the only organizations capable of providing avenues of protest.[^cite_note-Wi-85]

The dynamic was repeated after the states had gone through a [democratic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization). In Indonesia, some secular political parties have contributed to the enactment of religious bylaws to counter the popularity of Islamist oppositions.[^cite_note-86] In Egypt, during the short period of the [democratic experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Egyptian_presidential_election), Muslim Brotherhood seized the momentum by being the most cohesive political movement among the opposition.[^cite_note-87]

## Influence



Algerian [secularist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) journalist [Tahar Djaout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahar_Djaout) was assassinated in 1993 by the [Armed Islamic Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Islamic_Group_of_Algeria)

Few observers contest the immense influence of Islamism within the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world).[^cite_note-murphy-160-88][^cite_note-cook-89][^cite_note-murphy-161-90] Following the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), political movements based on the liberal ideology of free expression and democratic rule have led the opposition in other parts of the world such as Latin America, Eastern Europe and many parts of Asia; however "the simple fact is that political Islam currently reigns \[circa 2002-3] as the most powerful ideological force across the Muslim world today".[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

The strength of Islamism also draws from the strength of [religiosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religiosity) in general in the Muslim world. Compared to other societies around the globe, "\[w]hat is striking about the Islamic world is that ... it seems to have been the least penetrated by [irreligion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion)".[^cite_note-cook-43-93] Where other peoples may look to the physical or social sciences for answers in areas which their ancestors regarded as best left to scripture, in the Muslim world, religion has become more encompassing, not less, as "in the last few decades, it has been the fundamentalists who have increasingly represented the cutting edge" of Muslim culture.[^cite_note-cook-43-93]

Writing in 2009, German journalist Sonja Zekri described Islamists in Egypt and other Muslim countries as "extremely influential. ... They determine how one dresses, what one eats. In these areas, they are incredibly successful. ... Even if the Islamists never come to power, they have transformed their countries."[^cite_note-qantara-94] Political Islamists were described as "competing in the democratic public square in places like [Turkey](11125639.md), [Tunisia](30188.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Indonesia](14579.md)".[^cite_note-95]

## Types

Islamism is not a united movement and takes different forms and spans a wide range of strategies and tactics towards the powers in place—"destruction, opposition, collaboration, indifference"—not because (or not just because) of differences of opinion,[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:24-23] but because it varies as circumstances change.[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:109-96][^cite_note-Valbjørn-POMEPS-97]<sup>p. 54</sup>

Moderate and reformist Islamists who accept and work within the democratic process include parties like the Tunisian [Ennahda Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennahda_Movement). Some Islamists can be religious [populists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist) or far-right.[^cite_note-k356-98] [Jamaat-e-Islami Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami_Pakistan) is basically a socio-political and "[vanguard party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard_party)" working within Pakistan's democratic political process, but has also gained political influence through military coups d'état in the past.[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:24-23] Other Islamist groups like [Hezbollah](13919.md) in [Lebanon](17771.md) and [Hamas](13913.md) in [Palestine](241405.md) claim to participate in the democratic and political process as well as armed attacks by their powerful paramilitary wings. [Jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) organizations like [al-Qaeda](1921.md) and the [Egyptian Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Islamic_Jihad), and groups such as the [Taliban](30635.md), entirely reject democracy, seeing it as a form of *[kufr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kufr)* (disbelief), calling for [offensive jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offensive_jihad) on a religious basis.

Another major division within Islamism is between what [Graham E. Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_E._Fuller) has described as the *conservative* "guardians of the tradition" ([Salafis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism), such as those in the [Wahhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabi) movement) and the *revolutionary* "vanguard of change and Islamic reform" centered around the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md).[^cite_note-Fuller,_2003_pp._194-99] [Olivier Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Roy_\(professor\)) argues that "[Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [pan-Islamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamism) underwent a remarkable shift in the second half of the 20th century" when the Muslim Brotherhood movement and its focus on Islamisation of [pan-Arabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism) was eclipsed by the [Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi) movement with its emphasis on "sharia rather than the building of Islamic institutions".[^cite_note-100] Following the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) (starting in 2011), Roy has described Islamism as "increasingly interdependent" with democracy in much of the [Arab Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), such that "neither can now survive without the other." ### Moderate and reformist Islamism

See also: [Islamic democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_democracy)

Throughout the 1980s and '90s, major moderate Islamist movements such as the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) and the Ennahda were excluded from democratic political participation. At least in part for that reason, Islamists attempted to overthrow the government in the [Algerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War) (1991–2002) and waged a [terror campaign in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Egypt) in the '90s. These attempts were crushed and in the 21st century, Islamists turned increasingly to non-violent methods,[^cite_note-Is-108] and "moderate Islamists" now make up the majority of the contemporary Islamist movements.[^cite_note-Ham-24][^cite_note-Fuller,_2003_pp._194-99][^cite_note-Om-109]

Among some Islamists, Democracy has been harmonized with Islam by means of *[Shura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shura)* (consultation). The tradition of consultation by the ruler being considered [Sunnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah) of the Islamic prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad),[^cite_note-Om-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] (*Majlis-ash-Shura* being a common name for legislative bodies in Islamic countries).

Among the varying goals, strategies, and outcomes of "moderate Islamist movements" are a formal abandonment of their original vision of implementing *[sharia](28840.md)* (also termed [Post-Islamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Islamism)) – done by the [Ennahda Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennahda_Movement) of Tunisia,[^cite_note-112] and [Prosperous Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosperous_Justice_Party) (PKS) of Indonesia.[^cite_note-Al-113] Others, such as the National Congress of Sudan, have implemented the sharia with support from wealthy, conservative states (primarily Saudi Arabia).[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch_Report-114][^cite_note-Fuller,_Graham_E._2003_p._108-115]

According to one theory – "inclusion-moderation" – the interdependence of political outcomes and strategy means that the more moderate the Islamists become, the more likely they are to be politically included (or unsuppressed), and the more accommodating the government is, the less "extreme" Islamists become.[^cite_note-116] A prototype of harmonizing Islamist principles within the modern state framework was the "[Turkish model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_model)", based on the apparent success of the rule of the Turkish [Justice and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_\(Turkey\)) (AKP) led by [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan).[^cite_note-117] Turkish model, however, came "unstuck" after [a purge and violations of democratic principles by the Erdoğan regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezi_Park_protests).[^cite_note-surreal-118][^cite_note-NYT-23-7-16-119] Critics of the concept – which include both Islamists who reject democracy and anti-Islamists – hold that Islamist aspirations are fundamentally incompatible with the democratic principles.

The contemporary [Salafi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement) is sometimes described as a variety of Islamism and sometimes as a different school of Islam,[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:35-120] such as a "phase between fundamentalism and Islamism".[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:31-121] Originally a reformist movement of [Jamal al-Din al-Afghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_al-Din_al-Afghani), Muhammad Abdul, and Rashid Rida, that rejected [maraboutism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marabout) (Sufism), the established schools of [fiqh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh), and demanded individual interpretation (*[ijtihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijtihad)*) of the Quran and [Sunnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah);[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:32-33-122] it evolved into a movement embracing the conservative doctrines of the medieval [Hanbali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanbali) theologian [Ibn Taymiyyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Taymiyyah). While all Salafis believe Islam covers every aspect of life, that sharia law must be implemented thoroughly, and that the Caliphate must be recreated to rule the Muslim world, they differ in strategies and priorities, which generally fall into three groups:&#x20;

- The "[quietist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_quietism_in_Islam#Salafists)" school advocates Islamization through preaching, educating the masses on [sharia](28840.md) and "purification" of religious practices, and ignoring government.

- Activist (or *haraki*) [Salafi activism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_Islamist) encourages political participation—opposing government loans with interest or normalization of relations with Israel, etc. As of 2013, this school makes up the majority of Salafism.

  [^cite_note-jof-123]

  &#x20;Salafist political parties in the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world) include the [Al-Nour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nour_Party) of Egypt, the [Al-Islah Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Islah_\(Yemen\)) of Yemen, and the [Al-Asalah Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Asalah) of Bahrain.

- [Salafi jihadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) (see below) is inspired by the ideology of [Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb) ([Qutbism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutbism), see below), and sees secular institutions as an enemy of Islam, advocating revolution to pave the way for the establishment of a new [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate).

### Militant Islamism/Jihadism

Main article: [Jihadism](7826589.md)

#### Qutbism

Main article: [Qutbism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutbism)

[Qutbism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutbism) refers to the [Jihadist](7826589.md) ideology formulated by [Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb), (an influential figure of the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) in [Egypt](30726095.md) during the '50s and '60s). Qutbism argued that not only was sharia essential for Islam, but that since it was not in force, Islam did not really exist in the Muslim world, which was in *Jahiliyya* (the state of pre-Islamic ignorance). To remedy this situation, he urged a two-pronged attack of 1) preaching to convert, and 2) jihad to forcibly eliminate the "structures" of *Jahiliyya*.[^cite_note-125] Defensive jihad against *Jahiliyya* Muslim governments would not be enough. "Truth and falsehood cannot coexist on this earth," so offensive Jihad was needed to eliminate *Jahiliyya* not only from the Islamic homeland but from the face of the Earth.[^cite_note-SOAGE-2009-192-126] In addition, vigilance against Western and Jewish conspiracies against Islam would-be needed.[^cite_note-carlisle.army.mil-127][^cite_note-Ha-128]

Although Qutb was executed before he could fully spell out his ideology,[^cite_note-Ke-129] his ideas were disseminated and expanded on by the later generations, among them [Abdullah Yusuf Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Yusuf_Azzam) and [Ayman Al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Al-Zawahiri), who was a student of Qutb's brother [Muhammad Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Qutb) and later became a mentor of [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] Al-Zawahiri helped to pass on stories of "the purity of Qutb's character" and persecution he suffered, and played an extensive role in the normalization of offensive Jihad among followers of Qutb.[^cite_note-132]

#### Salafi Jihadism

Main article: [Salafi jihadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism)

[Salafi Jihadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) or revolutionary Salafism[^cite_note-133] emerged prominent during the 1980s when [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) and thousands of other militant Muslims came from around the Muslim world to unite against the [Soviet Union after it invaded Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War).[^cite_note-By-134][^cite_note-deneoux-135][^cite_note-BLivesey-136][^cite_note-Kramer2003-137] Local Afghan Muslims ([mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen)) had declared jihad against the Soviets and were aided with [financial, logistical and military support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone) by [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and the United States, but after Soviet forces left Afghanistan, this funding and interest by America and Saudi ceased. The international volunteers, (originally organized by [Abdullah Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Azzam)), were triumphant in victory, away from the moderating influence of home and family, among the radicalized influence of other militants.[^cite_note-kepel-orig-138] Wanting to capitalize on financial, logistical and military network that had been developed[^cite_note-By-134] they sought to continue waging jihad elsewhere.[^cite_note-139] Their new targets, however, included the United States—funder of the mujahideen but "perceived as the greatest enemy of the faith"; and governments of majority-Muslim countries—perceived as apostates from Islam.[^cite_note-jihad-220-140][^cite_note-kepel-orig-138]

Salafist-jihadist ideology combined the literal and traditional interpretations of scripture of Salafists with the promotion and fighting of jihad against military and [civilian targets](30636.md) in the pursuit of the establishment of an [Islamic state](2646438.md) and eventually a new [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate).[^cite_note-kepel-orig-138][^cite_note-deneoux-135][^cite_note-Ha-128][^cite_note-141][^cite_note-143]

Other characteristics of the movement include the formal process of taking *[bay'ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay'ah)* (oath of allegiance) to the leader (*amir*), which is inspired by [Hadiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith) and early Muslim practice and included in Wahhabi teaching;[^cite_note-wright-12-12-16-144] and the concepts of "near enemy" (governments of majority-Muslims countries) and "far enemy" (United States and other Western countries). (The term "near enemy" was coined by [Mohammed Abdul-Salam Farag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Abdul-Salam_Farag), who led the assassination of [Anwar al-Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_al-Sadat) with [Egyptian Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Islamic_Jihad) (EIJ) in 1981.)[^cite_note-No-145] The "far enemy" was introduced and formally declared under attack by [al-Qaeda](1921.md) in 1996.[^cite_note-No-145][^cite_note-146]

The ideology saw its rise during the '90s when the Muslim world experienced numerous geopolitical crisis,[^cite_note-By-134] notably the [Algerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War) (1991–2002), [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) (1992–1995), and the [First Chechen War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Chechen_War) (1994–1996). Within these conflicts, political Islam often acted as a mobilizing factor for the local belligerents, who demanded financial, logistical, and military support from al-Qaeda, in exchange for active proliferation of the ideology.[^cite_note-By-134] After the [1998 bombings of US embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_United_States_embassy_bombing), [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) (2001), the [US-led invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan) (2001) and [Iraq](201936.md) (2003), Salafi Jihadism lost its momentum, being devastated by the US counterterrorism operations, culminating in [bin Laden's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Osama_bin_Laden) in 2011.[^cite_note-By-134] After the Arab Spring (2011) and subsequent [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) (2011–present), the remnants of al-Qaeda franchise in Iraq restored their capacity, rapidly developing into the [Islamic State](9087364.md) of Iraq and the Levant, spreading its influence throughout the conflict zones of [MENA region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MENA_region) and the globe. Salafi Jihadism makes up a minority of the contemporary Islamist movements.[^cite_note-Economist27Jun15-147]

### Shi'i Islamism

Main article: [Islamist Shi'ism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamist_Shi'ism)

Although most of the research and reporting about Islamism or political Islam has been focused on Sunni Islamist movements,[^cite_note-148] Islamism exists in [Twelver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver) [Shia Islam](26961.md) (the second largest branch of Islam that makes up approximately 10% of all Muslims.[^cite_note-149]). Islamist Shi'ism, also known as Shi'i Islamism, is primarily but not exclusively [^cite_note-150] associated with the thought of Ayatollah [Ruhollah Khomeini](26232.md), with the [Islamist Revolution](347268.md) he led, [Islamic Republic of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran) that he founded, and the religious-political activities and resources of the republic.

Compared to the "Types" of Islamism mentioned above, [Khomeinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khomeinism) differs from [Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Shi'ism#Modern_times) (which does not consider Shi'ism truly Islamic), [Salafism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism) (both orthodox or Jihadi—Shi'a do not consider some of the most prominent [salaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaf) worthy of emulation), reformist Islamism (the Islamic Republic executed more than 3,400 political dissidents between June 1981 and March 1982 in the process of consolidating power).[^cite_note-\:1-151][^cite_note-152]

Khomeini and his followers helped translate the works of Maududi and Qutb into Persian and were influenced by them, but their views differed from theirs and other Sunni Islamists in being "more leftist and more clerical":[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:2-153]

- more leftist in the propaganda campaign leading up to the revolution, emphasizing exploitation of the poor by the rich and of Muslims by imperialism;

  [^cite_note-KEA1993\:30-154]

  [\[note 5\]](#cite_note-158)

- more clerical in the new post-revolutionary state, where clerics were in control of the levers of power (the [Supreme Leader](36814344.md), [Guardian Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_Council), etc., under the concept of [Velayat-e Faqih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardianship_of_the_Islamic_Jurist).

  [\[note 6\]](#cite_note-160)

  ).

Khomeini was a "radical" Islamist,[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:36-161] like Qutb and unlike Maudidi. He believed that foreigners, Jews and their agents were conspiring "to keep us backward, to keep us in our present miserable state".[^cite_note-IaR1981\:34-162] Those who call themselves Muslims but were secular and Westernizing, were not just corrupt or misguided, but "agents" of the Western governments, helping to "plunder" Muslim lands as part of a long-term conspiracy against Islam.[^cite_note-Khomeini_(1981),_p._54-163] Only the rule of an Islamic jurist, administering Sharia law, stood between this abomination and justice, and could not wait for a peaceful, gradual transition. It is the duty of Muslims to "destroy" "all traces" of any other sort of government other than true Islamic governance because these are "systems of [unbelief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kufr)".[^cite_note-IaR1981\:48-164] "Troublesome" groups that cause "corruption in Muslim society," and damage "Islam and the Islamic state" are to be eliminated just as [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) eliminated the Jews of [Bani Qurayza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bani_Qurayza).[^cite_note-IaR1981\:89-165] Islamic revolution to install "the form of government willed by Islam" will not end with one Islamic state in Iran. Once this government comes "into being, none of the governments now existing in the world" will "be able to resist it;" they will "all capitulate".[^cite_note-IaR1981\:122-166]

#### Ruling Islamic Jurist

[Khomeini's form of Islamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khomeinism) was particularly unique in the world because it completely swept the old regime away, created a new regime with a new constitution, new institutions, and a new concept of governance (the [*Velayat-e Faqih*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardianship_of_the_Islamic_Jurist)). As a historical event, it changed militant Islam from a topic of limited impact and interest to a topic that few, either inside or outside the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world), were aware of.[^cite_note-Kepel,_Jihad,_2002,_p._106-167] As he originally described it in lectures to his students, the system of "[Islamic Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Government)" was one where the leading Islamic jurist would enforce sharia law—law which "has absolute authority over all individuals and the Islamic government".[^cite_note-168] The jurist would not be elected, and no legislature would be needed since divine law called for rule by jurist and "there is not a single topic in human life for which Islam has not provided instruction and established a norm".[^cite_note-169] Without this system, injustice, corruption, waste, exploitation and sin would reign, and Islam would decay. This plan was disclosed to his students and the religious community, but not widely publicized.[^cite_note-170] The constitution of the Islamic Republic, written after the revolution, did include a legislature and president, but supervising the entire government was a "[Supreme Leader](36814344.md)"/guardian jurist.

Islamist Shi'ism has been crucial to the development of worldwide Islamism because the Iranian regime attempted to export its revolution.[^cite_note-halliday-171] Although, the Islamist ideology was originally imported from Muslim Brotherhood, Iranian relations between the Muslim Brotherhood and Islamic Republic of Iran deteriorated due to its involvement in the Syrian civil war.[^cite_note-172] However, the majority [Usuli Shi'ism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuli) rejects the idea of an Islamist State in the period of [Occultation of the Hidden Imam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occultation_\(Islam\)).[^cite_note-Ghobadzadeh_1005–1027-173]

#### Shi'ism and Iran

Twelver Shia Muslims live mainly in a half dozen or so countries scattered around the Middle East and South Asia.[^cite_note-176] The Islamic Republic of Iran has become "the de facto leader"[^cite_note-Bokhari-2013-177] of the Shi'i world by virtue of being the largest Shia-majority state, having a long history of national cohesion and Shia-rule, being the site of the first and "only true"[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:168-178] [Islamist revolution](347268.md) (see History section below), and having the financial resources of a major petroleum exporter. Iran's influence has spread into a cultural-geographic area of "Irano-Arab Shiism", establishing Iranian regional power,[^cite_note-179] supporting "Shia militias and parties beyond its borders",[^cite_note-BBC-175][^cite_note-181] intertwining assistance to fellow Shi'a with "Iranization" of them.[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:168-178]

## Explanations for the growth and popularity of Islamism

### Sociological, economic and political

Some Western political scientists see the unchanging socio-economic condition in the Muslim world as a major factor. Olivier Roy believes "the socioeconomic realities that sustained the Islamist wave are still here and are not going to change: poverty, uprootedness, crises in values and identities, the decay of the educational systems, the North-South opposition, and the problem of immigrant integration into the host societies".[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:27-194]

#### Charitable work

Islamist movements such as the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) are, according to Graham Fuller, "well known for providing shelters, educational assistance, free or low-cost medical clinics, housing assistance to students from out of town, student advisory groups, facilitation of inexpensive mass marriage ceremonies to avoid prohibitively costly dowry demands, legal assistance, sports facilities, and women's groups." All this compares very favourably against incompetent, inefficient, or neglectful governments whose commitment to social justice is limited to rhetoric.[^cite_note-Fuller-Future-28-195]

#### Economic stagnation

The [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world)—the original heart of the Muslim world—has been afflicted with [economic stagnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_stagnation). For example, it has been estimated that in the mid-1990s the exports of [Finland](10577.md), a country of five million, exceeded those of the entire Arab world of 260 million, excluding oil revenue.[^cite_note-196]

#### Sociology of rural migration

[Demographic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_transition) (caused by the gap between the decline in death rates from medical advances and the decline in fertility rates) leads to population growth beyond the capacity of housing, employment, public transit, and sewer and water systems to provide. Combined with economic stagnation, [urban agglomerations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomerations) have emerged in Cairo, Istanbul, Tehran, [Karachi](17123.md), [Dhaka](56656.md), and [Jakarta](16275.md), each with well over 12 million residents, millions of whom are young and unemployed or underemployed.[^cite_note-Fuller-Future-68-197] Such a demographic, alienated from the [westernized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westernization) ways of the urban elite, but uprooted from the comforts and more passive traditions of the villages they came from, is understandably favourably disposed to an Islamic system promising a better world[^cite_note-198]—an ideology providing an "emotionally familiar basis for group identity, solidarity, and exclusion; an acceptable basis for legitimacy and authority; an immediately intelligible formulation of principles for both a critique of the present and a program for the future."[^cite_note-199] One American anthropologist in Iran in the early 1970s (before the revolution), when comparing a "stable village with a new urban slum", discovered that where "the villagers took religion with a grain of salt and even ridiculed visiting preachers", the slum dwellers—all recently dispossessed peasants – "used religion as a substitute for their lost communities, oriented social life around the mosque, and accepted with zeal the teachings of the local mullah".[^cite_note-200]

[Gilles Kepel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_Kepel) also notes that Islamist uprisings in Iran and Algeria, though a decade apart, coincided with the large numbers of youth who were "the first generation taught en masse to read and write and had been separated from their own rural, illiterate progenitors by a cultural gulf that radical Islamist ideology could exploit". Their "rural, illiterate" parents were too settled in tradition to be interested in Islamism and their children "more likely to call into question the utopian dreams of the 1970s generation", but they embraced revolutionary political Islam.[^cite_note-Kepel-jihad-365-201] Olivier Roy also asserts "it is not by chance that the Iranian Revolution took place the very year the proportion of city-dweller in Iran passed the 50% mark".[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:53-202] and offers statistics in support for other countries (in 1990 Algeria, housing was so crowded that there was an average of eight inhabitants to a room, and 80% of youth aged 16 to 29 still lived with their parents). "The old clan or ethnic solidarities, the clout of the elders, and family control are fading little by little in the face of changes in the social structure ..."[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:54-5-203] This theory implies that a decline in illiteracy and rural emigration will mean a decline in Islamism.

## Geopolitics

### State-sponsorship

#### Saudi Arabia

See also: [International propagation of conservative Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_propagation_of_conservative_Sunni_Islam)

Starting in the mid-1970s the Islamic resurgence was funded by an abundance of money from Saudi Arabian oil exports.[^cite_note-204] The tens of billions of dollars in "[petro-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro-Islam)" largesse obtained from the recently heightened price of oil funded an estimated "90% of the expenses of the entire faith."[^cite_note-205]

Throughout the Muslim world, religious institutions for people both young and old, from children's [madrassas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah) to high-level scholarships received Saudi funding,[^cite_note-206] "books, scholarships, fellowships, and mosques" (for example, "more than 1500 mosques were built and paid for with money obtained from public Saudi funds over the last 50 years"),[^cite_note-207] along with training in the Kingdom for the preachers and teachers who went on to teach and work at these universities, schools, mosques, etc.[^cite_note-208]

The funding was also used to reward journalists and academics who followed the Saudis' strict interpretation of Islam; and satellite campuses were built around Egypt for [Al-Azhar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Azhar_University), the world's oldest and most influential Islamic university.[^cite_note-209]

The interpretation of Islam promoted by this funding was the strict, conservative Saudi-based [Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism) or [Salafism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism). In its harshest form it preached that Muslims should not only "always oppose" infidels "in every way," but "hate them for their religion ... for Allah's sake," that democracy "is responsible for all the horrible wars of the 20th century," that [Shia](26961.md) and other non-Wahhabi Muslims were [infidels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Islam), etc.[^cite_note-210] While this effort has by no means converted all, or even most Muslims to the Wahhabist interpretation of Islam, it has done much to overwhelm more moderate local interpretations, and has set the Saudi-interpretation of Islam as the "gold standard" of religion in minds of some or many Muslims.[^cite_note-211]

#### Qatar

Further information: [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md)

Though the much smaller Qatar could not provide the same level of funding as Saudi Arabia, it was also a petroleum exporter and also sponsored Islamist groups. Qatar backed the Muslim Brotherhood in Egypt even after the [2013 overthrow of the MB regime of Mohamed Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Egyptian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), with Qatar ruler Sheikh [Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani](6146151.md) denouncing the coup.[^cite_note-Islam_Hassan-212] In June 2016, [Mohamed Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Morsi) was sentenced to life for passing state secrets to Qatar.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214]

Qatar has also backed Islamist factions in Libya, Syria and Yemen. In Libya, Qatar supported Islamists with tens of millions of dollars in aid, military training and "more than 20,000 tons of weapons", both before and after the 2011 fall of Muammar Gaddafi.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-\:2-216][^cite_note-\:3-217]

Hamas, in Palestine, has received considerable financial support as well as diplomatic help.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-\:3-217][^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220]

#### Pakistan

Pakistan's support for Islamism is a complex, long-standing, and multifaceted policy rooted in state-sponsored Islamization efforts. While officially condemning terrorism, Pakistan has been accused of utilizing extremist Islamist groups for geopolitical leverage in Afghanistan and Jammu & Kashmir, leading to domestic security challenges and global concern.[^cite_note-221]

#### Western support of Islamism during and after the Cold War

Further information: [Green Belt Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Belt_Theory), [CIA activities in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_activities_in_Afghanistan), [Operation Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone), and [Afghan mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen)



Afghan mujahideen representatives with [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_the_United_States) [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) at the [White House](33057.md) in 1983.

During the [Cold War](325329.md), particularly during the 1950s, during the 1960s, and during most of the 1970s, the U.S. and other countries in the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc) occasionally attempted to take advantage of the rise of Islamic religiousity by directing it against secular [leftist](18499.md)/[communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism)/[nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) insurgents/adversaries, particularly against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) states, whose ideology was not just secular but anti-religious.

In 1957, U.S. President [Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) and senior U.S. foreign policy officials, agreed on a policy of using the communists' lack of religion against them: "We should do everything possible to stress the '[holy war](16203.md)' aspect" that has currency in the Middle East.[^cite_note-222]

During the 1970s and sometimes later, this aid sometimes went to fledgling Islamists and Islamist groups that later came to be seen as dangerous enemies.[^cite_note-Berman-223] The US spent billions of dollars to aid the [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War#Foreign_involvement) Muslim Afghanistan enemies of the Soviet Union, and non-Afghan [veterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Arabs#Attitude_to_the_West) of the war (such as [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden)) returned home with their prestige, "experience, ideology, and weapons", and had considerable impact.[^cite_note-ForeignAffairsNovember2005-224]

Although it is a strong opponent of Israel's existence, [Hamas](13913.md), officially founded in 1987, traces its origins back to institutions and clerics which were supported by Israel in the 1970s and 1980s. Israel tolerated and supported Islamist movements in Gaza, with figures like [Ahmed Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Ahmed_Yassin), as Israel perceived them preferable to the secular and then more powerful [al-Fatah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fatah) with the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLO).[^cite_note-wsj-24-1-09-225][^cite_note-226]

Egyptian President [Anwar Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Sadat) – whose policies included opening Egypt to Western investment (*[infitah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infitah)*); transferring Egypt's allegiance from the Soviet Union to the United States; and [making peace with Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Israel_peace_treaty)—released Islamists from prison and welcomed home exiles in tacit exchange for political support in his struggle against leftists. His "encouraging of the emergence of the Islamist movement" was said to have been "imitated by many other Muslim leaders in the years that followed."[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228] This "gentlemen's agreement" between Sadat and Islamists broke down in 1975 but not before Islamists came to completely dominate university student unions. Sadat was later assassinated and a [formidable insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Egypt) was formed in Egypt in the 1990s. The French government has also been reported to have promoted Islamist preachers "in the hope of channeling Muslim energies into zones of piety and charity."[^cite_note-Berman-223]

Western support for Islamist groups during the [War on terror](13425800.md) often arose from geopolitical expediency, where supporting specific, seemingly aligned Islamist militants against common enemies (such as in Iraq, Libya and Syria) sometimes resulted in unintended, long-term strengthening of extremist threats. This strategy was sometimes driven by alliances with Gulf Arab nations supporting Wahhabi ideologies. [Operation Timber Sycamore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber_Sycamore) was a classified CIA-led program to arm and train [Syrian rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_rebels) aiming to unseat President [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md). While aiming to support "moderate" opposition, the program suffered from poor oversight, resulting in weapons and training flowing to extremist groups, including Al-Qaeda affiliates and ISIL.[^cite_note-229]

### Early history

The end of the 19th century saw the dismemberment of most of the Muslim [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) by non-Muslim European colonial powers,[^cite_note-239] despite the empire's spending massive sums on Western civilian and military technology to try to modernize and compete with the encroaching European powers. In the process the Ottomans went deep into debt to these powers.

Preaching Islamic alternatives to this humiliating decline were Jamal ad-din [al-Afghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Afghani) (1837–97), [Muhammad Abduh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Abduh) (1849–1905) and [Rashid Rida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Rida) (1865–1935).[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244] Abduh's student Rida is widely regarded as one of the "ideological forefathers" of contemporary Islamist movement,[^cite_note-245] and along with early Salafiyya [Hassan al-Banna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_al-Banna), and [Mustafa al-Siba'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_al-Siba'i), preached that a truly Islamic society would follow sharia law, reject [taqlid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taqlid), (the blind imitation of earlier authorities),[^cite_note-246] restore the [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate).[^cite_note-ORFPI1994\:33-247]

#### Sayyid Rashid Rida

See also: [Islamic Political Doctrines of Rashid Rida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Rida#Islamic_Political_Theory) and [The Caliphate or the Supreme Imamate (book)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Caliphate_or_the_Supreme_Imamate_\(book\))



[Sayyid Muhammad Rashid Rida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Rida) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): سيد رشيد رضا; 23 September 1865 – 22 August 1935).

Syrian-Egyptian Islamic cleric Muhammad Rashid Rida was one of the earliest 20th-century Sunni scholars to articulate the modern concept of an [Islamic state](2646438.md), influencing the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) and other Sunni Islamist movements. In his influential book *al-Khilafa aw al-Imama al-'Uzma* ("*The Caliphate or the Grand Imamate*"); Rida explained that the societies that properly obeyed *[Sharia](28840.md)* would be successful alternatives to the disorder and injustice of both [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) and [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism).[^cite_note-auto-248]

This society would be ruled by a Caliphate; the ruling [Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph) (*Khalifa*) governing through *[shura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shura)* (consultation), and applying [Sharia](28840.md) (Islamic laws) in partnership with Islamic juristic clergy, who would use *Ijtihad* to update *[fiqh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh)* by evaluating scripture.[^cite_note-249] With the *[Khilafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate)* providing true Islamic governance, Islamic civilization would be revitalised, the political and legal independence of the Muslim *umma* (community of Muslim believers) would be restored, and the heretical influences of Sufism would be cleansed from Islam.[^cite_note-250] This doctrine would become the blueprint of future Islamist movements.[^cite_note-251] Following Jamal al-Din al-Afghani and Muhammad Abduh, Rashid Rida saw the Crusades as a period when Europeans saw a true civilization in the Islamic world which led Christian Europe to Reformation and progress[^cite_note-252].

#### Muhammad Iqbal

Main article: [Muhammad Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Iqbal)

See also: [Two-nation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-nation_theory)

[Muhammad Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Iqbal) was a philosopher, poet and politician[^cite_note-aml.org.pk-253] in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj),[^cite_note-aml.org.pk-253][^cite_note-Iqbal_Academy_Pakistan-254] widely regarded as having inspired the [Islamic Nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-Nation_Theory) and [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement) in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India).[^cite_note-aml.org.pk-253][^cite_note-goethezeitportal-255][^cite_note-rahnemaa01-256]

Iqbal expressed fears of [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) and secular [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) weakening the spiritual foundations of Islam and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) society, and of India's [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu)-majority population crowding out Muslim heritage, culture and political influence. In 1930, Iqbal outlined a vision of an independent state for Muslim-majority provinces in northwestern India which inspired the [Pakistan movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_movement).

He also promoted [pan-Islamic unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamism) in his travels to Egypt, Afghanistan, [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) and Syria.

His ideas later influenced many [reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformism) Islamists, e.g., [Muhammad Asad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Asad), [Sayyid Abul Ala Maududi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Abul_Ala_Maududi) and [Ali Shariati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Shariati).

#### Sayyid Abul Ala Maududi

Main article: [Abul Ala Maududi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Ala_Maududi)

See also: [Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami)

[Sayyid Abul Ala Maududi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Abul_Ala_Maududi)[^cite_note-autogenerated5-257][^cite_note-bonney1-258] was an important early twentieth-century figure in the Islamic revival in India, and then after independence from Britain, in Pakistan. Maududi was an Islamist ideologue and Hanafi Sunni scholar active in [Hyderabad Deccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad_State) and later in [Pakistan](23235.md). Maududi was born to a clerical family and got his early education at home. At the age of eleven, he was admitted to a public school in [Aurangabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangabad). In 1919, he joined the [Khilafat Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khilafat_Movement) and got closer to the scholars of [Deoband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deobandi_movement).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERahnema2005100-259] He commenced the *[Dars-i Nizami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dars-i_Nizami)* education under supervision of Deobandi seminary at the Fatihpuri mosque in Delhi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERahnema2005101-70] Trained as a lawyer he worked as a journalist, and gained a wide audience with his books (translated into many languages) which placed Islam in a modern context. His writings had a profound impact on [Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb). Maududi also founded the [Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami_Pakistan) party in 1941 and remained its leader until 1972.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERahnema2005104–110-260]

In 1925, he wrote a book on Jihad, [*al-Jihad fil-Islam*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jihad_fil_Islam) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الجهاد في الاسلام), that can be regarded as his first contribution to Islamism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERahnema2005102-261] Maududi believed that Muslim society could not be Islamic without Sharia (influencing Qutb and Khomeini), and the establishment of an Islamic state to enforce it.[^cite_note-262] The state would be based on the principles of: *[tawhid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawhid)* (unity of God), *[risala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risalah_\(fiqh\))* (prophethood) and *[khilafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khilafa)* (caliphate).[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266] Maududi was uninterested in violent revolution or populist policies such as those of the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md), but sought gradual change in the hearts and minds of individuals from the top of society downward through an educational process or *da'wah*.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268] Maududi believed that Islam was all-encompassing: "Everything in the universe is 'Muslim' for it obeys God by submission to His laws."[^cite_note-269] "The man who denies God is called [Kafir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafir) (concealer) because he conceals by his disbelief what is inherent in his nature and embalmed in his own soul."[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-towards-ICNA-1986-271]

#### Muslim Brotherhood

Main article: [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md)



[Hasan al-Banna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_al-Banna)

Roughly contemporaneous with Maududi was the founding of the Muslim Brotherhood in Ismailiyah, Egypt in 1928 by [Hassan al Banna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_al_Banna). His was arguably the first, largest and most influential modern Islamic political/religious organization. Under the motto "the Qur'an is our constitution",[^cite_note-272] it sought Islamic revival through preaching and also by providing basic community services including schools, mosques, and workshops. Like Maududi, Al Banna believed in the necessity of government rule based on Shariah law implemented gradually and by persuasion, and of eliminating all Western imperialist influence in the Muslim world.[^cite_note-273]

Some elements of the Brotherhood did engage in violence, assassinating Egypt's premier [Mahmoud Fahmy El Nokrashy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_El_Nokrashy_Pasha) in 1948. MB founder [Al-Banna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_al_Banna) was assassinated in retaliation three months later.[^cite_note-274] The Brotherhood has suffered periodic repression in Egypt and has been banned several times, in 1948 and several years later following confrontations with Egyptian president [Gamal Abdul Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdul_Nasser), who jailed thousands of members for several years.

The Brotherhood expanded to many other countries, particularly in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world). In Egypt, despite periodic repression—for many years it was described as "semi-legal"[^cite_note-275]—it was the only opposition group in Egypt able to field candidates during elections.[^cite_note-multiref1-276] In the [2011–12 Egyptian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Egyptian_parliamentary_election), the political parties identified as "Islamist" (the Brotherhood's [Freedom and Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_and_Justice_Party_\(Egypt\)), Salafi [Al-Nour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nour_Party) and liberal Islamist [Al-Wasat Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Wasat_Party)) won 75% of the total seats.[^cite_note-277] [Mohamed Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Morsi), the candidate of the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md)'s party, was the first democratically elected president of Egypt. However, he was deposed during the [2013 Egyptian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Egyptian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), after mass protests against what were perceived as undemocratic moves by him. Today, the Muslim Brotherhood is designated as a [terrorist organization](63578.md) by [Bahrain](18933277.md), Russia, [Syria](7515849.md), [Egypt](8087628.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).

#### Sayyid Qutb (1906–1966)

Main article: [Milestones (book)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_\(book\))

See also: [Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb) and [Qutbism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutbism)



[Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb)

Qutb, a leading member of the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) movement, is considered by some (Fawaz A. Gerges) to be "the founding father and leading theoretician" of modern jihadists, such as [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden).[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280] He was executed for allegedly participating in a presidential assassination plot in 1966.

Maududi's political ideas influenced Sayyid Qutb. Like Maududi, he believed Sharia was crucial to Islam, so the restoration of its full enforcement was vital to the world. Since Sharia had not been fully enforced for centuries, Islam had "been extinct for a few centuries".[^cite_note-281] Qutb preached that Muslims must engage in a two-pronged attack of converting individuals through [preaching Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawah) peacefully but also using "physical power and jihad".[^cite_note-282] Force was necessary because "those who have usurped the authority of God" would not give up their power through friendly persuasion.[^cite_note-283] Like Khomeini, whom he influenced he believed the West was engaged in a vicious centuries long war against Islam.[^cite_note-284]

### Six-Day War (1967)

Main article: [Six-Day War](29328.md)

The defeat of the armies of several Arab states by [Israel](9282173.md) during the [Six-Day War](29328.md) marked a significant moment in the Arab world. The loss, coupled with economic stagnation in these countries, was attributed by some to the secular [Arab nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nationalism) of the ruling regimes. This period saw a decline in the popularity and credibility of secular, socialist, and nationalist ideologies, such as [Ba'athism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'athism), [Arab socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_socialism), and Arab nationalism. In contrast, various Islamist movements, both democratic and anti-democratic, inspired by figures like [Maududi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maududi) and [Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb), began to gain influence.[^cite_note-Mayer1-285]

### Iranian Revolution (1978–1979)

Main article: [Iranian Revolution](347268.md)

See also: [Consolidation of the Iranian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidation_of_the_Iranian_Revolution) and [Guardianship of the Islamic Jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardianship_of_the_Islamic_Jurists)

The first modern "Islamist state" (with the possible exception of Zia's Pakistan)[^cite_note-286] was established among the [Shia](26961.md) of Iran. In a major shock to the rest of the world, Muslim and non-Muslim, a revolution led by [Ayatollah](63899.md) [Ruhollah Khomeini](26232.md) overthrew the secular, oil-rich, well-armed, pro-American monarchy of Shah [Muhammad Reza Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Reza_Pahlavi). The revolution was an "indisputable sea change";[^cite_note-Parvaz-shook-2014-287] Islamism had been a topic of limited impact and interest before 1979, but after the revolution, "nobody within the Muslim world or outside it" remained unaware of militant Islam.[^cite_note-Kepel,_Jihad,_2002,_p._106-167]

Enthusiasm for the Iranian revolution in the Muslim world could be intense;[^cite_note-289] and there were many reasons for optimism among Islamists outside Iran. Khomeini was implementing Islamic law.[^cite_note-Ataie-LSE-2021-290] He was interested in Pan-Islamic (and pan-Islamist) unity and made efforts to "bridge the gap" between Shiites and Sunnis, declaring "it permissible for Shiites to pray behind Sunni imams",[^cite_note-291] and forbidding Shiites from "criticizing the Caliphs who preceded [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali)" (revered by Sunnis but not Shia).[^cite_note-292] The Islamic Republic also downplayed Shia rituals (such as the [Day of Ashura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Ashura)), and shrines [^cite_note-295] Before the Revolution, Khomeini acolytes (such as today's [Supreme Leader of Iran](36814344.md), [Ali Khamenei](385653.md)), translated and championed the works of the Muslim Brotherhood jihadist theorist, [Sayyid Qutb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Qutb),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhalaji2009-183] and other Sunni Islamists/revivalists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhalaji2009-183]

This campaign did not survive his death however. As previously submissive Shia (usually minorities) became more assertive, Sunnis saw mostly "Shia mischief" and a challenge to Sunni dominance.[^cite_note-296] "What followed was a Sunni-versus-Shia contest for dominance, and it grew intense."[^cite_note-Nasr,_revival,_148-9-297] Animosity between the two sects in Iran and its neighbors is systemic as of 2014,[^cite_note-298] with thousands killed from sectarian fighting in Iraq and Pakistan.[^cite_note-CFR-Tensions-1979-2021-299] Also tarnishing the revolution's image have been "purges, executions, and atrocities",[^cite_note-300] and periodic and increasingly widespread [domestic unrest and protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsa_Amini_protests) by young Iranians.

Among the "most important by-products of the Iranian revolution" (according to Mehrzad Boroujerdi as of 2014) include "the emergence of [Hezbollah](13919.md) in Lebanon, the moral boost provided to Shia forces in Iraq, the regional cold war against Saudi Arabia and Israel, lending an Islamic flavour to the anti-imperialist, anti-American sentiment in the Middle East, and inadvertently widening the Sunni-Shia cleavage".[^cite_note-Parvaz-shook-2014-287] The Islamic Republic has also maintained its hold on power in Iran in spite of [US economic sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States-Iran_relations), and has created or assisted like-minded Shia terrorist groups in Iraq ([SCIRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCIRI))[^cite_note-301] and Lebanon (Hezbollah)[^cite_note-302] (two Muslim countries that also have a large percentage of Shiites).

The campaign to overthrow the shah led by Khomeini had had a strong class flavor (Khomeini preached that the shah was widening the gap between rich and poor; condemning the working class to a life of poverty, misery, and drudgery, etc.);[^cite_note-KEA1993\:30-154] and the "pro-rural and pro-poor"[^cite_note-Isfahani-economy-2019-303] approach has led to almost universal access to electricity and clean water,[^cite_note-Isfahani-40-Brookings-304] but critics of the regime complain of promises made and not kept: the "sons of the revolution's leaders and the business class that decides to work within the rules of the regime ... flaunt their wealth, driving luxury sportscars around Tehran, posting Instagram pictures of their ski trips and beach trips around the world, all while the poor and the middle class are struggling to survive or maintain the appearance of a dignified life" (according to Shadi Mokhtari).[^cite_note-Priborkin-40years-2019-305] One commitment made (to his followers if not the Iranian public) that has been kept is [Guardianship by the Islamic jurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardianship_of_the_Islamic_Jurist). But Rather than strengthening Islam and eliminating secular values and practices, the "regime has ruined the Iranian people's belief in religion" ("anonymous expert").[^cite_note-Priborkin-40years-2019-305]

### Grand Mosque seizure (1979)

Further information: [Grand Mosque seizure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mosque_seizure)

The strength of the Islamist movement was manifest in an event which might have seemed sure to turn Muslim public opinion against [fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamentalism), but did just the opposite. In 1979 the [Grand Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid_al-Haram) in [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) Saudi Arabia was seized by an armed fundamentalist group and held for over a week. Scores were killed, including many pilgrim bystanders[^cite_note-306] in a gross violation of one of the most holy sites in Islam (and one where arms and violence are strictly forbidden).[^cite_note-307][^cite_note-308]

Instead of prompting a backlash against the movement that inspired the attackers, however, Saudi Arabia, already very conservative, responded by shoring up its fundamentalist credentials with even more Islamic restrictions. Crackdowns followed on everything from shopkeepers who did not close for prayer and newspapers that published pictures of women, to the selling of dolls, teddy bears (images of animate objects are considered [haraam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haraam)), and dog food (dogs are considered unclean).[^cite_note-309]

In other Muslim countries, blame for and wrath against the seizure was directed not against fundamentalists, but against Islamic fundamentalism's foremost geopolitical enemy—the United States. Ayatollah [Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khomeini) sparked attacks on American embassies when he announced: "It is not beyond guessing that this is the work of criminal [American imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_imperialism) and international Zionism", despite the fact that the object of the fundamentalists' revolt was the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, America's major ally in the region. Anti-American demonstrations followed in the Philippines, Turkey, Bangladesh, India, the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE), Pakistan, and Kuwait. The US Embassy in Libya was burned by protesters chanting pro-Khomeini slogans and the embassy in [Islamabad](51153.md), Pakistan was burned to the ground.[^cite_note-310]

### Pakistan's Islamization (1979)

In 1979, after the coup by Zia al-Haq, the leader brought in Hudud Ordinances. Some of these laws continue to exist in Pakistan to this day.

### Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (1979–1989)



Afghan Mujahideen of [Hezb-i Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezb-i_Islami_Khalis), 1986

In 1979, the [Soviet Union deployed its 40th Army into Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), attempting to suppress an Islamic rebellion against an allied Marxist regime in the [Afghan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(1978%E2%80%93present\)). The conflict, pitting indigenous impoverished Muslims ([mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen)) against an anti-religious superpower, galvanized thousands of Muslims around the world to send aid and sometimes to go themselves to fight for their faith. Leading this pan-Islamic effort was Palestinian ['alim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'alim) [Abdullah Yusuf Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Yusuf_Azzam#Life_in_Pakistan_and_Afghanistan). While the military effectiveness of these "[Afghan Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Arabs)" was marginal, an estimated 16,000[^cite_note-Atkins-311] to 35,000 Muslim volunteers[^cite_note-Commins-174-312] came from around the world to fight in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-Commins-174-312][^cite_note-rashid-129-313]

When the Soviet Union abandoned the Marxist Najibullah regime and withdrew from Afghanistan in 1989 (the regime finally fell in 1992), the victory was seen by many Muslims as the triumph of Islamic faith over superior military power and technology that could be duplicated elsewhere.

> The jihadists gained legitimacy and prestige from their triumph both within the militant community and among ordinary Muslims, as well as the confidence to carry their jihad to other countries where they believed Muslims required assistance.[^cite_note-for-aff-bergen-314]

The collapse of the Soviet Union itself, in 1991, was seen by many Islamists, including Bin Laden, as the defeat of a superpower at the hands of Islam. Concerning the \$6 billion in aid given by the US and Pakistan's military training and intelligence support to the mujahideen,[^cite_note-315] bin Laden wrote: "\[T]he US has no mentionable role" in "the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Soviet_Union)... rather the credit goes to [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah) and the *[mujahidin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahidin)*" of Afghanistan.[^cite_note-316]

### Persian Gulf War (1990–1991)

See also: [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War)

Another factor in the early 1990s that worked to radicalize the Islamist movement was the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), which brought several hundred thousand US and allied non-Muslim military personnel to Saudi Arabian soil to put an end to [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein)'s [occupation of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Kuwait). Prior to 1990 Saudi Arabia played an important role in restraining the many Islamist groups that received its aid. But when Saddam, secularist and [Ba'athist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'athism) dictator of neighboring Iraq, attacked Kuwait (his enemy in the war), western troops came to protect the Saudi monarchy. Islamists accused the Saudi regime of being a puppet of the west.

These attacks resonated with conservative Muslims and the problem did not go away with Saddam's defeat either, since American troops remained stationed in the kingdom, and a de facto cooperation with the Palestinian-Israeli peace process developed. Saudi Arabia attempted to compensate for its loss of prestige among these groups by repressing those domestic Islamists who attacked it (bin Laden being a prime example), and increasing aid to Islamic groups (Islamist madrassas around the world and even aiding some violent Islamist groups) that did not, but its pre-war influence on behalf of moderation was greatly reduced.[^cite_note-317] One result of this was a campaign of attacks on government officials and tourists in [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Gama'a_al-Islamiyya), a bloody civil war in [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Algerian_massacres_of_the_1990s) and [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden)'s terror attacks climaxing in the [9/11 attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks).[^cite_note-318]

### Social and cultural triumph in the 2000s and 2010s

By the beginning of the twenty first century, "the word secular, a label proudly worn" in the 1960s and '70s was "shunned" and "used to besmirch" political foes in Egypt and the rest of the Muslim world.[^cite_note-murphy-161-90] Islamists surpassed the small secular opposition parties in terms of "doggedness, courage," "risk-taking" or "organizational skills".[^cite_note-murphy-160-88] As of 2002,

> In the Middle East and Pakistan, religious discourse dominates societies, the airwaves, and thinking about the world. Radical mosques have proliferated throughout Egypt. Book stores are dominated by works with religious themes ... The demand for sharia, the belief that their governments are [unfaithful to Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takfir) and that Islam is the answer to all problems, and the certainty that the West has declared [war on Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_Islam_controversy); these are the themes that dominate public discussion. Islamists may not control parliaments or government palaces, but they have occupied the popular imagination.[^cite_note-319]

Opinion polls in a variety of Islamic countries showed that significant majorities opposed groups like [ISIS](9087364.md), but also wanted religion to play a greater role in public life.[^cite_note-320]

### "Post-Islamism"

See also: [Post-Islamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Islamism)

By 2020, approximately 40 years after the Islamic overthrow of the Shah of Iran and the seizure of the Grand Mosque by extremists, a number of observers ([Olivier Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Roy_\(professor\)), Mustafa Akyol, Nader Hashemi) detected a decline in the vigor and popularity of Islamism. Islamism had been an idealized/utopian concept to compare with the grim reality of the status quo, but in more than four decades it had failed to establish a "concrete and viable blueprint for society" despite repeated efforts (Olivier Roy);[^cite_note-Roy-2004-321] and instead had left a less than inspiring track record of its impact on the world (Nader Hashemi).[^cite_note-Hashemi-40-2021-322] Consequently, in addition to the trend towards moderation by Islamist or formerly Islamist parties (such as [PKS of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosperous_Justice_Party), [AKP of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_\(Turkey\)), and [PAS of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Islamic_Party_\(PAS\))) mentioned above, there has been a social/religious and sometimes political backlash against Islamist rule in countries like Turkey, Iran, and Sudan (Mustafa Akyol).[^cite_note-Akyol-ruining-323]

Writing in 2020, Mustafa Akyol argues there has been a strong reaction by many Muslims against political Islam, including a weakening of religious faith—the very thing Islamism was intended to strengthen. He suggests this backlash against Islamism among Muslim youth has come from all the "terrible things" that have happened in the Arab world in the twenty first century "in the name of Islam"—such as the "sectarian civil wars in [Syria](30741795.md), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Iraq_\(2013%E2%80%932017\)) and [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\))".[^cite_note-Akyol-ruining-323]

Polls taken by [Arab Barometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Barometer) in six Arab countries – Algeria, Egypt, Tunisia, Jordan, Iraq and Libya – found "Arabs are losing faith in religious parties and leaders." In 2018–19, in all six countries, fewer than 20% of those asked whether they trusted Islamist parties answered in the affirmative. That percentage had fallen (in all six countries) from when the same question was asked in 2012–14. Mosque attendance also declined more than 10 points on average, and the share of those Arabs describing themselves as "not religious" went from 8% in 2013 to 13% in 2018–19.[^cite_note-324][^cite_note-Akyol-ruining-323] In Syria, Sham al-Ali reports "rising apostasy among Syrian youths".[^cite_note-325][^cite_note-Akyol-ruining-323]

Writing in 2021, Nader Hashemi notes that in Iraq, Sudan, Tunisia, Egypt, Gaza, Jordan and other places were Islamist parties have come to power or campaigned to, "one general theme stands. The popular prestige of political Islam has been tarnished by its experience with state power."[^cite_note-Zakaria-10-years-29-4-21-326][^cite_note-Hashemi-40-2021-322] In Iran, hardline Ayatollah Mohammad-Taqi Mesbah Yazdi has complained, "Iranians are evading religious teachings and turning to secularism."[^cite_note-327] Even Islamist terrorism was in decline and tended "to be local" rather than pan-Islamic. As of 2021, Al-Qaeda consisted of "a bunch of militias" with no effective central command (Fareed Zakaria).[^cite_note-Zakaria-10-years-29-4-21-326]

An Islamist protester in [London](17867.md) on 6 February 2006 carries a sign reading "Freedom go to hell"

Islamism, or elements of Islamism, have been criticized on numerous grounds, including repression of free expression and individual rights, rigidity, hypocrisy, [anti-semitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_Islam),[^cite_note-328] misinterpreting the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) and [Sunnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah), lack of true understanding of and innovations to Islam ([bid'ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bid'ah)) – notwithstanding proclaimed opposition to any such innovation by Islamists.
