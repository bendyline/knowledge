**António José Martins Seguro**[^cite_note-1] (born 11 March 1962) is a Portuguese politician who has been the [president of Portugal](516093.md) since 2026, having won the [2026 Portuguese presidential election](68734153.md). A member of the [Socialist Party](403237.md) (PS), Seguro previously served as [secretary-general of the PS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_Socialist_Party_\(Portugal\)) from 2011 to 2014, during which time he was also the [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Portugal\)).

## Early life and education



António José Seguro as a child, in the 1960s

Seguro was born on 11 March 1962 in [Penamacor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penamacor), the third and youngest son of Domingos Sanches Seguro (1925–2017) and Maria do Céu Martins (1927–2012). The family comprised mostly rural workers from the [Beira Baixa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beira_Baixa_Province) region; Seguro's father, Domingos, ran a [newsagent's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsagent's_shop).[^cite_note-\:2-2]

His early studies were undertaken in Penamacor in the Preparatory School Ribeiro Sanches and in the Nossa Senhora do Incenso Day School, before he went to [Castelo Branco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelo_Branco,_Portugal) to finish high school in the Industrial and Commercial School.[^cite_note-\:3-3] During this time, he was arrested before the [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution), while still a minor.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

In his youth, Seguro ran a local newspaper, *A Verdade de Penamacor*, with his cousin [Jorge Seguro Sanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Seguro_Sanches). In 1982, during a visit of President [António Ramalho Eanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Ramalho_Eanes) to Penamacor, Seguro diverged First Lady [Manuela Eanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuela_Ramalho_Eanes) from the presidential entourage, interviewing her.[^cite_note-\:2-2]

He entered politics at a very young age and became a member of the Portuguese [Socialist Party](403237.md) (PS) in February 1981, when he was 18 years old. He attended the 1st cycle program in business organization and management at the [ISCTE – Lisbon University Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISCTE_%E2%80%93_Lisbon_University_Institute), but he did not graduate. Seguro has a degree in [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) awarded later by the [Autonomous University of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidade_Aut%C3%B3noma_de_Lisboa).[^cite_note-\:3-3]

## Career

### Early activism

Seguro became involved in political activities from a very young age, always linked to the PS. As a member of the [Socialist Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Youth_\(Portugal\)) (JS), he was elected as president of the National Youth Council (CNJ) in 1985, an office he held until 1990, and was in the following year involved in [Mário Soares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Soares)' [1986 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Portuguese_presidential_election). As president of the CNJ, we was responsible for several negotiations with the government of [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%ADbal_Cavaco_Silva) that introduced the youth card and the network of youth hostels.[^cite_note-\:1-4] He also later became chairman of the Youth Forum of the European Communities.

In 1986, after a temporary short stay in the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md) during the [4th Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Legislature_of_the_Third_Portuguese_Republic),[^cite_note-\:4-5] Seguro was a candidate for Chair of the [Castelo Branco Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelo_Branco_District) of the PS against the incumbent [José Sócrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_S%C3%B3crates), who was seeking reelection to a second two-years term. Sócrates ended up beating Seguro, winning 56 delegates against Seguro's 20.[^cite_note-6]

In 1990, Seguro was elected as Secretary.general of the Socialist Youth with 85% of the delegates, succeeding José Apolinário. At the time, the JS was facing financial and mobilization issues. In his first campaign as leader of the JS, in the [1991 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Portuguese_legislative_election), he organized a groundbreaking campaign, distributing 250,000 [condoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condom).[^cite_note-\:1-4] Despite the PS and [Jorge Sampaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Sampaio)'s defeat, Seguro ended up being elected for the first time to Parliament.[^cite_note-\:0-7] As Secretary-general of the JS, Seguro fought in parliament for the end to the [compulsory military service](5735.md) and against the [Indonesian occupation of East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_occupation_of_East_Timor), which led to his arrest in the [Expo '92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'92) in Seville for protesting in front of the Indonesian Pavillion.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

During the election night of the [1991 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Portuguese_legislative_election), Seguro spent the night in [António Guterres](471806.md)' house, being one of the first people to support Guterres' bid for the leadership of the PS. After Guterres was elected as Secretary-general of the party in 1992, Seguro joined his secretariat, becoming a part of the inner circle of the leadership of the Party.[^cite_note-8]

After being re-elected in December 1991 in a landslide, despite opposition from two lists headed by Luís Miguel Duarte and Daniel Adrião, Seguro left the leadership of the Socialist Youth in March 1994, being succeeded by [Sérgio Sousa Pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9rgio_Sousa_Pinto).[^cite_note-9]

### Secretary of state, MEP and minister

In the lead up to the [1995 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Portuguese_legislative_election), Seguro was one of the people tasked with organizing Guterres' campaign, alongside [Jorge Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Coelho_\(politician\)) as the national campaign director. Seguro organized the national caravan that was held following the *Estados Gerais*, inspired by [Clinton](3356.md)-[Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore) caravan in the [1992 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election). With the purpose of replacing the party's aesthetic in favour of a more national inspired identity for the campaign, Seguro conceived for this caravan several graphic elements that became strongly associated with António Guterres and the Socialist Party, such as replacing the party flags with the [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Portugal) and using the song [Conquest of Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Paradise_\(song\)) to replace the party's anthem.[^cite_note-10]



Official portrait as an MEP, 1999

With Seguro as the main candidate from [Guarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarda_\(Assembly_of_the_Republic_constituency\)), the PS won the legislative elections, leaving Guterres to form a [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XIII_Constitutional_Government_of_Portugal). Seguro then joined the government for the first time as Secretary of State for Youth Affairs and later, in 1997, he became Secretary of State Assistant to the Prime Minister.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

During this time, Seguro rose in the ranks of the PS, becoming the coordinator of the Standing Committee of PS and being the party's coordinator for the [1997 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Portuguese_local_elections), being elected as President of the Municipal Assembly of Penamacor in that same election.[^cite_note-11]

Seguro left the government to run in the [1999 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_European_Parliament_election_in_Portugal), being the second name in a list led by former President [Mário Soares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Soares).[^cite_note-\:1-4] Seguro served as an MEP between July 1999 and July 2001, being an effective member of the Committee on Constitutional Affairs (in these functions he was co-author of the Report on the [Treaty of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nice) and the Future of the European Union),[^cite_note-12] and was also a substitute for the Commission for Employment and Social Affairs. He was also president of the Delegation for Relations with Central America and Mexico, vice president of the [Socialist Group in the European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance_of_Socialists_and_Democrats) and president of the Portuguese Socialist delegation.[^cite_note-13]

Seguro resigned as an MEP in July 2001 to serve as [Minister in the Cabinet of the Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio_\(Portugal\)), again under António Guterres.[^cite_note-\:0-7] After Guterres' resignation and the party's narrow defeat in the [2002 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Portuguese_legislative_election), he returned to the Assembly of the Republic, serving as the Socialist [Parliamentary leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_leader) from 2004 to 2005. He accumulated this position with the membership in the Municipal Assembly of [Gouveia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouveia,_Portugal) after being elected in the [2001 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Portuguese_local_elections).[^cite_note-14]

After [Ferro Rodrigues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Ferro_Rodrigues)' resignation as [Secretary-general of PS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_Socialist_Party_\(Portugal\)) in 2004, Seguro was considered as a potential candidate for the [leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Portuguese_Socialist_Party_leadership_election), but he was convinced by [Jorge Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Coelho_\(politician\)) not to run since it was "not his time".[^cite_note-\:1-4] From then on, he became a [backbencher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backbencher), only taking slightly relevant positions in the leadership of the Parliamentary committees of Education and Science, and later of Economic Affairs, Innovation and Energy. He also opposed the leadership of [José Sócrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_S%C3%B3crates) in several moments, breaking party discipline to vote against the party finances law, oppose [consumption tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_tax) increases and defending a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratification_of_the_Treaty_of_Lisbon) on the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon).[^cite_note-\:1-4]

### Secretary-general of the Socialist Party



Seguro on the election night of the [2014 European Parliament election in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Parliament_election_in_Portugal)

After Prime Minister José Sócrates resigned as PS General Secretary on the election night of [5 June 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Portuguese_legislative_election), having lost the general election by a margin higher than expected, Seguro was elected leader of the party on [23 July 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2011_Portuguese_Socialist_Party_leadership_election), winning 68% of the vote against his challenger, [Francisco Assis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Assis), who got 32%.[^cite_note-economico-15]

As secretary-general, Seguro decided to abstain in the 2012 State Budget proposed by the [Passos Coelho government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XIX_Constitutional_Government_of_Portugal), citing his decision as a "violent, but constructive abstention",[^cite_note-16] a decision that ended up attracting criticism from inside the PS. Under Seguro's leadership, the Socialist Party managed to achieve one of its best results ever in the [2013 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Portuguese_local_elections) and won the [2014 European Parliament election in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Parliament_election_in_Portugal) with [Francisco Assis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Assis) as the main candidate, although by a narrow margin. The results of the 2014 elections were considered narrow and insufficient, which motivated the Mayor of Lisbon [António Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Costa) to defy Seguro and run for the party leadership.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

At the [first primaries open to party supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Portuguese_Socialist_Party_prime_ministerial_primary), Costa defeated Seguro by a landslide. Seguro resigned from the leadership the same day, leaving Costa as Secretary-general.[^cite_note-17] Seguro then retired to private life. Having successfully defended his Master thesis, he started teaching in the Department of International Relations of the Autonomous University of Lisbon.[^cite_note-18]

In October 2024, then PS leader [Pedro Nuno Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Nuno_Santos) mentioned Seguro as a potential candidate for president from the Socialist Party in the [2026 presidential election](68734153.md).[^cite_note-19] In November 2024, after being mostly out of the spotlight since 2014, Seguro gave an interview to [CNN Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_Portugal) as he was starting a role as a [political commentator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pundit) on the channel, stating that he was interested in running for president.[^cite_note-20] Seguro then positioned himself as the main candidate from the party in opposition to others like [Mário Centeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Centeno), [António Vitorino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Vitorino) and [Augusto Santos Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Santos_Silva).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] He also founded the movement UPortugal, with the intention of promoting a greater participation from the citizens and fighting [misinformation](2203174.md).[^cite_note-23] In June 2025, he announced he was running for president, stating that "the country needs change and hope in a better life", defining his candidacy as a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) alternative to the other [conservative](6675.md) candidates, such as [Luís Marques Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Marques_Mendes) and [Henrique Gouveia e Melo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrique_Gouveia_e_Melo).[^cite_note-24]



Seguro delivering the victory speech on the night of the first round of the 2026 election

Seguro launched his campaign on 15 June 2025, in [Caldas da Rainha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldas_da_Rainha), with the presence of major PS personalities like [Francisco Assis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Assis), [Alberto Martins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Martins), [Maria de Belém Roseira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_de_Bel%C3%A9m_Roseira), [João Soares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Soares_\(politician\)) and Álvaro Beleza,[^cite_note-25] saying that, after leaving politics at a time when he could divide the party, he was now returning with the intention to unite the country.[^cite_note-26] In the coming weeks, Seguro gained the support of most of the establishment of the PS, receiving the endorsement of more than half of the party federation chairs[^cite_note-27] and more than 100 incumbent mayors.[^cite_note-28] Despite that, [José Luís Carneiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Lu%C3%ADs_Carneiro), the leader of PS, said that the party would only officially back any of the presidential candidates after the [12 October local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Portuguese_local_elections).[^cite_note-29]

Seguro received the official support of the Socialist Party on 19 October 2025, with the proposal for his support being written by the Secretary-general [José Luís Carneiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Lu%C3%ADs_Carneiro) and by the party's president [Carlos César](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_C%C3%A9sar).[^cite_note-30] Seguro chose scientist [Maria do Carmo Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_do_Carmo_Fonseca) as his national campaign chair,[^cite_note-31] MP [Paulo Lopes Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Lopes_Silva) as his campaign director,[^cite_note-32] and former minister [Guilherme d'Oliveira Martins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilherme_d'Oliveira_Martins) as campaign chair for the [Lisbon district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_district).[^cite_note-33] Seguro formalized his candidacy on 15 December 2025, delivering 10,000 signatures to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_\(Portugal\)).[^cite_note-34]

In the [first round](68734153.md#Results) of the 2026 presidential election on 18 January, no candidates secured a [majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority); Seguro won a [relative majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\)) of 31%, and [André Ventura](60708639.md) (leader of the [right-wing populist](8354382.md) party [Chega](60708659.md)) came second with 23.5%; the two most-voted candidates faced each other in a second round run-off on 8 February.[^cite_note-35] This was only the second time that a direct Portuguese presidential election was not won on the first ballot, as in the [1986 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Portuguese_presidential_election).[^cite_note-36] In his first-round victory speech, in [Caldas da Rainha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldas_da_Rainha) Cultural and Congress Centre, a hoarse Seguro underlined the non-partisan nature of his campaign and invited all "democrats, progressives, and humanists" to support him in the second round in order to "defeat extremism and those who sow hatred and division among the Portuguese", distancing himself from his opponent.[^cite_note-37] On the election night and the following day, Seguro received the endorsement of several important figures of the [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) to [right-wing](56522.md) political parties [PSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)), [IL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Initiative) and [CDS–PP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDS_%E2%80%93_People's_Party).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

Seguro won the second round by a landslide, beating André Ventura with 66.8% of the votes, being elected [President](516093.md).[^cite_note-40] He won the record of the highest number of votes cast for him in a presidential election, after receiving more than 3.5 million votes.[^cite_note-41] In his victory speech, he promised that things would not be kept the same and that he would be the president of "all, all, all of the Portuguese".[^cite_note-42]

## Presidency (2026–present)



Seguro taking the oath of office

António José Seguro took office on 9 March 2026, in the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md), having in attendance King [Felipe VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_VI) of Spain, president [João Lourenço](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Louren%C3%A7o) of Angola, president [Daniel Chapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Chapo) of Mozambique, president [José Ramos-Horta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Ramos-Horta) of Timor-Leste, president [José Maria Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Maria_Neves) of Cape Verde and president [Carlos Vila Nova](68450389.md) of São Tomé e Príncipe.[^cite_note-43]

On 2 April 2026, Seguro was present in the solemn ceremony for the 50th anniversary of the [1976 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Portugal), where he said that the country's problems do not come from the constitution itself, but from its non-compliance, amidst talks about a constitutional review done between [PSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)), [Chega](60708659.md) and [IL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Initiative).[^cite_note-44]

As President, Seguro brought back the Open Presidencies (*Presidências Abertas*) that had been started by [Mário Soares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Soares), with his first open presidency being in the areas impacted by [Storm Kristin](82263504.md) in [Leiria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiria_District), [Castelo Branco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelo_Branco_District), [Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbra_District) and [Santarém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santar%C3%A9m_District).[^cite_note-45]

On 3 May 2026, Seguro promulgated the revised Nationality Law, which doubles the standard naturalisation residency requirement from five to ten years (seven for EU and [CPLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Portuguese_Language_Countries) nationals). While signing the decree, he stated that a reinforced-value law of such importance "should also be based on greater consensus around its essential principles", and stressed that pending applications must not be adversely affected by the legislative change and that statutory naturalisation timelines must not be undermined by administrative delays of the state.[^cite_note-46] He did not promulgate a separate decree introducing loss of nationality as an accessory criminal penalty, which would be shut down by the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Portugal).[^cite_note-47]

Seguro's style as president has been described as different from the previous presidents, being more silent but focused on pointing to the problems and producing documents to register his concerns and orientations about the issues.[^cite_note-48]
