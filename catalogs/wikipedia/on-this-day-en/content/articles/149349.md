**Dortmund** (German: [\[ˈdɔʁtmʊnt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0e/De-Dortmund.ogg/De-Dortmund.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0e/De-Dortmund.ogg/De-Dortmund.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Westphalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalian_language): *Düörpm* [\[ˈdyːœɐ̯pm̩\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)) is the third-largest city in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), after [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) and [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf), and the [ninth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population) in [Germany](11867.md). With a population of 614,495 inhabitants,[^cite_note-3] it is the largest city (by area and population) of the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) as well as the largest city of [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia).[^cite_note-4] It lies on the [Emscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emscher) and [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_\(river\)) rivers ([tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributaries) of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine)) in the [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr) Metropolitan Region, the [second biggest metropolitan region by GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_EU_metropolitan_regions_by_GDP#2021_ranking_of_top_four_German_metropolitan_regions) in the European Union, and is the administrative, commercial, and cultural centre of the eastern Ruhr. Dortmund is the second-largest city in the [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) dialect area, after [Hamburg](13467.md).

Founded around 882,[^cite_note-WM-document-882-5] Dortmund became an [Imperial Free City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Free_City). Throughout the 13th to 14th centuries, it was the "chief city" of the Rhine, Westphalia, and the Netherlands Circle of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League). During the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), the city was destroyed and decreased in significance until the onset of [industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation). The city then became one of Germany's most important coal, steel, and beer centres. It was one of the most heavily bombed cities in Germany during [World War II](32927.md). The devastating bombing raids of 12 March 1945 destroyed 98% of the buildings in the inner city. Involving more than 1,110 aircraft, the raids were the largest targeting a single city during World War II.[^cite_note-backtonormandy.org-6] Today, around 30% of the city's buildings date from before the war.[^cite_note-Zensus_2011-7]

Since the collapse of its century-long steel and coal industries, the region has adapted and shifted to high-technology [biomedical technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_technology), [micro systems technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_systems_technology), and also services. Other key sectors include retail,[^cite_note-8] leisure and the visitor economy,[^cite_note-9] creative industries,[^cite_note-10] and logistics.[^cite_note-11] With [its central station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Hauptbahnhof) and [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Airport), the third-busiest in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), Dortmund is an important transport junction for the surrounding Ruhr area as well as the [Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux) countries. [Dortmund Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Port) is the largest canal port in Europe and has a connection to important seaports on the [North Sea](21179.md).

Dortmund is home to several institutions of higher education, including the [Technical University of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Dortmund), the [Dortmund University of Applied Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_University_of_Applied_Sciences_and_Arts), and the [International School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_of_Management,_Germany). The city's cultural institutions include the [Museum Ostwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Ostwall), [Museum of Art and Cultural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_f%C3%BCr_Kunst_und_Kulturgeschichte), and [German Football Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Museum), as well as theatres and music venues like the [Theater Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_Dortmund) or the [Dortmund Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Opera_House). Nearly half the municipal territory consists of waterways, woodland, agriculture, and green spaces with spacious parks such as [Westfalenpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenpark) and [Rombergpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rombergpark). This stands in a stark contrast with nearly a hundred years of extensive [coal mining](208413.md) and [steel milling](1143008.md) in the past, which created a rich *[Gründerzeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCnderzeit)* architectural heritage.[^cite_note-12] [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) is one of the most successful German football clubs.

## Etymology



"Seal of Dortmund, the city of Westphalia" [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Sigillum Tremonie Civitatis Westfalie*

Dortmund was first mentioned in the [Werden Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werden_Abbey), which was built between 880 and 884. The Latin entry reads: *In Throtmanni liber homo Arnold viii den nob solvit* ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *In Throtmanni zahlt uns der freie Mann Arnold 8 Pfennige*, and English: *In Throtmanni the free man Arnold pays us 8 pfennigs*).[^cite_note-Reimann1-13] According to this, there are a large number of different names, but they all go back to the same phoneme stem. Their respective use in the sources appears arbitrary and random.

Dortmund first appears in records around 890 under the name *Throtmanni*, later documented in 947 as *Throtmennia*, between 1033 and 1050 as *moneta Thrutminensis*, and in 1074 as *Drutmunne*.[^cite_note-niemeyer-14] From the 13th century on, the *Dortmunde* appeared for the first time, but it was not until a few centuries later that it became generally accepted.

In 1389, when the city had withstood a siege of 1,200 knights led by the [Archbishop of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne), it adopted a motto that is still upheld today by traditional societies: *So fast as Düörpm* ([High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_German_languages): 'as firm as Dortmund').

In the past, the city was called *Dortmond* in [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language), *Tremonia* in [Spanish](26825.md), and *Trémoigne* in [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French). However, these exonyms have fallen into disuse and the city is now internationally known by its German name of *Dortmund*. The common [abbreviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbreviation) for the name of the city is "DTM", the [IATA code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association_airport_code) for [Dortmund Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Airport).

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Dortmund).

### Early history



Historical view of Dortmund by Georg Braun and Franz Hogenberg (between 1572 and 1618)

The [Sigiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigiburg) was a [hillfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillfort) in the south of present-day Dortmund, overlooking the River [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_\(river\)) near its confluence with the River [Lenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenne). The ruins of the later [Hohensyburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syberg) castle now stand on the site of the Sigiburg. The hillfort is presumably of [Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxons) origin, but there is no archeological or documentary proof of this. During the [Saxon Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Wars), it was taken by the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) under [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) in 772, retaken by the Saxons (possibly under [Widukind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widukind)) in 774, and taken again and refortified by Charlemagne in 775. Archaeological evidence suggests the Sigiburg site was also occupied in the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) era.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The first time Dortmund was mentioned in official documents was around 882 as **Throtmanni** – In throtmanni liber homo arnold\[us] viii den\[arios] nob\[is] soluit \[solvit].[^cite_note-WM-document-882-5][^cite_note-17] In 1005 the "Ecclesiastical council" and in 1016 the "Imperial diet" met in Dortmund.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritannica1910-18]

### Middle Ages and early modern period



St. Marys and St. Reinolds in 1470

After it was destroyed by a fire, the Holy Roman Emperor [Frederick I (Barbarossa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I_\(Barbarossa\)) had the town rebuilt in 1152 and resided there (among other places) for two years. In 1267 St. Mary's Church, Dortmund, and three years later in 1270 [St. Reinold's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Reinold's_Church,_Dortmund) first mentioned. The combination of crossroad, market place, administrative centre – town hall, made Dortmund an important centre in Westphalia. It became an [Imperial Free City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Free_City) and one of the first cities in Europe with an official [Brewing right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewing_right) in 1293.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERing1995-19] Throughout the 13th to 14th centuries, it was the "chief city" of the Rhine, Westphalia, the Netherlands Circle of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League).



Old market [St. Reinolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Reinoldi_\(Dortmund\))

After 1320, the city appeared in writing as "Dorpmunde". In the years leading up to 1344, the English King, [Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III), even borrowed money from well-heeled Dortmund merchant families Berswordt and Klepping, offering the regal crown as security. In 1388, the [Count of Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Mark) joined forces with the Archbishop of [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) and issued declarations of a feud against the town. Following a major siege lasting 18 months, peace negotiations took place and Dortmund emerged victorious. In 1400 the seat of the first Vehmic court ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Freistuhl*) was in Dortmund, in a square between two [linden trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linden_tree), one of which was known as the *[Femelinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerichtslinde)*. With the growing influence of Cologne during the 15th century, the seat was moved to [Arnsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnsberg) in 1437. After Cologne was excluded after the [Anglo-Hanseatic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Hanseatic_War) (1470–74), Dortmund was made capital of the Rhine-Westphalian and Netherlands Circle. This favors the founding of one of the oldest schools in Europe in 1543 – [Stadtgymnasium Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtgymnasium_Dortmund?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERing1995-19] In [1661 an earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Germany) made the [Reinoldikirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinoldikirche) collapse.

### 18th, 19th and early 20th centuries



Pre-industrial Dortmund in 1804

With the *[Reichsdeputationshauptschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsdeputationshauptschluss)* resolution in 1803, Dortmund was added to the [Principality of Nassau-Orange-Fulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Nassau-Orange-Fulda), with as a result that it was no longer a [free imperial city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city). [William V, Prince of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_V,_Prince_of_Orange)-Nassau did not want stolen areas and therefore let his son [Prince Willem Frederik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_the_Netherlands) (the later King William I of the Netherlands) take possession of the city and the principality. This prince held its entry on 30 June 1806, and as such the [County of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Dortmund) then became part of the principality. On 12 July 1806, most of the Nassau principalities were deprived of their sovereign rights by means of the [Rhine treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Confederation_of_the_Rhine). In October of the same year, the County of Dortmund was occupied by French troops and was added to the [Grand Duchy of Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Berg) on 1 March 1808. It is the capital of the Ruhr department. In 1808 Dortmund becomes capital of French satellite [Ruhr (department)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_\(department\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritannica1910-18] At the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) in 1815, the entire Grand Duchy of Berg, including Dortmund, was added to the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia). The state mining authority of the [Ruhr area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_area) was founded in 1815 and moved from Bochum to Dortmund. Within the Prussian [Province of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Westphalia), Dortmund was a district seat within [Regierungsbezirk Arnsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regierungsbezirk_Arnsberg) until 1875, when it became an urban district within the region.



French troops in Dortmund c. 1923–1925

During the [industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation) of Prussia, Dortmund became a major centre for coal and steel. The town expanded into a city, with the population rising from 57,742 in 1875 to 379,950 in 1905. Sprawling residential areas like the North, East, Union and Kreuz district sprang up in less than 10 years. In 1920, Dortmund was one of the centres for resistance to the [Kapp Putsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapp_Putsch) – a right-wing military coup launched against the [Social Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic)-led government. In the [Ruhr uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_uprising), radical workers formed the 50,000-man [Ruhr Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_Red_Army) in hopes of setting up a soviet-style government. They were defeated with considerable loss of life by government and [Freikorps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freikorps) units.[^cite_note-20] On 11 January 1923, French and Belgian troops [occupied the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Ruhr). French Prime Minister [Raymond Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Poincar%C3%A9) was responding to Germany's failure to comply with the [reparations demands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I_reparations) of the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles).[^cite_note-21] The occupation lasted until August 1925.

### World War II



Part of the city centre around Liebfrauen Church in 1945

Under [Nazi Germany](21212.md), the [Old Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Synagogue_\(Dortmund\)), which had opened in 1900, was destroyed in 1938. With a capacity of 1,300 seats, it was one of the largest Jewish houses of worship in Germany. Also, the [Aplerbeck Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aplerbeck_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Dortmund transferred mentally and/or physically disabled patients to the [Hadamar Killing Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamar_Killing_Facility) as part of *[Aktion T4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktion_T4)*, where they were murdered. An additional 229 children were murdered in the "Children's Specialist Department", which was transferred from Marburg in 1941.

Dortmund was the location of the Stalag VI-D [prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) for Polish, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II), Belgian, British, Serbian, Soviet and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Military_Internees) POWs with some 300 [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) units in the city alone,[^cite_note-22] a camp for [Sinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinti) and [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (see *[Romani Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_Holocaust)*),[^cite_note-23] a [subcamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_subcamps_of_Buchenwald) of the [Buchenwald concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp) for 800 predominantly Polish women,[^cite_note-24] and a detachment of the 3rd [SS construction brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_construction_brigade).[^cite_note-25] In September 1943, the local [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) carried out a mass execution of 17 [Polish POWs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_atrocities_committed_against_Polish_prisoners_of_war), who escaped the [Oflag VI-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oflag_VI-B) POW camp, but were soon captured.[^cite_note-26]

Bombing targets of the [Oil Campaign of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_Campaign_of_World_War_II) in Dortmund included [Hoesch-Westfalenhütte AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenh%C3%BCtte), the "[Hoesch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoesch_AG)-Benzin GmbH" synthetic oil plant, and the *Zeche Hansa*.[^cite_note-27] The bombings destroyed about 66% of Dortmund homes.[^cite_note-28] The devastating bombing raids of 12 March 1945 with 1,108 aircraft (748 [Lancasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lancaster), 292 [Halifaxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifaxes), 68 [Mosquitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Mosquito)) destroyed 98% of buildings in the inner city centre, and 4,851 tonnes of bombs were dropped on Dortmund city centre and the south of the city; this was a record for a single target in the whole of World War II.[^cite_note-backtonormandy.org-6]

The Allied ground advance into Germany reached Dortmund in April 1945. The US [95th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) attacked the city on 12 April 1945 against a stubborn German defense. The division, assisted by close air support, advanced through the ruins in urban combat and completed its capture on 13 April 1945.[^cite_note-29]

### Postwar period





Rebuilt and modern reconstruction around St. Reynolds

Post-war, most of the historic buildings in the city centre were not restored, and large parts of the inner city area were completely rebuilt in the style of the 1950s.[^cite_note-30] A few historic buildings such as the main churches [Reinoldikirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinoldikirche) and [Marienkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienkirche,_Dortmund) were restored or rebuilt, and extensive parks and gardens were laid out. The simple but successful postwar rebuilding has resulted in a very mixed and unique inner cityscape. Today nearly 30% of the city consists of buildings from before World War II.[^cite_note-Zensus_2011-7] Dortmund was in the British zone of occupation of Germany, and became part of the new state (*Land*) of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) in 1946. The [LWL-Industriemuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zollern_II/IV_Colliery) was founded in 1969.[^cite_note-31] In 1987, the pit *Minister Stein* closed, marking the end of more than 150 years of coal mining. Dortmund[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHennings1990-32] has since adapted, with its century-long steel and coal industries having been replaced by high-technology areas, including [biomedical technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_technology), [micro systems technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_systems_technology), and [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_\(economics\)). This has led Dortmund to become a regional centre for hi-tech industry.

In 2005, a new era began for the district of [Hörde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6rde). After 160 years of industrial history, work started on the [Phoenix-See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix-See?action=edit\&redlink=1), an artificially created lake. The development of the Phoenix See area was carried out by a subsidiary of the Stadtwerke AG. On 1 October 2010, the largest and most highly anticipated milestone could be celebrated: the launch of the flooding of the Phoenix See. Since 9 May 2011, the fences disappeared and the Phoenix See has been completed.[^cite_note-33] In 2009, Dortmund was classified as a *Node city* in the Innovation Cities Index published by 2thinknow.[^cite_note-innovation-cities.com-34] In a 2014 ranking, the city was rated the most sustainable city in Germany.\[^cite_note-[www.nachhaltigkeitspreis.de-35](http://www.nachhaltigkeitspreis.de-35/)]

On 3 November 2013, more than 20,000 people were evacuated after a [4,000-pound bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockbuster_bomb) from [World War II](32927.md) was found. German authorities safely defused the bomb. The bomb was found after analysing old [aerial photographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_photography) while searching for unexploded bombs dropped by [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) aircraft over Germany's industrial [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) region.[^cite_note-36]

## Geography

### Location



[Emscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emscher) River in Dortmund

Dortmund is an independent city located in the east of the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) area, one of the largest [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) in Europe (see also: [megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolis_\(city_type\))), comprising eleven [independent cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city) and four [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District) with some 5.3 million inhabitants. The city limits of Dortmund itself are 87 km (54 mi) long and border twelve cities, two independent and ten *kreisangehörig* (i.e., belonging to a district), with a total population of approximately 2.4 million. The following cities border Dortmund (clockwise starting from west): [Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochum), [Castrop-Rauxel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castrop-Rauxel), [Waltrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltrop), [Lünen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCnen), [Kamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamen), [Unna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unna), [Holzwickede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzwickede), [Schwerte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerte), [Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen), [Herdecke](1159548.md), and [Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witten). Historically speaking, Dortmund is a part of [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia) which is situated in the Bundesland North Rhine-Westphalia. Moreover, Dortmund is part of [Westphalian Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalian_Lowland) and adjoins with the [Ardey Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardey_Hills), in the south of the city, to the [Sauerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerland).

The [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_\(river\)) forms the [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_\(water\)) on the [Hengsteysee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengsteysee) next to the borough of [Syburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syburg) in the south of Dortmund between the cities of [Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen) and [Herdecke](1159548.md), North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. The [Klusenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klusenberg), a hill that is part of the [Ardey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardey_Hills) range, is located just north of the Hengsteysee and the highest point of Dortmund 254.3 m (834 ft). There is also a [pumped-storage plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped-storage_hydroelectricity) on this reservoir, named Koepchenwerk. The lowest point can be found in the northern borough of Brechten at 48.9 m (160 ft).

The [Emscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emscher) is a small river and has its wellspring in [Holzwickede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzwickede), east of the city of Dortmund, and flows west through Dortmund. Towns along the Emscher take in Dortmund, [Castrop-Rauxel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castrop-Rauxel), [Herne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herne,_Germany), [Recklinghausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recklinghausen), [Gelsenkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelsenkirchen), [Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essen), [Bottrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottrop), [Oberhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberhausen), and [Dinslaken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinslaken), where it flows into the Rhine.

### Boroughs



Municipalities and neighbouring communities



Aerial view of urban [Kreuzviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreuzviertel)

Dortmund comprises 62 neighbourhoods which in turn are grouped into twelve boroughs (called "***Stadtbezirke***"), often named after the most important neighbourhood. Three boroughs cover the area of the inner city (Innenstadt-West (City centre West), Innenstadt-Nord (City centre North), Innenstadt-Ost (City centre East)) and the remaining nine boroughs make up the surrounding area ([Eving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eving), [Scharnhorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scharnhorst_\(Dortmund\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Brackel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackel_\(Dortmund\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Aplerbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aplerbeck), [Hörde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6rde), [Hombruch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hombruch), [Lütgendortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCtgendortmund?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Huckarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huckarde), [Mengede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengede?action=edit\&redlink=1)). Each "***Stadtbezirk***" is assigned a Roman numeral and has a local governing body of nineteen members with limited authority. Most of the boroughs were originally independent municipalities but were gradually annexed from 1905 to 1975. This long-lasting process of annexation has led to a strong identification of the population with "their" boroughs or districts and to a rare peculiarity: The borough of Hörde, located in the south of Dortmund and independent until 1928, has its own coat of arms.

The centre can be subdivided into historically evolved city districts, whose borders are not always strictly defined, such as:

- Stadtzentrum *(City centre)*

- Hafenviertel *(Harbour Quarter)*

- Nordmarkt *(Northern market)*

- Borsigplatz

- Kaiserviertel *(Emperor Quarter)*

- Kronenviertel *(Crown Quarter)*

- Kreuzviertel *(Cross Quarter)*

- Klinikviertel *(Clinical Quarter)*

- Saarlandstraßenviertel *(Saarland street Quarter)*

- Unionviertel *(Union Quarter)*

- Gartenstadt *(Garden Town)*

### Climate

Dortmund is situated in the [temperate climate zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperateness) with [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*). Winters are cool; summers are warm. The average annual temperature lies at approximately 9 to 10 °C (48 to 50 °F), the total average annual amount of precipitation lies at approximately 800 mm (31 in). Precipitation evenly falls throughout the year; steady rain (with some snow), prevails in the wintertime, isolated showers dominate the summer season. Dortmund features characteristics of densely populated areas as for example the occurrence of [urban heat islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_islands) is typical.[^cite_note-37]

## Demographics

Dortmund's population grew rapidly in the time of the 19th century industrialisation, when coal mining and steel processing in the city began. 1904 marks the year when Dortmund saw a population of more than 100,000 for the first time in its history. During the 19th century the area around Dortmund, called [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr), attracted up to 500,000 ethnic [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), [Masurians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masurians), and [Silesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesians) from [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia) and [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia), in a migration known as *[Ostflucht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostflucht)* (flight from the east). Most of the new inhabitants came from Eastern Europe, but immigrants also came from France, Ireland, and the United Kingdom. Almost all their descendants today speak German as a mother tongue, and for various reasons, they do not identify with their Polish roots and traditions; often only their Polish family names remaining as a sign of their past. Not taking the fluctuation of war years into account, the population figures rose constantly to 657,804 in 1965. As a result of the city's post-industrial decline, the population fell to just under 580,000 in 2011.

Contrary to earlier projections, population figures have been on the rise in recent years due to net migration gains. Dortmund has seen a moderate influx of younger people (18 to 25 years of age) mainly because of its universities.[^cite_note-dortmund.de-42] Data of the [EU-wide 2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_EU_census) revealed discrepancies between the census data and the official population statistics, which resulted in a statistical "loss" of 9,000 inhabitants in Dortmund.[^cite_note-43] In 2016, it was announced that the population was back above 600,000.[^cite_note-44] As of 2024, with a population of 603,462, Dortmund is [the ninth largest city in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population) (after [Berlin](3354.md), [Hamburg](13467.md), [Munich](19058.md), [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), [Frankfurt](10992.md), [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), and [Leipzig](17955.md)) and the largest city in the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) agglomeration.

### Religion

As of 2022, the largest Christian denominations were Protestantism (23.7%) and [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) (22.6% of the population).[^cite_note-48] Furthermore, in Dortmund the [Greek Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church), the [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church), and the [Macedonian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Orthodox_Church) are represented. The Church of the Holy Apostles (gre. I.N. Αγίων Αποστόλων Ντόρτμουντ – I.N. Agíon Apostólon Dortmund) was the first Greek church in Germany to be founded due to the influx of "guest workers". Also Dortmund is home of the [New Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Apostolic_Church) in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), with more than 84,944 community members.

The Jewish community has a history dating back to Medieval times and has always ranked among the largest in [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia). Dortmund is home to the National Association of Jewish Communities of Westfalen-Lippe. The synagogues operate there in City center, Hörde, and Dorstfeld. Due to the growing immigration of people from Muslim countries beginning in the 1960s, Dortmund has a large Muslim community with more than 30 mosques.

In June 2019 Dortmund hosted the 37th Evangelischer Kirchentag – [German Evangelical Church Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Evangelical_Church_Assembly).[^cite_note-49]

## Government and politics



Dortmund coat of arms



Townhall Dortmund



Townhall Aplerbeck, one of twelve district councils

Dortmund is one of nineteen [independent district-free cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city) (*[kreisfreie Städte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_districts_of_Germany)*) in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), which means that it does not form part of another general-purpose local government entity, in this case it is not part of a [Landkreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rural_districts_of_Germany). Since 1975, Dortmund is divided into twelve administrative districts. Each district (*Bezirk*) has its own elected district council (Bezirksvertretung) and its own district mayor (Bezirksbürgermeister). The district councils are advisory only.

Dortmund is often called the *Herzkammer der [SPD](60585.md)* (roughly translated as "heartland of the Social democrats"), after the politically dominant party in the city. During the Nazi era (1933–1945), mayors were installed by the Nazi Party. After World War II, the military government of the British occupation zone installed a new mayor and a municipal constitution modeled on that of British cities. The first major elected by the population of Dortmund was [Fritz Henßler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Hen%C3%9Fler). Since the end of the war, the SPD has held a plurality in the city council, except for the period from 1999 to 2004.

## Cityscape

### City centre



Dortmund with RWE-Tower and churches of Reinoldi, Petri and Marien on the right

Dortmund's city centre offers a picture full of contrasts. Historic buildings like [Altes Stadthaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altes_Stadthaus,_Dortmund) or the Krügerpassage rub shoulders with post-war architecture like Gesundheitshaus and concrete constructions with Romanesque churches like the [Reinoldikirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinoldikirche) and the [Marienkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienkirche,_Dortmund). The near-complete destruction of Dortmund's city centre during World War II (98%) has resulted in a varied architectural landscape. The reconstruction of the city centre followed the style of the 1950s, while respecting the old layout and naming of the streets. The downtown of Dortmund still retains the outline of the medieval city. A ring road marks the former city wall, and the [Westen-/Ostenhellweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellweg), part of a medieval salt trading route, is still the major (pedestrian) street bisecting the city centre.

Thus, the inner city today is characterized by simple and modest post-war buildings, with a few interspersed pre-war buildings which were reconstructed due to their historical importance. Some buildings of the "Wiederaufbauzeit" (era of reconstruction), for example the opera house are nowadays regarded as classics of modern architecture.[^cite_note-51]

### Urban districts

Unlike the Dortmund city centre, much of the inner districts around the old medieval centre escaped damage in the second world war and post war redevelopment.

#### Kreuzviertel



Typical Wilhelminian style houses

The [Kreuzviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreuzviertel) is characterised by old buildings, the majority of which come from the turn of the 20th century (1884 to 1908). Over 80% of all housing in this area was constructed before 1948, with the oldest building the Dortmund University of Applied Sciences and Arts still standing being from 1896. In the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), relatively few buildings were destroyed in comparison to other areas of the city. Today, [Kreuzviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreuzviertel) forms a nearly homogeneous historic building area. Over 100 buildings remain protected as historic monuments, like the Kreuzkirche at Kreuzstraße and the first concrete church in Germany St.-Nicolai. Nowadays the Kreuzviertel is a trendy district with pubs, restaurants, cafés, galleries, and little shops. Moreover, local efforts to beautify and invigorate the neighbourhood have reinforced a budding sense of community and artistic expression. The West park is the green lung of the Kreuzviertel and in the months between May and October a centre of the student urban life. The district has the highest real estate prices in Dortmund.[^cite_note-52]

Even today many artists choose Kreuzviertel as their residence: [Sascha Schmitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasha_\(German_singer\)), [Christina Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Hammer), and players of [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund).

#### Nordstadt



Dockland – young restaurant and bars scene

The northern downtown part of Dortmund called Nordstadt, situated in a territory of 14.42 km<sup>2</sup> (5.57 sq mi) is shaped by a colorful variety of cultures. As the largest homogeneous old building area in [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) the Nordstadt is a melting pot of different people of different countries and habits just a few steps from the city center. The Nordstadt is an industrial [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) that was mainly developed in the 19th century to serve the [Westfalenhütte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenh%C3%BCtte) steelworks, port and rail freight depot. All of the residents live in a densely populated 300 hectare area (the most densely populated residential area in the state of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) with steelworks, port and railway lines acting as physical barriers cutting off the area from the city centre and other residential districts).[^cite_note-Dortmund.de-53]

The area has been badly affected by the deindustrialisation of these heavy industries, with the target area developing a role as the home for growing numbers of immigrants and socially disadvantaged groups partly because of the availability of cheaper (although poor quality) accommodation.[^cite_note-54] Nevertheless, two parks – Fredenbaumpark and Hoeschpark – are situated there. There is also much equipment for children to spend their free time. For example, the 35 meters high Big Tipi, which was brought in from the [Expo 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2000) in Hanover. All of that should attract families to settle in, but low prices of apartments and a variety of renting offers speak for the contrary. This developed into the youngest population of Dortmund is living and created a district with art house cinemas to ethnic stores, from exotic restaurants to student pubs.[^cite_note-Dortmund.de-53]

The Borsigplatz is probably one of the best known squares in Germany. Ballspielverein [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) was founded nearby, north-east of the main railway station. The streets radiating outward to form a star shape, the sycamore in the middle of the square and the tramline running diagonally across the square give Borsigplatz its very own flair.[^cite_note-55]

#### Kaiserviertel



Cherry Blossom Avenue

The Kaiserstraßen District is located east of the former ramparts of Dortmund and follows the course of the [Westenhellweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westenhellweg). In this district numerous magnificent buildings from the 1900s and new buildings from the 1950s are located next to the heritage-protected State Mining Office Dortmund, several Courts, Consulate and the East Cemetery. The district is characterize by the employee of the Amtsgericht, Landgericht (the first and second instances of ordinary jurisdiction) and the Prison.

Today the historical Kaiserbrunnen and the entrance sign for Kaiserstraße are important starting points for a tour to the popular shopping district. The Moltkestreet also known as the Cherry Blossom Avenue, became famous after photographers started posting pictures of blooming trees. Every spring, usually in April, the street in the Kaiserstraßen district is booming with pink blossoms and attracts tourists.

#### Unionviertel



Rheinische Straße

The Union District is located west of the former ramparts of Dortmund and follows the course of the [Westenhellweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westenhellweg). For a long time, the neighbourhood at the Dortmunder U and along the Rheinische Straße was marked by vacancy and social distortion due to structural change. Today it is developing an inspiring young artist scene, with more and more students thanks to cheaper apartments near the university and a vibrant gastronomy. This development benefits strongly from the new, widely visible beacon, the art and creative centre Dortmunder U, opened in 2010. Yet, for a time, it was mainly the Union Gewerbehof activists and other single stakeholders who initiated change.[^cite_note-56]

#### Hörde on Lake Phoenix



Hörde castle

Hörde is borough in the south of the city of Dortmund. Originally Hörde was a separate town (until 1929) and was founded by the Counts of Mark in opposition to their principal enemy, the town of Dortmund. In 1388, the "Großen Dortmunder Fehde" (great feud of Dortmund) took place, where the city of Dortmund battled against the alliance of surrounding towns. The struggle ended in 1390, with defeat for Hörde and its allies of [Herdecke](1159548.md), [Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witten), [Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochum), [Castrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castrop), [Lünen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCnen), [Unna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unna), and [Schwerte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerte). Today Hörde is a part of Dortmund with restored old buildings combined with modern architecture. The Hörder Burg (Hörde castle) was built in the 12th century and is located in the east of the town, close to the [Emscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emscher) and Lake Phoenix.

Lake Phoenix was one of the largest urban redevelopment projects in [Europe](9239.md). On the area of the former blast furnace and steel plant site of [ThyssenKrupp](944221.md) newly formed and developed a new urban resident and recreational area 3 kilometers (2 miles) from the city centre of Dortmund. The development of the Phoenix See area cost €;170 million.[^cite_note-57] The lake is 1.2 km long directed to east–west and 320 meters wide in north–south direction. The water surface area of 24 acres is larger than the [Hamburg](13467.md) [Alster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alster). Lake Phoenix is a shallow water lake with a depth of 3 to 4 meters and a capacity of around 600,000 cubic meters. Attractive high priced residential areas were thus created on the southern and northern sides of the Lake. On the western lakeside, the existing district centre of Hörde is enlarged by a city port and a mixed functional urban area.

Together with the renatured Emscher, the Lake forms a water landscape of 33 hectares, which, as a linking area, is an important element of the Emscher landscape park. ## Transportation

### Road transport



The Ruhrschnellweg Section East Dortmund

Dortmund also serves as a major European and German crossroads for the Autobahnsystem. The [Ruhrschnellweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_40) follows old Hanseatic trade routes to connect the city with the other metropolises of the [Ruhr Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_Area). It crosses the [Dutch](21148.md)-German border as a continuation of the Dutch [A67](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A67_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) and crosses the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), leads through the [Ruhr valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_valley) toward [Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochum), becoming B 1 ([Bundesstraße 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesstra%C3%9Fe_1)) at the Kreuz Dortmund West and eventually merging into the A 44 near [Holzwickede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzwickede). It has officially been named Ruhrschnellweg (Ruhr Fast Way), but locals usually call it Ruhrschleichweg (Ruhr Crawling Way) or "the Ruhr area's longest parking lot". According to *Der Spiegel*, it is the most congested motorway in Germany.

Connections to more distant parts of Germany are maintained by Autobahn routes [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_1) and [A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_2), which traverse the north and east city limits and meet at the [Kamener Kreuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamener_Kreuz) interchange north-east of Dortmund. In combination with the Autobahn [A45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_45) to the west these form the [Dortmund Beltway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Beltway?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*Dortmunder Autobahnring*).

### Cycling

Cycling in Dortmund is supported by urban planners – an extensive network of cycle paths exists which had its beginnings in the 1980s. Dortmund was admitted to the German "Association of Pedestrian and Bicycle-Friendly Cities and Municipalities in NRW" (AGFS) on 8 August 2007. Dortmund is connected to a number of long-distance cycle paths and a [Bike freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_freeway) called the *Radschnellweg Ruhr* (Ruhr Area Fast Cycle Path).

### Rail transport



Dortmund central railway station

The central train station, [Dortmund Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Hauptbahnhof), is the third largest long-distance traffic junction in Germany. The station is served by various [Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn) [InterCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity) and [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCityExpress) long-distance services. There are frequent ICE trains to other German cities, including [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main), [Berlin](3354.md) and other cities in the Rhein-Ruhr region. The station is also served by open-access rail operator [Eurostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostar), whose long-distance trains link Dortmund with international destinations including [Amsterdam Centraal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Centraal_station), [Brussels-South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels-South_railway_station) and [Paris Gare du Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_du_Nord).

Regional train services are tendered by [Verkehrsverbund Rhein-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkehrsverbund_Rhein-Ruhr) (VRR), the local association of public transport companies in the Ruhr area, which provides a uniform fare structure in the whole region. Currently, regional train services in Dortmund are operated by [DB Regio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_Regio), [National Express Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Express_Germany), [Eurobahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobahn), [Vias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vias_\(rail_company\)) and [Hessische Landesbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessische_Landesbahn). Within the VRR region, tickets are valid on lines of all of its member companies as well as Deutsche Bahn's [railway lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_line) (except high-speed trains) and can be bought at ticket machines and service centres of DSW21, all other members of VRR, and DB.

### Public transportation

Local public transportation is largely operated by the city-owned transit operator [Dortmunder Stadtwerke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmunder_Stadtwerke) (DSW21). The city has an extensive light rail ([Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Stadtbahn)) and bus system. The Stadtbahn consists of eight lines (U41 to U47 and U49) serving Dortmund and the neighbouring city of [Lünen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCnen) to the north. The lines are mostly overground with tracks running in the alongside streets, with three underground sections in the city centre shared by multiple lines. The minimum service interval is 2.5 minutes, although the usual pattern is that each line runs at 5 to 10 minute intervals. On Sundays the trains run at a 15-minute interval. In April 2008, the newly constructed east–west underground light rail line was opened, completing the underground service in the city centre and replacing the last [trams](30733.md) on the surface.[^cite_note-62]

A number of bus lines complete the Dortmund public transport system. [Night buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_service_\(public_transport\)) replace Stadtbahn services between 1\:30 am and 7\:30 am on weekends and public holidays. The central junction for the night bus service is Reinoldikirche in the city centre, where all night bus lines start and end.

The [H-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-Bahn) at [Technical University of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Dortmund) is a hanging monorail built specifically to shuttle passengers between the university's two campuses,[^cite_note-63] which are now also flanked by research laboratories and other high-tech corporations and startups. A nearly identical monorail system transfers passengers at [Düsseldorf Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf_Airport).[^cite_note-64]

### Air transport

[Dortmund Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Airport) is a minor international airport 13 km (8.1 mi) east of the city centre. Whilst located within city limits in [Wickede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wickede_\(Dortmund\)), it is only accessible via the neighbouring muncipality of [Holzwickede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzwickede).[^cite_note-65] The airport serves the area of the [Ruhrgebiet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhrgebiet), [Sauerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerland), [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia) and parts of the [Netherlands](21148.md), with airlines offering flights mostly to leisure destinations including [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca_Airport) and various eastern European cities. The airport is served by an express bus to [Dortmund main station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Hauptbahnhof), a shuttle bus to the nearby railway station *[Holzwickede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzwickede_station)/Dortmund Flughafen*, a bus to the city's metro line *U47*, as well as a bus to the neighbouring city of [Unna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unna).

In 2019, the airport served 2,719,563 passengers[^cite_note-66] mainly used for low-cost and leisure charter flights. The closest major international airport is [Düsseldorf Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf_Airport).

## Economy



Central business district "Wallring"



Central Business District "Ruhrallee"



Future location Lake Phoenix and Phoenix-East

Dortmund has adapted since the collapse of its century long steel, coal and beer industries. The region has shifted to high technology, [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics), [biomedical technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_technology), [micro systems technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_systems_technology), engineering, tourism, finance, education, and [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_\(economics\)), and is thus one of the most dynamic new-economy cities in Germany. In 2009, Dortmund was classified as a *Node city* in the *Innovation Cities Index* published by 2thinknow.[^cite_note-innovation-cities.com-34]

Hundreds of [SMEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium_enterprises) are still based in and around Dortmund (often termed *[Mittelstand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittelstand)*). Dortmund is also home to a number of medium-sized information technology companies,[^cite_note-67] many linked to the local university [TU Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dortmund) at the first technology center in [Germany](11867.md) named "Technologiepark Dortmund" opened in the 1980s. With around 280 companies, like [Boehringer Ingelheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boehringer_Ingelheim) and [Verizon Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verizon_Communications), and more than 8,500 employees, TechnologiePark Dortmund is one of the most successful technology parks in Europe. The city works closely with research institutes, private universities, and companies to collaborate on the commercialisation of science initiatives.[^cite_note-68] Furthermore, 680 IT and software companies with 12,000 employees are based in Dortmund, making the city one of Germany's biggest software locations. Two of the top 10 IT service providers in Germany are based in Dortmund – [adesso SE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adesso_SE?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Materna Group.[^cite_note-69]

Dortmund is home to many insurance companies e.g. [Signal Iduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Iduna), Continentale Krankenversicherung, Bundesinnungskrankenkasse Gesundheit (BIG direkt), and Volkswohl Bund. In recent years a service sector and high-tech industry have grown up. Some of its most prominent companies of these sectors include [Amprion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amprion) and [RWE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWE)-Westnetz (Electricity), [Rhenus Logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenus_\(company\)) (Logistics), [Wilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilo_SE), [KHS GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHS_GmbH), [Elmos Semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmos_Semiconductor), ABP Induction Systems, [Nordwest Handel AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordwest_Handel_AG?action=edit\&redlink=1) – all of whom have their headquarters here. Companies with operations in or around Dortmund include [Zalando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalando), [Daimler AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimler_AG): EvoBus, [RapidMiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidMiner), [Gap Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gap_Inc.), and [ThyssenKrupp](944221.md).

Dortmund is also the headquarters of [Century Media Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_Media_Records), a [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) [record label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_label) with offices in the United States and [London](17867.md). In August 2015, Century Media was acquired by [Sony Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Music) for US\$17 million.[^cite_note-70]

### Tourism



Cityring Concert, Freedom Square

Tourism in Dortmund is a fast-growing economic factor every year: new overnight records can be announced, new hotels open and new visitor magnets are added.[^cite_note-71] Starting in the mid-1990s, Dortmund, formerly an industrial centre, saw rapid development that expanded its cultural and tourism, and transformed it into a vibrant city. An important strategic step was the start of construction the [Konzerthaus Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konzerthaus_Dortmund), the reuse of vacant industrial buildings like the [Zollern II/IV Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zollern_II/IV_Colliery), [Kokerei Hansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokerei_Hansa?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Dortmund U-Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_U-Tower), and the reorientation of the [Dortmund Christmas Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Christmas_Market) with over 300 stalls packed around a Christmas tree that stands 45 metres tall – reputed to be the biggest in the world. A Tourist Information centre next to the U-Tower.[^cite_note-72] In 2017, Dortmund had 1,450,528 overnight stays; making it one of the most popular destinations in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md).[^cite_note-73]

The majority of tourists are domestic visitors, coming from Germany. International travellers arrive from the United Kingdom, [Netherlands](21148.md), [Austria](26964606.md), and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwitzerland). Dortmund also draws [business tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_tourism), having been equipped with facilities like WILO, Amprion next to [Westfalenhallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenhallen) and football tourism with Fans of [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund).[^cite_note-74] The top 5 most visited attractions were the Christmas market, with more than three and a half million visitors, [Signal Iduna Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Iduna_Park), [Deutsches Fußballmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Fu%C3%9Fballmuseum), [Dortmund U-Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_U-Tower), [Zollern II/IV Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zollern_II/IV_Colliery), and [Westfalenpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenpark).[^cite_note-75]

### Shopping



Westenhellweg

The [Westenhellweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westenhellweg) is a popular shopping destination and with nearly 13,000 visitors per hour it was Germany's most frequented shopping street in 2013.[^cite_note-76] During the Middle Ages, Dortmund was the only free imperial city in Westphalia, having already been regarded as an important centre of trade. Today some of the most reputed shops, department stores have stores here. It is a pedestrian-only area and is bordered by the [Reinoldikirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinoldikirche) in the east and [U-Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_U-Tower) in the west. The Westenhellweg has one of the highest rents for retail and office space in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md).[^cite_note-77]

Three more shopping malls occupy the Thier-Galerie; [Galeria Kaufhof](305874.md) and [Karstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcandor), as well as large fashion retail clothing stores. During the month before [Christmas](6237.md), the extended pedestrian-only zone is host to [Dortmund Christmas Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Christmas_Market), one of the largest and oldest Christmas markets in Germany. With more than 3.5 million visitors and 300 stalls around a gigantic Christmas tree that stands 45 metres tall, it is one of the most visited and popular market in the world.[^cite_note-78]

In close proximity to the Dortmund concert hall lies the Brückstraßenviertel – a quarter hub especially for young people. The "Rue de Pommes Frites", which is what the Dortmund citizens have called the Brückstraße, has turned into a modern shopping promenade, geared towards a younger market.

For a long time, the Kampstraße had a shadowy existence as a parallel street to the Westenhellweg and Ostenhellweg, but it has become a grand boulevard containing specialist stores. Right next to the Kampstraße is the Kleppingstraße – a shopping street with a high concentration of gastronomy and expensive, prestigious shops. It is located between the Ostenhellweg and Neutor to Wallring.

### Port and logistics



Logistic Hub Harbour

Dortmund is one of the most important [logistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) hubs in Germany, more than 900 companies working in logistics, as well as nationally and internationally recognised scientific institutes. The variety of different activities taking place at the Fraunhofer Institute Material Flow and Logistics ([Fraunhofer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraunhofer_Society)) has, over the past few years, led to a bundling of skills in the areas of logistics and digitalisation in the city. ## Education

Dortmund has 160 schools and 17 business, technical colleges teach more than 85,000 pupils. The city has a 4-year primary education program. After completing primary school, students continue to the Hauptschule, Realschule, Gesamtschule or Gymnasium (college preparatory school). The Stadtgymnasium Dortmund which was founded in 1543 as Archigymnasium is one of the [oldest schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_schools) in Europe.[^cite_note-89] The Leibniz Gymnasium, a bilingual public school located in the Kreuzviertel district, is particularly popular with children of the English-speaking expatriate community. The school is an International Baccalaureate school.[^cite_note-90] The [Goethe-Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe-Gymnasium,_Dortmund) was founded in 1867 as the first school offering higher education to girls in the city. It has been a NRW Sportschule, focused on sports, from 2009.

### Higher education

[TU Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dortmund) (Technical University of Dortmund) was founded in 1968 and is located in the southern part of the city. It has about 30,000 students and offers a wide range of subjects in of [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics), [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [spatial planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_planning), and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics). The university has a dedicated railway station at the campus's main gate, the journey from the city centre lasting merely seven minutes. The university is highly ranked in terms of its research performance in the areas of [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics), [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics).[^cite_note-91] The university's most noticeable landmark is the [H-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-Bahn), a [monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monorail) train which connects the north and south campuses.

[Dortmund University of Applied Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_University_of_Applied_Sciences_and_Arts) is a [Fachhochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fachhochschule) with 12,300 students, and 669 staff, 232 of which are professors. The Fachhochschule was created by a merger of several institutions of higher learning in 1971. Owing to its history as separate institutions, it consists of three campuses in different parts of Dortmund. The departments of mechanical and electrical engineering are located at Sonnenstraße near the city center. The department of design has its own campus at Max-Ophüls-Platz while the departments of social work, economics, computer science and architecture are housed in several buildings next to the [Technical University of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Dortmund) campus in the suburb of Eichlinghofen. Additional offices in the city centre are used for administrative purposes.

The city is the site of several other universities, colleges and academies, which attract about 45,000 students.[^cite_note-dev.statistik.dortmund.de-47] Among them there are:

- *FOM Hochschule für Oekonomie & Management, Standort Dortmund*: academy for management, founded in 1993.

- *Fachhochschule für öffentliche Verwaltung Nordrhein-Westfalen*: academy for public administration.

- *International School of Management*: private academy focussing on management and economics, founded in 1990.

- *IT-Center Dortmund*: private college founded in 2000.

- *International University of Applied Sciences Bad Honnef – Bonn*: is a private, state-recognised university of business and management.

## Livability and quality of life



Dortmund is one of the least stressful cities in the world.

In November 2017, according to a study by data of the German National Statistics Office, the National Employment Agency, [Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_\(consulting_firm\)), [Handelsblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handelsblatt), [Numbeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbeo), and Immowelt, Dortmund was ranked on position seven of the *most livable cities in Germany for expats*.[^cite_note-93] In September 2017, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* praised the city of Dortmund, which has been adapting since the collapse of its century-old steel and coal industries and has shifted to high technology biomedical technology, micro systems technology and other services, as the *hidden star of structural change* providing a good quality of life for employees.[^cite_note-94] According to *the 2017 Global Least & Most Stressful Cities Ranking*, Dortmund is one of the least stressful cities in the world. It is ranked 27th out of 150, between [Copenhagen](5166.md) and [Vancouver](32706.md), and is highly ranked in the categories traffic & public transport, gender equality and debt per capital.[^cite_note-95]

*Like a Phoenix Rising from the Ashes* and exemplary for structural transformation – This was the title of an article in the online version on *[Neue Zürcher Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Z%C3%BCrcher_Zeitung)* of the urban livability and new exceptional architecture in Dortmund.[^cite_note-96]

In a 2015/2016 survey centred on *student life in Germany*, Dortmund ranked as seventh-best.[^cite_note-97]

In a 2012 study of the *most livable biggest cities in Germany*, Dortmund ranked on position ten between [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) and [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) and first of all large cities in Germany due to sport, gastronomy, and shopping opportunities.[^cite_note-98]

In 2009, Dortmund was classified as a *Node city* in the Innovation Cities Index published by 2thinknow,[^cite_note-innovation-cities.com-34] and in 2014 acclaimed as the most sustainable city in [Germany](11867.md).\[^cite_note-[www.nachhaltigkeitspreis.de-35](http://www.nachhaltigkeitspreis.de-35/)]

## Culture



The new [opera house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_Dortmund) is one of the biggest theaters in Germany.



The [Konzerthaus Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konzerthaus_Dortmund) is one of the most outstanding concert halls in Europe.

The city has a long tradition of music and theatre. The orchestra was founded in 1887 and is now called [Dortmunder Philharmoniker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmunder_Philharmoniker). The first opera house was built in 1904, destroyed in World War II and opened again in 1966 as [Opernhaus Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opernhaus_Dortmund). It is operated by [Theater Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_Dortmund) together with other locations, including (since 2002) the Konzerthaus Dortmund. The Konzerthaus Dortmund is listed in the ECHO list as one of the 21 most outstanding concert halls in Europe.[^cite_note-99]

The Domicil Jazz Club is one of the "100 best jazz venues world wide" according to the American jazz magazine *[DownBeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DownBeat)*.[^cite_note-100]

The Dortmund U-Tower, which was once a brewery, is now European centre for creative economy and the [Museum am Ostwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_am_Ostwall). The area around the U-Tower called "Union Viertel" is part of the Creative.Quarters Ruhr and are rooted in the European Capital of Culture [RUHR.2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RUHR.2010).

Dortmund leading cabaret-stage is the Cabaret Queue, which is located next to Lake phoenix. Some other famous cabaret-stages are the [Fletch Bizzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fletch_Bizzel) and the theatre Olpktetal. The most important cabaret event is the RuhrHOCHdeutsch, which is one of the most successful cabaret festivals in Germany. It features artists from around the world.

Dortmund is also famous for its Christmas market, which draws well over three and a half million visitors to its 300 stalls around a gigantic Christmas tree creation that stands 45 metres tall. The market is famous for its handmade ornaments and delicacies.

### Recreation



Botanischer Garten Rombergpark



[Florianturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florianturm), Westfalenpark



Juicy Beats Festival, Westfalenpark

The [Botanischer Garten Rombergpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanischer_Garten_Rombergpark), or informally Rombergpark, is an extensive municipal [arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum) and [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) located in the south of the city center of Dortmund. With its total area of 65 hectares the Rombergpark is one of the largest botanical gardens in the world. The garden was established in 1822 as the Romberg family's English landscape park. In 1927–1929 it was acquired by the city and under city planning director Richard Nose enhanced by a small herb garden. The park and castle were badly damaged in [World War II](32927.md), but starting in 1950 director Gerd Krüssmann rebuilt it as an [arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum), adding some 4,500 species to the park. Today the garden contains a historic English landscape park with monuments; an arboretum containing thousands of species of woody plants, including some of the largest trees in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md); a terrace with [palm trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_tree); and four [greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse) (1,000 m<sup>2</sup> total area) for [cactus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus) and [succulents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succulent), [ferns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern), [tropical plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_plant), and [camellias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camellia), [jasmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine), and [lemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon).

The [Dortmund Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Zoo) is the zoological garden with 28 hectares next to the Rombergpark and was founded 1953. With 1,800 animals belonging to 250 species, the Dortmund Zoo is the second largest in the Ruhr Valley. It is specialized in the keeping and breeding of [South American](26769.md) species and is leading in the breeding of the [giant anteater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_anteater), the [tamandua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamandua), and the [giant otter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_otter).[^cite_note-101]

The [Westfalenpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenpark) is Dortmunds's most popular inner-city park. The park is 72 hectares in size and is one of the largest urban gardens of Germany. It was first opened in 1959 as the second [Bundesgartenschau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesgartenschau) ([abbr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbreviation) BUGA) in [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md). With the National Rosarium with 3,000 different rose varieties, theme gardens, an environmental protection centre, the German Cookbook Museum, a geological garden, cafés, and recreation areas, it provides numerous opportunities for a day of diverse activities. Dortmund's Westfalenpark is also a popular location for events in the Ruhr area- with parties, festivals, events, theatre, music, and flea and garden markets. One of the best views across the whole Ruhr valley is offered by the visitors platform and the revolving restaurant in the 209-metre-high [Florian tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florian_tower?action=edit\&redlink=1). Another summer attraction is the chair lift, which opened in 1959 and runs on Sundays between a "Mountain" and "Valley" station 500 metres apart.[^cite_note-102]

### Museums

With more than 20 museums, Dortmund has one of the largest variety of museums in the Ruhr Valley, one of which, the [LWL Industrial Museum Zollern II/IV Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zollern_II/IV_Colliery), is an anchor point on the [European Route of Industrial Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Route_of_Industrial_Heritage).[^cite_note-103]



Museum of Art and Cultural History

The [Museum am Ostwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_am_Ostwall) (known as Museum am Ostwall until 2010) is a museum of [modern](163543.md) and [contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_art). It was founded in the late 1940s, and has been located in the [Dortmund U-Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_U-Tower) since 2010. The collection includes [paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paintings), [sculptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculptures), objects, and [photographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographs) from the 20th century, plus over 2,500 [graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_art), spanning [Expressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionism) through classic modern art to the present day. At the heart of the collection are works by [Ernst Ludwig Kirchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Ludwig_Kirchner), [Otto Mueller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Mueller), [Emil Nolde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Nolde), and graphics by [Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso) from the 1940s and '50s, plus others by [Joan Miró](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mir%C3%B3), [Marc Chagall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Chagall), and [Salvador Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD).

The [German Football Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Museum) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Deutsches Fußballmuseum*), aka [DFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Association)-Museum, is the national museum for [German football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Germany). It is located close to the [Dortmund Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Hauptbahnhof) and is part of an art and culture mile between the creative center [Dortmund U-Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_U-Tower) and the [Theater Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_Dortmund), founded to preserve, conserve and interpret important collections of football memorabilia. In its permanent exhibition, the museum presents the history of [Germany national football team](250204.md) and the [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga).



DASA Arbeitswelt Ausstellung

The [Museum für Kunst und Kulturgeschichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_f%C3%BCr_Kunst_und_Kulturgeschichte) or MKK (*Museum of Art and Cultural History*) is a municipal museum located in an [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) building which was formerly the Dortmund Savings Bank. The collection includes paintings, sculptures, furniture, and applied art, illustrating the cultural history of Dortmund from early times to the 20th century. There are regular temporary exhibitions of art and culture, as well as a permanent exhibition on the history of [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying), with rare geodetic instruments.[^cite_note-104]

The [Steinwache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinwache) is a memorial museum of the exhibition Widerstand und Verfolgung in Dortmund 1933–1945 ("Resistance and Persecution in Dortmund 1933–1945"), which demonstrates the persecution under National Socialism with many photographs, short texts and sometimes with reports from contemporary witnesses. The museum is located in an old prison and had a reputation as Die Hölle von Westdeutschland ("The hell of western Germany"). Between 1933 and 1945 more than 66,000 people were imprisoned in the Steinwache prison.

### Festivals and nightlife



Mayday 2009 Westfalenhallen

Dortmund offers a variety of restaurants, bars and clubs. Clubs concentrate in and around the city centre (Wallring) and in the Kreuzviertel district. Furthermore, Dortmund is one of the main centres of the Electronic dance music and techno subculture. With the [Mayday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayday_\(music_festival\)) and Syndicate festivals, the Westfalenhalle Arena has become one of the most important techno strongholds in [Europe](9239.md).

After negotiations with several German cities, it was announced that the [Love Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Parade) would move to the [Ruhr Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_Area) for five years (2007–2012). After [Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essen) in 2007 the festival took 2008 place on the [Bundesstraße 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesstra%C3%9Fe_1) under the motto *Highway of Love*. The event was planned as a "Love Weekend", with parties throughout the region. For the first time the Turkish electronic scene was represented by its own float, called "[Turkish Delights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Delights_\(music_project\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)". The official estimate is that 1.6 million visitors attended, making it the largest parade to date.[^cite_note-105]

Every year, the 'Juicy Beats' music festival turns the Westfalenpark into a huge festival ground for pop, rap, electro, indie, alternative, reggae, and urban beats – most recently with over 50,000 visitors.

### Cuisine



Traditional bakery for Dortmunder Salzkuchen



Dortmunder Export



Panhas

Traditional meals in the region are [Pfefferpotthast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfefferpotthast) (a form of Goulash, though containing more beef), [Balkenbrij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkenbrij), [Heaven and Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himmel_und_erde) (Himmel und Äd; [black pudding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_pudding) with stewed apples mixed with [mashed potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashed_potato)), [Currywurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currywurst), and [Pumpernickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumpernickel) with Griebenschmalz (German lard with crispy pieces of pork skin).

In summer the people like to eat a Dortmunder Salzkuchen (bread buns with caraway seeds, salt, meat, and onions). Also a special meal in the winter is [Reibekuchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reibekuchen) (fried potato pancake served with apple sauce).

Dortmund had more than 550 years of brewing tradition; some of the oldest [breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewery) in [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia) are founded around the Old Market in Dortmund. Dortmund is known for its pale lager beer called [Dortmunder Export](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmunder_Export) or Dortmunder; it became popular with industrial workers and was responsible for Dortmunder Union becoming Germany's largest brewery and Dortmund having the highest concentration of breweries in Germany. Popular and traditionally beer brands are [Dortmunder Actien Brauerei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmunder_Actien_Brauerei), Bergmann Bier, [Kronen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronen), [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_U-Tower), Brinkhoff's, [Dortmunder Hansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmunder_Actien_Brauerei), Hövels, Ritter, Thier, and Stifts.[^cite_note-106]

"Stösschen" is a beer in a small glass "Stösschen" 0.2 litres and can be drunk in about two draughts. The idea of a Stößchen came about in the 19th century, when people would have to wait at the level crossing to cross the Nordstadt Railway Line that divided the city centre from the Nordstadt district. A local innkeeper realised the potential of serving quick drinks to waiting people, and thus began a Dortmund tradition.[^cite_note-107]

The Dortmunder Tropfen [Schnaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnaps) is a type of [liqueur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liqueur) that is flavored with [herbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb) or [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spices) and traditionally drunk [neat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neat_\(bartending\)) as a [digestif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ap%C3%A9ritif_and_digestif).[^cite_note-108]

## Sports



Headquarters of the Borussia Dortmund sports club (BVB)



Headquarter DHB

Dortmund calls itself *Sportstadt* (City of Sports). The city is the home of the biggest handball association in the world, the [German Handball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Handball_Association) (German: Deutscher Handballbund) (DHB), and the German professional handball league [Handball-Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handball-Bundesliga) (HBL). Furthermore, Dortmund is the designated residence of the *[Olympic Centre of Westphalia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiast%C3%BCtzpunkt%20Nordrhein-Westfalen)*.

The city is home to many sports clubs, iconic athletes and annually organises several world-renowned sporting events, such as the [Ruhrmarathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhrmarathon?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Sparkassen Chess-Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Sparkassen_Chess_Meeting).

### Football



[Signal Iduna Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Iduna_Park), the home stadium of [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) club [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), is the biggest stadium in Germany.

Dortmund is home to the sports club [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), one of the most successful clubs in German [football](10568.md) history. Borussia Dortmund are former [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) champions, most recently in 2011–12.[^cite_note-109] Borussia Dortmund won the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) and the [Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Cup_\(1960%E2%80%932004\)) in 1997, as well as the UEFA Cup Winners' Cup in 1966. 'Die Borussen' are eight-time German Champions and have won five [German Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFB-Pokal). Borussia Dortmund play at [Westfalenstadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenstadion), currently known as Signal Iduna Park. It was built for the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) and also hosted some matches of the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup). It is Germany's largest football stadium, with a maximum capacity of 81,359 spectators.[^cite_note-110]
