**Ezhou** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): [鄂州](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%84%82%E5%B7%9E)), also [known by another name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_postal_romanization) **O-chou** is a [prefecture-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_city) in eastern [Hubei](83319.md) Province, China. As of the 2020 census, the city had a population of 1,079,353, of which 695,697 lived in the core [Echeng District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echeng). The Ezhou - [Huanggang](4088485.md) built-up (*or metro*) area was home to 1,152,559 inhabitants made of the Echeng and Huangzhou, Huanggang Districts.

## Geography

Ezhou lies on the southern bank of the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) east of the southern section of [Wuchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang,_Hubei), across the river from the city of [Huanggang](4088485.md), to which it is connected by the [Ehuang Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehuang_Bridge). Lying between the cities of Wuhan and [Huangshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangshi), Ezhou has a relatively small area of 1,504 square kilometers (581 sq mi).[^cite_note-4]

There are many lakes in Ezhou, including the [Liangzi Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangzi_Lake) in [Liangzihu District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangzihu_District) and [Yanglan Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanglan_Lake?action=edit\&redlink=1), along with more than 133 lakes and pools. The city is the origin of [Wuchang Bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_Bream) and as a result is nicknamed "city of one hundred lakes" and "the land of fish and rice".

## History



A 1915 map showing a small section of Wuchang (today's Ezhou), across the river from Hwang-Chow (Huangzhou), the modern central urban area of [Huanggang](4088485.md), along with a much larger area further west (adjacent to [Hanyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanyang_District) and [Hankou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankou))

The name "Ezhou" dates to the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) (206 BCE – 220 CE) and derives from the nearby ancient [Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty) (1046–256 BCE) vassal [State of E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_\(state\)). Although the administrative seat of the city changed several times, the name remained unchanged until the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) era (220−280) at which time it became the capital of the state of [Eastern Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu) ruled by [Sun Quan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Quan) and was renamed "Wuchang". This name remained in use as late as 1915, which proved confusing to travelers, as it was also applied to the much larger city of Wuchang, itself shortly to become part of Wuhan.[^cite_note-7]

Throughout China's history, Ezhou has remained an important city politically, economically, and militarily with its strategic position along the middle part of the Yangtze River. It is also an important location in the history of the [Pure Land Sect of Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Land_Buddhism).

## Economy



Ezhou television company tower

Ezhou has six ports on the Yangtze including one than can handle up to 10,000-tonne (9,800-long-ton; 11,000-short-ton) vessels. Many important rail lines and national highways cross the area. This infrastructure make it a major logistics and distribution center. Important industries include metallurgy, construction materials, textiles, chemicals, and machinery.

This city is also known to host the world's largest [pig farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_farm) that is constructed in a 26-storey building where [pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig) get fed, then eventually slaughtered to distribute pork to restaurants all across the country and abroad.[^cite_note-10]

## Transportation

Ezhou is served by the E-Huang Expressway, [China National Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highways) [106](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_106) and [316](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_316), the [Wuhan–Jiujiang Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan%E2%80%93Jiujiang_Railway), the Wuhan–Huangshi intercity railway of the [Wuhan Metropolitan Area Intercity Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_Metropolitan_Area_Intercity_Railway) and [Ezhou Huahu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezhou_Huahu_International_Airport). Ezhou has [two bridges over the Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_bridges_and_tunnels).

[Ezhou railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezhou_railway_station) ([30°21′48″N 114°51′38″E  /  30.363343°N 114.860499°E  / 30.363343; 114.860499](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Ezhou\&params=30.363343_N_114.860499_E_)), located southwest of downtown, has an interesting layout, somewhat similar to that of the [Secaucus Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secaucus_Junction) in [New Jersey](21648.md). Until 2014, there was just one rail line there: the north–south [Wuhan–Jiujiang Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan%E2%80%93Jiujiang_Railway), which is served by a large number of trains traveling from Wuhan to [Nanchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang) and points further east and south (Fujian, Zhejiang, etc.). Until 2013, there were no commuter trains in per se in Hubei, but it took only about one hour (usually, with a stop at [Huarong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huarong_District)) to reach one of Wuhan's train stations (such as [Wuchang railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_railway_station)) train station from Ezhou using any of those trains. The distance from the Ezhou railway station to Wuchang by rail is 82 kilometers (51 mi).[^cite_note-11] In 2014, the Wuhan-Huangshi intercity railway of the [Wuhan Metropolitan Area Intercity Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_Metropolitan_Area_Intercity_Railway), the region's new commuter line system, was opened. This line, which runs east–west in the area, has crossed the Wuhan–Jiujiang Railway tracks at the right angle just south of the previously existing Ezhou station. To accommodate the new line, the Ezhou Station was expanded in 2013–2014, with platforms constructed on the new commuter line, providing a possibility for an easy transfer between the two lines. This is said to be the first station in China with this layout.[^cite_note-12]

The trains on the commuter line travel to [Wuhan Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_Railway_Station) (56 km or 35 mi, 30–40 min from Ezhou).

The commuter line has one more station in Ezhou, farther to the east. It is Ezhou East, 64 km (40 mi) from Wuhan Railway Station.

## Tourism



Top [Tourist City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_city) of China Ezhou Statue

Ezhou has several tourism sites, including the West Hill (which is in the center of the city), and the nearby Lotus Hill. There are also three lakes, [Liangzi Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangzi_Lake), [Yanglan Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanglan_Lake?action=edit\&redlink=1) (洋澜湖) and Honglian Lake.
