**Boven Digoel Regency** is an inland [regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_\(Indonesia\)) (*kabupaten*) in the northeastern part of the Indonesian [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) of [South Papua](59803872.md). It was split off from [Merauke Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merauke_Regency) (of which it used to be a part) on 12 November 2002. It is bordered to the south by the residual Merauke Regency, to the west by [Mappi Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mappi_Regency), and to the north by the province of [Highland Papua](71191966.md). At the same time, to the east lies the international border with [Papua New Guinea](22943.md).

The regency covers an area of 27,108.29 km<sup>2</sup> (10,466.57 sq mi), and the total population was 55,784 at the 2010 Census[^cite_note-Biro_Pusat_Statistik_2011-2] and 64,285 at the 2020 Census;[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-3] the official estimate as of mid-2024 was 71,997 (comprising 38,130 males and 33,867 females).[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2025-1] The administrative centre is the town of [Tanah Merah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanahmerah) (or Persatuan *kampung*) in the Mandobo District.

## History



Communist prisoners to Boven Digoel in 1927 (1927)

In the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) era, the present Boven Digoel Regency was known as *Digul Atas* (Upper Digul), located on the banks of the Digul River.

[Boven-Digoel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boven-Digoel_concentration_camp) was a Dutch prison camp in the Dutch East Indies at the headwaters of the Digul River, where Indonesian nationalists and communists were interned between 1928 and 1942. Initially set to accommodate prisoners of the [1926 revolt led by the Communist Party of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Indonesia#1926_revolt), Boven-Digoel later was used as an exile for the national movement figures with a recorded number of prisoners of 1,308 people. Among the figures exiled, were [Mohammad Hatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hatta), [Sutan Sjahrir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutan_Sjahrir), [Sayuti Melik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayuti_Melik), and [Marco Kartodikromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Kartodikromo).



Statue in rememberence of Mohammad Hatta near the airport and former prison building.

When the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) broke out and [Japan occupied Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies), Boven-Digoel prisoners were evacuated by the Dutch to Australia. The transfer was based on concerns that the prisoners would rebel if they remained at Boven-Digoel. It was hoped that the Indonesians brought to Australia would help the Dutch. It turned out that these political prisoners influenced the Australian trade union to boycott the Dutch ships that landed in the country. After the Allies won, the prisoners were returned to their original places in Indonesia.

The [camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boven-Digoel_concentration_camp) was reused to imprison Indonesian nationalists from Papua during [the West New Guinea dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_New_Guinea_dispute) such as [J.A. Dimara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Abraham_Dimara), Petrus Korwa, and Hanoch Rumbrar.[^cite_note-Materay_2020_pp._1–18-4][^cite_note-Sitompul_2020-5]
