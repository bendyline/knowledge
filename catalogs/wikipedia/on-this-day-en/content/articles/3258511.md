**Western Ukraine** or **West Ukraine** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Західна Україна, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Zakhidna Ukrayina*, IPA: [\[ˈzɑxidnɐ ʊkrɐˈjinɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian))[^cite_note-term-6] refers to the western territories of [Ukraine](31750.md). There is no universally accepted definition of the territory's boundaries, but the contemporary Ukrainian administrative regions ([oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblasts)) of [Chernivtsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi_Oblast), [Ivano-Frankivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk_Oblast), [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Oblast), [Ternopil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast) and [Zakarpattia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarpattia_Oblast) (which were part of the former [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire)) are typically included. In addition, [Volyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volyn_Oblast) and [Rivne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivne_Oblast) oblasts (parts of the territory annexed from the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) during its [Third Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland)) are also usually included. In modern sources, [Khmelnytskyi Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytskyi_Oblast) is often included because of its geographical, linguistic and cultural association with Western Ukraine, although this cannot be confirmed from a historical and political point of view. It includes several historical regions such as [Carpathian Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Ruthenia), [Halychyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ruthenia) including [Pokuttia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokuttia) (the eastern portion of [Eastern Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Galicia)), most of [Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia), northern [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina) and the [Hertsa region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertsa_region), and [Podolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolia). Western Ukraine is sometimes considered to include areas of eastern Volhynia, Podolia, and the small northern portion of [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia).

The area of Western Ukraine was ruled by various polities, including the [Principality of Galicia–Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia), which became part of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth), but also the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) and [Principality of Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia); it would then variously come under rule of the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic), the [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania), and finally the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (via the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)) in 1939 and 1940 following the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) and the [Soviet occupation of Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina), with the borders finalized after the end of [World War II](32927.md). After the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), it became part of the independent Ukrainian state.

Western Ukraine is known for its exceptional natural and cultural heritage, several sites of which are on the [List of World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Ukraine). Architecturally, it includes the [fortress of Kamianets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamianets-Podilskyi_Castle), the [Old Town of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_\(Lviv\)), the former [Residence of Bukovinian and Dalmatian Metropolitans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_of_Bukovinian_and_Dalmatian_Metropolitans), the [Tserkvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_Tserkvas_of_the_Carpathian_Region_in_Poland_and_Ukraine), the [Khotyn Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khotyn_Fortress) and the [Pochayiv Lavra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pochayiv_Lavra). Its landscapes and natural sites also represent a major tourist asset for the region, combining the mountain landscapes of the [Ukrainian Carpathians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Carpathians) and those of the [Podolian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolian_Upland). These include Mount [Hoverla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoverla), the highest point in Ukraine, [Optymistychna Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optymistychna_Cave), the largest in Europe, [Bukovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovel) Ski Resort, [Synevyr National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synevyr_National_Nature_Park), [Carpathian National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_National_Park) or the [Uzh National Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzh_National_Nature_Park) protecting part of the [primary forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_and_Primeval_Beech_Forests_of_the_Carpathians_and_Other_Regions_of_Europe) included in the [Carpathian Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Biosphere_Reserve).[^cite_note-UNESCO-7]

The city of [Lviv](49968.md) is the main cultural center of the region and was the historical capital of the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia. Other important cities are [Chernivtsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi), [Rivne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivne), [Ivano-Frankivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk), [Ternopil](512798.md), [Lutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutsk), [Khmelnytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytskyi,_Ukraine) and [Uzhhorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod).

## History

See also: [General Government of Galicia and Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government_of_Galicia_and_Bukovina)



"Moneta Rvssiє" coined in 1382 based on [groschen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groschen)

Western Ukraine, takes its roots from the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia, a successor of Kievan Rus' formed in 1199 after the weakening of Kievan Rus' and attacks from the Golden Horde.

Following the 14th century [Galicia–Volhynia Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia_Wars), most of the region was transferred to the [Crown of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Poland) under [Casimir the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_the_Great), who received the lands legally by a downward agreement in 1340 after his nephew's death, [Bolesław-Jerzy II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw-Jerzy_II). The eastern [Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia) and most of [Podolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolia) was added to the territory of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) by [Lubart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubart).

The territory of [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina) was part of [Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia) since its formation by voivode [Dragoș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drago%C8%99,_Voivode_of_Moldavia), who was departed by the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), during the 14th century.

The territory of [Zakarpattya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarpattia_Oblast) was the part of the Principality later [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), [Austria Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), [Czekhoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) from 9 till 20 century. [^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

After the 18th century [partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) ([Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth)), the territory was split between the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) and the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). The modern south-western part of Western Ukraine became the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria), after 1804 crownland of the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire). Its northern flank with the cities of [Lutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutsk) and [Rivne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivne) was acquired in 1795 by Imperial Russia following the third and [final partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland). Throughout its existence [Russian Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Partition) was marred with violence and intimidation, beginning with the [1794 massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_Praga), imperial land-theft and the deportations of the [November](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising) and [January Uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising).[^cite_note-Davies-60-11] By contrast, the [Austrian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Partition) with its [Sejm of the Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Land) in the cities of [Lviv](49968.md) and [Stanyslaviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk) (Ivano-Frankivsk) was freer politically perhaps because it had [a lot less to offer economically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Austrian_Galicia).[^cite_note-Crowley-12] Imperial Austria did not persecute Ukrainian organizations.[^cite_note-SerhyYUBoaMN-13] In 1846, the Austrian government used the [peasant uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Peasant_Uprising_of_1846) to decimate Polish nobles, who were organising an uprising against Austria.[^cite_note-14] In later years, Austria-Hungary de facto encouraged the existence of Ukrainian political organizations in order to counterbalance the influence of [Polish culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_culture) in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)). The southern half of West Ukraine remained under Austrian administration until the collapse of the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) at the end of World War One in 1918.[^cite_note-SerhyYUBoaMN-13]

Further information on West Ukraine's sociopolitical background: [Austrian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Partition) and [Polish culture in the Interbellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_culture_in_the_Interbellum)

In 1775, following the Russo-Turkish [Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_K%C3%BC%C3%A7%C3%BCk_Kaynarca), Moldavia lost to the Habsburg monarchy its northwestern part, which became known as [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina), and remained under Austrian administration until 1918.

Further information: [Duchy of Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bukovina)

### Interbellum and World War II

See also: [West Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukrainian_People's_Republic) and [Soviet annexation of Eastern Galicia and Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_annexation_of_Eastern_Galicia_and_Volhynia)

Following the defeat of [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic) (1918) in the [Soviet–Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Ukrainian_War) of 1921, Western Ukraine was partitioned by the [Treaty of Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Riga) between Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, and the Soviet Russia acting on behalf of the Soviet Belarus and the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) with capital in [Kharkiv](2788558.md). The [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) gained control over the entire territory of the short-lived Ukrainian People's Republic east of the border with Poland.[^cite_note-endSkoro-15] In the [Interbellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbellum) most of the territory of today's Western Ukraine belonged to the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic). Territories such as [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina) and [Carpatho-Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpatho-Ukraine) belonged to [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), respectively.

See also: [Soviet invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland), [The Holocaust in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Ukraine), [Massacres of Poles in Volhynia and Eastern Galicia](464698.md), and [Soviet occupation of Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina)

At the onset of [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) by Nazi Germany, the region became occupied by Nazi Germany in 1941. The southern half of West Ukraine was incorporated into the [semi-colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-colony) *[Distrikt Galizien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distrikt_Galizien)* (District of Galicia) created on August 1, 1941 (Document No. 1997-PS of July 17, 1941 by Adolf Hitler) with headquarters in [Chełm Lubelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m), bordering district of [General Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government) to the west. Its northern part ([Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia)) was assigned to the *[Reichskommissariat Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine)* formed in September 1941. Notably, the District of Galicia was a separate administrative unit from the actual *Reichskommissariat Ukraine* with capital in [Rivne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivne). They were not connected with each other politically for Nazi Germans.[^cite_note-Bewersdorf-16] The division was administrative and conditional, in his book "From Putyvl to the Carpathian" [Sydir Kovpak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydir_Kovpak) never mentioned about any border-like divisions. Bukovina was controlled by the Nazi-allied Kingdom of Romania.

### Post-War

See also: [Polish population transfers (1944–1946)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_population_transfers_\(1944%E2%80%931946\))

After the defeat of Germany in World War II, in May 1945 the Soviet Union incorporated all territories of current Western Ukraine into the Ukrainian SSR.[^cite_note-endSkoro-15] Between 1944 and 1946, a [population exchange between Poland and Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Poland_and_Soviet_Ukraine) occurred in which all ethnic Poles and Jews who had Polish citizenship before September 17, 1939 (date of the Soviet Invasion of Poland) were transferred to post-war Poland and all ethnic Ukrainians to the Ukrainian SSR, in accordance with the resolutions of the Yalta and Tehran conferences and the plans about the new Poland–Ukraine border.[^cite_note-17]

#### Recent history

See also: [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)

During the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, Russia attacked Ukrainian military facility near the city of [Lviv](49968.md),[^cite_note-18] in Western Ukraine with cruise missiles. Later in March Russia performed missile attacks on oil depots in [Lviv](49968.md),[^cite_note-19] [Dubno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubno)[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] and [Lutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutsk).[^cite_note-22]

## Divisions



Souvenir from [Bukovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovel)



The [Carpathians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathians) in the [Zakarpattia Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarpattia_Oblast) are the highest mountain range in Ukraine

Western Ukraine includes such lands as [Zakarpattia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Ruthenia), [Volyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volyn), [Halychyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halychyna) ([Prykarpattia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prykarpattia), [Pokuttia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokuttia)), [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina), [Polissia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polissia), and [Podillia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podillia).

The history of Western Ukraine is closely associated with the history of the following lands:

- Easternmost [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina), historical [region of Central Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_regions_of_Central_Europe) in official use since 1775, controlled by the [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) after World War I, and half of it [ceded to the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina) in 1940 (reconfirmed by [Paris Peace Treaties, 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Treaties,_1947))

- [Eastern Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Galicia) ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): *Halychyna*), once a small kingdom with Lodomeria (1914), province of the Austrian Empire until the dissolution of Austria-Hungary in 1918. See also: crownland of the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria)

- [Red Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ruthenia) since medieval times in the area known today as Eastern Galicia.

- [West Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukrainian_People's_Republic) declared in late 1918 until early 1919 and claiming half of Galicia with mostly Polish city dwellers (historical sense).

- [Carpatho-Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpatho-Ukraine) region within Czechoslovakia (1939) under Hungarian control until the Nazi occupation of Hungary in 1944.

- [General Government of Galicia and Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government_of_Galicia_and_Bukovina) captured from Austria-Hungary during World War I.

- [Ținutul Suceava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%9Ainutul_Suceava) ([Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania))

- [Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia), historic region straddling Poland, Ukraine, and Belarus to the north. The alternate name for the region today is Lodomeria after the city of Volodymyr. See also: Polish unofficial term [Kresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kresy) (Borderlands, 1918–1939) that includes the [West Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Belarus) as well as Volhynia.

- [Zakarpattia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Ruthenia) or Carpathian Ruthenia presently in the Zakarpattia Oblast of western Ukraine.

## Cultural characteristics

### Differences with rest of Ukraine

> "Perhaps, if Ukraine did not have its western regions, with [Lviv](49968.md) at the centre, it would be easy to turn the country into another [Belarus](3457.md). But Galicia ([Halychyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halychyna)) and [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina), which became part of [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine) under the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact), brought to the country a rebellious and free spirit."

— [Andrey Kurkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kurkov) in an opinion piece about [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) on [BBC News Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News_Online) (28 January 2014)

[^cite_note-23]

[Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) is the dominant language in the region. Back in the schools of the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) learning [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) was mandatory; currently, in modern Ukraine, in schools with Ukrainian as the language of instruction, classes in Russian and in other minority languages are offered.[^cite_note-SerhyYUBoaMN-13][^cite_note-24]

In terms of religion, the majority of adherents share the [Byzantine Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Rite) of [Christianity](5211.md) as in the rest of Ukraine, but due to the region escaping the 1920s and 1930s [Soviet persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Soviet_Union), a notably greater church adherence and belief in religion's role in society is present. Due to the complex [post-independence religious confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Christianity_in_Ukraine) of several church groups and their adherents, the historical influence played a key role in shaping the present loyalty of Western Ukraine's faithful. In Galician provinces, the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church) has the strongest following in the country, and the largest share of property and faithful. In the remaining regions: Volhynia, Bukovina and Transcarpathia the [Orthodoxy](10186.md) is prevalent. Outside of Western Ukraine the greatest in terms of Church property, clergy, and according to some estimates, faithful, is the [Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_\(Moscow_Patriarchate\)). In the listed regions (and in particular among the Orthodox faithful in Galicia), this position is notably weaker, as the main rivals, the [Ukrainian Orthodox Church – Kyiv Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_%E2%80%93_Kyiv_Patriarchate) and the [Ukrainian Autocephalous Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Autocephalous_Orthodox_Church), have a far greater influence. Within the lands of the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church), the largest Eastern Catholic Church, priests' children often became priests and married within their social group, establishing a [tightly-knit hereditary caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_clergy_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-Subtelny2009-25]

Noticeable cultural differences in the region (compared with the rest of Ukraine especially [Southern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine) and [Eastern Ukraine](13065940.md)) are more "negative views" on the [Russian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language_in_Ukraine)[^cite_note-RatingJuly12-26][^cite_note-27] and on [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)[^cite_note-KIISS1313-28] and more "positive views" on [Ukrainian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_nationalism).[^cite_note-29] A higher percentage of voters in Western Ukraine supported Ukrainian independence in the [1991 Ukrainian independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum) than in the rest of the country.[^cite_note-ESlower-30][^cite_note-31]



Kyiv International Institute of Sociology (KIIS) geographical division of Ukraine used in their polls.

In a poll conducted by [Kyiv International Institute of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_International_Institute_of_Sociology) in the first half of February 2014 0.7% of polled in West Ukraine believed "Ukraine and [Russia](25391.md) must unite into a single state", nationwide this percentage was 12.5. The Russian-occupied parts of the [Luhansk](494904.md) and [Donetsk](494889.md) regions of Ukraine were not polled.[^cite_note-32]

During [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Ukraine) voters of Western [oblasts](994124.md) (provinces) vote mostly for parties ([Our Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Ukraine%E2%80%93People's_Self-Defense_Bloc), [Batkivshchyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batkivshchyna))[^cite_note-33] and presidential candidates ([Viktor Yushchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko), [Yulia Tymoshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Tymoshenko)) with a [pro-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Western) and state reform [platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_platform).[^cite_note-EWparties-34][^cite_note-Umland-35][^cite_note-36] Of the regions of Western Ukraine, Galicia tends to be the most pro-Western and pro-nationalist area. Volhynia's politics are similar, though not as nationalist or as pro-Western as Galicia's. Bukovina-Chernivtsi's electoral politics are more mixed and tempered by the region's significant Romanian minority. Finally, Zakarpattia's electoral politics tend to be more competitive, similar to a Central Ukrainian oblast. This is due to the region's distinct historical and cultural identity as well as the significant Hungarian and Romanian minorities. The politics in the region was dominated by such Ukrainian parties as [Andriy Baloha's Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriy_Baloha's_Team), [Social Democratic Party of Ukraine (united)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Ukraine_\(united\)), Congress of Carpathian Ruthenians led by the [Rusyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyns) [Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_\(Moscow_Patriarchate\)) bishop [Dimitry Sydor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitry_Sydor) and [KMKSZ – Hungarian Party in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Hungarians_of_Ukraine_\(KMKSZ\)).

Percentage of Ukrainians in each oblast (2001 census)

According to a 2016 survey of [religion in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Ukraine) held by the [Razumkov Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razumkov_Center), approximately 93% of the population of western Ukraine declared to be believers, while 0.9% declared to non-believers, and 0.2% declared to [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism).

Of the total population, 97.7% declared to be [Christians](18337522.md) (57.0% [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox), 30.9% members of the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church), 4.3% simply Christians, 3.9% members of various [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) churches, and 1.6% [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) [Catholics](606848.md)), by far more than in all other regions of Ukraine, while 0.2% were [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_in_Ukraine). Non-believers and other believers not identifying with any of the listed major religious institutions constituted about 2.1% of the population.[^cite_note-Razumkov2016-37]
