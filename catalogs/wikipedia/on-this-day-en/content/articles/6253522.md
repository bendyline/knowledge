**Jeffrey Edward Epstein**[^cite_note-6] (January 20, 1953 – August 10, 2019) was an American financier and [child sex offender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_sex_offender). He began his career as a math teacher at the [Dalton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_School) in [New York City](645042.md), before entering the banking and finance sector. Over several decades, he made much of his fortune providing tax and estate services to billionaires, and cultivated an [elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite) social circle of [prominent individuals](82113328.md). In 2008, he was convicted of soliciting a minor for prostitution, and was indicted in 2019 for [sex trafficking](2101268.md) minors in the 2000s. [He died](61499571.md) in custody awaiting his trial and his death was ruled a [suicide](16831059.md).

In 2005, police in [Palm Beach, Florida](109617.md), began investigating Epstein after a woman reported that her 14-year-old stepdaughter had been paid by Epstein to strip and massage him. Investigators identified 36 girls between the ages of 14 and 17 with similar accounts of [sexual abuse](18721790.md). Prosecutors had concerns about taking him to trial, citing victim reluctance to testify and witness credibility. This influenced a now-controversial plea deal negotiation, under which Epstein [pleaded guilty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleaded_guilty) to state charges of [procuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procuring_\(prostitution\)) a minor for prostitution, and [soliciting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliciting) a prostitute. He was registered as a [sex offender](2957039.md), and served 13 months in jail with work release.

Following Epstein's conviction, victims pursued lawsuits and media scrutiny grew. A 2018 *Miami Herald* article profiled victim stories, and argued that Epstein had evaded justice under a lenient plea deal. This prompted federal investigators in New York to reinvestigate additional charges, and Epstein was indicted in July 2019 on charges of sex trafficking minors between 2002 and 2005. Epstein died in his jail cell on August 10; his death was ruled a suicide by hanging. His death is the subject of [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_didn't_kill_himself). Epstein maintained a long association with socialite [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md), who was convicted in 2021 for sex trafficking girls to him.

At the time of his death, Epstein's estate was valued at \$600 million, from which hundreds of millions have been paid to at least 200 women in legal settlements. Additional settlements of \$290 million from [JP Morgan](231001.md), and \$75 million from [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank), followed lawsuits which alleged these banks had enabled Epstein's abuse by retaining him as a client.[^cite_note-7]

Epstein was a friend or acquaintance of many public figures.[^cite_note-8] The [Epstein files](80415168.md), a collection of documents which has been partially released as part of the [Epstein Files Transparency Act](81594904.md), provide a public glimpse into the vast network he had cultivated over the late 20th and early 21st centuries.[^cite_note-9]

## Early life



Epstein's childhood neighborhood of [Sea Gate, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Gate,_Brooklyn)

Jeffrey Edward Epstein was born on January 20, 1953, in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn),[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] a [borough of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_New_York_City). His parents, Pauline "Paula" Stolofsky and Seymour George Epstein, were of [Lithuanian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litvaks) and [Polish Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland) ancestry, respectively, and had married in 1952 shortly before his birth.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-Volscho-2019-14] Pauline worked as a school aide and was a homemaker.[^cite_note-Volscho-2019-14][^cite_note-Daly-2019-15][^cite_note-The_Jerusalem_Post-2019-16] Seymour worked for the [New York City Department of Parks and Recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Parks_and_Recreation) as a [groundskeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundskeeper) and gardener.[^cite_note-Volscho-2019-14][^cite_note-The_Jerusalem_Post-2019-16][^cite_note-17]

Epstein was the older of two siblings; he and his brother [Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Epstein_\(property_developer\)) grew up in the neighborhood of [Sea Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Gate,_Brooklyn),[^cite_note-18] a then working-class gated community in [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island).[^cite_note-Daly-2019-15][^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19] A childhood friend described Paula as "a wonderful mother and homemaker",[^cite_note-20] and neighbors remembered the parents as being quiet and humble.[^cite_note-Daly-2019-15] Epstein attended local public schools, first Public School 188 and later nearby [Mark Twain Junior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain_Junior_High_School), and often earned money by tutoring classmates. Acquaintances considered Epstein "sweet and generous" although "quiet and nerdy", and nicknamed him "Eppy". Of Epstein, a female friend later said, "He was just an average boy, very smart in math, slightly overweight, freckles, always smiling."[^cite_note-Daly-2019-15]

In 1967, Epstein attended the National Music Camp at [Interlochen Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlochen_Center_for_the_Arts).[^cite_note-21] He began playing the [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano) when he was five, and was regarded as a talented musician by friends.[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22] He graduated in 1969 from [Lafayette High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_High_School_\(New_York_City\)) at age 16, having skipped two grades. Later that year, he attended advanced math classes at [Cooper Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union) until he changed colleges in 1971. From September 1971, he attended the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), where he studied [mathematical physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physiology), but left without receiving a degree in June 1974.[^cite_note-Volscho-2019-14][^cite_note-Thomas-2002-23]

## Career

### Dalton School (1974–1976)

In September 1974, at age 21, Epstein started working as a [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics) teacher at the [Dalton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_School) on the [Upper East Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_East_Side) of [Manhattan](45470.md). The school's newspaper announced the hiring of Epstein.[^cite_note-Volscho-2019-14][^cite_note-Baker-2019-24] [Donald Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Barr), who served as the [headmaster](833159.md) until June 1974,[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] was known to have made several unconventional recruitments at the time, although it is unclear whether he had a direct role in hiring Epstein.[^cite_note-Baker-2019-24][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-Ferreira-2019-29] Three months after Barr's departure, Epstein began to teach at the school, despite his lack of credentials.[^cite_note-Ferreira-2019-29][^cite_note-Baker-2019-24]

One parent recalled his son's belief that Epstein was "popular with students and the school's young female staff members".[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19] In 2019, several former students alleged that Epstein paid inappropriate attention to female students, and that he once showed up at a student party, although none said he made physical contact with them.[^cite_note-Baker-2019-24]

In early 1976, Epstein attended an art event where he met a Dalton father who worked on [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_street_trader). The man thought Epstein was "wasting his time" at Dalton, given his knowledge of mathematics, and rang [Alan Greenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_C._Greenberg) – a senior executive at [Bear Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Stearns) – to recommend a potential hire.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19] The timing was suitable, as Epstein was soon dismissed from Dalton due to poor performance.[^cite_note-Baker-2019-24][^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

### Bear Stearns (1976–1981)

In 1976, Epstein was interviewed at [Bear Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Stearns), where he reportedly impressed Alan Greenberg, who preferred hiring men of modest backgrounds with "a deep desire to become rich." Epstein met senior trader Michael Tennenbaum, who recalled Epstein as "a hell of salesman".[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19] Epstein began at the firm as a low-level junior assistant to a floor trader.[^cite_note-Metcalf-2019-30][^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

In late 1976, Bear Stearns discovered that Epstein had falsely claimed to have two university degrees on his résumé. In Tennenbaum's recollection, Epstein calmly admitted to it, stating that "nobody would give me a chance" without degrees. Tennenbaum agreed to give Epstein another chance.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

Epstein swiftly moved up to become an [options trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Options_trading), working in the special products division,[^cite_note-Thomas-2002-23][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] and in 1980, Epstein became a [limited partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_partner) at 27 years old with a salary of \$200,000 per year (over \$800,000 in 2025 dollars).[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

&#x20;Epstein began advising clients on [tax mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_mitigation) strategies. [Jimmy Cayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cayne), a [senior partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_partner) at the firm (and its later CEO) held Epstein in high regard, and introduced him to many of Bear Stearns' wealthiest clients. Epstein appealed to clients; he was reportedly charismatic and offered "complex trading strategies that could save ultrawealthy clients huge amounts in taxes". Epstein began having regular lunches with CEOs and establishing a network of contacts, which according to Tennenbaum, is "what really catapulted" his later success. Alan Greenberg would come to view Epstein as his protégé.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]



Epstein was *[Cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitan_\(magazine\))* magazine's "bachelor of the month" in the July 1980 issue

In this period, Epstein was regularly flying to [Palm Beach](109617.md) to visit young women. *[Cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitan_\(magazine\))* named Epstein their "bachelor of the month", and encouraged interested women to write to him. On one work trip, Epstein purchased \$10,000 of jewelry and clothing for a girlfriend and charged it to Bear Stearns; although reportedly he faced no consequences.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

In 1981, trouble arose for Epstein when the firm investigated a \$15,000 personal loan he made to a former classmate, which may have breached federal brokerage rules. The firm also questioned [initial public offering](63879.md) shares held by his girlfriend.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

Following an internal investigation, Bear Stearns fined Epstein \$2,500 and placed him on a two-month suspension. Epstein denied wrongdoing, and resigned from the firm, ending his five-year tenure. His experience and contacts at Bear Stearns later helped him establish himself as an independent financial adviser on Wall Street.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19] He remained close to Cayne and Greenberg, and was a client of Bear Stearns until [its collapse in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Stearns#Lead-up_to_the_failure_%E2%80%93_increasing_exposure_to_subprime_mortgages).[^cite_note-Metcalf-2019-30]

### Advisor and financial troubleshooter (1981–1987)

In August 1981, Epstein founded his own financial consulting firm, Intercontinental Assets Group Inc. (IAG).[^cite_note-Herron-2019-33] While at Bear Sterns, Epstein started a relationship with Paula Heil, a well-connected saleswoman and former Miss [Indianapolis](57707.md). In the early 1980s, Epstein and Heil began taking trips to England. She introduced him to a wealthy defense contractor, Douglas Leese, who had connections in the British government and arms industry. Epstein began spending time in the Leese family's social circle, tutoring Leese's younger son Julian, and accompanying Leese to meetings with prominent figures while serving as a consultant.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19] Epstein traveled multiple times between the United States, Europe, and the Middle East.[^cite_note-Connor-2019-34][^cite_note-Winter-2019-35] Leese reportedly introduced Epstein to the financier [Steven Hoffenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Hoffenberg).[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22]

After several years, Epstein's relationship with Leese deteriorated. Leese accused Epstein of misusing his expense account for personal spending, including Concorde flights and luxury hotel stays.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

Epstein returned his focus to New York where he partnered with the lawyer [John Stanley Pottinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stanley_Pottinger),[^cite_note-36] renting office space on Central Park South, and pitching tax-avoidance strategies to wealthy clients. Epstein sourced clients through former Bear Stearns colleagues, including the businessman Michael Stroll. In 1982, Stroll invested \$450,000 with Epstein in what was presented as a [crude oil](23195.md) deal. A legal dispute followed and Stroll alleged that Epstein had promised but failed to return the money. The case ended in 1993 when a court ruled Epstein was not personally liable on technical grounds.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

In the early 1980s, Epstein reportedly bragged to friends that he was a "[bounty hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_hunter)" who assisted clients in recovering [embezzled](209411.md) funds.[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22][^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19] In 1982, Epstein was introduced to the Spanish actress and heiress [Ana Obregón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Obreg%C3%B3n) through a mutual friend, and the pair began dating. The Obregón family, and several other wealthy Spanish families, soon hired Epstein after losing millions of dollars during the collapse of Drysdale Government Securities, owing to fraudulent accounting practices.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19][^cite_note-37] Epstein recruited a federal prosecutor, Bob Gold, and the pair spent a year trying to find their missing funds that Drysdale had shifted to offshore bank accounts. In 1984, Epstein discovered his clients' funds in a [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands) branch of a Canadian bank. He hired a jet and had the branch manager hand over bond certificates for the funds. Epstein returned the funds to his Spanish clients, and they rewarded him significantly, making him a millionaire.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

Reportedly, another client that Epstein helped recover stolen funds was the Saudi Arabian businessman [Adnan Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adnan_Khashoggi), who he met through Douglas Leese.[^cite_note-38] Khashoggi was a middleman in transferring American [weapons from Israel to Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel's_role_in_the_Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_war) as part of the [Iran–Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair).[^cite_note-39] During this period, Epstein possessed an [Austrian passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_passport) under a [false name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym). The passport showed his place of residence as [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-Winter-2019-35] According to his lawyers, Epstein acquired the fake passport for security purposes, allowing him to conceal his Jewish identity while traveling in Arab countries, and that Epstein never needed to use it.[^cite_note-AP_and_ToI_Staff-2019-40]

By mid 1980s, Epstein was a valuable client to his former employer Bear Sterns. Epstein was calling his former manager Clark Schubach to place trades on stocks, bonds and options. Epstein also pursued a sexual relationship with Schubach's assistant, a 23 year old Patricia Schmidt, who he tasked with conducting research in the Bear Stearns library on potential clients.[^cite_note-Enrich-2025a-19]

### Towers Financial Corporation (1987–1993)



Epstein shaking hands with President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) at the [White House](33057.md), September 1993 (with [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md) in the background on the right)

[Steven Hoffenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Hoffenberg) hired Epstein in 1987 as a consultant for [Towers Financial Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towers_Financial_Corporation),[^cite_note-41] a [collection agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collection_agency) that bought debts people owed to hospitals, banks, and phone companies.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-Stewart-2019-43] Hoffenberg set Epstein up in offices in the [Villard Houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villard_Houses) in Manhattan and paid him US\$25,000 per month for his consulting work (equivalent to \$71,000 in 2025).[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22] Hoffenberg and Epstein then refashioned themselves as [corporate raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_raid) using Towers Financial as their raiding vessel. One of Epstein's first assignments was to attempt, unsuccessfully, to take over [Pan American World Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_World_Airways) in 1987. A similar unsuccessful bid in 1988 was made to take over [Emery Air Freight Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emery_Worldwide). During this period, Hoffenberg and Epstein worked closely together and traveled everywhere on Hoffenberg's private jet.[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22]

In 1993, Towers Financial Corporation imploded when it was exposed as one of the biggest [Ponzi schemes](47185.md) in American history, losing over US\$450 million of its investors' money (equivalent to \$1 billion in 2025).[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22] In court documents, Hoffenberg claimed that Epstein was intimately involved in the scheme.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] Epstein left the company by 1989 and was never charged for involvement in the massive [investor fraud](2902209.md) committed. It is unknown if Epstein acquired any stolen funds from the Towers Ponzi scheme.[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22]

### J. Epstein & Company (1988–2019)



Les Wexner's *[Limitless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limitless_\(luxury_yacht\))* moored in [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca)

In 1988, while Epstein was still consulting for Hoffenberg, he founded his financial management firm, J. Epstein & Company.[^cite_note-Stewart-2019-43][^cite_note-Herron-2019-33] According to Epstein, the company managed the assets of clients with more than US\$1 billion in net worth, although others have expressed [skepticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skepticism) about that number, as he was secretive of the clients that he took.[^cite_note-Thomas-2002-23]

The only publicly known billionaire client of Epstein was [Leslie Wexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Wexner), chairman and CEO of [L Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_Brands) (formerly The Limited, Inc.) and [Victoria's Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria's_Secret).[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22][^cite_note-46] In 1986, Epstein met Wexner through their mutual acquaintances, insurance executive Robert Meister and his wife, in [Palm Beach](109617.md). A year later, Epstein became Wexner's financial adviser and served as his right-hand man. Within the year, Epstein had sorted out Wexner's entangled finances.[^cite_note-Thomas-2002-23][^cite_note-Alexander-2019-47] In July 1991, Wexner granted Epstein full [power of attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_attorney) over his affairs. The power of attorney allowed Epstein to hire people, sign checks, buy and sell properties, borrow money, and do anything else of a legally binding nature on Wexner's behalf.[^cite_note-Steel-2019-48] Epstein managed Wexner's wealth and various projects such as the building of his yacht, the *[Limitless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limitless_\(luxury_yacht\))*.[^cite_note-Ward-2003-22] It was during this time that [Southern Air Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Air_Transport) relocated its headquarters to service Wexner's brands,[^cite_note-49] and that Epstein dated models like [Stacey Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stacey_Williams).[^cite_note-50] Epstein represented himself as a global talent scout for Victoria's Secret during this time and used this powerful position to sexually manipulate young women.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

By 1995, Epstein was a director of the [Wexner Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wexner_Foundation) and Wexner Heritage Foundation. He was also the president of Wexner's Property, which developed part of the town of [New Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Albany,_Ohio) outside [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio), where Wexner lived. Epstein made millions in fees by managing Wexner's financial affairs. #### Liquid Funding and the Bear Stearns explosion (2000–08)

Epstein was the president of the [Bermuda](3460.md)-incorporated company Liquid Funding Ltd. between 2000 and 2007.[^cite_note-Hall-2019-64][^cite_note-65] The company was a pioneer in expanding the kind of debt that could be accepted on repurchase, or the [repo market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repurchase_agreement), which involves a lender giving money to a borrower in exchange for [securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_\(finance\)) that the borrower then agrees to buy back at an agreed-upon later time and price. The innovation of Liquid Funding, and other early companies, was that instead of having [stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock) and [bonds](60737.md) as the underlying securities, it had [commercial mortgages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_mortgage) and investment-grade [residential mortgages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage_loan) bundled into [complex securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage-backed_security) as the underlying security. Liquid Funding was initially 40 percent owned by Bear Stearns. Through the help of [credit rating agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_rating_agency), the new bundled securities were created for companies so they received an AAA rating. The implosion of complex securities, because of their inaccurate ratings, led to the [collapse of Bear Stearns in March 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_intervention_during_the_subprime_mortgage_crisis#Bear_Stearns), the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) and subsequent [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession). If Liquid Funding were left holding large amounts of such securities as collateral, it could have lost large amounts of money.[^cite_note-Hall-2019-64][^cite_note-66]

In April 2007, the fund had a [leverage ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)) of 17\:1, which meant for every dollar invested there were 17 dollars of borrowed funds; therefore, the redemption of this investment would have been equivalent to removing \$1 billion from the thinly traded CDO market.[^cite_note-67] The selling of CDO assets to meet the redemptions that month began a repricing process and freeze in the CDO market. The repricing of the CDO assets caused the collapse of the fund three months later in July, and the [collapse of Bear Stearns in March 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_Bear_Stearns_in_March_2008). Losses to investors in the two Bear Stearns funds were \$1.6 billion.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] By the time the Bear Stearns fund began to fail in May 2007, Epstein had begun to negotiate a plea deal with the U.S. Attorney's Office concerning imminent charges for sex with minors.[^cite_note-Celarier-2019-70][^cite_note-Brown-2018a-71] In August 2007, a month after the fund collapsed, [Alexander Acosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Acosta), the U.S. attorney in Miami, entered into discussions about the plea agreement.[^cite_note-Brown-2018a-71] Acosta brokered a lenient deal.[^cite_note-Ward-2019-72][^cite_note-Schindler-2019-73]

The *[Miami Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Herald)* reported that, as part of the negotiations, Epstein provided "unspecified information" to the Florida federal prosecutors for a more lenient sentence and was supposedly "Unnamed investor #1" for the New York federal prosecutors in their unsuccessful June 2008 criminal case against Cioffi and Tannen, two managers of the failed Bear Stearns fund.[^cite_note-Brown-2018c-74][^cite_note-Celarier-2019-70] [Alan Dershowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Dershowitz), one of Epstein's attorneys in the 2008 criminal case, told [Fox Business Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Business_Network) in 2019, "We would have been touting that if he had \[cooperated]. The idea that Epstein helped in any prosecution is news to me."[^cite_note-75] [Moody's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's) reported that on April 18, 2008 "all outstanding rated liabilities" of Liquid Funding were "paid in full". At the time, the liquidator had not yet sold the beleaguered fund to its new owner as of May 1: JP Morgan.[^cite_note-76]

### First criminal case (2005–2011)

#### Initial developments (2005–2006)



Foldable massage table in [Epstein's Palm Beach mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein's_Palm_Beach_mansion) in 2005

In November 2004, police in [Palm Beach](109617.md), Florida received a tip that young women were coming and going from Epstein's home.[^cite_note-114] Epstein donated \$90,000 to the police department on December 14, which followed a previous donation in 2001 or 2002 to the Palm Beach Police Scholarship Fund, which pays scholarships for police officers' children.[^cite_note-Wile-2019-115]

While there, she was allegedly paid \$300 to strip to her underwear and massage Epstein.[^cite_note-Weiss-2007-116][^cite_note-The_Smoking_Gun-2006-117]

Palm Beach Police then opened a 13-month investigation (2005–2006), in which they monitored Epstein's home, and began finding potential victims.[^cite_note-Brown-2018a-71][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] Police identified approximately 35 girls under the age of consent, who had similar stories of sexual massages in return for payment. Some of the girls said that Epstein would pay them \$200 for every additional girl he could bring to massage him. Some of the girls told police they were encouraged to lie about being 18 by the older girls who recruited them. Other girls said that they told Epstein their true ages. Epstein soon became aware of the police investigation, and hired defense lawyers counsel, who in turn hired [private investigators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_investigator). #### State prosecutor presents case to grand jury

Palm Beach Police Department (PBPD) brought the case to Florida State Attorney Barry Krischer. Krischer says he was enthusiastic about pursuing the case; however, both prosecutors and the police held concerns about witnesses and victims.[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 14 </sup>

Epstein's lawyers began investigating victims to challenge their credibility and sending findings to the state prosecutor's office, including: victim criminal records, social media postings about drug use and sex, and victim statements which "appeared to undercut allegations of criminal activity and Epstein's knowledge of victims' ages".[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 13 </sup> State prosecutors were also concerned because their office had previously prosecuted 14-year-olds for prostitution.[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 14 </sup>

In May 2006, Palm Beach police filed a [probable cause](146751.md) affidavit that Epstein should be charged with four counts of [unlawful sex with minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlawful_sex_with_minors) and one count of [sexual abuse](7397019.md).[^cite_note-123]

In July 2006, State prosecutors put the case before a [grand jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_jury) to determine charges.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 15 </sup> A police detective and two of Epstein's victims testified. The second victim said that Epstein was aware that she was 16, and that sexual activity increased gradually, before Epstein initiated intercourse the day before her 18th birthday.[^cite_note-ABC7_Los_Angeles-2024-125]

The grand jury returned a single state charge of felony [solicitation of prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitation_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-126] Epstein was arrested on July 23, 2006, and pleaded not guilty to solicitation of a prostitute.[^cite_note-The_Smoking_Gun-2006-117][^cite_note-127] He was released from Palm Beach County jail on bond.[^cite_note-128]

#### Escalation to FBI and federal prosecutors

Palm Beach Police were outraged that the grand jury returned only state charges of prostitution. Police Chief [Michael Reiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Reiter) believed that given the number of girls involved, Epstein should face charges for sex offenses involving minors, and charges of sex trafficking – a more serious federal offense. Reiter accused the state prosecutor, Barry Krischer, of being too lenient and escalated the case to the federal agents at the FBI office in Miami.[^cite_note-Weiss-2007-116][^cite_note-Lewis-2015-129]

The FBI began an investigation into potential federal sex trafficking and interstate crimes. In July 2006, the FBI began its own investigation of Epstein, nicknamed "Operation Leap Year".[^cite_note-Brown-2018a-71] They conducted interviews with 34 minors whose allegations included corroborating details, and identified additional victims.[^cite_note-130]

#### Federal prosecutors

Federal prosecutors in the [U.S. Attorney's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Attorney's_Office) began working with the FBI with the knowledge of U.S. attorney [Alexander Acosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Acosta). The lead federal prosecutor, Matthew Menchel, told Villafaña that Acosta was not ready to go forward with the indictment.[^cite_note-131] Other prosecutors sought to have in-person or phone conversations with defense counsel.[^cite_note-132] According to reporting in *[Politico](9172097.md)* two women served as lead prosecutors beginning in 2006 when Epstein first faced charges and were "closely involved in crafting his federal non-prosecution agreement, plea deal and lenient sentence."[^cite_note-Palmeri-2021-133] Lilly Sanchez, one of the lawyers on Epstein's defense, was a former dating interest of Menchel's, who failed to inform his superiors and after he left federal service had several meetings with Epstein to discuss taking on legal work.[^cite_note-134] Epstein's lawyers met with federal prosecutors, asking them to end the federal investigation so Epstein could instead face a single Florida charge of solicitation of a prostitute, and provided numerous legal arguments as to problems with the charges.[^cite_note-135] Epstein's defense lawyers included [Roy Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Black_\(attorney\)), [Gerald Lefcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_B._Lefcourt), Harvard Law School professor [Alan Dershowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Dershowitz), U.S. special envoy to North Korea [Jay Lefkowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Lefkowitz), former U.S. attorney [Guy Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_A._Lewis), and former U.S. solicitor general [Ken Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Starr).[^cite_note-Brown-2018c-74][^cite_note-136] Linguist [Steven Pinker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Pinker) also assisted.[^cite_note-137]

According to a 2020 [Office of Professional Responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Professional_Responsibility) report, prosecutors in the U.S. Attorney's Office deliberated if they could successfully convict Epstein at trial.[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120] Prosecutors found many victims were reluctant to testify against Epstein; some feared public embarrassment, and prosecutor Villafaña said "no victims expressed a strong opinion that Epstein be prosecuted".[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 145 </sup> Prosecutors were concerned about several victims who may have misrepresented their ages to Epstein.[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120] They deliberated how teenage girls would fare under cross-examination in a court room – and how this might be traumatizing for them – as Epstein's lawyers planned to aggressively attack their credibility.[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 50, 146, 38 </sup> Legal questions arose over whether federal prosecutors should be pursuing crimes that are traditionally handled by states. Investigators had gathered flight logs with identified females on them, however prosecutors believed this was not strong evidence for trafficking charges because they had no names or ages listed.[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 17 </sup>

&#x20;Acosta was unhappy with the prospect that the case might return to the state level, where Epstein would receive only prostitution charges, and no prison sentence without registering as a sex offender.[^cite_note-138]



Epstein's October 2007 non-prosecution agreement

Federal prosecutors negotiated a plea deal under which Epstein would be convicted for soliciting a minor for prostitution, solicitation of a prostitute, and was required to register as a lifetime sex offender and serve 18 months. The deal also granted immunity from all federal criminal charges to Epstein, along with four named co-conspirators and any unnamed "potential co-conspirators".[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120][^cite_note-Brown-2018c-74] The agreement further provided that the parties anticipated it "will not be made part of any public record", and that if the government received a [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_\(United_States\)) request or other compulsory process commanding its disclosure, it would give Epstein notice before disclosing it.[^cite_note-JustSecurity-timeline-139]

According to the *Miami Herald*, the non-prosecution agreement (NPA) "essentially shut down an ongoing FBI probe into whether there were more victims and other powerful people who took part in Epstein's sex crimes."[^cite_note-Brown-2018c-74] Although press coverage has focused on Epstein's connections to high-profile figures as potential co-conspirators,[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 81 </sup> prosecutor Andrew Lourie argued that the "victims who recruited other underage girls to provide massages for Epstein 'theoretically' could have been charged as co-conspirators", and felt the immunity granted to co-conspirators was "a message to any victims that had recruited other victims that there was no intent to charge them".[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 168 </sup>

Under the agreement, once it was executed alongside a plea agreement with the state, the federal grand jury investigation was to be suspended and outstanding federal subpoenas held in abeyance, to be deemed withdrawn if Epstein completed its terms.[^cite_note-JustSecurity-timeline-139] The government also undertook to give Epstein's attorneys a list of the individuals it had identified as victims under 18 U.S.C. § 2255, and an attorney representative was to be chosen for those victims and paid for by Epstein. If such a victim then sued under that section, Epstein agreed not to contest the court's jurisdiction and waived his right to contest liability and to contest damages up to an agreed amount, provided the victim proceeded exclusively under § 2255 and waived other claims for damages; the waivers were not to be construed as an admission of liability.[^cite_note-JustSecurity-timeline-139] Reviewing those provisions in 2021, the [Eleventh Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Eleventh_Circuit) described the arrangement as "an odd set-up, and one that, it seems to us, was likely calculated to quickly and quietly resolve as many victim suits as possible".[^cite_note-InReWild-2021-140]

### Guilty plea

On June 30, 2008, in the 15th Judicial Circuit in Palm Beach County, Epstein pled guilty to one count of solicitation of prostitution and one count of solicitation of prostitution with a minor under the age of 18. He was sentenced to 18 months in prison and was required to register as a sex offender.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] Also as part of the deal he was required to pay restitution to three dozen victims identified by the FBI.[^cite_note-Brown-2018c-74][^cite_note-Lewis-2015-129]

A later lawsuit alleged the NPA had violated [victims' rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_Victims'_Rights_Act) by concealing the agreement from the victims.[^cite_note-Brown-2018c-74] In 2019, Judge [Kenneth Marra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Marra) for the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida adjudicated that the Acosta NPA document had violated the Crime Victims' Rights Act.[^cite_note-Palmeri-2021-133][^cite_note-Judge_rules_feds-62][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] However, in 2020 the [11th Circuit Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Circuit_Court_of_Appeals) overturned this decision, ruling that prosecutors had not violated victim rights.[^cite_note-145]

According to an investigation conducted by the Department of Justice's [Office of Professional Responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Professional_Responsibility), released in November 2020, Acosta showed "poor judgment" in granting Epstein a NPA and failing to notify Epstein's alleged victims about the agreement, but determined Acosta operated within the "scope of his authority", and that there was no evidence his "decision was based on corruption or other impermissible considerations, such as Epstein's wealth, status, or associations".[^cite_note-146]

#### Conviction and sentencing (2008–2011)

On June 30, 2008, after Epstein pleaded guilty to a state charge of procuring for prostitution a girl below age 18, he was sentenced to eighteen months in prison. While most convicted sex offenders in Florida are sent to state prison, Epstein was instead housed in a private wing of the Palm Beach County Stockade, a minimum security detention facility.[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 113 </sup> Epstein was housed at the Stockade's previously unstaffed infirmary, after agreeing to cover the cost of security to watch him.[^cite_note-Rosza-2019-147] Costs totaled around \$128,000, which he covered through the Florida Science Foundation, a foundation he created shortly before reporting to jail.[^cite_note-Wile-2019-115] The foundation was designed to provide grants to scientific projects, and was later dissolved after completing his sentence. In August 2008, a memo written by Capt. Mark Chamberlain stated that "For the time being, I am authorizing that his cell door be left unlocked and he be given liberal access to the attorney room where a TV will be installed". It is unclear for how long Epstein's door was unlocked, allowing him to move more freely within the facility.[^cite_note-Rosza-2019-147]

After 3+1⁄2 months, Epstein was granted work release privileges for up to twelve hours a day, six days a week, to work at the aforementioned Florida Science Foundation. According to the *Miami Herald*, Epstein's work release contravened the sheriff's policies requiring a maximum remaining sentence of ten months, and making sex offenders ineligible for the privilege.[^cite_note-Brown-2018b-148] His office was monitored by police permit deputies whose overtime was paid by Epstein,[^cite_note-Wile-2019-115] who wore suits and checked in any guests. Later, the Sheriff's Office said these guest logs were destroyed per the department's records retention rules, although the Stockade visitor logs were not.[^cite_note-Rosza-2019-147] Epstein was allowed to use his own driver to drive him between jail and his office and other appointments.[^cite_note-Brown-2018b-148][^cite_note-Rosza-2019-147]

## Alleged trafficking ring

### Trafficking ring allegations

In 2008, Epstein was convicted in Florida for soliciting a minor and served 13 months. According to police and prosecutors involved in his first criminal investigation, there were no reports from victims that Epstein's high-profile associates were involved.[^cite_note-Hill-189] In a 2020 [Office of Professional Responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Professional_Responsibility) (OPR) report, federal prosecutor Marie Villafaña said "none of the victims that we spoke with ever talked about any other men being involved in abusing them. It was only Jeffrey Epstein".[^cite_note-Office_of_Professional_Responsibility-2020-120]<sup>: 167 </sup> According to the OPR report, allegations implicating Epstein's famous contacts emerged years later.[^cite_note-Hill-189]

In 2009, Virginia Giuffre alleged she was "sexually exploited by Epstein's adult male peers, including royalty" while a minor when working as Epstein's personal masseuse.[^cite_note-190] The 2009 suit, filed anonymously, stated that "In addition to being continually exploited to satisfy defendant's \[Epstein] every sexual whim, \[Ms Giuffre] was also required to be sexually exploited by defendant's adult male peers, including "royalty, politicians, academicians, businessmen and or other professional and personal acquaintances."[^cite_note-191] At the time, she named Epstein and Maxwell but did not identify any of the men.[^cite_note-192]

In the 2010s, Virginia Giuffre alleged in lawsuits and media interviews, that Epstein had run a sex trafficking ring and "lent out" girls to numerous powerful men in the early 2000s, and directed her to have sex with them. She was interviewed by the FBI in 2011 and again in 2019. In 2026, documents from Epstein's 2019 investigation were declassified. FBI investigators were unable to corroborate Giuffre's allegation that Epstein was operating a ring in which he "lent out" girls to powerful men. During interviews, numerous women corroborated evidence of Epstein abusing them, but "no other victim has described being expressly directed by either Maxwell or Epstein to engage in sexual activity with other men".[^cite_note-Sisak-2026-170]\[^cite_note-[www.justice.gov-2019-193](http://www.justice.gov-2019-193/)]<sup>: 59 </sup>

When the FBI interviewed two of the victims that Giuffre had claimed were also "lent out", those women denied having such experiences. The FBI memo said Giuffre gave "shifting accounts" and "engaged in a continuous stream of public interviews about her allegations, many of which have included sensationalized if not demonstrably inaccurate characterizations of her experiences". It also said Giuffre publicly misrepresented her interactions with the FBI.[^cite_note-Sisak-2026-170]

The 2019 FBI memo also states that Giuffre's early draft memoir made mention of Epstein's lawyer [Alan Dershowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Dershowitz) without allegations of sexual activity, but in 2014, Giuffre raised trafficking allegations. Unsealed emails show that in 2011, Giuffre was communicating with *DailyMail* reporter Sharon Churcher, who she asked to help create a list of Epstein associates to include in her memoir. In one exchange, Churcher said "Don't forget Alan Dershowitz ... JE's buddy and lawyer -good name for your pitch ... We all suspect Alan is a pedo and tho no proof on that, you probably met him when he was hanging out with JE."\[^cite_note-[www.justice.gov-2019-193](http://www.justice.gov-2019-193/)]<sup>: 60 </sup>[^cite_note-Tangalakis-Lippert-2024-194] In 2014, Giuffre filed a lawsuit alleging that Epstein had trafficked her to figures including Alan Dershowitz, who she alleged had raped her on 6 occasions. Dershowitz denied her allegations and countersued her, alleging she was fabricating allegations to "extort money" from Epstein associates.[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2021-195][^cite_note-Tangalakis-Lippert-2024-194] In 2022, Giuffre withdrew her legal action against Dershowitz and said she "may have made a mistake" in identifying him.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-Tangalakis-Lippert-2024-194]

### Alleged tapes and blackmail operation

When police raided [his Palm Beach residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_Palm_Beach_mansion) in 2006, two hidden cameras were present in his home, one in the garage, and another in his office.[^cite_note-197] Police had helped to install these cameras in 2003,[^cite_note-198] when Epstein complained about an employee stealing cash and a gun from him. After an investigation, police identified the thief as Epstein's handyman Juan Alessi. Epstein asked the PBPD to call off the investigation when Alessi agreed to repay him. Police found images from the camera showing several women standing in the office, such as his assistants Sarah Kellen and [Nadia Marcinko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Marcinko).[^cite_note-199]

Virginia Giuffre later alleged that in the early 2000s, Epstein had secretly recorded her having sex with powerful men for blackmail purposes, and in 2015, accused the FBI of possessing alleged videos in a "major [cover-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover-up)".[^cite_note-200] In 2016, [Sarah Ransome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Ransome), who served as a witness for Giuffre, told a reporter that she had copies of sex tapes filmed by Epstein for blackmail purposes, that included [Donald Trump](4848272.md), [Bill Clinton](3356.md), [Richard Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Branson) and [Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor](125267.md). However, Ransome admitted to fabricating her claim in 2019.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202]

In a 2014 email exchange, Epstein instructed a staff member to purchase and install motion-activated hidden cameras at his Palm Beach residence, with the aide indicating they would be concealed in tissue boxes.[^cite_note-203] According to *The Telegraph*, footage captured on covert cameras was "largely innocuous and unlikely to constitute compromising material. They appear to have been filmed in the living room of Epstein's Palm Beach home, not in bedrooms or elsewhere".[^cite_note-204]

Following Epstein's death in August 2019, reports of there being cameras in his homes circulated in the media. *Vanity Fair* reported claims of a former friend of Ghislaine Maxwell, who said that [Epstein's island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein's_island) was "completely wired" with cameras, and speculated this was for blackmail purposes.[^cite_note-205] In November 2019, [Maria Farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Farmer), an artist who worked for Epstein in 1996, said that Epstein had shown her a hidden room in his [New York mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_N._Straus_House) where he had teams of men monitoring rooms in the home using "pinhole cameras", including the bedrooms and toilets.[^cite_note-206] In 2018, Epstein told *New York Times* reporter off the record, that he held incriminating information about powerful people, including information about their sexual proclivities and recreational drug use.[^cite_note-\:7-98] In July 2021, *Rolling Stone* reported that multiple sources, including former arms dealers and former intelligence figures, suggested that Epstein recorded influential individuals in compromising situations.[^cite_note-207] In 2022, Courtney Soerensen alleged that in 1988, Epstein sexually groped her in France while a videographer filmed the encounter.[^cite_note-six-208] In 2025, Marina Lacerda, an alleged minor victim recruited by Epstein in 2002, described his New York home as being full of cameras with an office in the front of the house dedicated to them.[^cite_note-baker-nytimes-108]

Federal investigators seized Epstein's devices during 2019 raids on his homes.[^cite_note-Sisak-2026-170] Authorities found CDs, photographs and tapes, some labeled "young \[name] + \[name]",[^cite_note-Stanglin-2019-171] and depicting girls who "seemed as if they might be minors". The FBI investigation, made public in 2026, found that none of the photos or videos depicted victims being sexually abused by Epstein or others. Investigators found between 15 and 20 images depicting [child sexual abuse material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_Sexual_Abuse_Imagery) (CSAM), but they determined that these were downloaded from the internet, not produced by Epstein.[^cite_note-Sisak-2026-170]

In 2019, two prominent lawyers representing Epstein's accusers, [David Boies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Boies) and [J. Stanley Pottinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Stanley_Pottinger), were contacted by a man purporting to be a hacker, who went by the pseudonym Patrick Kessler. Kessler claimed to possess sex tapes of Epstein and high profile men with victims, and told the lawyers he wanted the men to face justice. In message exchanges, Pottinger instead proposed using the videos to extract large financial settlements from the men and keeping the tapes private. ### Alleged adult victims

Following Epstein's 2008 conviction, Epstein reportedly began pursuing relationships with young adult women over the age of 18. According to victims lawyer [Brad Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_J._Edwards), Epstein "deliberately shifted to women over 18 to use their age as cover" for what he argues was a sex trafficking operation. Many of the adult women Epstein pursued were fashion models from Europe and [Russia](25391.md).[^cite_note-\:0-211]

Emails released in the Epstein Files identified one such woman as Svetlana Pozhidaeva, who, while an adult, began working an assistant to the sex offender from 2008 until his death in 2019. Previously unredacted emails showed she was gathering information on young women and models, and sending it to Epstein. Her name has since been redacted by the Department of Justice. According to lawyers speaking to the *[Wall Street Journal](173070.md),* Pozhidaeva is an example of an adult victim, who was coerced into assisting Epstein's trafficking. She is represented by a major Epstein victims lawyer, [Brad Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_J._Edwards), and denies allegations that she or her father were Russian spies.[^cite_note-\:0-211]

An investigation from the German newspapers [Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel) and [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDF) revealed that modeling scout [Daniel Siad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Siad) had introduced Epstein to a 22-year-old German woman referred to as "Michele" who has been missing since 2015.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214]

According to Safdar, redaction failures of emails in the files gave "ammunition" to online commentators, who cited friendly, warm or grateful messages between adult women and Epstein as evidence they were "willing participants", or as "evidence of complicity or consent". The lawyer Moira Penza argued against the idea that adult women "could have just left" Epstein, as she believes co-dependency means "consent just becomes irrelevant. There really is no way to consent".[^cite_note-\:0-211]

In 2025, a woman of Russian origin filed a class action lawsuit against [Bank of America](347756.md), alleging that while an adult,[^cite_note-\:4-215] the bank had enabled Epstein to traffic and control her life between 2011 until 2019. As of March 2026, the lawsuit has been tentatively settled.[^cite_note-Neumeister-216]

In the *Wall Street Journal*, [Michael Tracey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Tracey_\(journalist\)) argued that "excessive redactions" in Epstein files releases made the number of alleged adult victims unclear, writing: "as far as I can ascertain based on publicly available information, a significant majority of purported Epstein victims were adults at the time of their claimed victimization, not vulnerable children—an inconvenient detail that the dissemination of unredacted records would likely illuminate".[^cite_note-\:4-215]

### Alleged forced pregnancies and stolen babies

The release of the Epstein Files contained pages from a woman's journal, submitted to the Department of Justice by her lawyer, alleging that Epstein had forced her to bear several children in an "impregnation game". Her journals included images of [sonograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_ultrasound), and entries in which she wrote that Epstein was trying to create the "perfect gene pool". She wrote her baby was taken shortly after giving birth: "she was born, I heard her cries!... I saw this tiny head and body in between the doctor's hands. Ghislaine said she was beautiful. Where is she?".[^cite_note-\:8-217][^cite_note-218]

In April 2026, a judge ruled that the woman had falsified the sonogram images in her journals. The determination occurred in the course of a [Jane Doe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Doe) lawsuit she had brought against [Leon Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Black). The woman's lawyer, Jeanne Christensen, was also [sanctioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_\(law\)) for repeatedly lying to the court.[^cite_note-\:12-219] In a proceeding, Doe's claim of being an Epstein victim also came under question, pertaining to victim settlements from JP Morgan. Black's lawyers have urged authorities to investigate the woman for [fraud on the court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraud_on_the_court).[^cite_note-\:8-217] According to the *New York Times*, there is no evidence that Epstein ever fathered any children.[^cite_note-\:12-219]

## Death

Main article: [Death of Jeffrey Epstein](61499571.md)



At the time of his death, Epstein was being held at the [Metropolitan Correctional Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Correctional_Center,_New_York), awaiting trial for sex trafficking.

[^cite_note-Dienst-2019-228]

On July 23, 2019, Epstein was found injured and semiconscious at 1\:30 a.m. [EDT](496028.md) on the floor of his cell at the [Metropolitan Correctional Center, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Correctional_Center,_New_York) (MCC), with marks around his neck.[^cite_note-Watkins-2019b-229][^cite_note-Rashbaum-2019-230][^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233] His cellmate, [Nicholas Tartaglione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Tartaglione), was questioned about Epstein's condition. Tartaglione denied having any knowledge of what happened. Correctional staff suspected attempted suicide, but did not rule out the possibility it was staged or assault by another inmate.[^cite_note-Dienst-2019-228][^cite_note-Leonnig-2019-234][^cite_note-Drury-2019-172][^cite_note-235][^cite_note-Rashbaum-2019-230] After that incident, he was placed on [suicide watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_watch).[^cite_note-Watkins-2019b-229][^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] Six days later, on July 29, 2019, Epstein was taken off suicide watch and placed in a special housing unit with another inmate.[^cite_note-Watkins-2019b-229]

When Epstein was placed in the special housing unit, the jail informed the Justice Department that he would have a cellmate, and that a guard would look into the cell every 30 minutes. These procedures were not followed on the night of his death.[^cite_note-Watkins-2019a-239][^cite_note-Watkins-2019b-229][^cite_note-Benner-2019-240] On August 9, 2019, Epstein's cellmate was transferred out, but no one took his place.[^cite_note-241] Later in the evening, contrary to the jail's normal procedure, Epstein was not checked every 30 minutes.[^cite_note-Watkins-2019a-239][^cite_note-Watkins-2019b-229][^cite_note-Benner-2019-240] The two guards assigned to check his unit that night fell asleep and did not check on him for three hours; the guards falsified related records.[^cite_note-Watkins-2019b-229][^cite_note-242] The two cameras in front of Epstein's cell were claimed to have malfunctioned that night.[^cite_note-Hosenball-2019-243]

Epstein was found dead in his cell at 6\:30 a.m. on August 10, 2019.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245] Emergency responders were called and he was taken to a hospital. On August 10, the [Federal Bureau of Prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Prisons) and U.S. attorney general [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr) called the death an apparent suicide, although no final determination had been made.[^cite_note-Zapotosky-2019b-246] The [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md)'s Inspector General's investigation report released in 2023, criticized jail officials for repeated "negligence, misconduct, and outright job performance failures". It denied the suggestion that what happened was anything other than a suicide.[^cite_note-247] In May 2025, the FBI Deputy Director [Dan Bongino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bongino) stated that the surveillance footage from the night of Epstein's death shows Epstein alone in his cell, with no evidence of outside involvement, reaffirming the official ruling of suicide.[^cite_note-248]

### Autopsy

Epstein's body moved from New York hospital to medical examiner's office

On August 11, 2019, an [autopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopsy) was performed.[^cite_note-249] It appeared likely that Epstein had thrown himself violently off the cell's top bunk, which would explain the damage he suffered, other than strangulation.[^cite_note-250] The preliminary result of the autopsy found that Epstein sustained breaks in his neck bones, including the [hyoid bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyoid_bone). Such breaks of the hyoid bone can occur from those who hang themselves from a substantial height, e.g. jumping from a chair into the rope, but are more common in victims of strangulation.[^cite_note-251]

On August 16, 2019, [Barbara Sampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Sampson), the [Chief Medical Examiner of the City of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Chief_Medical_Examiner_of_the_City_of_New_York), ruled Epstein's death a [suicide by hanging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_by_hanging).[^cite_note-252] The medical examiner, according to Epstein's defense counsel, only saw nine minutes of footage from one security camera to help her arrive at her conclusion. Epstein's defense lawyers were not satisfied with the conclusion and said the evidence concerning Epstein's death was "far more consistent" with murder.[^cite_note-Stockler-2019-253][^cite_note-254] [Michael Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Baden), an independent pathologist hired by the Epstein estate, observed the autopsy. In October 2019, Baden said that Epstein had experienced a number of injuries—among them a broken bone in his neck—that "are extremely unusual in suicidal hangings and could occur much more commonly in homicidal strangulation."[^cite_note-255]

### Final will

On August 18, 2019, it was reported that Epstein had signed his last [will and testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_and_testament) on August 8, two weeks after being found injured in his cell and two days before his death.[^cite_note-Dienst-2019-228] Until this time, Epstein had been depositing money in other inmates' [commissary accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison_commissary) to avoid being attacked.[^cite_note-Watkins-2019b-229] Epstein's will left \$50 million in cash and most of his property to girlfriend Karyna Shuliak, \$50 million to lawyer Darren Indyke, \$25 million to accountant Richard Kahn, \$10 million to pilot Larry Visoski, \$10 million to [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md), and \$10 million to his brother Mark. Other female employees were to get a million dollars each.[^cite_note-256] The trust's enforceability remained uncertain.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] As of February 2026, Kahn and Indyke control [Epstein's estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein's_estate).[^cite_note-Goldstein-2026-259] Due to ongoing lawsuits and victim settlements, Epstein's beneficiaries will only receive a share of what is left after these are resolved.[^cite_note-260][^cite_note-Goldstein-2026-259]

### Burial

Following the autopsy, Epstein's body was claimed by his brother Mark.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] On September 5, 2019, Epstein's body was interred in an unmarked crypt next to those of his parents at the I.J. Morris Star of David Cemetery in [Palm Beach, Florida](109617.md). The names of his parents were removed to prevent vandalism.[^cite_note-263]

### Investigations

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Jeffrey Epstein](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category\:Jeffrey%20Epstein).

Attorney General Barr ordered an investigation by the [U.S. Department of Justice Office of the Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Justice_Office_of_the_Inspector_General) in addition to the FBI investigation, saying he was "appalled" by Epstein's death in federal custody.[^cite_note-Zapotosky-2019b-246][^cite_note-264] Two days later Barr said there had been "serious irregularities" in the prison's handling of Epstein, promising "there will be accountability."[^cite_note-265]

CCTV footage released in July 2025 showing part of a common area within the Metropolitan Correctional Center where Epstein was being held. A minute is missing from the footage, where the clock jumps from 11\:58\:58 to 12\:00\:00.

The president of the Council of Prison Locals C-33 E. Young, stated that prisons "can't ever stop anyone who is persistent on killing themselves."[^cite_note-Zapotosky-2019a-266] The previous inmate suicide in that MCC facility was in 1998.[^cite_note-267] Union leader Young said it was unclear if there was video of Epstein's hanging or direct observations by jail officials. He said that while cameras are ubiquitous, he did not believe the interior of inmates' cells was within their range.[^cite_note-Zapotosky-2019a-266]

President Trump and Attorney General [Pam Bondi](4550623.md) briefly mentioning Epstein during a cabinet meeting on July 8, 2025.

Epstein's attorneys asked district judge [Richard M. Berman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_M._Berman), to probe the death, alleging they could provide evidence the incident resulting in his death was "far more consistent with assault" than suicide.[^cite_note-Stockler-2019-253] At least one camera in the hallway outside Epstein's cell had footage that was unusable, though other usable footage was recorded in the area.[^cite_note-Leonnig-2019-234] Two cameras that malfunctioned in front of Epstein's cell were sent to an [FBI lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_Laboratory) for examination.[^cite_note-Hosenball-2019-243] Federal prosecutors subpoenaed 20 correctional officers concerning the cause of Epstein's death.[^cite_note-268]

On November 19, 2019, prosecutors charged guards Michael Thomas and Tova Noel with creating false records, and conspiracy, after footage revealed that Epstein had, against regulation, been in his cell unchecked for eight hours prior to being found dead.[^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271] On May 22, 2021, the guards admitted they falsified records but were spared jail time under a deal.[^cite_note-272] On May 25, they pleaded guilty to falsifying records and conspiracy to defraud the U.S. They were sentenced to six months of supervisory release and 100 hours of community service.[^cite_note-273]

In December 2023, judge [Loretta Preska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Preska) ordered a list with names of 170 Epstein associates to be unsealed on January 1, 2024.[^cite_note-274] Anyone on the list had until January 1 to appeal to have their name removed.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279] In February 2025, the [second Trump administration](78298108.md)'s attorney general [Pam Bondi](4550623.md) stated that Epstein's [client list](80415168.md) was "sitting on my desk" for review, and in June, [Elon Musk](909036.md) alleged Trump was in the files. In July, Bondi and FBI head [Kash Patel](62202727.md) announced there was no list, no evidence Epstein had blackmailed anyone, that Epstein had killed himself, and released footage showing a partial view of a common area and obscured view of the stairs leading to Epstein's cell block—though that footage was not independently verified.[^cite_note-280][^cite_note-281][^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283] A minute was found missing, where the clock jumps from 11\:58\:58 to 12\:00\:00.[^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285] An investigation found the video had been modified despite the FBI claim it was raw,[^cite_note-286] and three minutes were cut out of the video.[^cite_note-287] On July 15, 2025, Representative [Thomas Massie](28005891.md) submitted [House Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Resolution) 119–581, co-sponsored by [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna), to force the Department of Justice to release the Epstein files.[^cite_note-288][^cite_note-289][^cite_note-290] They announced on August 12 they would bring victims to the Capitol on September 3.[^cite_note-291][^cite_note-292]

On August 25, 2025, the [House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Oversight_Committee) issued [subpoenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subpoena) to [Bill Clinton](3356.md), [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) and former attorneys general [Loretta Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynch), [Eric Holder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Holder), [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr), [Merrick Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrick_Garland), [Jeff Sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Sessions), and [Alberto Gonzales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Gonzales). Acosta testified in October about his plea deal with Epstein, where he defended his decision to pursue a state-level case due to perceived weaknesses in federal evidence, and denied knowledge of Epstein's extensive financial crimes. These claims were disputed by Democrats, highlighting contradictions with later evidence of his office investigating financial aspects and calling his testimony non-credible.[^cite_note-293]

On September 4, 2025, [James O'Keefe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_O'Keefe) published an exposé on Deputy Chief Joseph Schnitt of DOJ Special Operations, in which the latter said there were "thousands and thousands of pages of files" relating to Epstein and the DOJ would "redact every Republican or conservative person in those files", while "\[leaving] all the liberal, Democratic people in those files".[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295][^cite_note-296][^cite_note-297] Epstein's brother Mark alleged the same, stating he had heard from a "good source" that a team in Virginia was "sanitizing" and "scrubbing the files to take Republican names out."[^cite_note-298] On November 12, [Adelita Grijalva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelita_Grijalva) gave the final 218th signature to Massie's discharge position, forcing the creation of the [Epstein Files Transparency Act](81594904.md). It requires the attorney general to "make publicly available" all files pertaining to the prosecution of Epstein within 30 days, and give the Judiciary Committees an unredacted "list of all government officials and politically exposed persons" named in the files.[^cite_note-299]

## Estate and victim settlements

### Epstein estate settlements

Main article: [Estate of Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_of_Jeffrey_Epstein)

Epstein's estate was valued around \$600 million at the time of his death.[^cite_note-Goldstein-2026-259] His estate has become the focus of extensive litigation and settlements. Epstein's estate created a victims' compensation program that paid about \$121 million to 136 claimants.[^cite_note-Breuninger-2022-300] The estate later paid an additional \$49 million to settle a victims lawsuit.[^cite_note-301] In 2026, the estate paid \$35 million to settle another victims lawsuit.[^cite_note-302] In 2022, the estate also settled a lawsuit with the government of the U.S. Virgin Islands to resolve civil claims that Epstein used the territory for trafficking, paying \$105 million and agreeing to share proceeds from the sale of his private islands.[^cite_note-Breuninger-2022-300]

### Bank lawsuits

In November 2023, JPMorgan Chase agreed to pay a settlement of \$290 million to resolve a class-action lawsuit brought by Epstein accusers, who alleged the bank enabled his abuse by continuing to provide him their financial services. JP Morgan did not admit liability as part of the settlement.[^cite_note-Goldstein-2023-303] The lawyers received 30% of the settlement.[^cite_note-304]

Around the same time, Deutsche Bank agreed to pay \$75 million to settle a similar lawsuit brought by Epstein accusers concerning the period when Epstein was a client of the bank, between 2013 and 2018.[^cite_note-305] JPMorgan later paid \$75 million to settle claims by the government of the U.S. Virgin Islands related to allegations that the bank had enabled Epstein's trafficking.[^cite_note-306]

In 2025, victim lawsuits were also filed against [Bank of America](347756.md) and the [Bank of New York Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNY) (BNY), alleging these banks enabled Epstein's trafficking. Both banks said they would fight the lawsuits, with BNY describing the lawsuit as "meritless".[^cite_note-307] In February 2026, BNY won a dismissal of all claims in the lawsuit. 4 out of 6 claims against Bank of America were dismissed, with two proceeding.[^cite_note-\:3-308]

## Epstein files releases

Main articles: [Epstein files](80415168.md) and [Jeffrey Epstein's birthday book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_birthday_book)

The Epstein files are a U.S. government release of millions of documents pertaining to investigations of Epstein.

In September 2025, the [House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Oversight_and_Accountability) released a 2003 birthday album created for Epstein's 50th birthday, titled *[The First Fifty Years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_birthday_book)*. The album contained letters and drawings from various associates.[^cite_note-309] One letter in the collection was attributed to [Donald Trump](4848272.md), although Trump has denied writing or signing it and his legal team has challenged its authenticity.[^cite_note-310]

In January 2026, the Justice Department published about three million further pages, together with some 2,000 videos and 180,000 images, in compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act.[^cite_note-DOJ-2026-01-30-311] The release drew criticism over redaction failures. The department published dozens of unredacted nude images of young women, which were largely removed after *The New York Times* notified it, and attorneys for survivors said that the names of victims who had never previously been linked publicly to Epstein appeared unredacted.[^cite_note-Hill-2026-01-30-312][^cite_note-Kamin-2026-02-03-313]

## Aftermath and legacy

Further information: [Epstein didn't kill himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_didn't_kill_himself) and [Epstein files](80415168.md)



Graffiti featuring the phrase on an overpass on [Interstate 71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_71) in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati)

### Public reaction and institutional scrutiny

Epstein's death in 2019 fueled widespread controversy and debate. The belief that he was the victim of murder in service of a wider cover-up subsequently became a popular [internet meme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_meme) and conspiracy theory.[^cite_note-314][^cite_note-315]

The partial release of the [Epstein files](80415168.md) by the U.S. Department of Justice in response to the [Epstein Files Transparency Act](81594904.md) prompted renewed public and media scrutiny of the case and contributed to a marked shift in public discourse about [elite accountability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountability).[^cite_note-316][^cite_note-317][^cite_note-318] Legal scholars, investigative journalists, and human rights experts described the disclosures as intensifying scrutiny of longstanding [institutional failures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_decay) which had allowed Epstein to avoid accountability, despite repeated allegations.[^cite_note-319][^cite_note-320] Commentators noted that claims concerning Epstein's extensive connections within the political, financial, and social [elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite)–which had long been dismissed by many officials and media figures as speculative or unsubstantiated–were reassessed following the document release.[^cite_note-321] The case has been frequently cited as illustrative of broader [class-based disparities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality) in law enforcement and prosecutorial decision-making, and as an example of systemic [corruption](25239439.md) and elite impunity within the [criminal justice system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_justice), particularly regarding the treatment of [wealthy or well-connected individuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_class).[^cite_note-322]

### Victims' later deaths

At least three women who are confirmed or alleged to have been abused by Epstein have died of drug overdoses or by suicide. In 2018, Leigh "Skye" Patrick, who was sexually abused by Epstein at the age of 16, died of an accidental drug overdose in a Florida motel.[^cite_note-MiamiHerald-323] In 2023, Carolyn Andriano, a victim of Epstein who testified at the trial of Ghislaine Maxwell, died of an accidental drug overdose, also in a Florida hotel.[^cite_note-MiamiHerald-323] In 2025, [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre) died by suicide at her home in [Australia](4689264.md) at the age of 41.[^cite_note-Roberts-2025-324][^cite_note-325]

### Artworks

On July 1, 2020, a statue of Epstein was left outside the City Hall in [Albuquerque, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico) as a satirical commentary on opposition to the [removal of Confederate monuments and memorials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removal_of_Confederate_monuments_and_memorials).[^cite_note-326][^cite_note-327] A sculpture of Epstein frolicking with Trump titled *[Best Friends Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Friends_Forever_\(sculpture\))* was produced by an anonymous art group aliased "The Secret Handshake" in protest of their relationship. Its September 2025 debut at the [National Mall](167796.md) made national news when the [United States Park Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Park_Police) assigned with protecting the sculpture dismantled it.[^cite_note-328]

### Documentaries

As of October 2019, [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) was creating a limited series on Epstein's life and death to be directed and executive produced by [Adam McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_McKay).[^cite_note-329][^cite_note-330] [Sony Pictures Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Television) is additionally developing a miniseries based on Epstein's life.[^cite_note-331] The [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) documentary series *[Jeffrey Epstein: Filthy Rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein:_Filthy_Rich)* premiered in May 2020.[^cite_note-332] The [Lifetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifetime_\(TV_network\)) documentary *[Surviving Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surviving_Jeffrey_Epstein)* premiered in August 2020.[^cite_note-333]
