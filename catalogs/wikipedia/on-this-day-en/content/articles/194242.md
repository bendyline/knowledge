The **Red River** is a major river in the [Southern United States](179553.md).[^cite_note-3] It was named for its reddish water color from passing through [red-bed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Beds_of_Texas_and_Oklahoma) in its [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin).[^cite_note-4] It is known as the **Red River of the South** to distinguish it from the [Red River of the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North), which flows between [Minnesota](19590.md) and [North Dakota](21651.md) into the Canadian province of [Manitoba](18926.md). Although once a [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River), the Red River now is a tributary of the [Atchafalaya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atchafalaya_River), a [distributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributary) of the Mississippi that flows separately into the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md). This confluence is connected to the Mississippi River by the [Old River Control Structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_River_Control_Structure).

The south bank of the Red River formed part of the [US–Mexico border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US%E2%80%93Mexico_border) from the [Adams–Onís Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams%E2%80%93On%C3%ADs_Treaty) (in force only in 1821) until the [Texas Annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Annexation) and the [Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo).

The Red River basin is the second-largest in the southern [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains).[^cite_note-benke-5] It rises in two branches in the [Texas panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_panhandle) and flows eastward, serving as a border between the states of [Texas](29810.md) and [Oklahoma](22489.md). It forms a short border between Texas and [Arkansas](1930.md) before entering Arkansas. It forms much of the eastern border of [Miller County, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_County,_Arkansas), turning south near [Fulton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulton,_Arkansas) and flowing into [Louisiana](18130.md), where it feeds the Atchafalaya River. The total length of the river is 1,360 miles (2,190 km), with a mean flow of over 57,000 cubic feet per second (1,600 m<sup>3</sup>/s) at its mouth.

### Course

The Red River begins at the junction of Buck Creek and [Prairie Dog Town Fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Dog_Town_Fork_Red_River) in [Harmon County, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmon_County,_Oklahoma).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-USGS-2][^cite_note-7] Specialists have debated whether the [North Fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Fork_Red_River) or the Prairie Dog Town Fork is the true stem.[^cite_note-benke-5]

In 1852, [Randolph B. Marcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_B._Marcy)'s expedition had followed the Prairie Dog Town Fork.[^cite_note-benke-5] Because of a [cartographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) error, the land between the north and south forks was claimed by both the state of Texas and the United States federal government. Originally called [Greer County, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greer_County,_Texas), the [US Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Supreme_Court) ruled that it belonged to the United States, which at the time oversaw the [Oklahoma Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_Territory). That territory was later incorporated into the state of [Oklahoma](22489.md), whose southern border follows the south fork. Today, the southern Prairie Dog Town Fork is considered the main fork, though the North Fork is as long and normally has a greater water flow.[^cite_note-benke-5]

The southern fork, about 120 miles (190 km) long, is formed in [Randall County, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randall_County,_Texas), near [Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon,_Texas), by the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of [Tierra Blanca Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_Blanca_Creek) and intermittent Palo Duro Creek (not to be confused with another [Palo Duro Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Duro_Creek) 75 miles to the north, which drains into the [North Canadian River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Canadian_River)). It turns and flows southeast through [Palo Duro Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Duro_Canyon) at an elevation of 3,440 feet (1,050 m)[^cite_note-benke-5] and past [Newlin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newlin,_Texas), to cross the Oklahoma state line. About 2 miles south of [Elmer, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer,_Oklahoma), the [Salt Fork of the Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Fork_Red_River) joins from the north. These combined waters receive the North Fork Red River from the north about 10 mi (16 km) west-southwest of [Frederick, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick,_Oklahoma), to form the Red River proper.

Crossing the Red River at the Texas–Oklahoma border from [I-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-35)



The Red River took a new channel near [Natchitoches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natchitoches,_Louisiana), [Louisiana](18130.md), and left behind [Cane River Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cane_River).

The combined river follows a [meandering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meander) course east through one of the most arid parts of the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains), receiving the [Wichita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_River) about 25 miles northeast of [Wichita Falls, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_Falls,_Texas). Near [Denison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denison,_Texas), the river exits the eastern end of [Lake Texoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Texoma), a reservoir formed by the [Denison Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denison_Dam). (The lake is also fed by the [Washita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washita_River) from the north.) Beyond the dam it runs generally east towards Arkansas, receiving Oklahoma's [Muddy Boggy Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muddy_Boggy_Creek) on the left bank before turning southward near [Texarkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texarkana,_Arkansas).

The waterway crosses south into Louisiana. The sister cities of [Shreveport](150473.md) and [Bossier City](115504.md) were developed on either bank of the river, as were the downriver cities of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Louisiana) and [Pineville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineville,_Louisiana). After being joined from the north by the [Black River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_\(Louisiana\)) (downstream name of the [Ouachita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_River), its largest tributary) about 1.5 miles south of [Acme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acme,_Louisiana), the river broadens into a complex network of marshlands west of the Mississippi River. Its waters eventually become a [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the Atchafalaya River[^cite_note-Piazza2014-8] and flow generally southward into the Gulf of Mexico.

### Tributaries

Tributaries include the [Little Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Red_River_\(Texas\)), [Prairie Dog Town Fork Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Dog_Town_Fork_Red_River), [Salt Fork Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Fork_Red_River), [North Fork Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Fork_Red_River), [Cross Bayou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Bayou), [Pease River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pease_River), [Washita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washita_River), [Kiamichi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiamichi_River), [Wichita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_River), [Little Wichita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Wichita_River), [Little River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_River_\(Red_River\)), [Sulphur River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulphur_River), [Bayou Brevelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayou_Brevelle), [Loggy Bayou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggy_Bayou) (through [Lake Bistineau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Bistineau) and [Dorcheat Bayou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorcheat_Bayou?action=edit\&redlink=1)), the [Blue River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_River_\(Oklahoma\)), as well as the [Ouachita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_River) (also known as the Black River at that point) not far (at Acme, Louisiana) from the mouth.

### Salinity



Salt beds in the [Little Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Red_River_\(Texas\)), a tributary of the upper Red River

The river is salty through tributaries above Lake Texoma. The saltiness is caused by [Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian) salt deposits, mostly sodium chloride. Deposition eventually buried the deposits, but the salt continues to leach through natural seeps in tributaries above Lake Texoma, sending as much as 3,450 tons of salt per day flowing down the Red River.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## Watershed

The watershed covers 65,590 square miles (169,900 km<sup>2</sup>)[^cite_note-benke-5] and is the southernmost major river system in the Great Plains. Its drainage basin is mostly in Texas and Oklahoma but also covers parts of New Mexico, Arkansas and Louisiana. Its basin is characterized by flat, fertile agricultural land, with only a few major cities. The drainage basin is arid. As a result, much of the river above the Texas–Oklahoma border is intermittent, and the flow varies widely until the river is beyond its great bend south in Arkansas. Most of the agriculture in the basin is sustained by [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater), which is recharged with rainfall and river flow. The lower course flows through a series of [marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh) and [swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp), which dramatically moderate its flow. The Red River is said to have once carried five times the [sediment load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_load) of the Mississippi.[^cite_note-11]

## History

### Native Americans

Native American cultures along the river were diverse, developing specialized adaptations to the many different environments.[^cite_note-benke-5] Starting near the headwaters, the Plains division of the [Lipan Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipan_Apache_people) dominated the western Red River area until the 18th century, when they were displaced by invading [Comanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comanche) from the north.[^cite_note-benke-5] The middle part of the river was dominated by the [Wichita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_people) and [Tonkawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonkawa). This area was [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie), where Native Americans constructed portable and temporary [tepees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepee) for housing. They practiced limited farming and followed game in seasonal, [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomadic) hunting cycles.[^cite_note-benke-5] By the time of European contact, the eastern [Piney Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piney_Woods) of the lower river courses were dominated by the numerous historic tribes of the [Caddo Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo). They found plentiful game and fish, and also had good land for cultivating staple crops.[^cite_note-benke-5]

### American exploration and settlement, 1806 onwards



Crossing the North Fork of the Red River near [Granite, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite,_Oklahoma), 1921.



[Pontoon bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontoon_bridge) crossing Red River at [Lake End, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_End,_Louisiana) in 1942

In 1806, President [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson) commissioned the [Red River Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Expedition_\(1806\)) to explore parts of the new lands of the 1803 [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) by traveling up the Red River. Jefferson wrote in a letter to explorer [William Dunbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Dunbar_\(explorer\)) that the river was "in truth, next to the [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), the most interesting water of the Mississippi".[^cite_note-12] Having threaded the maze of [bayous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayous) at the river's confluence and the "[Great Raft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Raft)" of lodged driftwood, the expedition was stopped by the Spanish near present-day [New Boston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Boston,_Texas).

In 1806, Lieutenant [Zebulon Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebulon_Pike), under orders to ascertain the source of the Red River, ascended the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River), made his way downstream on what turned out to be the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande), and was sent home by the Spanish authorities. A more successful exploration of the river's upper reaches to both its sources came with the 1852 expedition under Captain [Randolph Barnes Marcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_Barnes_Marcy), assisted by Brevet Captain [George B. McClellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._McClellan).[^cite_note-13] A decade later McClellan became an important general in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War).

In April 1815, Captain Henry Miller Shreve was the first person to bring a steamboat, the *Enterprise*, up the Red River. Fulton and Livingston, who claimed the exclusive right to navigate Louisiana waters by steamboat, sued Shreve in the District Court of New Orleans. The judge ruled that the monopoly claimed by the plaintiffs was illegal. That decision, along with a similar outcome in *[Gibbons v. Ogden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbons_v._Ogden)*, freed navigation on every river, lake or harbor in the United States from interference by monopolies.[^cite_note-COO-Harbour-14]

When [John Quincy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams) became [Secretary of State](32293.md) in 1817, one of his highest priorities was to settle with Spain the boundaries of the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase). He negotiated with [Luis de Onís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_On%C3%ADs), the Spanish minister to the United States, and finally concluded the [Adams–Onis Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams%E2%80%93Onis_Treaty), also known as the Treaty of 1819. The treaty defined the south bank of the river as the boundary between the United States and Spain, as of when it was surveyed and demarcated following 1819. That boundary continued to be recognized when [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Empire) gained its independence from Spain in 1821, and ongoingly when [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas) became independent from [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralist_Republic_of_Mexico) in 1835–1836. It remained the official boundary[^cite_note-COO-Harbour-14] until the United States Congress consented in October 2000 to the Red River Boundary Compact, which had been adopted in 1999 by the states of Oklahoma and Texas <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-106publ288/html/PLAW-106publ288.htm>. This set the jurisdictional boundary between these states at the vegetation line on the south bank, leaving title of adjacent property owners at the south bank. (The Red River actively meanders, as shown by comparing current maps of the political boundaries with those defined by the river's course decades ago.)

The [Red River Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Campaign) (March–May 1864) was fought along the Red River Valley in Louisiana during the Civil War. It was part of a failed attempt by the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) to occupy eastern Texas. [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army) commander [Richard Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Taylor_\(Confederate_general\)) succeeded in repelling an army under [Nathaniel Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_P._Banks) that was three times bigger than his own.

The area of present-day [Natchitoches Parish, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natchitoches_Parish,_Louisiana), was settled by French Creole and mixed-race [Louisiana Creole people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Creole_people), starting before 1800. The [Cane River National Heritage Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cane_River_National_Heritage_Area), with [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_in_the_American_South) and churches founded by Louisiana Creoles, marks this area of influence. Some of the sites are designated as destinations on the [Louisiana African American Heritage Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_African_American_Heritage_Trail), established from 2008 onwards.

The area along the lower Red River of [Grant Parish, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Parish,_Louisiana), and neighboring parishes has a mixture of hill-country farms and cotton plantations.

### Great Raft



Red River, Texas

In the early 19th century, settlers found that much of the river's length in Louisiana was unnavigable because of a collection of fallen trees that formed a [Great Raft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Raft) over 160 miles (260 km) long. In 1839, Captain [Henry Miller Shreve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Miller_Shreve) began clearing the log jam, but it was not completely cleared until the 1870s, when [dynamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamite) became available. The river was thereafter navigable, although north of [Natchitoches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natchitoches,_Louisiana) it was restricted to small craft. Removal of the raft further connected the Red and [Atchafalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atchafalaya_River) rivers, accelerating the development of the Atchafalaya River channel.[^cite_note-Piazza2014-8]

### "Toll Bridge War"

The [Red River Bridge War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Bridge_War) of 1931 was a boundary conflict between Oklahoma and Texas over an existing toll bridge and a new free bridge crossing the Red River. A joint project to build a free bridge between [Durant, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durant,_Oklahoma), and Denison, Texas, turned into a major dispute when the [Texas Governor](214883.md) [Ross S. Sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_S._Sterling) blocked traffic from entering his state on the new bridge. The Red River Bridge Company of Texas owned the original toll bridge and had a dispute over its purchase deal. [Oklahoma Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Oklahoma) [William H. Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Murray) sent the Oklahoma National Guard to reopen the bridge that July. Texas had to retreat when lawyers determined that Oklahoma had jurisdiction over both banks of the river.[^cite_note-15]

### 2015 Red River flood

In June 2015, the [Red River flooded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Louisiana_floods) parts of northeast Texas, southwest Arkansas, southeast Oklahoma and Louisiana, from [Denison Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denison_Dam) to just south of [Alexandria, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Louisiana).[^cite_note-16] The river reached its highest level in over 70 years, cresting in most of the affected areas at around 6–9 feet over the flood levels.[^cite_note-theweathernetwork-17] At 4 PM on June 9, the river reached its maximum height of 37.14 feet.[^cite_note-18]

## Recreation

In 1943, Denison Dam was built on the Red River to form Lake Texoma, a large [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_\(water\)) of 89,000 acres (360 km<sup>2</sup>), some 70 miles (110 km) north of [Dallas](53838.md). Other reservoirs on the river's tributaries serve as [flood control](20913655.md).
