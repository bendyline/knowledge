**Budakeszi** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Wudigess* or *Wudigeß*) is a town in [Pest County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_County), in the [Budapest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_metropolitan_area), [Hungary](13275.md). It is located beyond the [János Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A1nos_Hill) at the western city limits of [Budapest](36787.md), about 12 km (7 mi) west of the [Zero Kilometre Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_Kilometre_Stone_\(Budapest\)) in the city centre. A popular recreational area, the landscape is characterized by forests, predominantly [oaks](39696.md), by vineyards and by orchards.

## Meaning of the name

[Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda) is the western side of Budapest, *Keszi* was one of the seven ancient [Hungarian tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyar_tribes).

## History

The settlement in the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_in_the_Middle_Ages) was first mentioned about 1270, it was completely devastated during the Ottoman [Siege of Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Buda_\(1541\)) in 1541. In the aftermath of the Ottoman defeat at the 1683 [Battle of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna), the depopulated area was re-settled with "[Danube Swabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians)" (most of them actually descending from [Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine_\(region\)), the [Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatinate_\(region\)) and [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace)) immigrants by the order of the [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) King [Leopold I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). Budakeszi was for centuries a predominantly "Schwabian" ([ethnic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_of_Hungary)) village.

After [World War II](32927.md) Budakeszi's history was influenced by the [deportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_\(1944%E2%80%931950\)) of its ethnic German population according to Article 12 of the 1945 [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement). Though the expulsion was ordered under pressure of the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) forces, many Hungarians relished in the humiliation of ethnic Germans throughout the region and collectively took part in organized, forced deportations of German-speaking citizens to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany). Most of these expellees had never been to Germany before, as even their great-grand parents had been born in Hungary. Budakeszi lost a great number of its citizens due to the above and the vacuum was later filled with the settlement of families from other regions, such as [Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania).

## Budakeszi today

Many inhabitants commute to work to the capital Budapest daily. With mass transportation (No. 22 bus or Volánbusz intercity bus) this normally takes about 15 minutes.

Budakeszi has a historic main street and a baroque Roman Catholic church in its valley. There is also a Protestant church, the cemetery of which has largely German-named headstones, some of which date back to the early 19th century. (There are also about a dozen [WW2](32927.md) Polish soldiers buried in the cemetery).

Today Budakeszi's population is mixed; Hungarians, [Schwabian-Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians), and Transylvanians share this small town. Near Budakeszi there is an airfield for [sailplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glider_\(aircraft\)) (gliders) and for small planes, as well as a recently established World War 2 German military cemetery.

## Notable citizens

Among the ethnic Germans expelled from Budakeszi in 1946 were the parents of [Joseph "Joschka" Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joschka_Fischer), born in 1948, German foreign minister from 1998 to 2005. Fischer today holds an honorary citizenship.

- [András Balczó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A1s_Balcz%C3%B3), Olympic gold medalist ([Modern Pentathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Pentathlon), 1972)

- [AWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWS_\(band\)), [metalcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalcore) band

- [Gáspár Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A1sp%C3%A1r_Nagy), [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet), [Kossuth Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kossuth_Prize) winner

- [Csaba Pléh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csaba_Pl%C3%A9h), [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist), [linguist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), [Széchenyi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9chenyi_Prize) winner
