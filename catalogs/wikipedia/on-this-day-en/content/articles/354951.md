The **Greater Tokyo Area** is the third most populous [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in the world, consisting of the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region) of [Japan](15573.md) (including [Tokyo Metropolis](30057.md) and the prefectures of [Chiba](179796.md), [Gunma](212632.md), [Ibaraki](181029.md), [Kanagawa](61757.md), [Saitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture), and [Tochigi](180960.md)) as well as the prefecture of [Yamanashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamanashi_Prefecture) of the neighboring [Chūbu region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu_region). In [Japanese](15606.md), it is referred to by various terms, one of the most common being **Capital Region** (首都圏, *Shuto-ken*).

As of 2026, the [United Nations](31769.md) estimates the total population at 36,954,512, making it the [third-most populous urban area in the world](14649921.md).[^cite_note-UNpopulation-2] It is the second-largest single metropolitan area in the world in terms of built-up or urban function landmass at 8,547 square kilometres (3,300 sq mi), behind only the [New York City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) at 4,495 square miles (11,640 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-3] With around US\$1.8 trillion in GDP, Tokyo remains the [second-largest metropolitan economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP) in the world, also behind New York. According to the [WIPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Intellectual_Property_Organization) [Innovation Cluster Rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation_Cluster_Rating) 2025, the region is one of the three leading science and technology [clusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_cluster) in the world.[^cite_note-4]

## Definition



Growth of densely inhabited districts (*[DIDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Densely_Inhabited_District?action=edit\&redlink=1), defined as >4,000 people/km<sup>2</sup> or >10,355 people/mi<sup>2</sup>*) of suburb [Saitama city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_\(city\)) (outlined) of northern Greater Tokyo, 1960s to 2010. Notice how the densely populated zones grow along railway lines and stations, the purple circles and lines. Green southern border is Tokyo.

There are various definitions of the Greater Tokyo Area, each of which tries to incorporate different aspects. Some definitions are clearly defined by law or government regulation, some are based coarsely on administrative areas, while others are for research purposes such as commuting patterns or distance from [Central Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Tokyo). Each definition has a different population figure, granularity, methodology, and spatial association.

### National Capital Region

The **National Capital Region** (首都圏, *Shutoken*) of Japan refers to the Greater Tokyo Area as defined by the [Metropolitan Area Readjustment Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Area_Readjustment_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1) (首都圏整備法, *Shutoken-seibi-hō*) of 1956, which defines it as "Tokyo and its surrounding area declared by [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government) ordinance." The government ordinance defined it as Tokyo and all six prefectures in the Kantō region plus Yamanashi Prefecture.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] While this includes all of Greater Tokyo, it also includes sparsely populated mountain areas as well as the far-flung [Bonin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonin_Islands) which are administered under Tokyo.

### International comparison

Using the "One Metropolis Three Prefectures" definition, Tokyo is 13,555.65 square kilometres (5,233.87 sq mi), a similar size to that of [Los Angeles County](79734.md), and almost two-thirds smaller than the [combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) of [New York City](645042.md), at 30,671 square kilometres (11,842 sq mi) and 21.9 million people. Other metropolitan areas such as [Greater Jakarta](1340014.md) are considerably more compact as well as more densely populated than Greater Tokyo.

### Metropolitan Area definition ambiguities and issues

- The South Kantō region (南関東, *Minami Kantō*) is a potentially ambiguous term. Informally, it may mean the One Metropolis, Two Prefectures, or the area without Saitama Prefecture. Formally, it may mean the South Kantō Block, which is not the Greater Tokyo Area, but a [proportional representation](23298.md) block of the national election, comprising Kanagawa, Chiba, and Yamanashi Prefectures.

- In informal occasions, the term [National Capital Region](#National_Capital_Region) (首都圏, *Shuto-ken*) often means Greater Tokyo Area. Officially, the term refers to a much larger area, namely the whole Kantō region and Yamanashi Prefecture.

- Tokyo as a metropolis includes some 394 km<sup>2</sup>\
  <sub></sub> of islands (the [Izu](728303.md) and [Ogasawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonin_Islands) islands), as well as some mountainous areas to the far west (331 km<sup>2</sup>\
  <sub></sub>), which are officially part of Greater Tokyo, but are wilderness or rustic areas.

### Cities within Tokyo

Tokyo is legally classified as a *to* (都), which translates as "metropolis", and is treated as one of the forty-seven [prefectures of Japan](74517.md). The metropolis is administered by the [Tokyo Metropolitan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government) as a whole.

#### Eastern Tokyo Metropolis

Central Tokyo, situated in the eastern portion of Tokyo Metropolis, was once incorporated as [Tokyo City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_City), which was dismantled during [World War II](32927.md). Its subdivisions have been reclassified as [special wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_wards_of_Tokyo) (特別区, *tokubetsu-ku*). The twenty three special wards currently have the legal status of cities, with individual mayors and city councils, and they call themselves "cities" in English. However, when listing [Japan's largest cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Japan), Tokyo's twenty three wards are often counted as a single city.

#### Border areas

Tighter definitions for Greater Tokyo do not include adjacent metropolitan areas of [Numazu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numazu)-[Mishima](328990.md) (approx. 450,000) to the southwest, [Maebashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maebashi)-[Takasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takasaki)-[Ōta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cta,_Gunma)-[Ashikaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashikaga,_Tochigi) (approx. 1,500,000 people) on the northwest, and [Greater Utsunomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Utsunomiya?action=edit\&redlink=1) (approx. 1,000,000) to the north. If they are included, Greater Tokyo's population would be around 39 million. Takasaki-Maebashi is included as part of the Tokyo-Yokohama area in the definition of urban areas by Demographia.

## Geography

At the center of the main urban area (approximately the first 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) from [Tokyo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Station)) are the 23 special wards, formerly treated as a single city but now governed as separate municipalities, and containing many major commercial centers such as [Shinjuku](147734.md), [Shibuya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibuya,_Tokyo), [Ikebukuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikebukuro) and [Ginza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginza). Around the 23 special wards are a multitude of suburban cities which merge seamlessly into each other to form a continuous built up area, circumnavigated by the heavily travelled [Route 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_16) which forms a (broken) loop about 40 kilometres (25 mi) from central Tokyo. Situated along the loop are the major cities of Yokohama (to the south of Tokyo), Hachiōji (to the west), Ōmiya (now part of Saitama City, to the north), and Chiba (to the east).

Within the Route 16 loop, the coastline of [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay) is heavily industrialised, with the [Keihin Industrial Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihin_Industrial_Area) stretching from Tokyo down to Yokohama, and the [Keiyō Industrial Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiy%C5%8D_Industrial_Zone) from Tokyo eastwards to Chiba. Along the periphery of the main urban area are numerous new suburban housing developments such as the [Tama New Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_New_Town). The landscape is relatively flat compared to most of Japan, most of it comprising low hills.

Outside the Route 16 loop the landscape becomes more rural. To the southwest is an area known as [Shōnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dnan), which contains various cities and towns along the coast of [Sagami Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_Bay), and to the west the area is mountainous.

Many rivers run through the area, the major ones being [Arakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakawa_River_\(Kanto\)) and [Tama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_River).

### Air

The Greater Tokyo Area has two major airports, [Tokyo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport), commonly known as Haneda Airport (once chiefly domestic, now turning international) and [Narita International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita_International_Airport) (chiefly international as well). Minor facilities include [Chōfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%8Dfu_Airport) and [Ibaraki Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaraki_Airport). [Tokyo Heliport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Heliport) serves helicopter traffic, including police, fire, and news. [Japan Civil Aviation Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Civil_Aviation_Bureau) handles air traffic in large part but various military facilities handle air traffic in part: [Hyakuri Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaraki_Airport) ([Japan Air Self-Defense Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Air_Self-Defense_Force)), [Utsunomiya Air Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utsunomiya_Air_Field) ([Japan Ground Self-Defense Force](2978818.md)), and [Yokota Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokota_Air_Base) ([United States Air Force](32090.md)).[^cite_note-15]

### Rail

Greater Tokyo has an extensive railway network comprising [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen), [commuter rails](6286.md), [subways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit), [monorails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monorail), [private lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_railway), [trams](30733.md) and others. There are around 136 individual rail lines in the Greater Tokyo Area, and between 1,000 and 1,200 railway stations depending on one's definition of the area, most designed for heavy use, usually long enough to accommodate 10-car (200 metres (660 ft) long) trains. Stations are designed to accommodate hundreds of thousands of passengers at any given time, with miles of connecting tunnels linking vast department stores and corporate offices. [Tokyo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Station) has underground connections that stretch well over 4 kilometres (2.5 mi), and [Shinjuku Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Station) has well over 200 exits. Greater Tokyo's Railway Network is easily considered the world's largest in terms of both daily passenger throughput with a daily trips of over 40 million (20 million different passengers) as well as physical extent with approximately 2,578 kilometres (1,602 mi) of track. Shinjuku station is used by an average of 3.34 million people per day, making it the world's busiest train station. Some 57 percent of all Greater Tokyo residents used rail as their primary means of transport in 2001.[^cite_note-16]

[JR East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Japan_Railway_Company) and many other carriers crisscross the region with a network of rail lines. The most important carriers include [Keihin Kyūkō Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihin_Electric_Express_Railway) (*Keikyū*), [Keisei Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Electric_Railway), [Keiō Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keio_Electric_Railway), [Odakyū Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odakyu_Electric_Railway), [Seibu Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seibu_Railway), [Tōbu Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobu_Railway), and [Tōkyū Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyu_Corporation). In addition to Tokyo's two subway systems — [Tokyo Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metro) and [Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Bureau_of_Transportation) (*[Toei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toei_Subway)* and *[Toden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Toden)* lines), Yokohama also has two [subway lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama_Municipal_Subway).

The [Tokyo Monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Monorail) provides an important shuttle service between Haneda Airport and Hamamatsucho station on the Yamanote line.

### Others

The [Shuto Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuto_Expressway) system connects to other national [expressways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressways_of_Japan) in the capital region.

Tokyo and Yokohama are major commercial seaports, and both the [Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force](2370744.md) and [United States Navy](20518076.md) maintain naval bases at [Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fleet_Activities_Yokosuka).
