**Omaha**[^cite_note-8] is the [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Nebraska) in the U.S. state of [Nebraska](21647.md). It is located in the [Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) along the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), about 10 mi (15 km) north of the mouth of the [Platte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platte_River). Omaha had a population of 486,051 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), and with a 2025 population estimate of 488,797, it is the [42nd-most populous U.S. city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population).[^cite_note-QuickFacts-9] The eight-county [Omaha–Council Bluffs metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha%E2%80%93Council_Bluffs_metropolitan_area) extending into [Iowa](26810748.md) has approximately 1 million residents, the [55th-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area#United_States) in the U.S.[^cite_note-2024Pop-5] It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Douglas County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-GR6-10]

Omaha's pioneer period began in 1854, when the city was founded along the Missouri River by speculators from neighboring [Council Bluffs, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Bluffs,_Iowa). It originally comprised a crossing called [Lone Tree Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_Tree_Ferry) earning the city its nickname, the "Gateway to the West". Omaha introduced this new West to the world in 1898, when it played host to the World's Fair, dubbed the [Trans-Mississippi Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Mississippi_Exposition). During the 19th century, Omaha's central location in the United States spurred the city to become an important national [transportation hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Omaha). Throughout the rest of the 19th century, the transportation and [jobbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wholesale) sectors were important in the city, along with its [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroads_in_Omaha) and [breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breweries). In the 20th century, the [Omaha Stockyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Stockyards), once the world's largest, and its [meatpacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meatpacking) plants gained international prominence.

Omaha is home to the headquarters of four [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies: [Berkshire Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_Hathaway), [Kiewit Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiewit_Corporation), [Mutual of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_of_Omaha), and [Union Pacific Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Corporation).[^cite_note-11] Other companies headquartered in the city include [First National Bank of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_National_Bank_of_Omaha), [Gallup, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup,_Inc.), [Green Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Plains), [Intrado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrado), [Valmont Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmont_Industries), [Werner Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Enterprises), [WoodmenLife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WoodmenLife), and three of the nation's ten largest architecture and engineering firms ([DLR Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DLR_Group), [HDR, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDR,_Inc.), and [Leo A Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_A_Daly)).[^cite_note-BDC_2010-12] Notable cultural institutions include the [Henry Doorly Zoo and Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Doorly_Zoo_and_Aquarium), [Old Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)), [Durham Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Museum,_Omaha,_Nebraska), [Lauritzen Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauritzen_Gardens), and the annual [College World Series](536107.md). Modern Omaha inventions include the [Reuben sandwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuben_sandwich);[^cite_note-13] cake mix, developed by [Duncan Hines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Hines); [center-pivot irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center-pivot_irrigation);[^cite_note-14] [Raisin Bran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin_Bran); the first [ski lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_lift) in the U.S.;[^cite_note-15] the [Top 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_40) radio format, which was first used in the U.S. at [KOWH Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KCRO#History); and the [TV dinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_dinner).[^cite_note-16]

## History

Main article: [History of Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Omaha,_Nebraska)

See also: [History of North Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_North_Omaha,_Nebraska)



[Logan Fontenelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Fontenelle), an interpreter for the [Omaha Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Tribe) when it ceded the land that became the city of Omaha to the U.S. government

Various [Native American tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_tribes_in_Nebraska) had lived in the land that became Omaha since the 17th century, including the [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_people) and [Ponca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponca), [Dhegihan-Siouan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhegihan_languages) people who had originated in the lower [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) valley and migrated west by the early 17th century; [Pawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawnee_people), [Otoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otoe), [Missouria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouria), and [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_people). The word *Omaha* ([Omaha–Ponca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha%E2%80%93Ponca_language): *Umoⁿhoⁿ* or *Umaⁿhaⁿ*) in the Omaha language means 'Upstream People' or 'Against the Current'.

In 1804 the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition) passed the riverbanks where the city of Omaha would be built. Between July 30 and August 3, 1804, members of the expedition, including [Meriwether Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriwether_Lewis) and [William Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clark), met with Oto and Missouria tribal leaders at the [Council Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Bluff,_Nebraska) at a point about 20 mi (32 km) north of present-day Omaha.[^cite_note-17] Immediately south of that area, Americans built several fur trading outposts in succeeding years, including [Fort Lisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lisa_\(Nebraska\)) in 1812;[^cite_note-18] [Fort Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Atkinson_\(Nebraska\)) in 1819;[^cite_note-19] [Cabanné's Trading Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabann%C3%A9's_Trading_Post), built in 1822, and [Fontenelle's Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontenelle's_Post) in 1823, in what became [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-20] There was fierce competition among fur traders until [John Jacob Astor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jacob_Astor) created the monopoly of the [American Fur Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Fur_Company). The [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons) built a town called [Cutler's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutler's_Park) in the area in 1846.[^cite_note-21] While it was temporary, the settlement provided the basis for further development.[^cite_note-22]

Through 26 separate treaties with the United States federal government, [Native American tribes in Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_tribes_in_Nebraska) gradually ceded the lands that now make up the state. The treaty and cession involving the Omaha area occurred in 1854 when the [Omaha Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Tribe) ceded most of east-central Nebraska.[^cite_note-23] [Logan Fontenelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Fontenelle), an interpreter for the Omaha and signatory to the 1854 treaty, played an essential role in those proceedings.

### Pioneer Omaha

Before it was legal to claim land in [Indian Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Country), [William D. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_D._Brown) operated the [Lone Tree Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_Tree_Ferry) that brought settlers from Council Bluffs, Iowa, to the area that became Omaha. Brown is generally credited as having the first vision for a city where Omaha now sits.[^cite_note-Administration1-24] The passage of the [Kansas–Nebraska Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas%E2%80%93Nebraska_Act) in 1854 was presaged by the staking out of claims around the area to become Omaha by residents from neighboring Council Bluffs. On July 4, 1854, the city was informally established at a picnic on Capital Hill, current site of [Omaha Central High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Central_High_School).[^cite_note-25] Soon after, the [Omaha Claim Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Claim_Club) was formed to provide [vigilante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilante) justice for [claim jumpers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_claim) and others who infringed on the land of many of [the city's founding fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_figures_of_Omaha,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-26] Some of this land, which now wraps around Downtown Omaha, was later used to entice [Nebraska Territorial legislators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Territorial_Legislature) to an area called [Scriptown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scriptown).[^cite_note-27] The Territorial capitol was in Omaha, but when Nebraska became a state in 1867, the capital was relocated to [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska), 53 mi (85 km) southwest of Omaha.[^cite_note-28] The [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) later ruled against numerous landowners whose violent actions were condemned in *[Baker v. Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_v._Morton)*.[^cite_note-29]

Many of Omaha's founding figures stayed at the [Douglas House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_House_\(Omaha\)) or the [Cozzens House Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozzens_House_Hotel).[^cite_note-30] [Dodge Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Street) was important early in the city's early commercial history; [North 24th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_24th_Street) and [South 24th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Omaha_Main_Street_Historic_District) also developed independently as business districts. Early pioneers were buried in [Prospect Hill Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_Hill_Cemetery_\(North_Omaha,_Nebraska\)) and Cedar Hill Cemetery.[^cite_note-31] Cedar Hill closed in the 1860s and its graves were moved to Prospect Hill, where pioneers were later joined by soldiers from [Fort Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Omaha), [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) and early [European immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Omaha,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-32] There are several other [historical cemeteries in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cemeteries_in_Omaha,_Nebraska), historical [Jewish synagogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_and_Judaism_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) and historical [Christian churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) dating from the pioneer era, as well. Two sculpture parks, Pioneer Courage and Spirit of Nebraska's Wilderness and [The Transcontinental Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_Railroad), celebrate the city's pioneering history.[^cite_note-33]

### 19th century



[Hotel Fontenelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Fontenelle), formerly in downtown Omaha

The [economy of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Omaha) boomed and busted through its early years. In 1858, the *Omaha Daily Republican* was founded by the *Omaha Printing Company (rebranded Aradius Group, 2016)*, it was Nebraska's first regional newspaper–founded before Nebraska claimed statehood. Omaha was a stopping point for settlers and prospectors heading west, either overland or by the Missouri River. The steamboat *[Bertrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_\(steamboat\))* sank north of Omaha on its way to the goldfields in 1865. Its massive collection of artifacts is on display at the nearby [Desoto National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desoto_National_Wildlife_Refuge). The [jobbing and wholesaling district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jobber's_Canyon) brought new jobs, followed by the [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroads_in_Omaha) and the stockyards.[^cite_note-34] Groundbreaking for the [First transcontinental railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad) in 1863, provided an essential developmental boom for the city.[^cite_note-35] In 1862, the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) allowed the [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad) to begin building westward railways;[^cite_note-Union_Pacific_RR_1867-36][^cite_note-37] in January 1866 it commenced construction out of Omaha.[^cite_note-Dunbar-38]

The [Union Stockyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Stockyards_\(Omaha\)), another important part of the city's development, were founded in South Omaha in 1883.[^cite_note-39] Within 20 years, Omaha had four of the five major [meatpacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meatpacking) companies in the United States. By the 1950s, half the city's workforce was employed in meatpacking and processing. Meatpacking, jobbing and railroads were responsible for most of the growth in the city from the late 19th century through the early decades of the 20th century.[^cite_note-40]

[Immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) soon created [ethnic enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_enclave) throughout the city, including Irish in [Sheelytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheelytown) in South Omaha; Germans in the [Near North Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_North_Side_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)), joined by the European Jews and black migrants from [the South](179553.md); and [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)) and [Little Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Bohemia_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)) in South Omaha.[^cite_note-41] Beginning in the late 19th century, Omaha's upper class lived in posh enclaves throughout the city, including the south and [north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Historic_District_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)) Gold Coast neighborhoods, [Bemis Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemis_Park), [Kountze Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kountze_Place), [Field Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Club) and throughout [Midtown Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Omaha). They traveled the city's sprawling [park system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) on [boulevards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevards_in_Omaha) designed by renowned [landscape architect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_architect) [Horace Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Cleveland).[^cite_note-42] The [Omaha Horse Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Horse_Railway) first carried passengers throughout the city, as did the later [Omaha Cable Tramway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Cable_Tramway_Company) and several similar companies. In 1888, the [Omaha and Council Bluffs Railway and Bridge Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_and_Council_Bluffs_Railway_and_Bridge_Company) built the [Douglas Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Street_Bridge), the first pedestrian and wagon bridge between Omaha and Council Bluffs.[^cite_note-43]

Gambling, drinking and prostitution were widespread in the 19th century, first rampant in the city's [Burnt District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnt_District,_Omaha) and later in the [Sporting District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_District_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)).[^cite_note-44] Controlled by Omaha's [political boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_boss) [Tom Dennison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Dennison_\(Political_boss\)) by 1890, [criminal elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Omaha) enjoyed support from Omaha's "perpetual" mayor, ["Cowboy Jim"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dahlman) Dahlman, nicknamed for his eight terms as mayor.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

Calamities such as the [Great Flood of 1881](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood_of_1881) did not slow down the city's violence.[^cite_note-47] In 1882, the [Camp Dump Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Dump_Strike) pitted state militia against unionized strikers, drawing national attention to Omaha's labor troubles. The [Governor of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Nebraska) had to call in [U.S. Army](32087.md) troops from nearby Fort Omaha to protect [strikebreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikebreakers) for the [Burlington Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Railroad), bringing along [Gatling guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatling_gun) and a [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) for defense. When the event ended, one man was dead and several were wounded.[^cite_note-48] In 1891, a mob hanged [Joe Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Coe), an African-American porter after he was accused of raping a white girl.[^cite_note-Bristow_2000-49] There were also several other [riots and civil unrest events in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_riots_and_civil_unrest_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) during this period.

In 1898, Omaha's leaders, under the guidance of [Gurdon Wattles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdon_Wattles), held the [Trans-Mississippi and International Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Mississippi_and_International_Exposition), touted as a celebration of agricultural and industrial growth throughout the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest).[^cite_note-50] The [Indian Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Congress), which drew more than 500 [American Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) from across the country, was held simultaneously. More than 2 million visitors attended these events at [Kountze Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kountze_Park) and the [Omaha Driving Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Driving_Park) in the Kountze Place neighborhood.[^cite_note-51]

### 20th century

#### To the 1960s



Men working at Omaha's Union Stockyards

With dramatically increasing population in the 20th century, competition and fierce labor struggles led to major civil unrest.[^cite_note-52] In 1900, Omaha was the center of a national uproar over the [kidnapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnap) of [Edward Cudahy, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Cudahy,_Jr.), the son of a local meatpacking magnate.[^cite_note-53] The city's labor and management clashed in bitter strikes, [racial tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_tension_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) escalated as blacks were hired as strikebreakers, and ethnic strife broke out.[^cite_note-54] A [major riot by earlier immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Town_riot) in South Omaha destroyed the city's [Greek Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Town) in 1909, completely driving out the Greek population.[^cite_note-55]

The [civil rights movement in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Movement_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) traces to 1912, when the first chapter of the [National Association for the Advancement of Colored People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Colored_People) west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) was founded in the city.[^cite_note-56] The [Omaha Easter Sunday Tornado of 1913](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Easter_Sunday_Tornado_\(1913\)) destroyed much of the city's [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) community, in addition to much of Midtown Omaha.[^cite_note-57] During that year, future [United States President](24113.md) [Gerald R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford) was born in Omaha.[^cite_note-58] Today, there is a [museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford_Birthsite_and_Gardens) dedicated to his birthplace.[^cite_note-59]

In 1919, the city was caught up in the [Red Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Summer) riots when thousands of whites marched from South Omaha to the courthouse to lynch a black worker, Willy Brown, a suspect in an alleged rape of a white woman. The mob burned the [Douglas County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County_Courthouse_\(Nebraska\)) to get the prisoner, causing more than \$1 million damage. They hanged and shot Will Brown, then burned his body.[^cite_note-60] Troops were called in from Fort Omaha to quell the riot, prevent more crowds gathering in South Omaha, and to protect the black community in North Omaha.[^cite_note-61]

The [culture of North Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_in_North_Omaha,_Nebraska) thrived throughout the 1920s through 1950s, with several creative figures, including [Tillie Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillie_Olsen), [Wallace Thurman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Thurman), [Lloyd Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Hunter), and [Anna Mae Winburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Mae_Winburn) emerging from the vibrant Near North Side.[^cite_note-62] Musicians created their own world in Omaha, and also joined national bands and groups that toured and appeared in the city.[^cite_note-63]

After the tumultuous [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) of the 1930s, Omaha rebounded with the development of [Offutt Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offutt_Air_Force_Base) just south of the city. The [Glenn L. Martin Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_L._Martin_Company) operated a factory there in the 1940s that produced 521 [B-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-29) *Superfortresses*, including the *[Enola Gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enola_Gay)* and *[Bockscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockscar)* used in [the atomic bombing of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) in World War II.[^cite_note-64] The construction of [Interstates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstates) [80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80), [480](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_480_\(Iowa%E2%80%93Nebraska\)) and [680](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_680_\(Iowa%E2%80%93Nebraska\)), along with the [North Omaha Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Omaha_Freeway), spurred development. There was also controversy, particularly in North Omaha, where new routes bisected several neighborhoods.[^cite_note-65] [Creighton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creighton_University) hosted the [DePorres Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePorres_Club), an early civil rights group whose use of sit-in strategies for integration of public facilities predated the national movement.[^cite_note-66]

Following the development of the Glenn L. Martin Company bomber manufacturing plant in Bellevue at the beginning of World War II, the relocation of the [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) to the Omaha suburb in 1948 provided a major economic boost to the area.[^cite_note-67]

Insurance boomed from the 1950s through the 1960s: more than 40 insurance companies were headquartered in Omaha, including [Woodmen of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodmen_of_the_World) and [Mutual of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_of_Omaha). The city rivaled but never surpassed the United States insurance centers of [Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut), New York City, and [Boston](24437894.md).[^cite_note-68] After surpassing Chicago in [meat processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_processing) by the late 1950s, Omaha suffered the loss of 10,000 jobs as both the railroad and meatpacking industries restructured. The city struggled for decades to shift its economy as workers suffered. Poverty became more entrenched among families who remained in North Omaha. In the 1960s, three major race riots along North 24th Street destroyed the Near North Side's economic base, with recovery slow for decades.[^cite_note-69] In 1969, [Woodmen Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodmen_Tower) was completed and became Omaha's tallest building and first major skyscraper at 478 ft (146 m), a sign of renewal.

#### 1970s and onwards

Since the 1970s, Omaha has continued expanding and growing, mostly to available land to the west. [West Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Omaha) has become home to the majority of the city's population. North and South Omaha's populations continue to be centers of new immigrants, with economic and racial diversity. In 1975 a [major tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Omaha_tornado_outbreak), along with a [major blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Storm_of_1975), caused more than [\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/\$)100 million in damages in 1975 dollars.[^cite_note-70]

Downtown Omaha has since been rejuvenated in numerous ways, starting with the development of [Gene Leahy Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Leahy_Mall)[^cite_note-71] and [W. Dale Clark Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Dale_Clark_Library)[^cite_note-72] in the late 1970s. In the 1980s, Omaha's fruit warehouses were converted into a shopping area called the [Old Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)). The demolition of [Jobber's Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jobber's_Canyon) in 1989 led to the creation of the [ConAgra Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConAgra_Foods) campus.[^cite_note-73] Several nearby buildings, including the [Nash Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_Block), have been converted into condominiums. The stockyards were taken down; the only surviving building is the [Livestock Exchange Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock_Exchange_Building_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)), which was converted to multi-use and listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).[^cite_note-74]

A [historic preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_preservation) movement in Omaha has led to a number of historic structures and districts being designated [Omaha Landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Landmark) or listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Much of the push toward preservation came after Omaha gained the notorious designation of having, in 1989, demolished the largest-ever [National Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) historic district in the United States, a record that still stands as of 2013. The [Jobbers Canyon Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jobbers_Canyon_Historic_District), along the Missouri River, was felled for a new headquarters campus for ConAgra Foods, a company which threatened to relocate if Omaha did not allow them to raze the city's historic district. The Jobber's Canyon warehouses had before then been allowed to deteriorate and were the scene of several fires set by the homeless population that had come to live in the abandoned buildings. At the time, there were no plans in place for revitalizing the buildings.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

In the 1980s and 1990s, Omaha also saw major company headquarters leave the city, including [Enron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enron), founded in the city in 1930 and taken to Houston in 1987 by the now-notorious [Kenneth Lay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Lay). [First Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Data) Corporation, a large credit-card processor, also was founded in Omaha in 1969; as of 2009, its headquarters are in Atlanta.

[Inacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inacom), founded in Omaha in 1991, was a technology company that customized computer systems for large businesses, and was on the Fortune 500 list from 1997 until 2000, when it filed for bankruptcy. [Northwestern Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Bell), the [Bell System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_System) affiliate for Northwestern states, had its headquarters in Omaha from its founding in 1896 until it moved to Denver in 1991 as [US West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_West). [Level 3 Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_3_Communications), a large [Tier 1 network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tier_1_network) provider, was founded in Omaha in 1985 as Kiewit Diversified Group, a division of [Kiewit Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiewit_Corporation), a Fortune 500 construction and mining company still headquartered in Omaha; Level 3 moved to Denver in 1998. [World Com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Com) was founded by a merger with Omaha's MFS Communications, started as [Metropolitan Fiber Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Fiber_Systems) in 1993. MFS, backed by Kiewit Corporation CEO [Walter Scott Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Scott_Jr.) and Omaha native [Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett), purchased [UUNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UUNET), one of the largest Internet backbones in the world, for \$2 billion in 1996. The now-infamous [Bernie Ebbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Ebbers) purchased the much larger MFS for \$14.3 billion in 1997 under his World Com. He moved headquarters of the merged company from Omaha to Mississippi.[^cite_note-78]

### 21st century



[First National Bank Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_National_Bank_Tower), the tallest building in Omaha



Omaha at night, in 2021, as seen from the intersection of 10th and Capitol streets



[Bob Kerrey Pedestrian Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Kerrey_Pedestrian_Bridge)

Around the start of the 21st century, several downtown skyscrapers and cultural institutions were built.[^cite_note-79]

The [First National Bank Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_National_Bank_Tower) on Dodge Street was completed in 2002 and is the [tallest building in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) and [the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Nebraska), surpassing the Woodmen Tower as the tallest in both at 634 ft (193 m). The creation of the city's new [North Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NoDo) included the construction of the [CenturyLink Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CenturyLink_Center_Omaha) and the [Slowdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slowdown_\(venue\))/[Film Streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Streams) development at North 14th and Webster Streets.[^cite_note-80] Construction of the new [TD Ameritrade Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TD_Ameritrade_Park), also in the North Downtown area, began in 2009 and was completed in 2011. TD Ameritrade Park is now the home of the [College World Series](536107.md), an event tourists flock to each year. The [Union Pacific Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Center) and the [Holland Performing Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_Performing_Arts_Center) opened in 2004 and 2005, respectively.

Important retail and office developments occurred in West Omaha, such as the Village Pointe shopping center and several business parks.[^cite_note-81] The site of the former [Ak-Sar-Ben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak-Sar-Ben_\(arena\)) arena was redeveloped into a [mixed-use development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-use_development) [Aksarben Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksarben_Village). In January 2009, [Blue Cross Blue Shield of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Cross_and_Blue_Shield_Association) announced plans to build a 10 story, \$98 million headquarters in the Aksarben Village which it completed in Spring 2011.[^cite_note-82] Another major mixed-use development to come to Omaha was [Midtown Crossing at Turner Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Crossing_at_Turner_Park). Developed by Mutual of Omaha, the development includes several condominium towers and retail businesses built around Omaha's Turner Park.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-bydesign-84]

There have also been several developments along the Missouri River waterfront near downtown. The [Bob Kerrey Pedestrian Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Kerrey_Pedestrian_Bridge) was opened to foot and bicycle traffic on September 28, 2008.[^cite_note-85] Started in 2003,[^cite_note-86] [RiverFront Place Condos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RiverFront_Place_Condos) first phase was completed in 2006 and the second phase was opened in 2011. The development along Omaha's riverfront is attributed with prompting the City of Council Bluffs to move their own riverfront development time line forward.[^cite_note-87]

In the summers of 2008, 2012, 2016, and 2021 the [United States Olympic Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_Summer_Olympics) swimming trials were held in Omaha, at the Qwest/Century Link Center.[^cite_note-swimmingteam-88] These events were highlights in [the city's sports community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Omaha),[^cite_note-89] as well as a showcase for redevelopment in the downtown area.

In the 2020s, a number of large projects have been either completed or planned in an attempt to revitalize downtown Omaha.[^cite_note-90] These include the redevelopment of the Gene Leahy Mall, a large park near Omaha's Riverfront,[^cite_note-91] and the [Omaha Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Streetcar), a nearly \$500 million system of public transit.[^cite_note-92] A new skyscraper, the [Mutual of Omaha Headquarters Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_of_Omaha_Headquarters_Tower), at 677 feet (206 m), will be the new tallest building in Omaha and the state upon its completion in 2026.

Douglas County treasurer [John Ewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ewing_Jr.) was [elected mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Omaha_mayoral_election) in 2025, ending Stothert's 12-year long administration. He is the first black mayor of Omaha.[^cite_note-93]

## Geography

See also: [Geography of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Omaha) and [Omaha-Council Bluffs metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha-Council_Bluffs_metropolitan_area)

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 130.58 sq mi (338.20 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 127.09 sq mi (329.16 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 3.49 sq mi (9.04 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_files-94] Situated in the Midwestern United States on the bank of the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) in eastern Nebraska, much of Omaha is built in the [Missouri River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River_Valley). Other significant bodies of water in the Omaha-Council Bluffs metropolitan area include Lake Manawa, [Papillion Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papillion_Creek), [Carter Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Lake_\(Iowa%E2%80%93Nebraska\)), Platte River and the [Glenn Cunningham Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Cunningham_Lake). The city's land has been altered considerably with substantial [land grading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grading) throughout Downtown Omaha and scattered across the city.[^cite_note-95] [East Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Omaha) sits on a [flood plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_plain) west of the Missouri River. The area is the location of Carter Lake, an [oxbow lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbow_lake). The lake was once the site of East Omaha Island and Florence Lake, which dried up in the 1920s.

### Metropolitan area

The Omaha-Council Bluffs metropolitan area consists of eight counties; five in Nebraska and three in Iowa.[^cite_note-96] The metropolitan area now includes [Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_County,_Iowa), [Pottawattamie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottawattamie_County,_Iowa), and [Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mills_County,_Iowa) Counties in Iowa and [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Nebraska), Douglas, [Sarpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarpy_County,_Nebraska), [Cass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cass_County,_Nebraska), and [Saunders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_County,_Nebraska) Counties in Nebraska. This area was formerly referred to only as the Omaha Metropolitan Statistical Area and consisted of only five counties: Pottawattamie in Iowa, and Washington, Douglas, Cass, and Sarpy in Nebraska.[^cite_note-97] The Omaha-Council Bluffs [combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) comprises the Omaha-Council Bluffs [metropolitan statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) and the [Fremont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont,_Nebraska) [Micropolitan statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micropolitan_statistical_area); the CSA has a population of 858,720 (2005 Census Bureau estimate). Omaha ranks as the [41st-most populous city in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population), and is the core city of its 60th-largest metropolitan area.[^cite_note-98] There are no [consolidated city-counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_city-counties) in the area; the [City of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Omaha) studied the possibility extensively through 2003 and concluded, "The City of Omaha and Douglas County should merge into a municipal county, work to commence immediately, and that functional consolidations begin immediately in as many departments as possible, including but not limited to parks, [fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_vehicle) management, facilities management, [local planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_planning), purchasing and personnel."[^cite_note-99]

Geographically, Omaha is considered as being in the "Heartland" of the United States. Important environmental impacts on the natural habitat in the area include the spread of [invasive plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_plant) species, restoring [prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) and [bur oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bur_oak) [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) habitats, and managing the [whitetail deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitetail_deer) population.[^cite_note-100]

Omaha is home to several hospitals, mostly along Dodge Street (US6). Being the county seat, it is also the location of the county courthouse.

### Neighborhoods

Main article: [Neighborhoods of Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Omaha,_Nebraska)



Downtown: lime, Midtown: blue-gray, North: red, South: pink, West: lavender



View from above West Omaha

Omaha is generally divided into six geographic areas: Downtown, Midtown, North Omaha, South Omaha, West Omaha, and East Omaha. West Omaha includes the Miracle Hills, [Boys Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_Town,_Nebraska), Regency, and Gateway areas.[^cite_note-bydesign-84] The city has a wide range of historical and new neighborhoods and [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbs) that reflect its [socioeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic) diversity. Early neighborhood development happened in ethnic enclaves,[^cite_note-101] including [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)), [Little Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Bohemia_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)), Little Mexico and [Greek Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Town).[^cite_note-Compr1980-102]<sup>: 79 </sup> According to U.S. Census data, five European ethnic enclaves existed in Omaha in 1880, expanding to nine in 1900.[^cite_note-103]

Around the start of the 20th century. the City of Omaha annexed several surrounding communities, including [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence,_Nebraska), [Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee,_Nebraska) and [Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benson,_Nebraska). At the same time, the city annexed all of South Omaha, including the [Dahlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlman_neighborhood) and [Burlington Road neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Road_neighborhood). From its first annexation in 1857 (of East Omaha) to its controversial annexation of [Elkhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elkhorn,_Nebraska) in 2007, Omaha has continually had an eye towards growth.[^cite_note-104]

Starting in the 1950s, development of highways and new housing led to the movement of the middle class to suburbs in West Omaha. Some of the movement was designated as [white flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_flight) from racial unrest in the 1960s.[^cite_note-105] Newer and poorer migrants lived in older housing close to downtown; those residents who were more established moved west into newer housing. Some suburbs are [gated communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gated_communities) or have become [edge cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_cities).[^cite_note-106] Recently, Omahans have made strides to revitalize the downtown and Midtown areas with the redevelopment of the Old Market, Turner Park, Gifford Park, and the designation of the [Omaha Rail and Commerce Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Rail_and_Commerce_Historic_District).[^cite_note-107]

### Climate



Climate chart for Omaha

Omaha, due to its latitude of 41.26˚ N and location far from moderating bodies of water or mountain ranges, displays a hot-summer [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfa*).[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] July averages 76.7 °F (24.8 °C), with average relative humidity around 70% which then leads to relatively frequent thunderstorms. Temperatures reach 90 °F (32 °C) on 29 days and 100 °F (38 °C) on 1.7 days annually. The January daily average is 23.5 °F (−4.7 °C), with lows reaching 0 °F (−18 °C) on 11 days annually. The lowest temperature recorded in the city was −32 °F (−35.6 °C) on January 5, 1884,[^cite_note-Jan_Averages-110] and the highest 114 °F (45.6 °C) on [July 25, 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_North_American_heat_wave).[^cite_note-Jul_Averages-111] Average yearly precipitation is 30.6 in (777 mm), falling mostly in the warmer months. Snow is the most common precipitation in winter, with average seasonal snowfall being 28.7 in (72.9 cm).

Based on 30-year averages obtained from [NOAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA)'s [National Climatic Data Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Climatic_Data_Center) for the months of December, January and February, [Weather Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Channel) ranked Omaha the 5th coldest major U.S. city as of 2014.[^cite_note-112]

### Crime

Main article: [Crime in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Omaha,_Nebraska)

Further information: [Gambling in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling_in_Omaha,_Nebraska)

As a major industrial city into the mid-20th century, Omaha shared in social tensions that came with rapid growth and the arrival of large numbers of immigrants and migrants. Persistent poverty resulting from racial discrimination and job losses generated different crimes in the late 20th century, with the drug trade and drug abuse becoming associated with violent crime rates, which climbed after 1986 as Los Angeles gangs made affiliates in the city.[^cite_note-132]

Gambling in Omaha has been an important part of the city's history. From its founding in the 1850s through the 1930s, the city was known as a "wide-open" town where gambling of all sorts was openly accepted. By the 1950s, at the same time large-scale restructuring of the railroads, the meatpacking industry and other sectors caused widespread job losses and unemployment, Omaha reportedly had more illicit gambling than any other city in the nation.[^cite_note-133] From the 1930s through the 1970s, a Mafia-based criminal element controlled gambling in the city.[^cite_note-134]

As most forms of gambling are currently restricted in Nebraska, gambling in Omaha is limited to [keno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keno), [lotteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotteries), and [parimutuel betting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parimutuel_betting). This leaves Omahans to drive across the Missouri River to Council Bluffs, Iowa, where casinos are legal and many businesses operate. Recently, the [National Indian Gaming Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Indian_Gaming_Commission) approved a controversial proposal made by the [Ponca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponca) tribe of Nebraska. It will allow the tribe to build a casino in [Carter Lake, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Lake,_Iowa), which sits on the west side of the Missouri River, adjacent to Omaha, where casinos are illegal.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

### People

Main article: [Ethnic groups in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Omaha,_Nebraska)



[Saint Cecilia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Cecilia_Cathedral)

[Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_tribes_in_Nebraska) were the first residents of the Omaha area. The city of Omaha was established by white settlers from neighboring Council Bluffs who arrived from the [Mid-Atlantic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_states) a few years earlier. While much of the early population was of [Upland Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South) stock, over the next 100 years numerous [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) moved to the city. In 1910, the Census Bureau reported Omaha's population as 96.4% White and 3.6% Black.[^cite_note-138] [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) immigrants in Omaha originally moved to an area in present-day North Omaha called [Gophertown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gophertown), as they lived in dug-out [sod houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sod_house).[^cite_note-Bristow_2000-49] That population was followed by [Polish immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) in the [Sheelytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheelytown) neighborhood, and many immigrants were recruited for jobs in South Omaha's [stockyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Stockyards_\(Omaha\)) and meatpacking industry.[^cite_note-139] The [German community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) in Omaha was largely responsible for founding its once-thriving beer industry,[^cite_note-140] including the [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metz_Brewery), [Krug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krug_Brewery), [Falstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falstaff_Brewing_Corporation) and [Storz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storz_Brewery) breweries.

Since its founding, [ethnic groups in the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) have clustered in [enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_enclave) in [north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Omaha,_Nebraska), [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Omaha,_Nebraska) and [downtown Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Omaha). In its early days, the [sometimes lawless nature of a new frontier city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Omaha,_Nebraska) included [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Omaha), such as [illicit gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) and [riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_riots_and_civil_unrest_in_Omaha,_Nebraska).

In the early 20th century, [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_and_Judaism_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) immigrants set up many businesses along the [North 24th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_24th_Street) commercial area. It suffered with the loss of industrial jobs in the 1960s and, later, the shifting of population west of the city. The commercial area is now the center of the [African-American community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans_in_Omaha,_Nebraska), concentrated in North Omaha.[^cite_note-Compr1980-102]<sup>: 54 </sup> The African American community has maintained its social and religious base, while it is experiencing an economic revitalization.

The [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy_\(Omaha\)) neighborhood grew south of downtown, as many Italian immigrants came to the city to work in the [Union Pacific shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad_Omaha_Shops_Facility).[^cite_note-141] Scandinavians first came to Omaha as [Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon) settlers in the [Florence neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) had a strong political and cultural voice in Omaha,[^cite_note-144] and were involved in a variety of trades and businesses, including banks, wholesale houses and funeral homes. The [Notre Dame Academy and Convent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_Academy_and_Convent) and [Czechoslovak Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Museum) are legacies of their residence.[^cite_note-145] The legacy of the city's early European immigrant populations is evident in many social and cultural institutions in Downtown and South Omaha.

[Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicans_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) originally immigrated to Omaha to work in the rail yards. Today they account for most of South Omaha's Hispanic population and many have taken jobs in [meat processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_processing).[^cite_note-146] Other large early ethnic populations in Omaha included [Danes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes_in_Omaha,_Nebraska), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Omaha), and [Swedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedes_in_Omaha,_Nebraska).

A growing number of African immigrants have made their homes in Omaha in the last twenty years. There are approximately 8,500 [Sudanese](27421.md) living in Omaha, including the largest population of [Sudanese refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_refugees) in the United States. Most have immigrated since 1995 because of [warfare in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War). They represent ten ethnic groups, including the [Nuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuer_people), [Dinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinka), [Equatorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatoria), [Maubans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauban) and [Nubians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubians). Most Sudanese people in Omaha speak the [Nuer language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuer_language).[^cite_note-147] Other Africans have immigrated to Omaha as well, with one-third from [Nigeria](21383.md), and large populations from [Kenya](188171.md), [Togo](30138.md), [Cameroon](5447.md) and [Ghana](12067.md).[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

With the expansion of railroad and industrial jobs in meatpacking, Omaha attracted many immigrants and migrants. As the major city in Nebraska, it has historically been more racially and ethnically diverse than the rest of the state.[^cite_note-151] At times rapid population change, overcrowded housing and job competition have aroused [racial and ethnic tensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_Tension_in_Omaha,_Nebraska). Around the start of the 20th century, violence towards new immigrants in Omaha often erupted out of suspicion and fear.[^cite_note-152]

In 1909, anti-Greek sentiment flared after increased Greek immigration, and worsened their tendency to become [strikebreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikebreakers). The killing of a policeman of Irish descent enraged the Irish community; an angry mob violently stormed the Greek neighborhood in Omaha in what would become known as the [Greek Town Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Town_Riot).[^cite_note-ILL_Transatlantic-153]<sup>: 185 </sup> That mob violence forced the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) immigrant population to flee from the city.[^cite_note-ILL_Transatlantic-153][^cite_note-154] By 1910, 53.7% of Omaha's residents and 64.2% of South Omaha's residents were foreign born or had at least one parent born outside of America.[^cite_note-155]

Six years after the Greek Town Riot, in 1915, a mob killed Juan Gonzalez, a Mexican immigrant, near [Scribner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scribner,_Nebraska), a town in the Greater Omaha metropolitan area. The event occurred after an [Omaha Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Police_Department) officer investigated a criminal operation that sold goods stolen from the nearby railroad yards. [Racial profiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_profiling) targeted Gonzalez as the culprit. After escaping the city, he was trapped along the [Elkhorn River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elkhorn_River), where the mob, including several policemen from Omaha, shot him more than twenty times. It was discovered Gonzalez was unarmed, and he had a reliable alibi for the time of the murder. No one was ever indicted for his killing.[^cite_note-156]

In the fall of 1919, following [Red Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Summer), postwar social and economic tensions, the earlier hiring of African Americans as strikebreakers, and job uncertainty contributed to a mob from South Omaha lynching [Willy Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Brown) and the ensuing [Omaha Race Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Race_Riot). Trying to defend Brown, the city's mayor, [Edward Parsons Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Parsons_Smith), was lynched also, surviving only after a quick rescue.[^cite_note-Bristow_2000-49]

Like other industrial cities in the U.S., Omaha suffered severe job losses in the 1950s, more than 10,000 in all, as the railroad and meatpacking industries restructured. Stockyards and packing plants were located closer to ranches, and union achievements were lost as wages declined in surviving jobs.[^cite_note-157] Many workers left the area if they could get to other jobs. Poverty deepened in areas of the city whose residents depended on those jobs, specifically North and South Omaha. At the same time, with reduced revenues, the city had less financial ability to respond to longstanding problems.

Despair after the April 1968 [assassination of Martin Luther King Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Martin_Luther_King_Jr.) contributed to [riots in North Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_assassination_riots), including one at the [Logan Fontenelle Housing Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Fontenelle_Housing_Project).[^cite_note-158] For some, the [civil rights movement in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights_movement_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) evolved towards [black nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_nationalism), as the [Black Panther Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party) was involved in tensions in the late 1960s. Organizations such as the [Black Association for Nationalism Through Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Association_for_Nationalism_Through_Unity) became popular among the city's African-American youth. This tension culminated in the *[cause célèbre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cause_c%C3%A9l%C3%A8bre)* trial of the [Rice/Poindexter Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice/Poindexter_Case), in which an Omaha Police Department officer was killed by a bomb while answering an emergency call.

Whites in Omaha have followed the [white flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_flight) pattern, [suburbanizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban) to West Omaha.[^cite_note-159] In the late 1990s and early 2000s, gang violence and incidents between the [Omaha Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Police_Department) and Black residents undermined relations between groups in North and South Omaha.[^cite_note-160]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Omaha,_Nebraska)



[Old Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)) in [Downtown Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Omaha)

With diversification in several industries, including banking, insurance, telecommunications, architecture/construction, and transportation, Omaha's economy has grown since the early 1990s,[^cite_note-161] and six national fiber optic networks converge in Omaha.[^cite_note-162]

Omaha's most prominent businessman is [Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett), nicknamed the "Oracle of Omaha", who for decades has ranked as one of the [richest people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_billionaires_\(2007\)). Four Omaha-based companies: [Berkshire Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_Hathaway), [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad), [Mutual of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_of_Omaha), and [Kiewit Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiewit_Corporation), are among the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500).[^cite_note-163]

Omaha is the headquarters of several other major corporations, including [the Gallup Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gallup_Organization), [Werner Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Enterprises), [First National Bank of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_National_Bank_of_Omaha), [WoodmenLife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WoodmenLife), [Gavilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavilon), [Scoular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoular) and First Comp Insurance. Many other large national firms have major operations or operational headquarters in Omaha, including [Bank of the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_the_West), [First Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Data), [Sojern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sojern), [PayPal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal), [LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn), [Pacific Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Life), [MetLife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetLife) and [Conagra Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conagra_Brands). The city is also home to three of the 30 largest architecture firms in the United States, including [HDR, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDR,_Inc.), [DLR Group, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DLR_Group,_Inc.), and [Leo A Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_A_Daly).[^cite_note-164]

### Tourism



Office buildings in downtown Omaha

Main article: [Tourism in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Omaha,_Nebraska)

Tourist attractions in Omaha include history, sports, outdoors and cultural experiences. Its principal tourist attractions are the [Henry Doorly Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Doorly_Zoo) and the [College World Series](536107.md).[^cite_note-Nebraska1-166] The [Old Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)) in [Downtown Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Omaha) is another major attraction and is important to the city's retail economy. Famous early visitors included British author [Rudyard Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling) and General [George Crook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Crook). In 1883 Omaha hosted the first official performance of the [Buffalo Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bill)'s [Wild West Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_West_Show) for 8,000 attendees.[^cite_note-167] In 1898 the city hosted more than 1 million visitors from across the United States at the [Trans-Mississippi and International Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Mississippi_and_International_Exposition), a [world's fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_fair) that lasted for more than half the year.[^cite_note-168]

Research on [leisure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leisure) and [hospitality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitality) situates Omaha in the same tier for tourists as the neighboring cities of [Des Moines, Iowa](9163.md); [Topeka, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas); [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri); [Oklahoma City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City), [Oklahoma](22489.md); [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver), [Colorado](5399.md); and [Sioux Falls, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_Falls,_South_Dakota).[^cite_note-Goss-169] A recent study found investment of \$1 million in cultural tourism generated approximately \$83,000 in state and local taxes, and provided support for hundreds of jobs for the metropolitan area, which in turn led to additional tax revenue for government.[^cite_note-Nebraska1-166][^cite_note-Goss-169]<sup>: 11 </sup>

## Arts and culture

Main articles: [Culture of Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Omaha,_Nebraska) and [Culture in North Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_in_North_Omaha,_Nebraska)



Joslyn Art Museum

Several national newspapers, including the *[Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Globe)*[^cite_note-170] and *[The New York Times](30680.md)*[^cite_note-171] have lauded Omaha's historical and cultural attractions.

The city is home to the [Omaha Community Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Community_Playhouse), the largest [community theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_theater) in the United States.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] The [Omaha Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Symphony_Orchestra) and its modern [Holland Performing Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_Performing_Arts_Center),[^cite_note-174] the [Opera Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Omaha) at the [Orpheum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheum,_Omaha) theater, the [Blue Barn Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Barn_Theatre), [American Midwest Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Midwest_Ballet), and [The Rose Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rose,_Omaha) form the backbone of Omaha's [performing arts community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_in_Omaha). Opened in 1931, the [Joslyn Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joslyn_Art_Museum) has large art collections.[^cite_note-175] Since its inception in 1976, [Omaha Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Children's_Museum) has been a place where children can challenge themselves, discover how the world works and learn through play. The [Bemis Center for Contemporary Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemis_Center_for_Contemporary_Arts), one of the nation's premier urban artist colonies, was founded in Omaha in 1981,[^cite_note-176] and the [Durham Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Museum,_Omaha,_Nebraska) is accredited with the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution) for traveling exhibits.[^cite_note-177] The city is also home to the largest singly funded mural in the nation, "Fertile Ground",[^cite_note-178] by [Meg Saligman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meg_Saligman).[^cite_note-179] The annual [Omaha Blues, Jazz, & Gospel Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Blues,_Jazz,_&_Gospel_Festival) celebrates local music along with the [Omaha Black Music Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Black_Music_Hall_of_Fame).

In 1955, Omaha's [Union Stockyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Stockyards_\(Omaha\)) overtook Chicago's stockyards as the United States' meat packing center. This legacy is reflected in the [cuisine of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Omaha), with renowned steakhouses such as [Gorat's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorat's) and the recently closed [Mister C's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mister_C's), as well as the retail chain [Omaha Steaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Steaks).

### Henry Doorly Zoo

Main article: [Henry Doorly Zoo and Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Doorly_Zoo_and_Aquarium)



Desert Dome at Henry Doorly Zoo and Aquarium

The [Henry Doorly Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Doorly_Zoo) is widely considered a premier zoo.[^cite_note-180] The zoo is home to the world's largest nocturnal exhibit and indoor swamp; the world's largest indoor rainforest, the world's largest indoor desert,[^cite_note-181] and the largest [geodesic dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_dome) in the world (13 stories tall).[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] The zoo is Nebraska's number-one paid attendance attraction and has welcomed more than 25 million visitors over the past 40 years.[^cite_note-184]

### Old Market

Main article: [Old Market (Omaha, Nebraska)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\))

The [Old Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)) is a major [historic district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_district) in Downtown Omaha listed on the National Register of Historical Places. Today, its warehouses and other buildings house shops, restaurants, bars, coffee shops, and art galleries.[^cite_note-185] Downtown is also the location of the Omaha Rail and Commerce Historic District, which has several art galleries and restaurants. [Lauritzen Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauritzen_Gardens) features 100 acres (40 [ha](24919513.md)) with a variety of landscaping, and the new [Kenefick Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenefick_Park) recognizes Union Pacific Railroad's long history in Omaha.[^cite_note-186] North Omaha has several historical [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_in_North_Omaha,_Nebraska) attractions including the [Dreamland Historical Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewell_Building), Love's Jazz and Art Center, and the John Beasley Theater.[^cite_note-187] The annual River City Roundup is celebrated at Fort Omaha, and the neighborhood of [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence,_Nebraska) celebrates its history during "Florence Days". [Native Omaha Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Omaha_Days) is a biennial event celebrating Near North Side heritage.[^cite_note-188]

### Religion

Religious institutions reflect the city's heritage.[^cite_note-189] The [city's Christian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) has several historical churches dating from the founding of the city. There are also all sizes of congregations, including small, medium and [megachurches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megachurch). Omaha hosts the only [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) [temple in Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Quarters_Nebraska_Temple) along with a large [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_and_Judaism_in_Omaha,_Nebraska). There are 152 parishes in the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Omaha), and several [Eastern Orthodox](10186.md) congregations throughout the city.[^cite_note-190]

### Lauritzen Gardens

Lauritzen Gardens is a [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) located near [South Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Omaha). The garden contains several large [greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse), and outdoor plant exhibits. Covering over 100 acres of land, Lauritzen Gardens welcomes over 200,000 guests annually, making it one of the most popular attractions in Omaha.[^cite_note-191]

### Music

Main article: [Music of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Omaha)

Omaha's rich history in rhythm and blues, and jazz gave rise to a number of influential bands, including [Anna Mae Winburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Mae_Winburn)'s [Cotton Club Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Club_Boys_\(territory_band\)) and [Lloyd Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Hunter)'s Seranaders. [Rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll) pioneer [Wynonie Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wynonie_Harris), jazz great [Preston Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston_Love), drummer [Buddy Miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Miles), and [Luigi Waites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Waites) are among the city's homegrown talent. [Doug Ingle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Ingle) from the late 1960s band [Iron Butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Butterfly) was born in Omaha, as was [indie folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_folk) singer-songwriter [Elliott Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Smith), though both were raised elsewhere. [Musical theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theater) star [Andrew Rannells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Rannells) was also born in Omaha and is known amongst his fans for mentioning it in most of his interviews.

Today, the diverse [culture of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Omaha,_Nebraska) includes a variety of performance venues, museums, and musical heritage, including the historically significant jazz scene in North Omaha and the modern and influential "[Omaha Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Sound)".[^cite_note-OPS-192][^cite_note-Otto_2010-193]

Contemporary music groups either in or originally from Omaha include [Mannheim Steamroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim_Steamroller), [Bright Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_\(band\)), [The Faint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Faint), [Cursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursive_\(band\)), [Azure Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azure_Ray), [Tilly and the Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilly_and_the_Wall), and [311](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/311_\(band\)). During the late 1990s, Omaha became nationally known as the birthplace of [Saddle Creek Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_Creek_Records), and the subsequent "Omaha Sound" was born from their bands' collective style.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195]

Omaha also has a fledgling [hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop) scene. Long-time bastion [Houston Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Alexander), a one-time graffiti artist and professional [Mixed Martial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_Martial_Arts) competitor, is a local hip-hop radio show host.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] Cerone Thompson, known as Scrybe, had a number one hit on college radio stations across the United States. He also had number one hits, "Lose Control" and "Do What U Do", on the local hip hop station.\[^cite_note-media.[www.unogateway.com-198](http://www.unogateway.com-198/)] Other notable artists include Stylo of Mastered Trax Latino who holds a strong following in South Omaha and Mexico / Latin America.\[^cite_note-media.[www.unogateway.com-198](http://www.unogateway.com-198/)]

Many ethnic and cultural bands have come from Omaha. The Omaha Black Music Hall of Fame celebrates the city's long history of African-American music and the Strathdon Caledonia Pipe Band carries on a [Scottish](26994.md) legacy. Internationally renowned composer [Antonín Dvořák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k) wrote his [Ninth ("New World") Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_\(Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k\)) in 1893 based on his impressions of the region after visiting Omaha's robust [Czech community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs_in_Omaha,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-199] In the period surrounding World War I [Valentin J. Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_J._Peter) encouraged [Germans in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_in_Omaha) to celebrate their rich musical heritage, too. [Frederick Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Metz), [Gottlieb Storz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottlieb_Storz) and [Frederick Krug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Krug) were influential brewers whose [beer gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_garden) kept many German bands active.

### Landmark preservation

Main articles: [Landmarks in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmarks_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) and [List of Registered Historic Places in Douglas County, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Registered_Historic_Places_in_Douglas_County,_Nebraska)



[Joslyn Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joslyn_Castle)

Omaha is home to dozens of nationally, regionally and locally significant landmarks.[^cite_note-200] The city has more than a dozen historic districts, including [Fort Omaha Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Omaha_Historic_District), [Gold Coast Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Historic_District_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)), [Omaha Quartermaster Depot Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Quartermaster_Depot_Historic_District), Field Club Historic District, Bemis Park Historic District, and the South Omaha Main Street Historic District. Omaha is notorious for its 1989 demolition of 24 buildings in the Jobbers Canyon Historic District, which represents to date the largest loss of buildings on the National Register.[^cite_note-201] The only original building surviving of that complex is the [Nash Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_Block).

Omaha has almost [one hundred individual properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Registered_Historic_Places_in_Douglas_County,_Nebraska) listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places), including the [Bank of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Florence), [Holy Family Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Family_Catholic_Church_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)), the [Christian Specht Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Specht_Building) and the [Joslyn Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joslyn_Castle). There are also three properties designated as [National Historic Landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark).[^cite_note-202]

[Locally designated landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Landmarks), including residential, commercial, religious, educational, agricultural and socially significant locations across the city, honor Omaha's cultural legacy and important history. The [City of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Omaha) [Landmarks Heritage Preservation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmarks_Heritage_Preservation_Commission) is the government body that works with the [mayor of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Omaha) and the [Omaha City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_City_Council) to protect historic places. Important history organizations in the community include the [Douglas County Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County_Historical_Society).[^cite_note-203]

Built in 1962, Omaha's [Cinerama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinerama) was called [Indian Hills Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Hills_Theater). Its demolition in 2001 by the [Nebraska Methodist Health System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Methodist_Health_System) was unpopular, with objections from local historical and cultural groups and luminaries from around the world.[^cite_note-204] The [Dundee Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee_Theatre) is the lone surviving single-screen [movie theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_theater) in Omaha and still shows films.[^cite_note-205] A recent development to the Omaha film scene was the addition of [Film Streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Streams)'s Ruth Sokolof Theater in North Downtown. The two-screen theater is part of the [Slowdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slowdown_\(venue\)) facility. It features American independents, foreign films, documentaries, classics, themed series, and director retrospectives. In addition to the five [Douglas Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Theatres) venues in Omaha, two more are opening, including [Midtown Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Omaha#Neighborhoods) Theatres, on 32nd and Farnam Streets near the [Mutual of Omaha Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_of_Omaha_Building). [Westroads Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westroads_Mall) has a modern multiplex movie theater with 14 screens, operated by [Rave Cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rave_Cinemas).[^cite_note-206]

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Omaha,_Nebraska)



Charles Schwab Field

Sports have been important in Omaha for more than a century, and the city plays host to three minor-league professional sports teams.

Omaha has hosted the annual June NCAA [College World Series](536107.md) men's [baseball](3850.md) tournament since 1950.[^cite_note-207] It has been played at the downtown [Charles Schwab Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Schwab_Field) since 2011.[^cite_note-208]

The Omaha Sports Commission is a quasi-governmental nonprofit organization that coordinates much of the professional and amateur athletic activity in the city, including the 2008, 2012 and 2016 US Olympic Swimming Team Trials and the building of a [new stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TD_Ameritrade_Park_Omaha) in North Downtown.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] The [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](323058.md) and the Commission co-hosted the 2008 [National Collegiate Athletic Association](60706.md) (NCAA) [Division One Women's Volleyball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Women's_Volleyball_Championship) in December of that year.[^cite_note-212] The 2016 [Big 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_10) Baseball Championship was also played at the College World Series Stadium. Another quasi-governmental board, the Metropolitan Entertainment and Convention Authority (MECA), was created by city voters in 2000,[^cite_note-213] and is responsible for maintaining the [CHI Health Center Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHI_Health_Center_Omaha) (formerly CenturyLink Center Omaha).[^cite_note-214]

The Omaha Storm Chasers play at [Werner Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Park).[^cite_note-217] They won seven championships (in 1969, 1970, 1978, 1990, 2011, 2013, and 2014). Omaha is also home to the Omaha Diamond Spirit, a collegiate summer baseball team that plays in the MINK league.

The [Omaha Supernovas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Supernovas) are a professional [indoor volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_volleyball) team based in Omaha, Nebraska. The team competes in the [Pro Volleyball Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Volleyball_Federation) (PVF). The Supernovas began play in the league's inaugural [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pro_Volleyball_Federation_season). The team plays its home games at CHI Health Center Omaha. The Supernovas won the inaugural championship in May 2024.[^cite_note-218] During its championship run in the inaugural PVF season, Omaha and the Supernovas became the league's shining star, hosting 134,969 fans across the 15 matches held at the CHI Health Center. That includes a whopping 9,656 average mark for the 12 Supernovas' home matches, plus the 19,094 spectators who attended the PVF Semifinals and Championship. The Supernovas' 9,656 attendance match average has been the No. 1 mark amongst professional volleyball teams in the world. The team set a record with 12,090 spectators packing into the CHI Health Center to see the Supernovas beat the [Orlando Valkyries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Valkyries) in four sets.[^cite_note-provolleyball.com-216]

## Parks and recreation

Main articles: [Parks in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_in_Omaha) and [Trails in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trails_in_Omaha)

Omaha has a thriving running community and many miles of paved running and biking trails throughout the city and surrounding communities. The Omaha Marathon involves a half-marathon and a 10 km (6.2 mi) race that takes place annually in September.[^cite_note-227] Omaha also has a history of [curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling), including multiple junior national champions.[^cite_note-228]

The city's historic [boulevards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevards_in_Omaha) were originally designed by [Horace Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Cleveland) in 1889 to work with the parks to create a seamless flow of trees, grass and flowers throughout the city. [Florence Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Boulevard) and Fontenelle Boulevard are among the remnants of this system.[^cite_note-229] Omaha boasts more than 80 mi (129 km) of [trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trails_in_Omaha) for pedestrians, bicyclists and hikers.[^cite_note-230] They include the [American Discovery Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Discovery_Trail), which traverses the entire United States, and the [Lewis and Clark National Historic Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_National_Historic_Trail) passes through Omaha as it travels 3,700 mi (5,950 km) westward from Illinois to Oregon. Trails throughout the area are included in comprehensive plans for the city of Omaha, the Omaha metropolitan area, Douglas County, and long-distance coordinated plans between the municipalities of southeast Nebraska. The city also has a park dedicated to pollinating bees and insects called 'Pacific Preserve'[^cite_note-231]

## Government

Main article: [Government of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Omaha)



City Building in Downtown Omaha

Omaha has a [strong mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-council_government) form of [government](12229.md), along with a city council elected from seven districts across the city. The [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Omaha) is [John Ewing Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ewing_Jr.), who was elected in May 2025. The longest-serving mayor in Omaha's history was ["Cowboy" Jim Dahlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dahlman), who served 20 years over eight terms. He was regarded as the "wettest mayor in America" because of the flourishing number of bars in Omaha during his tenure.[^cite_note-232] Dahlman was a close associate of [political boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_boss) Tom Dennison.[^cite_note-233] During Dahlman's tenure, the city switched from its original strong-mayor form of government to a [city commission government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_commission_government).[^cite_note-234] In 1956, the city switched back.[^cite_note-235]

The [city clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_clerk) is Elizabeth Butler.[^cite_note-236] The City of Omaha administers twelve departments, including finance, [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Police_Department), human rights, [libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Public_Library) and planning.[^cite_note-237] The Omaha City Council is the legislative branch and has seven members elected from districts across the city. The council enacts [local ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ordinance) and approves the city [budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget). Government priorities and activities are established in a budget ordinance approved annually. The council takes official action through the passage of ordinances and resolutions. Nebraska's constitution grants the option of [home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) to cities with more than 5,000 residents, meaning they may operate under their own charters. Omaha is one of only three cities in Nebraska to use this option, out of 17 eligible.[^cite_note-238] The City of Omaha is considering [consolidating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_city-county) with Douglas County government.[^cite_note-239]

Although registered [Republicans](32070.md) outnumbered [Democrats](5043544.md) in the [2nd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska's_2nd_congressional_district), which includes Omaha, Democratic presidential candidate [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) opened three campaign offices in the city with 15 staff members to cover the state in fall 2008.[^cite_note-240] Mike Fahey, the Democratic mayor of Omaha, said he would do whatever it took to deliver the district's electoral vote to Obama; and the [Obama campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_2008_presidential_campaign) considered the district "in play".[^cite_note-241] Former Nebraska [U.S. Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senator) [Bob Kerrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Kerrey) and then-U.S. Senator [Ben Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Nelson) campaigned in the city for Obama,[^cite_note-242] and in November 2008 Obama won the district's electoral vote. This was a historic win, as Obama became the first Democratic presidential candidate to win an electoral vote in Nebraska since 1964, only made possible by [Nebraska's split electoral vote system.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_District_Method)[^cite_note-243]

In 2011, Nebraska lawmakers moved Offutt Air Force Base and the town of Bellevue—an area with a large minority population—out of the Omaha-based 2nd district and shifted in the Republican-heavy Omaha suburbs in Sarpy County. The move is expected to dilute the city's urban Democratic vote.[^cite_note-2nd-244]

The 2nd district sent its single electoral vote for [Joe Biden](145422.md) in the 2020 election.[^cite_note-245] Biden's victory, by more than 20,000 votes, shows Omaha's and the 2nd district's continuing trend toward Democratic politics in recent years.[^cite_note-246]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Omaha,_Nebraska)

### Primary and secondary education

Omaha has many public and private educational institutions, including [Omaha Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Public_Schools), the largest [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(government_funded\)) [school district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) in Nebraska, which serves more than 47,750 students in more than 75 schools.[^cite_note-247] After a contentious period of uncertainty, in 2007 the [Nebraska Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Legislature) approved a plan to create a [learning community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_community) for Omaha-area school districts with a central administrative board.[^cite_note-248]

The [Westside Community Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westside_Community_Schools), also known as District 66, is a district in the heart of Omaha. It serves students in pre-kindergarten through the 12th grade and recorded a district enrollment of 6,123 students K–12 for the 2015–16 school year.[^cite_note-249] Through annexations Omaha also has the [Millard Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millard_Public_Schools) and [Elkhorn Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elkhorn_Public_Schools). Omaha is also home to [Brownell-Talbot School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownell-Talbot_School), Nebraska's only preschool through grade 12, independent college preparatory school.

The [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Omaha) operates [numerous private Catholic schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Omaha#Omaha_Catholic_schools) with 21,500 students in 32 elementary schools and nine high schools.[^cite_note-250] They include [St. Cecilia Grade School](https://web.archive.org/web/20120618145254/http://stcecilia.omaha.org/) in Midtown Omaha, Holy Cross in Morton Meadows, St. Robert Bellarmine School at 120th and Pacific Street, St. Stephen the Martyr School in Millard, and Creighton Preparatory School, all of which have received the [U.S. Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Education) [Blue Ribbon School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ribbon_School) award.

### Higher education

There are eleven [colleges and universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) among Omaha's [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education) institutions. The largest public school is [University of Nebraska Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska_Omaha), which was founded in 1908 and is currently an NCAA Division I school with over 15,000 students. The [University of Nebraska Medical Center](5079518.md) in midtown Omaha is home to the [Eppley Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eppley_Cancer_Center), one of 66 designated Cancer Centers by the [National Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Institute) in the United States. The University of Nebraska College of Medicine is also on the UNMC campus.

Omaha's largest private university is [Creighton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creighton_University). It is a Jesuit institution that is ranked the top non-doctoral regional university in the [Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States). Its campus is just outside Downtown Omaha in the new North Downtown district. The university has a combined 6,700 students in its undergraduate, graduate, medical, and law schools.

&#x20;Omaha is also home to a number of smaller colleges and universities. [Clarkson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkson_College) is a small private college focusing on health sciences and affiliated with the [Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)). [Nebraska Methodist College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Methodist_College) is a small private school focusing on health careers and education. The [College of Saint Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Saint_Mary) is a Catholic woman's school known for its healthcare offerings. [Bellevue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue_University) is a mid-size private university. [Doane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doane_University) is the oldest private university in Nebraska, with campuses in Crete and Lincoln. Its residential campus is in Crete with a smaller campus in Omaha.

### Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Omaha)

Further information: [Railroads in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroads_in_Omaha) and [Trails in Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trails_in_Omaha)



[Ak-Sar-Ben Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak-Sar-Ben_Bridge) toll booth in 1938



Interstate 480 leaving Omaha



Omaha's [Eppley Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eppley_Airfield) in East Omaha

Omaha's central role in the history of transportation across America earned it the nickname "Gate City of the West".[^cite_note-Mullens,_P.A._1901_p._24-1] Despite [President Lincoln's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) decree that [Council Bluffs, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Bluffs,_Iowa), be the starting point for the Union Pacific Railroad, construction began from Omaha on the eastern portion of the first transcontinental railroad.[^cite_note-262] By the middle of the 20th century, nearly every major railroad served Omaha.

Today, the Omaha Rail and Commerce Historic District celebrates this connection, along with the listing of the [Burlington Train Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Station_\(Omaha,_Nebraska\)) and the [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Omaha\)) on the National Register of Historic Places. First housed in the former [Herndon House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon_House), the Union Pacific Railroad's corporate headquarters have been in Omaha since the company began.[^cite_note-263] Their new headquarters, the [Union Pacific Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Center), opened in Downtown Omaha in 2004.

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak), the national passenger rail system, provides service through Omaha, with the *[California Zephyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Zephyr)* serving [Omaha station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_station) once daily in each direction. The [intercity bus terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Bus_Station) is at 1601 Jackson St. in downtown Omaha. The terminal also service to [Jefferson Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lines), [Burlington Trailways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Trailways), and [Express Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_Arrow?action=edit\&redlink=1). [Metro Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Area_Transit), previously known as Metro Area Transit, is the [local bus system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transportation).

Omaha's position as a transportation center was finalized with the 1872 opening of the [Union Pacific Missouri River Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Missouri_River_Bridge) that linked the transcontinental railroad to the railroads terminating in Council Bluffs.[^cite_note-264] In 1888, the first road bridge, the [Douglas Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Street_Bridge), opened. In the 1890s, the [Illinois Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Central) drawbridge opened as the largest bridge of its type in the world. Omaha's Missouri River road bridges are now entering their second generation, including the [Works Progress Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_Progress_Administration)-financed [South Omaha Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Omaha_Bridge), now called Veteran's Memorial Bridge, which was added to the National Register of Historic Places. In 2006, Omaha and Council Bluffs announced joint plans to build the [Missouri River Pedestrian Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River_Pedestrian_Bridge), which opened in 2008.[^cite_note-265]

The primary mode of transportation in Omaha is by automobile, with [I-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_in_Nebraska), [I-480](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_480_\(Iowa-Nebraska\)), [I-680](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_680_\(Iowa-Nebraska\)), [I-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_29), and [U.S. Route 75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_75) (JFK Freeway and North Freeway) providing [freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway) service across the metropolitan area.[^cite_note-266] The expressway along West Dodge Road ([U.S. Route 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_6) and [Nebraska Link 28B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Link_28B)) and [U.S. Route 275](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_275) has been upgraded to freeway standards from I-680 to [Fremont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont,_Nebraska). City-owned Metro Transit, formerly MAT Metro Area Transit, provides public bus service to hundreds of locations throughout the Metro.

A 2017 study by [Walk Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_Score) ranked Omaha 26th-most-walkable of the fifty largest U.S. cities.[^cite_note-267] Of the top 50 most walkable cities, only Omaha saw its Walk Score decline from the previous year.[^cite_note-268] There is an extensive [trail system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trails_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) throughout the city for walkers, runners, bicyclists, and other pedestrian modes of transportation.

Omaha is laid out on a [grid plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan), with 12 blocks to the mile with a north-to-south [house numbering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_numbering) system.[^cite_note-269] Omaha is the location of a [historic boulevard system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevards_in_Omaha) designed by [H.W.S. Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.W.S._Cleveland) who sought to combine the beauty of parks with the pleasure of driving cars.[^cite_note-270] The historic [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Boulevard) and Fontenelle Boulevards, as well as the modern [Sorenson Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorenson_Parkway?action=edit\&redlink=1), are important elements in this system.[^cite_note-271] The City of Omaha has proposed the [Omaha Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Streetcar) through the city's urban core, with proposed extensions to Council Bluffs, Iowa, [Eppley Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eppley_Airfield), [North Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Omaha), [West Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Omaha), and [Bellevue, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-GaWC-272]

Eppley Airfield, Omaha's airport, serves the region with over 5 million passengers in 2018.[^cite_note-273] [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines), [Southwest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Airlines), [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines), [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines), [Alaska Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Airlines), [Allegiant Air](1372018.md), [Frontier Airlines](163354.md), and [Sun Country Airlines](1241139.md) serve the airport with direct and connecting service. As of 2018, the airport has non-stop service to 34 destinations. [General aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airports that serve the area include the Millard Municipal Airport, [North Omaha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Omaha_Airport) and the [Council Bluffs Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Bluffs_Airport). Offutt Air Force Base continues to serve as a military airbase; it is at the southern edge of Bellevue, which in turn lies immediately south of Omaha.
