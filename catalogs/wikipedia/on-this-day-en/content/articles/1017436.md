The **Senegal national football team** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Équipe de football du Sénégal*), nicknamed ***Les Lions de la Teranga*** (English: *The Lions of Teranga*), represents [Senegal](27278.md) in men's international [association football](10568.md) and is operated by the [Senegalese Football Federation](4601182.md).

One of Africa's most famous national football teams, Senegal reached the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage) of the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), the second team from Africa to do so (after [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) in [1990](157232.md)). They managed to upset defending world champions [France](250197.md), finish second in their group, and beat [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) in extra time in the round of 16, before losing to [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage).

Senegal has won one [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) title, in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations). Their first appearance in the competition was in [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_African_Cup_of_Nations), when they lost 1–0 to [Ivory Coast](1039856.md) for fourth place. They hosted the [1992 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_African_Cup_of_Nations), where they made it to the quarter-finals, and finished as runners-up in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and [2025](57061770.md).

## History

### Early history

Senegal gained its independence from [France](5843419.md) on 4 April 1960, and the [Senegalese Football Federation](4601182.md) (FSF) was founded that year. The first Senegal match took place on 31 December 1961 against Dahomey (now [Benin](3459.md)), a 3–2 loss. The FSF has been affiliated with [FIFA](11049.md) since 1962 and has been a member of the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF) since 1963. Senegal's first appearance in the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) was in 1965, where they finished second in their group, and lost 1–0 to [Ivory Coast](1039856.md) to finish in fourth place. After a group stage exit at the AFCON three years later, they would not qualify for the tournament until 1986.

### 1990s and 2000s

In the [1990 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Senegal finished fourth; they hosted the [1992 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), where after finishing second in their group, they were eliminated by Cameroon in the quarterfinals. Senegal lost the [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) final on a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) after drawing 0–0 with [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team).[^cite_note-2] Later that year, Senegal made their debut appearance at the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup). After defeating defending world champions France in their opening game, they drew with [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and [Uruguay](679782.md) to progress from the group stage, then beat [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) in extra time in the round of 16 to reach the quarter-finals, one of only four African teams to do so (alongside Cameroon in [1990](157232.md), [Ghana](1029620.md) in [2010](168079.md) and [Morocco](1024865.md) in [2022](17742072.md)). There, they lost to [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) in extra time.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

Senegal qualified for the [2008 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), but finished third in their group with two points. They failed to make the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) in South Africa, the first World Cup to be held in Africa.

### 2010s

Senegal was eliminated from the [2012 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) with zero wins and zero points.

After former manager [Bruno Metsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Metsu) died on 14 October 2013, many Senegalese players were recalled to appear and have a moment of silence in memory of the manager who helped them reach the quarter-final in the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup). All activities of the national league and the national team were suspended for a few days in his memory.

The West African nation narrowly missed the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) after losing in a round-robin match against Ivory Coast in the final qualification round. Senegal qualified for two [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) tournaments before the next World Cup, being eliminated in the group stage in 2015 and reaching the quarterfinals in 2017. On 10 November 2017, after defeating [South Africa](1018598.md) 2–0,[^cite_note-5] Senegal qualified for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md),[^cite_note-6] their first since 2002.[^cite_note-7] Senegal defeated [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) 2–1 in their opening group match,[^cite_note-8] thanks to an [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) by [Thiago Cionek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Cionek) and a [M'Baye Niang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M'Baye_Niang) strike.[^cite_note-9] In the next group stage match, Senegal drew 2–2 against [Japan](993546.md), with goals from [Sadio Mané](34758489.md) and [Moussa Wagué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Wagu%C3%A9).[^cite_note-10] A 1–0 loss to [Colombia](680175.md) in their final match[^cite_note-11] meant they finished level on points with Japan, who progressed thanks to a superior fair play record.[^cite_note-12] Thus, Senegal was eliminated in the group stage for the first time in its World Cup history.[^cite_note-13]

[Aliou Cissé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliou_Ciss%C3%A9), who participated in the 2002 AFCON, managed Senegal to a runner-up campaign in the [2019 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-14] Having lost 1–0 to [Algeria](1029528.md) earlier in the tournament, Senegal lost 1–0 to them again in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_Final).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

### 2020s

Deprived of many players due to [COVID-19](63030231.md), Senegal participated in the [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), postponed to 2022 because of [the pandemic](62750956.md); they beat [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team) in their first match 1–0 and drew their next two games, enough to finish first in their group.[^cite_note-17] In the round of 16, Senegal faced [Cape Verde](1205575.md). Mané recorded a shot that hit the post in the first minute. [Patrick Andrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Andrade) was sent off in the 21st minute, after intervention of the [video assistant referee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee). Despite their dominance, the first half ended without a single shot on target; Mané opened the scoring a few minutes into the second half, following a corner.

Senegal faced [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals. The Lions opened the scoring half an hour into the game, by [Famara Diédhiou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famara_Di%C3%A9dhiou) on a pass from Mané; Senegal eventually won 3–1.[^cite_note-18] In the semi-finals, Senegal faced [Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso_national_football_team), winning 3–1 again.[^cite_note-19] In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_Final), Senegal faced [Egypt](1022953.md), who eliminated hosts [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) in the semi-finals. In a penalty shoot-out, Mané scored the winning penalty,[^cite_note-20] to bring Senegal its first Africa Cup of Nations title.[^cite_note-21] Senegal returned home and took part in a victory parade that took place in the capital, [Dakar](44251.md). During the 2021 Africa Cup of Nations final, Egyptian goalkeeper Mohamed Abou Gabal, known as Gabaski, used a water bottle with notes indicating the preferred penalty shot directions of Senegalese players.[^cite_note-22] It ended up becoming the biggest party in the country's history.[^cite_note-23]

Senegal faced Egypt twice after the AFCON final, eliminating the Egyptians on penalties after being tied 1–1 on aggregate, to qualify for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md). Mané eliminated his [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) teammate [Mohamed Salah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Salah) after scoring the winning penalty again.[^cite_note-24] The penalty shoot-out was however full of controversies with lasers being pointed at Egypt's penalty takers and goalkeeper. FIFA fined Senegal's football federation 175,000 Swiss francs as a result of the fan disorder.[^cite_note-25]

For the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, Senegal were drawn in Group A along with the hosts [Qatar](1111350.md), [Ecuador](1016797.md) and the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team). Star man Mané missed out due to injury, but Senegal managed to progress from the group nonetheless. Though they lost their first game against the Netherlands 2–0, Senegal went on to claim six points against the hosts and then Ecuador in their final game, progressing to the Round of 16, where they lost 3–0 to [England](9904.md). It marked the second time Senegal had progressed past the group stage, in only their third appearance.

On 10 June 2025, Senegal won 3–1 against England, becoming the first African country to defeat England in a football match at senior level.[^cite_note-26]

On 18 January 2026, Senegal won their second African Cup of Nations after beating host nation [Morocco](1024865.md) 1–0 after extra time in the [final](81129500.md).[^cite_note-27] However, the match was marred by a walkout by Senegal's players in protest at the awarding of a stoppage-time penalty to Morocco. This led to the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) overturning the result on 17 March 2026, calling the walkout a forfeiture of the match by Senegal, and awarding the trophy to Morocco instead.[^cite_note-\:0-28] The Senegalese Football Federation said it will appeal against this ruling.[^cite_note-\:0-28]

On 26 June 2026, during their final group stage match of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), Senegal defeated [Iraq](1039652.md) 5–0, securing the largest victory by an African team in World Cup history and surpassing Algeria's 4–2 win over South Korea in [2014](656933.md). Despite consecutive losses to France and Norway, this historic win allowed them to advance to the knockout rounds with just 3 points.[^cite_note-29]

On 1 July 2026, during the round of 32 of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, they blew a 2–0 lead against Belgium in the 85th minute, ultimately being eliminated 3–2 after a late penalty by Belgian captain [Youri Tielemans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youri_Tielemans), right before the match would have gone to a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md).[^cite_note-30]

### Coaching history



[Aliou Cissé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliou_Ciss%C3%A9), the former coach of the national team from 2015 to 2024, is considered the most successful coach in the team's history, leading the team to win the [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and to participate in the 2018 and 2022 World Cups. He was the captain of the team that reached the quarter-finals of the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup).



[Bruno Metsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Metsu), coach of the national team between 2000 and 2002, led the team to the final of the [2002 African Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations) and to participate for the first time in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), where it reached the quarter-finals.

### Africa Cup of Nations

Main article: [Senegal at the Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_at_the_Africa_Cup_of_Nations)

Historically, Senegal was seen as a weaker side in the strong [West African](67393.md) region. Although they finished in fourth place in two AFCON editions,[^cite_note-40] Senegalese performance was overall still deemed as poor. Senegal remained under the shadow of West African giants [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team), [Ivory Coast](1039856.md) and [Ghana](1029620.md) for the majority of the 20th century.

In the 2000s, Senegal began to surge and became a more competitive opponent in the Africa Cup of Nations. Following the [2002 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) tournament, where Senegal suffered a defeat to [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) 2–3 on [penalties](511302.md) after a goalless draw in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_Final), they would establish themselves as a new powerhouse in Africa by having a successful [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) debut in 2002, in which the side reached the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-41] Senegal once again finished as runners-up in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), losing the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_Final) 0–1 to [Algeria](1029528.md),[^cite_note-42] and finally won their first AFCON title in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).
