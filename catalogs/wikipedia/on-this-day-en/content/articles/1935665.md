The **Musandam Peninsula**,[^cite_note-3] or **Ruus Al Jibal**,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-B-5] is a mountainous, northeastern extension of the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), lying between the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) to the west and the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md) to the east; the connecting [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) lies to the north. Political control is divided between the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) and [Oman](22316.md).

## Geography

See also: [Geology of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Oman), [Geology of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates), [Geography of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Oman), and [Geography of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates)



[Topographic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_map) of the tip of the Musandam Peninsula, including the sinuous coast north and west of the isthmus between [Elphinstone Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elphinstone_Inlet) and [Malcolm's Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm's_Inlet)

The peninsula lies to the south of the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), between the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md).[^cite_note-Pavyand2011-6]

It is inhabited by the [Bedouin](61770.md) [Shihuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shihuh) tribe and the [Iranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples) [Kumzari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumzari_people) people.

It is mainly governed by [Oman](22316.md) as the [Musandam Governorate](1718644.md) with certain parts governed by the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), including [Ras Al Khaimah](61770159.md) and parts of [Dibba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibba).[^cite_note-Edmonds2006-1][^cite_note-Spalton2006-2]

Land features include the [Western Hajar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hajar_Mountains). Since these are the northernmost of the [Hajar range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajar_Mountains), they and the peninsula are referred to as [Ruʾūs al-Jibāl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajar_Mountains#Ru'us_al-Jibal) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): رُؤُوْس ٱلْجِبَال, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Capes of the Mountains*).[^cite_note-Edmonds2006-1][^cite_note-Spalton2006-2] The largest [wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi) in Musandam is [Wadi Bih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Bih), which forms the central [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin).[^cite_note-Lancaster\&Lancaster2011-7][^cite_note-Allen2016-8] The highest mountain in Musandam, and Ru'us al Jibal, is [Jebel Harim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Al_Harim).

### Climate

See also: [Climate of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Oman) and [Climate of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates)

During winter, the region can be fairly cool, particularly the mountains of Jais, Yanas and Mebrah.[^cite_note-GulfNews_03-2019-9][^cite_note-GulfNews_02-2019-10]

## Environment

See also: [Wildlife of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Oman) and [Wildlife of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates)

[Fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) include the [Ruus al Jibal fan-footed gecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruus_al_Jibal_fan-footed_gecko),[^cite_note-Simó-Riudalbas_etal.2017-11] [Arabian tahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_tahr) and [caracal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracal). It is unknown if the [Arabian leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_leopard) is still present.[^cite_note-Edmonds2006-1][^cite_note-Spalton2006-2]

### Important Bird Area

The mountainous northern end of the peninsula has been designated an [Important Bird Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_Area) (IBA) by [BirdLife International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BirdLife_International) because it supports significant populations of bird species, whether resident, breeding, wintering or on passage. These include [pallid scops-owl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallid_scops-owl), [plain leaf-warbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_leaf-warbler), [hooded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooded_wheatear), [variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_wheatear), [Hume's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hume's_wheatear) and [red-tailed wheatears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-tailed_wheatear), and [pale rockfinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_rockfinch).[^cite_note-bli-12]

The islands scattered around the tip of the peninsula are in a separate IBA because of their importance for [seabirds](147019.md), including [red-billed tropicbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-billed_tropicbird), [Persian shearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_shearwater), [Socotra cormorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_cormorant) and [bridled tern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridled_tern).[^cite_note-bli2-13]

## History

In the past, the region was unstable and was repeatedly invaded by Persian empires such as the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and the [Sassanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire). These empires then declined until the Islamic era arrived.

### Modern era

In the modern era, occupation of the Peninsula continued. In 1507, [Afonso de Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_de_Albuquerque) captured key areas along the Omani coast, including [Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sur,_Oman), [Muscat](57053.md), and [Khasab](1658322.md)[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-15]. From then, until 1650, the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) used [Khasab](1658322.md) as a supply point for the dates trade as well as a safe passage for their ships in the region. By the early 1600s, native tribes began asserting more control, and the Portuguese presence in the area dwindled. Three decades after the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran) retook [Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormuz_Island) from the Portuguese in 1622, the [Ya'rubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya'rubids), the ruling house of [Oman](22316.md), occupied the Musandam Peninsula first under Imam [Nasir bin Murshid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_bin_Murshid), then completed by a new Imam, [Sultan bin Saif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_bin_Saif)[^cite_note-\:0-14], south of Hormuz Island, which gives the [strait](29005.md) between the peninsula and island its modern name.

In the 19th century, the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) occupied the area as part of the [Trucial States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucial_States), and in 1891, [Muscat and Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_and_Oman) became a [British Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_protectorate);[^cite_note-16] however, the Khasab region, not suitable for port nor hospitable for settlement due to a harsh climate, was not developed. In the late 20th century, the [UAE](69328.md) was formed, with most sheikdoms in the area joining, except notably [Ras Al Khaimah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Ras_Al_Khaimah).[^cite_note-sterling-20220525-17][^cite_note-hughes-18] However, in 1972, the Emirate entered the UAE, leading to discussions on the Emirati-Omani border eventually settling on the Musandam Peninsula being granted to Oman.

## In popular culture

In *[The Secret of the Swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_of_the_Swordfish)*, a secret underground British military base lies beneath the Musandam Peninsula.
