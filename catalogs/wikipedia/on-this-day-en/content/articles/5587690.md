**Extended-hours trading** (or **electronic trading hours**, **ETH**) is [stock trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_trading) before or after the [trading day](5195796.md) regular trading hours (RTH) of a [stock exchange](28168.md), i.e., **pre-market trading** or **after-hours trading**.[^cite_note-1]

[Market makers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_maker) and specialists generally do not participate in after-hours trading, which can limit liquidity.[^cite_note-2]

Since 1985, the regular trading hours for major exchanges in the United States, such as the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md) and the [Nasdaq](21559.md) stock market, have been from 9\:30 a.m. to 4\:00 p.m. [Eastern Time](496028.md) (ET).[^cite_note-3] Pre-market trading occurs from 4\:00 a.m. to 9\:30 a.m. ET, although the majority of the volume and liquidity come to the pre-market at 8\:00 a.m. ET.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-schedule-5] After-hours trading on a day with a normal session occurs from 4\:00 p.m. to 8\:00 p.m. ET.[^cite_note-schedule-5] **Overnight trading** occurs from 8\:00 p.m. to 4\:00 a.m. ET, and only via [electronic communication networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_communication_network) (ECNs) as opposed to stock exchanges.[^cite_note-6]



Example chart of extended-hours trading, via Google Finance

Trading outside regular hours is not a new phenomenon, but used to be limited to high-net-worth [investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investor) and [institutional investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_investor) like [mutual funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund).[^cite_note-sec-risks-7] The emergence of private trading systems known as [electronic communication networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_communication_network) (ECNs) has allowed individual investors to participate in after-hours trading. Extended-hours trading is all processed through ECNs including [NYSE Arca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYSE_Arca).[^cite_note-8] For instance, the *Blue Ocean Session* is an overnight trading session available Sunday to Thursday on the Blue Ocean [Alternative Trading System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_trading_system) (Blue Ocean ATS; BOATS) since June 2021.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] [Yahoo Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo_Finance) began displaying BOATS pricing data circa June 2025.[^cite_note-12]

[Financial Industry Regulatory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Industry_Regulatory_Authority) (FINRA) members who voluntarily enter quotations during the after-hours session are required to comply with all applicable limit order protection and display rules (e.g., the [Manning rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manning_rule) and the [SEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) order handling rules).[^cite_note-13]

## Risks and limitations

Compared to trading during regular hours, extended-hours trading comes with additional risks. There is typically less [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_\(finance\)) in extended-hours trading compared to regular hours, which generally implies there will be less [liquidity](56121.md), wider [bid–ask spreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bid%E2%80%93ask_spread), and greater [volatility](11930108.md) in extended-hours trading.[^cite_note-sec-risks-7] There is no guarantee that prices will carry over between regular sessions and extended-hours sessions as one ends and the other begins.[^cite_note-sec-risks-7] The ratio of professional traders to [retail investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retail_investor) participating in extended-hours trading may be greater than during regular hours,[^cite_note-sec-risks-7] thus increasing the risk that the former can exploit the latter.

Many extended-hours ECNs only allow placing [limit orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_order) and not other types of buy/sell orders, decreasing flexibility for traders.[^cite_note-sec-risks-7] Access to these ECNs is also somewhat balkanized. Any given broker might only provide its clients access to certain extended-hours ECNs, thus decreasing liquidity within each ECN.[^cite_note-sec-risks-7]
