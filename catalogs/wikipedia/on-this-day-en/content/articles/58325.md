# Vigilantism

"Vigilante" redirects here. For other uses, see [Vigilante (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilante_\(disambiguation\)).

Not to be confused with [lynching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynching).



[Tarring and feathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarring_and_feathering) became a form of vigilantism among [Bostonians](24437894.md) following the passage of the [Tea Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Act) by the [British Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain). Illustrated is the [extrajudicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_punishment) [torture](47702.md) of British customs official [John Malcolm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Malcolm) in 1774

**Vigilantism** ([/ˌvɪdʒɪˈlæntɪzəm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is an act commonly summarized as "taking the law into one's own hands"[^cite_note-\:0-1] which, according to Merriam Webster, means "to try to punish someone for breaking a law even though one does not have the right to do that."[^cite_note-2] A **vigilante** is a person who practices or partakes in vigilantism.

## Definition

The term is borrowed from Spanish *vigilante*, which means 'wide-awake' or 'watcher', from Latin *vigilāns*.[^cite_note-3] There are many different attempts to define what constitutes vigilantism and how can it be distinguished from acceptable forms of helping law enforcement without any legal authority, such as [citizen's arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen's_arrest) and [private investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_investigator).

According to political scientist Regina Bateson, vigilantism is "the extralegal prevention, investigation, or punishment of offenses."[^cite_note-\:1-4] The definition has three components:

1. Extralegal: Vigilantism is done outside of the law (not necessarily in violation of the law)

2. Prevention, investigation, or punishment: Vigilantism requires specific actions, not just attitudes or beliefs

3. Offense: Vigilantism is a response to a perceived crime or violation of an authoritative norm

Other scholars have defined "collective vigilantism" as "group violence to punish perceived offenses to a community."[^cite_note-\:2-5]

Les Johnston argues that vigilantism has six necessary components:[^cite_note-6]

- it is planned or premeditated

- it is carried out by private volunteers

- it is a social movement

- it involves or threatens the use of force

- it occurs when established societal norms are perceived to be threatened

- its primary goal is to enforce safety and security, especially to its participants, by combating crime

## History

Vigilantism and the vigilante ethos existed long before the word *vigilante* was introduced into the English language. There are conceptual parallels between the medieval [aristocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy_\(class\)) custom of [private war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_war) or [vendetta](292136.md) and the modern vigilante philosophy.[^cite_note-7]

Elements of the concept of vigilantism can be found in the biblical account in [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) 34 of the abduction and rape (or, by some interpretations, seduction) of [Dinah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinah), the daughter of [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob), in the [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan) city of [Shechem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shechem) by the eponymous son of the ruler, and the violent reaction of her brothers [Simeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_\(Hebrew_Bible\)) and [Levi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi), who slew all of the males of the city in revenge, rescued their sister and plundered Shechem. When Jacob protested that their actions might bring trouble upon him and his family, the brothers replied "Should he \[i.e., Shechem] treat our sister as a harlot?"

In the [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) literary and cultural tradition, characteristics of vigilantism have often been vested in folkloric heroes and [outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw) (e.g., [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood)[^cite_note-8]).

During medieval times, punishment of felons was sometimes exercised by such [secret societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_societies) as the courts of the [Vehm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehm)[^cite_note-9] ([cf.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cf.) the medieval Sardinian *Gamurra* later become *Barracelli*, the Sicilian [Vendicatori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendicatori) and the [Beati Paoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beati_Paoli)), a type of early vigilante organization, which became extremely powerful in Westphalian Germany during the 15th century.

### Vigilantism in Mexico

In some regions of Mexico, mainly in the state of [Michoacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michoacan), people affected by criminal groups like [Los Zetas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Zetas) and [La Familia Michoacana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Familia_Michoacana), created vigilante groups called [Grupos de autodefensa comunitaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupos_de_autodefensa_comunitaria) in 2013. Their most well-known leader [Hipólito Mora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip%C3%B3lito_Mora), was assassinated in 2023.

### Other notable acts of vigilantism

- In the early 20th century, the [White Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whites_\(Finland\)) founded the [Suojeluskunta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suojeluskunta) (Protection Corps) as a paramilitary vigilante organization in [Finland](10577.md). It formed the nucleus of the White Army in the [Finnish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War).

- A number of vigilante organizations were founded in the US around the time of World War I including the [American Protective League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Protective_League), [National Security League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_League), [Knights of Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_Liberty_\(vigilante_group\)), and [Boy Spies of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Spies_of_America), with the goal of targeting those suspected to be pro-German or insufficiently loyal.

  [^cite_note-\:3-12]

- In the 1920s, the [Big Sword Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sword_Society) of [China](5405.md) protected life and property in a state of [anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy).

- The [Secret Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Six_\(Chicago\)), a well-funded vigilante effort launched by the [Chicago Association of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicagoland_Chamber_of_Commerce) in 1930, investigated and solved [kidnappings](169376.md), [extortions](162449.md), bombings, robberies, and other crimes, helped launch [Eliot Ness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliot_Ness) and his [Untouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untouchables_\(law_enforcement\)), was credited by [Al Capone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Capone) for destroying his illegal liquor empire, inspired [The Secret Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_Six) movie, and was briefly emulated in cities across the nation until it was brought down by errors and scandal in 1933.

- After [World War II](32927.md), many alleged [Nazi collaborators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany_and_Fascist_Italy) were beaten up or killed for their activities by vigilantes.

- Recognized since the 1980s, [Sombra Negra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sombra_Negra) ("Black Shadow") of [El Salvador](9356.md) is a group of mostly retired police officers and military personnel whose sole duty is to cleanse the country of impure social elements by killing criminals and gang members. Along with several other organizations, Sombra Negra are a remnant of the [death squads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_squads) from the civil war of the 1970s and 1980s.

  [^cite_note-13]

- On the 5th of May, 1981 [Marianne Bachmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianne_Bachmeier) pulled out a handgun from the right side of her trench coat and shot her seven year old daughter's sexual abuser and murderer dead during his trial in the courtroom of Lübeck District Court.

- On March 16, 1984, [Gary Plauché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Plauch%C3%A9) shot and killed Jeff Doucet, who was set to be arraigned on charges of aggravated kidnapping of Plauché's son. The case received wide publicity because some people questioned whether Plauché - acting on his belief Doucet had been sexually abusing his son - should have been charged with murder.

  [^cite_note-14]

- In the Philippines in the mid-eighties, the [Alsa Masa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsa_Masa) and the [Kuratong Baleleng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuratong_Baleleng) were formed to fight Communist insurgents.

- During the 1990s, the group [City without Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uralmash_gang) publicly beat and murdered drug dealers and forced addicts to quit doing drugs in the city of [Yekaterinburg](66524.md), [Russia](25391.md).

- On the 28th of November, 1994, [Jeffrey Dahmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Dahmer), an infamous Milwaukee [serial killer](42626.md) and [cannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_cannibalism) was beaten to death by [Christopher Scarver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Scarver), a fellow inmate at the [Columbia Correctional Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Correctional_Institution_\(Wisconsin\)) in [Portage, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage,_Wisconsin).

- Formed in 1996, the [People Against Gangsterism and Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Against_Gangsterism_and_Drugs) of [Cape Town, South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town,_South_Africa) fights drugs and gangs in their region. They have been linked to terrorism since they bombed some American targets in Cape Town.

- Formed in 1998, the [Bakassi Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakassi_Boys) of [Nigeria](21383.md) were viewed as instrumental in decreasing the region's high crime.

- [Los Pepes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Pepes) was a group formed in [Colombia](5222.md) during the 1990s that committed acts of vigilantism against drug lord [Pablo Escobar](161570.md) and his associates within the [Medellín Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_Cartel).

- After the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001, [Jonathan Idema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Idema), a self-proclaimed vigilante, entered [Afghanistan](737.md) and captured many people he claimed to be [terrorists](30636.md). Idema claimed he was collaborating with, and supported by, the [United States Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Government). He sold news-media outlets tapes that he claimed showed an [al-Qaeda](1921.md) training camp in action. His operations ended abruptly when he was arrested with his partners in 2004 and sentenced to 10 years in a notorious Afghan prison, before being pardoned in 2007.

- On November 24, 1963, [Jack Ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Ruby) killed [Lee Harvey Oswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Harvey_Oswald). Oswald was arrested on November 22, roughly 75 minutes after he was suspected to have shot [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy).

- On August 13, 2004, [Akku Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akku_Yadav) was lynched by a mob of around 200 women from Kasturba Nagar, India. It took them 15 minutes to hack to death the man they say raped them with impunity for more than a decade. Chilli powder was thrown in his face and stones hurled. As he flailed and fought, one of his alleged victims hacked off his penis with a vegetable knife. A further 70 stab wounds were left on his body.

- [Salwa Judum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salwa_Judum), the anti-[Naxalite](4760079.md) group formed in 2005 in India are suspected to be helping the security forces in their fight against Naxals.

- In Hampshire, England, during 2006, a vigilante slashed the tires of more than twenty cars, leaving a note made from cut-out newsprint stating "Warning: you have been seen while using your mobile phone".

- [Republican Action Against Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Action_Against_Drugs) (RAAD) are an [Irish Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republicanism) vigilante organization active predominantly in and around [Derry](9055.md). Although often attributed as being a front for "[Dissident Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissident_Republican)" groups by the media, the organization claim to have no allegiance to any particular Republican party or paramilitary. Formed in late 2008, RAAD originally offered an "amnesty" to all drug dealers, asking them to make themselves known to the group before giving an assurance that they had stopped dealing.

  [^cite_note-derryjournal.com-27]

  &#x20;In an interview with the *Derry Journal* in August 2009, the group's leadership explained: "We would monitor the actions of those who have come forward and, given an adequate period of time, interest in those drug dealers would cease and they could start to lead normal lives".

[Óglaigh na hÉireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93glaigh_na_h%C3%89ireann_\(Real_IRA_splinter_group\)) for example in 2011 claimed responsibility for an arson attack on a taxi depot on Oldpark Road, Belfast, which led to the owners fleeing the country. It claimed that the owners were using the depot as a cover for drug dealing.

  [^cite_note-30]

- On April 15, 2011, a group of women in [Cherán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher%C3%A1n) armed with rocks and fireworks attacked a bus carrying [illegal loggers](465780.md) armed with machine guns in [Michoacán](195805.md) associated with the [Mexican drug cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_drug_cartel) [La Familia Michoacana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Familia_Michoacana). Vigilante activity spread to the nearby community of [Opopeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_del_Cobre). They established [Community self-defence groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupos_de_Autodefensa_Comunitaria). The government of Mexico has recognized Cherán as a self-governing indigenous community, but criminals continue to murder residents in the forest.

- On June 13, 2014, Darius, a 16-year-old Romani residing in France and who has been several times interrogated by the police on the account of suspected burglaries and larcenies, was kidnapped, beaten up, and then left in a supermarket trolley by an unknown party after rumors circulated of him being implicated in a housebreaking, which happened several hours before in the city of Pierrefite-sur-Seine.

  [^cite_note-32]

- Since the [May 9, 2016 Philippine elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_general_election) and the start of [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md)'s term as the President of the Philippines, numerous suspects (particularly drug users and pushers) were killed by various unknown hitmen labelled as a [summary execution](1227309.md) during his [war on drugs](51032591.md).

  [^cite_note-33]

  &#x20;Duterte has been accused of being linked to the [Davao Death Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Death_Squad), a vigilante group active since the mid-1990s in [Davao City](489401.md), where Duterte had previously served as mayor.

  [^cite_note-34]

The vigilantes then often hand the predators over to the police, or assault and/or blackmail the predators.
