**Lubusz Voivodeship** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *województwo lubuskie* [\[vɔjɛˈvut͡stfɔ luˈbuskʲɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/60/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubuskie.wav/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubuskie.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/60/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubuskie.wav/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubuskie.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland) ([province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province)) in western [Poland](22936.md) with a population of 972,140. Its regional capitals are [Gorzów Wielkopolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorz%C3%B3w_Wielkopolski) and [Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_G%C3%B3ra). The region is characterized by a landscape of forests, lakes, and rivers, and is [bordered by Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Poland_border) to the west.

The functions of regional capital are shared between two cities – Gorzów Wielkopolski and Zielona Góra. Gorzów serves as the seat of the centrally-appointed [voivode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivode) (*wojewoda*), or governor, and Zielona Góra is the seat of the elected regional assembly ([*sejmik*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_sejmik)) and the executive elected by that assembly, headed by a marshal (*marszałek*). In addition, the voivodeship includes a third city ([Nowa Sól](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowa_S%C3%B3l)) and a number of towns.

Lubusz Voivodeship borders [West Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pomeranian_Voivodeship) to the north, [Greater Poland Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Voivodeship) to the east, [Lower Silesian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Silesian_Voivodeship) to the south, and [Germany](11867.md) ([Brandenburg](3765.md) and [Saxony](28395.md)) to the west.

It was created on January 1, 1999, out of the former [Gorzów Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorz%C3%B3w_Voivodeship) and [Zielona Góra Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_G%C3%B3ra_Voivodeship), pursuant to the [Polish local government reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_local_government_reforms) adopted in 1998. The province's name recalls the historic [Lubusz Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_Land)[^cite_note-4] (*[Lebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebus)* or *Lubus*), although parts of the voivodeship belong to the historic regions of [Lower Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Silesia), [Greater Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland), and [Lower Lusatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Lusatia).

## History



Medieval town walls of [Żary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBary), one of the oldest towns in the province, first mentioned in 1007

During the formation and early expansion of the Polish state under its first rulers, [Mieszko I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mieszko_I) and [Bolesław I the Brave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_I_the_Brave), the entire territory of the present Lubusz Voivodeship became part of Poland by 1002, including the historic [Lubusz Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_Land), part of which is located on the western bank of the [Oder River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder_River), including its capital Lubusz, now [Lebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebus) in Germany. The oldest towns in the region, dating back over 1,000 years, include [Trzciel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trzciel), [Skwierzyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skwierzyna), [Iłowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C5%82owa), [Szprotawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szprotawa), [Jasień](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasie%C5%84,_Lubusz_Voivodeship), [Krosno Odrzańskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno_Odrza%C5%84skie), [Międzyrzecz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzyrzecz) and [Żary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBary), with most other towns also founded in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), including the current regional capitals of [Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_G%C3%B3ra) and [Gorzów Wielkopolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorz%C3%B3w_Wielkopolski). The youngest towns are [Łęknica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C4%99knica), [Czerwieńsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czerwie%C5%84sk), [Nowa Sól](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowa_S%C3%B3l), [Szlichtyngowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlichtyngowa) and [Zbąszynek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zb%C4%85szynek), all either first mentioned or established in the later periods.



[Żagań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBaga%C5%84) with its castle was a ducal seat for several centuries

Following the fragmentation of Poland into smaller provincial duchies, various portions of the present Lubusz Voivodeship were part of various duchies, initially the duchies of [Greater Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Greater_Poland) and [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Silesia), and later also [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Pozna%C5%84), [Legnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Legnica), [Głogów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_G%C5%82og%C3%B3w), [Lubusz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Lubusz) and [Żagań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_%C5%BBaga%C5%84), ruled by various lines of the [Piast dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piast_dynasty). Over time, portions of the present Lubusz Voivodeship were lost by Poland. In 1250 the Lubusz Land was acquired by the [Ascanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Ascania) [margraves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrave) of [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg). In 1319–1326 it was contested by various Polish and German rulers, before falling back to Brandenburg. After Brandenburg passed to the [Bohemian Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lands_of_the_Bohemian_Crown) in 1373, [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) made a peaceful attempt to regain the northern portion of the area. In 1402, the Bohemian rulers reached an agreement with Poland in [Kraków](16815.md). Poland was to buy and re-incorporate the northern outskirts of the present Lubusz Voivodeship,[^cite_note-5] but eventually the Bohemian rulers sold the area to the [Teutonic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Order), who in turn sold it back to Brandenburg in 1454 to raise funds for [war against Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Years'_War_\(1454%E2%80%931466\)). The southern part of the current voivodeship remained part of the duchies of Żagań and Głogów, ruled by the houses of Piast and [Jagiellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_dynasty), with the Żagań duchy eventually passing to houses of foreign background, including Czech, Saxon and French, whereas other areas were gradually incorporated directly into the [Kingdom of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia). In 1635, most of the south-western part of the present Lubusz Voivodeship passed from Bohemia to [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Saxony), and from 1697 formed part of [Poland-Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland-Saxony). In the 18th century, [Wschowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wschowa) was an important [royal city of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Poland), as it often hosted Polish kings and several sessions of the Polish Senate, hence being dubbed the "unofficial capital of Poland". King [Augustus III of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_III_of_Poland) also often stopped in [Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brody,_%C5%BBary_County).[^cite_note-6] Wschowa and [Gubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gubin,_Poland) had important [mints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_\(facility\)) that struck Polish coins.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]



[Wschowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wschowa), important [royal city of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Poland) in the 18th century, dubbed the "unofficial capital of Poland"

In 1701, the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) was established, which included Brandenburg-held Lubusz Land, and various areas were eventually gradually annexed by Prussia in the following centuries, starting with the south-eastern part of the current voivodeship in 1742, followed by eastern portions (western outskirts of Greater Poland) in [1793](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Partition_of_Poland) (briefly regained by Poles in 1807–1815 as part of the short-lived [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw)), and the south-western part in 1815. Within Prussia and Germany the territory was divided between the provinces of [Neumark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumark)/[Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg), [South Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Prussia)/[Posen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Posen), and [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Silesia)/[Lower Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lower_Silesia).

During [World War II](32927.md), the [Oflag II-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oflag_II-C), [Stalag III-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_III-C), [Stalag VIII-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_VIII-C) and [Stalag Luft III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_Luft_III) major [German prisoner-of-war camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) for Polish, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II), British, Belgian, Canadian, Serbian, [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Military_Internees), American, Australian, New Zealander, Soviet, Norwegian, Czech, Slovak, South African, Dutch, Greek, Yugoslav, [Senegalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_Tirailleurs), Algerian and Moroccan POWs were operated in the territory. The latter was the site of the "Great Escape" in 1944. There are museums at the site of the camps in [Dobiegniew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobiegniew) and [Żagań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBaga%C5%84), and a memorial to the victims of the [Stalag Luft III murders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_Luft_III_murders) in Żagań.[^cite_note-lpd43-9] Particularly infamous camps in the region were the Oderblick [labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) education camp in [Świecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awiecko) and the [Sonnenburg concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnenburg_concentration_camp) in [Słońsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82o%C5%84sk), in which Polish, Belgian, French, Bulgarian, Dutch, Yugoslav, Russian, Italian, Ukrainian, Luxembourgish, Danish, Norwegian, Czech, Slovak and other prisoners were held, and many died.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] There were also eleven [subcamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_subcamps_of_Gross-Rosen) of the [Gross-Rosen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross-Rosen_concentration_camp) and a subcamp of the [Sachsenhausen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp), in which mostly Jewish and Polish, but also French, Russian, Czech, Italian, Greek, Yugoslav, Dutch, Romanian, Hungarian, Lithuanian and German prisoners were held.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] [Obrzyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzyrzecz) was the place of *[Aktion T4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktion_T4)* murders of mentally ill and disabled people. The region was the site of fierce fighting during the war in 1945. During the liberation of the Stalag III-C camp, Soviet troops killed some American POWs mistaking them for enemy troops.[^cite_note-14] On 18 March 1945, the Germans shot down an American bomber near [Bucze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucze,_%C5%9Awiebodzin_County).[^cite_note-lpd-15]



Polish [Border Protection Troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Protection_Troops) in [Olszyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olszyna,_Lubusz_Voivodeship) in 1969

Under the terms laid down by [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) in the [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement), the borders of Poland and Germany were redrawn and the area of the Lubusz Voivodeship fell within the new borders of Poland.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In 1998, [the government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Jerzy_Buzek) of [Jerzy Buzek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Buzek) decided to introduce an [administrative reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Poland), with its principles including the restoration of counties and a steep reduction in the number of voivodeships. A general consensus existed among scholars that the local administration exercised through the [49 existing voivodeships established in 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland_\(1975%E2%80%931998\)) was inefficient, anachronistic, impractical, detrimental to maintaining regional identity, and untenable. However, the reform draft accepted by the government surprised the public and caused widespread outcry, as its authors foresaw creation of only 12 large voivodships, thus going much further than the widely expected reconstitution of the 17 voivodeships existing prior to the [1975 reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland_\(1975%E2%80%931998\)). As a consequence, the original draft made no provision for a separate Lubusz voivodeship – Gorzów was to become along with [Kostrzyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrzyn,_Greater_Poland_Voivodeship), [Strzelce Krajeńskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strzelce_Kraje%C5%84skie) and [Drezdenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drezdenko) a part of [West Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pomeranian_Voivodeship), Zielona Góra was to be included along with Krosno, Nowa Sól, Żagań, Gubin and Żary in the [Lower Silesian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Silesian_Voivodeship), while a narrow horizontal strip encompassing [Międzyrzecz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzyrzecz), [Sulęcin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sul%C4%99cin), [Świebodzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awiebodzin), [Słubice](656704.md) and [Sulechów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulech%C3%B3w) was to be assigned to the [Greater Poland Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Voivodeship) as a bizarre sort-of corridor to the German border. However, mass protests broke out as a result in the cities such as Bydgoszcz, Koszalin, Opole or Kielce. Many of the people opposing the draft reform initially demanded retaining as many as 25 voivodeships (including the 2 ones seated in Gorzów and Zielona Góra), a number nevertheless widely regarded as a demand intentionally excessive to serve as an initial negotiating bargain, actually aiming to restore the 17 voivodeships existing prior to 1975 as an ultimate compromise. As Poland was at the time governed under political cohabitation, the opposition party constituting the political background of the President decided to capitalize on the popular discontent which erupted against the government on an unanticipated scale; the most obvious mean readily available for the opposition was a presidential veto, which in fact ensued. In order to salvage the reform from being killed altogether, the government was, in the face of lacking the supermajority required to overturn the veto at the time, forced to reconsider the original shape of the reform and to reconcile it with the reservations of the President and his political background, with the result of a compromise adjustment increasing the number of voivodeships to 16, with Lubusz Voivodeship included among the four additional ones created according to the agreement.

The path leading to such and outcome was far from smooth. The government made an effort to highlight and exploit the decades-long animosity between the approximately same-size two principal cities, spreading scare against its inevitable re-ignition and explosion in any of these two cities after designating the other one as the voivodeship capital, and hoping to use the engineered scare as the main argument in the ongoing discussions against creating the Lubusz voivodeship, The animosity, existing indeed between the cities, has been historically rooted in a widespread perception among Gorzów inhabitants that the 1950 decision to designate Zielona Góra as the voivodeship capital instead of their larger and more populous city, was taken by the anticlerical communist government due to a hidden motivation of punishing Gorzów for becoming the see of the newly established Roman Catholic [apostolic administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_administration) governing the majority of the [Recovered Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovered_Territories), with the ensuing discrimination of the city by the voivodeship authorities in the years 1950–1975 in terms of establishing any new public cultural and educational institutions, other public investments or public funds allocations, in vivid contrast to the unjust favoring of their own seat, the city of Zielona Góra; a sentiment reinforced further by the surprise relocation of the see of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Gorzów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Gorz%C3%B3w) to Zielona Góra in 1992, renamed as a result the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Zielona Góra-Gorzów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Zielona_G%C3%B3ra-Gorz%C3%B3w), and finally and perhaps most importantly, by the historical, perpetual and almost sacred rivalry between the [motorcycle speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedway_in_Poland) clubs located in both cities. The objective of the local elites in Zielona Góra was in turn to become a single capital centre, reverting to the situation before 1975, while any prospect of sharing the governing institutions was for a long time treated with their hostility. In spite of that, the looming threat of a "everybody lose" scenario set to materialize in case of a possible implementation of the original reform draft, paved the way for neutralizing this argument through forcing both rival sides into the breakthrough reconciliation accord known as the Paradyż Agreement, brokered by the Roman Catholic Diocese of Zielona Góra-Gorzów and formalized in a document signed during a highly publicized local summit in the [Gościkowo-Paradyż Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go%C5%9Bcikowo) on 13 March 1998. This compromise agreement, was negotiated and concluded between the delegations of both rival cities, composed of the respectively aligned most powerful local and national scene politicians and business people, with its most important provision being the unusual arrangement to divide and distribute the governing institutions of the voivodeship more or less equally between the two cities. On the basis of this broadly supported agreement, an effective public pressure endorsed jointly by the two centers was successfully exerted on the central government which ultimately acquiesced to the demand of establishing Lubusz Voivodeship.[^cite_note-18]



[Polish Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Land_Forces) barracks in [Żagań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBaga%C5%84)

Nevertheless, creating any new type of public institution at voivodeship level in Poland continues to ignite almost automatically a fierce battle in the Lubusz Voivodeship regarding the seat of the institution. There have also been numerous attempts to relocate some of the existing public institutions under various pretexts from one city to another, in some cases successful, as well as of merging a pair of equal institutions of a type existing in both cities, in order to make one of them a branch of the other, with obscure or no justification in most cases for such merger. Nevertheless, a general local majority consensus prevails that the compromise, although unsatisfactory for any of the two cities, spared both of them the fate of a number of cities which lost in 1999 entirely the status of a voivodeship capital and all voivodeship-level institutions, along with the associated attractiveness and prestige of the city as a place to live, crucial for its growth, with the ensuing profoundly detrimental phenomena.

## Geography

The Lubusz Voivodeship is a land of forests and lakes; forests cover 48% of the area. The river [Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder), flowing through the voivodeship, is one of the few large European rivers retaining [broadleaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_broadleaf_and_mixed_forests) and [riparian forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riparian_forest). Areas with the highest natural values are protected as national parks ([Drawa National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawa_National_Park) and [Warta Mouth National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta_Mouth_National_Park)), [landscape parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_park_\(protected_area\)) and [wildlife reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve). The 19th century [Muskau Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskau_Park), located on both sides of the [Polish–German border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Poland_border), has entered the [UNESCO World Heritage List](44940.md). The voivodeship abounds in lakes, especially in its central and northern parts; around those lakes numerous bathing resorts, holiday centres and farms offering tourist services have been established.[^cite_note-19]

## Cuisine



2012 Zielona Góra Wine Fest

The voivodeship, especially its northern part, is a notable for production of honey, with several varieties listed as [traditional foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_food) by the [Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Rural_Development_\(Poland\)), and the Saint Michael's Honey Fair held annually in Gorzów Wielkopolski.

The southern part of the voivodeship with [Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_G%C3%B3ra) is one of the leading [winemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winemaking) regions of Poland, and other traditional beverages from the voivodeship are [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_in_Poland), [mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mead), [nalewki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalewka) and [vodka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodka). Zielona Góra hosts the annual Zielona Góra Wine Fest, the largest wine fest in Poland.

Various types of traditional Polish [kiełbasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kie%C5%82basa), also designated as traditional foods by the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of Poland, are produced in the [Nowa Sól](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowa_S%C3%B3l_County), [Wschowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wschowa_County), [Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_G%C3%B3ra_County), [Żagań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBaga%C5%84_County) and [Żary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBary_County) counties in the southern part of the Lubusz Voivodeship, whereas [Siedlisko, Nowa Sól County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siedlisko,_Nowa_S%C3%B3l_County) produces a variety of traditional cheeses and [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark). [Sława](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82awa) with its environs is one of the main poultry-farming regions in Poland, particularly for [turkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_\(bird\)), and specializes in the production of various turkey sausages and other meat products.[^cite_note-21]

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the province was 10.8 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 2.2% of Polish economic output. The GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 17,600 euros or 58% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 67% of the EU average.[^cite_note-22]

### Rail

Main article: [Polregio (Lubusz)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polregio_\(Lubusz\))

The dominant [rail operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_railway_companies_in_Poland) of the voivodeship is the [Lubusz branch of Polregio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_branch_of_Polregio), which operates [regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail) passenger trains under a contract with the Lubusz Voivodeship.[^cite_note-23] The branch is based in [Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_G%C3%B3ra), and its services cover much of the region, with some extending to neighbouring voivodeships.[^cite_note-24]

Since 2024, the Lubusz Voivodeship has been planning to operate passenger trains under its own company, [Lubusz Public Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_Public_Transport?action=edit\&redlink=1) (LTP), which was founded in the following year.[^cite_note-25] The voivodeship is currently on a 10-year contract with Polregio; but is considering to terminate it early. This came after the Lubusz Voivodeship complained about Polregio's "mass train cancellations"[^cite_note-26] and "shortage of trains", implying that the [rolling stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_stock) of the Lubusz branch is very limited, consisting of mostly older stock. The president of Polregio, [Andrzej Pawłowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Paw%C5%82owski_\(railway_official\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), said "establishing your own company will not be a miracle solution that will automatically heal the rolling stock situation".[^cite_note-27]

Furthermore, there are several preserved old towns with historic town halls and market squares (e.g. [Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_G%C3%B3ra), [Wschowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wschowa), [Świebodzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awiebodzin) and [Bytom Odrzański](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bytom_Odrza%C5%84ski)). Of the historic town halls, the one in [Kargowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargowa) was the site of an armed defense against the annexation by Prussia in the [Second Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Partition_of_Poland) in 1793. Several towns contain entirely or partly preserved medieval town walls with towers and gates, e.g. [Kożuchów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%BCuch%C3%B3w), [Strzelce Krajeńskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strzelce_Kraje%C5%84skie), [Gorzów Wielkopolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorz%C3%B3w_Wielkopolski),[^cite_note-35] [Ośno Lubuskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%C5%9Bno_Lubuskie), Wschowa. There are multiple castles, including Piast Royal and Ducal castles in [Krosno Odrzańskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno_Odrza%C5%84skie), [Kożuchów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%BCuch%C3%B3w), [Międzyrzecz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzyrzecz) and [Żagań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBaga%C5%84). There are also numerous palaces, including at [Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brody,_%C5%BBary_County), [Dąbrówka Wielkopolska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%85br%C3%B3wka_Wielkopolska), [Glisno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glisno,_Gmina_Lubniewice), [Jędrzychowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C4%99drzychowice,_Wschowa_County), [Kalsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalsk), [Mierzęcin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mierz%C4%99cin,_Lubusz_Voivodeship), [Trzebiechów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trzebiech%C3%B3w,_Zielona_G%C3%B3ra_County), [Żary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBary). The village of [Łagów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81ag%C3%B3w,_%C5%9Awiebodzin_County), which hosted the [Knights Templar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Templar) and the [Sovereign Military Order of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_Military_Order_of_Malta) in the past, contains the [Castle of the Order of St. John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81ag%C3%B3w_Castle), and several other medieval structures. [Kostrzyn nad Odrą](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrzyn_nad_Odr%C4%85) and its surroundings contain fortifications of the Kostrzyn Fortress with Fort Sarbinowo, which housed a [German strict regime prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_I) for [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) officers during [World War I](4764461.md), considered the only German WWI POW camp from which no one managed to escape.[^cite_note-36]

Major museums dedicated to the history of the region are located in Gorzów Wielkopolski and Zielona Góra. There are museums dedicated to [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) prisoners of war at the former [German POW camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) in [Dobiegniew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobiegniew) and Żagań.[^cite_note-lpd43-9] In Żagań, there is a memorial to the victims of the [Stalag Luft III murders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_Luft_III_murders) of Allied POWs, perpetrated by Nazi Germany in World War II. There are multiple other memorials to victims of Nazi Germany in the region, including at the sites of the most notorious atrocities in [Międzyrzecz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzyrzecz), [Słońsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82o%C5%84sk) and [Świecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awiecko). In [Bucze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucze,_%C5%9Awiebodzin_County) there is a memorial to American airmen shot down by the Germans in 1945.[^cite_note-lpd-15] The garrison town of Żagań hosts Poland's oldest monument of [Wojtek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojtek), the soldier bear of the [Polish II Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/II_Corps_\(Poland\)).[^cite_note-37]

[Sława](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82awa) is a popular summer holiday destination in the voivodeship, due to Lake Sławskie, the largest lake of the voivodeship, with beaches,[^cite_note-38] a marina, holiday resorts and the Ptasi Raj ("Bird Paradise") viewing platform, which allows visitors to observe the birdlife of the lake and its islands,[^cite_note-39] home to various species of birds, part of a bird sanctuary of European importance.[^cite_note-40]

[*Kuna*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuna_\(icebreaker\)), the world's oldest in service [river icebreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_icebreaker), is docked in the river port of Gorzów Wielkopolski and is open to the public as a [museum ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_ship).[^cite_note-41]

One of the world's tallest Christ statues, the [Christ the King Statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_the_King_Statue,_%C5%9Awiebodzin) is located in [Świebodzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awiebodzin), whereas [Słubice](656704.md) hosts the [Wikipedia Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_Monument).

## Education

The chief [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_Poland) institutions in the Lubusz Voivodeship are the [University of Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zielona_G%C3%B3ra) and the [Jacob of Paradies University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_of_Paradies_University?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Gorzów Wielkopolski. Gorzów is also home to the Faculty of Physical Culture in Gorzów Wielkopolski of the [Poznań University of Physical Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84_University_of_Physical_Education?action=edit\&redlink=1), whereas [Słubice](656704.md) is home to the [Collegium Polonicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Polonicum), a branch of the [Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz_University_in_Pozna%C5%84). [Private colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_college) are the Lusatian Higher School in [Żary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBary)[^cite_note-42] and the Higher School of Business in Gorzów Wielkopolski.[^cite_note-43]
