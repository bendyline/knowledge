The **Centre Party** ([Swedish](26689.md): *Centerpartiet* [\[ˈsɛ̂nːtɛrpaˌʈiːɛt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/29/Sv-Centerpartiet.ogg/Sv-Centerpartiet.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/29/Sv-Centerpartiet.ogg/Sv-Centerpartiet.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, **C**) is a [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism)[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-Nordsieck-7][^cite_note-8] [political party in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Sweden), founded in 1913. The party focuses on the [national economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy), the [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment), political [decentralisation](49139.md) and [social integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_integration). It is represented in all of the [Riksdag](158651.md)'s parliamentary committees, currently holding 24 seats.

Traditionally part of the [Nordic agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_agrarian_parties) family of political parties, the Centre Party has increasingly switched focus towards [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism), [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection), [equality of the sexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality) and [decentralisation](49139.md) of governmental authority.[^cite_note-SR-9][^cite_note-BischoffWind2015-10] The party describes itself as [liberal feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_feminism),[^cite_note-11] campaigning for policies which enhance gender equality on an [individualist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism) basis. Its environmental policies stress the importance of consent and voluntary action,[^cite_note-12] including working with [foresters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forester) and private landowners to promote [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) within a mutually agreeable framework.[^cite_note-13]

The Centre Party has produced two [prime ministers of Sweden](195846.md): [Axel Pehrsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Pehrsson-Bramstorp) who held the prime minister post for three months in 1936 and [Thorbjörn Fälldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorbj%C3%B6rn_F%C3%A4lldin) who held the post for a total of five years, from [1976 to 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_I_cabinet) and then again from [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_II_cabinet) to [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_III_cabinet). It is a member of the [Alliance of Liberals and Democrats for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Liberals_and_Democrats_for_Europe_Party), the [Liberal International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_International) and [Renew Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renew_Europe). It was originally named the **Farmers' League** (Swedish: *Bondeförbundet* [\[ˈbʊ̂nːdɛfœrˌbɵndɛt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f5/Sv-Bondef%C3%B6rbundet.ogg/Sv-Bondef%C3%B6rbundet.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f5/Sv-Bondef%C3%B6rbundet.ogg/Sv-Bondef%C3%B6rbundet.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; **B**).

## History



Farmers' League 1945 election poster

The party was founded in 1913 as the **Farmers' League** (*Bondeförbundet*, B). In 1921, it merged with the [National Agrarian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Agrarian_Union). It adopted the name *Centre Party* in 1958.[^cite_note-14] It was a [coalition](6036.md) partner of the [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics) [Swedish Social Democratic Party](202978.md) between [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansson_II_cabinet) and [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansson_III_cabinet) as well as between 1951 and 1957. However, it later revised this strategy in order to establish a closer long-term alliance between the [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) *borgerlig* ("bourgeois" or "nonsocialist") parties that achieved power between [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_I_cabinet) and [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_III_cabinet), [1991 and 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bildt_cabinet), as well as between [2006 and 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinfeldt_cabinet).[^cite_note-\:2-15]

Centre Party leader [Axel Pehrsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Pehrsson-Bramstorp) served as [prime minister of Sweden](195846.md) for three months in 1936. The party held the prime minister post a second time with [Thorbjörn Fälldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorbj%C3%B6rn_F%C3%A4lldin) who served as prime minister from [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_I_cabinet) until [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_III_cabinet), except for a short interregnum between [1978 and 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ullsten_cabinet) led by [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberals_\(Sweden\)) leader [Ola Ullsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ola_Ullsten). Following the [1991 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Swedish_general_election), the Centre Party joined [another centre-right government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bildt_cabinet), led by [Moderate Party](202985.md) leader [Carl Bildt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bildt).[^cite_note-\:2-15] During the leaderships of [Maud Olofsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maud_Olofsson) and [Annie Lööf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_L%C3%B6%C3%B6f) in the 2000s, the party positioned itself clearly on the [political right](56522.md) as a [small-business](276495.md)-friendly party, advocating [market liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_liberalism) policies and viewing the Social Democrats as its main opponent.[^cite_note-DN-16][^cite_note-SR-9][^cite_note-local-17] Between 2006 and 2014, it was a member of the centre-right [Reinfeldt cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinfeldt_cabinet).[^cite_note-\:2-15] It provided [confidence and supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) for the Social Democratic led [Löfven II cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6fven_II_cabinet) between 2019 and 2021.[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-18] Since 2022, it is in opposition against the right-wing [Kristersson cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristersson_cabinet) because of the cabinet's collaboration with the nationalist [Sweden Democrats](65873.md).[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-19]

In 2005, the Centre Party sold the newspaper group it owned, [Centertidningar AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centertidningar_AB), for 1.8 billion [SEK](16709.md),[^cite_note-20] which made it the richest political party in the world at the time.[^cite_note-21]

## Ideology and political position

The Centre Party sits on the [centre](39619035.md)[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] to [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics),[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Bittner2011-27] even [right-wing](56522.md)[^cite_note-\:1-28], of the [political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_spectrum). The party has also described as [economically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism)[^cite_note-\:1-28][^cite_note-29] and [socially liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_liberalism),[^cite_note-\:1-28] and "ecological-liberal".[^cite_note-30] It describes itself as a [green-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_liberalism) party,[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] while it has been traditionally associated with [agrarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarianism) and the [Nordic agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_agrarian_parties) party family.[^cite_note-ErssonLane-33][^cite_note-MarksWilson-34][^cite_note-Blandford-35]

### National economy

The party has been described as one of Sweden's most [market liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_liberalism) parties in liberal, socialist and conservative media.[^cite_note-36] It describes itself as "a party with a green, social and decentralised liberalism".[^cite_note-centerpartiet.se-37] The party advocates lower taxes, greatly reduced employer contributions, a freer market and an increased [RUT-deduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RUT-deduction?action=edit\&redlink=1). The party is a major supporter of the interests of [small businesses](276495.md), farmers and entrepreneurs.[^cite_note-38] It also favours investments in infrastructure and transportation, to allow employees to work in bigger cities but still live in the rural areas and vice versa. On economic policy, it views the [Social Democrats](202978.md) and the [Sweden Democrats](65873.md) as its opponents, though it supported a government led by the Social Democrats till 2022.

### Immigration

The party is liberal on immigration, seeking to combine a generous immigration policy with an initially more restrictive contribution policy to the immigrants. After the [European migrant crisis](46415102.md), the party proposed to replace the existing establishment grants with establishment loans, similar to the [Swedish student loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_financial_aid_in_Sweden).[^cite_note-39]

The balance of the state responsibility of accepting [refugees](45547.md) with their responsibility for integration into Swedish society is at the core of the party policy. In January 2016, the party for example proposed to give all immigrants compulsory civic education in both rights and expectations from the society.[^cite_note-40]

### European Union

The party is a decentralist [pro-European](631596.md)[^cite_note-41] party that considers the [European Union](9317.md) important for the preservation of peace, freedom and trade in Europe. The party also advocates a smaller but sharper European Union focused on promoting democracy, peace, free movement, free trade, vigorous action against climate change and collaboration against organized crime, while also believing that Sweden should stay outside the [Economic and Monetary Union of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Monetary_Union_of_the_European_Union) and keep the [krona](16709.md) as its currency.[^cite_note-42]

The party is a member of the [ALDE Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Liberals_and_Democrats_for_Europe_Party) and its affiliated [European Parliament](9581.md) group [Renew Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renew_Europe).[^cite_note-43] In the [European Committee of the Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Committee_of_the_Regions), the Centre Party sits with the [Renew Europe CoR group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renew_Europe_in_the_European_Committee_of_the_Regions) with one full and one alternate member for the 2025-2030 mandate.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

## Publications

The Centre Party owned a media consortium called Centertidningar AB. It included newspapers that the party had either started on their own or brought from competitors. It included *[Hallands Nyheter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallands_Nyheter)*, *[Södermanlands Nyheter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6dermanlands_Nyheter)*, *[Länstidningen i Södertälje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A4nstidningen_i_S%C3%B6dert%C3%A4lje?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Nynäshamns Posten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyn%C3%A4shamns_Posten?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Norrtelje Tidning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrtelje_Tidning?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Lidingö Tidning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liding%C3%B6_Tidning?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Ljusdalsposten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljusdalsposten?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Östersunds-Posten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96stersunds-Posten)*, *[Hälsingekuriren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A4lsingekuriren?action=edit\&redlink=1)* and *[Hudiksvalls Tidning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudiksvalls_Tidning)*. The consortium was split in 2005 and sold to [Mittmedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittmedia?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Stampen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stampen_Group) and [VLT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestmanlands_L%C3%A4ns_Tidning) for a total of 1.815 billion [Swedish kronor](16709.md).

Traditionally, most of the party's voters come from rural areas and include farmers and agricultural producers. Since the takeover of [Maud Olofsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maud_Olofsson) in recent years, the party has been attracting [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) voters from urban areas in central Sweden. It is believed that voters from the [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberals_\(Sweden\)) have been moving to the Centre Party due to changes in both parties.[^cite_note-46]

## Leaders of the Centre Party
