The **Lithuanian Armed Forces** ([Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language): *Lietuvos ginkluotosios pajėgos*) are the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) of [Lithuania](17675.md). The Lithuanian Armed Forces consist of the [Lithuanian Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Land_Forces), the [Lithuanian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Navy), the [Lithuanian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Air_Force) and the [Lithuanian Special Operations Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Special_Operations_Force). In wartime, the [Lithuanian State Border Guard Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_State_Border_Guard_Service) (which is under the supervision of the [Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Lithuania\)) in peacetime) becomes part of the Lithuanian Armed Forces.

The purpose of the Lithuanian Armed Forces are to be the principal deterrent against any security threat to the nation. Lithuania's defence system is based on the concept of "total and unconditional defence" mandated by Lithuania's *National Security Strategy*. The goal of Lithuania's defence policy is to prepare their society for general defence and to integrate Lithuania into Western security and defence structures. The [Ministry of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defence_\(Lithuania\)) is responsible for combat forces, [search and rescue](172599.md), and intelligence operations.[^cite_note-WB-4]

Male [conscription](5735.md) is in place since 2015, when it was reinstated after being ended in 2008, due to concerns about the [geopolitical environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Cold_War) in light of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEČepinskytė2016-5] Female service in Lithuania is currently voluntary, but it has become the subject of growing political and public debate, with defence officials and policymakers advocating a greater role for women in the armed forces and some leaders warning that Lithuania may ultimately need to introduce female or universal conscription in response to long-term security challenges, demographic constraints, and the evolving regional security environment.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

In early 2022, Lithuania's defence budget for 2022 was approximately [€](9472.md)1.05 billion,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHackett2022124-1]<sup>[\[c\]](#endnote_cnone)</sup> but it was increased to €1.5 billion on 17 March 2022.[^cite_note-9] In 2024, the budget was raised to €2.3 billion and is projected to reach 3.03% of GDP.[^cite_note-lt-mil-budget-3]

## History

### Grand Ducal Lithuanian Army



Seal of [Grand Duke Kęstutis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%99stutis) from 1379 depicting a Lithuanian infantryman

The Lithuanian military originates in the [Grand Ducal Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) Army, active from the 13th century to 1795.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] It fought many major battles, such as the [Battle of Blue Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blue_Waters) (1362/63), [Battle of Grunwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grunwald) (1410), [Battle of Orsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Orsha) (1514) and [Battle of Kircholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kircholm) (1605). After the [Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin) in 1569, the Lithuanian Army remained equal to the Polish [Crown army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Army) in the [military of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) until the [Third Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland) in 1795.

Similarly to other [medieval European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_European) states, the army was [raised by the nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pospolite_ruszenie) during the Late Middle Ages. By the 17th century, it was mostly outclassed by professional forces and a [standing army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_army) was instituted.

#### 13th century

From the 12th century's end and into the 13th century, Lithuania frequently went to war against the western and southwestern [Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') states, [Kingdom of Galicia-Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia) and [Duchy of Masovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Masovia), and also devastated the lands along the [Daugava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugava) and elsewhere.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] From the early 1200s, Lithuanians fought against the [Swordbrothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordbrothers), then from 1237 against the [Livonian Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_Order), and from the second half of the 13th century's against the State of the Teutonic Order.**[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]** Fighting on Lithuania's northern and western frontiers was unceasing, the Lithuanian state expanded southwards and eastwards in the [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages).**[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]** The Lithuanian army was mobile, as it had to fight on many fronts: the [State of the Teutonic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Teutonic_Order) to the west, the Livonian Order to the north, the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde) and its vassal [Muscovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscovy_\(Grand_Duchy\)) to the east, and the [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) khanates to the south.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] According to 13th-century sources, Lithuanian soldiers rode horses on military expeditions but fought on foot, arranged in three rows during battles.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] The best-armed and most experienced fought in the front, while the least experienced and lightly armed were in the last row.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Furthermore, the Lithuanians were skilled at fighting using spears, especially on horseback. The earliest written mention of such tactics, from 1208, says that Lithuanians on horses threw spears at their enemies.[^cite_note-11]

Although the Germans initially had superior weaponry in the 13th century, the Lithuanians won the Battles of [Saule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Saule) (1236), [Durbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Durbe) (1260), [Karuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Karuse) (1270) and the [Aizkraukle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aizkraukle) (1279).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] However they were less successful against enemy fortifications, especially brick castles.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Eventually the front lines stabilised over time, and the one against the Livonian Order more or less followed the modern [Latvia–Lithuania border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia%E2%80%93Lithuania_border), while the one against the Teutonic Order was close to the [Nemunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemunas).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10][^cite_note-\:0-12] A castle system fortified the Lithuanian side of the border along the river.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

#### 14th century

Over the 14th century, the Teutonic and Livonian orders organised raids into Lithuania.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Lithuanians reciprocated by raiding their respective territories, but the Lithuanian raids were fewer in number.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] The Lithuanians won the [Battle of Medininkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Medininkai) (1320), but lost the [Battle of Strėva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Str%C4%97va) (1348).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] More and more, the Teutonic Order destroyed the Lithuanian castle system along the Nemunas and built their own castles near the Lithuanian ones.**[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]** As the German and Livonian orders were constantly reinforced by Christian European countries, it became increasingly difficult to defend Lithuania solely by military means.**[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]** A new generation of the Lithuanian Grand Dukes, [Jogaila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogaila) and [Vytautas the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vytautas_the_Great), used not only military, but also diplomatic and political means, for example [Lithuanian baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Lithuania#Christianization_by_Jogaila_and_Vytautas) in 1387, to protect Lithuania.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

Meanwhile, on the other side of Lithuania, the Golden Horde's army was destroyed in the [Battle of Blue Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blue_Waters) (1362–1363).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In 1368, 1370, and 1372, the Lithuanian Grand Duke [Algirdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algirdas) led a military expeditions against Muscovy.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] However, the [Battle of the Vorksla River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Vorskla_River) (1399) was a decisive victory for the Golden Horde.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

#### 15th century

Finally, the German Teutonic Order was crushed in the [Battle of Grunwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grunwald) (1410) (known as Battle of Žalgiris in Lithuanian historiography), which was the largest Middle Age battle in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In this key battle, the Lithuanian Army was led by Vytautas the Great.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Under him, the Lithuanian Army attacked the lands of the Republics of [Pskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Republic), in 1426, and [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Republic), in 1428.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] The Grand Duchy of Lithuania had internal civil wars in the first half of the 15th century.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] The Lithuania Army engaged in [biological warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_warfare) already in 1422, when it catapulted manure made from infected victims into an opposing [Bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia) town as part of the [Hussite Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite_Wars).[^cite_note-13] In 1435, [Sigismund Kęstutaitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund_K%C4%99stutaitis)' army defeated the opposing army, which included troops of the [Livonian Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_Order), led by [Švitrigaila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0vitrigaila) in the [Battle of Wiłkomierz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wi%C5%82komierz).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

#### 16th century

This century was marked by war of Lithuania against Muscovy and the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate), against whom they won the [Battle of Kletsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kletsk) in 1506.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] The strengthening Grand Duchy of Moscow starting in the late 15th century waged [unceasing wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscovite%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Wars) over Lithuania's eastern territories.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In 1514, during the fourth war, the Lithuanians triumphed over the numerically larger Muscovite army in the famous [Battle of Orsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Orsha).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] However, Lithuania lost a part of its eastern lands, most notably the strategically important fortress of [Smolensk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolensk).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Lithuania fought against Muscovy in the [Livonian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_War) and won against the Muscovite army, twice its size in the [Battle of Ula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ula) in 1564.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Three years into the Livonian War, the [Treaty of Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Vilnius_\(1561\)).[^cite_note-\:0-12] made Livonia part of Lithuania in 1561 with the [Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin) concluded in 1569.[^cite_note-\:0-12] In the late 1570s and early 1580s, the Lithuanian and Polish armies cooperated in Stephen Báthory's [incursions into Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_B%C3%A1thory#War_with_Muscovy).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

#### 17th century

The 17th century was marked by wars against Sweden, the [Tsardom of Muscovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Muscovy) and the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] The Lithuanian army and the Polish army fought against the Ottoman forces notably at both Battles of Khotyn in [1621](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khotyn_\(1621\)) and [1673](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khotyn_\(1673\)).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] During the [war with Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Swedish_War_\(1600%E2%80%931629\)) from 1600 to 1629, the Lithuanian army defeated Swedish forces three times their number at the [Battle of Kircholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kircholm) in 1605.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] However, this war highlighted the Commonwealth's difficulty recruiting and retaining enough troops, better arming its soldiers with firearms and bettering their use.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Sweden seized a significant part of Livonia as part of [Swedish Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Livonia).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In the first half of the 17th century, Smolensk returned to Lithuania following the [Smolensk War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolensk_War).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

The Commonwealth's military weakness in the middle and late 17th century was evidenced in the [Deluge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deluge_\(history\)).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In 1655, the much-smaller Lithuanian army could not defend the Lithuanian capital of [Vilnius](32597.md) against Muscovite attack.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] This was the first time that Vilnius was occupied by a foreign state.[^cite_note-\:0-12] The Swedish and Muscovite armies occupied large parts of Lithuania.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Nevertheless, Lithuania succeeded in holding out and liberated Vilnius, [Kaunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas), [Samogitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samogitia) and the eastern Voivodeships, except for [Smolensk Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolensk_Voivodeship) and other parts.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Militarily speaking, however, the Grand Duchy of Lithuania was weakening.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

#### 18th century



The Lithuanian Army's Life Dragoons Regiment, part of the [Royal Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Guards_\(Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth\)) (1775)

During the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) the Lithuanian Army no longer defended the country and the [Lithuanian magnates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnates_of_Poland_and_Lithuania)' private armies supported different sides.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] Throughout the 18th century, the many nobles [confederations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_\(Poland%E2%80%93Lithuania\)) sought different political goals.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] The [Bar Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Confederation) (1768–1772), which happened partly in Lithuania, attempted but failed to stem the increasing influence of Imperial Russia and the [First partition of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_partition_of_the_Polish-Lithuanian_Commonwealth) followed.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In the face of the possible loss of independence, military reforms in the [Four Years' Sejm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Years'_Sejm) (1788–1792) significantly expanded the Lithuanian army, which reached a strength of 17,500.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10][^cite_note-\:0-12] Attempts to make the Commonwealth's armies equal those of neighbouring [absolute monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy) were unsuccessful and the unsuccessful [War of 1792](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1792) resulted in the [Second Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Partition_of_the_Polish-Lithuanian_Commonwealth).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

The Polish and Lithuanian armies put up a spirited fight against the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) and the [Prussian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Army) in the [Kościuszko Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%9Bciuszko_Uprising).[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In addition to regular forces, many *ad hoc* units such as the [Vilnian National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnian_National_Guard) also fought, as well as many irregular units.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] In the end, the Uprising was defeated and much of the remainder of the Lithuanian Army was lost in the [Battle of Praga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Praga) on 4 November 1794.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10] With the uprising defeated, the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) ended with the [Third Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_the_Polish-Lithuanian_Commonwealth), and their respective armies being disbanded.[^cite_note-lt-military-medieval-10]

After Lithuania's [restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Independence_of_Lithuania) on 16 February 1918, the country immediately began creating an army.[^cite_note-lt-military-interwar-22] The Ministry of Defence's first order, issued on 23 November 1918, is considered to mark the establishment of the modern Lithuanian Armed Forces.[^cite_note-lt-military-hist-23] The newly-formed army almost immediately fought [three wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Wars_of_Independence). Having won the [Lithuanian–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian%E2%80%93Soviet_War) and the [war against the Bermontians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Wars_of_Independence#War_against_the_Bermontians), Lithuania lost large chunks of territory, including its capital [Vilnius](32597.md) in the [Polish–Lithuanian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_War). Armoured equipment in the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period), primarily consisted of [light tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_tank) and [armored cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_car_\(military\)): French [Renault FT-17s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_FT), British [Vickers Carden-Loyd M1934s and M1936s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_tanks_of_the_United_Kingdom#Commercial_Carden-Loyd_tanks), Swedish [Landsverk-181](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsverk_L180,_L181_and_L182), and German [Ehrhardt E-V/4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehrhardt_E-V/4).[^cite_note-lt-military-interwar-22] In 1935, the country opened an advanced [military research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Science) laboratory, specialising in chemical materials for ammunition as well as defence against [chemical warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_warfare).[^cite_note-24] The construction of the laboratory was supervised by [Juozas Vėbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juozas_V%C4%97bra). In 1940, Lithuania had a [considerable Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Air_Force#1919%E2%80%931940), consisting of 118 aircraft with about half of them designed and produced locally. During the [World War II](32927.md), Lithuania was invaded by [both Nazis and Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_in_Lithuania_during_World_War_II), which eventually concluded in [Soviet occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Baltic_states). The Lithuanian Armed Forces transformed into the [Lithuanian People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_People's_Army) in 1940 under the [People's Government of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Government_of_Lithuania).[^cite_note-25] Despite [Soviet deportations from Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_deportations_from_Lithuania), [armed Lithuanian resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_partisans) lasted until the 1950s.

### Restoration and NATO



Lithuanian soldiers shaking hands with the [American soldiers](32087.md) during the [Operation Atlantic Resolve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atlantic_Resolve) in 2014

Following the [restoration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_the_Re-Establishment_of_the_State_of_Lithuania), the defence forces were formally reestablished on 25 April 1990 with the creation of the Department of National Defence. After the [January Events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Events), the [Voluntary National Defence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_National_Defence_Volunteer_Forces) was formed of lightly armed volunteers. The Lithuanian Armed Forces were officially restored on 19 November 1992.[^cite_note-lt-military-hist-23] [Western European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European) countries, especially Sweden, helped to arm the initial force by selling or donating excess equipment. Lithuania began the modernisation of its military, becoming the first European country to acquire the US-made [FGM-148 Javelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FGM-148_Javelin) systems in 2001 and the [FIM-92 Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger) systems in 2002.[^cite_note-lt-military-modern-26]

Lithuania applied for [NATO membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_NATO) in 1994 and eventually [joined the alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO) in 2004. It has modernised its armed forces and participated in various international missions including the [NATO-led mission in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force). [Conscription](5735.md) ended in September 2008, but was reintroduced in 2015 due to deteriorating [geopolitical environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War_II) after the [Russia's military intervention in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Russian_military_intervention_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-Ministry_of_National_Defence,_Lithuania-27][^cite_note-Delfi-28] NATO also responded by establishing the [NATO Enhanced Forward Presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Enhanced_Forward_Presence) in 2017 with a battlegroup in Lithuania which was led by Germany.[^cite_note-29]

### After the Russian invasion of Ukraine

After the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Lithuania rapidly increased the defence spending, becoming one of the top spenders by GDP in the NATO military alliance.[^cite_note-30] The following years marked major acquisitions, including [M142 HIMARS](147011.md) with the [ATACMS](147015.md) [tactical ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_ballistic_missile), additional [NASAMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASAMS) medium-range air defence systems and artillery ammunition.[^cite_note-31] In 2023, Lithuanian leadership approved the plans to form an army [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\)), based on the three army brigades.[^cite_note-army-division-32] As part of the plan, the country also decided to acquire [Leopard 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_2) tanks.[^cite_note-33] In 2023, Germany agreed to deploy a [brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade) in Lithuania on a permanent basis.[^cite_note-34] The [Bundeswehr](288188.md)'s [45th Panzer Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Panzer_Brigade_\(Bundeswehr\)), consisting of \~5,000 troops, is scheduled to be deployed by 2027.[^cite_note-35] On 28 January 2025, the [1st Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Lithuania\)) was re-created.

### Land Forces

Main article: [Lithuanian Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Land_Forces)



Lithuanian [1st Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Lithuania\)) soldiers during „Allied Spirit 2025“ exercise

The core of the Lithuanian Land Forces structure is the [Mechanised Infantry Brigade Iron Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanised_Infantry_Brigade_Iron_Wolf) (MIB "Iron Wolf") consisting of four [mechanized infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanized_infantry) battalions and an [artillery battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Romualdas_Giedraitis_Artillery_Battalion), supported by the *Žemaitija* [Motorized infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorized_infantry) Brigade, which has three battalions and one artillery battalion as well. The third, *Aukštaitija* [Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Infantry) Brigade, is a reserve formation with active training. Its command, signal and logistic units are manned by professional soldiers.

The [Volunteer Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_National_Defence_Volunteer_Forces) form another brigade-size force, consisting of six territorial units. Other auxiliary units include *Juozas Vitkus Engineer Battalion* and *[Juozas Lukša](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juozas_Luk%C5%A1a) Land Forces Training Center*.



Lithuanian [PzH 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzerhaubitze_2000) battery of [General Romualdas Giedraitis Artillery Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Romualdas_Giedraitis_Artillery_Battalion) during an exercise



[1st Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Lithuania\)) [HIMARS](147011.md) battery during Operation „Baltic Alliance“

The Lithuanian Land forces use equipment compatible with NATO standards. Since 2007, the standard assault rifle is the German [Heckler & Koch G36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_G36). Units are supplied with modern variants of anti-tank weapons ([M72 LAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M72_LAW), [Carl Gustaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustaf_8.4_cm_recoilless_rifle), [AT4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT4), [FGM-148 Javelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FGM-148_Javelin)) as well as [man-portable air-defense systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-portable_air-defense_systems) ([PZR Grom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZR_Grom), [RBS-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RBS-70), [FIM-92 Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger)). Modern armoured equipment includes: [Oshkosh L-ATV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh_L-ATV) [armoured cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_car_\(military\)), [Boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_\(armoured_fighting_vehicle\)) [infantry fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle) (local designation IVF "Vilkas") armed with [Spike-LR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_\(missile\)) anti-tank missiles and [PzH 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PzH_2000) [self-propelled howitzers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-propelled_howitzer). Lithuanian Land forces have carried out major modernization and acquired more new weapons and heavier armour.

Lithuania has been restructuring its armed forces so that one-tenth of the Land Forces could at any given time be deployed for international operations, while half of the Land Forces would be prepared to deploy outside Lithuania's borders. The volunteers have already successfully participated in international operations in the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq.

In May 2023, the State Defence Council approved the plan to form an army [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\)), based on the three army brigades.[^cite_note-army-division-32] The [1st Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Lithuania\)) would have enhanced capabilities, including new tank, reconnaissance, engineering and artillery battalions.[^cite_note-army-division-32]

### Air Force

Main article: [Lithuanian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Air_Force)



Lithuanian Air Force [Zokniai Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai_Air_Base).

The Lithuanian Air Force (LAF) is an integral part of the Lithuanian Armed Forces. The LAF is formed by professional military servicemen and non-military personnel. Units are located at various bases across Lithuania:

- [Kaunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas) (Headquarters and the [Airspace Surveillance and Control Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airspace_Surveillance_and_Control_Command_\(Lithuania\)));

- [Karmėlava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karm%C4%97lava) (Air Space Control Centre);

- [Nemirseta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemirseta) (providing basing for sea search and rescue detachment);

- [Šiauliai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai) (Zokniai Air Base, Air Force Armament and Equipment Repair Depot);

- [Radviliškis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radvili%C5%A1kis) ([Air Defence Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_defence_battalion_\(Lithuania\))).

  [^cite_note-42]

&#x20;The initial formation of the LAF was the 2nd transport squadron with the transfer of 20 [An-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-2) aircraft from civilian to military use, with initial basing at the [Barysiai Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barysiai_Airport) on 27 April 1992. These were joined by four [L-39C Albatros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-39_Albatros) aircraft to be used by the 1st fighter (training) squadron. These were in addition to [Mil Mi-8](957233.md) helicopters and a short-range transport aircraft [L-410](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-410), all of which went through a capital overhaul, upgrade and modernisation in the 2000s.



Lithuanian Air Force [Air Defence Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Defence_Battalion_\(Lithuania\)) [NASAMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASAMS) battery during "Joint Silver Arrow 2023" exercise.&#x20;

Following the initial acquisitions, the LAF began its aircraft's modernisation by ordering three [C-27J Spartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-27J_Spartan) transporters in 2006. In 2013, three [Eurocopter AS365 Dauphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_AS365_Dauphin) helicopters were acquired from France and, in 2020, Lithuania announced an order or four [Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_UH-60_Black_Hawk) helicopters from the USA. Simultaneously, new medium-range and long-range radars were acquired for the [Airspace Surveillance and Control Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airspace_Surveillance_and_Control_Command_\(Lithuania\)).

Air space is patrolled by jet fighters from other [NATO members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_members) and they are based in [Zokniai Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zokniai_Air_Base), near the city of [Šiauliai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai) (see [Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing)). The [external border of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_border_of_the_European_Union) (with [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad) and [Belarus](3457.md)) is patrolled by the Aviation Unit of the Lithuanian [State Border Guard Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Border_Guard_Service_\(Lithuania\)) which, since the 2000s, uses helicopters [EC-120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EC-120), [EC-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC135) and [EC-145](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EC-145).

### Navy

Main article: [Lithuanian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Navy)



Lithuanian Naval Forces *Flyvefisken*-class ship *Sėlis*

The Navy consists of the Warship Flotilla, the Sea Coastal Surveillance System, the Naval Logistic Service, Training Center and Maritime Rescue Coordination Center. The flotilla is the core component of the Navy and consists of the Mine Countermeasures Squadron, the Patrol Ships Squadron, and the Harbour Boats Group.[^cite_note-43] The current [Commander in Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Chief) of the Lithuanian Navy is Flotilla Admiral Giedrius Premeneckas.&#x20;



Marine fusiliers Boarding team

The Naval base and Headquarters are located in the city of [Klaipėda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaip%C4%97da).[^cite_note-44] The Navy uses patrol ships for coastal surveillance. The four newly acquired [*Flyvefisken*-class patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyvefisken-class_patrol_vessel) replaced the older [*Storm*-class patrol boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm-class_patrol_boat) and [Grisha-class corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grisha-class_corvette).

#### Marine fusiliers

From 1 August 2022, in order to eliminate the [coastal and port defense](25242026.md) gap and perform other functions assigned to the [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines), the Naval Forces Port and Coastal Defense Service was established and partially formed by merging the [Explosive Ordnance Disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_disposal) (EOD) Divers Team and the Naval Forces Logistics Service Security Company into one structure, which includes two infantry ([fusilier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusilier)) companies. In the process of forming the unit's traditions, the soldiers serving in the unit began to be called marine [fusiliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusilier) ([marine infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines)), and the service itself is unofficially called the Fusilier Battalion.[^cite_note-45]

In April 2025, the Navy announced that the marine unit would be officially renamed the General Kazimieras Nestoras Sapiega Fusiliers Battalion and would continue the traditions of the 7th Fusiliers Regiment.[^cite_note-46]

### Special Operations Forces

Main article: [Lithuanian Special Operations Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Special_Operations_Forces)



Lithuanian Special Forces squadron *[Aitvaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitvaras)* deployed in [Afghanistan](737.md).



[Special Purpose Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Special_Operations_Forces#Structure_and_tasks) (YPT) operator during joint [counter-terrorism](83392.md) exercise with [ARAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARAS_\(Lithuania\)).&#x20;

The Lithuanian Special Operations Forces of Lithuanian Armed Forces have been in operation *de facto* since 2002 and it were established *de jure* on 3 April 2008, when amendments of National Defence System organisation and military service law came into force.[^cite_note-47]

The Special Operations Forces are responsible for [special reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_reconnaissance), [direct actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_actions), and military support. They have also in charge of other tasks, e.g., protection of VIPs in peacetime. They are formed by the Special Purpose Service, Vytautas the Great Jaeger Battalion and Combat Divers Service. The Special Operations Forces organization is flexible, which makes it easy to form squadrons intended for specific operations and missions. The Special Operations Forces can be called upon inside the territory of Lithuania when [law enforcement agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency) lack or do not have the necessary capabilities to react to terrorist attacks.[^cite_note-SF-48]

The Special Operations Forces Squadron "Aitvaras" was deployed to [Afghanistan](737.md) for [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom). From 2005 to 2006 its squadrons were on standby as part of the [NATO Response Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Response_Force).[^cite_note-SF-48]

## International cooperation



Lithuanian [Bandvagn 206](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandvagn_206) helping an [Afghan National Police](13327465.md) vehicle in the snow

Lithuania has been a member of [NATO](21133.md) [military alliance](250766.md) since 2004. In the [European Union](9317.md), Lithuanian Armed Forces have also taken part in the [Nordic Battle Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Battle_Group) since 2008. ### NATO membership

See also: [Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing) and [NATO Enhanced Forward Presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Enhanced_Forward_Presence)



Lithuanian Air Force [L-39ZA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-39ZA) with two [Royal Netherlands Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Air_Force) [F-16AM Fighting Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16AM_Fighting_Falcon)

Soon after the restoration of independence, Lithuania [applied for NATO membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO) in January 1994. Together with another six Central and Eastern European countries, Lithuania was invited to join the North Atlantic Treaty Organization in the [2002 Prague summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Prague_summit) and became a member of the Alliance in March 2004. Lithuania entered [NATO](21133.md) on full-fledged rights immediately after the procedures of joining the [North Atlantic Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty) were completed and acquired rights to participate in the political decision-making process of the Alliance. Integration into the military structures of NATO became a long-term task of the Lithuanian Armed Forces. [Mechanised Infantry Brigade "Iron Wolf"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Wolf_Mechanised_Infantry_Brigade) was affiliated to the [Danish Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Division) based on agreements signed by [Denmark](76972.md) and Lithuania in August 2006. Lithuanian Armed Forces started to boost the Brigade's ability to cooperate with the forces of other NATO members.

#### NATO operations and deployments in Baltics and Lithuania

[Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing) was established by NATO allies since Lithuania and the other [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) do not have capabilities to secure their airspace. Fighter jets of NATO members are permanently deployed in [Zokniai airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zokniai_airport) near the city [Šiauliai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai) to provide cover for the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) airspace.[^cite_note-49] In 2013, NATO Energy Security [Centre of Excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Centres_of_Excellence) was established in [Vilnius](32597.md).

Following the [2016 Warsaw summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Warsaw_summit), [NATO Enhanced Forward Presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Enhanced_Forward_Presence) was deployed in the Baltic States with the multinational battalion battle group in Lithuania being [led by Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Enhanced_Forward_Presence#Lithuanian_multinational_battalion_battle_group).

### Cooperation between the Baltic States



Lithuanian soldiers with their [NATO](21133.md) allies during Iron Sword 2014.

Lithuania also cooperates with the two other Baltic states – [Latvia](17514.md) and [Estonia](28222445.md) in several trilateral Baltic defence co-operation initiatives:

- Baltic Battalion *(BALTBAT)* – infantry battalion for participation in international peace support operations, headquartered near [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), Latvia;

- [Baltic Naval Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Naval_Squadron) *(BALTRON)* – naval force with mine countermeasures capabilities, headquartered near [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn), Estonia;

- [Baltic Air Surveillance Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Surveillance_Network) *(BALTNET)* – air surveillance information system, headquartered near [Kaunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas), Lithuania;

- Joint military educational institutions: [Baltic Defence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Defence_College) *(BALTDEFCOL)* in [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu), Estonia, Baltic Diving Training Centre in [Liepāja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liep%C4%81ja), Latvia and Baltic Naval Communications Training Centre in [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn), Estonia.

  [^cite_note-50]

- [Baltic Defense Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Defense_Line) – defensive fortication line along the borders of Baltic countries and Russia/Belarus.

In January 2011, the Baltic states were invited to join [Nordic Defence Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Defence_Cooperation), the defence framework of the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries).[^cite_note-51] In November 2012, the three countries agreed to create a joint military staff in 2013.[^cite_note-52] Future co-operation will include sharing of national infrastructures for training purposes and specialisation of training areas *(BALTTRAIN)* and collective formation of battalion-sized contingents for use in the [NATO rapid-response force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Response_Force).[^cite_note-53]

### Foreign missions and operations

Lithuanian soldiers have taken part in international operations since 1993. From the summer of 2005 until 2014, [Lithuania](17675.md) has been part of the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) in Afghanistan (ISAF), leading a [Provincial Reconstruction Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Reconstruction_Team) (PRT) in the town of [Chaghcharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaghcharan) in the province of [Ghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghor). The PRT included personnel from [Denmark](76972.md), [Iceland](14531.md) and the US. There have also been special operation forces units in Afghanistan. They were placed in [Kandahar](17260.md) province.

Since joining international operations in 1993, Lithuania has lost two soldiers. 1st Lt. Normundas Valteris fell in [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia) (17 April 1996), Sgt. Arūnas Jarmalavičius in Afghanistan (22 May 2008).[^cite_note-54]

## Military industry

After the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, Lithuania has sought to develop its military industrial base.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

Lithuania has an ammunition producer, AB Giraitė Ammunition Plant (small caliber ammunition).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Lithuania also has a state-owned explosives manufacturer, Detonas, that is aiming to start producing military explosives (RDX, PETN, HMX).[^cite_note-60]

Lithuania has made a contract with the company [Rheinmetall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinmetall) about establishing an artillery ammunition and artillery charge (gunpowder) production plant in Lithuania. The building of the plant started in November 2025.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] Also a conctract with [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman) and [Nammo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammo) for production of medium-calibre (30mm) ammunition in Lithuania has been signed.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] Also new armored fighting vehicle assembly and maintenance facilities have been set up.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Lithuania has also developed its drone industry.[^cite_note-70]
