**Bethlehem**[^cite_note-3] is a city in the [West Bank](33209.md), Palestine, located about ten kilometres (six miles) south of [Jerusalem](16043.md), and the capital of the [Bethlehem Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Governorate). It had a population of 28,591 people, as of 2017.[^cite_note-palestinepopulations-1] The city's economy is strongly linked to [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_State_of_Palestine), especially during the [Christmas](6237.md) period, when [Christians](18337522.md) embark on a pilgrimage to the [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity), which is revered as the location of the [birth of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_of_Jesus).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-BH-5]

A possible first mention of Bethlehem is in the [Amarna correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letters) of [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), dated to 1350–1330 BCE, although that reading is uncertain. In the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible), the period of the [Israelites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites) is described; it identifies Bethlehem as the birthplace of [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David).[^cite_note-6] In the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament), the city is identified as the birthplace of [Jesus of Nazareth](1095706.md). Under the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the city of Bethlehem was destroyed by [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian), but later rebuilt by [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great), who commissioned the Church of the Nativity in 327 CE. In 529, the Church of the Nativity was heavily damaged by [Samaritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans) involved in the [Samaritan revolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan_revolts); following the victory of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), it was rebuilt by [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I).

Later, during the rule of several [Caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphates), Bethlehem became part of [Jund Filastin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jund_Filastin) in 637. [Muslims](19541.md) continued to rule the city until 1099, when it was conquered by the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades), who replaced the local Christian [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church) clergy with [Catholic](606848.md) ones. In the mid-13th century, Bethlehem's walls were demolished by the [Mamluk Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate). However, they were rebuilt by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in the 16th century when it came to control the region.[^cite_note-BMH-7] After the dissolution of the Ottoman Empire at the end of [World War I](4764461.md), Bethlehem was part of [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) until 1948, and later of the West Bank that was [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) by [Jordan](7515964.md) following the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War). During the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md), Bethlehem was occupied by Israel along with the rest of the West Bank. Since the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) between Israel and the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority), Bethlehem has been designated as part of [Area A of the West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves), nominally rendering it as being under [Palestinian control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_administered_by_the_Palestinian_Authority),[^cite_note-BMH-7] but it remains under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md). Movement around the city is limited due to the [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier).

Historically, it was a city of [Arab Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christians), who made up about 86% of the population in 1950, although in 2022, *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* reported that this community had dwindled about 20%, with a large majority of [Arab Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims).[^cite_note-8]

## Etymology

The current name for Bethlehem in local languages is [*_Bayt Laḥm_*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/58/ArBethlehem.ogg/ArBethlehem.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> /Bēt laḥm/ in Arabic ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بيت لحم), literally meaning "house of meat", and *_Bet Leḥem_* in Hebrew ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): בֵּית לֶחֶם), literally "house of bread" or "house of food".[^cite_note-CIIP-9][^cite_note-10] The city was called in [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Βηθλεέμ Ancient Greek pronunciation: [\[bɛːtʰle.ém\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek) and in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Bethleem*.[^cite_note-Losch-11] In Aramaic, the name of Bethlehem was simply the Hebrew name בית לחם, and was pronounced as Beit Lekhem. Evidence for this spelling can be inferred based on the fact that the spelling ܒܝܬܠܚܡ can be found in the Syriac Aramaic version of the bible in Matthew 2[^cite_note-SyrBib-12] as well as other parts of the book. The letters ܒܝܬܠܚܡ transliterate to ביתלחם. [Amarna letter EA290](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letter_EA290?action=edit\&redlink=1) makes reference to a town bīt-ninurta which has been read as Bit-Lachmi by scholar [W. F. Albright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._F._Albright), following a proposal by Otto Schroeder in 1815 and making it a potential first historical reference to Bethlehem. This reading is, however, uncertain and has met with objections.[^cite_note-13]

Canaanite and Israelite toponyms starting with *beth* are interpreted to mean "house of", with 'house' understood as 'temple' and the second part of the name indicating the deity the local temple was dedicated to.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Mum-15] Accordingly, one longstanding suggestion in scholarship is that the name Bethlehem derives from the [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_myths) or [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanite_religion) fertility god [Laḫmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahmu) and his consort sister [Lahamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahamu),[^cite_note-16] *lahmo* being the [Chaldean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic) word for "fertility".[^cite_note-Losch-11][^cite_note-Mum-15] Biblical scholar [William F. Albright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Albright) believed that this hypothesis, first put forth by Otto Schröder, was "certainly accurate".[^cite_note-18] Albright noted that the pronunciation of the name had remained essentially the same for 3,500 years, even if the perceived meaning had shifted over time: "'Temple of the God Lakhmu' in Canaanite, 'House of Bread' in Hebrew and [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic_language), 'House of Meat' in Arabic."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlbright1936-17] While Schröder's theory is not widely accepted,[^cite_note-CIIP-9] it continues to find favour in academic literature over the later literal translations.[^cite_note-19]

Another suggestion is an association with the root *l-h-m* "to fight", leading to the meaning of "house of war" or "house of fighting", but this is thought unlikely.[^cite_note-CIIP-9]

## History

### Canaanite period

The earliest reference to Bethlehem appears in the [Amarna correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letters) (c. 1400 BCE). In one of his six letters to Pharaoh, [Abdi-Heba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdi-Heba), the Egyptian-appointed governor of Jerusalem, appeals for aid in retaking *Bit-Laḫmi* in the wake of disturbances by [Apiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BFApiru) mercenaries:[^cite_note-OX-20] "Now even a town near Jerusalem, Bit-Lahmi by name, a village which once belonged to the king, has fallen to the enemy... Let the king hear the words of your servant Abdi-Heba, and send archers to restore the imperial lands of the king!"

It is thought that the similarity of this name to its modern forms indicates that it was originally a settlement of Canaanites who shared a Semitic cultural and linguistic heritage with the later arrivals.[^cite_note-IDHP4-21] *[Laḫmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahmu)* was the [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_mythology) god of fertility,[^cite_note-22] worshipped by the [Canaanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan#In_Jewish_and_Christian_scriptures) as *Leḥem*. Some time in the third millennium BCE, Canaanites erected a temple on the hill now known as the Hill of the Nativity, probably dedicated to [Laḫmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahmu). The temple, and subsequently the town that formed around it, was then known as *Beit Lahama*, "House (Temple) of Lahmu". By [1200 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1200s_BC_\(decade\)), the area of Bethlehem, as well as much of the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), was conquered by the [Philistines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philistines), which led the region to be known to the Greeks as "*Philistia*", later corrupted to "[Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\))".[^cite_note-Loschp51-23]

A burial ground discovered in spring 2013, and surveyed in 2015 by a joint Italian–Palestinian team found that the necropolis covered 3 hectares (more than 7 acres) and originally contained more than 100 tombs in use between roughly 2200 BCE and 650 BCE. The archaeologists were able to identify at least 30 tombs.[^cite_note-24]

### Israelite and Judean period

Archaeological confirmation of Bethlehem as a city in the [Kingdom of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah) was uncovered in 2012 at the archaeological dig at the [City of David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_David_\(historic\)) in the form of a *[bulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulla_\(seal\))* (seal impression in dried clay) in [ancient Hebrew script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Hebrew_alphabet) that reads "From the town of Bethlehem to the King". According to the excavators, it was used to seal the string closing a shipment of grain, wine, or other goods sent as a tax payment in the 8th or 7th century BCE.[^cite_note-25]



David, pouring out water drawn from the well of Bethlehem in this 1860 woodcut by [Julius Schnorr von Karolsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Schnorr_von_Karolsfeld), which illustrates [2 Samuel 23\:15–17](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible%20\(King%20James\)/2%20Samuel#23\:15)

[Biblical scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_studies) believe Bethlehem, located in the "hill country" of [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea), may be the same as the Biblical [Ephrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephrath). The [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament) describes it as the "City of David".[^cite_note-qjukjz-26] It is first mentioned in the Bible as the place where the matriarch [Rachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel) died and was buried. [Rachel's Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel's_Tomb), the traditional grave site, stands at the entrance to Bethlehem. According to the [Book of Ruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Ruth), the valley to the east is where [Ruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_\(biblical_figure\)) of [Moab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moab) gleaned the fields and returned to town with [Naomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_\(biblical_figure\)). In the [Books of Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books_of_Samuel), Bethlehem is mentioned as the home of [Jesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_\(biblical_figure\)),[^cite_note-27] father of King [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David) of [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Israel_\(united_monarchy\)), and the site of David's [anointment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anointing) by the prophet [Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel).[^cite_note-28]

Writing in the 4th century, the [Pilgrim of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itinerarium_Burdigalense) reported that the sepulchers of David, [Ezekiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel), [Asaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asaph_\(biblical_figure\)), [Job](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_\(biblical_figure\)), [Jesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_\(biblical_figure\)), and [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon) were located near Bethlehem.[^cite_note-29]

According to the [Gospels of Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew) and [Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke), [Jesus](1095706.md) was born in Bethlehem.

The [Gospel of Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew)[^cite_note-33] and the [Gospel of Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke)[^cite_note-34] represent Jesus as having been born in Bethlehem.[^cite_note-Brownrigg-30][^cite_note-Sanders1993-31][^cite_note-Casey2010-32] Scholars regard the two accounts as contradictory, as it was common for ancient biographers and historians to display differences in reporting with the gospel accounts being typical of ancient historical biographies.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-Sanders1993-31][^cite_note-Casey2010-32] James Barker suggests that Luke intended to supplement Matthew’s nativity by adding Mary’s perspective to Matthew’s focus on Joseph.[^cite_note-\:5-36] The [Gospel of Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark), the earliest gospel, mentions nothing about Jesus having been born in Bethlehem, saying only that he came from [Nazareth](21702.md).[^cite_note-Casey2010-32] Current scholars are divided on the actual birthplace of Jesus: some believe he was actually born in Nazareth,[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] while others still hold that he was born in Bethlehem.[^cite_note-40]

Nonetheless, the tradition that Jesus was born in Bethlehem was prominent in the early church.[^cite_note-Brownrigg-30] Around 155, the apologist [Justin Martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Martyr) recommended that those who doubted Jesus was really born in Bethlehem could go there and visit the very cave where he was supposed to have been born.[^cite_note-Brownrigg-30] The same cave is also referenced by the apocryphal [Gospel of James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_James) and the fourth-century church historian [Eusebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius).[^cite_note-Brownrigg-30] After the [Bar Kokhba revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Kokhba_revolt) (c. 132–136 CE) was crushed, the Roman emperor [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian) converted the Christian site above the Grotto into a shrine dedicated to the Greek god [Adonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adonis), to honour his favourite, the Greek youth [Antinous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinous).[^cite_note-Giuseppe_Ricciotti_1948_p._276-41][^cite_note-42]

Around 395 CE, [Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome) wrote in a letter: "Bethlehem... belonging now to us... was overshadowed by a grove of [Tammuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumuzid), that is to say, Adonis, and in the cave where once the infant Christ cried, the lover of [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_\(mythology\)) was lamented."[^cite_note-Taylor1993-43] Many scholars have taken this letter as evidence that the cave of the nativity over which the [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity) was later built had at one point been a shrine to the ancient Near Eastern fertility god Tammuz.[^cite_note-Taylor1993-43][^cite_note-44] Eusebius, however, mentions nothing about the cave having been associated with Tammuz[^cite_note-Taylor1993-43] and there are no other Patristic sources that suggest Tammuz had a shrine in Bethlehem.[^cite_note-Taylor1993-43] Peter Welten has argued that the cave was never dedicated to Tammuz[^cite_note-Taylor1993-43] and that Jerome misinterpreted Christian mourning over the [Massacre of the Innocents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_the_Innocents) as a pagan ritual over Tammuz's death.[^cite_note-Taylor1993-43] Joan E. Taylor has countered this contention by arguing that Jerome, as an educated man, could not have been so naïve as to mistake Christian mourning over the Massacre of the Innocents as a pagan ritual for Tammuz.[^cite_note-Taylor1993-43]

In 326–328, the empress [Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_\(empress\)), widowed [consort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_consort) of Emperor [Constantius Chlorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantius_Chlorus) and mother of the ruling emperor, [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great), made a pilgrimage to Syria-Palaestina, in the course of which she visited the ruins of Bethlehem.[^cite_note-BMH-7][^cite_note-Brownrigg-30] The [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity) was built at her initiative over the cave where Jesus was purported to have been born.[^cite_note-Brownrigg-30] During the [Samaritan revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan_Revolts) of 529, Bethlehem was sacked and its walls and the Church of the Nativity destroyed; they were rebuilt on the orders of the Emperor [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I).[^cite_note-BMH-7][^cite_note-Brownrigg-30] In 614, the [Persian Sassanid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire), supported by [Jewish rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_revolt_against_Heraclius), invaded [Palestina Prima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Prima) and captured Bethlehem.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlein2018234-45] A story recounted in later sources holds that they refrained from destroying the church on seeing the [magi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magi) depicted in [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_art) clothing in a mosaic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERussell1991523–528-46][^cite_note-BMH-7]

### Middle Ages



A 1698 sketch by [Cornelis de Bruijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_de_Bruijn)

In 637, shortly after [Jerusalem was captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_\(637\)) by the [Muslim armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_army), ['Umar ibn al-Khattāb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar), the second caliph, promised that the Church of the Nativity would be preserved for Christian use.[^cite_note-BMH-7] A [mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_of_Omar_\(Bethlehem\)) dedicated to Umar was built upon the place in the city where he prayed, next to the church.[^cite_note-ATT-47] Bethlehem then passed through the control of the Islamic caliphates of the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) in the 8th century, then the [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasids) in the 9th century. A Persian geographer recorded in the mid-9th century that a well preserved and much venerated church existed in the town. In 985, the Arab geographer [al-Muqaddasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Muqaddasi) visited Bethlehem, and referred to its church as the "Basilica of Constantine, the equal of which does not exist anywhere in the country-round."[^cite_note-48] In 1009, during the reign of the sixth Fatimid Caliph, [al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hakim_bi-Amr_Allah), the Church of the Nativity was ordered to be demolished, but was spared by local Muslims, because they had been permitted to worship in the structure's southern transept.[^cite_note-49]

In 1099, Bethlehem was captured by the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade), who fortified it and built a new monastery and cloister on the north side of the Church of the Nativity. The [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church) clergy were removed from their [sees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see) and replaced with [Latin](606848.md) clerics. Up until that point the official Christian presence in the region was Greek Orthodox. On Christmas Day 1100, [Baldwin I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_I_of_Jerusalem), first king of the Frankish [Kingdom of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem), was crowned in Bethlehem, and that year a Latin episcopate was also established in the town.[^cite_note-BMH-7]

In 1187, [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin), the Sultan of [Egypt](8087628.md) and [Syria](7515849.md) who led the [Muslim Ayyubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty), captured Bethlehem from the Crusaders. The Latin clerics were forced to leave, allowing the Greek Orthodox clergy to return. Saladin agreed to the return of two Latin priests and two deacons in 1192. However, Bethlehem suffered from the loss of the pilgrim trade, as there was a sharp decrease of European pilgrims.[^cite_note-BMH-7] [William IV, Count of Nevers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_IV,_Count_of_Nevers) had promised the Christian bishops of Bethlehem that if Bethlehem should fall under Muslim control, he would welcome them in the small town of [Clamecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clamecy,_Ni%C3%A8vre) in present-day [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundy), France. As a result, the Bishop of Bethlehem duly took up residence in the hospital of Panthenor, Clamecy, in 1223. Clamecy remained the continuous '[in partibus infidelium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see)' seat of the Bishopric of Bethlehem for almost 600 years, until the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) in 1789.[^cite_note-50]

Bethlehem, along with Jerusalem, [Nazareth](21702.md), and [Sidon](28366.md), was briefly ceded to the Crusader [Kingdom of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) by a treaty between [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and Ayyubid Sultan [al-Kamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kamil) in 1229, in return for a ten-year truce between the Ayyubids and the Crusaders. The treaty expired in 1239, and Bethlehem was recaptured by the Muslims in 1244.[^cite_note-51] In 1250, with the coming to power of the [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) under [Rukn al-Din Baibars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baibars), tolerance of Christianity declined. Members of the clergy left the city, and in 1263 the town walls were demolished. The Latin clergy returned to Bethlehem the following century, establishing themselves in the monastery adjoining the Basilica of the Nativity. The Greek Orthodox were given control of the basilica and shared control of the Milk Grotto with the Latins and the [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church).[^cite_note-BMH-7]

### Ottoman era



A painting of Bethlehem by [Vasily Polenov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Polenov), 1882



View of Bethlehem, Christmas Day 1898

From 1517, during the years of [Ottoman](22278.md) control, custody of the Basilica was bitterly disputed between the Catholic and Greek [Orthodox churches](10186.md).[^cite_note-BMH-7] By the end of the 16th century, Bethlehem had become one of the largest villages in the District of Jerusalem, and was subdivided into seven quarters.[^cite_note-Singer-52] The Basbus family served as the heads of Bethlehem among other leaders during this period.[^cite_note-53] The Ottoman tax record and census from 1596 indicates that Bethlehem had a population of 1,435, making it the 13th largest village in Palestine at the time. Its total revenue amounted to 30,000 [akce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-Petersen141-54]

Bethlehem paid taxes on wheat, barley and grapes. The Muslims and Christians were organized into separate communities, each having its own leader. Five leaders represented the village in the mid-16th century, three of whom were Muslims. Ottoman tax records suggest that the Christian population was slightly more prosperous or grew more grain than grapes (the former being a more valuable commodity).[^cite_note-55]



Bethlehem, from an 1810 illustration by [Luigi Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Mayer)

From 1831 to 1841, Palestine was under the rule of the [Muhammad Ali Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_dynasty) of [Egypt](8087628.md). During this period, the town suffered [an earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee_earthquake_of_1837) as well as the destruction of the Muslim quarter in 1834 by Egyptian troops, apparently as a reprisal for the murder of a favored loyalist of [Ibrahim Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Pasha_of_Egypt), during the [Peasants' revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasants'_revolt_in_Palestine).[^cite_note-56] In 1841, Bethlehem came under Ottoman rule once again and remained so until the end of World War I. Under the Ottomans, Bethlehem's inhabitants faced unemployment, [compulsory military service](5735.md), and heavy taxes, resulting in mass emigration, particularly to [South America](26769.md).[^cite_note-BMH-7] An American missionary in the 1850s reported a population of under 4,000, nearly all of whom belonged to the Greek Church. He also noted that a lack of water limited the town's growth.[^cite_note-57]

[Socin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Socin) found from an official Ottoman village list from about 1870 that Bethlehem had a population of 179 Muslims in 59 houses, 979 "Latins" in 256 houses, 824 "Greeks" in 213 houses, and 41 Armenians in 11 houses, a total of 539 houses. The population count only included men.[^cite_note-58] [Hartmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Hartmann) found that Bethlehem had 520 houses.[^cite_note-59]

### Modern era



Bethlehem 1937



2018 [United Nations](1602284.md) map of the area, showing the [Israeli occupation](59076253.md) arrangements.



1927 stamp from the [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) period, showcasing [Rachel's Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel's_Tomb) (or Bilal bin Rabah Mosque) in Bethlehem

Bethlehem was part of [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) from 1920 to 1948.[^cite_note-60] In the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md)'s [1947 resolution to partition Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine), Bethlehem was included in the [international enclave of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_separatum_\(Jerusalem\)) to be administered by the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-61] [Jordan](7515964.md) captured the city during the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War).[^cite_note-62] Many refugees from areas captured by [Israeli](9282173.md) forces in 1947–48 fled to the Bethlehem area, primarily settling in what became the official refugee camps of ['Azza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Azza) (Beit Jibrin) and ['Aida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aida_Camp) in the north and [Dheisheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dheisheh) in the south.[^cite_note-63] The influx of refugees significantly transformed Bethlehem's Christian majority into a Muslim one.[^cite_note-64]

Jordan retained control of the city until the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, when Bethlehem was captured by Israel, along with the rest of the [West Bank](33209.md). Following the Six-Day War, Israel took control of the city.

&#x20;\
&#x20;During the early months of [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), on 5 May 1989, Milad Anton Shahin, aged 12, was shot dead by [Israeli soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_army). Replying to a Member of [Knesset](74656.md) in August 1990 Defence Minister [Yitzak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzak_Rabin) stated that a group of reservists in an observation post had come under attack by stone throwers. The commander of the post, a senior non-commissioned officer, fired two [plastic bullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_bullets) in deviation of operational rules. No evidence was found that this caused the boy's death. The officer was found guilty of illegal use of a weapon and sentenced to 5 months imprisonment, two of them actually in prison doing public service. He was also demoted.[^cite_note-65]

On December 21, 1995, Israeli troops withdrew from Bethlehem,[^cite_note-66] and three days later the city came under the administration and military control of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) in accordance with the [Interim Agreement on the West Bank and the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord).[^cite_note-67] During the [Second Palestinian Intifada](71717.md) in 2000–2005, Bethlehem's infrastructure and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) industry were damaged.[^cite_note-OCHA-68][^cite_note-69] In 2002, it was a primary combat zone in [Operation Defensive Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Defensive_Shield), a major military counteroffensive by the [Israeli Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF).[^cite_note-BBC-70] The IDF [besieged the Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_the_Church_of_the_Nativity_in_Bethlehem), where dozens of Palestinian militants had sought refuge. The siege lasted 39 days. Several militants were killed. It ended with an agreement to exile 13 of the militants to foreign countries.[^cite_note-siege_timeline-71]

&#x20;Today, the city is surrounded by two bypass roads for [Israeli settlers](15123.md), leaving the inhabitants squeezed between thirty-seven Jewish enclaves, where a quarter of all West Bank settlers, roughly 170,000, live; the gap between the two roads is closed by the 8-metre high [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier), which cuts Bethlehem off from its sister city Jerusalem.[^cite_note-72]&#x20;



[The Walled Off Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walled_Off_Hotel), owned and decorated by [Banksy](396241.md)

Christian families that have lived in Bethlehem for hundreds of years are being forced to leave as land in Bethlehem is seized, and homes bulldozed, for construction of thousands of new Israeli homes.[^cite_note-Plp-73] Land seizures for Israeli settlements have also prevented construction of a new hospital for the inhabitants of Bethlehem, as well as the barrier separating dozens of Palestinian families from their farmland and Christian communities from their places of worship.[^cite_note-Plp-73] Christians have reportedly suffered persecution under the Palestinian Authority, leading to emigration.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

## Geography



Residence of the [Congregation of the Sacred Heart of Jesus of Betharram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus_of_Betharram), 2008

Bethlehem is located at an elevation of about 775 meters (2,543 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metres_above_sea_level), 30 meters (98 ft) higher than nearby [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-76] Bethlehem is situated on the [Judean Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaean_Mountains).

The city is located 73 kilometers (45 mi) northeast of [Gaza City](55387.md) and the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), 75 kilometers (47 mi) west of [Amman](45007.md), Jordan, 59 kilometers (37 mi) southeast of [Tel Aviv](31453.md), Israel and 10 kilometers (6 mi) south of Jerusalem.[^cite_note-77] Nearby cities and towns include [Beit Safafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Safafa) and Jerusalem to the north, [Beit Jala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Jala) to the northwest, [Husan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husan) to the west, [al-Khadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khader) and [Artas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artas,_Bethlehem) to the southwest, and [Beit Sahour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Sahour) to the east. Beit Jala and the latter form an [agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomeration) with Bethlehem. The [Aida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aida_Camp) and [Azza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Azza) refugee camps are located within the city limits.[^cite_note-78]

In the center of Bethlehem is its old city. The old city consists of eight quarters, laid out in a mosaic style, forming the area around the Manger Square. The quarters include the Christian an-Najajreh, al-Farahiyeh, al-Anatreh, al-Tarajmeh, al-Qawawsa and Hreizat quarters and al-Fawaghreh—the only Muslim quarter.[^cite_note-BPS-79] Most of the Christian quarters are named after the Arab [Ghassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghassanids) clans that settled there.[^cite_note-80] Al-Qawawsa Quarter was formed by [Arab Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christians) emigrants from the nearby town of [Tuqu'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuqu') in the 18th century.[^cite_note-Zeiter-81] There is also a [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) quarter outside of the old city,[^cite_note-BPS-79] whose inhabitants originate from [Midyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midyat) and [Ma'asarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96merli,_Mardin) in [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-82] The total population of the old city is about 5,000.[^cite_note-BPS-79]

### Climate

Bethlehem has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*), with hot and dry summers and mild, wetter winters. Winter temperatures (mid-December to mid-March) can be cool and rainy. January is the coldest month, with temperatures ranging from 1 to 13 degree Celsius (33–55 °F). From May through September, the weather is warm and sunny. August is the hottest month, with a high of 30 degrees Celsius (86 °F). Bethlehem receives an average of 700 millimeters (28 in) of rainfall annually, 70% between November and January.[^cite_note-BMC-83]

Bethlehem's average annual relative [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity) is 60% and reaches its highest rates between January and February. Humidity levels are at their lowest in May. Night dew may occur in up to 180 days per year. The city is influenced by the Mediterranean Sea breeze that occurs around mid-day. However, Bethlehem is affected also by annual waves of hot, dry, sandy and dust *Khamaseen* winds from the [Arabian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Desert), during April, May and mid-June.[^cite_note-BMC-83]

[Mosque of Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_of_Omar_\(Bethlehem\)), the [Evangelical Lutheran Christmas Church](23371382.md), and the Salesian Church of the [Sacred Heart of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus)

According to [Ottoman tax records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter), Christians made up roughly 60% of the population in the early 16th century, while the Christian and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) populations became equal by the middle of that century. However, there were no Muslim inhabitants counted by the end of the century, with a recorded population of 287 adult male taxpayers. Christians, like all non-Muslims throughout the Ottoman Empire, were required to pay the [jizya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizya) tax.[^cite_note-Singer-52] In 1867, an American visitor describes the town as having a population of 3,000 to 4,000, of whom about 100 were [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), 300 were Muslim and "the remainder belonging to the Latin and Greek Churches with a few Armenians."[^cite_note-Miller-86] Another report from the same year puts the Christian population at 3,000, with an additional 50 Muslims.[^cite_note-Malet1868-92] An 1885 source put the population at approximately 6,000 of "principally Christians, Latins and Greeks" with no Jewish inhabitants.[^cite_note-JewishIntelligenceVol1-93]

The [census of 1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) lists Bethlehem as having 6,658 residents (5,838 Christians, 818 Muslims, and two Jews),[^cite_note-94] increasing in [1931](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to 6,804 (5,588 Christians, 1,219 Muslims, five with no religion, and two Jews) with 506 in nearby suburbs (251 Muslims, 216 Christians, and 39 Jews).[^cite_note-95]

The 1938 village statistics list the population as 7,520 with 499 in nearby suburbs (including 42 Jews).[^cite_note-96] The [1945 village statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) list Bethlehem's population as 8,820 (6,430 Christians, 2,370 Muslims, and 20 "other").[^cite_note-97]

In 1948, the religious makeup of the city was 85% Christian, mostly of the Greek Orthodox and Roman Catholic denominations, and 13% Muslim.[^cite_note-AP-98] In the 1967 census taken by Israel authorities, the town of Bethlehem proper numbered 14,439 inhabitants, its 7,790 Muslim inhabitants represented 53.9% of the population, while the Christians of various denominations numbered 6,231 or 46.1%.[^cite_note-99]

In the PCBS's 1997 census, the city had a population of 21,670, including a total of 6,570 [refugees](74681.md), accounting for 30.3% of the city's population.[^cite_note-PCBSCensus-90][^cite_note-100] In 1997, the age distribution of Bethlehem's inhabitants was 27.4% under the age of 10, 20% from 10 to 19, 17.3% from 20 to 29, 17.7% from 30 to 44, 12.1% from 45 to 64 and 5.3% above the age of 65. There were 11,079 males and 10,594 females.[^cite_note-PCBSCensus-90] In the 2007 PCBS census, Bethlehem had a population of 25,266, of which 12,753 were males and 12,513 were females. There were 6,709 housing units, of which 5,211 were households. The average household consisted of 4.8 family members.[^cite_note-PCBS07-101] By 2017, the population was 28,591.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-91]

### Christian population

See also: [Palestinian Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Christians)



Four Bethlehemi Christian women, 1911

After the [Muslim conquest of the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Levant) in the 630s, the local Christians were [Arabized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabization) even though large numbers were ethnically Arabs of the Ghassanid clans.[^cite_note-beth-102] Bethlehem's two largest Arab Christian clans trace their ancestry to the Ghassanids, including al-Farahiyyah and an-Najajreh.[^cite_note-beth-102] The former have descended from the Ghassanids who migrated from [Yemen](350939.md) and from the [Wadi Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Musa) area in present-day [Jordan](7515964.md) and an-Najajreh descend from [Najran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran).[^cite_note-beth-102] Another Bethlehem clan, al-Anatreh, also trace their ancestry to the Ghassanids.[^cite_note-beth-102]

The percentage of Christians in the town has been in a steady decline since the mid-twentieth century.[^cite_note-AP-98][^cite_note-Malek2017-103][^cite_note-Lidman2016-104][^cite_note-O'Connor2013-105] In 1947, Christians made up 85% of the population, but by 1998, the figure had declined to 40%.[^cite_note-AP-98][^cite_note-Malek2017-103] In 2005, the [mayor of Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bethlehem), [Victor Batarseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Batarseh), explained that "due to the stress, either physical or psychological, and the bad economic situation, many people are emigrating, either Christians or Muslims, but it is more apparent among Christians, because they already are a minority."[^cite_note-VOA-106] The Palestinian Authority is officially committed to equality for Christians, although according to Israel there have been incidents of violence against them by the [Preventive Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preventive_Security_Force) and militant factions.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

In 2006, a [Zogby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Zogby) poll that interviewed more than 1,000 Palestinian Christians from Bethlehem found that 79% of the respondents cited the [Israeli occupation](59076253.md) as source of difficulties leading the emigration of their community.[^cite_note-AJ1-109] In the same year, the Palestinian Centre for Research and Cultural Dialogue conducted a poll among the city's Christians according to which 90% said they had had Muslim friends, 73.3% agreed that the PNA treated Christian heritage in the city with respect and 78% attributed the exodus of Christians to the Israeli blockade.[^cite_note-110] The only mosque in the Old City is the [Mosque of Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_of_Omar_\(Bethlehem\)), located in the Manger Square.[^cite_note-ATT-47] By 2016, the Christian population of Bethlehem had declined to only 16%.[^cite_note-Lidman2016-104] The Christian population's proportion of Bethlehem fell from 87% in the 1950s to 12% in 2016.[^cite_note-111]

A study by [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) concluded that the decline in the Arab Christian population of the area was partially a result of a lower [birth rate](515898.md) among Christians than among Muslims,[^cite_note-Lidman2016-104][^cite_note-Connor-112] but also partially due to the fact that Christians were more likely to emigrate from the region than any other religious group.[^cite_note-Lidman2016-104][^cite_note-Connor-112] The seizure of Christian land by Muslim mafias and the bias of the Palestinian Judicial system have been cited as reasons leading to emigration.[^cite_note-113] Amon Ramnon, a researcher at the [Jerusalem Institute for Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Institute_for_Policy_Research), stated that the reason why more Christians were emigrating than Muslims is because it is easier for Arab Christians to integrate into western communities than for Arab Muslims, since many of them attend church-affiliated schools, where they are taught European languages.[^cite_note-Lidman2016-104] A higher percentage of Christians in the region are urban-dwellers, which also makes it easier for them to emigrate and assimilate into western populations.[^cite_note-Lidman2016-104] A [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics) of the Christian exodus cited lack of economic and educational opportunity, especially due to the Christians' [middle-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class) status and [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education).[^cite_note-114] Since the [Second Intifada](71717.md), 10% of the Christian population have left the city.[^cite_note-VOA-106] However, it is likely that there are many other factors, most of which are shared with the Palestinian population as a whole.[^cite_note-115]

## Economy



High-rise construction in Bethlehem



[Jacir Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacir_Palace)

Shopping is a major attraction, especially during the [Christmas](6237.md) season. The city's main streets and old markets are lined with shops selling [Palestinian handicrafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_handicrafts), [Middle Eastern](19323.md) spices, jewelry and oriental sweets such as [baklawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baklava).[^cite_note-116] Olive [wood carvings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_carving)[^cite_note-BethTour-117] are the item most purchased by tourists visiting Bethlehem.[^cite_note-handicrafts-118] Religious handicrafts include ornaments handmade from [mother-of-pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacre), as well as olive wood statues, boxes, and crosses.[^cite_note-BethTour-117] Other industries include stone and marble-cutting, textiles, furniture and furnishings.[^cite_note-ChamberInfo-119] Bethlehem factories also produce paints, plastics, [synthetic rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_rubber), pharmaceuticals, construction materials and food products, mainly pasta and confectionery.[^cite_note-ChamberInfo-119] [Cremisan Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremisan_Valley), founded in 1885, is a winery run by monks in the Monastery of Cremisan. The grapes are grown mainly in the [al-Khader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khader) district. In 2007, the monastery's wine production was around 700,000 liters per year.[^cite_note-120]

In 2008, Bethlehem hosted the largest [economic conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Investment_Conference) to date in the [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories). It was initiated by [Palestinian Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_Palestinian_National_Authority) and former [Finance Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Minister_of_the_Palestinian_National_Authority) [Salam Fayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salam_Fayyad) to convince more than a thousand businessmen, bankers and government officials from throughout the [Middle East](19323.md) to invest in the West Bank and [Gaza Strip](12047.md). A total of 1.4 billion US dollars was secured for business investments in the Palestinian territories.[^cite_note-121] Tourism is Bethlehem's main industry.[^cite_note-O'Connor2013-105][^cite_note-OCHA-68] Unlike other Palestinian localities prior to 2000, the majority of the employed residents did not have jobs in Israel.[^cite_note-OCHA-68] More than 20% of the working population is employed in the industry.[^cite_note-CityEconomy-122] Tourism accounts for approximately 65% of the city's economy and 11% of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority).[^cite_note-123] The city has more than two million visitors every year.[^cite_note-CityEconomy-122] Tourism in Bethlehem ground to a halt for over a decade after the [Second Intifada](71717.md),[^cite_note-O'Connor2013-105] but gradually began to pick back up in the early 2010s.[^cite_note-O'Connor2013-105] [Schneider Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schneider_Electric) operates a facility in the Multidisciplinary Industrial Park of Bethlehem, which was developed by the [Palestinian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_government) and [France](5843419.md).[^cite_note-124]

The [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity) is one of Bethlehem's major [tourist attractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_attraction) and a magnet for Christian pilgrims. It stands in the center of the city — a part of the [Manger Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manger_Square) — over a grotto or cave called the Holy Crypt, where Jesus is believed to have been born. Nearby is the Milk Grotto where the Holy Family took refuge on their Flight to Egypt and next door is the cave where [St. Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome) spent thirty years creating the [Vulgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulgate), the dominant Latin version of the Bible until the Reformation.[^cite_note-BMH-7] There are over thirty hotels in Bethlehem.[^cite_note-Patience-125] [Jacir Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacir_Palace), built in 1910 near the church, is one of Bethlehem's most successful hotels and its oldest. It was closed down in 2000 due to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, but reopened in 2005 as the Jacir Palace InterContinental at Bethlehem.[^cite_note-126] The hotel is managed by world renowned international brand — the [Intercontinental Hotel Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Hotel_Group) (IHG) and is second IHG hotel in the country after [IHG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Arches_Hotel) in [Jerusalem](16043.md).

## Religious significance and commemoration

### Birthplace of Jesus

Further information: [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity) and [Nativity of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus)



Silver star marking the place where Jesus was born according to Christian tradition



Altar of the Magi opposite the Holy Manger, Nativity Grotto



[Christmas tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree) in Bethlehem; behind it, the [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity), 2014

In the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament), the [Gospel of Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke) says that [Jesus](1095706.md)' parents traveled from [Nazareth](21702.md) to Bethlehem, where Jesus was born.[^cite_note-qjukjz-26] The [Gospel of Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew) mentions Bethlehem as the place of birth,[^cite_note-127] and adds that [Herod the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great) was told that a 'King of the Jews' had been born in the town, prompting the [Massacre of the Innocents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_the_Innocents). [Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph), warned of Herod's impending action by an [angel of the Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_of_the_Lord), decided to [flee to Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_into_Egypt) with his family, The family later settled in Nazareth after Herod's death.

Early Christian traditions describe [Jesus](1095706.md) as being born in Bethlehem: in one account, a verse in the [Book of Micah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Micah) is interpreted as a prophecy that the [Messiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messiah) would be born there.[^cite_note-128] The second-century [Christian apologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_apologetics) [Justin Martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Martyr) stated in his *[Dialogue with Trypho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue_with_Trypho)* (written c. 155–161) that the [Holy Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Family) had taken refuge in a cave outside of the town and then placed Jesus in a manger.[^cite_note-129] [Origen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origen) of Alexandria, writing around the year 247, referred to a cave in the town of Bethlehem which local people believed was the birthplace of Jesus.[^cite_note-130] This cave was possibly one which had previously been a site of the cult of [Tammuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammuz_\(deity\)).[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133] The [Gospel of Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark) and the Gospel of John do not include a nativity narrative, but refer to him only as being from Nazareth.[^cite_note-134] In a 2005 article in *[Archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_\(magazine\))* magazine, archaeologist Aviram Oshri points to an absence of evidence for the settlement of Bethlehem near Jerusalem at the time when Jesus was born, and postulates that Jesus was born in [Bethlehem of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_of_Galilee).[^cite_note-135] However, other archaeologists argue that there is evidence that Bethlehem of Judea was inhabited at that time.[^cite_note-136]<sup>: 6–10 </sup> In a 2011 article in *[Biblical Archaeology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Archaeology_Review)* magazine, [Jerome Murphy-O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Murphy-O'Connor) argues for the traditional position that Jesus was born in Bethlehem near Jerusalem.[^cite_note-137]

### Christmas celebrations



Christmas pilgrims, 1890

Christmas rites are held in Bethlehem on three different dates: December 25 is the traditional date by the Roman Catholic and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) denominations, but Greek, [Coptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria) and [Syrian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church) Christians celebrate Christmas on January 6 and [Armenian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) Christians on January 19. Most Christmas processions pass through Manger Square, the plaza outside the [Basilica of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity). Roman Catholic services take place in [St. Catherine's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Catherine,_Bethlehem) and Protestants often hold services at Shepherds' Fields.[^cite_note-138]

In 2023 and 2024, celebrations were cancelled due to the [Gaza war](74998519.md). They were restarted in 2025 after a [peace deal](81215079.md) was reached.[^cite_note-139]

### Other religious festivals

Bethlehem celebrates festivals related to saints and prophets associated with Palestinian folklore. One such festival is the annual [Feast of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George's_Day) ([al-Khadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khidr)) on May 5–6. During the celebrations, Greek Orthodox Christians from the city march in procession to the nearby town of [al-Khader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khader) to baptize newborns in the waters around the [Monastery of St. George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George's_Monastery,_Al-Khader) and sacrifice a sheep in ritual.[^cite_note-140] The Feast of St. [Elijah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah) is commemorated by a procession to Mar Elias, a Greek Orthodox monastery north of Bethlehem.

## Culture

### Embroidery

See also: [Palestinian costumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_costumes)



Woman in traditional Bethlehem costume

The women embroiderers of Bethlehem were known for their bridalwear.[^cite_note-PCA2-141] Bethlehem embroidery was renowned for its "strong overall effect of colors and metallic brilliance."[^cite_note-142] Less formal dresses were made of indigo fabric with a sleeveless coat (*bisht*) from locally woven wool worn over top. Dresses for special occasions were made of striped silk with winged sleeves with a short *taqsireh* jacket known as the Bethlehem jacket. The taqsireh was made of [velvet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet) or [broadcloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcloth), usually with heavy embroidery.[^cite_note-PCA2-141]

Bethlehem work was unique in its use of couched gold or silver cord, or silk cord onto the silk, wool, felt or velvet used for the garment, to create stylized floral patterns with free or rounded lines. This technique was used for "royal" [wedding dresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_dress) (*thob malak*), taqsirehs and the *shatwehs* worn by married women. It has been traced by some to [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium), and by others to the formal costumes of the Ottoman Empire's elite. As a Christian village, local women were also exposed to the detailing on church vestments with their heavy embroidery and silver brocade.[^cite_note-PCA2-141]

### Mother-of-pearl carving

Main article: [Mother-of-pearl carving in Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother-of-pearl_carving_in_Bethlehem)



Craftsmen working with [mother-of-pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother-of-Pearl_carving_in_Bethlehem), early 20th century

The art of mother-of-pearl carving is said to have been a Bethlehem tradition since the 15th century when it was introduced by [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) friars from [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-143] A constant stream of [pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim) generated a demand for these items, which also provided jobs for women.[^cite_note-144] The industry was noted by [Richard Pococke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Pococke), who visited Bethlehem in 1727.[^cite_note-145]

### Cultural centers and museums

Bethlehem is home to the [Palestinian Heritage Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Heritage_Center), established in 1991. The center aims to preserve and promote Palestinian embroidery, [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_art) and folklore.[^cite_note-146] The International Center of Bethlehem is another cultural center that concentrates primarily on the culture of Bethlehem. It provides language and guide training, woman's studies and [arts and crafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicraft) displays, and training.[^cite_note-BH-5]



Inside of the [Palestinian Heritage Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Heritage_Center)

The Bethlehem branch of [the Edward Said National Conservatory of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Edward_Said_National_Conservatory_of_Music) has about 500 students. Its primary goals are to teach children music, train teachers for other schools, sponsor music research, and the study of Palestinian folklore music.[^cite_note-147]

Bethlehem has several museums: The Crib of the Nativity Theatre and Museum offers visitors 31 three-dimensional models depicting the significant stages of the life of Jesus. Its theater presents a 20-minute animated show. The [Badd Giacaman Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badd_Giacaman_Museum), located in the Old City of Bethlehem, dates back to the 18th century and is primarily dedicated to the history and process of [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) production.[^cite_note-BH-5] [Baituna al-Talhami Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baituna_al-Talhami_Museum), established in 1972, contains displays of Bethlehem culture.[^cite_note-BH-5] The International Museum of Nativity was built by [United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization](21786641.md) (UNESCO) to exhibit "high artistic quality in an evocative atmosphere".[^cite_note-BH-5] The [Palestine Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Museum_of_Natural_History) is the first of its kind and is based on Bethlehem University campus.[^cite_note-148]

## Local government

Bethlehem is the *muhfaza* (seat) or district capital of the [Bethlehem Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Governorate).

Bethlehem held its first municipal elections in 1876, after the *mukhtars* ("heads") of the quarters of Bethlehem's Old City (excluding the [Syriac Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Quarter?action=edit\&redlink=1)) made the decision to elect a local council of seven members to represent each clan in the town. A [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_law) was established so that if the victor for mayor was a [Catholic](606848.md), his deputy should be of the [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church) community.[^cite_note-BME-149]



[Bethlehem Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Governorate) building

Throughout, Bethlehem's rule by the British and Jordan, the Syriac Quarter was allowed to participate in the election, as were the Ta'amrah [Bedouins](61770.md) and Palestinian refugees, hence ratifying the number of municipal members in the council to 11. In 1976, an amendment was passed to allow women to vote and become council members and later the voting age was increased from 21 to 25.[^cite_note-BME-149]

There are several branches of political parties on the council, including [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), [Islamist](15012.md), and secular. The leftist factions of the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) such as the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) (PFLP) and the [Palestinian People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_People's_Party) (PPP) usually dominate the reserved seats. [Hamas](13913.md) gained the majority of the open seats in the 2005 [Palestinian municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_local_elections,_2004%E2%80%932005).[^cite_note-150]

## Education

According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS), in 1997, approximately 84% of Bethlehem's population over the age of 10 was literate. Of the city's population, 10,414 were enrolled in schools (4,015 in [primary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_education), 3,578 in secondary and 2,821 in high school). About 14.1% of high school students received diplomas.[^cite_note-154] There were 135 schools in the [Bethlehem Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Governorate) in 2006; 100 run by the [Education Ministry of the Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_Minister_of_the_Palestinian_National_Authority), seven by the [United Nations Relief and Works Agency](32270.md) (UNRWA) and 28 were private.[^cite_note-155]



[Bethlehem University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_University) main building

Bethlehem is home to [Bethlehem University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_University), a [Catholic](606848.md) Christian co-educational institution of higher learning founded in 1973 in the Lasallian tradition, open to students of all faiths. Bethlehem University is the first university established in the West Bank, and can trace its roots to 1893 when the [De La Salle Christian Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_the_Brothers_of_the_Christian_Schools) opened schools throughout Palestine and Egypt.[^cite_note-156]

## Transportation



A street in Bethlehem

Bethlehem has three bus stations owned by private companies which offer service to Jerusalem, Beit Jala, Beit Sahour, [Hebron](38577.md), [Nahalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahalin), [Battir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battir), [al-Khader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khader), [al-Ubeidiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubeidiya,_West_Bank) and [Beit Fajjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Fajjar). There are two taxi stations that make trips to Beit Sahour, Beit Jala, Jerusalem, [Tuqu'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuqu') and [Herodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodium). There are also two car rental departments: Murad and 'Orabi. Buses and taxis with West Bank licenses are not allowed to enter Israel, including Jerusalem, without a permit.[^cite_note-157]

The Israeli construction of the [West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier) has affected Bethlehem politically, socially, and economically. The barrier is located along the northern side of the town's built-up area, within distance of houses in the Aida [refugee camp](438046.md) on one side, and the Jerusalem municipality on the other.[^cite_note-OCHA-68] Most entrances and exits from the Bethlehem agglomeration to the rest of the [West Bank](33209.md) are currently subjected to [Israeli checkpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_checkpoint) and roadblocks. The level of access varies based on Israeli security directives. Travel for Bethlehem's Palestinian residents from the West Bank into [Jerusalem](16043.md) is regulated by a permit-system.[^cite_note-158] Palestinians require a permit to enter the Jewish holy site of [Rachel's Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel's_Tomb). Israeli citizens are barred from entering Bethlehem and the nearby biblical [Solomon's Pools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon's_Pools).[^cite_note-OCHA-68]
