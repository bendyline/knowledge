**Chakwal** ([Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) and [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): چکوال) is a city in [Chakwal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakwal_District) in the [Punjab](24751.md) province of Pakistan.[^cite_note-cp-1]

It is the [48th most populous city of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_cities_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-2] Chakwal is located 90 kilometres (56 mi) south-west of the federal capital, [Islamabad](51153.md) and 270 kilometres (170 mi) from the provincial capital, [Lahore](125315.md). It is accessible by both the [Islamabad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_International_Airport) and the [Lahore International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_International_Airport).[^cite_note-Tribune-3]

## Geography

Chakwal is part of the [Pothohar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau) region, with flat to gently undulating land broken up by gullies and low hill ranges.[^cite_note-cgiar-4] Chakwal's landscape features the canyons in Thirchak-Mahal. There are man-made and natural lakes around the city in neighbouring communities.[^cite_note-AdamsonShaw1981-5] The climate is [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) to [sub-humid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhumid_temperate_climate), with highly erratic rainfall patterns that occur during the [monsoon season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season), ranging from 250 mm to 1500 mm annually. These variations often cause [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion) and occasional flooding.[^cite_note-cgiar-4]

## Administration

Chakwal was created as an independent district of Rawalpindi in 1985 by combining sub division Chakwal of district Jhelum, sub division Talagang of district Attock and the police station [Choa Saidan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choa_Saidan_Shah), carved out of sub division [Pind Dadan Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pind_Dadan_Khan) of district Jhelum.[^cite_note-6]

In addition to being the district headquarters, Chakwal city is also the administrative centre of [Chakwal Tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakwal_Tehsil) (a [subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) of the district). The Chakwal District is divided into three tehsils: [Kalar-Kahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalar-Kahar), [Choa Saidan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choa_Saidan_Shah) and [Chakwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakwal_Tehsil) itself. The city of Chakwal itself is divided into five [Union councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Councils_of_Pakistan),[^cite_note-NRB-7] and Chakwal district is divided into 68 union councils.

## Economy

Much like the other semi-rural areas within [Punjab](24751.md), Chakwal has a predominantly agricultural economy, which includes a mix of [livestock](25160767.md) and [crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop), particularly [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) and [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) (*Arachis hypogaea*, generally called *groundnuts*).[^cite_note-cgiar-4] Groundnuts are especially important for Pakistan's economy, as the country is the 7th largest producer of groundnuts in the world.[^cite_note-lament-24] Chakwal relies heavily on groundnut production because [dryland crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dryland_crop) serve as a key source of [food security](216361.md) in semi-arid regions like this. However, the district's agricultural production suffers from a lack of modern farming practices and outdated technology, which leads to inefficient yields; farmers mainly rely on hand-sowing methods with little to no mechanization.[^cite_note-GER-25][^cite_note-26]

This agricultural economy, which is vital for both Chakwal and Pakistan as a whole, faces several natural, structural, and fiscal challenges. Farmers are highly dependent on irregular rainfall, which can cause significant crop and soil damage during periods of heavy precipitation. The region also experiences low levels of formal education, limiting the availability of skilled labor necessary for improved farming methods and mechanization.[^cite_note-lament-24] Poverty and socioeconomic inequality, caused by high population growth and unemployment, further contribute to the area's challenges. Women play an active role in agricultural labor but often have limited access to formal employment opportunities.[^cite_note-GER-25]

Chakwal's agricultural sector is also influenced by external factors, such as Pakistan's reliance on imported groundnuts from foreign producers like India. This influx of cheaper imported groundnuts lowers local market prices and discourages domestic production.[^cite_note-27] Farmers and local organizations have called for government interventions to address these challenges and enhance agricultural productivity. One such proposed intervention involves expanding the value of groundnuts beyond their edible use by establishing [groundnut oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundnut_oil) extraction plants in Chakwal.[^cite_note-lament-24]

## Culture

Chakwal has a longstanding military tradition that predates the establishment of Pakistan. Economic hardship in the region led the [British Army](4887.md) to recruit physically capable but unemployed workers; 460 residents were enlisted during [World War I](4764461.md), and more followed in [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-heroes-28] Several soldiers from Chakwal lost their lives during these conflicts and were awarded British honors, including the [Victoria Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Cross). The area is marked by cannons, monuments, and memorials that commemorate these contributions.[^cite_note-vote-29] Some community members have expressed a desire for greater recognition of these sacrifices from Britain.[^cite_note-heroes-28]

After the [partition of the Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_the_Indian_subcontinent), Chakwal remained one of the key recruitment areas and continued to produce military personnel who served in the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md). The region has become known for its military heritage, and several individuals from Chakwal have attained high-ranking positions, including [generals](148537.md) such as Abdul Majeed Malik. Due to Pakistan's broader military culture, some soldiers from Chakwal, including Malik, have also held positions in political office.[^cite_note-vote-29]
