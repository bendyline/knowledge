The **Transitional Sovereignty Council** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مجلس السيادة الإنتقالي, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Majlis al-Siyādah al-Intiqālī*)[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] is the internationally recognised[^cite_note-4] collective [head of state of Sudan](450116.md), formed on 21 August 2019,[^cite_note-5] by the [August 2019 Draft Constitutional Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_transition_to_democracy_\(2019%E2%80%932021\)#Draft_Constitutional_Declaration).[^cite_note-raisethevoices_4Aug2019_const_dec-6][^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7][^cite_note-AJE_17Aug_signing-8] The initial council was dissolved by its Chairman [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md) in the [October 2021 Sudanese coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2021_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and reconstituted the following month with new membership,[^cite_note-al_Jazeera_11/2021-1] effectively changing it from a [unity government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_government) to a [military junta](216815.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Under Article 10.(b) of the Draft Constitutional Declaration, it is composed of five civilians chosen by the [Forces of Freedom and Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_of_Freedom_and_Change) alliance (FFC), five military representatives chosen by the [Transitional Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Military_Council_\(2019\)) (TMC), and a civilian selected by agreement between the FFC and TMC. The chair for the first 21 months was to be a military member, [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md), and for the remaining 18 months the chair was to be a civilian member, under Article 10.(c).[^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7] The original Sovereignty Council was mostly male, with only two female members: [Aisha Musa el-Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisha_Musa_el-Said) and [Raja Nicola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Nicola).[^cite_note-SudTribune_SovCouncil_exists-11] Under Article 19 of the Draft Constitutional Declaration, the Sovereignty Council members are ineligible to run in the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Sudanese_general_election) scheduled to follow the [transition period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_transition_to_democracy_\(2019%E2%80%932021\)).[^cite_note-raisethevoices_4Aug2019_const_dec-6][^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7]

## Background

Sudan had multi-member Sovereignty Councils holding the [role of head of state of Sudan](450116.md) several times during the twentieth century. Following more than half a year of [sustained civil disobedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Revolution) and a shift of the presidency from [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) to the [Transitional Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Military_Council_\(2019\)) (TMC) in April 2019 by a coup d'état, the TMC and the [Forces of Freedom and Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_of_Freedom_and_Change) alliance (FFC) made a July 2019 Political Agreement[^cite_note-AJE_transition_plan-12][^cite_note-TMC_FFC_20190717_agreement-13] and completed it by the August 2019 Draft Constitutional Declaration.[^cite_note-raisethevoices_4Aug2019_const_dec-6][^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7][^cite_note-AJE_17Aug_signing-8] Articles 9.(a) and 10.(a) of the August 2019 Draft Constitutional Declaration both transfer the role of head of state to the Sovereignty Council.[^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7]

## Structure and membership

Article 10.(b) of the Draft Constitutional Declaration defines the Sovereignty Council to consist of five civilians chosen by the FFC, five military chosen by the TMC, and a civilian "selected by agreement" between the FFC and TMC.[^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7]

Under Article 10.(c) of the Draft Constitutional Declaration, for the first 21 months of the 39-month transitional period defined by the document, the chair of the Sovereignty Council was to be chosen by the five military members of the council. For the following 18 months, the chair was to be chosen by the five civilian members selected by the FFC.[^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7]

The military membership of the Sovereignty Council included General [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md), General [Hemedti](60669608.md), Lieutenant-General [Yasser al-Atta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_al-Atta),[^cite_note-AJE_17Aug_signing-8] General [Shams al-Din Khabbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams_al-Din_Khabbashi) and Major-General [Ibrahim Jabir Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Jabir_Karim).[^cite_note-SudTribune_SovCouncil_exists-11][^cite_note-allafrica-14] Hemedti has been the deputy chairman of the Sovereignty Council in the past.[^cite_note-allafrica-14]

The five civilians chosen by the FFC are [Aisha Musa el-Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisha_Musa_el-Said) of the *National Gathering Initiative*; [Siddiq Tawer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddiq_Tawer), a prominent member of the [Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Region of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Region_of_Sudan)[^cite_note-SudTribune_SovCouncil_exists-11] (and thereby a member of the [National Consensus Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Consensus_Forces))[^cite_note-Dabanga_SCouncil_appointed-15] from the [Nuba Mountains](1139195.md) in [South Kordofan](767653.md); [Mohamed al-Faki Suleiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Alfaki) of the *Unionist Gathering* from [Northern Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_state,_Sudan); Hassan Sheikh Idris (or *Hassan Mohamed Idris*),[^cite_note-ThReut_5civilians_SovCouncil-16][^cite_note-SudTribune_SovCouncil_exists-11] a prominent member of the [National Umma Party](1184790.md) (and thereby a member of *Sudan Call*)[^cite_note-Dabanga_SCouncil_appointed-15] from [Kassala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassala_\(state\)); and [Mohammed Hassan Osman al-Ta'ishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Hassan_Osman_al-Ta'ishi)[^cite_note-SudTrib_FFC_5SovCouncil-17][^cite_note-SudTribune_SovCouncil_exists-11] of the [Sudanese Professionals Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Professionals_Association).[^cite_note-Dabanga_SCouncil_appointed-15] Taha Othman Ishaq (or *Osman*), a lawyer and member of the FFC negotiating committee, declined his nomination by the FFC to the Sovereignty Council on the grounds that the FFC alliance had earlier agreed that members of the negotiating committee should not become members of the Sovereignty Council.[^cite_note-SudTrib_not_Othman-18]

[Raja Nicola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Nicola) was the civilian member of the Sovereignty Council mutually chosen by the FFC and TMC.[^cite_note-SudTribune_Nicola-19] The choice of Nicola, as a member of the [Sudanese Copt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copts_in_Sudan) community, is seen as a symbol of respect for diversity, in particular to Sudanese Christians.[^cite_note-SudTribune_Nicola-19]

On 4 February 2021, al-Burhan issued a decree to add three new members to the Council.[^cite_note-20] The new members are [Sudanese Revolutionary Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Revolutionary_Front) leader El Hadi Idris Yahya, [Sudan People's Liberation Movement-North](30884032.md) leader [Malik Agar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Agar), and Sudan Liberation Movement for Justice-Karbino leader El Tahrir Abubakr Hajar.

### Proposed reform in 2025

Amendments made to the transitional constitution in February 2025, propose to maintain the council as the collective head of state of Sudan, and empower it to appoint a prime minister, cabinet, state governors and justices. The reformed council is to have eleven members, with six nominated by the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) and three from signatories to the peace agreement.[^cite_note-31]

### Women's participation

The Sovereignty Council was mostly male, with two of the eleven members being female: [Aisha Musa el-Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisha_Musa_el-Said) and [Raja Nicola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Nicola).[^cite_note-Dabanga_women_excluded-32][^cite_note-SudTrib_FFC_5SovCouncil-17] At a lower level, [Siham Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siham_Osman?action=edit\&redlink=1) was nominated Under-Secretary of the Minister of Justice, and acts on behalf of the [Minister of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Justice_\(Sudan\)) with his authorisation when he is on travels.[^cite_note-33]

The [Sudanese Women's Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Women's_Union) argued that women had played as significant a role as men in the political changes of 2019 and that Sudanese women "claim an equal share of 50-50 with men at all levels, measured by qualifications and capabilities".[^cite_note-Dabanga_women_excluded-32]

### Ineligibility in 2022

Under Article 19 of the August 2019 Draft Constitutional Declaration, the eleven members of the Sovereignty Council of the transitional period were forbidden (along with ministers and other senior transition leaders) from running in the [2022 Sudanese general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sudanese_general_election) scheduled to end the transitional period.[^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7][^cite_note-raisethevoices_4Aug2019_const_dec-6]

## Powers

Article 11.(a) lists 17 political powers held by the Sovereignty Council, including the appointment of the [Prime Minister](1881046.md), confirmation of leaders of certain state bodies, the right to declare war or a state of emergency, and signing and ratifying national and international agreements.[^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7]

### Separation of powers

On 24 October, the [Sudanese Professionals Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Professionals_Association) (SPA) claimed that civilian members of the Sovereignty Council violated the constitutional constraints on their power by appearing to coordinate with [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF) and override the Ministry of Health's role in managing [vector control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_control) against the spread of [dengue fever](39669.md) and [chikungunya](366112.md). The SPA stated, "the campaign appeared to be the scene of direct interaction of the \[RSF] with the health situation in the concerned states, in the absence of health departments at the federal or state level. ... \[The] whole issue of health is not the prerogative of the Sovereign Council." *[Sudan Tribune](6227213.md)* expressed concern that the RSF and its leader [Hemedti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Hamdan_Dagalo) were trying to improve the RSF's image,[^cite_note-SudTrib_SovCouncil_abuse_power-34] damaged by its carrying out of [crimes against humanity](61099.md) in the [War in Darfur](5043637.md)[^cite_note-HRW_Hemetti_Darfur2014-35] and human rights violations during the 3 June 2019 [Khartoum massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_massacre).[^cite_note-DailyBeast_Hemeti_responsibility-36]

## Decision-making

Under Article 11.(c) of the Draft Constitutional Declaration, the Sovereignty Council makes decisions either [by consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making), or when consensus is not possible, by a two-thirds majority (eight members).[^cite_note-Const_Dec_En_unofficial-7]

## Actions

The Council announced a state of emergency in [Port Sudan](213572.md) during tribal clashes which resulted in the death of 16 people on 26 August 2019.[^cite_note-37]

In November 2019, [Abdalla Hamdok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdalla_Hamdok)'s government repealed all laws restricting women's freedom of dress, movement, association, work and study. On 22 April 2020, the transitional government issued an amendment to its criminal legislation which declares that anyone who performs [female genital mutilation](11408.md) either in a medical establishment or elsewhere will be punished by three years' imprisonment and a fine.[^cite_note-38]

## Dissolution

On 25 October 2021, in the [2021 Sudan coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Sudan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), Chairman [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md) dissolved the Council and removed Prime Minister [Abdalla Hamdok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdalla_Hamdok) from office.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] He re-formed the TSC with new membership on November 11, 2021.[^cite_note-al_Jazeera_11/2021-1] On November 21, 2021, al-Burhan signed a deal with Hamdok that reinstated him as prime minister, provided for the release of all political prisoners detained in the coup, and restored the 2019 Draft Constitutional Declaration.[^cite_note-42]
