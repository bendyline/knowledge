Legislative elections are scheduled to be held in [Israel](9282173.md) on 27 October 2026, to elect all 120 members of the [26th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_Knesset) and determine the governing coalition that succeeds the [37th government](72545811.md) led by Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md).

The outgoing [25th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Knesset) became the first Israeli legislature to complete its full four-year term without early dissolution since 1988, ending the prolonged [political crisis of 2018–2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932022_Israeli_political_crisis) that saw five consecutive snap elections. However, the government's tenure was marked by political turmoil, including [mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform_protests) over [judicial reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform), the [7 October attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_October_attacks) and subsequent [Gaza war](74998519.md), and recurring coalition crises over [Haredi military conscription](82822441.md).

In this proportional representation system, parties must meet a 3.25% [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) to enter the legislature.

## Background

See also: [Impact of the Gaza war § Netanyahu government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_Gaza_war#Netanyahu_government), and [Haredi conscription in Israel](82822441.md)

After the [36th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel) lost its majority, [snap elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election) were called in 2022. They resulted in [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md)'s bloc gaining a majority,[^cite_note-9] and a [government](72545811.md) was successfully negotiated between [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud), [Otzma Yehudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzma_Yehudit), [Noam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_\(political_party\)), [Religious Zionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_Party), [United Torah Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Torah_Judaism), and [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas). The coalition was sworn in on 29 December 2022.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

In January 2023, the 37th government announced a package of proposed [judicial reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform) that would have significantly changed the balance of power between the judiciary and the executive and legislative branches.[^cite_note-12] The proposals triggered one of the [largest protest movements in Israeli history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform_protests), with weekly demonstrations taking place across the country for several months. Following mass protests, a nationwide general strike, and growing opposition from business leaders, military reservists and civil society organizations, Netanyahu announced in March 2023 that the legislative process would be suspended to allow negotiations with the opposition.[^cite_note-13]

Negotiations later broke down, and the coalition resumed advancing parts of the reform. In July 2023, the Knesset approved an amendment to *Basic Law: The Judiciary* preventing courts from applying the "reasonableness" standard to decisions of the government and its ministers.[^cite_note-14] On 1 January 2024, the [Supreme Court](1666510.md), sitting as the High Court of Justice, ruled by an 8–7 majority that the amendment was unconstitutional and struck it down.[^cite_note-15]

Following the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), the opposition party [National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_\(Israel\)) joined the government to form an emergency national unity coalition, with [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz) and [Gadi Eizenkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadi_Eizenkot) joining the newly formed [war cabinet](75030844.md).[^cite_note-toi12oct-16] While the emergency coalition provided short-term stability, Netanyahu's government simultaneously faced sustained public and political pressure to resign and call for early elections.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-thenationalnews20231105-20] The emergency coalition eventually fractured, with [Gideon Sa'ar](485357.md)'s [New Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope_\(Israel\)) faction splitting from National Unity and resigning from the government in March 2024 before rejoining in September[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] and ultimately agreeing in 2025 to merge with Likud.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The remaining National Unity alliance formally departed the government on 9 June 2024.[^cite_note-25] [Otzma Yehudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzma_Yehudit) temporarily exited the coalition in January 2025 in protest of a [Gaza ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2025_Gaza_war_ceasefire),[^cite_note-26] and rejoined in March following the agreement's collapse.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

The coalition's stability was repeatedly tested by internal ideological fractures, most notably regarding the politically volatile issue of Haredi military conscription.[^cite_note-29] The instability reached a critical phase in July 2025, when both factions of [United Torah Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Torah_Judaism) ([Degel HaTorah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degel_HaTorah) and [Agudat Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agudat_Yisrael)) resigned from the government in protest over the stalled conscription legislation.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Shortly after, [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas) also withdrew its ministers from the government, further destabilizing the coalition.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-tjp17july-34][^cite_note-35] While these parties often returned to their posts or remained in the coalition to avoid toppling the government, the recurring threat of collapse became a constant feature of Netanyahu's premiership. These fractures persisted until May 2026, when legislative momentum for dissolution grew across the political spectrum.[^cite_note-\:1-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] However, with the Knesset nearing the completion of its full four-year term, these efforts would have only moved the election date forward by a few weeks.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Ultimately, the coalition managed these pressures.

## Date and electoral schedule

The Knesset formally dissolved on 17 July 2026.[^cite_note-ynet16july-41] By completing its full four-year term, the 25th Knesset became the first Israeli legislature to serve its full duration without early dissolution since 1988, and the 37th government became the first to complete a full term since the [1973 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Israeli_legislative_election).[^cite_note-42]

The candidate list submission deadline was 8 September,[^cite_note-43] while the [surplus-vote agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparentment) deadline is 16 October.[^cite_note-44]

Voting at [diplomatic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_missions) and embassies is scheduled to take place on 20 October, while election day in Israel is 27 October.[^cite_note-tjp13july-45]

The official results will be confirmed on 4 November.[^cite_note-tjp13july-45]

## Electoral system

Further information: [Elections in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Israel)

The 120 seats in the [Knesset](74656.md) are elected by [closed list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_list) [proportional representation](23298.md) in a single nationwide constituency. The [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) for the election is 3.25%.[^cite_note-46]

### Expatriate voting and eligibility dispute

Because Israeli law does not permit absentee voting at embassies for ordinary citizens, participation requires physical travel.[^cite_note-jpost0831-47] Ahead of the vote, more than 33,000 expatriates registered with the nonpartisan Fly\&Vote initiative,[^cite_note-jpost0831-47] drawing threats of legal action from Prime Minister Netanyahu over concerns they would lean opposition.[^cite_note-toi0831-48] Separately, a High Court petition by some 100 expats, removed from voter registries under the Interior Ministry's "Code 51" non-resident designation,[^cite_note-jta0828-49] was unanimously dismissed on procedural grounds on 2 September 2026.[^cite_note-jta0906-50][^cite_note-bagatz-51]

### Surplus-vote agreements

Two parties can sign a surplus-vote agreement that allows them to compete for leftover seats as if they were running together on the same list. The [Bader–Ofer (D'Hondt) method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method) slightly favours larger lists, meaning that alliances are more likely to receive leftover seats than parties would be individually. If the alliance receives leftover seats, the Bader–Ofer calculation is applied again, to determine how the seats are divided among the two allied lists.[^cite_note-52]

## Leadership elections and primaries

For a more comprehensive list, see [Party lists for the 2026 Israeli legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_lists_for_the_2026_Israeli_legislative_election).

[Leadership elections](4527291.md) have been held by some parties to determine party leadership ahead of the election. [Primary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_elections) are held by some parties in advance of the national election to determine the composition of their [party list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_list).

### Balad

[Balad](44494596.md) held its party primary on 27 June 2026. [Sami Abu Shehadeh](61848927.md) was re-elected as party leader, while the rest of the list included Bechar Aouda, Maher Karkabi Sabah, Hassan Nasarsa and [Orly Noy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orly_Noy?action=edit\&redlink=1), board chairwoman at [B'Tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Tselem) and the list's first Jewish candidate.[^cite_note-140]

### The Democrats

Main article: [2026 The Democrats primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_The_Democrats_primaries)

Following [Labor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Labor_Party) poor electoral performance in the 2022 election, in which it won the minimum seats needed for entry into the Knesset and [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz) failed to cross the electoral threshold,[^cite_note-141] party chair [Merav Michaeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merav_Michaeli) announced in December 2023 that she planned to resign.[^cite_note-142] [Yair Golan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Golan) won a landslide victory in Labor's May 2024 [leadership primary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election) on a platform promising to unify the political left.[^cite_note-143] Labor and Meretz agreed to merge in July 2024, forming a new political party called [The Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Democrats_\(Israel\)), with Golan serving as the unified party leader.[^cite_note-jp12-144] The new party approved the merger in May 2026.[^cite_note-145] The Democrats held [a digital primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_The_Democrats_primaries) on 20 July 2026, with 97,000 of its 112,000 registered members casting ballots, an 86 per cent turnout.[^cite_note-146] Party leader Golan secured the top spot on the list, followed by serving MKs [Naama Lazimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naama_Lazimi), [Gilad Kariv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilad_Kariv), and [Efrat Rayten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efrat_Rayten).[^cite_note-147]

### Hadash

[Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash) closed its primary nominations on 14 May; the parties committee, known as the National Council, was allowed to cast ballots.[^cite_note-148] The party held its primary two days later; former MK [Yousef Jabareen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_Jabareen) defeated Shukry Awouda for the position of chairman. Incumbent leader [Ayman Odeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Odeh) and MK [Aida Touma-Suleiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aida_Touma-Suleiman) did not stand for re-election.[^cite_note-walla16may-62] It was the first party to select its electoral slate before the election.[^cite_note-149] The Hadash list included Jabareen, Jafar Farah, [Ofer Cassif](60517491.md), [Youssef Atauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youssef_Atauna) and Nihaya Wishahy.[^cite_note-150] Farah, who was placed fourth on the [Joint List](45229828.md), withdrew from the race following sexual harassment claims[^cite_note-151] and was replaced by Faten Ghattas.[^cite_note-152]

### Likud

Main article: [2026 Likud primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Likud_primary)

[Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) formalized an agreement in August 2025 for the [New Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope_\(Israel\)) party to merge into it ahead of the next election.[^cite_note-153] The merger has not been approved by the Knesset [House Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Committee_\(Israel\)) as of July 2026.[^cite_note-154]

Following multiple legal battles and postponements, the party's primary elections were rescheduled from 4 to 17 August 2026.[^cite_note-155] The internal race has been marked by disputes over Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's push to expand reserved slots, alongside a party court ruling that barred incumbent lawmakers and ministers from running in regional district spots.[^cite_note-156]

On 17 August, the [2026 Likud primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Likud_primary) was held with a 53% turnout for a total of 76,068 party members voting.[^cite_note-157] Party members voted for their preferred candidates with reserved slots for specific regional districts and for party leader [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md).[^cite_note-158] The top candidates in the resulting party list are Netanyahu, [Eli Cohen](46195208.md) and [Amir Ohana](48869232.md).[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160]

### Ra'am

Main article: [2026 Ra'am primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ra'am_primary)

The [Ra'am](39385583.md) party announced in late July 2026 that it would hold its first independent primary on 22 August.[^cite_note-161] The party's list includes [Mansour Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Abbas), [Walid Taha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walid_Taha), [Waleed Alhwashla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waleed_Alhwashla), [Iman Khatib-Yasin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iman_Khatib-Yasin) (in the reserved woman's slot) and [Yasser Hujirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Hujirat).[^cite_note-162]

### Religious Zionist Party

The [Religious Zionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_Party) re-elected party leader [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) in September 2025.[^cite_note-163] In July 2026, the party held primaries to determine its electoral slate for the upcoming election,[^cite_note-164] with 130 members of the parties' Central Committee voting to select candidates.[^cite_note-165] Smotrich retained his position at the head of the list, followed by incumbent lawmakers [Orit Strook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orit_Strook), [Simcha Rothman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simcha_Rothman), [Zvi Sukkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvi_Sukkot), and [Ohad Tal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohad_Tal).[^cite_note-tjp26july-166] The final list remains subject to adjustments, as Smotrich retains the authority to appoint two reserved seats, including an expected spot for [Tzvika Mor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzvika_Mor).[^cite_note-as26july-167]

### The Reservists

[Yoaz Hendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoaz_Hendel) was elected head of the [The Reservists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reservists_\(political_party\)) party on 9 June.[^cite_note-168] A week later, the party held their primaries.[^cite_note-169]

## Campaign

As the campaign officially began in early September 2026, the leading contenders were [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) and [Yashar](81096332.md).

### Amcha Yisrael

[Amcha Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amcha_Yisrael), a "right-wing party", was launched by [Ofer Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Winter), a former IDF brigadier general, on 25 August 2026.[^cite_note-170] The party is in favor of joining a "broad government made up of Zionist parties" and supports increasing Haredi involvement in the [IDF](39237.md), while the party's slate includes activist [Yoseph Haddad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoseph_Haddad) and former [Jerusalem](16043.md) deputy mayor [Fleur Hassan-Nahoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleur_Hassan-Nahoum).[^cite_note-171]

### Blue and White

[Blue and White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_and_White_\(political_party\)) leader [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz) has outlined his party's position to "put an end to boycotts and the war between blocs" and "form a broad Zionist government without extremists".[^cite_note-pivot-121] He also stressed that "Iran is never allowed to revive its nuclear program" and the importance of "preventing Hezbollah and Hamas from rebuilding their military capabilities".[^cite_note-172] In August, Gantz reiterated the priorities of a unity government as "security, service for all, a constitution, and public education" and also ruled out supporting a Netanyahu government.[^cite_note-173] Parties which are in favor of pursuing a unity government are known as the Third Bloc.[^cite_note-174]

### The Color Black

The Color Black launched its campaign on 28 September 2026 in [Bnei Brak](314671.md) at a march which protested against the detention of Haredi draft evaders.[^cite_note-175]

### The Democrats

[The Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Democrats_\(Israel\)) head [Yair Golan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Golan) issued a statement in May 2026 calling for the Zionist opposition to work with [Ra'am](39385583.md).[^cite_note-176] Several days later, Golan called for [Gadi Eisenkot](5249926.md) to either join his party or the [Bennett-Lapid alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett%E2%80%93Lapid_alliance), to avoid [splitting the vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splitting_the_vote).[^cite_note-177] The party launched its election campaign in [Rabin Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabin_Square) on 25 July 2026.[^cite_note-178] The party is working on "key issues" it plans to raise, including "democracy, the rule of law" and the judiciary, ahead of potential coalition negotiations.[^cite_note-179]

### Haredi Public

[Haredi Public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_Public) launched its election campaign on 23 August 2026.[^cite_note-toi24aug-180]

### Joint List

The four parties of the 2020 [Joint List](45229828.md) ([Ra'am](39385583.md), [Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash), [Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'al) and [Balad](44494596.md)) signed a preliminary agreement in January 2026 to work toward reviving the alliance, with support from the Arab public.[^cite_note-181] All parties (with the exception of Ra'am) agreed in late August 2026 to run together.[^cite_note-182] Joint List member [Ahmad Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tibi) raised the possibility in a September interview of replacing [Speaker of the Knesset](1284678.md) [Amir Ohana](48869232.md) following the election, with the support of the Zionist opposition, in order to control the "parliamentary agenda." He also stated that the Joint List would "do more than" support an opposition candidate for prime minister.[^cite_note-183]

### Likud

Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md), of [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud), has warned against voters supporting small parties on the right, noting that if they don't meet the [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold), a right-wing government may not retain power.[^cite_note-184] [Ze'ev Elkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ze'ev_Elkin), the only [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) speaker on its list, was appointed the head of immigrant outreach on 25 August.[^cite_note-185]

### New Economic Party–The Reservists

In July 2026, [Yoaz Hendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoaz_Hendel) of [The Reservists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reservists_\(political_party\)) and [Hili Tropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hili_Tropper) announced they would run in an alliance named Foundations of Israel.[^cite_note-186] Later in the month, it rebranded to Zionist Home – The Reservists.[^cite_note-187] However, the alliance ended on 6 September after Tropper decided to leave the alliance and join [Yashar](81096332.md). In response, Hendel joined forces with economist [Yaron Zelekha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaron_Zelekha) to form a new alliance between the [New Economic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Economic_Party), which Zelekha heads, and The Reservists,[^cite_note-188] with [Einat Wilf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einat_Wilf) joining the Reservists the day after.[^cite_note-189] The party platform focuses on the need to "fight tycoons and the cost of living" and supports "full universal service" for all Israelis.[^cite_note-190] Hendel reportedly wanted the party to sign a surplus-vote agreement with [Amcha Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amcha_Yisrael), but its leader [Ofer Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Winter) rejected it.[^cite_note-191]

### Noam

[Noam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_\(political_party\)) leader [Avi Maoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avi_Maoz) announced in July 2026 that the party would rebrand itself as "Noam for Israel." The party's election campaign, launched on 29 July, also revealed the party’s initial list of Knesset candidates, former [Kiryat Arba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiryat_Arba) Council head [Eliyahu Libman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliyahu_Libman), and [Beersheba](312755.md) Deputy Mayor Shimon Tubul.[^cite_note-192] The party announced in mid-August that it had added Yisca Hayimov, the sister of Shin Bet chief [David Zini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Zini), as well as Liora Alon, an immigrant from France and former advisor to the [Mayor of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Jerusalem).[^cite_note-NoamYB-193]

### Otzma Yehudit

[Otzma Yehudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzma_Yehudit) is expected to pursue voters from Likud and Shas who are dissatisfied with those parties.[^cite_note-194]

### Ra'am

[Ra'am](39385583.md) opened its electoral slate in January 2026 to Jewish candidates.[^cite_note-195] Later that year, in August, party leader [Mansour Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Abbas) and former Yesh Atid MK [Yoav Segalovitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Segalovitz) confirmed that Segalovitz joined the party's electoral list, he is reportedly expecting to receive the party's second slot.[^cite_note-196] Abbas finalized the party's formal separation from the southern branch of the [Islamic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_in_Israel) in July 2026,[^cite_note-197] pivoting from [Islamism](15012.md) towards an independent civic platform.[^cite_note-198] Ra'am announced in July that it would run separately from the proposed [Joint List](45229828.md).[^cite_note-raam-199] The following week, the party launched its campaign in [Kafr Kanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Kanna), with Ra'am positioning itself as a pragmatic partner seeking to replace the Netanyahu government.[^cite_note-200]

### Religious Zionist Party

[Religious Zionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_Party) head [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) called for increasing Jewish immigration in [Galilee](12639.md) and the [Negev](142273.md) in a 27 August speech.[^cite_note-201] The party began negotiations with [Zehut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zehut) head [Moshe Feiglin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Feiglin) over a joint run, as the emergence of Winter's party led to polling where the RZP was near or below the [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold).[^cite_note-202] The two parties reached an agreement on 1 September to run as a technical bloc and were open to an alliance with other right-wing parties, while Winter dismissed the idea.[^cite_note-203] The RZP Central Committee approved the Zehut merger on 6 September.[^cite_note-204] The party released a [political platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_platform) in September calling for more extensive judicial reform, in addition to limiting the [standing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_\(law\)) of who is allowed to approach the court, as well as allowing the Supreme Court president and vice president to be selected by sitting judges, rather than the [Judicial Selection Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Selection_Committee_\(Israel\)).[^cite_note-205]

### Shas

[Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas) leader [Aryeh Deri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryeh_Deri) launched the party's election campaign on 20 August 2026 in [Holon](231871.md).[^cite_note-206]

### Together

[Naftali Bennett](39754840.md) registered a new party called [Bennett 2026](79671657.md) in April 2025[^cite_note-207] and announced the following November that he would contest the 2026 election.[^cite_note-JNS20251112-208] Bennett announced a new initiative in April 2026 geared towards those with disabilities.[^cite_note-209] Later that month, Bennett 2026 and [Yesh Atid](35515608.md) announced their intention to contest the election jointly in an alliance named [Together](2834313.md).[^cite_note-210] Bennett and [Yair Lapid](2448072.md), who are the respective heads of their parties, had served as rotating prime ministers during the [thirty-sixth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel).[^cite_note-211] The party launched their election campaign on 12 May with an event at [Expo Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_Tel_Aviv).[^cite_note-212] Lapid stated in a September interview with The Jerusalem Post that the party would support [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) rights and [civil marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_marriage) in the next government.[^cite_note-213] Together initially targeted voters on the right, but the rise of [Yashar](81096332.md) has led to a shift, with Bennett seeking to draw [Liberal Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Zionist) voters.[^cite_note-214]

### United Torah Judaism

[United Torah Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Torah_Judaism) announced in September 2026 that its three factions ([Agudat Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agudat_Yisrael), [Degel HaTorah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degel_HaTorah) and the newly formed [Homat Torat Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homat_Torat_Yisrael)), would run their election campaign through one organization, unlike previous elections.[^cite_note-215]

### Yashar

[Gadi Eisenkot](5249926.md) announced on 30 June 2025 that he would leave [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz)'s [National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_\(Israel\)), in part over disputes over a leadership primary.[^cite_note-216] MK [Matan Kahana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matan_Kahana) announced the following day that he would be also be leaving the party.[^cite_note-toi1july-217] The two were among the founders of a new political party, [Yashar](81096332.md), which was formed on 16 September 2025.[^cite_note-azulay-218] Ex-Shin Bet Director [Yoram Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoram_Cohen) joined the party on 26 April 2026.[^cite_note-219] After Eisenkot selected Cohen to be his number 2 listed individual, he stated that he would work with junior partners like Smotrich's Religious Zionism Party or the Likud if Netanyahu was no longer prime minister.[^cite_note-220] Eisenkot officially launched the party's campaign on 30 June 2026, centering his platform on government reform and the establishment of an inquiry commission into the 7 October attacks.[^cite_note-221] Yesh Atid MK [Elazar Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elazar_Stern) joined the party on 24 August.[^cite_note-222]

### Yisrael Beiteinu

On 5 August, it was reported that after leaving Likud, MK [Dan Illouz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Illouz) announced that he had joined [Yisrael Beiteinu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Beiteinu) and would run with the party in the next election. He stated that he joined because, according to Illouz, it is "the party that today represents the national and liberal values in which I believe."[^cite_note-223] On 23 August, the party reported the addition of Inbar Samovsky to its electoral list, a current Ra'anana City Council member.[^cite_note-NoamYB-193] Party leader [Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman) called for opposition parties to gain "seats from the coalition bloc", rather than taking votes from each other.[^cite_note-224] Several days before the deadline for the submission of electoral lists, Lieberman emphasized that parties in the opposition should ally with each other to ensure that they pass the threshold.[^cite_note-225]

## Campaign issues

### Constitutionalism

See also: [2023 Israeli judicial reform protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform_protests)

According to a poll in December 2025, an estimated 71% of Israelis support establishing a [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) for the country instead of the existing [Basic Laws of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Laws_of_Israel).[^cite_note-226] Bennett has campaigned on creating a constitution as part of its election platform.[^cite_note-227] Bennett implied that his support for a constitution is a response to the [government efforts at judicial reform in 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform) and [later again in 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Israeli_judicial_reform). He believes that the existing Basic Laws are unclear in how to legislate or repeal and by what majority, and that by contrast a constitution provides a "clear foundation".[^cite_note-Hayom0429-228] The party platform of [Yashar](81096332.md) also supports enacting a constitution for the state.[^cite_note-229] Yisrael Beitenu has also campaigned on the issue.[^cite_note-tjp11may-230] Zionist Home – The Reservists, an alliance headed by [Hili Tropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hili_Tropper) and [Yoaz Hendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoaz_Hendel), is in favor of creating what it calls a "Zionist constitution", which would "strengthen [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checks_and_balances)" in Israel.[^cite_note-231]

### Cost of living

In April 2026, a report found that the costs of living in Israel is about 20% higher than in wealthy European countries.[^cite_note-232] In response, Bennett announced a plan for "declaring an all-out war on monopolies" and would "require monopolies to divest brands" while "providing farmers with direct support through fixed grants".[^cite_note-233] Yashar also campaigned on lowering the cost of living.[^cite_note-234]

### Crime

See also: [Crime in Israel](15556664.md)

Crime rates have risen in recent years particularly in [Arab neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_localities_in_Israel), with [Tel Aviv District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_District) police chief calling it a "national emergency", and that "if we don't work together, we won't be able to win".[^cite_note-235] This issue has led to intense debates over potential causes and solutions.[^cite_note-236] In July 2026, the government approved a plan for the [Shin Bet](311752.md) to tackle organized violent crime.[^cite_note-237] Arab-majority parties have also campaigned on crime reduction, including from the [Joint List](45229828.md) made up of [Hadash–Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash%E2%80%93Ta'al) and [Balad](44494596.md).[^cite_note-JLlaunch-238]

### Investigation into the 7 October attacks

See also: [Gaza war](74998519.md)

A major campaign issue in the election is an investigation into the [7 October attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_October_attacks). On 6 February 2026, sitting prime minister Netanyahu released a document of his answers during an investigation by the [State Comptroller of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Comptroller_of_Israel), which pinned the failures on Netanyahu's political rivals while presenting himself in a positive image.[^cite_note-239] This led to criticism from opposition lawmakers, including from [Gadi Eisenkot](5249926.md), who founded Yashar to contest in the elections. Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of creating a "fabricated defense" and that the document was published "out of a desire to improve his image, distance himself from blame and harm the other candidates facing him".[^cite_note-240] [Gadi Eisenkot](5249926.md),[^cite_note-haaretz-june-30-2026-241] [Yair Golan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Golan) (The Democrats),[^cite_note-ynet-july-25-2026-242] [Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman) (Yisrael Beiteinu),[^cite_note-jerusalem-post-may-2-2026-243] and [Naftali Bennett](39754840.md) (Together)[^cite_note-termlimit-244] have all pledged to establish a state commission of inquiry into 7 October as part of their campaign platform.

On 8 September 2026, a report by *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* claimed that Netanyahu had received advanced warning by UAE President [Mohamed bin Zayed](2121945.md) of the 7 October attacks days prior, but did not warn Israeli security chiefs.[^cite_note-245] Netanyahu denied it as an "absolute lie", while Gadi Eisenkot, Naftali Bennett, Avigdor Lieberman, and Yair Golan issued a joint statement calling for "an immediate and thorough investigation" into Netanyahu.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]

### Military conscription

Since the start of the [Gaza war](74998519.md), the issues of [Haredi conscription in Israel](82822441.md) and [exemptions from military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exemption_from_military_service_in_Israel) have been heavily debated. During the war, support for Haredi conscription rose significantly, from 67% in January 2024 to 84.5% by January 2025.[^cite_note-248] In June 2024, the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md) declared any continued exemption of IDF conscription unlawful.[^cite_note-TOI20240625-249] In response, officials in the ultra-Orthodox [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas) party urged potential conscripts to ignore any call-ups from the IDF and to protest.[^cite_note-250] By the next month, the army began drafting 3,000 Haredi men.[^cite_note-251]

In 2025, [Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_and_Defense_Committee) chairman [Yuli Edelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuli_Edelstein) had been drafting a Haredi conscription bill, leading to internal division within the governing coalition as Haredi parties demanded continued [military exemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exemption_from_military_service_in_Israel).[^cite_note-252] On 23 July, [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) members voted to remove Edelstein and replace him with [Boaz Bismuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boaz_Bismuth) by a vote of 29 in favor, with four opposed.[^cite_note-253] A poll published by *[Ma'ariv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maariv_\(newspaper\))* reported that 52% of respondents found the move to be inappropriate, while 24% found it appropriate, and 24% were unsure.[^cite_note-Lazar25July-254]

Opposition leaders have criticized the coalition's unwillingness to enforce a full conscription on eligible Haredi citizens, and have called for an end to Haredi exemptions from military service.[^cite_note-255] [Inbar Harush Gity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inbar_Harush_Gity) of [Yashar](81096332.md) has named Haredi conscription "a top priority."[^cite_note-256] Similarly, the issue of Haredi conscription is considered a "central question" in the campaign of [Yesh Atid](35515608.md) and its leader [Yair Lapid](2448072.md).[^cite_note-257] Eisenkot indicated that he supports [military service exemptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exemption_from_military_service_in_Israel) for up to three percent of [Haredim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredim) enrolled in their [yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva) for religious study, but opposes the decades-old system that allowed significant exemptions to ultra-Orthodox men following the 7 October attacks.[^cite_note-258]

### Term limits

In September 2025, Bennett announced that he would work towards implementing [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits) for the prime minister if he was elected to lead a government.[^cite_note-termlimit-244] After forming the [Together](2834313.md) joint list with Lapid in April 2026, Bennett reiterated his platform of establishing term limits for the post of prime minister.[^cite_note-Hayom0429-228]

## Controversies

### AI videos

Netanyahu posted an [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI)-generated ad to his social media that appeared to show [Yashar](81096332.md) head [Gadi Eisenkot](5249926.md) bypassing a person wearing a shirt that said "Israeli unity" to instead embrace [Ra'am](39385583.md) head [Mansour Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Abbas); it was claimed by journalists [Guy Peleg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Peleg) and Barak Seri that the person with the shirt was meant to allude to Eisenkot's dead son, which was denied by the Likud.[^cite_note-259]

The chair of the [Central Elections Committee](4456077.md), [Noam Sohlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Sohlberg), ordered the [Religious Zionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_Party) on 26 July 2026 to remove an AI-generated campaign video, which appeared to show someone resembling Eisenkot meeting with Mansour Abbas and [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) president [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md), on the grounds that it could be "misleading to voters."[^cite_note-260]

Later that month, [Jonatan Urich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonatan_Urich), a campaign advisor for Netanyahu, removed an AI-generated video after Yashar threatened to file a lawsuit over it.[^cite_note-261]

Religious Zionist Party head [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) shared an AI video in mid-September that showed Supreme Court President [Yitzhak Amit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Amit), Justice [Daphne Barak-Erez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphne_Barak-Erez), and Attorney General [Gali Baharav-Miara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gali_Baharav-Miara) being "dragged at a protest." President [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md) and others, including Eisenkot, [Together](2834313.md) leader [Naftali Bennett](39754840.md) and [The Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Democrats_\(Israel\)) head [Yair Golan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Golan), strongly condemned the video.[^cite_note-262]

### Disqualification of individuals and parties

The Democrats filed a petition on 14 September before the [Central Elections Committee](4456077.md) (CEC) to bar [Otzma Yehudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzma_Yehudit).[^cite_note-263] Otzma Yehudit filed a petition against the Democrats that same day.[^cite_note-264] The panel voted on 24 September against disqualifying the Democrats by a vote of 14-2 and voted 19-10 against a petition to disqualify Otzma Yehudit. It also dismissed the disqualification of the [Religious Zionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_Party) by a vote of 21-3.[^cite_note-265] The decision to allow Otzma Yehudit to run, as well as petitions against [Itamar Ben-Gvir](54504115.md) and [Tally Gotliv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tally_Gotliv), was appealed to the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md) on 29 September.[^cite_note-tjp29sept-266]

On 23 September, the CEC barred [Ra'am](39385583.md) and the [Joint List](45229828.md), alongside its candidates [Ofer Cassif](60517491.md) and [Sami Abu Shehadeh](61848927.md), from contesting the election.[^cite_note-267] [Adalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adalah_\(legal_center\)) announced its intention to challenge this decision to the Supreme Court, with Joint List head [Yousef Jabareen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_Jabareen) criticising the decision as discouraging Arab citizens "representing more than 1.25 million voters" from voting.[^cite_note-268] Ra'am and the Joint List appealed their disqualifications to the Supreme Court on 29 September.[^cite_note-tjp29sept-266] The Supreme Court would have voted in favor of disqualifying Abu Shehadeh, but it allowed him to submit his resignation to the CEC on 2 October.[^cite_note-269] The Supreme Court overturned the disqualifications of Ra'am, the Joint List and Cassif the same day.[^cite_note-270]

### Shas campaign video

Shas released a campaign video in August, portraying a religious family on Shabbat, waiting for their son, who serves in the IDF, to arrive home for dinner. Notably, the party has supported legislation that would allow draft exemptions for Ultra-Orthodox men.[^cite_note-271] Yashar issued a formal complaint over the video, criticising the use of IDF imagery in the video. CEC head Solhberg called for the removal of the video. Shas did not remove the video at first, though it later removed the video the same day and criticised Sohlberg.[^cite_note-272]

### Yashar campaign in Cyprus

Yashar launched a billboard campaign in [Cyprus](5593.md), targeting Israelis who were vacationing abroad.[^cite_note-273] Cypriot [Member of the European Parliament](258909.md) [Fidias Panayiotou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidias_Panayiotou) was highly critical of the campaign.[^cite_note-274] Several days later, an image showing one of the billboards being vandalised circulated,[^cite_note-275] though it was later reported to be an AI-generated image.[^cite_note-276]
