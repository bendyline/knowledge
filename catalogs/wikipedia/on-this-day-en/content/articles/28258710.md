The **visa policy of the United Kingdom** is the policy by which [His Majesty's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Government) determines [visa](330265.md) requirements for visitors to the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and those seeking to work, study or reside there. The visa policy of the UK also applies to the [Crown dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_dependencies) of [Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailiwick_of_Guernsey), [Jersey](15693.md) and the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man), which each operate their own immigration enforcement and have separate [work permit](14666962.md) systems.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The visa policy does not apply to any of the [British Overseas Territories](207114.md), who generally apply their [own visa policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_policies_of_British_Overseas_Territories).

Anyone who does not have [right of abode in the United Kingdom](4608193.md) ([British citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_nationality_law) and some Commonwealth citizens, but not some categories of British national) requires [leave (permission) to enter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_to_enter) the UK. "Visa nationals" must obtain an [e-Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_visa) and "non-visa nationals" may travel as a visitor to the UK without a visa for up to 6 months.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Since April 2025, all non-visa nationals must apply for an [Electronic Travel Authorisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_\(United_Kingdom\)) (ETA).[^cite_note-5]

Visas are also required to live, study and work in the UK as a foreign national. Some EU, EEA and Swiss citizens who lived in the UK prior to [Brexit](41688778.md) are exempt under the [EU Settlement Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Settlement_Scheme),[^cite_note-6] and long-term foreign residents can apply for [indefinite leave to remain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_leave_to_remain), which is approximate to [permanent residency](1284324.md) in other countries.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

The UK maintains the [Common Travel Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Travel_Area) with the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) and the Crown Dependencies.[^cite_note-7] This means that most [citizens of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationality_law) are exempt from the requirement to hold a visa, residence permit or travel authorisation to enter, study and work in the UK or the Crown Dependencies, and vice versa.[^cite_note-8] The Common Travel Area does not apply to those who are not British or Irish citizens, so other citizens must have the correct permission to enter the UK when crossing the border.[^cite_note-9]

Non-visa nationals arriving in the UK from outside the [Common Travel Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Travel_Area) are assessed by UK immigration officers for "leave to enter." If granted for a regular visit, they receive a [passport stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport_stamp#United_Kingdom) stating 'Leave to enter for 6 months: employment and recourse to public funds prohibited,' except for nationals who use the [ePassport gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPassport_gates), who generally do not receive a stamp.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Those who entered using the now-defunct [Iris Recognition Immigration System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_Recognition_Immigration_System) (2004–2013) also did not receive a stamp.

UK Border Force officers can grant leave to enter via fax, email, or orally, including by telephone, for non-visa nationals visiting for up to 6 months. This does not apply when leave has been conferred by another Common Travel Area official.[^cite_note-22]

In some cases, such as certain [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation_in_the_United_Kingdom) flights, travellers may be 'remotely cleared' without inspection, receiving no passport stamp.[^cite_note-23]

Travellers arriving directly from the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) are not routinely checked by the UK Border Force. Under the Immigration (Control of Entry through Republic of Ireland) Order 1972, non-visa nationals are automatically granted 'deemed leave' for 6 months, without the right to work, and without receiving a passport stamp.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

Similarly, travel between the UK and the [Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands) or the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man) involves no routine immigration checks, and no passport stamp is issued. Leave to enter from these [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies) applies equally in the UK.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Travellers arriving in the Crown Dependencies directly from the Republic of Ireland are similarly exempt from routine checks and automatically receive deemed leave for 6 months, with no right to work.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

Since April 2025, all visitors with visa-exempt nationalities have needed an ETA to travel to the UK if they do not possess a valid UK visa or citizenship of the UK or Ireland. Since 27 November 2024, ETA applications have been opened for non-visa nationalities outside the European Union, who needed an ETA for any travel to the UK on or after 8 January 2025. This requirement was extended to EU nationals later with applications starting from 5 March and an ETA being mandatory from 2 April. There is a £16 fee for applying and an application can take up to three business days to be processed, though most should be approved or denied within a few hours. If approved, the ETA is valid for stays up to six months for a duration of two years or, if shorter, the date of the holder's passport expiring.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Since 20 May 2019, citizens from Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, Singapore, South Korea, Switzerland, and the United States generally do not receive a stamp, whether using [ePassport gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPassport_gates) or a staffed desk.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] However, three categories of these nationals must receive a stamp: those entering for a permitted paid engagement,[^cite_note-45] those with a Tier 5 Certificate of Sponsorship (up to 3 months),[^cite_note-46] or those otherwise required to visit a staffed desk.

## Transit

&#x20;There are 2 types of transit through the United Kingdom under the United Kingdom Transit Rules - **airside transit** and **landside transit**.[^cite_note-gov.uk-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] The Transit Without Visa facility for visa requiring nationals was abolished, effective 1 December 2014, and replaced with United Kingdom Transit Rules.[^cite_note-158] Notwithstanding the lists below, in general, even persons from 'Direct Airside transit visa-exempt nationalities' require a transit visa if transiting though the UK to other parts of the [Common Travel Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Travel_Area) including [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_\(state\)).

With the introduction of the [Electronic Travel Authorisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_\(United_Kingdom\)) system, an ETA or other permission to enter the UK will be required from any passenger needing to pass immigration control, even if that passenger is only in transit. An ETA will not be required for non-visa nationals transiting through Heathrow or Manchester airports to a non-UK or Irish destination without passing border control (i.e. Airside Transit in the list below).[^cite_note-when-not-need-eta-159]

Airside transit

- Available only at [London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport) and [Manchester Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport)

- Available only to passengers arriving and departing by air to an international destination (non-stop) other than [Ireland](14560.md) on the same day.

- Available only to passengers not leaving the airside zone of the airport and not passing through immigration control.

- Concerns passengers who would normally require a visitor visa to enter the United Kingdom but who hold a **Direct Airside Transit** visa, a passport of an airside transit visa-exempt country, or an airside transit visa exemption document, or a direct airside transit ETA.

Landside transit

- Available only to passengers arriving and departing by air by 23\:59 the next day who require passing through immigration control and leaving the airport building only for transit purposes.

- Concerns passengers who would normally require a visitor visa to enter the United Kingdom but who hold a **Visitor in Transit** visa, landside transit ETA, or a landside visa exemption document.

Airside transit visa exemption documents

The exemption applies where travellers:

- arrive and depart by air, **and**

- the onward flight must be confirmed, and must depart the same day, **and**

- have proper documentation for their destination (including a visa for the destination country if necessary), **and**

- fulfil **any one** of the below conditions:

1. have a valid visa for Australia, Canada, New Zealand or the US, whether or not travelling to or from those countries, **or**

2. have a valid Australian or New Zealand residence visa; **or**

3. have a valid Canadian permanent resident card issued on or after 28 June 2002; **or**

4. have a valid uniform format residence permit issued by an EEA state under Council Regulation (EC) number 1030/2002; **or**

5. have a valid Irish biometric visa endorsed BC or BC BIVS; **or**

6. have a valid uniform format category D visa for entry to a state in the European Economic Area (EEA); **or**

7. have a valid US I-551 permanent resident card issued on or after 21 April 1998; **or**

8. have an expired I-551 permanent resident card issued on or after 21 April 1998, accompanied by an I-797 extension letter; **or**

9. have a standalone US Immigration Form 155A/155B attached to an envelope; **or**

10. have a valid Schengen Approved destination Scheme (ADS) group tourism visa where the holder is travelling to the country that issued it or holds a valid airline ticket from the Schengen area, provided the holder can demonstrate they entered there no more than 30 days previously on the basis of a valid Schengen ADS visa;

eVisas or e-residence permits are not acceptable for airside transit unless the airline is able to verify it with the issuing country. Nationals of Syria who are holders of US B1/B2 visas are not visa-exempt.

Visitor in Transit visa exemption documents

The exemption applies where travellers:

- arrive and depart by air, **and**

- the onward flight must be confirmed, and must depart by 23\:59 the following day, **and**

- have proper documentation for their destination (including the correct visa or exemption document for the destination country ), **and**

- fulfil **any one** of the below conditions:

1. have a valid entry visa for Australia, Canada, New Zealand or the US, and a valid airline ticket for travel via the UK, as part of a reasonable journey to or from one of those countries, **or**

2. have a valid airline ticket for travel via the UK as part of a reasonable journey from Australia, Canada, New Zealand or the US, if they are transiting the UK no more than 6 months after the date when they last entered Australia, Canada, New Zealand or the US with a valid entry visa for that country; **or**

3. have a valid Australian or New Zealand residence visa; **or**

4. have a valid Canadian permanent resident card issued on or after 28 June 2002; **or**

5. have a valid uniform format residence permit issued by an EEA state under Council Regulation (EC) number 1030/2002; **or**

6. have a valid Irish biometric visa endorsed BC or BC BIVS and be travelling to Ireland; **or**

7. be travelling from Ireland and it is less than three months since given permission to stay in Ireland by the Irish immigration official with a valid Irish biometric visa, **or**

8. have a valid uniform format category D visa for entry to a state in the European Economic Area (EEA); **or**

9. have a valid US I-551 permanent resident card issued on or after 21 April 1998; **or**

10. have an expired I-551 permanent resident card issued on or after 21 April 1998, accompanied by an I-797 extension letter; **or**

11. have a standalone US Immigration Form 155A/155B attached to an envelope;

eVisas such as those regularly issued by Australia or e-residence permits are not acceptable for landside transit.[^cite_note-163]

## Electronic visa (e-Visa)

Home Office has introduced an e-Visa; keeping immigration records on the UKVI digital database and replacing physical documents, such as Biometric Resident Permits and Biometric Resident Cards which most of them expired by the end of 2024[^cite_note-164]; since April 2024. Most long-stay immigrants are invited to create a UKVI account on GOV.UK website and link it to their travel documents.[^cite_note-evisa2024-165]

This rollout brought some concerns over elderly who holds legacy immigration documents and have limitations in accessing digital infrastructure.[^cite_note-166] Also, there are several reports that various sectors, including the public service, refused eVisa as a proof of right to remain.[^cite_note-167]

Since 1 July 2026, all successful visa applicants will be issued only an e-Visa.[^cite_note-168]

Travelers must still submit their biometrics in person by going to a visa application center, unless they are eligible to use the ID Check app.[^cite_note-169] Currently, the ID Check app is only available to EU, EEA, Swiss, British National (Overseas), and Hong Kong Special Administrative Region passport holders.[^cite_note-170]

Visitors entering the UK, the Channel Islands and/or the Isle of Man who do not qualify for one of the visa exemptions listed above have to apply for a visa in advance through the [UK Visas and Immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Visas_and_Immigration) at a visa application centre.

All visitors must apply by registering an online account (except citizens of North Korea who must apply in person at the British Embassy), fill in the application form, pay the fee and attend an appointment at a visa application centre.[^cite_note-171]

A visitor's visa for a single stay or multiple stays of up to 6 months costs £135. A multiple-entry visitor's visa valid for 2 years costs £506, 5 years £506, and 10 years £1128.[^cite_note-UKBAvisitvisageneral-172][^cite_note-General_Visitor_visa-173] Mainland Chinese citizens can, for certain common forms of travel, obtain a 2-year, multiple-entry visitor's visa at a cost of £115.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] Being a [digital nomad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_nomad) is explicitly allowed on a standard visitor visa as long as the clients or employer are not based in the UK and there is no access to the UK jobs market.[^cite_note-primussolicitors.com-18]

Family members of EU, EEA and Swiss citizens who are not covered by one of the visa exemptions above can apply for an **[EEA Family Permit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Area_Family_Permit)** free of charge (instead of a visa).[^cite_note-176]

Applicants for most UK visas, including visitor visas and EEA Family Permits, must submit biometric identifiers (fingerprints and a digital facial image).[^cite_note-177] Exemptions from biometrics apply to diplomats, foreign officials, Commonwealth Forces, those transferring a visa vignette to a new passport, and travellers going directly to the Channel Islands or Gibraltar without passing through the UK or Isle of Man.[^cite_note-ECB17-178][^cite_note-179] Children must be accompanied when providing biometrics, which may be shared with foreign governments and are destroyed 10 years after capture.[^cite_note-180] Most visa applications are decided within 3 weeks.[^cite_note-General_Visitor_visa-173]

## History



Visa policy of the UK from 1960 to the present&#x20;

&#x20; United Kingdom or [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire)

&#x20; Visa-free access (subject to UK arrival with credible presentation as a visitor)

By early 1917, all aliens (i.e. persons who were not [British subjects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_subject)) were required to obtain visas from a British consul before embarking for the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-222] Visa requirements would then be maintained for aliens under the peacetime regime of immigration control retained after 1918.

Visas were mutually abolished between the UK and France in July 1921, although this did not apply to [French overseas territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Overseas_Departments_and_Territories) or the rest of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).[^cite_note-Hansard_1922-223]

Visa fee reduction between the UK and the United States had been discussed as early as 1925.[^cite_note-Hansard_1929-224] They were mutually reduced by 80 per cent on 1 April 1937; by February 1938, such fees had already been abolished between the US and most European countries,[^cite_note-Hansard_1938-225] but American citizens continued being required visas for entry into the UK until 1948.

### Post-World War II

Members of the British Empire had been considered British subjects before the war and did not generally need visas; this did not apply to [mandated territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate) such as [South West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Africa) or [Western Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Samoa), whose residents did need visas but were eligible to stay indefinitely.[^cite_note-Hansard_1942-226] As dominions became more independent and adopted their own nationality laws, however, visa restrictions started to be applied.

The abolition of visa requirements was increased in post-war era as part of a policy to make travel freer and easier; by 1949 visa requirements had been abolished in 20 countries.[^cite_note-Hansard_1949-227]

Although waiving visas or visa charges for United States citizens was discussed as early as 1930,[^cite_note-Hansard_1930-228] such visas would remain until 1948, when the UK abolished such visas and the US waived visa fees and doubled visa lengths for UK citizens, albeit stopping short at full visa abolition due to domestic considerations; MP [Phil Piratin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Piratin) was denied a visa.[^cite_note-Hansard_1948-229][^cite_note-Hansard_1952-230]

### Modern history

In March 2007, the [Home Office](225695.md) announced that it would carry out its first Visa Waiver Test to review the list of countries and territories outside the [European Union](9317.md), [European Economic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Area) and [Switzerland](26748.md) whose nationals are exempt from holding a visa for the UK.

After carrying out the review, in July 2008, [Jacqui Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqui_Smith), the [Home Secretary](149104.md), and [David Miliband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Miliband), the [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Affairs), announced to [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Parliament) that the results of the test showed a 'strong case' for introducing visa regimes for 11 countries ([Bolivia](3462.md), [Botswana](3464.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Mauritius](19201.md), [Namibia](21292.md), [South Africa](17416221.md), [Swaziland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaziland), [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) and [Venezuela](32374.md)) having taken into account the following factors (including the extent to which they were being addressed by the countries' authorities):[^cite_note-visawaiver-231][^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233]

- Passport security and integrity

- The degree of co-operation over deportation or removal of the countries' nationals from the UK

- Levels of illegal working in the UK and other immigration abuse (such as fraudulent asylum claims)

- Levels of crime and terrorism risk posed to the UK

Following the July 2008 announcement, the UK Government entered into a 6-month period of 'detailed dialogue' with the governments of the 11 countries 'to examine how risks can be reduced in a way that obviates the need for a visa regime to be introduced'. In order to maintain visa-free access to the UK, the 11 countries had to 'demonstrate a genuine commitment to put into effect credible and realistic plans, with clear timetables, to reduce the risks to the UK, and begin real implementation of these plans by the end of the dialogue period'.[^cite_note-visawaiver-231]

On 9 January 2009, the new visa rules announced required citizens of [Bolivia](3462.md), [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho), [South Africa](17416221.md) and [Swaziland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaziland) to obtain a visa, and only [Venezuelan](32374.md) nationals travelling on [biometric passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_passports) with an electronic chip issued since 2007 could continue to enter the UK without a visa.[^cite_note-234] The existing visa-free status for citizens of [Botswana](3464.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Mauritius](19201.md), [Namibia](21292.md) and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) was maintained.[^cite_note-235]

Starting from 3 March 2009, a transitional regime was put in place until 30 June 2009 for South African citizens - those who held a valid South African passport and had previously entered the UK lawfully using that passport could continue to enter the UK without a visa, whilst all other South African citizens were required to apply for a visa. On the same day, Taiwan citizens were able to enter the UK without a visa.[^cite_note-236] On 18 May 2009, Bolivian citizens were no longer able to enter the UK without a visa and Venezuelan citizens were required to present a [biometric passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_passport) to enter the UK without a visa.[^cite_note-237] On 1 July 2009, all South African citizens were required to apply for a visa to enter the UK. On the same day, citizens of Lesotho and Swaziland were required to apply for a visa to enter the UK.

On 30 March 2010, [Alan Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Johnson), the [Home Secretary](149104.md), and [David Miliband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Miliband), the [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Affairs), announced to [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Parliament) that, having carried out a review of visa regimes in relation to Eastern Caribbean countries, 5 countries ([Antigua and Barbuda](951.md), [Barbados](3455.md), [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), [St Kitts and Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kitts_and_Nevis) and [St Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)) would have their visa-free status maintained. At the same time, the UK Government would enter a six-month period of 'detailed dialogue' with the governments of 2 countries ([Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica) and [St Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Lucia)), who would have to 'demonstrate a genuine commitment to put into effect credible and realistic plans, with clear timetables, to reduce the risks to the UK, and begin implementing these plans by the end of the dialogue period' to maintain their visa-free status.[^cite_note-238] On 2 March 2011, [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May), the [Home Secretary](149104.md), and [William Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hague), the [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Affairs), announced to [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Parliament) that the governments of [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica) and [St Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Lucia) 'have made concrete improvements to the immigration, border control and identity systems which would not have happened without the test', and so the visa-free status for the 2 countries would be maintained.[^cite_note-239]

On 13 June 2011, new Immigration Rules were laid before [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Parliament) that came into force on 4 July 2011 introducing a new streamlined application procedure (waiving the normal requirements to provide documentary evidence of maintenance and qualifications at the time of application) for some non-visa nationals from 'low-risk countries' who wish to study in the UK for more than 6 months and apply for Tier 4 entry clearance. The following 15 countries and territories were categorised as 'low-risk' and included in 'Appendix H' of the [Immigration Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Rules): [Argentina](18951905.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), [Canada](5042916.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Croatia](5573.md), [Hong Kong](13404.md), [Japan](15573.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Taiwan](25734.md), [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-auto-240][^cite_note-241] Although the announcement did not relate to a Visa Waiver Test *per se*, it showed that the [UK Border Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Border_Agency) considers some countries and territories in the list of visa-free nationalities to be lower risk than others. In particular, [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), which was considered to be a high-risk country from a visa regime perspective in 2008 when the Visa Waiver Test was carried out, was now viewed by the UK Border Agency as a low-risk country. On 5 September 2012, two more countries ([Botswana](3464.md) and [Malaysia](3607937.md)) were added to the list of 'low-risk' nationalities for the purpose of Tier 4 entry clearance applications, i.e. 'Appendix H', (taking effect on 1 October 2012),[^cite_note-changes2012-242] whilst on 6 September 2013, [Barbados](3455.md) was also added to 'Appendix H' (taking effect on 1 October 2013).[^cite_note-243] Again, although the announcement did not relate to a Visa Waiver Test *per se*, it showed that [Botswana](3464.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Barbados](3455.md) (countries which were considered to be a high-risk countries from a visa regime perspective when the Visa Waiver Test was carried out in 2008 in the case of Botswana and Malaysia, and in 2010 in the case of Barbados) were now viewed by the UK Border Agency as low-risk countries.[^cite_note-changes2012-242][^cite_note-auto-240]

In March 2013, it was revealed that [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May), the Home Secretary, was considering removing Brazil from the list of visa-exempt nationalities due to concerns about illegal immigration, since Brazil was fifth in the top 10 of illegal immigrant nationalities in the UK according to Home Office figures for 2011, and was the only country on the list for which short-term visitors do not need a visa. However, the UK Government later decided to retain the visa exemption for Brazilian citizens, a decision which was seen as attempting to develop closer trading links with Brazil.[^cite_note-244]

On 1 January 2014, an electronic visa waiver (EVW) scheme was introduced, enabling citizens of [Oman](22316.md), [Qatar](8238258.md) and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) who have obtained an EVW authorisation online to visit and / or study in the UK for up to 6 months without a visa;[^cite_note-245] with [Kuwait](7515890.md) added to the EVW scheme during February 2016.[^cite_note-246]

After 'assessing countries against a list of risk and compliance criteria', the UK Government added Kuwait, Oman, Qatar and the UAE to 'Appendix H' (the list of 'low-risk' nationalities for the purpose of Tier 4 student visa applications) at various periods between 2014 and 2018 but according to the Cambridge Education Group, Oman was to be removed from this Appendix.[^cite_note-247] However, it doesn't seem like this may have occurred.

These 2 changes reflect the UK Government's view that Kuwait, Oman, Qatar and the UAE should now be regarded as low-risk countries from a visa regime perspective and it is possible that, in future, nationals of these four countries will be classified as non-visa nationals (enabling them to visit and/or study in the UK without a visa for up to 6 months without having to obtain an EVW authorisation online every time they wish to enter the UK).

On 13 March 2014, the UK Government announced that, with effect from 5 May 2014, Venezuelan citizens (including those with [biometric passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_passports)) would require a visa to enter the UK.[^cite_note-HC1138-248][^cite_note-249]

In January 2020, British ambassador to Ukraine announced that there would be no visa-free arrangements for Ukrainian citizens.[^cite_note-250]

On April 9, 2020, the [Home Office](225695.md) issued a new immigration rule imposing visa restriction on low-skilled people workers with effect from January 2021. The restriction introduced a new point-based immigration system, allotting points for certain skills, salaries, qualification and shortage occupations. Any worker with points falling below the given threshold will be restricted from applying for UK work visa, as per the new immigration rule.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252] Free Movement, a UK-based website updating, commenting, training and advising on immigration and asylum laws, claims that nurses, hospital porters, cleaners, postal workers, etc., are to be worst affected by the new immigration law.[^cite_note-253]

In February 2021, the UK's new visa scheme stated that Hong Kong residents with a British National (Overseas) passport can stay in the UK for five years and get full citizenship.[^cite_note-254]

In May 2022, a new visa scheme was offered to graduates from the world's top 50 universities, giving them a two year long work visa with the opportunity to prolong it if they meet certain requirements.[^cite_note-255]

From 4 August 2022, police registration is no longer required.[^cite_note-256]

## Reciprocity

Main article: [Visa requirements for British nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_requirements_for_British_nationals)

Of the countries and territories whose citizens are granted a visa-free stay for up to 6 months in the UK:

- The following fully reciprocate, granting [British citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_citizen) a visa-free stay for up to 6 months: [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra), [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md), [Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas), [Barbados](3455.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), [Hong Kong](13404.md), [Macao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macao), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Monaco](19261.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Panama](22997.md), [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](27228.md), [San Marino](27248.md), [Vatican City](32408.md). Some of these countries require an electronic authorisation before travel: Canada ([eTA](23674739.md) for travel by air, fee of 7 [CAD](101846.md)), New Zealand ([NZeTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Electronic_Travel_Authority), fee of 17 [NZD](161585.md) by app or 23 NZD online, plus [IVL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Visitor_Conservation_and_Tourism_Levy) of 100 NZD).

  [^cite_note-257]

- The following grant a visa-free stay for up to 90 days or 3 months: [Schengen Area](16565139.md), [Argentina](18951905.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), [Chile](5489.md), [Costa Rica](5551.md), [Cyprus](5593.md), [Guatemala](17238567.md), [Guyana](17238691.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Japan](15573.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Nicaragua](21362.md), [Paraguay](23420.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Saint Kitts and Nevis](27198.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [Solomon Islands](265083.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Taiwan](25734.md) (however, British citizens can extend this initial 90 days to 180 days once in Taiwan under the principle of reciprocity because Taiwanese citizens enjoy 180-day visa-free entry to the UK), [United States](3434750.md), [Uruguay](31843.md). Some of these countries require an electronic authorisation before travel: Australia ([eVisitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EVisitor), no fee), Israel ([ETA-IL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_policy_of_Israel), fee of 25 [ILS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_new_shekel)), United States ([ESTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_System_for_Travel_Authorization), fee of 21 [USD](18717338.md)).

- The following grant a visa-free stay for up to 30 days or 1 month: [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), [Kiribati](16674.md), [Maldives](19117.md), [Marshall Islands](19147.md), [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_States_of_Micronesia), [Palau](23372.md), [Seychelles](27288.md), [Tonga](30158.md), [Tuvalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvalu), [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).

- The following grant a visa-free stay for other periods: [Mauritius](19201.md) (60 days for tourism, 90 days for business), [Oman](22316.md) (14 days), [Saint Lucia](27208.md) (6 weeks), [Samoa](27238.md) (60 days).

- The following grant a visa on arrival or electronic visa: [Bahrain](18933277.md) (2 weeks to 6 months, 5 to 65 [BHD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahraini_dinar)), [Kuwait](7515890.md) (90 days, 3 [KWD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwaiti_dinar)), [Papua New Guinea](22943.md) (60 days, 100 [PGK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinean_kina) for tourism or 500 PGK for business), [Qatar](8238258.md) (30 days, no fee), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) (6 months).
