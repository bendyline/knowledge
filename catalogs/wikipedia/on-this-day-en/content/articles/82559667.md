The [2026 season](80906621.md) was the [Louisville Kings](81284343.md)’ first in the [United Football League](75688380.md) (UFL). They were led by first-year head coach [Chris Redman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Redman) and played their home games at [Lynn Family Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Family_Stadium).[^cite_note-1] The Kings finished the regular season with a 6–4 record, securing the No. 3 seed in the playoffs. They went on to win the [2026 United Bowl](83389083.md), defeating the [DC Defenders](59625699.md) 27–20. The Kings became the first team in league history to win a title in its inaugural season.

The Kings began the season 0–3 before defeating the [Houston Gamblers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Houston_Gamblers_season) 24–22 in [overtime](633242.md) to earn their first win. Following the victory, Louisville traded starting quarterback [Jason Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Bean_\(American_football\)), released starting running back [Benny Snell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Snell), and made significant changes along the offensive line. Quarterback [Chandler Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Rogers) then led the Kings to a 6–4 finish, with the team's only loss during that stretch coming against the [St. Louis Battlehawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_St._Louis_Battlehawks_season). The 5–1 stretch included two victories over the defending UFL champions DC Defenders. Under Rogers, Louisville scored at least 30 points in all of its wins, including two games with 42 or more points. The 2026 season saw Louisville continue its rise, clinching a playoff spot on May 30 after the [Birmingham Stallions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Birmingham_Stallions_season) lost to the Gamblers in Week 10, becoming the first team in UFL history to reach the playoffs after an 0–3 start. After defeating the [Columbus Aviators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus_Aviators) in the regular season finale, the Kings also became the first team in league history to finish with a winning record following that start. Louisville then defeated St. Louis 29–20 in the playoffs before going on to win the 2026 United Bowl 27–20 over DC.

Individually, [Cam Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_Gill) was named UFL Defensive Player of the Year and set a league record with 10 sacks.[^cite_note-2] Kicker [Tanner Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanner_Brown) was named UFL Special Teams Player of the Year and set a league record with 25 field goals, converting 89.3% of his attempts.[^cite_note-3]

## Offseason

### Coaching changes

On December 30, 2025, [Chris Redman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Redman) was named the team's inaugural head coach.[^cite_note-4] On February 23, 2026, the Kings announced their full 2026 coaching staff.[^cite_note-5]

On March 31, 2026, following the team's inaugural game, offensive line coach [Breno Giacomini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breno_Giacomini) was fired.[^cite_note-6] On April 3, 2026, his replacement, Charlie Eger, was announced.[^cite_note-7]

The Louisville Kings’ inaugural drive began with a dropped pass that was deflected and intercepted by the Stallions’ [Dyontae Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyontae_Johnson). The Stallions capitalized on the turnover, scoring on their ensuing possession with a five-yard run by [Snoop Conner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snoop_Conner), though the extra point attempt was unsuccessful. Louisville responded on its next drive with a 12-yard touchdown pass from [Jason Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Bean_\(American_football\)) to [Lucky Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_Jackson). The Stallions then had a field goal attempt blocked on their next possession, but later converted a 31-yard field goal by kicker Jonathan Garibay following a Kings punt. With under a minute remaining in the first half, Kings running back [Ian Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Wheeler_\(American_football\)) fumbled at the goal line, and the Stallions maintained a 9–7 lead at halftime. Singer [Russell Dickerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Dickerson) performed during the halftime show.[^cite_note-13]

To start the second half, following a Stallions failed fourth-down conversion, Kings kicker [Tanner Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanner_Brown) made a 37-yard field goal. Louisville then stopped Birmingham on a fourth-down attempt at the 38-yard line. Brown added a 21-yard field goal to extend the Kings’ lead to 13–9 with 8\:42 remaining. On the Stallions’ next drive, [Matt Corral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Corral) threw a 15-yard touchdown pass to [Justyn Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justyn_Ross), but Garibay missed the extra point, giving the Stallions a 15–13 lead with under two minutes remaining. [Jaden Shirden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaden_Shirden) returned the ensuing kickoff to midfield, but Bean was ultimately intercepted by [Hudson Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Clark). The Stallions were forced to punt with under a minute remaining, but their defense held Louisville, securing the victory.

After both teams exchanged field goals in the first quarter, Kings quarterback [Jason Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Bean_\(American_football\)) was replaced by backup quarterback [Chandler Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Rogers), who led the team to two additional field goals by [Tanner Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanner_Brown), giving the Kings a 9–6 lead.[^cite_note-14] Late in the first half, Storm starting quarterback [Jack Plummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Plummer) left the game due to injury. His replacement, [Dorian Thompson-Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_Thompson-Robinson), made his UFL debut and completed two of three passes for 19 yards before Plummer returned on a third-and-19 play, connecting with wide receiver [K. J. Hamler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._J._Hamler) for a 41-yard touchdown reception with under a minute remaining. The extra point was missed, and Orlando led 12–9 at halftime.

After a scoreless third quarter, Plummer found wide receiver [Elijhah Badger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijhah_Badger) for a 39-yard touchdown pass. Following a near interception return touchdown by [Mishael Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishael_Powell) off Rogers, Kings cornerback [Deantre Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deantre_Prince) intercepted Plummer in the end zone, giving the Kings one final opportunity. Bean re-entered the game at quarterback, but the offense was unable to score and was shut out in the second half. The Kings ultimately lost 19–9, finishing with just 27 rushing yards on 13 carries and 216 yards of total offense.[^cite_note-15]

In a rematch from the previous week, the Storm took a 10–6 lead into halftime, highlighted by an 8-yard touchdown pass from [Jack Plummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Plummer) to [Chris Rowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Rowland). Kings quarterback [Jason Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Bean_\(American_football\)) made his third consecutive start after being benched the prior week and played most of the first half, briefly exiting for a few plays due to injury.

To open the second half, Bean connected with [Zach Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Davidson) on a 23-yard touchdown pass, marking the Kings’ first touchdown since the first quarter of their season opener and ending a scoring drought of over 100 minutes of game time. Orlando responded with a 20-yard touchdown pass from Plummer to [Jashaun Corbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jashaun_Corbin). Plummer later added another second-half touchdown pass to [K. J. Hamler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._J._Hamler). Bean answered with three second-half touchdown passes of his own, including one to [Lucky Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_Jackson) and a second to Davidson in the final two minutes to tie the game at 27–27.

After neither team scored in the best-of-three [shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout), the Kings were stopped on their fourth [sudden-death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_death_\(sport\)) attempt. ### Matchup

The matchup marked the third meeting between the two teams during the season, with Louisville winning the previous two contests. DC made its third championship game appearance in franchise history and its second in the UFL. Although the game was originally designated as a neutral-site championship, it was held at the home stadium of the Defenders. The Defenders entered the game without [2025 UFL championship game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UFL_championship_game) MVP quarterback [Jordan Ta'amu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Ta'amu), who suffered a torn [ACL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anterior_cruciate_ligament_injury) during a Week 8 loss to the Kings.[^cite_note-41] Following an ineffective performance by backup quarterback [Spencer Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Sanders) against Orlando, DC turned to third-string quarterback [Jason Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Bean_\(American_football\)) for its Week 10 matchup against the Storm, a game the Defenders lost. Bean, however, later led DC to an upset playoff victory over top-seeded Orlando after the Storm had defeated the Defenders in each of their previous two meetings. Bean began the season as Louisville's starting quarterback, starting the first four games before being traded to DC in exchange for [Mike DiLiello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_DiLiello). The move opened the door for [Chandler Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Rogers), who went 5–1 as Louisville's starter during the regular season before leading the Kings to a playoff victory over the [St. Louis Battlehawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Battlehawks). The championship game also marked the first on-field meeting between Bean and Rogers, who were teammates at [Lake Ridge High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ridge_High_School) in [Mansfield, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield,_Texas). The only previous collegiate connection between the two came in 2019, when Rogers was redshirting at [Southern Miss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Miss_Golden_Eagles_football) and Bean served as a backup at [North Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Texas_Mean_Green_football). Rogers did not appear in the game, while Bean threw a touchdown pass in Southern Mississippi's 45–27 victory.[^cite_note-42]

#### First half

Following punts on both opening possessions, scoring opened with a [Matt McCrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_McCrane) 48-yard field goal for DC. After Louisville's second consecutive punt, DC running back [Xazavian Valladay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xazavian_Valladay) broke free for a 54-yard run before being chased down and tackled by Keaton Ellis, who [forced a fumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_Punch_\(football\)) that bounced 35 yards into the end zone where [Cameron Dantzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Dantzler) recovered for a Louisville touchback.

Early in the second quarter, Rogers was intercepted by [Kai Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Gray). On DC's ensuing possession, a completion from Bean to [Ty Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Scott) was forced loose by Corey Mayfield, and the fumble was recovered and returned 12 yards for a Louisville touchdown by [Eric Garror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Garror), giving the Kings a 7–3 lead. Valladay later redeemed himself on DC's next drive, scoring on a 51-yard touchdown run to regain the lead. Following a Louisville punt, McCrane added a 57-yard field goal for DC. On Louisville's next possession, Rogers was intercepted again by Gray, setting up a 28-yard McCrane field goal that extended DC's lead to 16–7 at halftime. Despite both quarterbacks being held to fewer than 50 passing yards, the Defenders outgained the Kings 227–62 and held a 209–41 advantage on the ground, while both teams committed two turnovers. Artist [50 Cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50_Cent) performed the halftime show.[^cite_note-43]

#### Second half

Louisville scored its first offensive points of the contest on its opening drive with a [Tanner Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanner_Brown) 57-yard field goal, set up by a 19-yard run from [James Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Robinson_\(running_back\)). On DC's ensuing possession, Bean suffered a left [shoulder separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_separation) on an incomplete third-down pass attempt, ruling him out for the remainder of the game. Brown later cut the deficit to three points with his second field goal of the game, a 38-yard kick. DC then turned to quarterback [E. J. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._J._Perry_\(American_football\)), who had been signed on May 25 and had not appeared in the Week 10 game or playoffs to that point. Following a DC three-and-out, Louisville running back [Ian Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Wheeler_\(American_football\)) broke free for a 44-yard go-ahead touchdown run on the first play of the fourth quarter, his eighth touchdown of the season.

After generating just six offensive yards in the third quarter, DC's ensuing drive was derailed when Perry's long completion to [Cornell Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_Powell) was negated by offensive pass interference. On the same drive, Perry was shortly afterwards intercepted on a deep [post route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_\(gridiron_football\)) by Dantzler, who returned it 47 yards to the DC 25-yard line. Two plays later, Robinson broke multiple tackles on a 16-yard touchdown run, extending Louisville's lead to 27–16 with just over 12 minutes remaining. A strong kickoff return by Valladay to midfield sparked a promising drive, but multiple penalties stalled the possession. McCrane salvaged the drive with a 60-yard four-point field goal, his fourth of the season, cutting the deficit to 27–20 with just over nine minutes remaining. Following a Louisville three-and-out, Perry led DC inside the red zone. On fourth down from inside the Kings’ 10-yard line, his pass sailed over Powell and fell incomplete with under two minutes remaining. With no timeouts remaining, DC was unable to stop the clock, and Louisville ran out the clock to secure a 27–20 victory and the championship. The Kings finished the season winning eight of their final nine games.[^cite_note-44]

Running back Ian Wheeler was named the game's most valuable player, rushing for 81 yards on 10 carries, including the go-ahead 44-yard touchdown run in the fourth quarter. He also returned a kickoff for 28 yards.[^cite_note-45]
