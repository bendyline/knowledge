**42nd Street** is a major crosstown street in the [New York City](645042.md) borough of [Manhattan](45470.md), spanning the entire breadth of [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), from [Turtle Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle_Bay,_Manhattan) at the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River), to [Hell's Kitchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell's_Kitchen,_Manhattan) at the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) on the [West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_\(Manhattan\)). The street has several major landmarks, including (from [east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Side_\(Manhattan\)) to west) the [headquarters of the United Nations](188955.md), the [Chrysler Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Building), [Grand Central Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Terminal), the [New York Public Library Main Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Public_Library_Main_Branch), [Times Square](31389.md), and the [Port Authority Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_Bus_Terminal).

The street is known for its [theaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater), especially near the intersection with [Broadway](4626.md) at [Times Square](31389.md), and as such is also the name of the region of the [theater district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_District,_Manhattan) (and, at times, the [red-light district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-light_district)) near that intersection. The street also has a section of [off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Broadway) theaters known as [Theatre Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Row_\(New_York_City\)).

## History

### Early history

During the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), a cornfield near 42nd Street and [Fifth Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue) was where General [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) angrily attempted to rally his troops after the British [landing at Kip's Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_at_Kip's_Bay), which scattered many of the American militiamen. Washington's attempt put him in danger of being captured, and his officers had to persuade him to leave. The rout eventually subsided into an orderly retreat.[^cite_note-2]

[John Jacob Astor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jacob_Astor) purchased a 70-acre (28 ha) farm in 1803 that ran from 42nd Street to 46th Street west of Broadway to the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River).[^cite_note-3]

### 19th century

The street was designated by the [Commissioners' Plan of 1811](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioners'_Plan_of_1811) that established the [Manhattan](45470.md) [street grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan) as one of 15 crosstown (east-west) streets that would be 100 feet (30 m) in width, while other streets were designated as 60 feet (18 m) in width.[^cite_note-4]

In 1835, the city's Street Committee, after receiving numerous complaints about lack of access for development above 14th Street, decided to open up all lots which had already been plotted on the city grid up to 42nd Street, which thus became – for a time – the northern boundary of the city.[^cite_note-5]

[Cornelius Vanderbilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Vanderbilt) began the construction of [Grand Central Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Depot) in 1869 on 42nd Street at Fourth Avenue as the terminal for his Central, [Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Line_\(Metro-North\)), [Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_Line) and [New Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven_Line) commuter rail lines, because city regulations required that trains be pulled by horse below 42nd Street.[^cite_note-6] The Depot, which opened in 1871, was replaced by [Grand Central Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Terminal) in 1913.[^cite_note-7]

Between the 1870s and 1890s, 42nd Street became the uptown boundary of the [mainstream theater district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_District,_Manhattan), which started around 23rd Street, as the entertainment district of the [Tenderloin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenderloin,_Manhattan) gradually moved northward.[^cite_note-8]

### Early 20th century

42nd Street was developed relatively late compared to other crosstown thoroughfares such as [14th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Street_\(Manhattan\)) and [23rd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Street_\(Manhattan\)), which had grown during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), and [57th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_Street_\(Manhattan\)), which became prominent in the 1890s. It was only after the beginning of the 20th century that the street saw entertainment venues being developed around [Times Square](31389.md) and upscale office space around Grand Central Terminal.[^cite_note-Stern_(1995)_p._452-9] In the first two decades of the 20th century, eleven venues for [legitimate theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimate_theater) were built within one block of West 42nd Street between Seventh and Eighth Avenues.[^cite_note-p1505606157-10]

The corner of 42nd Street and [Broadway](4626.md), at the southeast corner of Times Square, is the eastern terminus of the [Lincoln Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Highway), the first road across the United States, which was conceived and mapped in 1913.

An [elevated railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevated_railway) line, running above East 42nd Street from [Third Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) to the [Grand Central station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_station_\(IRT_42nd_Street_Branch\)), was closed in 1923,[^cite_note-11] leading to the development of such structures as the [Chanin Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanin_Building) and [110 East 42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/110_East_42nd_Street) west of [Lexington Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington_Avenue). The street east of Lexington Avenue continued to be made up of mostly low-rise buildings; these blocks were adjacent to the elevated [IRT Second Avenue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Second_Avenue_Line) and [IRT Third Avenue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Third_Avenue_Line), and accordingly, initially considered unattractive for major development.[^cite_note-12] By the 1920s, *The New York Times* reported that several high-rise developments were "radically changing the old-time conditions" along East 42nd Street,[^cite_note-13] including the Chanin, [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Grand_Central_Place), [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Building), and [Daily News Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_News_Building), as well as [Tudor City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_City).[^cite_note-14]

The block of 42nd Street between [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) and [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) Avenues was originally only 40 feet (12 m) wide, passing through a steep bluff known as Prospect Hill.[^cite_note-nyt-1949-12-22-15][^cite_note-p1336513318-16] On either side of the street, 30-foot-wide (9.1 m) service roads ascended to Tudor City Place, which crossed over 42nd Street.[^cite_note-p1336513318-16] To improve access to the newly developed [United Nations headquarters](188955.md), in 1948, the city government proposed widening that block of 42nd Street, eliminating the service roads, and constructing a viaduct to carry Tudor City Place over 42nd Street.[^cite_note-nyt-1949-12-22-15][^cite_note-p1327415594-17] Despite opposition from Tudor City residents,[^cite_note-nyt-1948-07-23-18] city officials said the street widening was necessary because 42nd Street already carried high amounts of vehicular traffic to and from the nearby [FDR Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDR_Drive).[^cite_note-nyt-1949-12-22-15] The [New York City Planning Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Planning_Commission) approved the plans in September 1948,[^cite_note-nyt-1948-09-17-19][^cite_note-p1326785598-20] and the Board of Estimate approved \$1.848 million for the project that December.[^cite_note-p1336513318-16][^cite_note-nyt-1948-12-17-21] The board provisionally authorized the street widening in June 1949, and Manhattan's borough president announced in December 1949 that work would commence shortly.[^cite_note-p13268250092-22][^cite_note-nyt-1949-12-22-15] The neighboring stretch of 42nd Street was temporarily closed from February 1951 to October 1952 while the widening was underway.[^cite_note-nyt-1952-10-022-23]

### Theatrical decline

West 42nd Street, meanwhile, prospered as a theater and entertainment district until [World War II](32927.md). According to historian [Robert A. M. Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._M._Stern), West 42nd Street's decline started in 1946, when the streetcars on 42nd Street were replaced by less efficient buses.[^cite_note-Stern_(1995)_p._452-9]

[Lloyd Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Bacon) and [Busby Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busby_Berkeley)'s 1933 film musical *[42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_\(film\))*, starring 30s heartthrobs [Dick Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Powell) and [Ruby Keeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Keeler), displays the bawdy and colorful mixture of [Broadway](725252.md) denizens and lowlifes in Manhattan during the [Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression). In 1980, it was turned into a successful [Broadway musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_\(musical\)) which ran until 1989, and which was revived for a four-year run in 2001.[^cite_note-24] In the words of the [Al Dubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Dubin) and [Harry Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Warren) [title song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_\(song\)), on 42nd Street one could find:

> Little nifties from the Fifties, innocent and sweet,
>
> Sexy ladies from the Eighties who are indiscreet,
>
> They're side by side, they're glorified,
>
> Where the underworld can meet the elite
>
> &#x20;Naughty, gawdy, bawdy, sporty, Forty-second Street!

From the late 1950s until the late 1980s, 42nd Street, nicknamed the "Deuce",[^cite_note-allen-25]<sup>: 63 </sup> was the cultural center of American [grindhouse theaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grindhouse), which spawned an entire subculture. The book *Sleazoid Express*, a travelogue of the 42nd Street grindhouses and the films they showed, describes the unique blend of people who made up the theater-goers:

> depressives hiding from jobs, sexual obsessives, inner-city people seeking cheap diversions, teenagers skipping school, adventurous couples on dates, couples-chasers peeking on them, people getting high, homeless people sleeping, pickpockets...[^cite_note-sleazoid-26]

While the street outside the theaters was populated with:

> phony drug salesman ... low-level drug dealers, chain snatchers ... \[j]unkies alone in their [heroin](14034.md)/[cocaine](7701.md) dreamworld ... predatory chickenhawks spying on underage trade looking for pickups ... [male prostitutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male_prostitutes) of all ages ... \[t]ranssexuals, hustlers, and closety gays with a fetishistic homo- or heterosexual itch to scratch ... It was common to see [porn stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porn_stars) whose films were playing at the adult houses promenade down the block. ... Were you a freak? Not when you stepped onto the Deuce. Being a freak there would get you money, attention, entertainment, a starring part in a movie. Or maybe a robbery and a beating.[^cite_note-sleazoid-26]

For much of the mid and late 20th century, the area of 42nd Street near Times Square was home to activities often considered unsavory,[^cite_note-27] including [peep shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peep_show).

East 42nd Street was, for some time, spared from similar decline, especially east of Third Avenue, where the development of the [United Nations](188955.md) supported a thriving business district and prompted the widening of that section of 42nd Street.[^cite_note-Stern_(1995)_p._452-9][^cite_note-nyt-1949-12-22-15] The demolition of the Second and Third Avenue elevated lines by the 1950s led to increased development on East 42nd Street, such as annexes to the Chrysler and Daily News Buildings, as well as the construction of the [Socony-Mobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socony-Mobil_Building) and [Ford Foundation Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation_Building).[^cite_note-28] By the 1960s, East 42nd Street between Park and Second Avenues contained more headquarters of industries than any other place in the United States except Chicago or Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-Stern_(1995)_p._457-29][^cite_note-30] During this time, there was much development outside the rundown entertainment district of Times Square, somewhat offsetting the perception of that part of 42nd Street.[^cite_note-31]

### Revitalization



Tourists observing [Manhattanhenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattanhenge), blocking the entire intersection of 42nd Street and the [Avenue of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenue_of_the_Americas), looking westward at the [sunset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset) on July 12, 2016

In the early 1990s, the city government encouraged a cleanup of the Times Square area. In 1990, the city government took over six of the historic theaters on the block of 42nd Street between [Seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) and [Eighth Avenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)), and [New 42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_42nd_Street), a [not-for-profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not-for-profit) organization, was formed to oversee their renovation and reuse, as well as to construct new theaters and a rehearsal space. In 1993, [Disney Theatrical Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Theatrical_Productions) bought the [New Amsterdam Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam_Theatre), which it renovated a few years later. Since the mid-1990s, the block has again become home to [mainstream theaters](725252.md) and several multi-screen mainstream movie theaters, along with shops, restaurants, hotels, and attractions such as [Madame Tussauds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Tussauds) wax museum and [Ripley's Believe It or Not](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripley's_Believe_It_or_Not) that draw millions to the city every year. This area is now co-signed as "New 42nd Street" to signify this change.

In the 1990s, the renovation of [Bryant Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryant_Park) between Fifth and Sixth Avenues, as well as the renovations of Times Square and Grand Central Terminal, led to increases in office occupancy along both sections of 42nd Street.[^cite_note-32]

In 2025, the [New York City Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Transportation) announced that the block of 42nd Street between Seventh and Eighth Avenues would be upgraded to protect it from terrorist attacks. The project, budgeted at \$57 million, would include adding steel bollards, installing sewage pipes, and widening the sidewalks and bus lanes.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

There are two subway lines under 42nd Street. The [42nd Street Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_Shuttle) ([S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_Shuttle) train) runs under 42nd Street between Broadway/Seventh Avenue ([Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_\(IRT_42nd_Street_Shuttle\))) and [Park Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) ([Grand Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_\(IRT_42nd_Street_Shuttle\))). The [IRT Flushing Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Flushing_Line) ([7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) and [<7>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7d_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\))​ trains) curves from [Eleventh Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) to 41st Street, under which it runs until [Fifth Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue); shifts to 42nd Street between Fifth and [Madison Avenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Avenue); and continues under the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River) to [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens). Each line stops at Times Square and Grand Central, though the [Fifth Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue_\(IRT_Flushing_Line\)) station is also served by the 7 and <7>​ trains.[^cite_note-submap-36]

In the past, every former IRT elevated line had a station at 42nd Street:

- [42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_station_\(IRT_Second_Avenue_Line\)) on the [IRT Second Avenue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Second_Avenue_Line)

- [42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_station_\(IRT_Third_Avenue_Line\)) on the [IRT Third Avenue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Third_Avenue_Line)

- [42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_station_\(IRT_Sixth_Avenue_Line\)) on the [IRT Sixth Avenue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Sixth_Avenue_Line)

- [42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_station_\(IRT_Ninth_Avenue_Line\)) on the [IRT Ninth Avenue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Ninth_Avenue_Line)

A [fifth station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_station_\(IRT_42nd_Street_Branch\)) extended over 42nd Street as a western spur from the Third Avenue Line to [Grand Central Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Depot), later Grand Central Station, and finally Grand Central Terminal.

### Bus

[MTA Regional Bus Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTA_Regional_Bus_Operations)'s [M42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M42_\(New_York_City_bus\)) bus runs the length of 42nd Street between the [Circle Line Sightseeing Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Line_Sightseeing_Cruises) ferry terminal on the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) and the [headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_headquarters) of the [United Nations](31769.md) on the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River). Westbound service originates at First Avenue.[^cite_note-37] Its predecessor, the [42nd Street Crosstown Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_Crosstown_Line_\(Manhattan_surface\)) streetcar, had used 42nd Street until 1946.[^cite_note-nyt-2005-04-18-38][^cite_note-39] In 2019, [bus lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_lanes_in_New_York_City) were installed along the length of the street.[^cite_note-40] There have also been proposals for light rail on 42nd Street over the years, such as in 2005.[^cite_note-nyt-2005-04-18-38]

42nd Street is also used by the [SIM8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM8_\(New_York_City_bus\)), [SIM22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM22_\(New_York_City_bus\)), [SIM25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM25_\(New_York_City_bus\)), [SIM26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM26_\(New_York_City_bus\)) and [SIM30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM30_\(New_York_City_bus\)) Staten Island express buses.[^cite_note-41]

## In popular culture

"Forty-deuce" is street slang for Manhattan's former live peep show district on 42nd Street. The 1982 film *[Forty Deuce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forty_Deuce)* and the 2017 TV series *[The Deuce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Deuce_\(TV_series\))* reference the phrase "forty-deuce".[^cite_note-42]

Notable CCP Politburo Standing Committee member [Wang Huning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Huning) referenced 42nd Street in his book [America Against America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_Against_America) mentioning its "pornographic culture" as an example of the "decadence of high commodification".[^cite_note-43]

An unspecified 42nd Street Subway Station is featured in *[Street Fighter III: Third Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_III:_Third_Strike)* as the stage, "Subway Station". Two versions of this stage exist for the characters [Alex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_\(Street_Fighter_character\)) and [Ken Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Masters). Alex's version is in a bright orange tint and features construction equipment,[^cite_note-44] while Ken's version has a blue tint and is clear of equipment.[^cite_note-45] The famous [Evo Moment 37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evo_Moment_37) occurred on the Subway Station stage since Ken was the challenging player.[^cite_note-46]
