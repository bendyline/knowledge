**Civilian checkpoints** or **security checkpoints** are distinguishable from [border](288329.md) or frontier checkpoints in that they are erected and enforced within contiguous areas under [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) or [paramilitary](146730.md) control. Civilian checkpoints have been employed within conflict-ridden areas all over the world to monitor and control the movement of people and materials in order to prevent [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence). They have also been used by [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) during peacetime to help counter terrorism.

## Contemporary examples

Though practices and enforcement vary, checkpoints have been used in:

- [Airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) and other transportation hubs across the world, including those managed by the [TSA](172933.md) in the [United States](3434750.md).

- Post [World War II](32927.md) checkpoints in Germany

- The former [Yugoslavia](34244.md) during the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars).

- [Northern Ireland](21265.md) by the [Official IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_IRA), [Provisional IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_IRA), [Irish National Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_National_Liberation_Army), and [Real IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_IRA) as well as by the [British Army](4887.md), [Royal Ulster Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Ulster_Constabulary), [Police Service of Northern Ireland](294068.md) and also by the [Ulster Defense Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Defense_Association) and the [Ulster Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Volunteer_Force_\(1966\)).

- [Colombia](5222.md), by military and paramilitary forces.

- [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories), by the [Israeli Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Defence_Force).

- [Mexico](3966054.md), by [military](23384265.md) and [police forces](9964117.md).

- Since the terrorist bombings in [Pakistan](23235.md), they are widely seen across all over [Pakistan](23235.md) specially on entrance and exit points of big cities.

- [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana), by the [National Gendarmerie](6330819.md).

- [Riau Islands](453745.md), by military and paramilitary forces.

## Advantages

Checkpoints provide many advantages, including the ability to control how people enter so that security personnel (be it governmental or civilian) can screen entrants to identify known [troublemakers](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/troublemaker) (be they [criminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal), [terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist), or simple rabble-rousers) and locate contraband items.

## Effects of checkpoints

Checkpoints typically lead to hardship for the affected civilians, though these effects range from inconvenience to mortal danger. [Birzeit University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birzeit_University), for example, has conducted several studies highlighting the effects of checkpoints in the Palestinian territories.[^cite_note-birzeit1-1][^cite_note-2]

In [Colombia](5222.md), the paramilitary forces of the [AUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Self-Defense_Forces_of_Colombia) have, according to [Amnesty International](18947898.md), imposed limits on the food entering villages, with over 30 people being killed at the checkpoint in one instance.[^cite_note-3]

In Ethiopia's [Amhara region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amhara_region), checkpoints have functioned as shifting sites of control during the civil conflict since 2020. Their operation has often been unpredictable, involving both state and non-state actors, and affecting not only mobility but also the daily experience of authority and fear.[^cite_note-4]
