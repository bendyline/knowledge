The **Norway national football team** ([Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language): *Norges herrelandslag i fotball*, or informally *Landslaget*) represents [Norway](21241.md) in men's international [football](10568.md), and is controlled by the [Norwegian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Football_Federation), the governing body for [football in Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Norway). Norway's home ground is [Ullevaal Stadion](712728.md) in [Oslo](22309.md), and their head coach is [Ståle Solbakken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A5le_Solbakken). Norway has participated in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) four times ([1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2026](36581929.md)) and once in the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) ([2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000)). After Euro 2000, Norway failed to qualify for the next twelve major tournaments from 2001 to 2025.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The team underwent their deepest run at a World Cup, advancing to the quarter-finals for the [first time in their history](31646563.md) at the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## History

Main article: [History of the Norway national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Norway_national_football_team)

The Norwegian team achieved third place in the [1936 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics), after beating hosts [Germany](250204.md) earlier in the tournament. Norway also qualified for the [1938 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), where they lost 2–1 after extra time against eventual champions [Italy](362466.md), with this being their last appearance until [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup).

In the post-war years, Norway did not qualify for a World Cup or European Championship. Despite this, Norway achieved victories such as a 3–0 win against [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) in 1965, a 1–0 away win against [France](250197.md) in 1968, and a 2–1 victory against [England](9904.md) in 1981 that prompted radio commentator [Bjørge Lillelien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rge_Lillelien)'s famous "Your boys took a hell of a beating" rant.[^cite_note-8]

Norway had their first football's [Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_age_\(metaphor\)) from 1990 to 1998 under coach [Egil "Drillo" Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egil_Olsen), with the team once being [ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Men's_World_Rankings) No. 2. Olsen started his training career with Norway with a 6–1 home victory against [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) on 31 October 1990. In [qualifying for the 1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), Norway topped their group, finishing above the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) and England, beating both teams in the process. During the [finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) in the United States, Norway was knocked out in the group stage after a win against [Mexico](808402.md), a defeat against Italy and a draw against the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team). Norway failed to qualify for the round of 16 as all four teams in the group finished with four points and identical goal difference.

At the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) in France, Norway was once again eliminated by Italy in the first round of the knockout stage after finishing second in their group, having drawn against [Morocco](1024865.md) and [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) and winning 2–1 against [Brazil](149286.md).

Former under-21 coach [Nils Johan Semb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nils_Johan_Semb) replaced Olsen after the latter's retirement. Under Semb's guidance, Norway qualified for [Euro 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), their last European Championship appearance to date. Semb resigned at the end of an unsuccessful qualifying campaign in 2003 and was replaced by [Åge Hareide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85ge_Hareide). Under Hareide, Norway came close to reaching both the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) and [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), but ultimately fell short on both occasions. Norway then failed to win a single game in the entirety of 2008, with Hareide resigning at the end of the year. His replacement, initially on a temporary basis, was Olsen, who began his second spell in charge with an away win against Germany, and subsequently signed a three-year contract. Olsen resigned in September 2013[^cite_note-9] after Norway lost at home to [Switzerland](681862.md) and had limited chances to qualify for the [2014 World Cup](656933.md) with one game to spare. He was replaced with [Per-Mathias Høgmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per-Mathias_H%C3%B8gmo). Olsen later claimed he was sacked.[^cite_note-10]

### 2020–present: Rise to Prominence

During the qualification campaign for [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), Norway finished third in their [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying_Group_F) behind Spain and Sweden, missing direct qualification for the tournament. The team subsequently contested a playoff semifinal against Serbia in October 2020, but was defeated 1–2 and eliminated from contention. Norway then participated in the [2020–21 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Nations_League) alongside Austria, Romania, and Northern Ireland, securing second place in the group and thereby retaining its position in [League B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Nations_League_B) for the following edition.

On 3 December 2020, former national team player [Ståle Solbakken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A5le_Solbakken) was appointed head coach following the departure of [Lars Lagerbäck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Lagerb%C3%A4ck). In March 2021, Solbakken named [Martin Ødegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_%C3%98degaard) as team captain. Norway failed to qualify for both the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) and [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024). In the [World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_G), they finished third in a group with the Netherlands and Turkey. Later, in the [Euro 2024 qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying_Group_A), they ended behind Spain and Scotland, missing out again. At the [2024–25 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League), Norway clinched direct promotion to [League A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_UEFA_Nations_League_A) after topping their group.



Norway in 2025

On 16 November 2025, Norway secured qualification for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), ending a 28-year absence from the tournament. The team won all eight of its qualifying matches and finished first in its [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_I). The campaign concluded with a 4–1 victory over [Italy](362466.md) in Milan, marking Italy's first-ever home defeat in a World Cup qualifying group-stage match. [Erling Haaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erling_Haaland) emerged as the [leading scorer](49459509.md#Top_goalscorers) across all qualifiers with 16 goals. For the tournament, Norway was drawn into [Group I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_I) alongside Iraq, Senegal, and France.

On 17 June 2026, Norway opened its World Cup campaign with a 4–1 victory over [Iraq](1039652.md), highlighted by two goals from Haaland, who became the first Norwegian player to score multiple goals in a World Cup match. It was also the first time Norway had scored more than two goals in a World Cup fixture. The team followed up with a 3–2 win against [Senegal](1017436.md), securing advancement to the [Round of 32](36581929.md#Round_of_32) prior to its final group-stage match against [France](250197.md). They were defeated 4-1 in a dead rubber, marking the first ever time they lost to a World Cup match to an opponent other than Italy (who failed to qualify for the tournament after losing on penalties to Bosnia and Herzegovina in the qualification playoffs). In the Round of 32, Norway won 2-1 against [Ivory Coast](1039856.md) and advanced to the Round of 16 match against Brazil. This was the first time Norway won a match outside the group stages. Norway is one of only three national teams with a winning record against [Brazil](149286.md), and the only team to have never lost against them, with three wins and two draws in five matches, including their 2026 round of 16 match.[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-12] The run ended in the quarter finals, where Norway lost 1–2 to [England](9904.md) after extra time.[^cite_note-13]

## Team image

### Crest



Original badge of the Norwegian national team

Norway has used the [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Norway) on a white circle as their badge since the 1920s. In May 2008, the [NFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Football_Federation) unveiled a new crest, a [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings)-style Dragon wrapped around the NFF logo. After massive public pressure the crest was dropped.[^cite_note-14] In the 1980s and the 1990s, Norway used the NFF logo in the opposite breast of the shirt together with the national flag on a white circle. On 12 December 2014, a new crest was presented. The crest primarily features the national flag; in addition, there are two lions taken from the [Norwegian coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Norway) on the top. The lions face each other while holding a blue miniature of the NFF logo, and between the lions and above the NFF logo is the word "NORGE" (Norway) in blue letters.[^cite_note-15] They also wore a special gold version of their logo against Italy on 16 November 2025, to commemorate their qualification for World Cup 2026 after a 28-year wait.
