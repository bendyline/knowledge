The **Royal Netherlands Navy** (**RNLN**) (Dutch: *Koninklijke Marine*, pronounced [\[ˈkoːnɪŋkləkə maːˈrinə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch), meaning 'Royal Navy') is the [maritime service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) branch of the [Netherlands Armed Forces](21162.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] It traces its history to 8 January 1488,[^cite_note-5] making it the [third-oldest navy in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_navies).

During the 17th and early 18th centuries, the [Dutch States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_States_Navy) was one of the most powerful navies in the world and played an active role in the [Anglo-Dutch Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Wars), [Franco-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Dutch_War), [Nine Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Years'_War) and [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession). However, by the late 18th century it had declined through neglect and was no longer a match for either the [British](26061.md) or [French navies](383256.md). The [Batavian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Navy) and [navy of the Kingdom of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_of_the_Kingdom_of_Holland) played an active role in the [French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_and_Napoleonic_Wars), though both were repeatedly yoked to French interests.

Officially formed in 1813 after the [Sovereign Principality of the United Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_Principality_of_the_United_Netherlands) was established, the Royal Netherlands Navy played an important role in protecting the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md), and would play a minor role in [World War II](32927.md), where it fought against the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy). Since [World War II](32927.md), the Royal Netherlands Navy has taken part in several peacekeeping missions, and frequently participates in [European Union](9317.md) and [NATO](21133.md) operations and exercises.

## Ship prefixes

The international prefix for ships of the Royal Netherlands Navy is HNLMS (*His/Her Netherlands Majesty's Ship*).[^cite_note-6] The Netherlands navy itself uses the prefixes Zr.Ms. ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Zijner Majesteits*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'His Majesty's') when a King is on the throne, and Hr.Ms. ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Harer Majesteits*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Her Majesty's') when there is a Queen. Changes happen automatically at the end of a monarch's reign.[^cite_note-7]

## History

Main article: [Naval history of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_history_of_the_Netherlands)

The modern Netherlands Navy dates its founding to a "statute of admiralty" issued by [Maximilian, King of the Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) (future Holy Roman Emperor Maximilian I), and his son [Philip the Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_I_of_Castile), the ruler of [Burgundian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundian_Netherlands) lands (a minor at that time) on 8 January 1488.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-Foundingdate-9]

### Netherlands Golden Age



The [Battle of Scheveningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Scheveningen) in 1653 during the [First Anglo-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Dutch_War).

The Netherlands navy was involved in several wars against other European powers from the late 16th century, initially for independence against Spain in European waters, later for shipping lanes, trade and colonies in many parts of the world, notably during the [Anglo-Dutch Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Wars). During the 17th century the [Dutch States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_States_Navy) was one of the most powerful navies in the world. As an organization, the Dutch navy consisted of five separate admiralties (three of them in [Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland), and one each in [Friesland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesland) and [Zeeland](52229.md)), each with its own ships, personnel, shipyards, command structures and revenues.[^cite_note-10]

### World War II



[HNLMS *Java*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Java_\(1921\)), ca. 1941.

Elements of the Royal Netherlands Navy on manoeuvres, 1936.



[Piet de Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_de_Jong), commanding officer of [HNLMS *Gelderland*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Gelderland_\(D811\)) in 1958.

At the start of WWII the Dutch had five cruisers, eight destroyers, 24 submarines, and smaller vessels, along with 50 aircraft. The Netherlands was conquered in 1940 by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in a matter of days, and two Dutch [light cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cruiser) and one [destroyer leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer_leader) and three [destroyers](8779.md) that were under construction were captured in their [shipyard](213654.md).

For the rest of the war, the Dutch navy was based in [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) countries: the Dutch navy had its headquarters in [London](17867.md), and smaller units in [Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ceylon) (modern day [Sri Lanka](26750.md)) and [Western Australia](33613.md). Around the world Dutch naval units were responsible for transporting troops, for example during [Operation Dynamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dynamo) at Dunkirk and on [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day), they escorted convoys and attacked enemy targets. Dutch submarines scored some victories, including one on a *[Kriegsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriegsmarine)* [U-boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat) [*U-95*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_submarine_U-95_\(1940\)) in the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), which was sunk by [*O 21*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_O_21), but during the war the Dutch Navy suffered heavy losses, particularly in the [Pacific Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_campaign).

A small force of [submarines](28825.md) based in Western Australia sank more Japanese ships in the first weeks after Japan joined the war than the entire British and American navies together during the same period, an exploit which earned [Admiral Helfrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Helfrich) the nickname "Ship-a-day Helfrich".[^cite_note-Dutchman-11] The aggressive pace of operations against the Japanese was a contributing factor to both the heavy losses sustained and the greater number of successes scored as compared to the British and Americans in the region.

But during the relentless Japanese offensive of February through April 1942 in the Dutch East Indies, the Dutch navy in Asia was virtually annihilated, particularly in the [Battle of the Java Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Java_Sea) (27 February 1942) in which the commander, [Karel Doorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Doorman), went down with his fleet along with 1,000 sailors. The Navy sustained losses of a total of 20 ships (including two of its three light cruisers) and 2,500 sailors killed in the course of the campaign.[^cite_note-12] The Dutch navy had suffered from years of underfunding and came ill-prepared to face an enemy with more and heavier ships with better weapons, including the [Long Lance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Lance)-torpedo, with which the cruiser [*Haguro*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cruiser_Haguro) sank the light cruiser [HNLMS *De Ruyter*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_De_Ruyter_\(1935\)).[^cite_note-13]

### Decolonization

Main articles: [Indonesian National Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution) and [West New Guinea dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_New_Guinea_dispute)

After the war, relations between the Netherlands and its colonial empire changed dramatically. The establishment of the [Republic of Indonesia](14579.md), two days after the Japanese surrender, thwarted Dutch plans for restoring colonial authority. The navy made a modest contribution to the [Dutch police actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Actions_\(Indonesia\)) and after four years of conflict, the Netherlands [ceded sovereignty to Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch%E2%80%93Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference) in 1949.

Elements of the Dutch navy remained stationed in Indonesia for the [Dutch Military Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Mission_for_Indonesia) until 1954 and in [Dutch New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_New_Guinea) until the [New York Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Agreement) came into effect in 1962. The latter had been preceded by a [combined Soviet–Indonesian military operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Trikora), which the Dutch navy successfully repulsed in the [Battle of Arafura Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Arafura_Sea).

The Dutch departure from Southeast Asia had immediate consequences for the purpose of the navy, as its focus shifted to [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) and securing [shipping lanes](2018420.md) in a joint European and North Atlantic context.

### European Union cooperation

The Navy has participated in joint European Union naval operations and exercises. Ten [separate Dutch vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta#Vessels_and_aircraft_in_former_rotation_periods) have contributed to the [EU Naval Force Operation Atalanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta), combating Somali piracy forces in the Gulf of Aden, Arabian Sea, and Indian Ocean since 2009. The last vessel was sent in 2018; since then the Navy has only contributed staff and advisors to the mission.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### NATO cooperation



[Standing NATO Maritime Group 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_NATO_Maritime_Group_1) in 2007 with [HNLMS *Evertsen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Evertsen_\(F805\)) second from the right.

With the creation of the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization), the military focus was on the army and air force; it was not until the [Korean War](16772.md) (1950–1953) that the navy got more recognition. The government allowed the creation of a balanced fleet consisting of two naval squadrons. Apart from the [aircraft carrier](2219.md) [HNLMS *Karel Doorman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Karel_Doorman_\(R81\)) the Dutch navy consisted of two [light cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cruiser) (two [*De Zeven Provinciën* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Zeven_Provinci%C3%ABn-class_cruiser)), 12 [destroyers](8779.md) (four [*Holland* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland-class_destroyer), eight [*Friesland* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesland-class_destroyer)), eight [submarines](28825.md), six [frigates](11464.md) ([*van Speijk*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Speijk-class_frigate)), and a considerable number of [minesweepers](217118.md).

As a member of [NATO](21133.md), the Netherlands developed its security policy in close cooperation with other members. The establishment of the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) in 1955 intensified the arms race between West and East. Technical innovations rapidly emerged, the introduction of radar and sonar were followed by [nuclear weapon](21785.md) systems and long-range missiles. The geopolitical situation allowed for a fixed military strategy. Beginning in 1965, the Dutch Navy joined certain permanent NATO squadrons like the [Standing Naval Force Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Naval_Force_Atlantic).

## Structure

The constituent parts of the Royal Netherlands Navy are:

### Naval squadron

Contains all surface combatants, replenishment ships, and amphibious support ships.

### Submarine service

Main article: [Royal Netherlands Navy Submarine Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Navy_Submarine_Service)

Houses the submarines and a support vessel.

### Mine Detection and Clearing Service

Main article: [Mine Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_Service)

Contains various minehunters.

### Hydrographic Service

The [Hydrographic Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dienst_der_Hydrografie) is responsible for relevant hydrographic surveys.[^cite_note-16]

### Social Medical Service

Provides healthcare to personnel of the Royal Netherlands Navy.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

### Directorate of Materiel Sustainment

The Directorate of Materiel Sustainment is responsible for the maintenance of ships, submarines and systems.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Naval aviation

Main article: [Netherlands Naval Aviation Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Naval_Aviation_Service)

Two squadrons equipped with [NH90 NFH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHIndustries_NH90#NFH:_NATO_Frigate_Helicopter) helicopter based at [De Kooy Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Kooy_Airfield).

### Netherlands Marine Corps

Main article: [Netherlands Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Marine_Corps)

- Two Marine Combat Groups (1 MCG and 2 MCG)

- One [Maritime Special Operations Force (NLMARSOF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Maritime_Special_Operations_Forces)

- One Surface Assault and Training Group (SATG)

- One Seabased Support Group (SSG)

- 32 Raiding Squadron (permanently stationed at [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba))

- [Rotterdam Marine Band of the Royal Netherlands Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Marine_Band_of_the_Royal_Netherlands_Navy)

### Dutch Caribbean Naval Command

[Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_\(military_formation\)) of the Royal Netherlands Navy that is responsible for national defense and international law enforcement in the [Dutch Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Caribbean).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### Netherlands and Dutch Caribbean Coastguard

Although the [Netherlands Coastguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Coastguard) is not an official part of the Navy, it is under its operational control. Also the [Dutch Caribbean Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Caribbean_Coast_Guard) is under the operational control of the Navy and is commanded by the commander of the Navy in the [Caribbean](18956035.md).

## Bases

The main naval base, [Nieuwe Haven Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Haven_Naval_Base) is situated in [Den Helder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_Helder), [North Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Holland). Secondary bases are situated around Den Helder, as well as in [Amsterdam](844.md), and [Willemstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willemstad) on the Caribbean island of [Curaçao](65205.md), Usage rights are also in place for port facilities in [Rotterdam](26049.md), [Vlissingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlissingen) and [Eemshaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eemshaven). The [Netherlands Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Marine_Corps) has barracks in Rotterdam, [Doorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doorn), [Texel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texel) and Den Helder, as well as in the Caribbean at [Suffisant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinekazerne_Suffisant) on Curaçao, and [Savaneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savaneta) on [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] There is also [Pointe Blanche Naval Support Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe_Blanche_Naval_Support_Point) in Sint Maarten.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Officer training

Officers of the Nederland Navy are trained at the Royal Naval Institute (*[Koninklijk Instituut voor de Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijk_Instituut_voor_de_Marine)*), which is part of the Netherlands defence academy (*Nederlandse Defensie Academie*) in [Den Helder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_Helder).[^cite_note-30] Around 100-120 people start training every year.

The renewed Dutch Navy will be a [green-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green-water_navy), having enough frigates and auxiliaries to operate far out at sea, while depending on land-based air support, and, with the large amphibious squadron, they will have significant [brown-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-water_navy) capabilities.

### Naval aviation - Maritime helicopters



Royal Netherlands Navy NH90 NFH at De Kooy Naval Air Station.

- 19 [NH90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHIndustries_NH90), 11 NATO Frigate Helicopter (NFH) and eight transport version of the NATO Frigate Helicopter (TNFH) for Marine Corps Air Lift Helicopter Squadron.

  [^cite_note-45]

  &#x20;One NFH was lost on 19 July 2020 as result of a crash in the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) near the island of [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba), killing two of the four crew on board.

In 2025 3 additional NH90 ASW helicopters were ordered with an option for 2 extra, to supplement the current inventory and increase availability.

Since the retirement of the Westland Lynx, the Royal Netherlands Air Force fills the gap of the Lynx's amphibious task with Airbus AS-532U2 Cougar helicopters. The Cougar's main task is to support the Royal Netherlands Marine Corps on board of the LPD's and JSS. Other tasks are to provide Medical air transport to and from these ships, but also support SOF units in amphibious missions and trainings.

In 2012 an [AH-64D Apache](37746.md) attack helicopter from the [Royal Netherlands Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Air_Force) made a deck landing on board [HNLMS *Rotterdam*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Rotterdam_\(L800\)) for the first time as part of an initial study into the possibilities for wider use of the helicopters as these will be upgraded to the AH-64E standard which has specific features for maritime operations.

The Dutch amphibious support ship [HNLMS *Johan de Witt*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Johan_de_Witt) and the JSS [HNLMS *Karel Doorman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Karel_Doorman_\(A833\)) are designed to handle Royal Netherlands Air Force [CH-47F Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_CH-47_Chinook) helicopters but still require additional anti corrosion measures (part of the ongoing upgrade of the CH-47F).

## Future changes

Main article: [Future of the Royal Netherlands Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_the_Royal_Netherlands_Navy)

In April 2018, the Dutch Government approved a multi-year investment programme and allocated funds for the 2018–2030 period, including:

- The [*Walrus*-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walrus-class_submarine) [replacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orka-class_submarine) with new boats planned for initial service entry in 2034.

  [^cite_note-navalnews.com-31]

  [^cite_note-Walrus_submarine_replacement-32]

  &#x20;The subs are currently undergoing a Service-life Extension Programme (SLEP), including new sonar, new optronic periscope and weapon upgrades for near shore operations. The Royal Dutch Navy is evaluating [Saab/Damen (A-26)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A26_submarine), [TKMS (Upgraded 212)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_212_submarine), [Navantia S-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-80-class_submarine) and a [Naval Group (SSK version of Barracuda)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Barracuda-class_submarine) proposal. In 2019 the S-80 option was dropped, originally with plans to place an order for the winning design in 2022 and having the first boat in service in 2028 and the first two by 2031.

  [^cite_note-63]

  [^cite_note-gain-2019-33]

  &#x20;However, in October 2021 it was reported that this timeline was no longer feasible. Instead, the Dutch Ministry of Defence signalled that the envisaged dates would have to be "substantially adjusted".

  [^cite_note-Walrus_delays-34]

  &#x20;In April 2022 it was announced that the revised schedule for the construction of the new replacement boats would likely see the first two replacement vessels entering service in the 2034 to 2037 timeframe.

  [^cite_note-navalnews.com-31]

  &#x20;On 15 March 2024 State Secretary for Defence [Christophe van der Maat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_van_der_Maat) officially announced that [Naval Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Group) has been selected as the winning bid.

  [^cite_note-Orka_class_1-64]

- Upgrading the [*De Zeven Provinciën*-class LCF frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Zeven_Provinci%C3%ABn-class_frigate) Theatre Ballistic Missile Defense, acquisition of [RIM-161 Standard Missile 3](15787389.md) (SM-3), a new [OtoMelara 127/64 LW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otobreda_127/64) canon, ESSM-2 and SLCM [BGM-109 Tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) and expanding the Mk41 VLS with an additional 8 cell unit.

  [^cite_note-65]

- Acquisition of initially two [Multifunctional Support Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunctional_Support_Ship) to supplement existing LCF Frigates with extra container based AAW [Barak-ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_8) missiles and [IAI Harop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Harop) [loitering munition](53703349.md) in support of amphibious forces.

- Replacement of the [*Karel Doorman*-class M frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Doorman-class_frigate) in the 2028/2030 period by 4 ships

  [^cite_note-66]

  (plus another 2 for the Belgian Navy), designed & built by Damen Shipyards.

  [^cite_note-nieuweschepenmarine-67]

  &#x20;See [Future Surface Combatant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Surface_Combatant_\(Koninklijke_Marine\)) for more information.

- Replacement of the 6 [*Alkmaar*-class MCM ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite-class_minehunter) from 2025

  [^cite_note-MCM_start-37]

  &#x20;including MCM Drones. 6 units each will be built for both the Belgian and Dutch navies for a total of 12 ships.

  [^cite_note-nieuweschepenmarine-67]

  &#x20;The contract was won by [Naval Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Group) on 15 March 2019 for the construction of 12 [City-class mine countermeasures vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-class_mine_countermeasures_vessel).

  [^cite_note-68]

  [^cite_note-69]

- Increasing the size of the [Royal Netherlands Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Marine_Corps) to remain highly integrated with the British Royal Marines. In 2017 the Ministry of Defence announced the formation of a Fleet Marine Squadron for the protection of merchant ships.

- In June 2023, the British and Dutch governments announced that the two countries would "explore opportunities" to jointly develop new specialist amphibious warships which for the Royal Netherlands Navy would likely replace the *Rotterdam*-class landing platform docks.

  [^cite_note-70]

- The German Navy Seebatallion (Marines) will be integrated into the [Royal Netherlands Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Marine_Corps).

- Cooperation with the [German Navy](12960.md) regarding Submarine & Amphibious Operations.

- Replacement of Zr.Ms. Mercuur, Zr.Ms. Pelikaan, the four diving support vessels, the diving training vessel Zr.Ms. Soemba, the hydrographic vessels Zr.Ms. Snellius en Zr.Ms. Luymes and the training vessel Van Kinsbergen from 2024 onwards.

- Acquisition of new landing craft from 2025 with additional capacity to support amphibious operations and the integration of the German Navy Marines (Seebatallion). In March 2023, a report to the Dutch Parliament outlined a plan for 12 Littoral Assault Craft (LAC) and 8 Littoral Craft Mobility (LCM) to replace the 12 existing light landing craft (Landing Craft Vehicles and Personnel - LCVP) and improve on their capability.

  [^cite_note-71]

- Main Naval Ship Based Weapons will be replaced by acquiring ESSM-2, new 127mm canons, Harpoon ASuW replacement, SM-3, SM-2 IIIC SAM, Goalkeeper CIWS replacement, MK 46 & MK 48 Torpedo replacement and SLCM.

### Theater ballistic missile defence

Together with the United States and several other [NATO](21133.md) members, the Dutch Navy is testing and updating its ships for [Tactical ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_ballistic_missile) defense capability. Although tests conducted concerning the capability of the APAR ([Active Phased Array Radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_Phased_Array_Radar)) have been very successful, in 2018 the Dutch Government approved plans to acquire the SM-3 missiles for integration into the existing weapon suite of the LCF frigates. The four LCF ships will be fitted out with eight SM-3 missiles each (they are provisioned for this VLS extension) through Foreign Military Sales (under discussion between the US and The Netherlands).
