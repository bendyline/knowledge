**Khartoum State** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ولاية الخرطوم, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Wilāyat al-Kharṭūm*) is one of the eighteen [states of Sudan](579458.md). Although it is the smallest state by area (22,142 km<sup>2</sup>), it is the most populous state in Sudan—5,274,321 in the 2008 census,[^cite_note-Geohive_Sudan-4][^cite_note-2008_census-5] and officially estimated at 7,993,900 in 2018. It contains the *Triangular capital* consisting of the [capital](181337.md) and largest city by population, [Khartoum](17320.md), as well as the cities of [Khartoum North](3145079.md) and [Omdurman](100414.md). Khartoum city is the capital of both Khartoum State and [Sudan](27421.md).

The three cities are located in the heart of Sudan at the confluence of the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile) and the [Blue Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Nile), where the two rivers unite to form the [River Nile](21244.md). The confluence of the two rivers creates a unique effect. As they join, each river retains its own color: the White Nile with its bright whiteness and the Blue Nile with its alluvial brown color. These colors are more visible in the flood season.

The state lies between longitudes 31.5 to 34°E and latitudes 15 to 16°N. It is surrounded by [River Nile State](767659.md) in the north-east, in the north-west by the [Northern State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_\(state\)), in the east and southeast by the states of [Kassala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassala_\(state\)), [Qadarif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qadarif_\(state\)), [Gezira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezira_\(state\)) and [White Nile State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile_\(state\)), and in the west by [North Kurdufan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kurdufan).

## History

Before the [Funj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funj) sultanate the area was home to the Anaj or known as the Nubians of [Alodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alodia), after Soba fell to Abdallah jama The Nubians of this region came to be known as The [Abdallab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallab) and continued to be the indigenous people of Khartoum State who mostly lived just North of the modern day city of Khartoum under the [Funj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funj) and later turkish control [^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Etymology

There are several etymologies for the name of Khartoum. The explanation suggested as "most correct" by the state government's website[^cite_note-Khartoum_govt_history-8] is that it comes from the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) for "elephant's trunk", which describes the shape of the site of the city at the confluence of the two Niles, where a stretch of land extends into the water. Other stories are that it is a corruption of "gurtoum", the name for seeds of the [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower) plant, supposedly used by Roman invaders at the current site of Khartoum to treat soldiers' wounds, or that it was derived from the words "Khor al-Tom".[^cite_note-Khartoum_govt_history-8]

## Climate

The northern region of the state is mostly desert because it receives barely any rainfall, whereas the other regions have semi-desert climates. The weather is rainy in the fall, and cold and dry in the winter. Average rainfall reaches 100–200 mm (4–8 in) in the north-eastern areas and 200–300 mm (8–12 in) in the northwestern areas.

The temperature in summer ranges from 25 to 40 °C (77–104 °F) from April to June, and from 20 to 35 °C (68–95 °F) in the months of July to October. In winter, the temperature declines gradually from 25 °C (77 °F) to 15 °C (59 °F) between November and March.

## Population and economy

The 2008 population census estimated the population of Khartoum State to be about 5,274,321 capita, composed of various tribes of the Sudan. The population was officially estimated to have risen to 7,993,900 in 2018.[^cite_note-10] The population in 2008 was 79% urban,[^cite_note-CBS_table_2008-11] and 74% of the state's population reported their region of origin to be outside Khartoum.[^cite_note-2008_census-5]

The areas of [Omdurman](100414.md) and the rural South are inhabited by the tribe of Gamowia, as well as by the Kordofani tribes displaced to these areas by the drought and [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification) that hit their areas in the early- and mid-1980s. These are the tribes of the Kababish and the Kawahla. In the northern countryside of Karari locality are the tribe of Shiheinat, and in Khartoum North the tribes of Abdallab and Batahin. In the East Nile are the tribes of Abu Dileig, Batahin, and Kawahla, with the tribe of Iseilat in Um-Dowan.[^cite_note-Khartoum_govt_history-8][^cite_note-Police_briefing-12]

Most of the population works in government service, the private sector, and banking. There is also a large number of merchants, and migrants and displaced people working in marginal activities. In the countryside most people are engaged in agriculture and grazing and thus supply the capital, Khartoum, with vegetables, fruits, and dairy products. There are also some residents living on the banks of the rivers engaged in the trades dependent on the rivers, such as pottery, brick-making and fishing. Above all stands the strong ambition to become the new [Dubai](211583.md) with the financial support coming from the oil and gas supplies of the country.[^cite_note-Khartoum_govt_history-8][^cite_note-Police_briefing-12] The State of Khartoum is now the largest center in Sudan for the manufacturing sector with more than 7500 different factories and more than ten industrial areas providing all types of goods such as food processing and electronics and household appliances as well as medicines and textiles as well as footwear and tanneries and chemicals. Within the next year five Chinese car manufacturers will start operations to make Khartoum a hub for vehicle manufacturing in Africa.

## Museums and archeological sites

Khartoum state contains archaeological sites dating back to different periods of civilization, both prehistoric and historical. Its museums include the largest in Sudan, the [National Museum of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Sudan), and a number of specialized museums, such as the [Khalifa House Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_House_Museum) and the Museums of Natural History, Folklore and the Popular Heritage. There are also a center for study of folklore under the National Authority for the Arts, and the Omdurman Museum of Ibrahim Hijazi.
