***Channel 4 News*** is a British television news programme broadcast on [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4) and produced by [ITN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITN). It has been broadcasting since Channel 4 launched on 2 November 1982. For almost forty years, the programme had only two lead presenters: [Peter Sissons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sissons) (1982—1989), and [Jon Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Snow_\(journalist\)) (1989—2021).

## Current productions

### *Channel 4 News*

*Channel 4 News* is the name of [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4)'s main evening news programme.

The editor is [Esme Wren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esme_Wren), appointed in 2022.[^cite_note-1] The programme is presented by [Krishnan Guru-Murthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnan_Guru-Murthy), [Matt Frei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Frei), [Jackie Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Long) and [Ciaran Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciaran_Jenkins) and is on the air Monday to Friday from 19\:00 to 19\:55, (Weather from around 19\:55), and at variable times at weekends and bank holidays. [Alex Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Thomson_\(journalist\)) is the chief correspondent.

*Channel 4 News* has been on air since the channel launched in 1982. The channel wanted its news to be very different from what was on offer elsewhere on UK television. As Channel 4's commissioner for news, Liz Forgan, put it, she wanted: "no sport, no royal stories, no plane crashes and lashings of foreign news."[^cite_note-\:0-2]

This was a problem for ITN, which had won the contract to produce the programme, and which specialised in exactly the kind of fast-moving tabloid-style bulletins Forgan did not want. A new team was put together to produce the show. At Channel 4's insistence, the editor, Derrik Mercer, was brought in from outside ITN, as were many of the staff. Mercer was a distinguished newspaperman but he had never worked in broadcasting. When the new show went on air there were many problems. The lead presenter, [Peter Sissons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sissons), later described it as "an unmitigated disaster."[^cite_note-\:0-2]

But, gradually, the team worked out production solutions. Mercer left the show. He was replaced by a senior ITN executive, Paul McKee; and then by a very experienced ITN journalist [Stewart Purvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Purvis). The ratings began to pick up. The show found its feet during the year-long UK miners' strike that started in 1984. By the time the strike ended, *Channel 4 News* had established itself as a force to be reckoned with.[^cite_note-\:0-2] It started to win awards—the first was an award from the Broadcasting Press Guild, which in 1985 named the show the Best News or Current Affairs Programme of 1984;[^cite_note-3] and in 1987 it won its first BAFTA for the Best News or Outside Broadcast of 1986.[^cite_note-4]

*Channel 4 News* went on to win many more awards, including, in 2006, a record five [Royal Television Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Television_Society) Television awards. These included TV Journalist of the Year for Jon Snow; the Home News Award; and the International News Award.

It won the News Coverage [British Academy Television Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award) [in 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Awards_2004) and the 2004 [International Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Emmy) for the best news programme produced and aired outside the United States. Jon Snow won the Richard Dimbleby [British Academy Television Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award) [in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Awards_2005) for outstanding contribution to the world of news and current affairs.

In November 2011, Liam Dutton became Channel 4's first ever weather presenter, joining from [BBC Weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Weather).[^cite_note-5]

The exposé of [Cambridge Analytica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Analytica) in conjunction with *The Guardian* and *The New York Times* which aired in 2018 won a [Peabody Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Award).[^cite_note-6]

In April 2021, Channel 4 and ITN announced that Snow would leave the programme after 32 years.[^cite_note-7] His last show was on 23 December 2021.[^cite_note-8]

### *Channel 4 News Summary*

A replacement for the *Channel 4 News at Noon* in the 12\:00 slot, it first aired on 21 December 2009, giving a five-minute summary of the news.

## Former productions

### *Channel Four News at Noon*

*Channel Four News at Noon* was first introduced in 2003 for the duration of the [Iraq War](5043324.md), and due to its instant success, it was kept on in [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4)'s daytime schedule (except when live horse racing was being broadcast). It was presented by [Krishnan Guru-Murthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnan_Guru-Murthy). Prior to this bulletin, the programme in the slot was *Powerhouse*, a political news programme, also produced for Channel 4 by ITN. As a consequence of the advertising slowdown during the 2009 recession, the programme was cancelled, along with *[More4 News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/More4_News)*, and replaced with the five-minute *Channel 4 News Summary*, the last broadcast airing on 18 December 2009.[^cite_note-Guardian090805-9]

### *More4 News*

Main article: [More4 News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/More4_News)

Aired Monday to Friday on sister channel [More4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/More4), *More4 News* was anchored by [Sarah Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Smith_\(journalist\)) then later [Kylie Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylie_Morris), it ran for 30 minutes, aiming to go in-depth into a certain issue. As a consequence of the advertising slowdown during the 2009 recession, the programme was cancelled, along with the *Channel Four News at Noon*, the last broadcast airing on 18 December 2009.[^cite_note-Guardian090805-9]

## Non-broadcast media

*Channel 4 News* also produces a variety of non-broadcast media, including a range of journalist authored blogs to deliver insight and analysis of the news from the news team.[^cite_note-16] *Channel 4 News* also produces *Snowmail*, a free daily email from the news reporter team, giving their personal take on the day's news agenda and behind-the-scenes newsroom goings-on.[^cite_note-17]

## Historical roles

In 2003, *Channel 4 News* broke the story of the [Dodgy Dossier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodgy_Dossier) which led to a political crisis in Britain.[^cite_note-Rush2003-18]

The story of the Conservative Party's election expenses scandal was first broken, and then pursued for over a year, by [Michael Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Crick).[^cite_note-19]

In March 2018, an undercover investigation by *Channel 4 News* explored the [campaign activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook%E2%80%93Cambridge_Analytica_data_scandal) of [Cambridge Analytica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Analytica).[^cite_note-20]

## Retractions and Ofcom criticism

On the evening of the [2017 Westminster attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Westminster_attack), *Channel 4 News* claimed they were able to name the dead attacker as Abu Izzadeen, also known as Trevor Brooks. The claim was repeated by *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* and the *[Daily Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mirror)*. However, *Channel 4 News* was forced to issue an on-air retraction during the same bulletin after Izzadeen's solicitor stated that he was alive and serving time in prison.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## Theme music

The music in the *Channel 4 News* titles is an orchestration of "Best Endeavours" by [Alan Hawkshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hawkshaw).[^cite_note-music-23] It was introduced a few months after the channel's launch, and has remained in use since its inception. It was originally a library piece and as such has appeared in other works, such as the trailer for the [Clint Eastwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Eastwood) film *[Pale Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_Rider)*.[^cite_note-music-23]
