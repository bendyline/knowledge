**Suwayda Governorate**[^cite_note-5] ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مُحافظة السويداء, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Muḥāfaẓat as-Suwaydāʾ*) is one of the fourteen [governorates](690220.md) (provinces) of [Syria](7515849.md). It is the country's southernmost governorate, covering an area of 5,550 km<sup>2</sup>, and is bordered by [Daraa Governorate](5317030.md) in the west, [Rif Dimashq Governorate](4521946.md) in the north and northeast, and the country of [Jordan](7515964.md) in the south and southeast. The capital and largest city of the governorate is [Suwayda](2590570.md).&#x20;

Geographically the governorate comprises almost all of [Jabal al-Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_al-Druze), the eastern part of [Lajat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajat), and a part of the arid eastern steppe of [Harrat al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrat_al-Sham). Both Suwayda and Daraa governorates are part of the historic [Hauran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauran) region.&#x20;

Most inhabitants of Suwayda are employed in agriculture, cultivating crops such as grapes, apples, olives, and wheat. Additionally, Suwayda is home to numerous archaeological sites.&#x20;

The governorate had a population of 313,231 in the 2004 census.[^cite_note-Census_2004-6]&#x20;

This governorate is unique in Syria as it has a [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) majority. Additionally, it has integrated [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christians) communities that have long [coexisted harmoniously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_Druze) with the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) in mountains[^cite_note-Balanche_2017_27-7] and a Sunni Muslim Bedouin minority.&#x20;

The governorate has a population of about 375,000 inhabitants (est. 2010).[^cite_note-cbssyr-9] It is the only governorate in Syria that has a [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) majority.[^cite_note-10] This is due to the large scale migration of the Druze from [Lebanon](17771.md) (and to a lesser extent, the [Galilee](12639.md)) from the mid-19th through the early 20th century.[^cite_note-11] There is also a sizable [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Antioch) minority, and a small [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim) Bedouin community. During the civil war, many Sunni Muslim refugees—mainly from [Daraa Governorate](5317030.md) as well as other parts of Syria—found refuge in Suwayda.[^cite_note-12]&#x20;

In the 1980s Druze made up 87.6% of the population, Christians (mostly [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Antioch)) 11% and Sunni Muslims 2%.[^cite_note-13] In 2010, the As-Suwayda governorate has a population of about 375,000 inhabitants, [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) made up 90%, [Christians](18337522.md) 7% and Sunni Muslims 3%.[^cite_note-DruzeAssad-3] Due to low birth and high [emigration rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_emigration), Christians proportion in Suwayda had declined.[^cite_note-DruzeAssad-3]&#x20;

Most of the inhabitants live in the western parts of the governorate, especially on the western slopes of Jabal al-Druze. Only nomadic [Bedouin](61770.md) tribes live in the barren region of Harrat al-Shamah.&#x20;

## Government

The Druze majority government has leaned towards a semi-independent status, rejecting central Syrian government for much of its history. During the Assad period, the Suwayda Government was largely free of serious conflict. However, there was regular passive resistance to Assad's rule, including constant public protests.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Also, citizens closed the Ba'ath Party offices and removed pro-Assad posters in public, though there was never escalation to armed revolution.[^cite_note-16]&#x20;

Despite the end of the Assad regime, attacks on minorities, in particular Druze peoples, have similarly shaken confidence in the post-Assad government.[^cite_note-17]&#x20;
