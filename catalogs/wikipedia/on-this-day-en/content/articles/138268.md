**Spokane** ([/spoʊˈkæn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/31/En-us-Spokane.ogg/En-us-Spokane.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/31/En-us-Spokane.ogg/En-us-Spokane.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*spoh-KAN*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-8] [Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_language): *sƛ̓x̌etkʷ*[^cite_note-9]) is a city in and the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Spokane County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_County,_Washington) in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Washington](13015878.md). It lies along the [Spokane River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_River), adjacent to the [Selkirk Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkirk_Mountains) and west of the [Rocky Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) foothills, 110 miles (180 km) south of the [Canadian border](630260.md), twenty miles (30 km) west of the [Idaho](14607.md) state line, 279 miles (449 km) southwest of [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary), and 280 miles (450 km) east of [Seattle](11388236.md), via [I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Washington).[^cite_note-10]

Spokane is the most populous city in [eastern Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington) and the [second-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Washington) in all of Washington (after Seattle), with a population of 228,989 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census),[^cite_note-census20100401-11][^cite_note-censuswa2020rank-12] while the [Spokane metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_metropolitan_area) has an estimated 605,000 residents.[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-13]

Spokane is the economic and cultural center of the [Inland Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Northwest). It is known as the birthplace of [Father's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father's_Day_\(United_States\)), and locally by the nickname of "Lilac City".[^cite_note-14] Officially, Spokane goes by the nickname of *Hooptown USA*, due to Spokane's annual hosting of the [Spokane Hoopfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Hoopfest), the world's largest 3-on-3 basketball tournament.[^cite_note-15] The city and the wider Inland Northwest area are served by [Spokane International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_International_Airport), 5 miles (8 km) west of [Downtown Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Spokane), which is located near another airfield at [Fairchild Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Air_Force_Base).

The first people to live in the area, the [Spokane tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_people) (their name meaning "children of the sun" in [Salishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Salish_language)), lived off plentiful game. [David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thompson_\(explorer\)) explored the area with the westward expansion and establishment of the [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company)'s [Spokane House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_House) in 1810. This trading post was the first long-term European settlement in Washington. Completion of the [Northern Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pacific_Railway) in 1881 brought many settlers from America to the Spokane area. The same year it was officially incorporated as a city under the name of *Spokane Falls* (it was re-incorporated under its current name ten years later).[^cite_note-16] In the late 19th century, gold and silver were discovered in the Inland Northwest. The local economy depended on mining, timber, and agriculture until the 1980s. Spokane hosted the first environmentally themed [World's fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_fair) at [Expo '74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'74).

Many of the downtown area's older [Romanesque Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_Revival)-style buildings were designed by architect [Kirtland Cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirtland_Cutter) after the [Great Spokane Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Spokane_Fire) of 1889, which damaged much of the downtown commercial district. The city is also home to the [Riverfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverfront_Park_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) and [Manito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manito_Park_and_Botanical_Gardens) parks, the Smithsonian-affiliated [Northwest Museum of Arts and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Museum_of_Arts_and_Culture), the [Davenport Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davenport_Hotel_\(Spokane\)), and the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Theater_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) and [Bing Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_Crosby_Theater) theaters. The [Cathedral of Our Lady of Lourdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) is the [seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedra) of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Spokane), and the [Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._John_the_Evangelist_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) serves as that of the [Episcopal Diocese of Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Diocese_of_Spokane). The [Spokane Washington Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Washington_Temple) in the east of the county serves [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints). [Gonzaga University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_University) was established in 1887 by the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus), and the private [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church) [Whitworth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitworth_University) was founded three years later and moved to north Spokane in 1914.

In sports, the region's professional and semi-professional sports teams include the [Spokane Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Indians) in [Minor League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball), the [Spokane Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Chiefs) in the [Western Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hockey_League), the [Spokane Velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Velocity) in [USL League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_One), and a women's first division team [Spokane Zephyr FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Zephyr_FC) in [USL Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Super_League). The [Gonzaga Bulldogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_Bulldogs) collegiate basketball team competes at the [Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_I_\(NCAA\)) level. As of 2010, Spokane's major daily newspaper, *[The Spokesman-Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spokesman-Review)*, had a daily circulation of over 76,000.

## History

Main article: [History of Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Spokane,_Washington)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Spokane,_Washington).

The first humans to live in the Spokane area were [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer) that lived off plentiful fish and game; early human remains have been dated to 8,000 to 13,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Ruby_p._5-17] The [Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_people) tribe, after which the city is named (the name meaning "children of the sun" or "sun people" in [Salishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Salish_language)),[^cite_note-Phillips-18][^cite_note-Spukcane-19]<sup>[\[a\]](#endnote_Spukcane)</sup> are believed to be either their direct descendants, or descendants of people from the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains).[^cite_note-Ruby_5-6-20] When asked by early white explorers, the Spokanes said their ancestors came from "up North."[^cite_note-Ruby_p._5-17] Early in the 19th century, the [Northwest Fur Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Fur_Company) sent two white fur trappers west of the Rocky Mountains to search for [fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur).[^cite_note-21] These were the first white men met by the Spokanes, who believed they were sacred, and set the trappers up in the [Colville River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colville_River_\(Washington\)) valley for the winter.[^cite_note-Ruby_35-22]

### Trading post

The explorer-geographer [David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thompson_\(explorer\)), working as head of the [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company)'s [Columbia Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Department), became the first European to explore the Inland Empire (now called the [Inland Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Northwest_\(United_States\))).[^cite_note-Stratton19-23] Crossing what is now the Canada–US border from [British Columbia](3392.md), Thompson wanted to expand the North West Company further south in search of furs. After establishing the [Kullyspell House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kullyspell_House) and [Saleesh House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleesh_House) trading posts in what are now Idaho and Montana, Thompson then attempted to expand further west. He sent out two trappers, [Jacques Raphael Finlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Raphael_Finlay) and Finan McDonald, to construct a [fur trading post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) on the Spokane River, which flows west from [Lake Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Coeur_d'Alene) to the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River), and trade with the local Indians.[^cite_note-SpokaneH-24] This post was established in 1810, at the confluence of the [Little Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Spokane_River) and [Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_River) rivers, becoming the first enduring European settlement of significance in what later became Washington state.[^cite_note-Stratton19-23] Known as the [Spokane House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_House), or simply "Spokane", it was in operation from 1810 to 1826.[^cite_note-Phillips-18] Operations were run by the British North West Company and later the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company), and the post was the headquarters of the fur trade between the Rocky and Cascade mountains for 16 years. After the latter business absorbed the North West Company in 1821, the major operations at the Spokane House were eventually shifted north to [Fort Colville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Colville), reducing the post's significance.[^cite_note-25]

&#x20;[Spokane Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Falls) in 1888

In 1836, Reverend [Samuel Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Parker_\(missionary\)) visited the area and reported that around 800 Native Americans were living in Spokane Falls.[^cite_note-26] A [medical mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitman_Mission_National_Historic_Site) was established by [Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Whitman) and [Narcissa Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcissa_Whitman) to cater for [Cayuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayuse_people) Indians and hikers of the Oregon Trail at [Walla Walla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walla_Walla,_Washington) in the south.[^cite_note-Essay10777-27] After [the Whitmans were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitman_massacre) by Indians in 1847, Reverend [Cushing Eells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushing_Eells) established [Whitman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitman_College) in their memory, also setting up the [first church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tshimakain_Mission) in the Spokane area.[^cite_note-Essay10777-27]

In 1853, two years after the establishment of the Washington Territory, the first governor, [Isaac Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Stevens), made an initial effort to make a treaty with [Chief Garry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Garry) and the Spokanes at Antoine Plantes' Ferry, not far from [Millwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwood,_Washington).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] After the last campaign of the [Yakima Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakima_War), the [Coeur d'Alene War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene_War) of 1858 was brought to a close by the actions of Col. [George Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Wright_\(general\)), who won decisive victories against a confederation of tribes in engagements at the battles of [Four Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Four_Lakes) and [Spokane Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Spokane_Plains).[^cite_note-30] The cessation of hostilities opened the inter-mountain valley of the Pacific Northwest to colonial expansion and safe habitation by settlers.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### American settlement

Spokane Falls in 1890

Spokane Falls, 1890

Joint American–British occupation of [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country), in effect since the [Treaty of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1818), eventually led to the [Oregon boundary dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_boundary_dispute) after a large influx of American settlers along the [Oregon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Trail). Great Britain ceded its claims to lands in [Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound) and the central and lower Columbia Basin by the [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty) of 1846. The Hudson's Bay Company wound up its operations in the area over the next few years.

In what is now Spokane, the first American settlers were J.J. Downing and S.R. Scranton, cattle ranchers who [squatted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatters) and established a claim at Spokane Falls in 1871.[^cite_note-p._19-33] Together they built a small sawmill on a claim near the south bank of the falls.[^cite_note-p._19-33][^cite_note-34] [James N. Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_N._Glover) and Jasper Matheney, Oregonians passing through the region in 1873, recognized the value of the Spokane River and its falls for the purpose of water power.[^cite_note-p._19-33] They realized the investment potential and bought the claims of 160 acres (65 ha) and the sawmill from Downing and Scranton for a total of \$4,000.[^cite_note-35] Glover and Matheney knew that the [Northern Pacific Railroad Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pacific_Railroad_Company) had received a government charter to build a main line across this [northern route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Transcon).[^cite_note-p._19-33] Amid many delays in construction and uncertainty over the completion of the railroad and its exact course, Matheney sold his interest in the claim to Glover.[^cite_note-p._20-36]<sup>[\[b\]](#endnote_Glover)</sup> Glover confidently held on to his claim and became a successful Spokane business owner and the city's second mayor.[^cite_note-37] He later came to be known as the "Father of Spokane".[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

In 1880, [Fort Spokane](51130340.md) was established by U.S. Army troops under Lt. Col. [Henry C. Merriam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_C._Merriam) 56 miles (90 km) northwest of Spokane, at the junction of the [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) and Spokane Rivers, to protect the construction of the Northern Pacific Railway and secure a place for U.S. settlement.[^cite_note-40] By June 30, 1881, the railway reached the city, bringing major European settlement to the area.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] The city was officially incorporated with a population of about 1,000 residents on November 29, 1881.[^cite_note-Durham_p._362-43][^cite_note-Incorporation-44]<sup>[\[c\]](#endnote_incorp)</sup> When Spokane was officially incorporated in 1881, [Robert W. Forrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Forrest?action=edit\&redlink=1) was elected as the first mayor of the city, with a Council of seven, S.G. Havermale, A.M. Cannon, Dr. L.H. Whitehouse, L.W. Rima, F.R. Moore, George A. Davis, and W\.C. Gray, all serving without pay.[^cite_note-Durham_p._362-43] The marketing campaigns of transportation companies with affordable fertile land to sell along their trade routes lured many settlers into the region they dubbed "Spokane Country".[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-Railroads-46]

The makeshift Daily Chronicle office after The Great Fire

The 1883 discovery of gold, silver, and lead in the Coeur d'Alene region of northern Idaho lured prospectors.[^cite_note-p._28-47] The Inland Empire erupted with numerous mining rushes from 1883 to 1892.[^cite_note-48] Mining and [smelting](28716.md) emerged as a major stimulus to Spokane. At the onset of the initial 1883 [gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_rush) in the nearby [Coeur d'Alene mining district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Valley,_Idaho), Spokane became popular with prospectors, offering low prices on everything "from a horse to a frying pan".[^cite_note-49] It would keep this status for subsequent [rushes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_rush) in the region due to its trade center status and accessibility to railroad infrastructure.[^cite_note-50]<sup>[\[d\]](#endnote_Transport)</sup>

Spokane's growth continued unabated until August 4, 1889, when a fire, now known as [The Great Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Spokane_Fire) (not to be confused with the [Great Fire of 1910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_1910), which happened nearby), began just after 6\:00 p.m., and destroyed the city's downtown commercial district.[^cite_note-GreatSpokaneFire-51] Due to technical problems with a pump station, there was no water pressure in the city when the fire started.[^cite_note-52] In a desperate bid to starve the fire, firefighters began razing buildings with dynamite. Eventually, the winds and the fire died down; 32 blocks of Spokane's downtown core had been destroyed and one person was killed.[^cite_note-GreatSpokaneFire-51]

The city of Spokane Falls circa 1895

Spokane ca. 1895

Despite this catastrophe, and in part because of it, Spokane experienced a building boom.[^cite_note-Schmeltzer_44-53]<sup>[\[e\]](#endnote_investment)</sup> The downtown was rebuilt, and the city was reincorporated under the present name of "Spokane" in 1891.[^cite_note-Incorporation-44] According to historian David H. Stratton, "From the late 1890s to about 1912, a great flurry of construction created a modern urban profile of office buildings, banks, department stores, hotels and other commercial institutions" which stretched from the Spokane River to the site of the Northern Pacific railroad tracks below the South Hill.[^cite_note-Creighton7-54] Yet the rebuilding and development of the city was far from smooth: between 1889 and 1896 alone, all six bridges over the Spokane River were destroyed by floods before their completion.[^cite_note-Creighton7-54]

In the 1890s, African-Americans from [Roslyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roslyn,_Washington) migrated to the city; they were looking for work after the closure of the area's mines. Two African-American churches, Calvary Baptist and Bethel African Methodist Episcopal, were founded in 1890.[^cite_note-55] Just three years after the fire, in 1892, [James J. Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J._Hill)'s [Great Northern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Railway_\(U.S.\)) arrived in the chosen site for Hill's [rail yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_yard), the newly created township of [Hillyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillyard,_Spokane,_Washington) (annexed by Spokane in 1924).[^cite_note-Hillyard-56] Spokane became an important rail shipping and transportation hub for the Inland Empire, connecting mines in the [Silver Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Valley,_Idaho) with agricultural areas around the [Palouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palouse) region.[^cite_note-Railroads-46][^cite_note-Schmeltzerp.41-57] The city's population ballooned to 19,922 in 1890, and to 36,848 in 1900 with the arrival of additional railroads.[^cite_note-Schmeltzer_44-53]

By 1910 the population had hit 104,000, and Spokane eclipsed [Walla Walla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walla_Walla,_Washington) as the commercial center of the Inland Empire.[^cite_note-58] In time the city came to be known as the "capital" of the Inland Empire and the heart of a vast tributary region.[^cite_note-p._28-47][^cite_note-Malone-59] After the arrival of the Northern Pacific, [Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad), Great Northern, and [Chicago, Milwaukee, St. Paul and Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Milwaukee,_St._Paul_and_Pacific) railroads, Spokane became one of the most important rail centers in the western U.S.[^cite_note-Schmeltzer_44-53][^cite_note-Malone-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

### Early 20th century

Construction of the Monroe Street Bridge, August 3, 1911

The [Monroe Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Street_Bridge_\(Spokane_River\)), 1911

Expansion abruptly stopped in the 1910s and was followed by a period of population decline,[^cite_note-Stagnation-62] due in large part to Spokane's slowing economy. Control of regional mines and resources became increasingly dominated by national corporations rather than local people and organizations, diverting capital outside of Spokane and decreasing growth and investment opportunities in the city.[^cite_note-Stagnation-62] During this time of stagnation, unrest was prevalent among the area's unemployed, who became victimized by "job sharks", who charged a fee for signing up workers in the logging camps. Job sharks and employment agencies were known to cheat itinerant workers, sometimes paying bribes to periodically fire entire work crews, thus generating repetitive fees for themselves.[^cite_note-63] Crime spiked in the 1890s and 1900s,[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] with eruptions of violent activity involving unions such as the [Industrial Workers of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Workers_of_the_World) (IWW), or "Wobblies" as they were often known, whose [free speech fights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_speech_fights#Spokane_free_speech_fight) had begun to garner national attention.[^cite_note-p._52-66] Now, with grievances concerning the unethical practices of the employment agencies, they initiated a free speech fight in September 1908 by purposely breaking a city ordinance on [soapboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soapbox).[^cite_note-p._52-66] With IWW encouragement, union members from many western states came to Spokane to take part in what had become a publicity stunt. Many Wobblies were incarcerated, including feminist labor leader [Elizabeth Gurley Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Gurley_Flynn), who published her account in the local *[Industrial Worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Worker)*.[^cite_note-p._52-66]

Riverside Avenue c. 1923

Riverside Avenue c. 1923

After mining declined at the turn of the 20th century, agriculture and logging became the primary influences in the Spokane economy.[^cite_note-Kenselp.25-67] The population explosion and the building of homes, railroads, and mines in northern Idaho and southern British Columbia fueled the logging industry.[^cite_note-Kenselp.25-67] Although overshadowed in importance by the vast timbered areas on the coastal regions west of the Cascades, and burdened with monopolistic rail freight rates and stiff competition, Spokane became a noted leader in the manufacture of doors, [window sashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sash_window), blinds, and other [planing mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planing_mill) products.[^cite_note-68] Rail freight rates were much higher in Spokane than the rates in coastal [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) cities such as Seattle and Portland, so much so that [Minneapolis](6097240.md) merchants could ship goods first to Seattle and then back to Spokane for less than shipping directly to Spokane, even though the rail line ran through Spokane on the way to the coast.[^cite_note-Malone-59][^cite_note-69]<sup>[\[f\]](#endnote_Hepburn)</sup>

The Inland Northwest region has also long been associated with farming, especially [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) production.[^cite_note-p._119-70] Initially, the Palouse was thought to be unsuitable for wheat production due to the hilly terrain, believing wheat could not be cultivated on the tops of the hills, but the region showed great promise for wheat production when it began in the late 1850s in part due to the hilltops.[^cite_note-p._119-70] The Palouse was and still is a [breadbasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadbasket) and was able to develop and grow with the completion of several railroad networks as well as a highway system that began to center around the city of Spokane, aiding farmers from around the region in distributing their products to market.[^cite_note-71] Inland Empire farmers exported wheat, livestock and other agricultural products to ports such as New York, Liverpool and Tokyo.[^cite_note-p._127-72]

Local morale was affected for years by the collapse of the [Division Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_Street_Bridge_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) early in the morning on December 15, 1915, which killed five people and injured over 20, but a new bridge was built (eventually replaced in 1994).[^cite_note-73] The 1920 census showed a net increase of just 35 individuals, which actually indicates that thousands left the city when considering the natural growth rate of a population.[^cite_note-Stagnation-62] Growth in the 1920s and 1930s remained slow but less drastically so, forcing city boosters to market the city as a quiet, comfortable place suitable for raising a family rather than a dynamic community full of opportunity.[^cite_note-74] The Inland Empire was heavily dependent on natural resources and [extractive](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/extractive) goods produced from mines, forests, and farms, which experienced a fall in demand.[^cite_note-p._38-75] The situation improved slightly with the start of World War II as aluminum production commenced in Spokane due to the area's cheap electricity (produced from regional dams) and the increased demand for airplanes.[^cite_note-p._38-75]

### Second half of the 20th century

Expo '74 in Riverfront Park

[Expo '74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'74) in [Riverfront Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverfront_Park_\(Spokane,_Washington\))

After decades of stagnation and slow growth, Spokane businessmen formed Spokane Unlimited in the early 1960s, an organization that sought to revitalize downtown Spokane.[^cite_note-76] A recreation park showcasing the [Spokane Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Falls) was the preferred option, and after successful negotiation to relocate the railroad facilities on Havermale Island,[^cite_note-WorldsFair-77] they executed on a proposal to host the first [environmentally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism) themed [World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Fair) in [Expo '74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'74) on May 4, becoming the smallest city at the time to host a World's Fair.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-CC-79] This event transformed Spokane's downtown, removing a century of railroad infrastructure and re-inventing the urban core. After Expo '74, the fairgrounds became the 100-acre (40 ha) Riverfront Park.[^cite_note-80]

The growth witnessed in the late 1970s and early 1980s was interrupted by another U.S. recession in 1981, in which silver, timber, and farm prices dropped.[^cite_note-Diverse-81] The period of decline for the city lasted into the 1990s and was also marked by a loss of many steady family-wage jobs in the manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82] At this time, market forces began to impact the local [Kaiser Aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Aluminum) plant and layoffs, pension cuts, a 1998–1999 labor strike, and eventually bankruptcy in 2002 followed.[^cite_note-Essay10119-83][^cite_note-Thumbnail-82] Although this was a tough period, Spokane's economy had started to benefit from some measure of economic diversification; growing companies such as [Key Tronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Tronic) and other research, marketing, and assembly plants for technology companies helped lessen Spokane's dependence on natural resources.[^cite_note-Diverse-81]

### 21st century

Spokane's skywalk network is among the nation's most extensive

Spokane has an extensive [Skywalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skywalk) network

As of 2014, Spokane is still trying to make the transition to a more [service-oriented economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_economy) in the face of a less prominent manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82] Developing the city's strength in the medical and health sciences fields has seen some success, resulting in the expansion of the University District with two medical school branches.[^cite_note-WWAMI-84][^cite_note-ElsonSFloyd-85][^cite_note-86] The city faces challenges such as a scarcity of high-paying jobs, pockets of poverty, and areas of high crime.[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82]

The opening of the [River Park Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Park_Square) in 1999 served as a [catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal) and sparked a downtown rebirth that included the building of the Spokane Arena and expansion of the [Spokane Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Convention_Center).[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82][^cite_note-87] Other major projects include the building of the Big Easy concert house (now the [Knitting Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knitting_Factory)) and renovation of the historic [Montvale Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montvale_Hotel), the Kirtland Cutter-designed Davenport Hotel (after being vacant for over 20 years), the Fox Theater (now home to the [Spokane Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Symphony))[^cite_note-Davenport-88][^cite_note-Fox-89] as well as the completion of the WSU Pharmaceutical and Biomedical Sciences Building in 2013 and the Davenport Grand Hotel in 2015, [Ridpath Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridpath_Hotel) in 2018 and the ongoing renovation of [Riverfront Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverfront_Park_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) (as of May 2019).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-RPreno-92] The Kendall Yards development on the west side of downtown Spokane is one of the largest construction projects in the city's history. Directly across the Spokane River from downtown, it will blend residential and retail space with plazas and walking trails.[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82]

#### 2026 wildfires

Main article: [Old Trails Fire](83843707.md)

In early August 2026, the fast-moving, ongoing [Old Trails Fire](83843707.md) destroyed over 600 buildings, marking the worst disaster in Spokane's history.[^cite_note-93]

## Geography

### Topography

The Spokane skyline at night from the southwest in Palisades Park

Spokane at night from the west, with [Krell Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krell_Hill) silhouetted by radio antennas

Spokane is located on the [Spokane River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_River) in [eastern Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington) at an elevation of 1,843 feet (562 m) above sea level,[^cite_note-94] about eighteen miles (29 km) west of the [Idaho](14607.md) border, 92 miles (148 km) south of the [Canadian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaline_Falls%E2%80%93Nelway_Border_Crossing), 229 miles (369 km) due east of Seattle, and 279 miles (449 km) southwest of [Calgary, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary), Canada.[^cite_note-95] The lowest elevation in the city of Spokane is the northernmost point of the Spokane River within city limits (in [Riverside State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_State_Park)) at 1,608 feet (490 m); the highest elevation is on the northeast side, near the community of [Hillyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillyard,_Spokane,_Washington) (though closer to Beacon Hill and the North Hill Reservoir) at 2,591 feet (790 m).[^cite_note-96] Spokane is part of the [Inland Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Northwest_\(United_States\)) region, consisting of eastern Washington, [north Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Panhandle), northwestern [Montana](19978.md), and northeastern [Oregon](26811621.md).[^cite_note-97] The city has a total area of 60.02 square miles (155.45 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 59.25 square miles (153.46 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 0.77 square miles (1.99 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_files-98]

The Spokane River rushes past Snxw Meneɂ island in Riverfront Park

The Upper [Spokane Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Falls) of the Spokane River flowing by Canada island

Spokane lies mostly within the [Spokane Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Valley) Outwash Plains at the periphery of the [North Central Rockies forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_Rockies_forests) ecoregion and partly within the eastern edge of the basaltic [Channeled Scablands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channeled_Scablands) steppe of the [Columbia Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Plateau_\(ecoregion\)) ecoregion, a plain that eventually rises sharply to the east towards the rugged, timbered [Selkirk Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkirk_Mountains).[^cite_note-NWS-99][^cite_note-ecoregion-100] The foothills of the [Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains)—the [Coeur d'Alene Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene_Mountains)—rise about 25 miles (40 km) to the east in north Idaho. The city is in a transition area between the barren landscape of the [Columbia Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River_Drainage_Basin) and the coniferous forests to the east; to the south are the lush [prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) and rolling hills of the [Palouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palouse).[^cite_note-ecoregion-100] The highest peak in Spokane County is [Mount Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Spokane), at an elevation of 5,883 feet (1,793 m), on the eastern side of the Selkirk Mountains.[^cite_note-101] The Spokane River is the area's most prominent water feature, a 111-mile (179 km) tributary of the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River), originating from [Lake Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Coeur_d'Alene) in northern Idaho.[^cite_note-Soltero-102] The river flows west across the Washington state line through downtown Spokane, meeting [Latah Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latah_Creek), then turns to the northwest, where it is joined by the [Little Spokane River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Spokane_River) on its way to the Columbia River, north of [Davenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davenport,_Washington).[^cite_note-Soltero-102] The Channeled Scablands and many of the area's numerous large lakes, such as Lake Coeur d'Alene and [Lake Pend Oreille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Pend_Oreille), were formed by the [Missoula Floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula_Floods) after the ice-dammed [Glacial Lake Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_Lake_Missoula) ruptured at the end of the last ice age.[^cite_note-103] The [Turnbull National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnbull_National_Wildlife_Refuge) south of Cheney is the closest natural reserve, the closest National Forest is the [Colville National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colville_National_Forest), the closest [National Recreation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Recreation_Area) is the [Lake Roosevelt National Recreation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Roosevelt_National_Recreation_Area) and the closest national park is [Mount Rainier National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rainier_National_Park), approximately a four-and-a-half hour drive from Spokane.

#### Neighborhoods

Main article: [Neighborhoods in Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_Spokane,_Washington)

Monroe Street corridor and surrounding Emerson-Garfield neighborhood

Monroe Street corridor and surrounding [Emerson/Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson/Garfield,_Spokane) neighborhood

Spokane's neighborhoods range from the [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture)-style South Hill and Browne's Addition, to the Davenport District of Downtown, to the more contemporary neighborhoods of north Spokane. Spokane's neighborhoods are gaining attention for their history, as illustrated by the city being home to 18 recognized [National Register Historical Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).[^cite_note-CC-79][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

Some of Spokane's best-known neighborhoods are [Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_Spokane,_Washington#Riverside), [Browne's Addition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_Spokane,_Washington#Browne.27s_Addition), and [Hillyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillyard,_Spokane). The Riverside neighborhood consists primarily of downtown Spokane and is the central business district of Spokane. The neighborhoods south of downtown Spokane are collectively known as the South Hill. Downtown Spokane contains many of the city's public facilities, including City Hall, Riverfront Park (site of Expo '74), and the Spokane Convention Center, First Interstate Center for the Arts and Spokane Arena. The Spokane County Courthouse and public safety campus is adjacent to downtown in the historic [West Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_Spokane,_Washington#West_Central) neighborhood. To the east of downtown is [East Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_Spokane,_Washington#East_Central) and the adjacent [University District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_District_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) and budding "International District". To the west of downtown is one of Spokane's oldest and densest neighborhoods, Browne's Addition.

A National Historic District west of Downtown, Browne's Addition was Spokane's first prestigious address, notable for its array of old mansions built by Spokane's early elite in the [Queen Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Anne_style_architecture_in_the_United_States) and early [American Craftsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Craftsman) styles.[^cite_note-BA-106] The area houses the Northwest Museum of Arts and Culture. In northeast Spokane, the Hillyard neighborhood began in 1892 as the chosen site for James J. Hill's Great Northern Railway yard, placed outside Spokane city limits to avoid "burdensome taxes".[^cite_note-Hillyard-56] The downtown Hillyard Business District, located on Market Street, was the first Spokane neighborhood listed in the National Register of Historic Places.[^cite_note-Hillyard-56] Many of the former town's houses were built to house railroad workers, mainly immigrant laborers working in the local yard, who gave Hillyard an independent, blue-collar character.[^cite_note-Hillyard-56] Hillyard has become a home for much of Spokane's growing [Russian](59510.md), [Ukrainian](145144.md), and [Southeast Asian](28741.md) communities.[^cite_note-Hillyard-56][^cite_note-TheTimes-107][^cite_note-TheSpokesman-108]

#### Architecture

Main article: [Architecture of Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Spokane,_Washington)

##### Commercial and public buildings

The Romanesque Revival-style Cathedral of Our Lady of Lourdes in Downtown Spokane

The Romanesque Revival-style [Cathedral of Our Lady of Lourdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes_\(Spokane,_Washington\))

Spokane neighborhoods contain a patchwork of architectural styles that give them a distinct identity and illustrate the changes throughout the city's history.[^cite_note-SpokaneStyle-109] Most of Spokane's notable buildings and landmarks are in the Riverside neighborhood and the downtown commercial district, where many of the buildings were rebuilt after the Great Fire of 1889 in the [Romanesque Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_Revival) style.[^cite_note-GreatSpokaneFire-51] Examples include the Great Northern clock tower, Review Building, [Cathedral of Our Lady of Lourdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes_\(Spokane,_Washington\)), [First Congregational Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congregational_Church_of_Spokane), Washington Water Power [Post Street substation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Street_Electric_Substation), Peyton Building, and The Carlyle.[^cite_note-Emporis-110]

The principal architect of many buildings of this period was [Kirtland Kelsey Cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirtland_Cutter).[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82] Self-taught, he came to Spokane in 1886, and began by designing "Chalet Hohenstein" for himself and other residences for his family, while also working as a bank teller.[^cite_note-111] Other structures designed by Cutter include the Spokane Club, Washington Water Power Substation, [Monroe Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Street_Bridge_\(Spokane_River\)) (featured in the city seal), the [Steam Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Steam_Heat_Plant), and the [Davenport Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davenport_Hotel_\(Spokane\)). Built in renaissance and Spanish Revival style, the Davenport Hotel cost two million dollars to complete and included new technologies at the time of its opening in September 1914, such as chilled water, elevators, and air cooling.[^cite_note-Davenport-88]

In the second half of the 20th century, Spokane again became noticed for its architecture, this time by a new generation of architects in the [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-century_modern) movement, which flourished in the city. During this period which lasted from 1948 to the mid-1970s, prolific architects in the city gave Spokane a great breadth of mid-century architecture.[^cite_note-112] The modernists in Spokane gave the city a new look and were instrumental in the developments, design, and legacy of the [Expo '74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'74) World's Fair in what is now [Riverfront Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverfront_Park_\(Spokane,_Washington\)).[^cite_note-113]

During this time, one of the city's foremost and influential architects was Warren C. Heylman.[^cite_note-114] Heylman's career was most productive during the 1960s and 1970s where his main body of work was done in the modernist style, designing numerous residential houses, apartment buildings, and architectural embellishments.[^cite_note-Heylman-115] Some of his most noteworthy works in Spokane include [The Parkade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Parkade), Spokane International Airport, Spokane Regional Health Building, and the Burlington Northern Latah Creek Bridge over Hangman Valley.[^cite_note-Heylman-115]

Kirtland Cutter's Renaissance Revival-style Davenport Hotel, widely considered his magnus opus

The Renaissance Revival-style Davenport Hotel designed by Kirtland Cutter

Other well-represented architectural styles downtown include [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_deco_architecture) (Spokane City Hall, [Paulsen Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Paulsen), [Fox Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Theater_\(Spokane,_Washington\)), [John R. Rogers High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Rogers_High_School), City Ramp Garage), [Renaissance Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Revival) (Steam Plant Square, [Thomas S. Foley Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Post_Office,_Courthouse,_and_Custom_House_\(Spokane,_Washington\)), [Legion Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Club_Building-Legion_Building), [San Marco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marco_\(Spokane,_Washington\))), [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) (Masonic Center, [Hutton Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutton_Building), [Bing Crosby Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_Crosby_Theater)), [Chicago School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_school_\(architecture\)) ([U.S. Bank Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_National_Bank_Building), Liberty Building, [Old City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_City_Hall_Building)) and [Modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism_\(architecture\)) ([The Parkade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkade_Plaza), [Ridpath Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridpath_Hotel), Bank of America Financial Center).[^cite_note-Emporis-110] The [tallest building in the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Spokane), at 288 feet (88 m), is the Bank of America Financial Center.[^cite_note-Emporis-110] Also of note is the Spokane County Courthouse in West Central (the building on the seal of Spokane County), the [Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._John_the_Evangelist_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) in Rockwood, the [Benewah Milk Bottles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benewah_Milk_Bottle) in Riverside and Garland, Mount Saint Michael in Hillyard, and the [Cambern Dutch Shop Windmill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambern_Dutch_Shop_Windmill) in South Perry.

##### Residential

The Patsy Clark Mansion in Browne's Addition

[Patsy Clark Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patsy_Clark_Mansion) in Browne's Addition

As an early affluent Spokane neighborhood, the Browne's Addition neighborhood and residences contain the largest variety of residential architecture in the city.[^cite_note-BA-106] These residences are lavish and personalized, featuring many architectural styles that were popular and trendy in the Pacific Northwest from the late 19th century to 1930, such as the Victorian and Queen Anne styles.[^cite_note-116] In high demand following his firms' design of the [Idaho Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Building_\(Chicago_World's_Fair\)) at the [Chicago World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition) in 1893, Cutter found work constructing many mansions for mining and railroad tycoons such as [Patrick "Patsy" Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patsy_Clark_Mansion) and Daniel C. Corbin and son Austin.[^cite_note-117]

The older neighborhoods of the early 20th century, such as West Central, East Central, [Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan,_Spokane), Hillyard, and much of the lower South Hill, feature a large concentration of American Craftsman style [bungalows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungalow). In Hillyard, the most architecturally intact neighborhood in Spokane, 85 percent of these buildings are historic.[^cite_note-118] As the city expanded mainly to the north in the middle of the 20th century, the bungalows in the "minimal traditional" style commonplace from the 1930s to the 1950s tend to predominate in the Northwest, North Hill, and [Bemiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemiss,_Spokane) neighborhoods. This architectural style occupies the neighborhoods where the integrity of Spokane's street grid pattern is largely intact (especially the areas north of downtown and south of Francis Ave.), and the houses have backyard [alleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alley) for carports, deliveries, and refuse collection. Contemporary suburbs and architecture are prevalent at the north and south edges of Spokane as well as in the new Kendall Yards neighborhood north of downtown.[^cite_note-119]

#### Parks and recreation

The European Duncan Garden in Manito Park and Botanical Gardens

Duncan Garden at Manito Park

In 1907, Spokane's board of park commissioners retained the services of the [Olmsted Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmsted_Brothers) to draw up a plan for Spokane's parks.[^cite_note-120] Much of Spokane's park land was acquired by the city prior to World War I, establishing it early on as a leader among Western cities in the development of a citywide park system.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] Spokane has a system of over 87 parks totaling 4,100 acres (17 km<sup>2</sup>) and includes six neighborhood aquatic centers.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] Some of the most notable parks in Spokane's system are [Riverfront Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverfront_Park_\(Spokane,_Washington\)), [Manito Park and Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manito_Park_and_Botanical_Gardens), [Riverside State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_State_Park), [Saint Michael's Mission State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Saint_Michael), [John A. Finch Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Finch_Arboretum), [High Bridge Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Bridge_Park) and [Liberty Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Park_\(Spokane,_Washington\)).

Riverfront Park, created after Expo '74 and occupying the same site, is 100 acres (40 ha) in downtown Spokane and the site of some of Spokane's largest events.[^cite_note-Riverfront-125] The park has views of the [Spokane Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Falls) and holds a number of civic attractions, including a skyride, a rebuilt [gondola lift](298681.md) that carries visitors across the falls from high above the river gorge.[^cite_note-Riverfront-125] The park also includes the historic hand-carved [Riverfront Park Looff carousel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverfront_Park_Carousel) created in 1909 by [Charles I. D. Looff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I._D._Looff).[^cite_note-Riverfront-125] Riverfront Park is currently being renovated and modernized (as of October 2016).[^cite_note-RPreno-92] Manito Park and Botanical Gardens on Spokane's South Hill features the Duncan Gardens, a classical [European Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Renaissance)-style garden and the Nishinomiya Japanese Garden designed by [Nagao Sakurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagao_Sakurai). Riverside State Park, close to downtown, is a site for outdoor activities such as hiking, mountain biking, and horse riding.[^cite_note-126]

The Spokane area has many trails and [rail trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_trails), the most notable of which is the [Spokane River Centennial Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_River_Centennial_Trail), which features over 37.5 miles (60.4 km) of paved trails running along the Spokane River from Spokane to the Idaho border.[^cite_note-127] This trail continues on towards [Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene,_Idaho) for 24 miles (39 km) as the [North Idaho Centennial Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Idaho_Centennial_Trail) and is often used for alternative transportation and recreational use. In the summer, it has long been popular to visit North Idaho's "Lake Country", such as [Lake Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Coeur_d'Alene), [Lake Pend Oreille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Pend_Oreille), [Priest Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priest_Lake), or one of the other nearby bodies of water and beaches.[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82][^cite_note-128] In the winter, the public has access to five ski resorts within a couple hours of the city. The closest of these is the [Mt. Spokane Ski and Snowboard Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Spokane),[^cite_note-129] which has trails for cross-country skiing, snowshoeing, snowmobiling, and dog sledding.[^cite_note-130]

Zoological parks in Spokane include [Cat Tales Zoological Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_Tales_Zoological_Park), a wildlife sanctuary primarily for big cats and the Blue Zoo an interactive aquarium in the [NorthTown Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NorthTown_Mall_\(Spokane,_Washington\)).[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

### Flora and fauna

Yellow bellied marmot

Urban-dwelling [yellow bellied marmots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_bellied_marmot) are resident in the city, although the rodents typically inhabit remote, mountainous locations.

[^cite_note-133]

The area supports an abundance of wildlife in part because of its varied geology and natural history. The area contains a wide range of vegetation, from densely wooded [coniferous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_coniferous_forest) to rolling grassy hills and meadows.[^cite_note-134] Ponderosa pine and Douglas fir are common in the drier and lower elevation areas throughout the region. The [ponderosa pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponderosa_pine) is the official tree of the City of Spokane, which is where specimens were first collected by botanist [David Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Douglas_\(botanist\)) in 1826.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]<sup>[\[g\]](#endnote_Douglas)</sup>

The Canadian Rockies ecoregion supports 70 mammals, 16 reptiles and amphibians, 168 birds, and 41 fish species.[^cite_note-wildlife-137] There is a high concentration of raptors in the area, bald eagles are a common sight near Lake Coeur d'Alene in December and January when [kokanee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sockeye_salmon#Landlocked_populations) spawn.[^cite_note-138] The most common fish present in area lakes is the Washington-native [rainbow trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_trout), which is the official fish of Washington state.[^cite_note-139] Big game common in eastern Washington include black and grizzly bears, [caribou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribou), [Rocky Mountain elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_elk), bighorn sheep, and cougar.[^cite_note-140] Whitetail deer, mule deer, and [moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose) are also found in abundance. The [gray wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_wolf) population has been making a recovery in the Inland Northwest. As of June 2016, there are 16 wolf packs in eastern Washington.[^cite_note-141] In August 2016, photo evidence confirmed a solitary wolf in Mount Spokane State Park.[^cite_note-142]

Although the ecoregion remains ecologically intact, it faces conservation challenges that include the negative effects of certain forestry management and logging practices, higher risks of forest fires due to the alteration of the trees that make up the forest composition, and [habitat fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_fragmentation) as a result of urban sprawl and development, which endangers the long-term survival of vulnerable species such as mountain caribou and the [American goshawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_goshawk).[^cite_note-143]

### Climate

The Great Northern clocktower seen amidst an orange haze from wildfire smoke and Rayleigh scattering

The Great Northern clocktower seen amidst an orange [haze](998156.md) from wildfire smoke and [Rayleigh scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_scattering)

Spokane has a [warm-summer humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dsb* under the [Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) classification),[^cite_note-144] a rare climate due to its elevation and significant winter precipitation; Spokane, however, is adjacent to and sometimes even classified as a [warm-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate#Warm-summer_Mediterranean_climate) (*Csb*) because the average temperature for the coldest month is over 26.6 °F (−3 °C),[^cite_note-NCEI_Summary_of_Monthly_Normals_-_1991-2020_-_SPOKANE_INTL_AP-145] though in the US this threshold is often defined to be 32 °F (0 °C).[^cite_note-146]

The area typically has a warm, arid climate during the summer months, bracketed by short spring and fall seasons. On average, the warmest month is July and the coolest month is December; July averages 71.0 °F (21.7 °C), while December averages 29.1 °F (−1.6 °C).[^cite_note-NCEI_Summary_of_Monthly_Normals_-_1991-2020_-_SPOKANE_INTL_AP-145]<sup>[\[h\]](#endnote_Averages)</sup> Daily temperature ranges are large during the summer, often exceeding 30 °F (17 °C), and small during the winter, with a range just above 10 °F (5.6 °C). The record high and low are 112 °F (44 °C)[^cite_note-NOWData_OTX-147] and −30 °F (−34 °C), but temperatures of more than 100 °F (38 °C) or less than −5 °F (−21 °C) are rare. Temperatures of 90 °F (32 °C)+ occur an average of 21 days annually, temperatures of 100 °F (38 °C)+ occur an average of only 1 day annually, and those at or below 0 °F (−18 °C) average 2.2 days a year.[^cite_note-NOWData_OTX-147]



Climate chart for Spokane

Spokane's location, between the [Cascades Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascades_Range) to the west and the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) to the east and north, protects it from weather patterns experienced in other parts of the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest). The Cascade Mountains form a barrier to the eastward flow of moist and relatively mild air from the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) in winter and cool air in summer.[^cite_note-ClimWA-148] As a result of the [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) effect of the Cascades, the Spokane area has 16.5 inches (420 mm) average annual precipitation, less than half of Seattle's 39.3 inches (1,000 mm).[^cite_note-NOWData_SEW-149] Precipitation peaks in December, and summer is the driest time of the year. The Rockies shield Spokane from some of the winter season's coldest air masses traveling southward across Canada.[^cite_note-ClimWA-148] In the summer season, Spokane, like much of the [western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States), has been experiencing drier conditions and more frequent and larger [wildfire](56106.md) events since the late 20th century; the fine particulate matter in the smoke can be carried by the wind and blanket the region in a [haze](998156.md) and impact Spokane's [air quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_quality_index).[^cite_note-150]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Spokane had a population of 228,989. The median age was 36.6 years, 21.0% of residents were under the age of 18, and 16.2% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 96.8 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 94.9 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-167]

According to the 2020 census, 99.9% of residents lived in urban areas and 0.1% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-168]

There were 94,494 households in Spokane, of which 26.3% had children under the age of 18 living in them, 37.9% were married-couple households, 22.0% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 30.6% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 34.2% of all households were made up of individuals and 12.9% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-167]

There were 99,938 housing units, of which 5.4% were vacant. The homeowner vacancy rate was 1.3% and the rental vacancy rate was 5.1%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-167]

### Religion

According to the [Association of Religion Data Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Religion_Data_Archives)' 2010 Metro Area Membership Report, the denominational affiliations of the Spokane MSA are 64,277 [Evangelical Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), 682 [Black Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_church), 24,826 [Mainline Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant), 754 [Orthodox](10186.md), 66,202 [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism), 31,674 Other, and 339,338 Unclaimed.[^cite_note-180] As of 2016, there are also at least three Jewish congregations.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

The Emanu-El congregation erected the first synagogue in Spokane and the state of Washington on September 14, 1892.[^cite_note-184] The city's first mosque opened in 2009 as the Spokane Islamic Center.[^cite_note-185] Spokane, like Washington and the Pacific Northwest region as a whole, is part of the [Unchurched Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unchurched_Belt), a region characterized by low church membership rates and religious participation.[^cite_note-186] The city serves as the [seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedra) of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Spokane), which was established in 1913,[^cite_note-187] and the [Episcopal Diocese of Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Diocese_of_Spokane), established in 1929.[^cite_note-episcopal-188] The [Spokane Washington Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Washington_Temple), established in 1999, serves [Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter-day_Saints) from the east of the county.[^cite_note-189]

Spokane has hosted an annual multicultural celebration, Unity in the Community, since 1995.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191] The city has become more diverse in recent decades. People from countries in the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (especially Russians and Ukrainians) form a comparatively large demographic in Spokane and Spokane County, the result of a large influx of immigrants and their families after the dissolution of the USSR in 1991.[^cite_note-TheTimes-107][^cite_note-KyivPost-192] According to the 2000 Census, the number of people of Russian or Ukrainian ancestry in Spokane County was reported to be 7,700 (4,900 residing in the city of Spokane), amounting to two percent of the county.[^cite_note-KyivPost-192] Among the fastest-growing demographics in Spokane is the [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander) ethnic group, which is estimated to be the third-largest minority group in the county, after the Russian and Ukrainian community and Latinos.[^cite_note-TheSpokesman-108] Spokane was once home to a sizable Asian community, mostly Japanese, centered in a district called [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Spokane) from the early days of the city until 1974.[^cite_note-Chinatown-193][^cite_note-194] As in many western [railway towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_town), the Asian community started off as an encampment for migrant laborers working on the railroads. The Chinatown Asian community thrived until the 1940s and experienced a population boom during WWII as Japanese families fled the exclusion zones along the coast, after which its population decreased and became integrated and dispersed, losing its Asian character; urban blight and the preparations leading up to Expo '74 led to Chinatown's eventual demolition.[^cite_note-Chinatown-193]

### Metropolitan area

Main article: [Spokane metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_metropolitan_area)

The Spokane metropolitan area consists of Spokane County. As of the 2022 census estimates, the Spokane metropolitan area had a population of 597,919. Directly east of Spokane County is the Coeur d'Alene Metropolitan Statistical Area, which consists of [Kootenai County, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kootenai_County,_Idaho), anchored by the city of [Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene,_Idaho). The urban areas of the two MSAs largely follow the path of Interstate 90 between Spokane and Coeur d'Alene. The Spokane area has suffered from suburbanization and [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) in past decades, despite Washington's use of [urban growth boundaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_growth_boundary); the city ranks low among major Northwest cities in population density and [smart growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_growth) according to the Sightline Institute, however [Smart Growth America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Growth_America) in a 2014 study ranked the census defined MSA as the 22nd most compact and connected in the nation using their Sprawl Index factors: development density, land use mix, activity centering, and street accessibility.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] The Spokane and Coeur d'Alene [Metropolitan Statistical Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Statistical_Area) (MSA) are now included in a single [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area) (CSA) by the [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget).[^cite_note-CSA-197] The [Spokane–Coeur d'Alene CSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane-Spokane_Valley-Coeur_d'Alene,_WA-ID_CSA) had around 781,497 residents in 2022.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Spokane,_Washington)

The Peyton Building in Spokane's Central Business District

The [Spokane Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Stock_Exchange) once occupied the Peyton Building

Spokane became an important rail and shipping center because of its location between mining and farming areas.[^cite_note-Railroads-46][^cite_note-Schmeltzerp.41-57] In the early 1880s, gold and silver were discovered in the Inland Empire; as a regional shipping center, the city furnished supplies to the miners who passed through on their way to the mineral-rich [Coeur d'Alene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Valley,_Idaho), [Colville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colville,_Washington) and [Kootenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kootenays) districts.[^cite_note-p._28-47] The mining districts are still considered among the most productive in North America.[^cite_note-198]

Natural resources have historically been the foundation of Spokane's economy, with the mining, logging, and agriculture industries providing much of the region's economic activity. After mining declined at the turn of the 20th century, agriculture and logging replaced mining as the primary influences in the economy.[^cite_note-Kenselp.25-67] [Lumberjacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumberjack) and millmen working in the hundreds of mills along the railroads, rivers, and lakes of northern Washington and Idaho were provisioning themselves in Spokane.[^cite_note-199] Agriculture has always been an important sector in the local economy. The surrounding area, especially to the south is the [Palouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palouse),[^cite_note-200] a region that has long been associated with farming, especially [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) production where it is one of the largest [wheat producing regions in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_production_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-p._119-70][^cite_note-201] As with the mining industry in the late 1880s, Spokane was an important agricultural market and trade center. Inland Empire farmers exported wheat, livestock and other agricultural products to the ports such as New York, Liverpool and Tokyo.[^cite_note-p._127-72] Today, a large share of the wheat produced in the region is shipped to [Far East](11672.md) markets.[^cite_note-202] The Inland Northwest also supports many [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_wine) and [microbreweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbrewery) as well.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204] By the early 20th century Spokane was primarily a commercial center rather than an industrial center.[^cite_note-205]

The Old National Bank Building in Spokane's Central Business District

The [Old National Bank Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_National_Bank_Building)

In Spokane, wood and food processing, printing and publishing, primary metal refining and fabrication, electrical and computer equipment, and transportation equipment are leaders in the manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-SCP-206] Gold mining company [Gold Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Reserve_\(company\)), and [Fortune 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_1000) company [Potlatch Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PotlatchDeltic) – a forest products company that operates as a [real estate investment trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_investment_trust) – are headquartered in the city proper.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208] Mining, forestry, and agribusiness remain important to the local and regional economy, but Spokane's economy has diversified to include other industries, including the high-tech and biotech sectors.[^cite_note-Diverse-81] Spokane is becoming a more service-oriented economy in the face of a less prominent manufacturing sector which declined in the 1980s, particularly as a medical and [biotechnology](4502.md) center;[^cite_note-Thumbnail-82] Fortune 1000 technology company [Itron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itron), for instance, is headquartered in the area.[^cite_note-209] Avista Corporation, the holding company of [Avista Utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avista_Utilities), is the only company in Spokane that has been listed in the [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500), ranked 299 on the list in 2002.[^cite_note-210] Other companies with head offices in the Spokane area include technology company [Key Tronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Tronic), vacation rental provider Stay Alfred, and [microcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcar) maker [Commuter Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_Cars).[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213] Despite diversification to new industries, Spokane's economy has struggled in recent decades. Spokane was ranked the #1 "Worst City For Jobs" in America in both 2012[^cite_note-214] and 2015,[^cite_note-215] while also ranking #4 in 2014.[^cite_note-216] Additionally, Forbes named Spokane the "Scam Capital of America" in 2009[^cite_note-217] due to widespread business fraud. Trends of fraud were noted as far back as 1988,[^cite_note-forbes.com-218] again in 2002,[^cite_note-219] and continuing through 2011.[^cite_note-forbes.com-218]

As of 2013, the top five employers in Spokane are the [State of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Washington), [Spokane Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Public_Schools), [Providence Sacred Heart Medical Center and Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Sacred_Heart_Medical_Center_and_Children's_Hospital), the [92d Air Refueling Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/92d_Air_Refueling_Wing), and [Spokane County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_County).[^cite_note-220] The largest military facility and employer, the 92d Air Refueling Wing, was stationed at [Fairchild Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Air_Force_Base) near [Airway Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway_Heights). The leading industries in Spokane for the employed population 16 years and older were educational services, health care, and social assistance (26.5 percent), retail trade (12.7 percent), and arts, entertainment, recreation, and accommodation food services (10.4 percent).[^cite_note-wwwcensusgov-179] As the metropolitan center of the Inland Northwest, as well as parts of southern [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Interior#Southern_Interior) and [Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Alberta), Spokane serves as a commercial, manufacturing, transportation, medical, shopping, and entertainment hub.[^cite_note-SCP-206][^cite_note-221] In 2017, the Spokane–Spokane Valley [MSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) had a gross metropolitan product of \$25.5 billion while the Coeur d'Alene metropolitan area was \$5.93 billion.[^cite_note-222]

As of 2014, economic development in the Spokane area primarily focuses on promoting the following industries: manufacturing (especially aerospace manufacturing), health sciences, professional services, information science and technology, finance and insurance as well as clean technology, and digital media.[^cite_note-SpokaneHub-223][^cite_note-224] To aid economic development, the eastern branch of Innovate Washington, a state-supported [business incubator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_incubator) was placed in the city.[^cite_note-225]

In recent years, Spokane has become a growing technology hub for both established companies and startups. [Fortune 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_1000) cybersecurity leader, [F5, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F5,_Inc.), has two offices in the area with over 250 employees whom are focused on hardware product development, software engineering, global services support, and digital sales.[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227] Other established firms are moving to Spokane, such as [Remitly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remitly), an app-based financial services corporation, which was founded by Josh Hug, a Whitworth University graduate.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229] Ignite Northwest, led by Silicon Valley entrepreneur Tom Simpson, has invested over \$100 million through the Spokane Angel Alliance and Ignite to fund and support early stage companies.[^cite_note-230]

### Arts and theater

Spokane's main art districts are located in the Davenport Arts District, the [Garland Business District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_Spokane,_Washington#Garland), and East Sprague.[^cite_note-231] The First Friday Artwalk, which occurs the first Friday of every month, is dedicated to local vendors and performers displaying art around downtown.[^cite_note-232] The two most important Artwalk dates (the first Friday of February and October) attract large crowds to the art districts. The Davenport Arts District has the largest concentration of art galleries and is home to many of Spokane's main performing arts venues, including the [Knitting Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knitting_Factory), [Fox Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Theater_\(Spokane,_Washington\)), and [Bing Crosby Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_Crosby_Theater). The Knitting Factory is a concert house that serves as a setting for many mainstream touring musicians and acts. The [Martin Woldson Theater at the Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Woldson_Theater_at_the_Fox), restored to its original 1931 Art Deco state after years of being derelict,[^cite_note-Fox-89] is home to the Spokane Symphony Orchestra. The Metropolitan Performing Arts Center was restored in 1988 and renamed the Bing Crosby Theater in 2006 to honor the former Spokanite.[^cite_note-233] Touring stand-up comedians are hosted by the Spokane Comedy Club.[^cite_note-234] Theater is provided by Spokane's only resident professional company, The Modern Theater,[^cite_note-235] though there are also the [Spokane Civic Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Civic_Theatre) and several other amateur community theaters and smaller groups. The [First Interstate Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Interstate_Center_for_the_Arts) often hosts large traveling exhibitions, shows, and tours. Spokane was awarded the [All-America City Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-America_City_Award) by the [National Civic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Civic_League) in 1974, 2004, and 2015.[^cite_note-236]

Spokane offers an array of musical performances catering to a variety of interests. Spokane's local music scene, however, is considered somewhat lacking by the Spokane All-Ages Music Initiative and other critics, who have identified a need for a legitimate all-ages venue for music performances.[^cite_note-237] The Spokane Symphony presents a full season of classical music, and the Spokane Jazz Orchestra, a full season of jazz music.[^cite_note-238] The Spokane Jazz Orchestra, formed in 1962, is a 70-piece orchestra and non-profit organization.[^cite_note-239]

### Museums





Northwest Museum of Arts in Culture

There are several museums in the city, most notably the [Northwest Museum of Arts and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Museum_of_Arts_and_Culture), located a few blocks from the center of downtown in Browne's Addition, amid the mansions of Spokane's late 19th-century "Age of Elegance". A [Smithsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian) affiliate museum, it houses a large collection of Native American artifacts as well as regional and national traveling art exhibits.[^cite_note-MAC-240][^cite_note-241]

The Mobius Science Center and the related Mobius Kid's Museum in downtown Spokane seek to generate interest in [science, technology, engineering, and math](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEM_fields) among the youth in a hands-on experience.[^cite_note-242] The Jundt Art Museum at Gonzaga University features 2,800 square feet (260 m<sup>2</sup>) of exhibition space and contains sizable collections of prints from the Bolker, Baruch, Jacobs, and [Corita Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corita_Kent) collections.[^cite_note-Jundt-243][^cite_note-244] The museum houses glass art by [Dale Chihuly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Chihuly), bronze sculptures by [Auguste Rodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Rodin), tapestries, paintings, ceramics, photographs, and a wide range of gifts, including from the Iris and B. Gerald Cantor Foundation and Collections.[^cite_note-Jundt-243] On the campus of Gonzaga University, the Crosby House, Bing Crosby's childhood home, houses the Bing Crosby Memorabilia Room, the world's largest Crosby collection with around 200 pieces.[^cite_note-245] A museum of flight showcasing historic airplanes and curated by the Historic Flight Foundation is located at Felts Field.[^cite_note-246]

### Events and activities

Spokane is known as the birthplace of the national movement started by [Sonora Smart Dodd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonora_Smart_Dodd) that led to the proposal and the eventual establishment of [Father's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father's_Day_\(United_States\)) as a [national holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_observances_in_the_United_States_by_presidential_proclamation) in the U.S.[^cite_note-247] The first observation of Father's Day in Spokane was on June 19, 1910.[^cite_note-248] Sonora conceived the idea in Spokane's Central Methodist Episcopal Church, while listening to a Mother's Day sermon.[^cite_note-249]

Runners participating in Spokane's annual Lilac Bloomsday Run

&#x20;Lilac Bloomsday Run

The [Lilac Bloomsday Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilac_Bloomsday_Run), held in the spring on the first Sunday of May, is a 7.46-mile (12.01 km) race for competitive runners as well as walkers that attracts international competition.[^cite_note-250] Also in May is the Lilac Festival which honors the military, celebrates youth, and showcases the region.[^cite_note-251] Spokane's unofficial nickname, the "[Lilac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilac) City", refers to a flowering shrub that has flourished since its introduction to the area in the early 20th century.[^cite_note-252] In June the city hosts [Spokane Hoopfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Hoopfest), a 3-on-3 basketball tournament, among the largest of its kind.[^cite_note-253] One of Spokane's most popular local events is Pig Out in the Park, an annual six-day food and entertainment festival where attendees may eat a variety of foods and listen to free live music concerts featuring local, regional, and national recording artists in Riverfront Park.[^cite_note-254]

The Spokane International Film Festival, held every February, is a small, juried festival that features documentaries and [shorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film) from around the world.[^cite_note-255] The Spokane Gay & Lesbian Film Festival, held every November, features contemporary, independent films of interest to the [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) community.[^cite_note-256]

Other notable events in the Spokane region include the Spokane County Interstate Fair, Japan Week, Spokane Pride Parade and the Lilac City Comicon. The Spokane County Interstate Fair is held annually in September at the Spokane County Fair and Expo Center in [Spokane Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Valley,_Washington).[^cite_note-257] Japan Week is held in April and celebrates the sister-city relationship with [Nishinomiya, Hyogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishinomiya,_Hyogo), demonstrating the many commonalities shared between the two cities.[^cite_note-258] Students from the Spokane campus of [Mukogawa Fort Wright Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukogawa_Women's_University), Gonzaga, Whitworth, and other area schools organize an array of Japanese cultural events. The gay and lesbian Spokane Pride Parade is held each June.[^cite_note-259] There is an annual [Renaissance fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_fair) and [Civil War reenactment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War_reenactment) as well.[^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261]

The City of Spokane operates under a [mayor–council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government) form of government, with executive and legislative branches that are elected in [non-partisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-partisan) elections.[^cite_note-262] [David Condon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Condon) was elected mayor in November 2011 and took office on the last business day of the year.[^cite_note-263] The previous mayor was [Mary Verner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Verner), who succeeded [Dennis P. Hession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_P._Hession) who himself succeeded the recalled [James "Jim" West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._West_\(politician\)). The city elected [James Everett Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Everett_Chase) as its first African-American mayor in 1981, and after his retirement, elected the city's first woman mayor, Vicki McNeil.[^cite_note-264] Spokane is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Spokane County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_County,_Washington), a position it wrested from [Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheney,_Washington) in 1886.[^cite_note-NAco-265][^cite_note-266] Spokane is a part of [Washington's 3rd legislative district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington's_3rd_legislative_district), which is represented in the [Washington State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Senate) by [Andy Billig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Billig).[^cite_note-WA_State_Lawmakers-267] The 3rd Legislative District is represented in the [Washington House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_House_of_Representatives) by [Marcus Riccelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Riccelli) and [Timm Ormsby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timm_Ormsby).[^cite_note-WA_State_Lawmakers-267]

Federally, Spokane is within [Washington's 5th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington's_5th_congressional_district), and has been represented in the [House of Representatives](19468510.md) by Republican [Michael Baumgartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Baumgartner) since 2025.[^cite_note-Leg-268] Washington State is represented nationally in the [Senate](24909346.md) by Democrat [Patty Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Murray) and Democrat [Maria Cantwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Cantwell).[^cite_note-Leg-268] In the [2012 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election), Spokane County favored [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney) for president over [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) by 51.5 to 45.7 percent; on the state ballot, the county supported the [legalization of recreational marijuana ballot measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Initiative_502) by 52.2 to 47.9 percent but opposed the [legalization of same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Referendum_74) by 55.9 to 44.1 percent.[^cite_note-2012Election-269] Spokane native [Tom Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Foley) was a Democratic [Speaker of the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_U.S._House_of_Representatives) and served as a representative of Washington's 5th district for 30 years, enjoying large support from Spokane, until his narrow defeat in the "[Republican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Revolution)" of 1994,[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-Foley-271] the only time U.S. voters have turned out a sitting Speaker of the House since 1860.[^cite_note-272]

The crime rate per 1,000 people in the Spokane metropolitan area (Spokane County) was 64.8 in 2012, higher than the Washington state average of 38.3; the violent crime rate of 3.8 and property crime rate of 61 also exceed the statewide averages of 2.5 and 35.8, respectively.[^cite_note-273] [NeighborhoodScout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeighborhoodScout) describes Spokane as "Safer than 2% of U.S. Cities".[^cite_note-NeighborhoodScout-274]

The Spokane County Courthouse in the West Central neighborhood

Spokane County Courthouse

Half of all property crimes are localized in about 6.5 percent of the city.[^cite_note-Crime-275] Spokane had the fourth-highest rate of auto theft in the U.S. in 2010 and 2011, according to the [National Insurance Crime Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Insurance_Crime_Bureau).[^cite_note-Crime-275] Drive-by shootings and drug use, particularly [crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine) use, became worse in the early 1990s, and four drive-by shootings were recorded in December 1993 alone.[^cite_note-Kienholz-276] In the 1990s, the [Spokane Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Police_Department) (SPD) established a special gang unit, with an officer "collecting intelligence on gang activity and disseminating it to street officers".[^cite_note-Kienholz-276] The 1990s also saw Spokane's most prolific serial killer, [Robert Lee Yates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lee_Yates), who killed thirteen prostitutes in Spokane's East Sprague red light district and confessed to two others in [Tacoma, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma,_Washington).[^cite_note-277] The transition of the Spokane Police Department to a community-policing [precinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_precinct) model has helped curb crime rates since its introduction downtown, and has been expanded citywide.[^cite_note-278] The crime woes are possibly due in part to an imbalance that Spokane County prisons receive of pre-release and work-release prisoners; An investigation by the *Tacoma News Tribune* found that while Spokane County accounts for 6.21 percent of the inmates in state prisons, it receives a disproportionate 16.73 percent of the inmate population to be released into the general population.[^cite_note-279]

Spokane and the Spokane Police Department have received national publicity and scrutiny in the 2000s and 2010s due to many [officer-involved shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_killings_by_law_enforcement_officers_in_the_United_States) and allegations of excessive force. The most high-profile of these incidents was the 2006 [death of Otto Zehm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Otto_Zehm), a mentally challenged man who was initially suspected of theft at a convenience store.[^cite_note-280] Zehm was later found to have committed no crime, but was struck with batons by several officers and tasered.[^cite_note-281] The increased pressure on the SPD prompted an independent review by a commission of the organization's use-of-force policies, an internal culture audit, and the purchase of [body cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_camera).[^cite_note-282]

### Housing

Restrictive zoning regulations were implemented in Spokane in the middle of the 20th century.[^cite_note-\:1-283][^cite_note-284] These zoning regulations were frequently motivated by a desire to keep lower-income families out of certain neighborhoods, in particular racial minorities.[^cite_note-\:1-283]

In 2022, Spokane relaxed its zoning regulations to permit on an interim basis duplexes, triplexes, quadplexes and townhomes in all residential zones of Spokane. In 2023, Spokane permanently permitted up to six housing units to be built on any lot in a residential area, as well as allow nonresidential businesses (such as grocery stores) and facilities (such as schools and churches) in residential areas.[^cite_note-\:1-283]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Spokane,_Washington)

### Public and private schools

[Spokane Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Public_Schools) (District 81) was organized in 1889, and is the largest public school system in Spokane, and the second-largest in the state, as of 2014, serving roughly 30,000 students in six high schools, six middle schools, and thirty-four elementary schools.[^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286] Other public school districts in the Spokane area include the [Mead School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mead_School_District) in north Spokane County, outside city limits. A variety of state-approved, independent [charter schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_school) and private and [parochial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parochial_school) elementary and secondary schools augment the public school system. The [Roman Catholic Diocese of Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Spokane) manages ten such schools in & around the area.[^cite_note-287]

### Higher education

St. Aloysius Church



[St. Aloysius Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Aloysius_Church_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) at Gonzaga University and the [WSU Health Sciences Spokane campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WSU_Health_Sciences_Spokane_campus), located in the [University District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_District_\(Spokane,_Washington\))

Spokane is home to many higher education institutions. They include the private universities [Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_University) and [Whitworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitworth_University), and the public [Community Colleges of Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Colleges_of_Spokane) system ([Spokane Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Community_College) and [Spokane Falls Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Falls_Community_College)) as well as a variety of technical institutes. Gonzaga University and [Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_University_School_of_Law) were founded by the Italian-born priest [Joseph Cataldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cataldo) and the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) in 1887.[^cite_note-288] Whitworth was founded in Tacoma, Washington in 1890 and moved to its present location in 1914.[^cite_note-289] It is affiliated with the [Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(U.S.A.\)) and had 2,500 students studying in 53 different undergraduate and degree programs as of 2011.[^cite_note-290] While Spokane is one of the larger cities in the U.S. to lack a main campus of a state-supported university within its city limits, [Eastern Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington_University) (EWU) and [Washington State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University) (WSU) have operations at the [Riverpoint Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverpoint_Campus) in the University District, just adjacent to downtown and across the Spokane River from the Gonzaga campus.[^cite_note-291][^cite_note-292] [Washington State University Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University_Spokane) is WSU's health sciences campus and houses the school's [College of Nursing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University_College_of_Nursing), College of Pharmacy, and [Elson S. Floyd College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elson_S._Floyd_College_of_Medicine).[^cite_note-ElsonSFloyd-85][^cite_note-293] The main EWU campus is located 15 miles (24 km) southwest of Spokane in nearby Cheney, and WSU is located 65 miles (105 km) to the south in [Pullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullman,_Washington). In addition to WSU's health science presence in Spokane, there is also a four-year [medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_school) branch affiliated with the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington)'s [WWAMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWAMI_Regional_Medical_Education_Program) program.[^cite_note-WWAMI-84] An international branch campus of the [Mukogawa Women's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukogawa_Women's_University), the Mukogawa Fort Wright Institute, is located in Spokane.[^cite_note-294]

### Libraries

Serving the general educational needs of the local population are two public library districts, the [Spokane Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Public_Library) (within city limits) and the [Spokane County Library District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_County_Library_District). Founded in 1904 with funding from philanthropist [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie), the Spokane Public Library system comprises a downtown library overlooking the Spokane Falls and five branch libraries.[^cite_note-295] Special collections focus on Inland Pacific Northwest history and include reference books, periodicals, maps, photographs, and other archival materials and government documents.[^cite_note-296]

## Sports

Numerica Veterans Arena

Spokane is close to dozens of lakes and rivers for outdoor sports and recreation. People use these for swimming, boating, kayaking, rafting, and fishing. Nearby mountains provide for skiing, hiking, biking and sightseeing.[^cite_note-LiveAndLaunch-297] The Spokane region's professional and semi-professional sports teams include the [Spokane Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Indians) in Minor League Baseball and the [Spokane Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Chiefs) in junior ice hockey.[^cite_note-Teams-298] Collegiate sports in Spokane focus on the local teams such as the [Gonzaga Bulldogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_Bulldogs) who compete in the [NCAA](60706.md)'s [Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_I_\(NCAA\)) [West Coast Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Conference) and the [Whitworth Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitworth_Pirates) playing in the [Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_III_\(NCAA\)) [Northwest Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Conference) and local media covers other regional teams, including the [Eastern Washington Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington_Eagles), [Washington State Cougars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Cougars), and the [Idaho Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Vandals).[^cite_note-Teams-298]

### Baseball

The Spokane Indians located in the suburb [Spokane Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Valley,_Washington), are a [Class High-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-A) baseball team in the [Northwest League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_League) (NWL) and have been a [farm team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_team) of the [Colorado Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rockies) since 2021.[^cite_note-299] The Indians play their home games at the 6,803-seat [Avista Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avista_Stadium) and have won seven NWL titles since their Short-Season-A debut in 1982. Prior to 1982, the Indians played at the [Triple-A level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-A_\(baseball\)). The team achieved considerable success in the early 1970s, winning the [Pacific Coast League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Coast_League) championship in 1970, and having a 94–52 record.[^cite_note-300] In the 1920s and 1930s the Spokane City League, a semiprofessional baseball league of teams of the Inland Empire, reached its peak.[^cite_note-301]

### Hockey

The Spokane Chiefs are a junior ice hockey team that play in the [Canadian Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Hockey_League)'s [Western Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hockey_League).[^cite_note-CBC-302] They play their home games in the Spokane Arena and have a regional rivalry with the [Tri-City Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-City_Americans). They have won the CHL's top prize, the [Memorial Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Cup), two times in club history, first in 1991 and again in 2008.[^cite_note-CBC-302]

### Soccer

Spokane is host to [United Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_League) professional men's [USL League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_One) ([Spokane Velocity FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Velocity)), a women's pre-professional [USL W League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_W_League) team, as well as a women's professional [USL Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Super_League) team ([Spokane Zephyr FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Zephyr_FC)).[^cite_note-\:0-303] They play their home games in [ONE Spokane Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Spokane_Stadium).

### Major events

The [Spokane Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Arena) is the city's premier sports venue. In the years since the Spokane Arena opened, it has played host to several major sporting events. The first major event was the [1998 Memorial Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Memorial_Cup), the championship game of the Canadian Hockey League.[^cite_note-304] Four years later in 2002, the city hosted the [2002 Skate America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Skate_America) figure skating competition[^cite_note-305] and then the [2007 U.S. Figure Skating Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_U.S._Figure_Skating_Championships) in the Spokane Arena.[^cite_note-FigureSkating-306] The latter event set an attendance record, selling nearly 155,000 tickets. Spokane later hosted the [2010 U.S. Figure Skating Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_U.S._Figure_Skating_Championships)[^cite_note-FigureSkating-306] – ending eighteen days before the start of the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) in [Vancouver, British Columbia](32706.md) and then the [2016 Team Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Team_Challenge_Cup).[^cite_note-307] Spokane is also home to [The Podium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Podium_\(sports_facility\)) indoor hydraulic track and event space, and multi-use [One Spokane Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Spokane_Stadium).

### Print

Newspaper service in Spokane is provided by its only major daily newspaper, *[The Spokesman-Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spokesman-Review)*, which has a daily circulation of 76,291 and Sunday circulation of 95,939.[^cite_note-308][^cite_note-309] *The Spokesman-Review* was formed from the merger of the *Spokane Falls Review* (1883–1894) and the *Spokesman* (1890–1893) in 1893 and was first published under the present name on June 29, 1894.[^cite_note-310][^cite_note-311] It later absorbed the competing afternoon paper *The [Spokane Daily Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Daily_Chronicle)*, a significant newspaper that existed from 1881 until 1982 and returned in 2021.[^cite_note-S-R-312][^cite_note-313]<sup>[\[j\]](#endnote_Spokesman)</sup> More specialized publications include the weekly [alternative newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_newspaper) *[Inlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inlander_\(newspaper\))*,[^cite_note-314] the bi-weekly *[Spokane Journal of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Journal_of_Business)*,[^cite_note-315] and the student-run *[Gonzaga Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gonzaga_Bulletin)*.[^cite_note-316] Monthly publications include [*The Black Lens*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lens_News), an African American community newspaper,[^cite_note-317] a newspaper for parents, *Kids Newspaper*,[^cite_note-318] and a home and lifestyle magazine, *Spokane Coeur d'Alene Living*.[^cite_note-319]

### Radio

According to [Arbitron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitron), Spokane is the 94th-largest radio market in the U.S., with 532,100 listeners aged 12 and over.[^cite_note-320] There are 28 AM and FM radio stations broadcast in the city. The five most listened-to stations are [KKZX-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KKZX) (classic rock), [KQNT-AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KQNT) (news/talk), [KXLY-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KXLY-FM) (country), [KISC-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KISC) (adult contemporary),[KZBD-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KZBD) (Contemporary Hit Radio), and [KZZU-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KZZU-FM) ([Hot AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Adult_Contemporary)).[^cite_note-321] Spokane's primary sources of non-commercial and community radio include Spokane's [NPR](29697232.md)-affiliate station [KPBX-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPBX-FM) and [KYRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KYRS), a full-power community radio station.[^cite_note-322]

### Television

Spokane is the 73rd-largest television market in the U.S., accounting for 0.366% of the total TV households in the U.S.[^cite_note-323] The city has six television stations, representing the major commercial networks and public television.[^cite_note-TV-324] Spokane is the television broadcast center for much of eastern Washington (except the [Yakima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakima,_Washington) and [Tri-Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-Cities,_Washington) area), northern Idaho, northwestern Montana, northeastern Oregon, and parts of southern Canada (by [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_television_stations_available_in_Canada#Shaw_Broadcast_Services_/_nationwide_coverage)). Spokane receives broadcasts in the [Pacific Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone), with weekday prime time beginning at 8 pm. Montana and Alberta, Canada are in the [Mountain Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Time_Zone) and receive Spokane broadcasts one hour later by their local time. The major network television affiliates include [KREM (TV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KREM_\(TV\)) 2 ([CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS)), [KXLY-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KXLY-TV) 4 ([ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)), [KHQ-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHQ-TV) 6 ([NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC); Spokane's first television station, on air on December 20, 1952), [KAYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAYU-TV) 28 ([FOX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company)), [KSKN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSKN) 22 ([The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW)), [KSPS-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSPS-TV) 7 ([PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS)), and [KCDT-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Public_Television) 26 (PBS; operating out of Coeur d'Alene, Idaho).[^cite_note-TV-324]

## Infrastructure



Street layout of Spokane city center

### Transportation

#### City streets

Spokane's streets use a [grid plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan) that is oriented to the four [cardinal directions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_direction); generally, the east–west roads are designated as avenues, and the north–south roads are referred to as streets. Major east–west thoroughfares in the city include Francis, Wellesley, Mission, Sprague, and 29th avenues. Major north–south thoroughfares include Maple–Ash, Monroe, Division, Hamilton, Greene–Market (north of I-90), and Ray–Freya (south of I-90) Streets. [Division Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_Street_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) divides the city into East and West, while [Sprague Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprague_Avenue_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) splits the city into North and South.[^cite_note-325] Division Street is Spokane's major [retail corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street); Sprague Avenue serves the same purpose in Spokane Valley. With over 40,000 vehicles per day in [average daily traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_daily_traffic) from [I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Washington) north to the intersection of US 2/US 395, North Division is Spokane's busiest corridor.[^cite_note-326]

Spokane's extensive [skywalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skywalk) system covers thirteen blocks in the downtown area and is among the largest in the United States; it is used for pedestrian travel in cold and inclement weather and retail space as well.[^cite_note-327][^cite_note-328] Despite this, the city has an average Walk Score of 49 as of 2020, indicating that most errands require a car. Its average Bike Score is 52.[^cite_note-WalkScore-329]

#### Mass transportation



STA [City Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Line_\(Spokane,_Washington\)) battery electric bus charging at [Spokane Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Community_College_Transit_Center)

See also: [Spokane Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Transit_Authority)

Before the influx of automobiles, Spokane's electric [streetcar and interurban lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_and_Inland_Empire_Railroad) played a dominant role in moving people and goods around Spokane. Streetcars were installed as early as 1888, when they were pulled by horses.[^cite_note-330] Many older side streets in Spokane still have visible streetcar rails embedded in them. Streetcar service was reduced due to declining ridership beginning in 1922, and by August 1936, all lines had been abandoned or converted to motor buses.[^cite_note-331]

Spokane has intercity rail and bus service provided by [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak), [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), [Flixbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flixbus) and [Jefferson Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lines) via the [Spokane Intermodal Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Intermodal_Center). The city is a stop for Amtrak's *[Empire Builder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Builder)* on its way to and from Chicago's [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Chicago\)) en route to [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_\(Amtrak_station\)) and [Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Portland\)).[^cite_note-332] Amtrak's through service to Seattle and Portland is a legacy of [BNSF Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF_Railway)'s old [Spokane, Portland and Seattle Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane,_Portland_and_Seattle_Railway) trackage.[^cite_note-Trains-333] Spokane is a major railway junction for the BNSF Railway and the [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad) and is the western terminus for the [Montana Rail Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Rail_Link).[^cite_note-Trains-333]

Public transportation throughout the Spokane area is provided by the [Spokane Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Transit_Authority) (STA), which operates a fleet of 164 buses. Its service area covers roughly 248 square miles (640 km<sup>2</sup>) and reaches 85 percent of the county's population.[^cite_note-334] The [STA Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STA_Plaza) in downtown Spokane acts as the regional hub for most STA routes.[^cite_note-335] As a part of the system's high performance transit network plan, STA introduced Eastern Washington's first bus rapid transit (BRT) route, [City Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Line_\(Spokane,_Washington\)), in July 2023.[^cite_note-336] STA is also planning a [Division Street BRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_Bus_Rapid_Transit) line from downtown Spokane to Mead that is scheduled to open by 2030.[^cite_note-337]

#### Freeways and highways



Overlooking Spokane and [Interstate 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Washington) from Sunset Hill

[I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Washington) runs east–west from Seattle, through downtown Spokane, and eastward through Spokane Valley, Liberty Lake, and onward to Coeur d'Alene and then [Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula,_Montana).[^cite_note-StateMap-338] Although they are not limited access highways like I-90, [US 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_2_in_Washington) and [US 395](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_395_in_Washington) enter Spokane from the west via I-90 and continue north through Spokane via Division Street. The two highways share the same route until they reach "The Y", a [fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-way_junction) where US 395 continues northward to [Deer Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_Park,_Washington), [Colville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colville,_Washington) then onward to Canada, and US 2 branches off to the northeast, continuing to [Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mead,_Washington), [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Washington), and [Sandpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandpoint,_Idaho). [US 195](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Route_195#Washington), also known as the Inland Empire Highway, connects to Interstate 90 west of Spokane near Latah Creek and travels south through the Palouse.[^cite_note-StateMap-338]

The [Washington State Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Department_of_Transportation) is tasked with improving local highways to keep up with the region's growth and to try to prevent congestion problems that plague many larger cities. The WSDOT is constructing the [North Spokane Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Spokane_Corridor). When completed, the corridor will be a 10.5-mile-long (16.9 km) limited-access highway that will run from I-90, in the vicinity of the Thor/Freya interchange, northward through Spokane, meeting the existing US 395 just south of the Wandermere Golf Course.[^cite_note-Corridor-339]

#### Airports



Concourse C, Spokane International Airport

[Spokane International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_International_Airport) (IATA: GEG, ICAO: KGEG) serves as the primary commercial airport for Spokane, Eastern Washington, and Northern Idaho. It is the second-largest airport in the state of Washington, and is recognized by the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) as a [small hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_hub_primary_airport), with service from nine passenger and five cargo airlines.[^cite_note-SIA-340] The 6,140-acre (24.85 km<sup>2</sup>) airport is located 5 miles (8.0 km) west of downtown Spokane and is approximately a 10-minute drive away.[^cite_note-341] The international airport's three-letter designation is "GEG", a result and legacy of the Geiger Field days prior to 1960, when the airport was named after Army aviator Major [Harold Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Geiger) in 1941.[^cite_note-Airports-342][^cite_note-343]

[Felts Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felts_Field) is a general aviation airport serving the Spokane area and is located in east Spokane along the south bank of the Spokane River. Aviation at Felts Field dates back to 1913 and the strip served as Spokane's primary airport until commercial air traffic was redirected to Geiger Field after World War II.[^cite_note-Airports-342] In 1927, the strip was one of the first in the western U.S. to receive official recognition as an airport by the U.S. Department of Commerce and is now named in honor of James Buell Felts, a Washington Air National Guard pilot.[^cite_note-Airports-342]

### Healthcare

Deaconess Medical Center in Spokane's "Medical District" on the lower South Hill

Deaconess Medical Center

The Spokane area has six major hospitals, four of which are full-service facilities.[^cite_note-344] The health-care industry is a large and increasingly important industry in Spokane; the city provides specialized care to many patients from the surrounding Inland Northwest and as far north as the Canada–US border.[^cite_note-345] The city's health-care needs are served primarily by non-profit Seattle-based [Providence Health & Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Health_&_Services) and non-profit Tacoma-based [Multicare Health System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiCare_Health_System), which run the two biggest hospitals, [Sacred Heart Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Sacred_Heart_Medical_Center_and_Children's_Hospital), and [Deaconess Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiCare_Deaconess_Hospital), respectively.[^cite_note-346] These two hospitals, the 102-bed [St. Luke's Rehabilitation Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Luke's_Rehabilitation_Institute),[^cite_note-347] 100-bed Inland Northwest Behavioral Health,[^cite_note-348] and most of Spokane's major health-care facilities, are located on Spokane's Lower-South Hill, just south of downtown, in what is known as the "Medical District" of Spokane.[^cite_note-349] Sacred Heart Hospital opened originally with just 31 beds[^cite_note-350] on Spokane Falls Boulevard on January 27, 1887, but later moved to its present location at 101 West Eighth Avenue.[^cite_note-351] As of 2014 it had 642 beds, with 28,319 admissions, 71,543 emergency room visits, and 2,982 births annually, and a full-time staff of 29 doctors and dentists and 583 registered nurses.[^cite_note-352] Deaconess Medical Center, the smaller of the two main hospitals, had 388 beds as of 2014.[^cite_note-353] Other hospitals in the area include the Spokane [Veterans Affairs Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Health_Administration) in the northwest part of town, Providence Holy Family Hospital on the north side, and MultiCare Valley Hospital in the [Spokane Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Valley,_Washington). One of 20 specialty orthopedic [Shriners Hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shriners_Hospitals_for_Children) in the U.S. is also located in Spokane.[^cite_note-354] One of Washington's two state [psychiatric hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatric_hospital), [Eastern State Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_State_Hospital_\(Washington\)), is located 15 miles (24 km) away in [Medical Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Lake,_Washington).[^cite_note-355]

### Utilities

The City of Spokane provides municipal [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_network), [wastewater management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment), and [solid waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_waste_management).[^cite_note-356] Spokane operates Washington's only [waste-to-energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste-to-energy) plant as well as two solid waste [transfer stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_station_\(waste_management\)) as part of the Spokane Regional Solid Waste System, a collaboration between the City of Spokane and Spokane County.[^cite_note-Roesler-357] Electricity generated by the waste-to-energy plant is used to operate the facility, with excess energy being sold to [Puget Sound Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound_Energy).[^cite_note-Roesler-357] Spokane draws its water from the [Spokane Valley–Rathdrum Prairie Aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_Valley%E2%80%93Rathdrum_Prairie_Aquifer); this 370-square-mile (958 km<sup>2</sup>) "sole source aquifer" is the only water supply for Spokane County in Washington, and for [Kootenai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kootenai_County,_Idaho) and [Bonner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonner_County,_Idaho) counties in Idaho.[^cite_note-358] Serving over 500,000 people, the aquifer is distinguished in being one of the [largest aquifers in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifers_in_the_United_States) at 10 trillion gallons, as well as having one of the fastest [flow rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater_discharge) in the country at 60 feet (18 m) per day, and for its purity.[^cite_note-aquifer-359]<sup>[\[i\]](#endnote_aquifer)</sup>

Natural gas and electricity are provided by the local utility, [Avista Utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avista_Utilities), while [CenturyLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CenturyLink) and [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) provide television, internet, and telephone service. Spokane hosts three [hydroelectric](381399.md) generation facilities on the Spokane River: the Upriver Dam, the [Upper Falls Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Falls_Power_Plant), and the Monroe Street Dam. The Upriver Dam is owned and operated by the City of Spokane, and generates the electricity needed to operate the municipal water supply's pressure pumps.[^cite_note-UpriverDam-360] The power generated in excess of that is sold to Avista Utilities.[^cite_note-UpriverDam-360] The Upper Falls and Monroe Street dams are owned and operated by Avista Utilities, and have respective generation capacities of 10 and 15 [MW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts).[^cite_note-SpokaneDams-361]
