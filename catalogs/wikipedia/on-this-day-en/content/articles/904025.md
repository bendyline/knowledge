The **lari** ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): ლარი, pronounced [\[ˈɫaɾi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Georgian); [ISO 4217](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_4217): GEL, sign: **[₾](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lari_sign)**) is the [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency) of [Georgia](48768.md). It is divided into 100 [tetri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetri) (თეთრი). The name *lari* is an old Georgian word denoting a hoard, property, while *tetri* is an old Georgian monetary term (meaning 'white') used in ancient [Colchis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchis) from the 6th century BC. Earlier Georgian currencies include the [maneti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_maneti) ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): მანეთი), [abazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_abazi) (აბაზი), and [kuponi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_kuponi) (კუპონი).

## Kuponi

Main article: [Georgian kuponi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_kuponi)

Georgia replaced the [Soviet ruble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_ruble) on 5 April 1993, with the kuponi ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): [კუპონი](https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%83%A5%E1%83%90%E1%83%A0%E1%83%97%E1%83%A3%E1%83%9A%E1%83%98%20%E1%83%99%E1%83%A3%E1%83%9E%E1%83%9D%E1%83%9C%E1%83%98)) at par. This currency consisted only of banknotes, had no subdivisions and suffered from [hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation). Notes were issued in denominations between 1 and 1 million kuponi, including the somewhat unusual 3,000, 30,000 and 150,000 kuponi.

## Lari

On 2 October 1995,[^cite_note-2] the government of [Eduard Shevardnadze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Shevardnadze) replaced the provisional coupon currency with the lari, at a rate of one million to one. It has remained fairly stable since then.

### Lari sign

Main article: [Lari sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lari_sign)



The Georgian lari currency sign, introduced on 8 July 2014.

The NBG announced the lari sign competition in December 2013. The temporary commission consisted of representatives of NBG, the Budget and Finance Committee of the Parliament of Georgia, the State Council of Heraldry, the Ministry of Culture and Monument Protection of Georgia and the Ministry of Education and Science of Georgia.[^cite_note-Lari_sign-3]

On 8 July 2014, Giorgi Kadagidze, Governor of the [National Bank of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Georgia) (NBG), unveiled the winning proposal for the sign of the national currency to the public.[^cite_note-4] The author of the winning sign was professional artist-ceramist, Malkhaz Shvelidze.[^cite_note-Lari_sign-3]

In choosing the winning sign, the commission gave priority to the samples based on the Georgian [Mkhedruli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mkhedruli) character and made a point of the following criteria: conception, design, accordance with Georgian alphabet, existence of elements marking the currency, ease of construction, and observance of requests and recommendations determined by competition rules.[^cite_note-Lari_sign-3]

The lari sign is based on an arched letter [ლ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%83%9A) (Lasi) of the [Georgian script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_script). It is common international practice for a currency sign to consist of a letter, crossed by one or two parallel lines. Two parallel lines crossing the letter Lasi are the basic components of the lari sign. The so-called “leg” of the letter, represented by a horizontal line, is a necessary attribute of the sign, adding monumental stability to the upper dynamic arc. The form of the letter is transformed in order to simplify its perception and implementation as a lari sign.[^cite_note-Lari_sign-3]

On 18 July 2014, Giorgi Melashvili, executive director of the National Bank of Georgia, sent a request letter to the Unicode Consortium to register the symbol in the [Currency Symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_Symbols_\(Unicode_block\)) block of the Unicode Standard as&#x20;

₾ `U+20BE GEORGIAN LARI SIGN`

[^cite_note-5]

On 17 June 2015, the Unicode Consortium released[^cite_note-6] Unicode V8.0, which includes the lari sign as&#x20;

U+20BE ₾ LARI SIGN

[^cite_note-7]

### Coins

Coins are issued in denominations of 5, 10, 20 and 50 tetri, as well as 1 and 2 lari.

The National Bank of Georgia announced on 5 October 2018 that it would discontinue circulation of 1 and 2 tetri coins as of 1 January 2021, and that these coins would lose their legal tender status. 1 and 2 tetri coins could be exchanged at the National Bank of Georgia and commercial banks in Georgia within one year from 1 January 2021, and can only be exchanged at the National Bank of Georgia from 1 January 2022.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] According to the new regulations on cash payments introduced by the National Bank of Georgia on 1 January 2019, 1 and 2 tetri are rounded to 0, and 3, 4, 6 and 7 tetri are rounded to 5.[^cite_note-10]

The National Bank of Georgia announced on 12 November 2015 that, effective 1 January 2018, the old 50 tetri coin, issued since 1995, would be withdrawn from circulation and the coins would lose their legal tender status. The old 50 tetri coin were exchangeable at the National Bank of Georgia and commercial banks of Georgia within one year from 1 January 2018, and from 1 January 2019 only at the National Bank of Georgia.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]
