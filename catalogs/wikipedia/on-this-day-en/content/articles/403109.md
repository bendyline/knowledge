**Venstre**[^cite_note-14] (Danish: [\[ˈvenstʁɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Danish), lit. 'Left', **V**), full name **Venstre, Danmarks Liberale Parti** (English: Left, Denmark's Liberal Party), is a [conservative-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_liberalism),[^cite_note-Kirchner1988-3][^cite_note-Slomp2011-4][^cite_note-Nordsieck-5] [agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_agrarian_parties)[^cite_note-KildalKuhnle2007-15] [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Denmark) in [Denmark](76972.md). Founded as part of a peasants' movement against the [landed aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landed_nobility), today it espouses an [economically liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism), pro-[free-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) [ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology).[^cite_note-BengtssonHansen2013-16]

Venstre is the [major party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_party) of the [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right) in [Denmark](76972.md), and the third-largest party in the country. The party has produced many [Prime Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Denmark). In the [2022 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Danish_general_election), Venstre received 13.3% of the vote and 23 out of 179 seats. Following the resignation of [Jakob Ellemann-Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ellemann-Jensen), the party is led by [Troels Lund Poulsen](15575631.md) who serves as the country's [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_\(Denmark\)).[^cite_note-17] From 2022 to 2026, the party was a junior partner in the [second Frederiksen government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_II_Cabinet).

The party is a member of [Liberal International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_International) and the [Alliance of Liberals and Democrats for Europe Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Liberals_and_Democrats_for_Europe_Party) (ALDE Party) and has two MEPs in the [European Parliament](9581.md).[^cite_note-Denmark-Results-18]

## History



*Venstre* 1945 election material *("Venstre has been dealt a good hand").*

### 1870–1910

Venstre was founded in 1870 under the name "Det Forenede Venstre" (lit. 'The United Left'). Originally, the party consisted of multiple conflicting groups, all united under liberalism, the safeguarding of farmers' interests and opposition to the then [right-wing](56522.md) [classical conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_conservatism) party [Højre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B8jre) (literally "Right"). After the party in 1872 gained an absolute majority in the [Folketing](429743.md), it became the leading voice in the battle for [parliamentarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentarism), whereafter the party in 1895 split in two, Venstrereformpartiet ("Venstre Reform Party") and Det Moderate Venstre ("The Moderate Left"). In 1905, social liberal factions split from the party and formed [Radikale Venstre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radikale_Venstre) (also known as the Danish Social Liberal Party), and in 1910 Venstrereformpartiet and Det Moderate Venstre reunited again under the name Venstre.[^cite_note-\:0-19] During the late Nineteenth Century, Venstre and the Social Democrats collaborated on social legislation; with several measures passed during this period that both parties supported, including old age pensions in 1891 and health insurance a year later.[^cite_note-20]

### 1910–2009

With the decreasing numbers of farms and the growing urbanisation, membership and voter support dropped in the 1950s. During the 1960s the party gradually evolved from being a traditional farmers' party to a more general liberal party. In 1984 [Uffe Ellemann-Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uffe_Ellemann-Jensen) was elected chairman, and by profiling the liberal ideology in sharp confrontation to the [Social Democrats](403131.md), for example by campaigning for a reduction of the public sector, increasing market management and privatisation, and by being pro-EU, the party returned to its historical position as the biggest liberal party in the 1990s.[^cite_note-\:0-19]

After a disappointing [1998 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Danish_general_election), Ellemann-Jensen resigned as chairman and [Anders Fogh Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Fogh_Rasmussen) was elected in his place. He immediately changed the party's usual confrontational strategy, instead appealing to the political centre. In the [2001 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Danish_general_election) the party campaigned for tighter immigration policies and a "tax stop", which proved successful and the party once again became the biggest in parliament, winning 31.2% of the vote and 56 seats. Venstre formed a coalition government with the [Conservative People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_People's_Party_\(Denmark\)) and the [Danish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_People's_Party). For the first time since 1929 a liberal government was no longer dependent on the centre parties. Despite a small decline in both the [2005 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Danish_general_election) (29% and 52 seats) and the [2007 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Danish_general_election) (26.2% and 46 seats), the party remained the biggest and the coalition government continued.[^cite_note-\:0-19]

On 5 April 2009, Fogh Rasmussen resigned as chairman, instead serving as [Secretary General of NATO](22194433.md). In his place [Lars Løkke Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_L%C3%B8kke_Rasmussen) was elected.[^cite_note-21]

### 2009–present

In the [2011 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Danish_general_election), the party gained 26.7% of the vote and 47 seats, but was not able to form a government, instead leading the opposition against Prime Minister [Helle Thorning-Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helle_Thorning-Schmidt)'s Social Democratic coalition.

Even though the party lost voter support in the [2015 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Danish_general_election), only gaining 19.5% of the vote, the party formed a minority government. This government was short-lived, and in 2016 Løkke Rasmussen invited the Conservative People's Party and the [Liberal Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Alliance_\(Denmark\)) to form a coalition government instead.[^cite_note-22]

During the campaign of the [2019 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Danish_general_election), Løkke Rasmussen published an autobiography, in which he opened up for the possibility of forming a government with the Social Democrats.[^cite_note-23] This was seen as controversial in the liberal "blue bloc", and Social Democratic leader [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) immediately declined the proposition.[^cite_note-24]



Troels Lund Poulsen, chairman 2023-

Following internal fighting in the party, Løkke Rasmussen and vice chairman [Kristian Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristian_Jensen) both resigned on 31 August 2019. On 21 September 2019, political spokesman and former Minister for Environment and Food [Jakob Ellemann-Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ellemann-Jensen) was elected the party's next chairman.[^cite_note-25]

Following the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Danish_general_election), in which Venstre suffered its worst result since 1988, Venstre joined a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition) government led by Social Democrat leader Mette Frederiksen, and also comprising the [Moderates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderates_\(Denmark\)), a Venstre splinter formed by former Prime Minister [Lars Løkke Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_L%C3%B8kke_Rasmussen).[^cite_note-26]

Venstre is categorised as [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) on the [political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_spectrum),[^cite_note-Colomer2008-10] although it has been also described as [centrist](39619035.md)[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] and [right-wing](56522.md).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-Parteiensysteme-32] Ideologically, it has been described as [conservative-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative-liberal),[^cite_note-Einhorn2023-33][^cite_note-Kirchner1988-3][^cite_note-Slomp2011-4][^cite_note-Nordsieck-5][^cite_note-Parteiensysteme-32][^cite_note-Spierings2019-34] [liberal-conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal-conservative),[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism),[^cite_note-Süddeutsche-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative),[^cite_note-Parteiensysteme-32][^cite_note-43] [classical-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical-liberal),[^cite_note-44] and [agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarianism).[^cite_note-Parteiensysteme-32] Additionally, Venstre takes a [nativist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_\(politics\)) stance regarding immigration and asylum-seeking; they had also said that "immigrants should learn Danish and understand and respect Danish culture and traditions".[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

Venstre is an [economically liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism) party[^cite_note-Süddeutsche-39][^cite_note-Almeida2012-47] within the [Nordic agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_agrarian_parties) tradition,[^cite_note-almeida-48] and today is notably more pro–[free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) than its sister parties.[^cite_note-49] Since the elections in 2001, Venstre has enacted a so-called "tax stop" in order to halt the growth in taxes seen during the previous eight years under the [Social Democrats](403131.md). This tax stop has been under heavy fire from the parties on the left bloc of Danish politics, allegedly for being "asocial" and "only for the rich."[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

## Organization

### Name and terms

The fact that the major centre-right political party in a country calls itself 'Left' is often confusing to foreign (and sometimes Danish) observers. The name has, however, its historical explanation. At the time of its foundation, *Venstre* affirmed then-progressive ideas in the Danish parliament. Their opponents, *Højre* (Right), the forerunner of the present-day [Conservative People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_People's_Party_\(Denmark\)), advocated for established interests, particularly the [Church of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Denmark) and the landed gentry. In current Danish politics there is a clear distinction between the concepts of *Venstre* (Left, i.e., the party bearing that name) and *venstrefløj* (left wing, i.e., socialist and other left-leaning parties). The use of the word for "left" in the name of the Danish political party *[Radikale Venstre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radikale_Venstre)* (literally: "[Radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_radicalism) Left") and the [Norwegian](21241.md) party *[Venstre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venstre_\(Norway\))* is meant to refer to [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) and not [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism).

Members of the party are referred to as *venstremænd* and *venstrekvinder*, respectively "Venstre men" and "Venstre women" (singular: -mand, -kvinde).
