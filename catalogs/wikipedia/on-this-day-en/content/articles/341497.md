The **A66** is a major road in [Northern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England), which in part follows the course of the [Roman road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_roads_in_Britain) from [Scotch Corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch_Corner) to [Penrith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith,_Cumbria).[^cite_note-3] It runs from east of [Middlesbrough](334775.md) in [North Yorkshire](143759.md) to [Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington) in [Cumbria](73021.md). The road has been progressively improved with dual carriageway sections, but with stretches of single carriageway road. The road is set to be completely dualled between Scotch Corner and Penrith, with a £1.3 billion scheme being announced in March 2024.

## Route

From its eastern terminus between [Redcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar) and Middlesbrough it runs past [Stockton-on-Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton-on-Tees) and [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington) mainly as two-lane [dual-carriageway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-carriageway) and single carriageway past Darlington, becoming motorway standard as the A66(M) shortly before meeting junction 57 of the [A1(M)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_road_\(Great_Britain\)). It shares the A1(M) route south to [Scotch Corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch_Corner), from where it continues west across the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines), past [Brough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brough,_Cumbria), [Appleby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleby,_Westmorland), [Kirkby Thore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkby_Thore), [Temple Sowerby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Sowerby) and [Penrith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith,_Cumbria) until it reaches Junction 40 of the [M6 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_motorway) at Skirsgill Interchange, where traffic going towards Western Scotland turns onto the northbound M6. The A66 continues past [Blencathra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blencathra) to [Keswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keswick,_Cumbria) and [Cockermouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockermouth) and on through the northern reaches of the [Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District) before arriving at the coastal town of [Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington). There is a short stretch of dual carriageway along the northern part of Bassenthwaite Lake between Keswick and Cockermouth. Whilst the eastbound section follows the straight line of the disused [Cockermouth, Keswick and Penrith Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockermouth,_Keswick_and_Penrith_Railway), the westbound section has numerous bends with climbs and dips. The westbound section was closed due to flood damage in December 2015; when it re-opened in May 2016, the road had been permanently reduced to a single lane. This section has a 50-mile-per-hour (80 km/h) limit monitored by [average speed cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_camera#Average_speed_cameras).[^cite_note-4]

## History

When road numbers were first [designated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_road_numbering_scheme) in the 1920s,[^cite_note-5] the A66 was assigned to the route between Penrith and [Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_upon_Hull) via Scotch Corner and [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York), mainly along former [Roman roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_road).[^cite_note-\:1-6] Today's route largely follows the original route between Penrith and Scotch Corner. The historic route between Scotch Corner and Hull follows what is now today's [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_road_\(Great_Britain\)), [A168](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A168_road), [B6265](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B6265_road), [A59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A59_road_\(Great_Britain\)) and [A1079](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1079_road).

It is [anomalously numbered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalously_numbered_roads_in_Great_Britain) since west of [Penrith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith,_Cumbria) it trespasses into numbering zone 5; this is because it originally terminated at the [A6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_road_\(England\)) in Penrith but was extended further west in order to create one continuous east–west route. Most of what is now the A66 west of Penrith was originally [A594](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A594_road_\(Cumbria\)) – only a small stub of this road numbering remains, from [Maryport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryport) to [Cockermouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockermouth).

## Proposed developments

### Trans-Pennine dualling

The middle 49.5 miles (79.7 km) section of the A66 between [Scotch Corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch_Corner) on the A1(M) and Penrith on the [M6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_motorway) forms one of the key trans-Pennines trunk routes and has one of the worst road-safety records in the UK. Various bypasses and upgrades have been constructed since the early 1970s, giving the current mix of single and dual-carriageway sections. In 2002, after many years of local campaigning, the Transport Minister, [John Spellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Spellar), gave support for the upgrading of the remaining single-carriageway sections by the [Highways Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_Agency).[^cite_note-\:0-7] The first three projects began construction in early 2006 and opened in 2007 and 2008. The whole route between the A1(M) and M6 was due to be dualled by 2011, by which time the upgrade of the A1 to motorway status at Scotch Corner was planned to be complete.

After the construction of several sections commenced, it was announced that those schemes currently in the planning phase would not go ahead until 2016 at the earliest. The Highways Agency website states "Other than those already committed, the Regions did not identify any other major schemes for the A66 as high priorities to receive funding. This means that there is currently no likelihood of any additional major schemes on this route being funded within the next ten-year period. However the Regional Funding Allocation process will be reviewed in due course and this will give an opportunity for the Regions to revise their priorities."

In September 2015, the government said that £500,000 would be invested into the study of the two Trans-Pennine routes of the A66 and the [A69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A69_road). The proposal would be for one or even both roads to be dualled wholly between the A1/A1(M) and the M6.[^cite_note-8] The following year the government announced that the A66 would be dualled, but not the A69.[^cite_note-9] In March 2019, project director Matt Townsend from [Highways England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_England) announced plans for a public consultation from May 2019, in which it would present its plans for a £500 million spend to complete dualling the Trans-Pennine section, together with improvements at the M6 and A1(M) interchanges.[^cite_note-2019_consultation-10]

In March 2024, Transport Secretary Mark Harper approved a £1.3 billion scheme to dual the A66 in its entirety between Scotch Corner and Penrith. Work would begin on the scheme in the latter half of 2024.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Safety

The section of road between Scotch Corner and Penrith accounted for 70 deaths over ten years up until 2002, which was above the national average for single lane carriageways.[^cite_note-\:0-7] Whilst the number of accidents was in line with the national average, the number of serious injuries and deaths was twice the national average; this high attrition rate was the reason for the go-ahead for the new dualled sections on the grounds of safety.[^cite_note-15]

Keith Buchan, a highly experienced transport planner, examined the safety record of the A66 and the evidence submitted during the planning examination for the £1.5 billion A66 Northern Trans-Pennine project. In the 'Improving safety on the A66' report, he makes recommendations for safety improvements on the A66 which could be implemented now, at low cost, rather than waiting five years for the £1.5 billion A66 scheme to be built.[^cite_note-16]

Snow gates were installed on the road between [Bowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowes) and [Brough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brough,_Cumbria).[^cite_note-17] This section is the moorland route over [Stainmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainmore) summit which reaches a height of 1,450 feet (440 m) is prone to heavy snow in the winter.[^cite_note-18] Both sets of gates have turnaround facilities to allow all traffic to change direction.

The A66(M) is a [spur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spur_route) from the [A1(M)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1\(M\)_motorway) at Junction 57. It was opened in 1965 along with the A1(M) as part of the *Darlington by-pass motorway*.[^cite_note-22] It can be accessed only by northbound traffic on the A1(M) and has an exit to this route southbound only.
