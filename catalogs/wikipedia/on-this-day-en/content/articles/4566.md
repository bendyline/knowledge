**Baku** ([US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/bɑːˈkuː, ˈbɑːkuː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/bæˈkuː, ˈbækuː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language): *Bakı* [\[bɑˈcɯ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Azerbaijani) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f0/Az-baku.ogg/Az-baku.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f0/Az-baku.ogg/Az-baku.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Azerbaijan) of [Azerbaijan](746.md), as well as the largest city on the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) and in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) region.[^cite_note-8] Baku is 28 metres (92 ft) below [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level), which makes it the [lowest lying national capital in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capital_cities_by_elevation) and the largest city in the world below sea level. Baku lies on the southern shore of the [Absheron Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absheron_Peninsula), along the Bay of Baku. Baku's [urban population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_population) is estimated at over two million people. Baku is the [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city) of Azerbaijan. It is the sole metropolis in the country, and about 25% of all inhabitants of the country live in Baku's [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area).

Baku is divided into [twelve administrative raions](#Administrative_divisions) and 48 townships. Among these are the townships on the islands of the [Baku Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Archipelago), as well as the industrial settlement of [Neft Daşları](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neft_Da%C5%9Flar%C4%B1) built on oil rigs 60 kilometres (37 miles) away from Baku, in the Caspian Sea. The [Old City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Baku\)), containing the [Palace of the Shirvanshahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Shirvanshahs) and the [Maiden Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_\(Baku\)), was designated as a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2000.[^cite_note-9]

The city is the scientific, cultural, and industrial centre of Azerbaijan. Many sizeable Azerbaijani institutions have their headquarters there. In the 2010s, Baku became a venue for major international events. It hosted the [57th Eurovision Song Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2012) in 2012, the [2015 European Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_European_Games), the [European Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Grand_Prix) in 2016, the 4th [Islamic Solidarity Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Solidarity_Games) in 2017, the [Azerbaijan Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Grand_Prix) since 2017, the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Europa_League_Final) of the [2018–19 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Europa_League), [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) and the [2024 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The [Baku International Sea Trade Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Baku) is capable of handling two million tonnes of general and dry bulk cargoes per year.[^cite_note-12] Baku is renowned for its harsh winds, reflected in its nickname, the "City of Winds".

## Etymology

Baku gets its name from the word *Baguan*/*Bagavan*/*Bagawan*/*Bhagawan* which comes from the word *Baga*, which means "God" in [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian),[^cite_note-13] and *[Bhaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaga)* in Sanskrit. It was called Bagavan during the Sassanian rule (22-651 CE).[^cite_note-14] and later became known by its [Perso-Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perso-Arabic) name باکو (*Bākū*). Early [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) sources also refer to the city as *Bākuh* and *Bākuya*,[^cite_note-15] all of which seem to come from a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) name. The further etymology is unclear.

A [popular etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology)[^cite_note-16] in the 19th century considered it to be derived from Persian بادکوبه (*Bâd-kube*, meaning "wind-pounded city", a compound of *bād*, "wind", and *kube*,[^cite_note-17] which is rooted in the verb کوبیدن *kubidan*, "to pound", thus referring to a place where wind would be strong and pounding,[^cite_note-Web.archive.org-18] as is the case of Baku, which is known to experience fierce winter snow storms and harsh winds). This popular name (*Badkubə* in modern Azerbaijani script) gained currency as a nickname for the city by the 19th century (e.g., it is used in *[Akinchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akinchi)*, volume 1, issue 1, p. 1), and is also reflected in the city's modern nickname as the "City of Winds" (Azerbaijani: *Küləklər şəhəri*). Another and even less probable folk etymology explains the name as deriving from *Baghkuy*, meaning "God's town". *Baga* (now *بغ* *bagh*) and *kuy* are the [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian_language) words for "god" and "town" respectively; the name *Baghkuy* may be compared with *[Baghdād](4492.md)* ("God-given") in which *dād* is the Old Persian word for "give".

During [Soviet rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), the city was spelled in Cyrillic as "Бакы" in Azerbaijani (while the Russian spelling was and still is "Баку", *_Baku_*). The modern Azerbaijani spelling, which has been using the Latin alphabet since 1991, is *Bakı*; the shift from the Perso-Arabic letter و (*ū*) to Cyrillic "ы" and, later, Latin "ı" may be compared to that in other Azerbaijani words (e.g. compare [قاپو](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%BE%D9%88#Azerbaijani) *qāpū* in old Perso-Arabic spelling with modern Azerbaijani *[qapı](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/qap%C4%B1#Azerbaijani)*, "door") or in suffixes, as و was often used to transcribe the [vowel harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vowel_harmony) in Azerbaijani (which was also the practice in [Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language)). (See also [Azerbaijani alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_alphabet).)

## History

Main articles: [History of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Baku) and [Timeline of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Baku)

### Antiquity



Roman stone inscription in [Gobustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobustan_State_Historical_and_Cultural_Reserve) dating back to AD 84–96

Traces of human settlement in the region of present-day Baku date back to the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age). [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) rock carvings have been discovered near Bayil, and a bronze figure of a small fish in the territory of the Old City. These have led some to suggest the existence of a Bronze-Age settlement within the city's territory.[^cite_note-19] Near [Nardaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nardaran), a place called Umid Gaya features a prehistoric observatory, where images of the sun and of various constellations are carved into rock together with a primitive astronomic table.[^cite_note-20] Further archeological excavations have revealed various prehistoric settlements, native temples, statues and other artifacts within the territory of the modern city and around it.

In the 1st century AD, the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) organised two Caucasian campaigns and reached what is today Baku. Near the city, in what is today [Gobustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobustan_State_Historical_and_Cultural_Reserve), Roman inscriptions dating from AD 84 to 96 survive – some of the earliest written evidences for a city there.[^cite_note-21]

According to the 6th-century archbishop and historian St. Sophronius of Cyprus, in 71, St. [Bartholomew the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_the_Apostle) was preaching Christianity in the city of Albana or Albanopolis,[^cite_note-22] with majority academic consensus associating it with [Greater Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)),[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] although some associate it with either present-day Baku[^cite_note-27] or [Derbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbent),[^cite_note-28] both located by the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). St. Bartholomew managed to convert even members of the local royal family who had worshipped the idol [Astaroth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astaroth), but was later martyred by being [flayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaying) alive and crucified head down on orders from the pagan king Astyages.[^cite_note-29] The remains of St. Bartholomew were secretly transferred to [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia).[^cite_note-30]

### Rise of the Shirvanshahs and the Safavid era

See also: [Shirvanshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirvanshah)



A [miniature painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniature_\(illuminated_manuscript\)) marking the downfall of the [Shirvanshahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirvanshahs) at the hands of the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids)

Baku was the realm of the [Shirvanshahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirvanshahs) during the 8th century AD. The city frequently came under assault from the [Khazars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars) and (starting from the 10th century) from the [Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'). [Akhsitan I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhsitan_I) built a navy in Baku and successfully repelled a Rus' assault in 1170. After a devastating earthquake struck [Shamakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamakhi), the capital of [Shirvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirvan), Shirvanshah's court moved to Baku in 1191.[^cite_note-31]



Relics from the sunken [Sabayil Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabayil_Castle)&#x20;

The Shirvan era greatly influenced Baku and the remainder of present-day Azerbaijan. Between the 12th and 14th centuries, massive fortifications were built in Baku and the surrounding towns. The [Maiden Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_\(Baku\)), the [Ramana Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramana_Tower), the [Nardaran Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nardaran_Fortress), the Shagan Castle, the [Mardakan Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrangular_castle_\(Mardakan\)), the [Round Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Castle_\(Mardakan\)) and also the [Sabayil Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabayil_Castle) on the island of the [Bay of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Baku) date from this period. The [city walls of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Baku\)) were also rebuilt and strengthened.

By the early 16th century, Baku's wealth and strategic position attracted the attention of its larger neighbours; in the previous two centuries, it was under the rule of the Iran-centred [Kara Koyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Koyunlu) and [Ak Koyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak_Koyunlu). The fall of the Ak Koyunlu brought the city immediately into the sphere of the newly formed Iranian [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty), led by king (*[shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah)*) [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I) (r. 1501–1524). Ismail I laid siege to Baku in 1501 and captured it; he allowed the Shirvanshahs to remain in power, under Safavid suzerainty. His successor, king [Tahmasp I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahmasp_I) (r. 1524–1576), completely removed the Shirvanshahs from power and made Baku a part of the [Shirvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Shirvan) province. Baku remained as an integral part of his empire and of successive Iranian dynasties for the next centuries, until ceded to the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) through the 1813 [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan). The House of Shirvan, which had ruled Baku since the 9th century, was extinguished in the course of Safavid rule.

At this time, the city was enclosed within lines of strong walls, which were washed by the sea on one side and protected by a wide trench on land. The Ottomans briefly gained control over Baku as a result of the [Ottoman-Safavid War of 1578–1590](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Safavid_War_\(1578%E2%80%931590\)); by 1607, it came under Iranian control again.[^cite_note-32] In 1604 Shah [Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_I_of_Persia) (r. 1588–1629) destroyed Baku fortress.



&#x20;[Atashgah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateshgah_of_Baku) is a temple built by Indian traders before 1745, west of the Caspian Sea. The inscription invokes Lord [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) at the Atashgah.

Baku had a reputation as a focal point for traders from across the world during the [Early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period); commerce was active and the area prospered. Notably, traders from the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) established themselves in the region. These Indian traders built the [Ateshgah of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateshgah_of_Baku) during 17th–18th centuries; the temple was used as a [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu), [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh), and [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrian) place of worship.[^cite_note-Ziyadov2012-33]

### Downfall of the Safavids and the Khanate of Baku

The Safavids temporarily lost power in Iran in 1722; Emperor [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) of Russia took advantage of the situation and invaded. As a result of the [Russo-Persian War of 1722–1723](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_of_1722%E2%80%931723), the Safavids were forced to cede Baku to Russia.[^cite_note-34] By 1730 the situation had deteriorated for the Russians; the successes of [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) (r. 1736–1747) led them to sign the [Treaty of Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ganja) near [Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganja,_Azerbaijan) on 10 March 1735, ceding the city and all other conquered territories in the Caucasus back to Iran.[^cite_note-35]

The eruption of instability following Nader Shah's death in 1747 gave rise to the various [Caucasian khanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanates_of_the_Caucasus).

### Russo-Persian Wars and Iran's cession



Painting of Baku's shoreline in 1861 by [Alexey Bogolyubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Bogolyubov)

From the late 18th century, [Imperial Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia) switched to a more aggressive geopolitical stance towards its two neighbours and rivals to the south, namely Iran and the Ottoman Empire. In the spring of 1796, by [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great)'s order, General [Valerian Zubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerian_Zubov)'s troops started [a large campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Expedition_of_1796) against [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) Persia.[^cite_note-36] Zubov had sent 13,000 men to capture Baku, and it was overrun subsequently without any resistance. On 13 June 1796, a Russian flotilla entered Baku Bay, and a garrison of Russian troops was stationed inside the city. Later, however, Emperor [Paul I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_I_of_Russia) of Russia ordered the cessation of the campaign and the withdrawal of Russian forces following Catherine's death. In March 1797 the tsarist troops left Baku, and the city became part of [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) again.

In 1813, following the [Russo-Persian War of 1804–1813](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%9313\)), Qajar Iran had to sign the [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan) with Russia. This provided for the cession of Baku and of most of Iran's territories in the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus) and [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus) to Russia. During the next and final bout of hostilities between the two, the [Russo-Persian War of 1826–1828](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1826%E2%80%9328\)), the Iranians briefly recaptured Baku. However, the militarily superior Russians ended this war with a victory as well, and the resulting [Treaty of Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay) (1828) made Baku's inclusion in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) definite.[^cite_note-37] When Baku was occupied by the Russian troops during the [war of 1804–13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%9313\)), nearly the entire population of some 8,000 people was ethnic [Tat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_people_\(Caucasus\)).[^cite_note-books.google.nl-38] In 1809, more than 95% of the population consisted of Azerbaijani Turks. By 1860, this proportion had slightly decreased to 94.4%. The city also included small minority communities. Baku within Russia was the administrative center of the [Baku Uyezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Uyezd), [Baku Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Governorate), and the [Baku Gradonachalstvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Gradonachalstvo).

After the capture of Baku, the [Baku Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Khanate) was abolished, and the city was governed under a military command system in the early years. In 1812, a city court was established where local customs and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) law were applied together. With the transition to civilian administration in 1840, Baku became a district center attached to the Caspian Province. Following the devastating earthquake in [Shamakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamakhi) in 1859, the city gained the status of a provincial capital and emerged as one of the major administrative and political centers of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus).[^cite_note-39]

The social structure consisted of privileged groups such as beys and [Islamic](6037917.md) religious figures, as well as merchants, craftsmen, and a wage-earning working class that developed in connection with port activities. The city was divided into two main areas: the Inner City ([Icherisheher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Baku\))) within the walls and the Forstadt outside the walls.[^cite_note-40] In the second half of the nineteenth century, steps toward modernization were taken in the fields of infrastructure, education, and healthcare.

### Discovery of oil

Main article: [Petroleum industry in Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Azerbaijan)



&#x20;Panoramic view of Baku bay in 1861

The Russians built the first [oil-distilling factory](195137.md) in [Balaxani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaxani) in 1837. The first person to drill oil in Baku was an ethnic [Armenian](387816.md) [Ivan Mirzoev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Mirzoev), who is also known as a 'founding father of Baku's oil industry.'[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Drilling for oil began in the 1840s, with the first [oil well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_well) drilled in the [Bibi-Heybat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibiheyb%C9%99t) suburb of Baku in 1846.[^cite_note-Smil-43] Large-scale [oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_exploration) started in 1872 when the Russian imperial authorities auctioned parcels of oil-rich land around Baku to private investors. The pioneer of oil extracting from the bottom of the sea was the Polish geologist [Witold Zglenicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Zglenicki). Soon after, investors appeared in Baku, including the [Nobel Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Brothers) in 1873 and the [Rothschilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_family) in 1882. An industrial area of oil refineries, better known as [Black Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_City_\(Baku\)) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Чёрный город), developed near Baku by the early 1880s.[^cite_note-44]



Oil workers digging an oil well by hand at Bibi-Heybat

Professor [A. V. Williams Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._V._Williams_Jackson) of Columbia University wrote in his work *From Constantinople to the Home of Omar Khayyam* (1911):

> Baku is a city founded upon oil, for to its inexhaustible founts of naphtha it owes its very existence, its maintenance, its prosperity... At present Baku produces one-fifth of the oil that is used in the world, and the immense output in crude petroleum from this single city far surpasses that in any other district where oil is found. Verily, the words of the Scriptures find illustration here: 'the rock poured me out rivers of oil. Oil is in the air one breathes, in one's nostrils, in one's eyes, in the water of the morning bath (though not in the drinking water, for that is brought in bottles from distant mineral springs), in one's starched linen – everywhere. This is the impression one carries away from Baku, and it is certainly true in the environs.[^cite_note-45]

By the beginning of the 20th century, half of the oil sold in international markets was extracted in Baku.[^cite_note-46] The [oil boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_boom) contributed to the massive growth of Baku. Between 1856 and 1910 Baku's population grew at a faster rate than that of London, Paris, New York City, or Tokyo.

### World War I

Main article: [Baku during World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_during_World_War_I)



&#x20;Soldiers and officers of the army of the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic shortly after the [Battle of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baku)&#x20;



[Neftchiler Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neftchiler_Avenue) in Baku, c. 1920

In 1917, after the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) and amidst the turmoil of [World War I](4764461.md) and the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution), Baku came under the control of the [Baku Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26_Baku_Commissars), led by the veteran [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) [Stepan Shahumyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Shahumyan). Seeking to capitalize on the existing ethnic conflicts, by spring 1918, Bolsheviks inspired and condoned civil war in and around Baku. During the [March Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_Days) of 1918, Bolsheviks and [Dashnaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation), seeking to establish control over Baku streets, faced armed Azerbaijani groups. The Azerbaijanis suffered defeat from the united forces of the Baku Soviet and were massacred by Dashnak teams in what was called the [March Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_Days). An estimated 3,000–12,000 Azerbaijanis were killed in their own capital.[^cite_note-smithmusavat-47][^cite_note-minahan-48] After the massacre, on 28 May 1918, the Azerbaijani faction of the [Transcaucasian Sejm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_Democratic_Federative_Republic) proclaimed the independence of the [Azerbaijan Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Democratic_Republic) (ADR) in [Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganja,_Azerbaijan), thereby founding the first Muslim-majority [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_republic) and secular republic.[^cite_note-49] The newly independent Azerbaijani republic, being unable to defend the independence of the country on their own, asked the Ottoman Empire for military support in accordance with clause 4 of the treaty between the two countries. Shortly after, Azerbaijani forces, with support of the [Ottoman Army of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Army_of_Islam) led by [Nuru Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuri_Killigil), started their advance on Baku, eventually capturing the city from the loose coalition of [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik), [SRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist-Revolutionary_Party), [Dashnaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation), [Mensheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menshevik) and British forces under the command of General [Lionel Dunsterville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Dunsterville) on 15 September 1918.

After the [Battle of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baku) of August–September 1918, the Azerbaijani irregular troops, with the tacit support of the Turkish command, conducted four days of pillaging and killing 10,000–30,000[^cite_note-Gen-50] [Armenians of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Baku). This [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom) became known as the "[September Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_Days)". Shortly after this, Baku was proclaimed the new capital of the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic.

The Ottoman Empire, recognising defeat in World War I by October 1918, signed the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros) with the British (30 October 1918); this meant the evacuation of Turkish forces from Baku. Headed by General [William Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Montgomery_Thomson), some 5,000 British troops, including parts of the former [Dunsterforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunsterforce), arrived in Baku on 17 November. Thomson declared himself military governor of Baku and implemented [martial law](50477.md) in the city until "the civil power would be strong enough to release the forces from the responsibility to maintain the public order". British forces left before the end of 1919.[^cite_note-51]

### Soviet period

The independence of the Azerbaijani republic was short-lived. On 28 April 1920, the [11th Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Army_\(RSFSR\)) [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_invasion_of_Azerbaijan) Baku and reinstalled the Bolsheviks, making Baku the capital of the [Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) within the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).

Baku was the major oil city of the Soviet Union. From 1922 to 1930 Baku became the venue for one of the major [trade fairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_fair) of the Soviet Union, serving as a commercial bridgehead to Iran and the Middle East.[^cite_note-52]

### World War II

Main article: [Baku during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_during_World_War_II)

The major powers continued to note Baku's growing importance as a major energy hub. During [World War II](32927.md) (1939–1945) and particularly during the [1942 Nazi German invasion of the southwestern Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Blue), Baku became of vital strategic importance to the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers). In fact, capturing the [oil fields of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Azerbaijan#World_War_II) was a primary goal of the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)'s [Operation Edelweiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Edelweiss), carried out between May and November 1942. However, the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(1935%E2%80%931945\)) reached only a point some 530 kilometres (329 miles) northwest of Baku in November 1942, falling far short of the city's capture before being driven back during the Soviet [Operation Little Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Little_Saturn) in mid-December 1942.

### Fall of the Soviet Union and later

After the 1991 [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), Baku embarked on a process of restructuring on a scale unseen in its history.[^cite_note-53] Thousands of [panel buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrushchevka) from the Soviet period were demolished to make way for a green belt on its shores; parks and gardens were built on the land reclaimed by filling up the beaches of the [Baku Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Bay). Improvements were made in general cleaning, maintenance, and garbage collection to bring these services up to Western European standards. The city is growing dynamically and developing at pace on an east–west axis along the shores of the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). Sustainability has become a key factor in future urban development.[^cite_note-54]

## Geography



Satellite image of the Absheron peninsula, [Landsat 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsat_5), 6 September 2010

Baku is situated on the western coast of the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). In the vicinity of the city there are a number of [mud volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_volcano) (Keyraki, Bogkh-bogkha, [Lokbatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokbatan) and others) and [salt lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_lake) ([Boyukshor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Boyukshor), Khodasan, etc.).

### Climate

Baku has a [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSk*) with hot and humid summers, cool and occasionally wet winters, and strong winds all year long. However, unlike many other cities with such climate features, Baku does not see extremely hot summers and substantial sunshine hours. This is largely because of its northerly [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude) and the fact that it is located on a peninsula on the shore of the Caspian Sea.

Baku, and the [Absheron Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absheron_Peninsula) on which it is situated, is the most arid part of Azerbaijan ([precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) here is around or less than 200 mm (8 in) a year). This is largely due to the [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) effect from the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains), with corresponding latitudes on the [Black Sea](3386.md) on average receiving 2,300 mm (91 in) or more. The majority of the light annual precipitation occurs in seasons other than summer, but none of these seasons is particularly wet.

Baku was a popular vacation destination in the Soviet times. The city's past as a Soviet industrial centre left it one of the most polluted cities in the world, as of 2008.[^cite_note-ForbesPollution-55]

At the same time, Baku is noted as a very windy city throughout the year, hence the city's nickname the "City of Winds", and gale-force winds, the cold northern wind *[khazri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazri)* and the warm southern wind *[gilavar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilavar)* are typical here in all seasons. Indeed, the city is renowned for its fierce winter snow storms and harsh winds.[^cite_note-Web.archive.org-18] The speed of the *khazri* sometimes reaches 144 km/h (89 mph), which can cause damage to crops, trees and roof tiles.[^cite_note-56]

The daily mean temperature in July and August averages 26.4 °C (79.5 °F), and there is very little rainfall during that season. During summer, the *khazri* sweeps through, bringing desired coolness. Winter is cool and occasionally wet, with the daily mean temperature in January and February averaging 4.3 °C (39.7 °F). During winter, the *khazri* sweeps through, driven by polar [air masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_mass); temperatures on the coast frequently drop below freezing and make it feel bitterly cold. Winter [snow storms](244878.md) are occasional; snow usually melts within a few days after each snowfall.

## Demographics

Until 1988, Baku had large Russian, [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_people), and Jewish populations. With the onset of the [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) and the [pogrom against Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom_of_Armenians_in_Baku) starting in January 1990, the city's large Armenian population was expelled.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] After the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Soviet_Union), Azerbaijani President [Heydar Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Aliyev) returned several synagogues and a Jewish college, nationalised by the Soviets, to the Jewish community, and encouraged the restoration of these buildings. Seven of the original 11 synagogues, including the Gilah synagogue, built in 1896, and the large Kruei Synagogue, were renovated.[^cite_note-66]

### Ethnic groups



The Armenian [Saint Gregory the Illuminator's Church, Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Gregory_the_Illuminator's_Church,_Baku)

Today, the vast majority of Baku's population is made up of ethnic [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_people), and the rest are [Talysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talysh_people), [Russians](59510.md), [Lezgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lezgins) and others. The intensive growth of the population started in the middle of the 19th century when Baku was a small town with a population of about 7,000 people. The population increased again from about 13,000 in the 1860s to 112,000 in 1897 and 215,000 in 1913, making Baku the largest city in the Caucasus region.[^cite_note-86]

Several times during its history, Azerbaijanis were not a majority ethnic group in Baku.[^cite_note-87] From the 1970s, ethnic Azerbaijanis became a majority group in the city.[^cite_note-88] In 2003 Baku had 153,400 [internally displaced persons](427408.md) and 93,400 refugees.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

### Religion



The 13th-century [Bibi-Heybat Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibi-Heybat_Mosque) was built over the tomb of a descendant of the Islamic prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad).

[^cite_note-Bibi-91]

The largest religious community in Baku are [Shia Muslims](26961.md).[^cite_note-92] The city's notable mosques include [Juma Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juma_Mosque_\(Baku\)), [Bibi-Heybat Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibi-Heybat_Mosque), [Muhammad Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Mosque_\(Baku\)) and [Taza Pir Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taza_Pir_Mosque).

By article 48 of its [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Azerbaijan), Azerbaijan is a [secular state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_state) and ensures religious freedom. Religious minorities in the city include [Russian Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Christians), [Catholic Levantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantines_\(Latin_Christians\)), [Georgian Orthodox Christians](433340.md), [Albanian-Udi Apostolic Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Caucasian_Albania), [Lutherans](23371382.md), [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Jews), [Molokans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molokans), and [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) Muslims. Baku is the seat of the [Catholic](606848.md) [Apostolic Prefecture of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Prefecture_of_Azerbaijan).

[Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism), although extinct in the city as well as in the rest of the country by the present time, had a long [history in Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism_in_Azerbaijan) and the [Zoroastrian New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz) (Nowruz) continues to be the main holiday in the city as well as in the rest of Azerbaijan.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Baku)



Heydar Aliyev Avenue, home to the headquarters of multiple [publicly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_company) owned companies and enterprises in Azerbaijan. Along with the [Bay of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Baku), it hosts the majority of the country's [skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Azerbaijan)



Satellite image of Baku at night

Baku's largest industry is petroleum, and its petroleum exports make it a large contributor to Azerbaijan's [balance of payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_payments). The existence of petroleum has been known since the 8th century. In the 10th century, the Arabian traveler, Marudee, reported that both white and black oil were being extracted naturally from Baku.[^cite_note-93] By the 15th century, oil for lamps was obtained from hand-dug surface wells. Commercial exploitation began in 1872, and by the beginning of the 20th century the [Baku oil fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Azerbaijan) were the largest in the world. Towards the end of the 20th century, much of the onshore petroleum had been exhausted, and drilling had extended into the sea offshore. By the end of the 19th century [skilled workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skilled_worker) and specialists flocked to Baku. By 1900 the city had more than 3,000 oil wells, of which 2,000 were producing oil at industrial levels. Baku ranked as one of the largest centres for the production of [oil industry](994887.md) equipment before World War II. The World War II [Battle of Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad) was fought to determine who would have control of Baku oil fields. Fifty years before the battle, Baku produced half of the world's oil supply.[^cite_note-94]

The oil economy of Baku is undergoing a resurgence, with the development of the massive [Azeri-Chirag-Guneshli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeri%E2%80%93Chirag%E2%80%93Guneshli) field (Shallow water Gunashli by [SOCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Oil_Company_of_Azerbaijan_Republic), deeper areas by a consortium led by [BP](18998720.md)), development of the [Shah Deniz gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Deniz_gas_field), the expansion of the [Sangachal Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangachal_Terminal) and the construction of the [BTC Pipeline](397567.md).

The [Baku Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Stock_Exchange) is Azerbaijan's largest [stock exchange](28168.md), and largest in the [Caucasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) region by [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization). A relatively large number of transnational companies are headquartered in Baku. One of the more prominent institutions headquartered in Baku is the [International Bank of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bank_of_Azerbaijan), which employs over 1,000 people. International banks with branches in Baku include [HSBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSBC), [Société Générale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale) and [Credit Suisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Suisse).[^cite_note-95]

## Culture



[Inner City of Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Baku\)) is the oldest area of the capital

Baku is often referred to as the "[Paris of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_of_the_East)". The city's historic center, known as [Icheri Sheher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Baku\)), the Inner City, or the Old City, has various landmarks dating back to at least the 12th century. Among these are the [Maiden Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_\(Baku\)) and the [Shirvanshahs' Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Shirvanshahs).[^cite_note-100] Baku has many museums and galleries, including the [National Art Museum of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Art_Museum_of_Azerbaijan) and the [National Museum of History of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_History_of_Azerbaijan), which display both [traditional Azerbaijani and contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_art). The city maintains certain traditional crafts such as [carpet-weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knotted-pile_carpet) and pottery, which are displayed in [Azerbaijan National Carpet Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Carpet_Museum). Baku's theaters include the [Azerbaijan State Academic Opera and Ballet Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_State_Academic_Opera_and_Ballet_Theater) and the [Azerbaijan State Academic National Drama Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_State_Academic_National_Drama_Theatre), which host both local and international performances. Music is an important part of Baku's cultural landscape, with mugham being a traditional genre of music that has been recognized by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as part of the [Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists).[^cite_note-101] Baku hosts events like the [Baku International Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_International_Jazz_Festival) and the Baku International Film Festival.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

In 2007, the [Heydar Aliyev Cultural Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Aliyev_Cultural_Centre), designed by [Pritzker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pritzker_Prize)-winning architect [Zaha Hadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaha_Hadid), was opened.[^cite_note-104] Baku also has many museums such as [Baku Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Museum_of_Modern_Art) and [National Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_History_of_Azerbaijan), most notably featuring historical artifacts and art. Many of the city's cultural sites were celebrated in 2009 when Baku was designated an Islamic Culture Capital.[^cite_note-105] Baku was chosen to host the [Eurovision Dance Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Dance_Contest) 2010. It has also become the first city to host the first [European Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Games) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_European_Games).[^cite_note-106]

### Architecture

Main article: [Architecture in Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_in_Baku)



[Maiden Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_\(Baku\)) in [Old Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Baku\)), a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) built in the 11th–12th century, recognised as the symbol of the city.



[Multani Caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multani_Caravanserai), Baku

Baku has varying architecture, ranging from the [Old City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Baku\)) core to modern buildings and the spacious layout of Baku port. Many of the city's landmarks were built during the early 20th century, when architectural elements of the European styles were combined in [eclectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism) style.[^cite_note-112] Baku has an original and unique appearance, earning it a reputation as the *Paris of the East*.[^cite_note-113] Baku joined [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s [Network of Creative Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) as a Design City on 31 October 2019 on the occasion of World Cities' Day.[^cite_note-114]

#### Hamams

There are a number of ancient [hamams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammam) in Baku dating back to the 12th, 14th and 18th centuries. Hamams play a very important role in the architectural appearance of Baku.[^cite_note-115]

#### Modern architecture

Late modern and postmodern architecture began to appear in the early 2000s. With economic development, old buildings such as Atlant House were razed to make way for new ones. Buildings with all-glass shells have appeared around the city, the most prominent examples being the International Mugham Center, [Azerbaijan Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Tower), [Heydar Aliyev Cultural Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Aliyev_Centre?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Flame Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flame_Towers), [Baku Crystal Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Crystal_Hall), [Baku White City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_White_City), [SOCAR Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCAR_Tower) and [DENIZ Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DENIZ_Mall?action=edit\&redlink=1). These projects also caught the attention of international media as notable programmes such as [Discovery Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Channel)'s [Extreme Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_Engineering) did pieces focusing in on changes to the city.[^cite_note-116]

The Old City of Baku, also known as the Walled City of Baku, refers to the ancient Baku settlement. Most of the walls and towers, strengthened after the Russian conquest in 1806, survived. This section is picturesque, with its maze of narrow alleys and ancient buildings: the cobbled streets past the [Palace of the Shirvanshahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Shirvanshahs), two [caravansaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai), the baths and the [Juma Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juma_Mosque_\(Baku\)) (which used to house the [Azerbaijan National Carpet and Arts Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_National_Carpet_Museum) but is now a mosque again). The old town core also has dozens of small mosques, often without any particular sign to distinguish them as such.

In 2003, UNESCO placed the Inner City on the [List of World Heritage in Danger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_in_Danger), citing damage from a [November 2000 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Baku_earthquake), poor conservation as well as "dubious" restoration efforts.[^cite_note-117] In 2009 the Inner City was removed from the List of World Heritage in Danger.[^cite_note-118]

### Nightlife

Many clubs that are open until dawn can be found throughout the city. Clubs with an eastern flavour provide special treats from the [cuisine of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Azerbaijan) along with local music. Western-style clubs target younger, more energetic crowds.[^cite_note-127] Most of the [public houses](24578.md) and bars are located near [Fountains Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_Square,_Baku) and are usually open until the early hours of the morning.

### Parks and gardens



Philarmony garden

Baku has large sections of greenery, either preserved by the National Government or designated as green zones. The city, however, continues to lack a green belt development as economic activity pours into the capital, resulting in massive housing projects along the suburbs.[^cite_note-128]

[Baku Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Boulevard) is a pedestrian promenade that runs parallel to Baku's seafront. The boulevard contains an amusement park, [yacht club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacht_club), [musical fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_fountain), statues and monuments. The park is popular with dog-walkers and joggers and is convenient for tourists. It is adjacent to the newly built [International Centre of Mugham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mugam_Centre_of_Azerbaijan?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the musical fountain.

Other parks and gardens include Heydar Aliyev Park, Samad Vurgun Park, Narimanov Park, [Alley of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alley_of_Honor) and the [Fountains Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_Square,_Baku). The [Martyrs' Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs'_Lane), formerly the Kirov Park, is dedicated to the memory of those who died during the [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](4695860.md) and also to the 137 people killed on [Black January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_January).

[UEFA Europa League Final 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Europa_League_final) was played at the [Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Olympic_Stadium) in Baku on 29 May 2019 between [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) sides [Chelsea](7473.md) and [Arsenal](2174.md).[^cite_note-138] The city's main football clubs is [Neftçi Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neftchi_Baku_PFK) of who first has nine Premier League titles, making Neftchi the second most successful Azerbaijani football club. Baku also has several football clubs in the premier and regional leagues, including [Zira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zira_FK) in the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Premier_League), and [Sabail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabail_FK) in the [First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_First_League). The city's second-largest stadium, [Tofiq Bahramov Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofiq_Bahramov_Stadium) hosts a number of domestic and international competitions and was the main sports centre of the city for a long period until the construction of [Baku Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Olympic_Stadium).

In the [Azerbaijan Women's Volleyball Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Women's_Volleyball_Super_League), Baku is represented by [Rabita Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabita_Baku), [Azerrail Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerrail_Baku), [Lokomotiv Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokomotiv_Baku) and [Azeryol Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeryol_Baku).

Baku has been designated the World Capital of Sport for 2026.[^cite_note-139]

## Transport



Baku black cab, introduced in 2011



Baku Metro

Throughout history, the transport system of Baku used the now-defunct [horsecars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsecar), trams and [narrow gauge railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_gauge_railways). As of 2011, 1,000 [black cabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackney_carriage) are ordered by Baku Taxi Company, and as part of a programme originally announced by the Transport Ministry of Azerbaijan, there is a plan to introduce London cabs into Baku.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] The move was part of £16 million agreement between [Manganese Bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese_Bronze) subsidiary [LTI Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Taxis_International) and Baku Taxi Company.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]

Local rail transport includes the [Baku Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Funicular) and the [Baku Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Metro). Baku Metro was opened in November 1967 and includes 3 lines and 25 stations.[^cite_note-144] In 2008, the plans to construct 41 new stations over the next 17 years were announced. These will serve the new [bus complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_International_Bus_Terminal) and the international airport.[^cite_note-145] In 2019, the [Baku suburban railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_suburban_railway) opened.

[Baku Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Railway_Station) is the terminus for national and international rail links to the city. The [Kars–Tbilisi–Baku railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars%E2%80%93Tbilisi%E2%80%93Baku_railway), which directly connects Turkey, Georgia and Azerbaijan, began to be constructed in 2007 and opened in 2017.[^cite_note-146]



[Baku Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Funicular)

Shipping services operate regularly from Baku across the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) to [Turkmenbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrkmenba%C5%9Fy,_Turkmenistan) (formerly Krasnovodsk) in Turkmenistan and to [Bandar Anzali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Anzali) and [Bandar Nowshar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowshahr) in Iran.[^cite_note-147] The commuter ferries, along with the high-speed catamaran *Seabus* (*Deniz Avtobusu*), also form the main connection between the city and the [Absheron peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absheron_peninsula).[^cite_note-148]

Baku Port was founded in 1902 and claims to be the largest Caspian Sea port. It has six facilities: the main cargo terminal, the container terminal, the ferry terminal, the oil terminal, the passenger terminal and the port fleet terminal. The port's throughput capacity reaches 15 million [tonnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonnes) of liquid bulk and up to 10 million tons of dry cargoes.[^cite_note-149] In 2010, the [Baku International Sea Trade Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_International_Sea_Trade_Port) began to be reconstructed. The construction was planned to take place in three stages and to be completed by 2016. The estimated costs were US\$400 million.[^cite_note-150] From April to November, Baku Port is accessible to ships loading cargoes for direct voyages from Western European and Mediterranean ports. The State Road M-1 and the [European route E60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E60) are the two main motorway connections between Europe and Azerbaijan.

The [Heydar Aliyev International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Aliyev_International_Airport) is the only commercial airport serving Baku.[^cite_note-151] There are also several [military airbases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_airbase) near Baku, such as [Baku Kala Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Kala_Air_Base).[^cite_note-152]
