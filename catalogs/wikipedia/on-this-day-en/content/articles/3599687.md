**Succession to the Liechtensteiner throne** is determined by the [Constitution of the Princely House of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Princely_House_of_Liechtenstein), where the throne is inherited by a sovereign's children or by a childless sovereign's nearest [collateral line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateral_descendant). In August 2026, [agnatic (male-only) primogeniture](136535.md) was abandoned, meaning that in future generations females will no longer be excluded from the line of succession.&#x20;

[Hans-Adam II, Prince of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Adam_II,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein) has been the sovereign since 1989, and his [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent) is his eldest son [Alois, Hereditary Prince of Liechtenstein](30873557.md). Alois's eldest son, [Prince Joseph Wenzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Joseph_Wenzel_of_Liechtenstein), is next in line.[^cite_note-1]

## Succession rules

In 1606, the first prince of Liechtenstein, [Karl I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_I,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein), and his younger brothers, [Maximilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_of_Liechtenstein) and [Gundakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundakar,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein), signed a Family Covenant, agreeing that the headship of the family should pass according to [agnatic primogeniture](136535.md) to the heir of the most senior line.[^cite_note-Embassy-2] The family continued to be governed by various statutes until 1993 when the new [house law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_law), the [Constitution of the Princely House of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Princely_House_of_Liechtenstein), was published on 6 December, repealing all statutes before it.[^cite_note-fuerstenhaus-3][^cite_note-4] According to the house law, the right to succeed to the throne of Liechtenstein was reserved for male [patrilineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrilineal) descendants of [Johann I Joseph, Prince of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_I_Joseph,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein) born to married parents, excluding issue born of marriage to which the sovereign did not consent. Should there be no more eligible patrilineal descendants left, the sovereign holds the right to adopt an [heir presumptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_presumptive).[^cite_note-fuerstenhaus-3][^cite_note-5] If a member of the house adopts a prince who is in the line of succession, the adoptee's place in the line will not be altered. The house law also provides for the possibility of renunciation of succession rights.[^cite_note-fuerstenhaus-3]

In addition, the house law allows for the reigning prince to appoint their heir presumptive as a [regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent).[^cite_note-succession-6] This is the case with the reigning prince [Hans-Adam II, Prince of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Adam_II,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein), who in 2004 appointed heir presumptive [Alois, Hereditary Prince of Liechtenstein](30873557.md) as his regent, where Alois holds the effective duties as prince but Hans-Adam II remains the prince and head of state officially.[^cite_note-7]

In 2004, [Hans-Adam II, Prince of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Adam_II,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein) publicly responded to criticism from a committee of the [United Nations](31769.md) which had voiced concerns about the exclusion of women from the line of succession, stating that the rule was older than the state itself.[^cite_note-Eccardt-8] Up until 2026, there was no scenario under which a woman could succeed to the throne of Liechtenstein.[^cite_note-Eccardt-8][^cite_note-\:0-9]

### 2026 Princely House Law

On 15 August 2026, as part of his speech on [Liechtenstein National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_National_Day),[^cite_note-\:1-10] Hereditary Prince Alois announced that the house law would be amended to move from [agnatic primogeniture](136535.md) to absolute primogeniture, meaning that in the future women will be eligible to inherit the throne.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-11] The changes were approved on 12 August by the required two-thirds of the princely family members with voting rights.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

As per the new house rules, all female descendants of the princely house will be granted the same participation rights in matters governed by the house as male descendants.[^cite_note-\:1-10] Alois specified that the new succession rules apply to future births in the princely family, and would not affect the current line of succession.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-13]
