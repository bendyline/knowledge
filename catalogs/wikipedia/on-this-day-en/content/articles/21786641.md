The **United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization** (**UNESCO** [/juːˈnɛskoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English))[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] is a [specialized agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_specialized_agencies_of_the_United_Nations) of the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) with the aim of promoting [world peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace) and [security](1946540.md) through international cooperation in education, arts, [sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences) and culture.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-history-5] It has 194 [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_UNESCO) and 12 associate members,[^cite_note-6] as well as partners in the [non-governmental](46539.md), [intergovernmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_organization) and [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector).[^cite_note-7] Headquartered in [Paris](22989.md), France, UNESCO has 53 regional field offices[^cite_note-8] and 199 [national commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Commissions_for_UNESCO).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

UNESCO was founded in 1945 as the successor to the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations)' [International Committee on Intellectual Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Intellectual_Cooperation).[^cite_note-intellectualcooperation-11] UNESCO's founding mission, which was shaped by the events of [World War II](32927.md), is to advance [peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace), [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development) and human rights by facilitating collaboration and dialogue among nations.[^cite_note-unesdoc.unesco.org-12] It pursues this objective through five major programme areas: education, [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science), [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science)/[human sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_science), culture and communication/information. UNESCO sponsors projects that improve [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy), provide technical training and education, advance science, protect independent media and [press freedom](105323.md), preserve [regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region) and [cultural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_history), and promote [cultural diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_diversity).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The organization prominently helps establish and secure [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) of [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage) and [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_heritage) importance.[^cite_note-16]

UNESCO is governed by the General Conference composed of member states and associate members, which meets biannually to set the agency's programmes and budget. It also elects members of the executive board, which manages UNESCO's work, and appoints every four years a Director-General, who serves as UNESCO's chief administrator.

## History

### Origins

UNESCO and its mandate for international cooperation can be traced to a [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) resolution on 21 September 1921, to elect a commission to study the feasibility of having nations freely share cultural, educational and scientific achievements.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] This new body, the [International Committee on Intellectual Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Intellectual_Cooperation) (ICIC), was created in 1922[^cite_note-intellectualcooperation-11] and counted such figures as [Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson), [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein), [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie), [Robert A. Millikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Millikan), and [Gonzague de Reynold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzague_de_Reynold) among its members (being thus a small commission of the League of Nations essentially centred on Western Europe[^cite_note-19]). The International Institute for Intellectual Cooperation (IIIC) was then created in Paris in September 1924, to act as the executing agency for the ICIC.[^cite_note-20] However, the onset of [World War II](32927.md) largely interrupted the work of these predecessor organizations.[^cite_note-21] As for private initiatives, the [International Bureau of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Education) (IBE) began to work as a [non-governmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental) organization in the service of international educational development since December 1925[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUNESCO1987-22] and joined UNESCO in 1969, after having established a joint commission in 1952.[^cite_note-23]

### Creation

After the signing of the [Atlantic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Charter) and the [Declaration of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_the_United_Nations), the Conference of Allied Ministers of Education (CAME) began meetings in London which continued from 16 November 1942 to 5 December 1945. On 30 October 1943, the necessity for an international organization was expressed in the Moscow Declaration, agreed upon by [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_government), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), the United States and the USSR. This was followed by the [Dumbarton Oaks Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumbarton_Oaks_Conference) proposals of 9 October 1944. Upon the proposal of CAME and in accordance with the recommendations of the [United Nations Conference on International Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_International_Organization) (UNCIO), held in San Francisco from April to June 1945, a United Nations Conference for the establishment of an educational and cultural organization (ECO/CONF) was convened in London from 1 to 16 November 1945 with forty-four governments represented. The idea of UNESCO was largely developed by [Rab Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rab_Butler), the [Minister of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Education) for the United Kingdom, who had a great deal of influence in its development.[^cite_note-24] At the ECO/CONF, the Constitution of UNESCO was introduced through Article 57, and signed by 41 countries, and a Preparatory Commission was established.[^cite_note-UNESCO_75Years-25] The Preparatory Commission operated between 16 November 1945, and 4 November 1946 — the date when UNESCO's Constitution came into force with the deposit of the twentieth ratification by a member state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnesco1945-26]

The first General Conference took place from 19 November to 10 December 1946, and elected [Julian Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Huxley) to Director-General.[^cite_note-27] United States Army colonel, university president and [civil rights](37071.md) advocate [Blake R. Van Leer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_R._Van_Leer) joined as a member as well.[^cite_note-28] The Constitution was amended in November 1954 when the General Conference resolved that members of the executive board would be representatives of the governments of the States of which they are nationals and would not, as before, act in their personal capacity.[^cite_note-29] This change in governance distinguished UNESCO from its predecessor, the ICIC, in how member states would work together in the organization's fields of competence. As member states worked together over time to realize UNESCO's mandate, political and historical factors have shaped the organization's operations in particular during the [Cold War](325329.md), the [decolonization](302084.md) process, and the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### Development

Among the major achievements of the organization is its work against racism, for example through influential [statements on race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Race_Question) starting with a declaration of anthropologists (among them was [Claude Lévi-Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_L%C3%A9vi-Strauss)) and other scientists in 1950 and concluding with the 1978 [Declaration on Race and Racial Prejudice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_statements_on_race#Declaration_on_Race_and_Racial_Prejudice_\(1978\)).[^cite_note-32]

In 1955, the [Republic of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Africa) withdrew from UNESCO saying that some of the organization's publications amounted to "interference" in the country's "racial problems".[^cite_note-33] It rejoined the organization in 1994 under the leadership of [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

One of the early work of UNESCO in the education field was a pilot project on fundamental education in the Marbial Valley, Haiti, which was launched in 1947. Following this project one of expert missions to other countries, included a 1949 mission to Afghanistan.[^cite_note-\:0-36] UNESCO recommended in 1948 that Member countries should make free primary education [compulsory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education) and universal.[^cite_note-\:0-36] The World Conference on Education for All, in [Jomtien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomtien), Thailand, started a global movement in 1990 to provide basic education for all children, youths and adults.[^cite_note-\:0-36] In 2000, [World Education Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Education_Forum) in [Dakar](44251.md), Senegal, led member governments to commit for achieving basic education for all in 2015.[^cite_note-\:0-36]

The [World Declaration on Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Declaration_on_Higher_Education) was adopted by UNESCO's World Conference on Higher Education on 9 October 1998,[^cite_note-37] with the aim of setting global standards on the ideals and accessibility of [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education).

UNESCO's early activities in culture included the [International Campaign to Save the Monuments of Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Campaign_to_Save_the_Monuments_of_Nubia), launched in 1960.[^cite_note-38] The purpose of the campaign was to move the Great Temple of [Abu Simbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel) to keep it from being swamped by the Nile after the construction of the [Aswan Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswan_Dam). During the 20-year campaign, 22 monuments and architectural complexes were relocated. This was the first and largest in a series of campaigns including [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro) (Pakistan), [Fes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fes) (Morocco), [Kathmandu](17168.md) (Nepal), [Borobudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borobudur) (Indonesia) and the [Acropolis of Athens](2076.md) (Greece).[^cite_note-39] The organization's work on heritage led to the adoption, in 1972, of the Convention concerning the Protection of the World Cultural and Natural Heritage.[^cite_note-40] In 1976, the [World Heritage Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Committee) was established and the first sites were included on the [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_List) in 1978.[^cite_note-41] Since then important legal instruments on cultural heritage and diversity have been adopted by UNESCO member states in 2003 (Convention for the Safeguarding of the [Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_Cultural_Heritage))[^cite_note-42] and 2005 ([Convention on the Protection and Promotion of the Diversity of Cultural Expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_the_Protection_and_Promotion_of_the_Diversity_of_Cultural_Expressions)).[^cite_note-43]

An intergovernmental meeting of UNESCO in Paris in December 1951 led to the creation of the [European Council for Nuclear Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council_for_Nuclear_Research), which was responsible for establishing the [European Organization for Nuclear Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Organization_for_Nuclear_Research) (CERN)[^cite_note-44] later on, in 1954.[^cite_note-45]

Arid Zone programming, 1948–1966, is another example of an early major UNESCO project in the field of [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences).[^cite_note-46]

In 1968, UNESCO organized the first intergovernmental conference aimed at reconciling the environment and development, a problem that continues to be addressed in the field of [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development). The main outcome of the 1968 conference was the creation of UNESCO's [Man and the Biosphere Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_and_the_Biosphere_Programme).[^cite_note-47]

UNESCO has been credited with the diffusion of national science bureaucracies.[^cite_note-48]

In the field of communication, the "free flow of ideas by word and image" has been in UNESCO's constitution since it was established, following the experience of the Second World War when control of information was a factor in indoctrinating populations for aggression.[^cite_note-\:1-49] In the years immediately following World War II, efforts were concentrated on reconstruction and on the identification of needs for means of mass communication around the world. UNESCO started organizing training and education for journalists in the 1950s.[^cite_note-\:1-49] In response to calls for a "[New World Information and Communication Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_Information_and_Communication_Order)" in the late 1970s, UNESCO established the International Commission for the Study of Communication Problems,[^cite_note-\:2-50] which produced the 1980 [MacBride report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacBride_report) (named after the chair of the commission, the Nobel Peace Prize laureate [Seán MacBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Se%C3%A1n_MacBride)).[^cite_note-\:2-50] The same year, UNESCO created the [International Programme for the Development of Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Programme_for_the_Development_of_Communication) (IPDC), a multilateral forum designed to promote media development in developing countries.[^cite_note-51] In 1993, UNESCO's General Conference endorsed the [Windhoek Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windhoek_Declaration) on [media independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_independence) and pluralism, which led the UN General Assembly to declare the date of its adoption, 3 May, as [World Press Freedom Day](220703.md).[^cite_note-52] Since 1997, UNESCO has awarded the [UNESCO / Guillermo Cano World Press Freedom Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO/Guillermo_Cano_World_Press_Freedom_Prize) every 3 May.[^cite_note-53]

### 21st century

### Changes in States' memberships

#### Israel, Palestine, and the United States

In 2011, UNESCO admitted Palestine as a member.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

Laws passed in the United States after Palestine applied for UNESCO and [WHO](33583.md) membership in April 1989[^cite_note-Sakran2019-57][^cite_note-58] mean that the United States cannot contribute financially to any UN organization that accepts Palestine as a full member.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] As a result, the United States withdrew its funding, which had accounted for about 22% of UNESCO's budget.[^cite_note-61] Israel also reacted to Palestine's admittance to UNESCO by freezing Israeli payments to UNESCO and imposing sanctions on the [Palestinian Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority),[^cite_note-62] stating that Palestine's admittance would be detrimental "to potential peace talks".[^cite_note-63]

In 2013, two years after stopping payment of its dues to UNESCO, the United States and Israel lost UNESCO voting rights, but without losing the right to be elected; thus, the United States was elected as a member of the executive board for the period from 2016 to 2019.[^cite_note-64] In 2019, Israel left UNESCO after 69 years of membership, with Israel's ambassador to the UN [Danny Danon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Danon) writing: "UNESCO is the body that continually rewrites history, including by erasing the Jewish connection to Jerusalem... it is corrupted and manipulated by Israel's enemies... we are not going to be a member of an organization that deliberately acts against us."[^cite_note-65]

Also in 2023, the United States stated its intent to rejoin UNESCO, five years after leaving, and to pay its US\$600 million of back dues.[^cite_note-usa2023-66] The United States was readmitted by the [UNESCO General Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_General_Conference) that July.[^cite_note-uspbs-67] Three years later, in 2025, however, the United States stated its intent to withdraw again, to be effective as of December 2026.[^cite_note-68]

#### Russia

In 2023, Russia was not renewed as member of the executive committee for the first time, after failing to get sufficient votes.[^cite_note-69]

### Cultural policies and sustainable development

In the context of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and United Nations-wide efforts to achieve the [2030 Sustainable Development Agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals), the UNESCO reactivated in 2022 the cycle of [MONDIACULT Conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Conference_on_Cultural_Policies_and_Sustainable_Development) (World Conference on Cultural Policies and Sustainable Development) whose first edition had been held in [Mexico City](18987.md) in 1982. The 2022 MONDIACULT conference was held again in [Mexico](3966054.md), and a 2025 edition is planned in [Barcelona](4443.md), [Spain](26667.md).

## Activities



UNESCO offices in [Brasília](4752.md).

UNESCO implements its activities through five programme areas: education, natural sciences, social and human sciences, culture, and communication and information.[^cite_note-70]

### New proposed lists

Two new UNESCO lists have been proposed.[^cite_note-newunescolists-95]

The first proposed list would focus on movable cultural heritage such as artifacts, paintings, and biofacts. The list could include cultural objects, such as the [Jōmon Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_Venus) of Japan, the *[Mona Lisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mona_Lisa)* of France, the [Gebel el-Arak Knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebel_el-Arak_Knife) of [Egypt](8087628.md), *[The Ninth Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ninth_Wave)* of Russia, the [Seated Woman of Çatalhöyük](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seated_Woman_of_%C3%87atalh%C3%B6y%C3%BCk) of Turkey, the [*David* (Michelangelo)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_\(Michelangelo\)) of Italy, the [Mathura Herakles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_Herakles) of India, the [Manunggul Jar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manunggul_Jar) of the Philippines, the [Crown of Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Baekje) of South Korea, *[The Hay Wain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hay_Wain)* of the United Kingdom, and the [Benin Bronzes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Bronzes) of Nigeria.[^cite_note-newunescolists-95]

The second proposed list would focus on the world's living species.[^cite_note-newunescolists-95][^cite_note-96]

## Media

UNESCO and its specialized institutions issue a number of magazines.

Created in 1945, *[The UNESCO Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_UNESCO_Courier)* magazine states its mission to "promote UNESCO's ideals, maintain a platform for the dialogue between cultures and provide a forum for international debate". Since March 2006 it has been available free online, with limited printed issues. Its articles express the opinions of the authors which are not necessarily the opinions of UNESCO. There was a hiatus in publishing between 2012 and 2017.[^cite_note-97]

In 1950, UNESCO initiated the quarterly review *Impact of Science on Society* (also known as *Impact*) to discuss the influence of science on society. The journal ceased publication in 1992.[^cite_note-98]

As of July 2023, UNESCO has 194 member states and 12 associate members.[^cite_note-130] Some members are not [independent states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_state) and some members have additional National Organizing Committees from some of their [dependent territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory).[^cite_note-Palestine-131] UNESCO state parties are the [United Nations member states](31969.md) (except Israel[^cite_note-132] and [Liechtenstein](17810.md)), as well as [Cook Islands](7067.md), [Niue](21228.md) and [Palestine](241405.md).[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] Israel left UNESCO on 31 December 2018.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

On 4 May 2025, Nicaragua announced its decision to withdraw from UNESCO, effective 31 December 2026.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

The United States left UNESCO in 1984, rejoined in 2003, left again in 2018, and rejoined in 2023.[^cite_note-usa2023-66][^cite_note-us_withdrawal-139][^cite_note-uspbs-67] On 22 July 2025, the United States informed the Director-General of its decision to withdraw again, effective 31 December 2026.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-us_withdrawal-139][^cite_note-141]

## Governing bodies

### Director-General

As of June 2023, there have been 12 Directors-General of UNESCO since its inception – nine men and two women. The 12 Directors-General of UNESCO have come from seven regions within the organization: West Europe (5), Central America (1), North America (2), West Africa (1), East Asia (1), East Europe (1), Middle East (1).

To date, there has been no elected Director-General from the remaining ten regions within UNESCO: Southeast Asia, South Asia, Central and North Asia, North Africa, East Africa, Central Africa, South Africa, Australia-Oceania, and South America.

## Offices and headquarters

Main article: [World Heritage Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Centre)

The UNESCO headquarters is located at [Place de Fontenoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_de_Fontenoy) in Paris, France. Several architects collaborated on the construction of the headquarters, including [Bernard Zehrfuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Zehrfuss), [Marcel Breuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Breuer) and Luigi Nervi.[^cite_note-153]



Main conference room at UNESCO headquarters (Paris).

It includes a *Garden of Peace* which was donated by the [Government of Japan](30858300.md).[^cite_note-154] This garden was designed by American-Japanese sculptor artist [Isamu Noguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isamu_Noguchi) in 1958 and installed by Japanese gardener Toemon Sano. In 1994–1995, in memory of the 50th anniversary of UNESCO, a [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation) room was built by [Tadao Ando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadao_Ando).[^cite_note-155]

UNESCO's field offices across the globe are categorized into four primary office types based upon their function and geographic coverage: cluster offices, national offices, regional bureaus and liaison offices.

## Controversies

### New World Information and Communication Order

UNESCO has been the centre of controversy in the past, particularly in its relationships with the United States, the United Kingdom, Singapore and the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). During the 1970s and 1980s, UNESCO's support for a "[New World Information and Communication Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_Information_and_Communication_Order)" and its [MacBride report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacBride_report) calling for democratization of the media and more egalitarian access to information was condemned in these countries as attempts to curb [freedom of the press](105323.md). UNESCO was perceived as a platform for communists and Third World dictators to attack the West, in contrast to accusations made by the USSR in the late 1940s and early 1950s.[^cite_note-160] In 1984, the United States withheld its contributions and withdrew from the organization in protest, followed by the United Kingdom in 1985.[^cite_note-161] Singapore withdrew also at the end of 1985, citing rising membership fees.[^cite_note-162] Following a change of government in 1997, the UK rejoined. The United States rejoined in 2003, followed by Singapore on 8 October 2007.[^cite_note-163]

### China

UNESCO has been criticized as being used by the People's Republic of China to present a [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) version of history and to dilute the contributions of [ethnic minorities in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China) such as [Uyghurs](31783.md) and [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

### Israel

Israel was admitted to UNESCO in 1949, one year after its creation. Israel has maintained its membership since then. In 2010, Israel designated the [Cave of the Patriarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_the_Patriarchs) in [Hebron](38577.md) and [Rachel's Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel's_Tomb) in [Bethlehem](4312.md) – both in the [West Bank](33209.md) – as [National Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Heritage_Sites_of_Israel) and announced restoration work, prompting criticism from the Obama administration and protests from Palestinians.[^cite_note-167] In October 2010, UNESCO's executive board voted to declare the sites as "al-Haram al-Ibrahimi/Tomb of the Patriarchs" and "Bilal bin Rabah Mosque/Rachel's Tomb" and stated that they were "an integral part of the [occupied Palestinian Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Palestinian_Territories)" and any unilateral Israeli action was a violation of [international law](8195726.md).[^cite_note-168] UNESCO described the sites as significant to "people of the Muslim, Christian and Jewish traditions", and accused Israel of highlighting only the Jewish character of the sites.[^cite_note-169] Israel in turn accused UNESCO of "detach\[ing] the Nation of Israel from its heritage", and accused it of being politically motivated.[^cite_note-170] The [Rabbi of the Western Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Rabinovitch) said that Rachel's tomb had not previously been declared a holy Muslim site.[^cite_note-171] Israel partially suspended ties with UNESCO. Israeli Deputy Foreign Minister [Danny Ayalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Ayalon) declared that the resolution was a "part of Palestinian escalation". [Zevulun Orlev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zevulun_Orlev), chairman of the [Knesset](74656.md) Education and Culture Committee, referred to the resolutions as an attempt to undermine the mission of UNESCO as a scientific and cultural organization that promotes cooperation throughout the world.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

On 28 June 2011, UNESCO's World Heritage Committee, at [Jordan](7515964.md)'s insistence, censured Israel's decision to demolish and rebuild the [Mughrabi Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughrabi-Bridge) Bridge in Jerusalem for safety reasons. Israel stated that Jordan had signed an agreement with Israel stipulating that the existing bridge must be dismantled for safety reasons; Jordan disputed the agreement, saying that it was only signed under U.S. pressure. Israel was also unable to address the UNESCO committee over objections from [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-174]

In January 2014, days before it was scheduled to open, UNESCO Director-General, [Irina Bokova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Bokova), "indefinitely postponed" and effectively cancelled an exhibit created by the [Simon Wiesenthal Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Wiesenthal_Centre) entitled "The People, The Book, The Land: The 3,500-year relationship between the [Jewish people and the Land of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_and_Judaism_in_the_Land_of_Israel)". The event was scheduled to run from 21 January through 30 January in Paris. Bokova cancelled the event after representatives of Arab states at UNESCO argued that its display would "harm the [peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab-Israeli_peace_process)".[^cite_note-175] The author of the exhibition, professor [Robert Wistrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wistrich) of the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University)'s [Vidal Sassoon International Center for the Study of Antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidal_Sassoon_International_Center_for_the_Study_of_Antisemitism), called the cancellation an "appalling act", and characterized Bokova's decision as "an arbitrary act of total cynicism and, really, contempt for the Jewish people and its history". UNESCO amended the decision to cancel the exhibit within the year, and it quickly achieved popularity and was viewed as a great success.[^cite_note-176]

On 1 January 2019, Israel formally left UNESCO in pursuance of the US withdrawal over perceived continuous anti-Israel bias.[^cite_note-177]

#### Occupied Palestine Resolution

Main article: [Occupied Palestine Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Palestine_Resolution)

On 13 October 2016, UNESCO passed a resolution on East Jerusalem that condemned Israel for "aggressions" by Israeli police and soldiers and "illegal measures" against the freedom of worship and Muslims' access to their holy sites, while also recognizing Israel as the occupying power. Palestinian leaders welcomed the decision.[^cite_note-UNESCO_adopts_anti-178] While the text acknowledged the "importance of the Old City of Jerusalem and its walls for the three monotheistic religions", it referred to the sacred hilltop compound in Jerusalem's Old City only by its Muslim name "Al-Haram al-Sharif", Arabic for Noble Sanctuary. In response, Israel denounced the UNESCO resolution for its omission of the words "Temple Mount" or "Har HaBayit", stating that it [denies Jewish ties to the key holy site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_denial).[^cite_note-UNESCO_adopts_anti-178][^cite_note-179] After receiving criticism from numerous Israeli politicians and diplomats, including [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) and [Ayelet Shaked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayelet_Shaked), Israel froze all ties with the organization.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181] The resolution was condemned by [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) and the Director-General of UNESCO, Irina Bokova, who said that Judaism, Islam and Christianity have clear historical connections to Jerusalem and "to deny, conceal or erase any of the Jewish, Christian or Muslim traditions undermines the integrity of the site.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] "Al-Aqsa Mosque \[or] Al-Haram al-Sharif" is also Temple Mount, whose Western Wall is the holiest place in Judaism."[^cite_note-184] It was also rejected by the Czech Parliament which said the resolution reflects a "hateful [anti-Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calls_for_the_destruction_of_Israel) sentiment",[^cite_note-timesofisrael.com-185] and hundreds of Italian Jews demonstrated in Rome over Italy's abstention.[^cite_note-timesofisrael.com-185] On 26 October, UNESCO approved a reviewed version of the resolution, which also criticized Israel for its continuous "refusal to let the body's experts access Jerusalem's holy sites to determine their conservation status".[^cite_note-186] Despite containing some softening of language following Israeli protests over a previous version, Israel continued to denounce the text.[^cite_note-187] The resolution refers to the site Jews and Christians refer to as the Temple Mount, or Har HaBayit in Hebrew, only by its Arab name – a significant semantic decision also adopted by UNESCO's executive board, triggering condemnation from Israel and its allies. U.S. Ambassador Crystal Nix Hines stated: "This item should have been defeated. These politicized and one-sided resolutions are damaging the credibility of UNESCO."[^cite_note-188]

In October 2017, the United States and Israel announced they would withdraw from the organization, citing in-part anti-Israel bias.[^cite_note-stategov-189][^cite_note-190]

### Palestine

#### Palestinian youth magazine controversy

In February 2011, an article was published in a Palestinian youth magazine in which a teenage girl described one of her four role models as [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md). In December 2011, UNESCO, which partly funded the magazine, condemned the material and subsequently withdrew support.[^cite_note-191]

#### Islamic University of Gaza controversy

In 2012, UNESCO decided to establish a chair at the [Islamic University of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_University_of_Gaza) in the field of [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics), and [space sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_science),[^cite_note-UNESCOIslamicUniversity-192] fueling controversy and criticism. Israel bombed the school in 2008 stating that they develop and store weapons there, which Israel restated in criticizing UNESCO's move.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-MFAUNESCO-194]

The head, [Kamalain Shaath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamalain_Shaath), defended UNESCO, stating that "the Islamic University is a purely academic university that is interested only in education and its development".[^cite_note-NationalPostUNESCO-195][^cite_note-ShalitGazaIslamicUniversity-196][^cite_note-BostonIUGProfile-197] Israeli ambassador to UNESCO [Nimrod Barkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimrod_Barkan) planned to submit a letter of protest with information about the university's ties to [Hamas](13913.md), especially angry that this was the first Palestinian university that UNESCO chose to cooperate with. He never provided any evidence to support his claim of ties to [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-HaaretzUNESCOGaza-198] The Jewish organization [B'nai B'rith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'nai_B'rith) criticized the move as well.[^cite_note-BnaiBrithUNESCO-199]

### Listing Nanjing Massacre documents

In 2015, Japan threatened to halt funding of UNESCO because of the organization's decision to include documents related to the 1937 [Nanjing massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_massacre) in the latest listing for its "Memory of the World" program.[^cite_note-200] In October 2016, Japanese Foreign Minister [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida) confirmed that Japan's 2016 annual funding of ¥4.4 billion had been suspended, although he denied any direct link with the Nanjing document controversy.[^cite_note-201]

### United States and UNESCO

The United States withdrew from UNESCO in 1984, citing the "highly politicized" nature of the organization, its ostensible "hostility toward the basic institutions of a [free society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_society?action=edit\&redlink=1), especially a [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) and a [free press](105323.md)", as well as its "unrestrained budgetary expansion", and poor management under then Director-General Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow of Senegal.[^cite_note-withdrawal-202]

On 19 September 1989, US Congressman [Jim Leach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Leach) stated before a congressional subcommittee:[^cite_note-cspan-203]

> The reasons for the withdrawal of the United States from UNESCO in 1984 are well-known; my view is that we overreacted to the calls of some who wanted to radicalize UNESCO, and the calls of others who wanted the United States to lead in emasculating the UN system. The fact is UNESCO is one of the least dangerous international institutions ever created. While some member countries within UNESCO attempted to push journalistic views antithetical to the values of the west, and engage in Israel bashing, UNESCO itself never adopted such radical postures. The United States opted for empty-chair diplomacy, after winning, not losing, the battles we engaged in... It was nuts to get out, and would be nuttier not to rejoin.

Leach concluded that the record showed Israel bashing, a call for a new world information order, money management, and arms control policy to be the impetuses behind the withdrawal; he asserted that before departing from UNESCO, a withdrawal from the [IAEA](14984.md) had been pushed on him.[^cite_note-cspan-203] On 1 October 2003, the United States rejoined UNESCO.[^cite_note-withdrawal-202]

On 12 October 2017, the United States notified UNESCO it would again withdraw from the organization, on 31 December 2018; Israel followed suit.[^cite_note-204] The [Department of State](31975.md) cited "mounting arrears at UNESCO, the need for fundamental reform in the organization, and continuing anti-Israel bias at UNESCO".[^cite_note-stategov-189]

The United States has not paid over \$600 million in dues[^cite_note-205] since it stopped paying its \$80 million annual UNESCO dues when Palestine became a full member in 2011. Israel and the United States were among the 14 votes against the membership out of 194 member countries.[^cite_note-bias-206] When the United States announced it was rejoining the body in 2023, it also pledged to pay all past-due payments.[^cite_note-usa2023-66]

On 4 February 2025, the White House said to conduct a review of US membership in UNESCO.[^cite_note-207] On 22 July, [Donald Trump](4848272.md) decided that US will again withdraw from UNESCO, effective at the end of 2026.[^cite_note-208]

### Kurdish–Turkish conflict

On 25 May 2016, Turkish poet and human rights activist [Zülfü Livaneli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Z._Livaneli) resigned as [Turkey](11125639.md)'s only UNESCO goodwill ambassador. He highlighted the [human rights situation in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Turkey) and the destruction of the historical [Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sur,_Diyarbak%C4%B1r) district of [Diyarbakir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyarbak%C4%B1r), the largest city in Kurdish-majority southeast Turkey, during [fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish%E2%80%93Turkish_conflict_\(2015%E2%80%93present\)) between the Turkish army and Kurdish militants as the main reasons for his resignation. Livaneli said: "To pontificate on peace while remaining silent against such violations is a contradiction of the fundamental ideals of UNESCO."[^cite_note-209]

### Campaigns against illicit art trading

In 2020 UNESCO stated that the size of the illicit trade in cultural property amounted to 10 billion dollars a year. A report that same year by the [Rand Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Organization?action=edit\&redlink=1) suggested the actual market is "not likely to be larger than a few hundred million dollars each year". An expert cited by UNESCO as attributing the 10 billion figure denied it, saying he had "no idea" where the figure came from. Art dealers were particularly critical of the UNESCO figure because it amounted to 15% of the total world art market.[^cite_note-210]

In November 2020, part of a UNESCO advertising campaign intended to highlight international trafficking in looted artefacts had to be withdrawn after it falsely presented a series of museum-held artworks with known provenances as recently looted objects held in private collections. The adverts claimed that a head of Buddha in the Metropolitan Museum's collection since 1930 had been looted from a Kabul Museum in 2001 and then smuggled into the US art market, that a funerary monument from Palmyra that the Met had acquired in 1901 had been recently looted from the Palmyra Museum by Islamic State militants and then smuggled into the European antiquities market, and that an Ivory Coast mask with a provenance that indicates it was in the United States by 1954 was looted during armed clashes in 2010–2011. After complaints by the Met, the adverts were withdrawn.[^cite_note-211]

### Information processing tools

UNESCO develops, maintains, and disseminates, free of charge, two interrelated software packages for database management (CDS/ISIS \[not to be confused with UK police software package ISIS]) and data mining/statistical analysis (IDAMS).[^cite_note-213]

- CDS/ISIS – a generalized information storage and retrieval system. The Windows version may run on a single computer or in a local area network. The JavaISIS client/server components allow remote database management over the Internet and are available for Windows, Linux, and Macintosh. Furthermore, GenISIS allows users to produce HTML Web forms for CDS/ISIS database searching. The ISIS_DLL provides an API for developing CDS/ISIS based applications.

- OpenIDAMS – a software package for processing and analysing numerical data developed, maintained and disseminated by UNESCO. The original package was proprietary, but UNESCO has initiated a project to provide it as open source.

  [^cite_note-214]

- IDIS – a tool for direct data exchange between CDS/ISIS and IDAMS
