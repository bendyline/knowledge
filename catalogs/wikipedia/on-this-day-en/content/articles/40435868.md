**Subrahmanyam Jaishankar** (born 9 January 1955) is an Indian retired [diplomat](82433.md) who currently serves as the  [Minister of External Affairs of India](812043.md) since 2019. He is the longest-serving minister for external affairs to hold the portfolio exclusively.[^cite_note-Nehru-1][^cite_note-2] A member of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP), he is a [member of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(India\)) in the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md), the upper house of the [Indian parliament](410238.md). He previously served as the [foreign secretary of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(India\)) from 2015 to 2018. In January 2019, he was conferred with the [Padma Shri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Shri), India's fourth-highest civilian honour.

Jaishankar was born in 1955 in Delhi, in a [Tamil Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Hindu) family. He did his schooling in Delhi, and Bengaluru, and completed his [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in chemistry from [St. Stephen's College, Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_College,_Delhi). He obtained his master's degree in political science and an [M.Phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Phil.) and [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in international relations from [Jawaharlal Nehru University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_University).

Jaishankar joined the [Indian Foreign Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Foreign_Service) in 1977, and during a diplomatic career spanning over 38 years, served in different capacities in India and abroad. He served as an [ambassador to the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_India_to_the_Czech_Republic) (2001–2004), [high commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_commissioner) to Singapore (2007–2009), [ambassador to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_India_to_China) (2009–2013), and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_India_to_the_United_States) (2014–2015). From 2004 to 2007, he was the joint secretary (Americas) at the ministry of external affairs, and was one of the officials involved in the negotiation of the [India–United States Civil Nuclear Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_States_Civil_Nuclear_Agreement). After serving as the foreign secretary, he retired in 2018.

On retirement, Jaishankar served as president of global corporate affairs with the [Tata Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Group) briefly from 2018 to early 2019. In May 2019, he was sworn in as a [cabinet minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Minister_\(India\)) in the [second Modi ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Modi_ministry). He is first former foreign secretary of India to head the ministry of external affairs as cabinet minister. In June 2024, he was re-appointed to the post in the [third Modi ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Modi_ministry) after the BJP-led coalition won the [2024 Indian general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_general_elections). Jaishankar has crafted the assertive foreign policy with India-first.[^cite_note-Paul-3][^cite_note-S_Jaishankar-2018-4] He emphasises national interest, strategic autonomy, freedom of choice and multi-vector diplomacy.[^cite_note-5]

## Early life and education

Jaishankar was born on 9 January 1955[^cite_note-6] in Delhi, India, to a prominent Indian civil servant [Krishnaswamy Subrahmanyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnaswamy_Subrahmanyam) and Sulochana Subrahmanyam.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-Sirohi-2013-8] He was brought up in a [Tamil Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Hindus) family.[^cite_note-9] He has a sister, Sudha Subrahmanyam, and two brothers: historian [Sanjay Subrahmanyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjay_Subrahmanyam) and [IAS officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) S. Vijay Kumar,[^cite_note-10] former rural development secretary of India.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Jaishankar did his schooling at [The Air Force School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Air_Force_School), [Delhi](37756.md), and at the [Bangalore Military School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore_Military_School), [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore). He then did his bachelor's degree in chemistry from [St. Stephen's College, Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_College,_Delhi).[^cite_note-13] He has an MA in political science and an M.Phil. and PhD in international relations from [Jawaharlal Nehru University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_University,_Delhi) (JNU), where he specialised in nuclear diplomacy.[^cite_note-Dr-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Diplomatic career (1979–2018)

After joining the IFS in 1977, Jaishankar served as third secretary and second secretary in the Indian mission to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in Moscow from 1979 to 1981. He returned to New Delhi, where he worked as a special assistant to diplomat [Gopalaswami Parthasarathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopalaswami_Parthasarathy_\(diplomat\)) and as undersecretary in the Americas division of India's [ministry of external affairs](10302410.md), dealing with United States. He was part of the team that resolved the dispute over the supply of US nuclear fuel to the [Tarapur Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapur_Atomic_Power_Station) in India.[^cite_note-Sirohi-2013-8] From 1985 to 1988, he was the first secretary at the [Indian embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_India,_Washington,_D.C.) in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-Dr-14]

From 1988 to 1990, he served in Sri Lanka as first secretary and political adviser to the [Indian Peacekeeping Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Peacekeeping_Force) (IPKF).[^cite_note-Dr-14][^cite_note-17] From 1990 to 1993, he was counsellor (commercial) at the Indian mission in [Budapest](36787.md). Returning to New Delhi, he served as director (East Europe) in the [ministry of external affairs](10302410.md) and as press secretary and speechwriter for [Shankar Dayal Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shankar_Dayal_Sharma), then [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India).[^cite_note-18]

Jaishankar was then deputy chief of mission at the Indian embassy in Tokyo from 1996 to 2000.[^cite_note-Dr-14] This period saw a downturn in [India–Japan relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Japan_relations) following India's [Pokhran-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-II) nuclear tests as well as a recovery after a visit to India by then Japanese prime minister [Yoshiro Mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshir%C5%8D_Mori).[^cite_note-19] Jaishankar is reported to have helped introduce future Japanese prime minister [Shinzō Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinz%C5%8D_Abe) to [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh).[^cite_note-20] In 2000, he was appointed India's ambassador to the [Czech Republic](5321.md).

From 2004 to 2007, Jaishankar was joint secretary (Americas) at the [ministry of external affairs](10302410.md) in New Delhi. In this capacity, he was involved in negotiating the [India–United States Civil Nuclear Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_States_Civil_Nuclear_Agreement) and improving defence cooperation, including during relief operations following the [2004 Indian Ocean tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Jaishankar was also involved with the conclusion of the 2005 New Defense Framework[^cite_note-23] and the [Open Skies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Skies) Agreement,[^cite_note-24] and he was associated with the launch of the India–United States Energy Dialogue,[^cite_note-25] the India-United States Economic Dialogue, and the India-United States CEO's Forum.[^cite_note-26] In 2006–2007, Jaishankar led the Indian team during the negotiations on the [123 Agreement](7865995.md) with the United States.[^cite_note-27] He also represented the Indian government at the [Carnegie Endowment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace) International Non-proliferation Conference in June 2007.[^cite_note-28]

Jaishankar was one of those considered for the post of India's [foreign secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(India\)) in 2013 but lost out to [Sujatha Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sujatha_Singh), who became the third woman to serve in the post.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### High Commissioner to Singapore

From 2007 to 2009, Jaishankar served as India's [high commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_commissioner) to Singapore.[^cite_note-Dr-14] During his tenure, he helped implement the [Comprehensive Economic Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Economic_Cooperation_Agreement) (CECA) that expanded India's business presence in Singapore,[^cite_note-31] and oversaw a defence arrangement by which Singapore keeps some of its military equipment in India on a permanent basis.[^cite_note-32] Jaishankar also promoted the [Pravasi Bharatiya Divas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravasi_Bharatiya_Divas),[^cite_note-33] and IIMPact[^cite_note-34] in Singapore.

### Ambassador to China



Jaishankar (left) while serving as [Indian ambassador to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_India_to_China) in 2013, during prime minister [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh)'s visit to China

Jaishankar was India's longest-serving [ambassador to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_India_to_China), with a four-and-a-half-year term.[^cite_note-Jaishankar_moving_to_U-35] In Beijing, Jaishankar was involved in improving economic, trade and cultural relations between China and India, and in managing the [Sino-Indian border dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_border_dispute).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Jaishankar's tenure as India's ambassador to China coincided with several major developments in relations between the two countries.[^cite_note-Jaishankar_moving_to_U-35] His 2010 briefing to the [Indian cabinet committee on security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Security) regarding China's refusal to issue a visa to the head of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_army)'s [Northern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Command_\(India\)) led to a suspension of Indian defence co-operation with China, before the situation was resolved in April 2011.[^cite_note-38] Also in 2010, Jaishankar negotiated an end to the Chinese policy of issuing stapled visas to Indians from [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)).[^cite_note-39] In 2012, in response to [Chinese passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_passport) showing [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh) and [Aksai Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksai_Chin) as parts of China, he ordered visas issued to Chinese nationals showing those territories as parts of India.[^cite_note-40] In May 2013, he negotiated the end of a stand-off resulting from the [encampment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depsang_incident) by China's [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) on [Ladakh](303611.md)'s [Depsang Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depsang_Plains), threatening to cancel [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) [Li Keqiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Keqiang)'s scheduled visit to India if Chinese forces did not withdraw.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Jaishankar advocated deeper Indian cooperation with China as long as India's "core interests" were respected,[^cite_note-43] and argued for better [market access](3236318.md) for Indian businesses operating in China on the grounds that more balanced trade was necessary for the bilateral economic relationship to be sustainable.[^cite_note-44] He was also involved in improving people-to-people contacts between India and China, promoting events that showcased Indian culture in 30 Chinese cities.[^cite_note-45]

### Ambassador to the United States



Jaishankar with US secretary of state [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) in Washington D.C. in 2014

Jaishankar was appointed India's ambassador to the United States in September 2013. He took charge on 23 December 2013, succeeding [Nirupama Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirupama_Rao).[^cite_note-Jaishankar_moving_to_U-35][^cite_note-46] He arrived in the United States amid the [Devyani Khobragade incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devyani_Khobragade_incident), and was involved in negotiating the Indian diplomat's departure from the country.[^cite_note-47] On 29 January 2014, Jaishankar addressed the [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace), where he argued that "the grand strategy underwriting \[Indian-American] ties is fundamentally sound" but that ties suffered from a "problem of sentiment."[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

On 10 March 2014, he formally presented his credentials to US President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) at the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office).[^cite_note-50] Jaishankar was involved in the planning of the Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md)'s maiden visit to the country in September 2014, welcoming him upon his arrival and hosting a dinner in his honour for members of the [Indian-American community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Americans).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

### Foreign Secretary (2015–2018)

Jaishankar was appointed [foreign secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(India\)) of India on 29 January 2015. The announcement of his appointment was made following a 28 January 2015 meeting of the [appointments committee of the cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointments_Committee_of_the_Cabinet) chaired by the Modi.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] His tenure extension of one year saw a transformation in the working of the ministry of external affairs.[^cite_note-Paul-3][^cite_note-S_Jaishankar-2018-4] His appointment came three days before the date on which he would ordinarily have retired and meant the unprecedented termination of Sujatha Singh's two-year tenure as foreign secretary. Singh was offered a sinecure as compensation but preferred to resign from government service. Jaishankar is widely criticised by Nepalese analysts for being the "original planner of [2015 Nepal blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Nepal_blockade)."[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] His tenure expired in January 2018.[^cite_note-58]

### Corporate career (2018-19)

After his retirement from his position as the foreign secretary in 2018, Jaishankar joined the [Tata Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Group) as president of global corporate affairs.[^cite_note-59]

## Minister of External Affairs (2019–present)

Jaishankar as Minister of External Affairs of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_trips_made_by_S._Jaishankar_as_Minister_of_External_Affairs_of_India) and [Foreign policy of the Modi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Modi_government)



Jaishankar with US secretary of state [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) in New Delhi in 2021

On 31 May 2019, Jaishankar was appointed to the office of the [minister of external affairs](812043.md) in the [second Modi ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Modi_ministry).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] He was sworn in as [cabinet minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers) on 30 May 2019.[^cite_note-62] He is first former foreign secretary of India to head the ministry of external affairs as cabinet minister.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] On 5 July 2019, he was elected as [member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(India\)) from the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP) to the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md) from [Gujarat](53707.md).[^cite_note-65] He succeeded [Sushma Swaraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushma_Swaraj) who was the [external affairs minister](812043.md) in [Narendra Modi's first government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Modi_ministry).

In October 2020, Jaishankar and the [Indian minister of defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(India\)), [Rajnath Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajnath_Singh), met with [US secretary of state](32293.md), [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) and [US secretary of defence](43998.md), [Mark T. Esper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Esper) to sign the Basic Exchange and Cooperation Agreement on Geospatial Cooperation (BECA), which facilitates the sharing of sensitive information and intelligence—including access to highly-accurate nautical, aeronautical, topographical, and geospatial data—between United States and India. The agreement had been under discussion for over a decade, but previous concerns over information security impelled the [United Progressive Alliance (UPA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Progressive_Alliance) coalition government to block it.[^cite_note-66] In response to the dialogue, [Wang Wenbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Wenbin), the Chinese spokesperson for the [ministry of foreign affairs](7453497.md), criticised the move and advised Pompeo to "abandon his Cold War mentality, zero-sum mindset, and stop harping on the 'China threat'."[^cite_note-67] In February 2021, when the [Modi government](52507144.md) came under scrutiny at the 46th session of the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) (UNHRC) for human rights violations, Jaishankar asserted that nations should refrain from interfering in the internal affairs of other countries and should respect national sovereignty.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]



Jaishankar (second from left) with at the [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS) foreign ministers meet in New York on 22 September 2022

In November 2022, during a joint press conference along with [Russian foreign minister](1316703.md), [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md), Jaishankar praised [Russia](25391.md) as an "exceptionally steady" and "time-tested" partner of India and advocated a return to dialogue and peace between Russia and Ukraine, amid the ongoing [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-70] In June 2023, the [Associated Press](18935732.md) (AP) reported that Jaishankar had announced that India will remain committed in its stance on not inviting Ukraine to the [2023 G20 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_G20_New_Delhi_summit) in New Delhi, India.[^cite_note-71] Jaishankar firmly defended buying [Russian oil](10450931.md) even after sanctions by numerous countries during the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and he criticised Europe for "double standards."[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] During the [GLOBSEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLOBSEC) 2022 forum in Slovakia, responding to a question on India's official position on Russia's invasion of Ukraine, he said, "Europe has to grow out of the mindset that Europe's problems are the world's problems but the world's problems are not Europe's problems."[^cite_note-75] In response, German chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) said, "he has a point," before adding "it wouldn't be Europe's problem alone if the law of the strong were to assert itself in international relations."[^cite_note-76] During his visit to Germany for the [Munich Security Conference](1464620.md), Jaishankar emphasised that Europe must recognise that India cannot adopt the same perspective on Russia as Europe does.[^cite_note-77]



Jaishankar (left) with British prime minister [Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak) (right) at the [10 Downing Street](226294.md) in London in 2023

In August 2022, Jaishankar defended India's continued diplomatic engagement with [Myanmar's military junta](66589972.md).[^cite_note-78] In January 2023, Jaishankar called Pakistan "The epicenter of terrorism" in an interview with Austrian broadcaster [ORF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORF_\(broadcaster\)). He added, "I can use 'much harsher words than epicentre' for Pakistan for its role in promoting cross-border terrorism as he underlined that the world needs to be concerned about terrorism."[^cite_note-79] Later in August 2024, he stated, "The era of uninterrupted dialogue with Pakistan is over. What I do want to say is that we are not passive, and whether events take a positive or negative direction, either way we will react."[^cite_note-80] He condemned the [Hamas-led attack on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamas-led_attack_on_Israel) on 7 October 2023 as a terrorist act, but also talked about the plight of the Palestinian people, suggesting a "two-state solution" through "dialogue and negotiation."[^cite_note-81] Jaishankar said, "We have always supported a negotiated [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution), towards establishment of a sovereign, independent and viable [State of Palestine](241405.md) within secure and recognised borders, living side by side in peace with Israel."[^cite_note-82]

In May 2023, during [Bilawal Bhutto Zardari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilawal_Bhutto_Zardari)'s visit to India for the [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation](152422.md) (SCO) meeting, Jaishankar sharply criticised Pakistan's record on terrorism. He described Bilawal Bhutto Zardari, then the [Pakistani minister for foreign affairs](9957956.md), as a "promoter, justifier and spokesperson of a terrorism industry," asserting that this industry was the mainstay of Pakistan.\[^cite_note-[www.ndtv.com-83](http://www.ndtv.com-83/)][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-The_Indian_Express-2023-85] Jaishankar stated, "Victims of terrorism do not sit together with perpetrators of terrorism to discuss terrorism," and emphasised that India would continue to defend itself, counter acts of terrorism, and delegitimise Pakistan's support for such activities.\[^cite_note-[www.ndtv.com-83](http://www.ndtv.com-83/)][^cite_note-The_Indian_Express-2023-85][^cite_note-Hindustan_Times-2023-86] He further remarked that Pakistan's credibility on terrorism was "depleting even faster than its forex reserves," and dismissed any suggestion of normal engagement while cross-border terrorism persisted.\[^cite_note-[www.ndtv.com-83](http://www.ndtv.com-83/)][^cite_note-Hindustan_Times-2023-86]



Jaishankar with Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) and Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at the [16th BRICS summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_BRICS_summit) in [Kazan](202295.md), Russia on 22 October 2024

Jaishankar has been credited for maintaining stable relations between India and China after the [Doklam standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_China%E2%80%93India_border_standoff).[^cite_note-87] In October 2024, responding on the relationship with [China](5405.md), he said at [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace) in Washington, "The overall relationship with China had 'not been great' over the last few years because China had reneged on certain agreements it had with India about how to keep the border between the two countries tranquil."[^cite_note-88] In September 2024, on trade with China, he said at the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) Joint Ministerial Meeting in Riyadh, "We are not closed to business from China. There is nobody who can say I will not do business with China. I think the issue is which sectors do you do business in and on what terms you do business. It is far more complicated than a black and white binary answer."[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] During an official visit to [London](17867.md) in March 2025, his security was breached.[^cite_note-91]



Jaishankar (front right) with the foreign ministers of [Quad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilateral_Security_Dialogue) in January 2025

During the [2025 India–Pakistan conflict](79896622.md), Jaishankar played a pivotal diplomatic role. Jaishankar in an undated video said, "At the start of the operation, we had sent a message to Pakistan, saying, 'We are striking at terrorist infrastructure and we are not striking at the military.'" This statement was criticised by the opposition leaders such as [Rahul Gandhi](305010.md) who accused Jaishankar of tipping-off Pakistan.[^cite_note-92] The MEA later said that Jaishankar had informed Pakistan after India launched the military operation.[^cite_note-93] According to Jaishankar, Pakistan initiated fire following India's strikes and the exchange ceased at Pakistan's request.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] In July 2025, he rejected claims of US mediation in the ceasefire with Pakistan.[^cite_note-97] However, Trump reiterated multiple times that he mediated ceasefire between India and Pakistan.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

On 22 September 2026, Mr. Jaishankar led a meeting with French Foreign Minister [Jean-Noël Barrot](54388162.md) and UAE Foreign Minister [Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed Al Nahyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Abdullah_bin_Zayed_Al_Nahyan), to discuss joint projects in space, artificial intelligence, and culture, as well as political developments in West Asia.[^cite_note-100]
