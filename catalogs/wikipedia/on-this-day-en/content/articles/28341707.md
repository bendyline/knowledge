**Liège** ([/li.ˈɛʒ, li.ˈeɪʒ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [*lee-EZH, lee-AYZH*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] French: [\[ljɛʒ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d3/Fr-Li%C3%A8ge.ogg/Fr-Li%C3%A8ge.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d3/Fr-Li%C3%A8ge.ogg/Fr-Li%C3%A8ge.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Walloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloon_language): *Lîdje* [\[liːtʃ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Walloon); [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Luik* [\[lœyk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/24/Nl-Luik.ogg/Nl-Luik.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/24/Nl-Luik.ogg/Nl-Luik.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Lüttich* [\[ˈlʏtɪç\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/88/De-L%C3%BCttich.ogg/De-L%C3%BCttich.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/88/De-L%C3%BCttich.ogg/De-L%C3%BCttich.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_Belgium) and [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_in_Belgium) in the Belgian [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communities,_regions,_and_language_areas_of_Belgium) of [Wallonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallonia), and the capital of the [eponymous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge_Province). The city is situated in the valley of the [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse), in the east of [Belgium](3343.md), not far from borders with the [Netherlands](21148.md) ([Maastricht](20125.md) is about 33 km (20.5 mi) to the north) and [Germany](11867.md) ([Aachen](1520.md) is about 53 km (32.9 mi) north-east). In Liège, the Meuse meets the river [Ourthe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ourthe). The city is part of the *[sillon industriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sillon_industriel)*, the former industrial backbone of Wallonia. It is still the principal economic and cultural centre of the region.

The [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) consists of the following *[sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deelgemeente)* (sub-municipalities): Liège proper, [Angleur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angleur), [Bressoux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bressoux), [Chênée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%AAn%C3%A9e), [Glain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glain), [Grivegnée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grivegn%C3%A9e), [Jupille-sur-Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupille-sur-Meuse), [Rocourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocourt,_Li%C3%A8ge), and [Wandre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandre). In January 2026, Liège had 198,102 inhabitants.[^cite_note-5] The [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), including the outer commuter zone, covers an area of 1,879 km<sup>2</sup> (725 sq mi) and had a total population of 749,110 on 1 January 2008.[^cite_note-BelMun2008-6][^cite_note-BelMetr-7] This includes a total of 52 municipalities, such as [Herstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herstal) and [Seraing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seraing). Liège ranks as the third most populous urban area in Belgium, after [Brussels](3708.md) and [Antwerp](32149462.md), and the fourth most populous municipality after Antwerp, [Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent) and [Charleroi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleroi).[^cite_note-BelMetr-7] The city is part of the [Euregio Meuse-Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euregio_Meuse-Rhine).

## Etymology

The name is of Germanic origin and is reconstructible as \**liudik-*, from the Germanic word \**liudiz* "people", which is found in for example Dutch *lui*(*den*), *lieden*, Polish *ludzie*, Czech *lidé*, German *Leute*, [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) *lēod* (English *[lede](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/lede)*), Icelandic *lýður* ("people"), Latvian *ļaudis* ("people"), Lithuanian *liaudis* ("people"). It is found in Ukrainian as *liudy* ("people"), in Russian as люди, romanised: *lyudi* ("people"), in Latin as *Leodicum* or *Leodium*, and in Middle Dutch as *ludic* or *ludeke*.[^cite_note-8]

Until 17 September 1946, the city's name was written *Liége*, with the [acute accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_accent) instead of a [grave accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grave_accent).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] This change is in conformity with changes in spelling other words from *-ége* to *-ège* (e. g. *collège*), which was made in the seventh edition of the [Dictionnaire de l'Académie française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_de_l'Acad%C3%A9mie_fran%C3%A7aise) (1878) to reflect a change in the pronunciation from the closed vowel \[e] to the open vowel \[ɛ]. This official change did not extend to the demonym, which persists as Liégeois/Liégeoise.

In French, *Liège* is associated with the epithet *la cité ardente* ("the fervent city"). This term, which emerged around 1905, originally referred to the city's history of rebellions against [Burgundian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burgundy) rule, but was appropriated to refer to its economic dynamism during the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).[^cite_note-12]

### Early Middle Ages

Although settlements already existed in [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) times, the first references to Liège are from 558, when it was known as Vicus Leudicus. Around 705, [Saint Lambert of Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambert_of_Maastricht) is credited with completing the [Christianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization) of the region, indicating that up to the early 8th century the religious practices of antiquity had survived in some form. [Christian conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity) may still not have been quite universal, since Lambert was murdered in Liège and thereafter regarded as a [martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_martyr) for his faith. To enshrine St. Lambert's [relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic), his successor, [Hubertus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubertus) (later to become St. Hubert), built a [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica) near the bishop's residence which became the true nucleus of the city.

A few centuries later, the city became the capital of a [prince-bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Li%C3%A8ge), which lasted from 985 till 1794. The first [prince-bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-bishop), [Notger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notker_of_Li%C3%A8ge), transformed the city into a major intellectual and ecclesiastical centre, which maintained its cultural importance during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). Pope [Clement VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_VI) recruited several musicians from Liège to perform in the Papal court at [Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon), thereby sanctioning the practice of [polyphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphony) in the religious realm. The city was renowned for its many churches, the oldest of which, St Martin's, dates from 682. Although nominally part of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), in practice it possessed a large degree of independence.

### Late medieval and early modern periods



Liège in 1650

See also: [Prince-Bishopric of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Li%C3%A8ge)

The strategic position of Liège has made it a frequent target of armies and insurgencies over the centuries. It was fortified early on with a castle on the steep hill that overlooks the city's western side. During this medieval period, three women from the Liège region made significant contributions to Christian spirituality: Elizabeth of [Spalbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spalbeek), [Christina the Astonishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_the_Astonishing), and [Marie of Oignies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_of_Oignies).[^cite_note-13]

In 1345, the citizens of Liège rebelled against Prince-Bishop [Engelbert III de la Marck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelbert_III_de_la_Marck), their ruler at the time, and defeated him in battle near the city. Shortly after, a unique political system formed in Liège, whereby the city's 32 [guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guild) shared sole political control of the municipal government. Each person on the register of each guild was eligible to participate, and each guild's voice was equal, making it the most democratic system that the [Low Countries](51402.md) had ever known. The system spread to [Utrecht](50333.md), and left a democratic spirit in Liège that survived the Middle Ages.[^cite_note-14]

At the end of the [Liège Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge_Wars), a rebellion took place against rule from [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Burgundy). In 1468 Duke [Charles the Bold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_the_Bold) of Burgundy, witnessed by King [Louis XI of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XI_of_France), captured and largely destroyed the city after a bitter siege which was ended with a successful surprise attack. The rebellion figures prominently in the plot of [Sir Walter Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Scott)'s 1823 novel *[Quentin Durward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Durward)*.

The [Prince-Bishopric of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Li%C3%A8ge) was technically part of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) which, after 1477, came under the rule of the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg). The reign of prince-bishop [Érard de La Marck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89rard_de_La_Marck) (1506–1538) coincides with the dawn of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).

During the [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation), the diocese of Liège was split and progressively lost its role as a regional power. By the 17th century, the bishopric of Liège became a virtual [Secundogeniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secundogeniture) of the Bavarian royal house of [Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittelsbach), with second sons of the Bavarian monarch ruling as prince-bishop. Beginning with the ascension of [Ernest of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_of_Bavaria) in 1581, Bavarian princes ruled over [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnster), and other bishoprics in the northwest of the Holy Roman Empire in addition to Liège. [Ferdinand of Bavaria (bishop)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_of_Bavaria_\(bishop\)) ruled from 1612 to 1650, and [Maximilian Henry of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_Henry_of_Bavaria) ruled from 1650 to 1688.

In 1636, during the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), the city was besieged by Imperial forces under [Johann von Werth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_von_Werth) from April to July. The army, mainly consisting of mercenaries, extensively and viciously plundered the surrounding bishopric during the siege.[^cite_note-15]

### 18th century to World War I



Liège in 1627

An Anglo-Dutch army under the [Duke of Marlborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Marlborough) and [Menno van Coehoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menno_van_Coehoorn) [captured the city in 1702](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Li%C3%A8ge_\(1702\)) from the Bavarian prince-bishop and his French allies in 1702 during the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession).

In the middle of the eighteenth century the ideas of the French *[Encyclopédistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A9distes)* began to gain popularity in the region. Bishop [François-Charles de Velbrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois-Charles_de_Velbr%C3%BCck) (1772–84), encouraged their propagation, thus prepared the way for the [Liège Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge_Revolution) which started in the episcopal city on 18 August 1789 and led to the creation of the [Republic of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Li%C3%A8ge) before it was invaded by counter-revolutionary forces of the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) in 1791.

In the course of the [1794 campaigns of the French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars:_Campaigns_of_1794), the [French Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Army) took the city and imposed strongly anticlerical regime, destroying [St. Lambert's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lambert's_Cathedral,_Li%C3%A8ge). The overthrow of the [Prince-Bishopric of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Li%C3%A8ge) was confirmed in 1801 by the [Concordat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordat_of_1801) co-signed by [Napoléon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napol%C3%A9on_Bonaparte) and [Pope Pius VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII). France lost the city in 1815 when the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) awarded it to the [United Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands). Dutch rule lasted only until 1830, when the [Belgian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Revolution) led to the establishment of an independent, Catholic and neutral Belgium which incorporated Liège. After this, Liège developed rapidly into a major industrial city which became one of continental Europe's first large-scale steel making centres. The [Walloon Jacquerie of 1886](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloon_Jacquerie_of_1886) saw a large-scale working class revolt.[^cite_note-16] No less than 6,000 regular troops were called into the city to quell the unrest,[^cite_note-17] while strike spread through the whole [sillon industriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sillon_industriel).



Quai de la Goffe in 1936

[Liège's fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortified_Position_of_Li%C3%A8ge) were redesigned by [Henri Alexis Brialmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Alexis_Brialmont) in the 1880s and a [chain of twelve forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortified_Position_of_Li%C3%A8ge) was constructed around the city to provide [defence in depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_in_depth). This presented a major obstacle to the [Imperial German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Army) in 1914, whose [Schlieffen Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlieffen_Plan) relied on being able to quickly pass through the Meuse valley and the [Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes) en route to France. The [German invasion of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Belgium_\(1914\)) on 5 August 1914 soon reached Liège, which was defended by 30,000 troops under General [Gérard Leman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Leman) in the [Battle of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Li%C3%A8ge). The forts initially held off General [Alexander von Kluck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Kluck)'s [German First Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Army_\(German_Empire\)) of about 100,000 men but were pulverised into submission by a five-day bombardment by heavy artillery, including thirty-two 21 cm mortars and two German 42 cm [Big Bertha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bertha_\(Howitzer\)) [howitzers](14392.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Due to faulty planning of the protection of the underground defence tunnels beneath the main citadel, one direct artillery hit caused a huge explosion, which eventually led to the surrender of the Belgian forces. The Belgian resistance was shorter than had been intended, but the twelve days of delay caused by the siege nonetheless contributed to the eventual failure of the German invasion of France. The city was subsequently occupied by the Germans until the end of the war. Liège received the [Légion d'Honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9gion_d'Honneur) for its resistance in 1914.

As part of Chancellor [Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theobald_von_Bethmann_Hollweg)'s [Septemberprogramm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septemberprogramm), Berlin planned to annexe Liege under the name Lüttich to the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in any post-war peace agreement.[^cite_note-20]

### World War II to the present



Inauguration of the statue of Charlemagne, 26 July 1868

The [Germans returned in 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Belgium_\(1940\)), this time taking the forts in only three days. Most [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Belgium) were saved, with the help of the sympathetic population, as many Jewish children and refugees were hidden in the numerous monasteries. [Liege was liberated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Belgium) by the [British Second Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Army_\(United_Kingdom\)) in September 1944.[^cite_note-21]

After the war ended, the [Royal Question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Question) came to the fore, since many saw King [Leopold III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_III_of_Belgium) as collaborating with the Germans during the war. In July 1950, [André Renard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Renard), leader of the [Liègian FGTB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Federation_of_Belgian_Labour) launched the [General strike against Leopold III of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_strike_against_Leopold_III_of_Belgium) and "seized control over the city of Liège".[^cite_note-22] The strike ultimately led to Leopold's abdication.



1944: Soldiers of the US Army 4185th Quartermaster Service Company loading a [Red Ball Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ball_Express) truck at Liège.

Liège began to suffer from a relative decline of its industry, particularly the coal industry, and later the steel industry, producing high levels of unemployment and stoking social tension. During the [1960–1961 Winter General Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960%E2%80%931961_Winter_General_Strike), disgruntled workers went on a rampage and severely damaged the central railway station Guillemins. The unrest was so intense that "army troops had to wade through [caltrops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltrop), trees, concrete blocks, car and crane wrecks to advance. Streets were dug up. Liège saw the worst fighting on 6 January 1961. In all, 75 people were injured during seven hours of street battles."[^cite_note-23]

On 6 December 1985, the city's courthouse was heavily damaged and one person was killed in a [bomb attack by a lawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Li%C3%A8ge_bombing).

Liège is also known as a traditionally [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) city. In 1991, powerful Socialist [André Cools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Cools), a former Deputy Prime Minister, was gunned down in front of his girlfriend's apartment. Many suspected that the assassination was related to a corruption scandal which swept the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(francophone_Belgium\)), and the [Belgian Federal Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Government_of_Belgium) in general, after Cools' death. Two men were sentenced to twenty years in jail in 2004, for involvement in Cools' murder.

Liège has shown some signs of economic recovery in recent years with the opening up of borders within the [European Union](9317.md), surging steel prices, and improved administration. Several new shopping centres have been built, and numerous repairs carried out.

On 13 December 2011, there was a [grenade and gun attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Li%C3%A8ge_attack) at [Place Saint-Lambert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_Saint-Lambert). An attacker, later identified as Nordine Amrani, aged 33, armed with grenades and an assault rifle, attacked people waiting at a bus stop. There were six fatalities, including the attacker (who shot himself), and 123 people were injured.[^cite_note-24]

On 29 May 2018, two female police officers and one civilian—a 22-year-old man—were [shot dead by a gunman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Li%C3%A8ge_attack) near a café on Boulevard d'Avroy in central Liège. The attacker then began firing at the officers in an attempt to escape, injuring a number of them "around their legs", before he was shot dead. Belgian broadcaster [RTBF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTBF) said the gunman was temporarily released from prison on 28 May where he had been serving time on drug offences. The incident is currently being treated as terrorism.[^cite_note-25]

## Climate

In spite of its inland position Liège has a [maritime climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) influenced by the mildening sea winds originating from the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream), travelling over Belgium's interior. As a result, Liège has very mild winters for its latitude and inland position, especially compared to areas in the [Russian Far East](173135.md) and the fellow Francophone province of [Quebec](7954867.md). Summers are also moderated by the maritime air, with average temperatures being similar to areas as far north as in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia). Being inland though, Liège has a relatively low winter [seasonal lag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_lag).

## Demographics

On 1 January 2013, the municipality of Liège had a total population of 197,013. The metropolitan area has about 750,000 inhabitants. Its inhabitants are predominantly [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language)-speaking, with German and Dutch-speaking minorities. Akin to the rest of Belgium, the population of minorities has grown significantly since the 1990s.[^cite_note-28] The city has become the home to large numbers of [Algerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerians), [Moroccan](5639315.md), [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), and [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_diaspora) immigrants. Liège also houses a significant [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africans) community.[^cite_note-29]

The city is a major educational hub in Belgium. There are 42,000 pupils attending more than 24 schools. The [University of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Li%C3%A8ge), founded in 1817, has 20,000 students.

Traditional Liègian puppets

The *Le Quinze Août* celebration takes place annually on 15 August in Outremeuse and celebrates the [Virgin Mary](73513.md). It is one of the biggest folkloric displays in the city, with a religious procession, a flea market, dances, concerts, and a series of popular games. Nowadays these celebrations start a few days earlier and last until the 16th. Some citizens open their doors to party goers, and serve "peket", the traditional local alcohol. This tradition is linked to the important folkloric character *Tchantchès* ([Walloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloon_language) for *François*), a hard-headed but resourceful [Walloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloons) boy who lived during [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne)'s times. *Tchantchès* is remembered with a statue, a museum, and a number of puppets found all over the city. The *Le Quinze Août* celebrations conclude on 16 August with the procession and [burial of Matî l'Ohê](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial_of_Mat%C3%AE_l'Oh%C3%AA), the ceremonial ham bone.

Liège hosts one of the oldest and biggest [Christmas Markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Market) in Belgium, and the oldest [kermesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermesse_\(festival\)), the [Foire de Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foire_de_Li%C3%A8ge) held each year from 28 October.

## Culture



Liège, the Sunday "Batte" market

The city is well known for its very crowded folk festivals. The 15 August festival ("Le 15 août") may be the best known. The population gathers in a quarter named *Outre-Meuse* with plenty of tiny pedestrian streets and old yards. Many people come to see the procession but also to drink alcohol (mostly [peket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peket)) and beer, eat cooked pears, [boûkètes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo%C3%BBk%C3%A8te) or sausages or simply enjoy the atmosphere until the early hours.[^cite_note-34] The [Saint Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas) festival around 6 December is organized by and for the students of the university; for a few days before the event, students (wearing very dirty lab-coats) beg for money, mostly for drinking.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Liège is renowned for its nightlife.[^cite_note-38] Within the pedestrian zone behind the Opera House, there is a square city block known locally as *Le Carré* (the Square) with many lively pubs which are reputed to remain open until the last customer leaves (typically around 6 am). Another active area is the Place du Marché.

The "Batte" market is where most locals visit on Sundays. The outdoor market goes along the river Meuse and also attracts many visitors to Liège. The market typically runs from early morning to 2 o'clock in the afternoon every Sunday year long. Produce, clothing, and snack vendors are the main concentration of the market.

Liège is home to the Opéra Royal de Wallonie (English: Royal Opera of Wallonia) and the Orchestre Philharmonique Royal de Liège (OPRL) (English: Liège Royal Philharmonic Orchestra).

The city annually hosts a significant electro-rock festival *[Les Ardentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Ardentes)* and jazz festival *Jazz à Liège*.

Liège has active alternative cinemas, Le Churchill, Le Parc and Le Sauvenière. There are also two mainstream cinemas, the [Kinepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinepolis) multiplexes.

Liège also has a particular [Walloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloon_language) dialect, sometimes said to be one of Belgium's most distinctive. There is a large Italian community, and Italian can be heard in many places.

## Sports



[Stade Maurice Dufrasne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Maurice_Dufrasne), home to football club [Standard Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Li%C3%A8ge).

The city has a number of football teams, most notably [Standard Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Li%C3%A8ge), which has won several championships and which was previously owned by [Roland Duchâtelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Duch%C3%A2telet); and [R.F.C. de Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.F.C._de_Li%C3%A8ge), one of the oldest football clubs in Belgium, known for having refused to release player [Jean-Marc Bosman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marc_Bosman), a case which led to the [Bosman ruling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosman_ruling).

In spring, Liège hosts the start and finish of the annual [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge) [cycling race](1755460.md), one of the [spring classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_cycle_races) and the oldest of the [five monuments of cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_cycle_races#The_%22Monuments%22). The race starts in the centre of Liège, before heading south to [Bastogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastogne) and returning north to finish in the industrial suburb of [Ans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ans,_Belgium). Traveling through the hilly [Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes), it is one of the longest and most arduous races of the season.[^cite_note-BBC-39]

Liège is the only city that has hosted stages of all three cycling [Grand Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_\(cycling\)). It staged the start of the [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Giro_d'Italia) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Giro_d'Italia) [Giro d'Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giro_d'Italia); as well as the *Grand Départ* of the [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Tour_de_France), [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Tour_de_France),[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Tour_de_France)[^cite_note-43] [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France) making it the first city outside France to host the *Grand Départ* twice or more times.[^cite_note-rdcc-route-44] In [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Vuelta_a_Espa%C3%B1a), the [Vuelta a España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuelta_a_Espa%C3%B1a) visited Liège after four stages in the Netherlands, making Liège the first city that has hosted stages of all three cycling Grand Tours.[^cite_note-45]

Liège is also home to boxer [Ermano Fegatilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ermano_Fegatilli), the current [European Boxing Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Boxing_Union) [Super Featherweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Featherweight) champion.[^cite_note-46]

Liège at night, photography taken from the [ISS](15043.md) in December 2012

[^cite_note-47]

Liège is the most important city of the Walloon region from an economic perspective. In the past, Liège was one of the most important industrial centres in Europe, particularly in steel-making. Starting in 1817, [John Cockerill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cockerill_\(industrialist\)) extensively developed the iron and steel industry. The industrial complex of [Seraing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seraing) was the largest in the world. It once boasted numerous [blast furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_furnace) and mills. Liège has also been an important centre for [gunsmithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunsmithing) since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) and the [arms industry](608296.md) is still strong today, with the headquarters of [FN Herstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_Herstal) and [CMI Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockerill_Maintenance_&_Ing%C3%A9nierie) being located in Liège.

The economy of the region is now diversified; the most important centres are: Mechanical industries ([Aircraft engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_engine) and [Spacecraft propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft_propulsion)), space technology, [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), [biotechnology](4502.md) and the production of [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water), [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer), and [chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate). Liège has an important group of headquarters dedicated to high-technology, such as [Techspace Aero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safran), which manufactures pieces for the [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) or the rocket [Ariane 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariane_5). Other stand-out sectors include [Amós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%B3s?action=edit\&redlink=1) which manufactures optical components for [telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope) and [Drytec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drytec?action=edit\&redlink=1), which produces compressed air dryers. Liège also has many other electronic companies such as [SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP_AG), [EVS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EVS_Broadcast_Equipment), [Gillam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillam_\(company\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [AnB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.N.B._Rimex?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Balteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balteau?action=edit\&redlink=1), [IP Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Trade?action=edit\&redlink=1). Other prominent businesses are the global leader in light armament [FN Herstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_Herstal), the [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer) company [Jupiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiler), the chocolate company [Galler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galler), and the water and soda companies [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_\(mineral_water\)) and [Chaudfontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudfontaine). A science park southeast of the city, near the University of Liège campus, houses [spin-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_spin-off) and high technology businesses.

### 1812 mine accident

In 1812, there were three coal pits (*Bure*) in close proximity just outside the city gates: Bure Triquenotte, Bure de Beaujone and Bure Mamonster. The first two shafts were joined underground, but the last one was a separate colliery. The shafts were 120 fathoms (720 ft; 220 m) deep. Water was led to a sump (*serrement*) from which it could be pumped to the surface. At 11\:00 on 28 February 1812 the sump in the Beaujone mine failed and flooded the entire colliery. Of the 127 men down the mine at the time 35 escaped by the main shaft, but 74 were trapped. \[These numbers are taken from the report, the 18 miner discrepancy is unexplained.] The trapped men attempted to dig a passageway into Mamonster. After 23 feet (7.0 m) there was a [firedamp](505455.md) explosion and they realised that they had penetrated some old workings belonging to an abandoned mine, Martin Wery. The overseer, Monsieur Goffin, led the men to the point in Martin Wery which he judged closest to Mamonster and they commence to dig. By the second day they had run out of candles and dug the remainder of a 36 feet (11 m) gallery in darkness.

On the surface the only possible rescue was held to be via Mamonster. A heading was driven towards Beaujone with all possible speed, including blasting. The trapped miners heard the rescuers and vice versa. Five days after the accident communication was possible and the rescuers worked in darkness to avoid the risk of a firedamp explosion. By 7pm that evening an opening was made, 511 feet (156 m) of tunnel had been dug by hand in five days. All of the 74 miners in Goffin's part survived and were brought to the surface.[^cite_note-48]

## Transport

### Air



Passenger terminal of [Liège Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge_Airport)

Liège is served by [Liège Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge_Airport), located in Bierset, a few kilometres west of the city. It is the principal axis for the delivery of freight and in 2011 was the world's 33rd busiest cargo airport.[^cite_note-49] Passenger services are very few. It is owned by the [Walloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallonia) government along with some private investors.

### Maritime

The [Port of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Li%C3%A8ge), located on the river [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse), is the 3rd largest [river port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_port) in Europe. Liège also has direct links to [Antwerp](32149462.md) through the [Albert Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Canal) and to Rotterdam via the river Meuse. It stretches over a distance of 26 kilometres and comprises 32 port areas and covers 3.7 square kilometres.

### Rail



[Liège-Guillemins railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge-Guillemins_railway_station)

Liège is served by many direct rail links with the rest of Western Europe. Its three principal stations are [Liège-Guillemins railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge-Guillemins_railway_station), Liège-Carré, and Liège-Saint-Lambert. The [InterCity Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity_Express) and [Thalys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalys) call at Liège-Guillemins, providing direct connections to [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) and [Frankfurt](10992.md) and [Paris-Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Nord) respectively.

Liège was once home to a [network of trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Li%C3%A8ge). However, they were removed by 1967 in favour of the construction of a new metro system. A prototype of the metro was built and a tunnel was dug underneath the city, but the metro was never built. Later, it was decided to build a modern tramway. By 2019, construction of the line was underway, but would experience various setbacks and delays.[^cite_note-LeSoir-2023-10-10-50] The new tramway opened on 28 April 2025.[^cite_note-Avenir-2025-04-23-51]

See article [Trams in Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Li%C3%A8ge).

### Road

Liège sits at the crossroads of a number of highways including the [European route E25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E25), the [European Route E42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E42), the [European Route E40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E40) and the [European Route E313](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E313).
