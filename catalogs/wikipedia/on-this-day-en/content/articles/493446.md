**Kampong Thom** ([Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_language): កំពង់ធំ, [UNGEGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNGEGN): *Kâmpóng Thum* [\[kɑmpɔŋ tʰom\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Khmer); lit. 'Great Port') is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Cambodia) (*khaet*) of [Cambodia](334751.md). It borders the provinces of [Siem Reap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siem_Reap_province) to the northwest, [Preah Vihear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preah_Vihear_province) to the north, [Stung Treng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stung_Treng_province) to the northeast, [Kratie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krati%C3%A9_province) to the east, [Kampong Cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampong_Cham_province) and [Kampong Chhnang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampong_Chhnang_province) to the south, and the [Tonle Sap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonle_Sap) to the west.

The provincial capital is [Stung Saen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stung_Saen), a town of approximately 30,000 people on the banks of the [Stung Sen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stung_Sen_River).

Kampong Thom is Cambodia's second largest province by area. There are a number of significant [Angkorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkorian) sites in the area, including Prasat [Sambor Prei Kuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambor_Prei_Kuk) and Prasat [Andet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andet?action=edit\&redlink=1) temples. As one of the nine provinces bordering Tonle Sap Lake, Kampong Thom is part of the [Tonlé Sap Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonl%C3%A9_Sap_Biosphere_Reserve).[^cite_note-2]

## Etymology

*Kampong Thom* in [Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_language) means 'great port' or 'great harbor'. *Kampong* in Khmer translates as 'port' or 'harbor'. The word *Kampong* is also shared in other Asian languages, for example in the [Malay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language), meaning 'village'. *Thom* in the Khmer language means 'big', 'grand', or 'large'.

## History

The previous name of the province was *Kampong Pous Thom* ('Port', 'City of the Great Snakes'). According to local legend, at a lakeside dock near the Sen River, a pair of large snakes inhabited a nearby cave. On every Buddhist holiday, the snakes would make appearances to the people nearby who then began to refer to the area as *Kampong Pous Thom*. Eventually the snakes disappeared and the name was shortened to *Kampong Thom*. During the [Colonial Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Cambodia) period, the French divided Cambodian territory into provinces and named most of them according to the local popular names for the respective areas.

Kampong Thom was a powerful capital in Southeast Asia during the [Funan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Funan) period. Prasat Sambor Prei Kuk, dating from the [Chenla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenla) era, is in Kampong Thom province.[^cite_note-3]

## Economy

Much of Kampong Thom is on the floodplain of the [Tonlé Sap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonl%C3%A9_Sap) lake. In 2003–2004, it was a significant producer of wild fish (18,800 tons) and the fourth largest producer of fish through [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture) in Cambodia (1,800 tons). Most of the fish-raising is done by home production, with a growing segment devoted to rice field aquaculture.[^cite_note-9]

Kampong Thom is also one of the largest producers of [cashew nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashew_nuts) in Cambodia, with 6,371 hectares under production in 2003–2004.[^cite_note-10]
