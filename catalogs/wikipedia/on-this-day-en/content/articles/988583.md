**Chitral District** ([Khowar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khowar_language): ݯھیترارو ضلع; [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): ضلع چترال) was a [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) in the [Malakand Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakand_Division) of the [Pakistani province](446017.md) of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) from 14 August 1947 to 2018. It was the northernmost[^cite_note-District_Government_Chitral-1] and the largest district of the province, covering an area of 14,850 km<sup>2</sup>, before the district was split in two forming the new districts of [Upper Chitral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Chitral_District) and [Lower Chitral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Chitral_District).[^cite_note-Dawn-2]

It shared district borders with [Swat](1236283.md) and [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Dir_District) to the south, a provincial border with [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md) to the east and the [Durand Line](203570.md) as international border with [Afghanistan](737.md) to the north and west.[^cite_note-3] Afghanistan's narrow strip of [Wakhan Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakhan_Corridor) separated Chitral from [Tajikistan](30108.md) in the north.[^cite_note-4]

## History

Chitral shared much of its history and culture with the neighbouring [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) territories of Gilgit-Baltistan, a region sometimes called "Peristan" because of the common belief in fairies (*peri*) inhabiting the high mountains.

The entire region that formed the Chitral District was an independent monarchical state until 1895, when the British negotiated a treaty with its hereditary ruler, the Mehtar, under which Chitral became a semi-autonomous [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) within the Indian Empire. The [princely state of Chitral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitral_\(princely_state\)) retained this status even after its accession to Pakistan in 1947, finally being made an administrative district of Pakistan on 14 August 1947, disestablishing the Princely State.[^cite_note-5]

## Topography and access



[Qaqlasht Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaqlasht_Meadows) above [Buni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buni)

Chitral was counted amongst the highest regions of the world, sweeping from 1,094 metres at [Arandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arandu,_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) to 7,726 metres at Tirichmir and packing over 40 peaks more than 6,100 metres in height. The terrain of Chitral was very mountainous, and [Tirich Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirich_Mir) (25,289 feet), the highest peak of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush), rises in the north of the former district.[^cite_note-6] Around 4.8 percent of the land was covered by forest, and 76 percent was mountains and glaciers.

Chitral was connected to the rest of Pakistan by two major road routes, the [Lowari Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowari_Pass) ( elevation. 10,23 ft.) from [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir_\(Pakistan\)) and [Shandur Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandur_Top) (elevation 12,200 ft.) from Gilgit. Both routes used to be closed in winter, but circa 2017 the highway [Lowari Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowari_Tunnel) under the Lowari Pass opened to vehicular traffic for at least ten hours per day.[^cite_note-Dawn_LowariTunnelChitralDevelopment-7] A number of other high passes, including [Darkot Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkot_Pass), [Thoi Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoi_Pass), and [Zagaran Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagaran_Pass), provided access on foot to Chitral from [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md).

- Darkot Pass, on the border between Chitral and [Gupis-Yasin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupis-Yasin_District)

## Demographics

The district had a population of about 414,000.[^cite_note-District_Government_Chitral-1] The general population was mainly made up of [Kho people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kho_people), who speak [Khowar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khowar_language), which is also spoken in parts of [Yasin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasin_Valley), [Gilgit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit_District), [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir_\(region\)), and [Swat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swat_\(Pakistan\)). Chitral was also home to the [Kalash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalash_people) tribe, who live in [Bumburet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumburet) and two other remote valleys southwest of Chitral town. A few thousand [Nuristani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristanis) were also known to live in Chitral.

The main language of the region is [Khowar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khowar). There are also smaller communities of speakers of [Dameli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dameli_language), [Gawar-Bati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gawar-Bati_language), [Gujari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujari_language), [Kalasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitral_Kalasha_language), [Kyrgyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_language), [Katë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kat%C3%AB_language), [Madaklashti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madaklashti_dialect), [Palula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palula_language), [Sarikoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarikoli_language), [Shughni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shughni_language), [Wakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakhi_language), and [Yidgha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yidgha_language).[^cite_note-8] [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) has official status.

## Chitral town

The town of [Chitral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitral) in the district is served as capital. It is situated on the west bank of the [Chitral River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitral_River) (also known as the Kunar River) at the foot of [Tirich Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirich_Mir), which at 7,708 m (25,289 ft) is the highest peak of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush). Formerly it served as the capital of the [princely state of Chitral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitral_\(princely_state\)) and now it has this role for the succeeding Lower Chitral district.
