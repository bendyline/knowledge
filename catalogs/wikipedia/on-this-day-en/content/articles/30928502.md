# Tongan nobles

There are 33 traditional [noble titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_titles) in the modern Kingdom of [Tonga](30158.md). They are all estate holders. Twenty titles were established by [Siaosi Tupou I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Tupou_I) with the [Constitution of 1875](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Tonga). In 1880, he added 11 more. [Tupou II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Tupou_II) created the titles Lasike in 1894 and Veikune in 1903. [Sālote Tupou III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C4%81lote_Tupou_III) created the title of Tupoutoʻa in 1921. In the beginning, it was forbidden for a noble to have more than one title. Later, this was made possible.

Some of the great chiefs who missed out on a noble's title (in 1910) were among others: [ʻAlipate Mafileʻo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BBAlipate_Mafile%CA%BBo?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Kolomotuʻa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomotu%CA%BBa), [SA Sipu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA_Sipu?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Kolomotuʻa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomotu%CA%BBa), [Iki Lolohea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iki_Tupou_Fulivai?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Haʻapai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Ha%CA%BBapai) (but later inherited the [Fulivai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Fulivai?action=edit\&redlink=1)), [Tēvita Tapueluelu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%93vita_Tapueluelu?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Vavaʻu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Vava%CA%BBu), [SF Tafolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SF_Tafolo?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tēvita Ula Afuhaʻamango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%93vita_Ula_Afuha%CA%BBamango?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Vavaʻu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Vava%CA%BBu), [Siosiua Niutupuʻivaha Kaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siosiua_Niutupu%CA%BBivaha_Kaho?action=edit\&redlink=1) (but later inherited the [Tuʻivakanō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Tu%CA%BBivakan%C5%8D?action=edit\&redlink=1)).

These unacknowledged chiefs were still lords in the traditional sense. However, their influence slowly decreased with each passing generation. Queen [Sālote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C4%81lote_Tupou_III) acknowledged this in some of her public speeches by paying respect to the chiefs then a separate respect to the Nobles of the Crown as: "Highly respectful for the Chiefs is also Highly respectful for the nobles in this land".

In the 21st century, King [George Tupou V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Tupou_V) created eight new noble titles but with no hereditary lands. The title is to remain with the holder for the rest of their life and is considered equivalent to the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s practice of appointing [life peers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer).
