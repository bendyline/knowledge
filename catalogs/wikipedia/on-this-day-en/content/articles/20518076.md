The **United States Navy** (**USN**) is the [maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare) [service branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md) and is designated as the [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) of the United States in the [Constitution](31644.md). With 290 combat vessels, it is the world's second largest navy, behind the [People's Liberation Army Navy](24918.md),[^cite_note-Unpacking_China_Naval_Buildup-8] and by far the largest by [displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_\(ship\)), at 4.5 million tons in 2021.[^cite_note-9] It has the world's largest [aircraft carrier](2219.md) fleet, with [eleven in service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aircraft_carriers_in_service), one undergoing trials, two new carriers under construction, and six other carriers planned as of 2024. The Navy is a part of the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) and is one of six armed forces and eight [uniformed services of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformed_services_of_the_United_States).

The Navy originates from the [Continental Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Navy), established in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). The [Naval Act of 1794](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Act_of_1794) provided for constructing [six heavy frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_six_frigates_of_the_United_States_Navy), the first ships of the Navy, in the prelude to the [Barbary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Wars). The [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) saw the [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_blockade) of the [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) and seizing control of its rivers. In [World War II](32927.md), the Navy was central in defeating the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) in the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) and *[Kriegsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriegsmarine)* [U-boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat) in the [Battle of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic), supplanting the [Royal Navy](26061.md) as the world's largest navy. During the [Cold War](325329.md), the Navy, rivalled by the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy), began providing [nuclear deterrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_deterrence) via [its ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41_for_Freedom). The Navy was a major participant in the [Korean](16772.md) and [Vietnam Wars](32611.md), and later the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars) and [war on terror](13425800.md). A global [power projecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) [blue-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy), current large deployments include the [Western Pacific](797909.md), the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_European_Command), and the [Indian Ocean](338482.md), and it is able to rapidly respond to regional crises, making it a frequent actor in US [foreign](7564.md) and military policy.&#x20;

The Navy is part of the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), alongside the coequal [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md), and is headed by the [secretary of the Navy](44001.md). The [chief of naval operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_naval_operations) (CNO) is the most senior Navy officer serving in the Department of the Navy.[^cite_note-10] With 336,978 personnel on [active duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_duty) and 101,583 in the [Ready Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_Reserve), the Navy is the third largest of US military service branches. The Navy maintains 290 deployable combat vessels as of 2023,[^cite_note-Unpacking_China_Naval_Buildup-8] including the world's largest fleet of [nuclear-powered ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion), between 11 aircraft carriers and 70 submarines.[^cite_note-11] The world's largest [naval aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_aviation) force with 4,012 operational aircraft,[^cite_note-12] combat fleets include 471 [F/A-18E/F Super Hornets](845012.md) and 458 [Sikorsky MH-60 Seahawk](202031.md) helicopters.[^cite_note-13] The Navy operates the [*Ohio*-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine), which are assigned 1,895 [nuclear warheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warheads), representing 51% of the [US nuclear stockpile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_the_United_States)[^cite_note-14] and 19% of the [global nuclear stockpile](400821.md).[^cite_note-15]

## Mission

> To recruit, train, equip, and organize to deliver combat ready Naval forces to win conflicts and wars while maintaining security and deterrence through sustained forward presence.

— Mission statement of the United States Navy.[^cite_note-MissionOfTheNavy-16]

The Navy's three primary areas of responsibility are:[^cite_note-17]

- The preparation of naval forces necessary for the effective prosecution of war.

- The maintenance of naval aviation, including land-based naval aviation, air transport essential for naval operations, and all air weapons and air techniques involved in the operations and activities of the Navy.

- The development of aircraft, weapons, [military tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactics), technique, organization, and equipment of [naval combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_combat) and service elements.

U.S. Navy training manuals state that the overall mission of the armed forces is "to be prepared to conduct prompt and sustained combat operations in support of the national interest." The Navy's five enduring functions are: [sea control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_control), [power projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection), [deterrence](735661.md), [maritime security](8111405.md), and [sealift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sealift).[^cite_note-18]

## History

Main article: [History of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Navy)

### Origins

> It follows then as certain as that night succeeds the day, that without a decisive naval force we can do nothing definitive, and with it, everything honorable and glorious.

— [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) 15 November 1781, to [Marquis de Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette)[^cite_note-FamousNavyQuotes-19]

> Would to Heaven we had a navy able to reform those enemies to mankind or crush them into non-existence.

— George Washington 15 August 1786, to Marquis de Lafayette[^cite_note-Washington_to_Lafayette-20]

> Naval power . . . is the natural defense of the United States.

— [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams)[^cite_note-21]

The Navy was rooted in the colonial seafaring tradition, which produced a large community of sailors, captains, and shipbuilders.[^cite_note-22] In the early stages of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), [Massachusetts](1645518.md) had its own [Massachusetts Naval Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Naval_Militia). The rationale for establishing a national navy was debated in the [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress) [Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress). Supporters argued that a navy would protect shipping, defend the coast, and make it easier to seek support from foreign countries. Detractors countered that challenging the British [Royal Navy](26061.md), then the world's preeminent naval power, was a foolish undertaking. Commander in Chief [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) resolved the debate when he commissioned the ocean-going [schooner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schooner) [USS *Hannah*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Hannah) to interdict British merchantmen and reported the captures to the Congress. On 13 October 1775, the Continental Congress authorized the purchase of two vessels to be armed for a cruise against British merchantmen; this resolution created the [Continental Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Navy) and is considered the first establishment of the U.S. Navy.[^cite_note-Continental-23] The Continental Navy achieved mixed results; it was successful in a number of engagements and raided many British merchant vessels, but it lost twenty-four of its vessels[^cite_note-love-24] and at one point was reduced to two in active service.[^cite_note-Howarth-25] In August 1785, after the Revolutionary War had drawn to a close, [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Confederation) had sold [*Alliance*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Alliance_\(1778\)), the last ship remaining in the Continental Navy due to a lack of funds to maintain the ship or support a navy.[^cite_note-Alliance-26][^cite_note-AbbotVIPICXV-27]

In 1972, the chief of naval operations, Admiral [Elmo Zumwalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmo_Zumwalt), authorized the Navy to celebrate its birthday on 13 October to honor the establishment of the Continental Navy in 1775.[^cite_note-Continental-23][^cite_note-New_Navy_Birthday-28]

### From re-establishment to the Civil War

See also: [Union Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Navy)

The United States was without a navy for nearly a decade, a situation that exposed U.S. merchant ships to attacks by the [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates). The sole armed maritime presence between 1790 and the launching of the U.S. Navy's first warships in 1797 was the [U.S. Revenue-Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Revenue_Cutter_Service), the primary predecessor of the [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard). Although the United States Revenue Cutter Service conducted operations against the pirates, the pirates' depredations far outstripped its abilities and Congress passed the [Naval Act of 1794](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Act_of_1794) that established a permanent standing navy on 27 March 1794.[^cite_note-US-29] The Naval Act ordered the construction and manning of [six frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_six_frigates_of_the_United_States_Navy) and, by October 1797,[^cite_note-love-24] the first three were brought into service: [USS *United States*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_United_States_\(1797\)), [USS *Constellation*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constellation_\(1797\)), and [USS *Constitution*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constitution). Due to his strong posture on having a strong standing Navy during this period, [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams) is "often called the father of the American Navy".[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] In 1798–99 the Navy was involved in an undeclared [Quasi-War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-War) with France.[^cite_note-32] From 1801 to 1805, in the [First Barbary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Barbary_War), the U.S. Navy defended U.S. ships from the Barbary pirates, blockaded the Barbary ports and executed attacks against the Barbary' fleets.

The U.S. Navy saw substantial action in the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), where it fought numerous engagements with Royal Navy. It emerged victorious in the [Battle of Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lake_Erie) and prevented the region from becoming a threat to American operations in the area. The result was a major victory for the U.S. Army at the [Niagara Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812#Niagara_frontier,_1813) of the war, and the defeat of [Tecumseh's confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh's_confederacy) at the [Battle of the Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Thames). Despite this, the U.S. Navy could not prevent the British from blockading its ports and landing troops.[^cite_note-multiple1-33] But after the War of 1812 ended in 1815, the U.S. Navy primarily focused its attention on protecting American shipping assets, sending squadrons to the Caribbean, the Mediterranean, where it participated in the [Second Barbary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Barbary_War) that ended piracy in the region, South America, Africa, and the Pacific.[^cite_note-love-24] From 1819 to the outbreak of the Civil War, the [Africa Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Squadron) operated to suppress the [slave trade](49005.md), seizing 36 slave ships, although its contribution was smaller than that of the much larger Royal Navy. After 1840 several [secretaries of the navy](44001.md) were southerners who advocated for strengthening southern naval defenses, expanding the fleet, and making naval technological improvements.[^cite_note-34]



[USS *Constitution* vs HMS *Guerriere*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constitution_vs_HMS_Guerriere) during the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812)

During the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) the U.S. Navy blockaded Mexican ports, capturing or burning the Mexican fleet in the [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California) and capturing all major cities in [Baja California](199793.md) peninsula. In 1846–1848 the Navy successfully used the [Pacific Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Squadron) under Commodore [Robert F. Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Stockton) and its marines and blue-jackets to facilitate the capture of California with large-scale land operations coordinated with the local militia organized in the [California Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Battalion). The Navy conducted the U.S. military's first large-scale amphibious joint operation by successfully landing 12,000 army troops with their equipment in one day at [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veracruz_\(city\)), Mexico. When larger guns were needed to bombard Veracruz, Navy volunteers landed large guns and manned them in the successful bombardment and capture of the city. This successful landing and capture of Veracruz opened the way for the capture of Mexico City and the end of the war.[^cite_note-multiple1-33] The U.S. Navy established itself as a player in [United States foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_policy) through the actions of [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(United_States\)) [Matthew C. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_C._Perry) in Japan, which resulted in the [Convention of Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Kanagawa) in 1854.

Naval power played a significant role during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), in which the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) had a distinct advantage over the [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) on the seas.[^cite_note-multiple1-33] A [Union blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_blockade) on all major ports shut down exports and the coastal trade, but blockade runners provided a thin lifeline. The [Brown-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-water_navy) components of the U.S. navy control of the river systems made internal travel difficult for Confederates and easy for the Union. The war saw [ironclad warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_warship) in combat for the first time at the [Battle of Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hampton_Roads) in 1862, which pitted [USS *Monitor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Monitor) against [CSS *Virginia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSS_Virginia).[^cite_note-35] For two decades after the war, however, the U.S. Navy's fleet was neglected and became [technologically obsolete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technologically_obsolete).[^cite_note-36]

### 20th century



The [Great White Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_White_Fleet) demonstrating U.S. naval power in 1907; it was proof that the U.S. Navy had [blue-water capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy).

A modernization program beginning in the 1880s when the first steel-hulled warships stimulated the American steel industry, and "the new steel navy" was born.[^cite_note-37] This rapid expansion of the U.S. Navy and its decisive victory over the outdated [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy) in 1898 brought a new respect for American technical quality. Rapid building of at first pre-dreadnoughts, then [dreadnoughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreadnoughts) brought the U.S. in line with the navies of countries such as Britain and Germany. In 1907, most of the Navy's battleships, with several support vessels, dubbed the [Great White Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_White_Fleet), were showcased in a 14-month circumnavigation of the world. Ordered by President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt), it was a mission designed to demonstrate the Navy's capability to extend to the global theater.[^cite_note-love-24] By 1911, the U.S. had begun building the super-dreadnoughts at a pace to eventually become competitive with Britain.[^cite_note-38] 1911 also saw the first naval aircraft with the navy[^cite_note-39] which would lead to the informal establishment of **United States Naval Flying Corps** to protect shore bases. It was not until 1921 [US naval aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Aeronautics) truly commenced.

#### World War I and interwar years

During [World War I](4764461.md), the U.S. Navy spent much of its resources protecting and shipping hundreds of thousands of soldiers and marines of the [American Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Expeditionary_Force) and war supplies across the Atlantic in [U-boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat) infested waters with the [Cruiser and Transport Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser_and_Transport_Force). It also concentrated on laying the [North Sea Mine Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_Mine_Barrage). Hesitation by the senior command meant that naval forces were not contributed until late 1917. [Battleship Division Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Battleship_Division_Nine_\(World_War_I\)) was dispatched to Britain and served as the Sixth Battle Squadron of the [British Grand Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Grand_Fleet). Its presence allowed the British to decommission some older ships and reuse the crews on smaller vessels. Destroyers and U.S. Naval Air Force units like the [Northern Bombing Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Bombing_Group) contributed to the anti-submarine operations. The strength of the United States Navy grew under an ambitious ship building program associated with the [Naval Act of 1916](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Act_of_1916).

Naval construction, especially of battleships, was limited by the [Washington Naval Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Naval_Conference) of 1921–22, the first arms control conference in history. The aircraft carriers [USS *Saratoga* (CV-3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Saratoga_\(CV-3\)) and [USS *Lexington* (CV-2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Lexington_\(CV-2\)) were built on the hulls of partially built battle cruisers that had been canceled by the treaty. The [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) used [Public Works Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Works_Administration) funds to build warships, such as [USS *Yorktown* (CV-5)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Yorktown_\(CV-5\)) and [USS *Enterprise* (CV-6)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Enterprise_\(CV-6\)). By 1936, with the completion of [USS *Wasp* (CV-7)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wasp_\(CV-7\)), the U.S. Navy possessed a carrier fleet of 165,000 tonnes [displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_\(ship\)), although this figure was nominally recorded as 135,000 tonnes to comply with treaty limitations. [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt), the number two official in the Navy Department during World War I, appreciated the Navy and gave it strong support. In return, senior leaders were eager for innovation and experimented with new technologies, such as magnetic torpedoes, and developed a strategy called [War Plan Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Plan_Orange) for victory in the Pacific in a hypothetical war with Japan that would eventually become reality.[^cite_note-40]

#### World War II

Main articles: [United States Navy in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_in_World_War_II) and [Naval history of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_history_of_World_War_II)



Battleship [USS *Idaho*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Idaho_\(BB-42\)) [shelling Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) on 1 April 1945

The U.S. Navy grew into a formidable force in the years prior to [World War II](32927.md), with battleship production being restarted in 1937, commencing with [USS *North Carolina* (BB-55)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_North_Carolina_\(BB-55\)). Though ultimately unsuccessful, Japan tried to neutralize this strategic threat with the surprise [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) on 7 December 1941. Following [American entry into the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consequences_of_the_attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), the U.S. Navy grew tremendously as the United States was faced with a two-front war on the seas. It achieved notable acclaim in the [Pacific Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War), where it was instrumental to the Allies' successful "[island hopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leapfrogging_\(strategy\))" campaign.[^cite_note-Howarth-25] The U.S. Navy participated in many significant battles, including the [Battle of the Coral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Coral_Sea), the [Battle of Midway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Midway), the [Solomon Islands Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands_Campaign), the [Battle of the Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Philippine_Sea), the [Battle of Leyte Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte_Gulf), and the [Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa). By 1943, the navy's size was larger than the combined fleets of all the other combatant nations in World War II.[^cite_note-Tread-41] By war's end in 1945, the U.S. Navy had added hundreds of new ships, including 18 aircraft carriers and 8 battleships, and had over 70% of the world's total numbers and total tonnage of naval vessels of 1,000 tons or greater.[^cite_note-Defense_Analysis;_Dec_2001,_Vol._17_Issue_3,_p._259–265-42][^cite_note-43] At its peak, the U.S. Navy was operating 6,768 ships on [V-J Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-J_Day) in August 1945.[^cite_note-44]



On [Navy Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Day#United_States), 27 October 1945, the U.S. Post Office issued a commemorative stamp in honor of the Navy and the end of WW2.

Doctrine had significantly shifted by the end of the war. The U.S. Navy had followed in the footsteps of the navies of Great Britain and Germany which favored concentrated groups of battleships as their main offensive naval weapons.[^cite_note-45] The development of the aircraft carrier and its devastating use by the Japanese against the U.S. at Pearl Harbor, however, shifted U.S. thinking. The Pearl Harbor attack destroyed or took out of action a significant number of U.S. Navy battleships. This placed much of the burden of retaliating against the Japanese on the small number of aircraft carriers.[^cite_note-46] During World War II some 4,000,000 Americans served in the United States Navy.[^cite_note-47]

#### Cold War and 1990s

See also: [1989 United States Navy order of battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_United_States_Navy_order_of_battle) and [American naval ballistic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_naval_ballistic_systems)



[USS *George Washington*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_George_Washington_\(SSBN-598\)), a ballistic missile submarine

The potential for armed conflict with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) during the [Cold War](325329.md) pushed the U.S. Navy to continue its technological advancement by developing new weapons systems, ships, and aircraft. U.S. naval strategy changed to that of forward deployment in support of U.S. allies with an emphasis on carrier battle groups.[^cite_note-48]

The navy was a major participant in the [Korean](16772.md) and [Vietnam Wars](32611.md), blockaded Cuba during the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis), and, through the use of [ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarines), became an important aspect of the United States' [nuclear strategic deterrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_assured_destruction) policy. The U.S. Navy conducted various combat operations in the Persian Gulf against Iran in 1987 and 1988, most notably [Operation Praying Mantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Praying_Mantis). The Navy was extensively involved in [Operation Urgent Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Urgent_Fury), [Operation Desert Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Shield_\(Gulf_War\)), [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm), [Operation Deliberate Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Deliberate_Force), [Operation Allied Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Allied_Force), [Operation Desert Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Fox) and [Operation Southern Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Southern_Watch).

The U.S. Navy has also been involved in search and rescue/search and salvage operations, sometimes in conjunction with vessels of other countries as well as with U.S. Coast Guard ships. Two examples are the [1966 Palomares B-52 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Palomares_B-52_crash) incident and the subsequent search for missing hydrogen bombs, and Task Force 71 of the Seventh Fleet's operation in search for [Korean Air Lines Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007), shot down by the Soviets on 1 September 1983.

### 21st century



U.S. Navy officers aboard the aircraft carrier [USS *Abraham Lincoln*](199106.md) monitor defense systems during early 2010s [maritime security operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_security_operations) exercises.

The U.S. Navy continues to be a major support for U.S. interests in the 21st century. Since the end of the Cold War, it has shifted its focus from preparations for large-scale war with the Soviet Union to special operations and strike missions in regional conflicts.[^cite_note-forward-49] The navy participated in [Operation Enduring Freedom](19666611.md), [Operation Iraqi Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iraqi_Freedom), and is a major participant in the ongoing [war on terror](13425800.md), largely in this capacity. Development continues on new ships and weapons, including the [*Gerald R. Ford*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier) aircraft carrier and the [Littoral combat ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship). Because of its size, weapons technology, and ability to project force far from U.S. shores, the current U.S. Navy remains an asset for the United States. Moreover, it is the principal means through which the U.S. maintains global order, namely by safeguarding global trade and protecting allied nations.[^cite_note-50]

In 2007, the U.S. Navy joined with the [U.S. Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marine_Corps) and U.S. Coast Guard to adopt a new maritime strategy called [A Cooperative Strategy for 21st Century Seapower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Cooperative_Strategy_for_21st_Century_Seapower) that raises the notion of prevention of war to the same philosophical level as the conduct of war. The strategy was presented by the [Chief of Naval Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations), the [Commandant of the Marine Corps](7708.md), and [Commandant of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant_of_the_Coast_Guard) at the International Sea Power Symposium in [Newport, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island) on 17 October 2007.[^cite_note-51]

The strategy recognized the economic links of the global system and how any disruption due to regional crises (man-made or natural) can adversely impact the U.S. economy and quality of life. This new strategy charts a course for the Navy, Coast Guard, and Marine Corps to work collectively with each other and international partners to prevent these crises from occurring or reacting quickly should one occur to prevent negative impacts on the U.S.

In 2010, Admiral [Gary Roughead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Roughead), Chief of Naval Operations, noted that demands on the Navy have grown as the fleet has shrunk and that in the face of future budget declines, the U.S. Navy must rely even more on international partnerships.[^cite_note-52]

In its 2013 budget request, the navy focused on retaining all eleven big deck carriers, at the expense of cutting numbers of smaller ships and delaying the SSBN replacement.[^cite_note-53] By the next year the USN found itself unable to maintain eleven aircraft carriers in the face of the expiration of budget relief offered by the [Bipartisan Budget Act of 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisan_Budget_Act_of_2013) and CNO [Jonathan Greenert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Greenert) said that a ten-ship carrier fleet would not be able to sustainably support military requirements.[^cite_note-54] The British [First Sea Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sea_Lord) [George Zambellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Zambellas) said that[^cite_note-thediplomat1-55] the USN had switched from "outcome-led to resource-led" planning.[^cite_note-56]

One significant change in U.S. policymaking that is having a major effect on naval planning is the [Pivot to East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_foreign_policy_of_the_Obama_administration). In response, the [Secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Navy) [Ray Mabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Mabus) stated in 2015 that 60 percent of the total U.S. fleet will be deployed to the [Pacific](23070.md) by 2020.[^cite_note-57] The Navy's most recent 30-year shipbuilding plan, published in 2016, calls for a future fleet of 350 ships to meet the challenges of an increasingly competitive international environment.[^cite_note-thediplomat1-55] A provision of the 2018 [National Defense Authorization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2018) called for expanding the naval fleet to 355 ships "as soon as practicable", but did not establish additional funding nor a timeline.[^cite_note-58]

## Organization

Main article: [Structure of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_the_United_States_Navy)



Organization of the United States Navy within the Department of Defense

The U.S. Navy falls under the administration of the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), under civilian leadership of the Secretary of the Navy (SECNAV). The most senior naval officer is the Chief of Naval Operations (CNO), a four-star admiral who is immediately under and reports to the Secretary of the Navy. At the same time, the Chief of Naval Operations is a member of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) (JCS), though the JCS plays only an advisory role to the President and does not nominally form part of the [chain of command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_hierarchy). The Secretary of the Navy and Chief of Naval Operations are responsible for organizing, recruiting, training, and equipping the Navy so that it is ready for operation under the commanders of the [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command).

- The [Operational Test and Evaluation Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Test_and_Evaluation_Force)

Fleet Forces Command controls a number of unique capabilities, including the [Naval Expeditionary Combat Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Expeditionary_Combat_Command) and the [Naval Information Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Information_Forces).

The United States Navy has seven active numbered fleets – [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Second_Fleet), [Third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Third_Fleet), [Fifth](418867.md), [Sixth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Sixth_Fleet), [Seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Seventh_Fleet) and [Tenth Fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Tenth_Fleet) are each led by a [vice admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral_\(United_States\)), and the [Fourth Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fourth_Fleet) is led by a [rear admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_admiral_\(United_States\)). These seven fleets are further grouped under Fleet Forces Command (the former Atlantic Fleet), Pacific Fleet, Naval Forces Europe-Africa, and Naval Forces Central Command, whose commander also doubles as Commander Fifth Fleet; the first three commands being led by four-star admirals. The [United States First Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_First_Fleet) existed after World War II from 1947, but it was redesignated the Third Fleet in early 1973. The Second Fleet was deactivated in September 2011 but reestablished in August 2018 amid heightened tensions with Russia.[^cite_note-61] It is headquartered in Norfolk, Virginia, with responsibility over the East Coast and North Atlantic.[^cite_note-62] In early 2008, the Navy reactivated the Fourth Fleet to control operations in the area controlled by Southern Command, which consists of US assets in and around Central and South America.[^cite_note-63] Other number fleets were activated during World War II and later deactivated, renumbered, or merged.

### Relationships with other service branches

#### United States Marine Corps

Main article: [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md)



A [Marine](17349325.md) [F/A-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18) from [VMFA-451](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMFA-451) preparing to launch from [USS *Coral Sea*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Coral_Sea_\(CV-43\))

In 1834, the [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) came under the Department of the Navy.[^cite_note-66] Historically, the Navy has had a unique relationship with the USMC, partly because they both specialize in seaborne operations. Together the Navy and Marine Corps form the Department of the Navy and report to the Secretary of the Navy. However, the Marine Corps is a distinct, separate service branch[^cite_note-NSA1947sec606-67] with its own uniformed service chief – the Commandant of the Marine Corps, a four-star general.

The Marine Corps depends on the Navy for medical support (dentists, [doctors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_doctor), nurses, medical technicians known as [corpsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_corpsman)) and religious support (chaplains). Thus, Navy officers and enlisted sailors fulfill these roles. When attached to Marine Corps units deployed to an operational environment they generally wear Marine camouflage uniforms, but otherwise, they wear Navy [dress uniforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_uniform) unless they opt to conform to Marine Corps grooming standards.[^cite_note-\:0-65]

In the operational environment, as an expeditionary force specializing in amphibious operations, Marines often embark on Navy ships to conduct operations from beyond territorial waters. Marine units deploying as part of a Marine Air-Ground Task Force (MAGTF) operate under the command of the existing Marine chain of command. Although Marine units routinely operate from amphibious assault ships, the relationship has evolved over the years much as the Commander of the Carrier Air Group/Wing (CAG) does not work for the carrier commanding officer, but coordinates with the ship's CO and staff. Some Marine aviation squadrons, usually fixed-wing assigned to carrier air wings train and operate alongside Navy squadrons; they fly similar missions and often fly sorties together under the cognizance of the CAG. Aviation is where the Navy and Marines share the most common ground since aircrews are guided in their use of aircraft by standard procedures outlined in a series of publications known as [NATOPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATOPS) manuals.

#### United States Coast Guard

Main article: [United States Coast Guard](32223.md)



A U.S. Coast Guard helicopter preparing to land on the flight deck of the amphibious assault ship [USS *Wasp*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wasp_\(LHD-1\))

The [United States Coast Guard](32223.md), in its peacetime role with the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md), fulfills its law enforcement and rescue role in the maritime environment. It provides [Law Enforcement Detachments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Enforcement_Detachments) (LEDETs) to Navy vessels, where they perform arrests and other law enforcement duties during naval boarding and interdiction missions. In times of war, the Coast Guard may be called upon to operate as a service within the Navy.[^cite_note-68] At other times, Coast Guard [Port Security Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Security_Unit) are sent overseas to guard the security of ports and other assets. The Coast Guard also jointly staffs the Navy's naval coastal warfare groups and squadrons (the latter of which were known as harbor defense commands until late-2004), which oversee defense efforts in foreign littoral combat and inshore areas.

## Personnel

Main article: [Personnel of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personnel_of_the_United_States_Navy)



[Navy SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs) at one of the entrances to the [Zhawar Kili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhawar_Kili) cave complex

The United States Navy has over 400,000 personnel, approximately a quarter of whom are in ready reserve. Of those on active duty, more than eighty percent are [enlisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlisted_rank) sailors and around fifteen percent are [commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officer); the rest are [midshipmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midshipmen) of the United States Naval Academy and midshipmen of the [Naval Reserve Officer Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Reserve_Officer_Training_Corps) at over 180 universities around the country and officer candidates at the Navy's [Officer Candidate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_Candidate_School_\(United_States_Navy\)).[^cite_note-status-4]

Enlisted sailors complete basic military training at [boot camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_Training_Command,_Great_Lakes,_Illinois) and then are sent to complete training for their individual [careers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_ratings).[^cite_note-69]

Sailors prove they have mastered skills and deserve responsibilities by completing Personnel Qualification Standards (PQS) tasks and examinations. Among the most important is the "warfare qualification", which denotes a journeyman level of capability in Surface Warfare, Aviation Warfare, Information Dominance Warfare, Naval Aircrew, Special Warfare, Seabee Warfare, Submarine Warfare or Expeditionary Warfare. Many qualifications are denoted on a sailor's uniform with [U.S. Navy badges and insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badges_of_the_United_States_Navy).

Warrant and chief warrant officer ranks are held by technical specialists who direct specific activities essential to the proper operation of the ship, which also require commissioned officer authority.[^cite_note-WO-4-74] Navy warrant officers serve in 30 specialties covering five categories. Warrant officers should not be confused with the [limited duty officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_duty_officer) (LDO) in the Navy. Warrant officers perform duties that are directly related to their previous enlisted service and specialized training. This allows the Navy to capitalize on the experience of warrant officers without having to frequently transition them to other duty assignments for advancement.[^cite_note-WO-5-75] Most Navy warrant officers are accessed from the [chief petty officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_petty_officer_\(United_States\)) pay grades, E-7 through E-9, analogous to a senior non-commissioned officer in the other services, and must have a minimum 14 years in service.[^cite_note-76]

#### Enlisted

See also: [List of United States Navy enlisted rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_enlisted_rates), [Ranks and insignia of NATO navies enlisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranks_and_insignia_of_NATO_navies_enlisted), and [List of United States Navy ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_ratings)

Sailors in pay grades E-1 through E-3 are considered to be in apprenticeships.[^cite_note-NAVYDAT2-77] They are divided into five definable groups, with colored group rate marks designating the group to which they belong: Seaman, Fireman, Airman, Constructionman, and Hospitalman. **E-4** to **E-6** are [non-commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer) (NCOs), and are specifically called [Petty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty_officer) in the Navy.[^cite_note-BUPERS-78] Petty Officers perform not only the duties of their specific career field but also serve as leaders to junior enlisted personnel. E-7 to E-9 are still considered Petty Officers, but are considered a separate community within the Navy. They have separate berthing and dining facilities (where feasible), wear separate uniforms, and perform separate duties.

After attaining the rate of Master Chief Petty Officer, a service member may choose to further their career by becoming a [Command Master Chief Petty Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_Master_Chief_Petty_Officer) (CMC). A CMC is considered to be the senior-most enlisted service member within a command, and is the special assistant to the [Commanding Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_Officer) in all matters pertaining to the health, welfare, job satisfaction, morale, use, advancement and training of the command's enlisted personnel.[^cite_note-MILCMDMC-79][^cite_note-IPUBCMC-80] CMCs can be Command level (within a single unit, such as a ship or shore station), Fleet level (squadrons consisting of multiple operational units, headed by a flag officer or commodore), or Force level (consisting of a separate community within the Navy, such as Subsurface, Air, Reserves).[^cite_note-OPNAV1306-81]

CMC insignia are similar to the insignia for Master Chief, except that the rating symbol is replaced by an inverted five-point star, reflecting a change in their rating from their previous rating (i.e., MMCM) to CMDCM. The stars for Command Master Chief are silver, while stars for Fleet, and gold stars for Force. Additionally, CMCs wear a badge, worn on their left breast pocket, denoting their title (Command/Fleet/Force).[^cite_note-IPUBCMC-80][^cite_note-BUPERSCPO-82]

#### Badges of the United States Navy

See also: [Badges of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badges_of_the_United_States_Navy)

Insignia and badges of the United States Navy are military "badges" issued by the Department of the Navy to naval service members who achieve certain qualifications and accomplishments while serving on both active and reserve duty in the United States Navy. Most naval aviation insignia are also permitted for wear on uniforms of the [United States Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badges_of_the_United_States_Marine_Corps).

As described in Chapter 5 of U.S. Navy Uniform Regulations,[^cite_note-navy_reg-85] "badges" are categorized as *breast insignia* (usually worn immediately above and below ribbons) and *identification badges* (usually worn at breast pocket level).[^cite_note-chapter_5-86] Breast insignia are further divided between *command* and *warfare and other qualification*.[^cite_note-section_2-87]

Insignia come in the form of metal "pin-on devices" worn on formal uniforms and embroidered "tape strips" worn on work uniforms. For the purpose of this article, the general term "insignia" shall be used to describe both, as it is done in Navy Uniform Regulations. The term "badge", although used ambiguously in other military branches and in informal speak to describe any pin, patch, or tab, is exclusive to *identification badges*[^cite_note-article_5201.2-88] and authorized *[marksmanship awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Shot_Badge)*[^cite_note-article_5310-89] according to the language in Navy Uniform Regulations, Chapter 5. ## Bases

Main article: [List of United States Navy installations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_installations)



Map of naval bases in the United States

The size, complexity, and international presence of the United States Navy requires a large number of navy installations to support its operations. While the majority of bases are located inside the United States itself, the Navy maintains a significant number of facilities abroad, either in U.S.-controlled territories or in foreign countries under a [Status of Forces Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Forces_Agreement) (SOFA).

### Eastern United States

The second largest concentration of installations is at [Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads), [Virginia](32432.md), where the navy occupies over 36,000 acres (15,000 ha) of land. Located at Hampton Roads are [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md), homeport of the Atlantic Fleet; [Naval Air Station Oceana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Oceana), a [master jet base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_jet_base); [Naval Amphibious Base Little Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Amphibious_Base_Little_Creek); and [Training Support Center Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_Support_Center_Hampton_Roads) as well as a number of Navy and commercial shipyards that service navy vessels. The Aegis Training and Readiness Center is located at the [Naval Support Activity South Potomac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_South_Potomac) in [Dahlgren, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlgren,_Virginia). Maryland is home to [NAS Patuxent River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Patuxent_River), which houses the Navy's [Test Pilot School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Test_Pilot_School). Also located in Maryland is the United States Naval Academy, situated in [Annapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapolis). [NS Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Newport) in Newport, Rhode Island is home to many schools and tenant commands, including the [Officer Candidate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_Candidate_School), [Naval Undersea Warfare Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Undersea_Warfare_Center), and more, and also maintains inactive ships.

There is also a naval base in Charleston, South Carolina. This is home to the Naval Nuclear Power Training Command, under which reside the Nuclear Field "A" Schools (for Machinist Mates (Nuclear), Electrician Mates (Nuclear), and Electronics Technicians (Nuclear)), Nuclear Power School (Officer and Enlisted); and one of two Nuclear Power Training Unit 'Prototype' schools. The state of Florida is the location of three major bases, [NS Mayport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Mayport), the Navy's fourth largest, in [Jacksonville, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida); [NAS Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Jacksonville), a Master Air Anti-submarine Warfare base; and [NAS Pensacola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Pensacola); home of the Naval Education and Training Command, the Naval Air Technical Training Center that provides specialty training for enlisted aviation personnel and is the primary flight training base for Navy and Marine Corps [Naval Flight Officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Flight_Officer) and enlisted [Naval Aircrewmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_aircrewman). There is also [NSA Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Panama_City), Florida which is home to the Center for Explosive Ordnance Disposal and Diving (CENEODIVE) and the Navy Diving and Salvage Training Center and [NSA Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Warfare_Center_Training_Systems_Division), Florida, which home to the Naval Air Warfare Center Training Systems Division (NAWCTSD).

The main U.S. Navy [submarine bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_base) on the east coast are located in [Naval Submarine Base New London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Submarine_Base_New_London) in [Groton, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groton,_Connecticut) and [NSB Kings Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Submarine_Base_Kings_Bay) in [Kings Bay, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Bay,_Georgia). The [Portsmouth Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Shipyard) near [Portsmouth, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_New_Hampshire),[^cite_note-90] which repairs naval submarines.[^cite_note-status-4] [NS Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Great_Lakes), north of Chicago, Illinois is the home of the Navy's [boot camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_Training_Command,_Great_Lakes,_Illinois) for enlisted sailors.

The [Washington Navy Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Navy_Yard) in Washington, D.C., is the Navy's oldest shore establishment and serves as a ceremonial and administrative center for the U.S. Navy, home to the chief of naval operations and numerous commands.[^cite_note-91]

### Western United States and Hawaii



[Underwater Demolition Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_Demolition_Team) members using the casting technique from a speeding boat

The U.S. Navy's largest complex is [Naval Air Weapons Station China Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Weapons_Station_China_Lake), California, which covers 1.1 million acres (4,500 km<sup>2</sup>) of land, or approximately one-third of the U.S. Navy's total land holdings.[^cite_note-status-4]

[Naval Base San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_San_Diego), California, is the main homeport of the Pacific Fleet, although its headquarters is located in [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor), Hawaii. [NAS North Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_North_Island) is located on the north side of [Coronado, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronado,_California), and is home to Headquarters for Naval Air Forces and Naval Air Force Pacific, the bulk of the Pacific Fleet's helicopter squadrons, and part of the West Coast [aircraft carrier](2219.md) fleet. [NAB Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Amphibious_Base_Coronado) is located on the southern end of the Coronado Island and is home to the navy's west coast SEAL teams and special boat units. NAB Coronado is also home to the [Naval Special Warfare Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Special_Warfare_Center), the primary training center for SEALs.

The other major collection of naval bases on the west coast is in [Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound), Washington. Among them, [NS Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Everett) is one of the newer bases and the navy states that it is its most modern facility.[^cite_note-92]

[NAS Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Fallon), Nevada serves as the primary training ground for navy strike aircrews and is home to the [Naval Strike Air Warfare Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Strike_Air_Warfare_Center). Master Jet Bases are also located at [NAS Lemoore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Lemoore), California, and [NAS Whidbey Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Whidbey_Island), Washington, while the carrier-based airborne early warning aircraft community and major air test activities are located at [NAS Point Mugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Point_Mugu), California. The naval presence in Hawaii is centered on [NS Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Pearl_Harbor), which hosts the headquarters of the Pacific Fleet and many of its subordinate commands.

### United States territories



[USS *Carl Vinson*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Carl_Vinson) pier side in [Apra Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apra_Harbor), Guam

Guam, an island strategically located in the Western Pacific Ocean, maintains a sizable U.S. Navy presence, including [NB Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Guam). The westernmost U.S. territory, it contains a natural Deepwater harbor capable of harboring aircraft carriers in emergencies. Its naval air station was deactivated in 1995 and its flight activities transferred to nearby [Andersen Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andersen_Air_Force_Base).

[Puerto Rico](23041.md) in the Caribbean formerly housed [NS Roosevelt Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Roads_Naval_Station), which was shut down in 2004 shortly after the controversial closure of the live ordnance training area on nearby [Vieques Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieques,_Puerto_Rico).[^cite_note-status-4]

### Foreign countries

The largest overseas base is the [United States Fleet Activities Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fleet_Activities_Yokosuka), Japan, which serves as the home port for the navy's largest forward-deployed fleet and is a significant base of operations in the Western Pacific.

European operations revolve around facilities in Italy ([NAS Sigonella](893813.md) and [Naval Computer and Telecommunications Station Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Computer_and_Telecommunications_Station_Naples,_Italy)) with [NSA Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Naples) as the homeport for the Sixth Fleet and Command Naval Region Europe, Africa, Southwest Asia (CNREURAFSWA), and additional facilities in nearby [Gaeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaeta). There is also [NS Rota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Rota,_Spain) in Spain and [NSA Souda Bay](49049283.md) in Greece.

In the Middle East, naval facilities are located almost exclusively in countries bordering the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), with [NSA Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain) serving as the headquarters of [U.S. Naval Forces Central Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Forces_Central_Command) and [U.S. Fifth Fleet](418867.md).

[NS Guantanamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_Naval_Base) in Cuba is the oldest overseas facility and has become known in recent years as the location of a [detention camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp) for suspected [al-Qaeda](1921.md) operatives.[^cite_note-ForeignFacilities-93]

### Ships

Main articles: [United States Navy ships](1176720.md) and [Future of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_the_United_States_Navy)

See also: [List of equipment of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_United_States_Navy), [List of current ships of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_ships_of_the_United_States_Navy), [List of currently active United States military watercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_currently_active_United_States_military_watercraft), and [United States ship naming conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_ship_naming_conventions)

The names of commissioned ships of the U.S. Navy are prefixed with the letters "USS", designating "United States Ship".[^cite_note-94] Non-commissioned, civilian-manned vessels of the navy have names that begin with "USNS", standing for "United States Naval Ship". The names of ships are officially selected by the secretary of the navy, often to honor important people or places.[^cite_note-95] Additionally, each ship is given a letter-based [hull classification symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_classification_symbol) (for example, CVN or DDG) to indicate the vessel's type and number. All ships in the navy inventory are placed in the [Naval Vessel Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Vessel_Register), which is part of "the Navy List" (required by article 29 of the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md)). The register tracks data such as the current status of a ship, the date of its commissioning, and the date of its decommissioning. Vessels that are removed from the register prior to disposal are said to be *stricken* from the register. The navy also maintains a [reserve fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_reserve_fleets) of inactive vessels that are maintained for reactivation in times of need.

The U.S. Navy was one of the first to install [nuclear reactors aboard naval vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion).[^cite_note-96] Today, [nuclear energy](22153.md) powers all active U.S. aircraft carriers and [submarines](28825.md).

In early 2010, the U.S. Navy had identified a need for 313 combat ships but could only afford 232 to 243 ships.[^cite_note-97] In March 2014, the Navy started counting self-deployable support ships such as minesweepers, surveillance craft, and tugs in the "battle fleet" to reach a count of 272 as of October 2016,[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] and it includes ships that have been put in "shrink wrap".[^cite_note-100] The number of ships generally ranged between 270 and 300 throughout the late 2010s.[^cite_note-CSR1-101] As of February 2022, the Navy has 296 battle force ships, however analyses state the Navy needs a fleet of more than 500 to meet its commitments.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

#### Aircraft carriers

Main article: [List of aircraft carriers of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aircraft_carriers_of_the_United_States_Navy)



Aerial view of [USS *Gerald R. Ford*](4050542.md) (bottom), a ship of the new [*Gerald R. Ford* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier), alongside [USS *Harry S. Truman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Harry_S._Truman) (top), a ship of the previous [*Nimitz* class](181160.md)

[Aircraft carriers](2219.md) act as [airbases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_airbase) for [carrier-based aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier-based_aircraft). They are the largest vessels in the Navy fleet and all are nuclear-powered.[^cite_note-CSR1-101] An aircraft carrier is typically deployed along with a host of additional vessels, forming a [carrier strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_strike_group). The supporting ships, which usually include three or four [Aegis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_combat_system)-equipped cruisers and destroyers, a frigate, and two attack submarines, are tasked with protecting the carrier from air, missile, sea, and undersea threats as well as providing additional strike capabilities themselves. Ready logistics support for the group is provided by a combined ammunition, oiler, and supply ship. Modern carriers are [named](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_ship_naming_conventions) after American admirals and politicians, usually presidents.[^cite_note-\:1-104]

The Navy has a statutory requirement for a minimum of 11 aircraft carriers.[^cite_note-105] All 11 carriers are currently active, ten [*Nimitz*-class](181160.md) and one [*Gerald R. Ford*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier).

#### Amphibious warfare ships

Main article: [List of United States Navy amphibious warfare ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_amphibious_warfare_ships)



[USS *Bataan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Bataan_\(LHD-5\)), a [*Wasp*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasp-class_amphibious_assault_ship) [amphibious assault ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship)

[Amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship) are the centerpieces of US amphibious warfare and fulfill the same power projection role as aircraft carriers except that their striking force centers on land forces instead of aircraft. They deliver, command, coordinate, and fully support all elements of a 2,200-strong [Marine Expeditionary Unit](160354.md) in an amphibious assault using both air and amphibious vehicles. Resembling small aircraft carriers, amphibious assault ships are capable of [V/STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/STOL), [STOVL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOVL), [VTOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTOL), tiltrotor, and rotary wing aircraft operations. They also contain a [well deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_deck) to support the use of [Landing Craft Air Cushion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Craft_Air_Cushion) (LCAC) and other amphibious assault watercraft. Recently, amphibious assault ships have begun to be deployed as the core of an [expeditionary strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_strike_group), which usually consists of an additional [amphibious transport dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_transport_dock) and [dock landing ship](641435.md) for amphibious warfare and an Aegis-equipped cruiser and destroyer, frigate, and attack submarine for group defense. Amphibious assault ships are typically named after World War II aircraft carriers.

[Amphibious transport docks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_transport_docks) are warships that embark, transport, and land Marines, supplies, and equipment in a supporting role during amphibious warfare missions. With a landing platform, amphibious transport docks also have the capability to serve as secondary aviation support for an expeditionary group. All amphibious transport docks can operate helicopters, LCACs, and other conventional amphibious vehicles while the newer *San Antonio* class of ships has been explicitly designed to operate all three elements of the Marines' "mobility triad": [Expeditionary Fighting Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_Fighting_Vehicle) (EFVs), the [V-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-22_Osprey) tiltrotor aircraft, and LCACs. Amphibious transport docks are typically named after U.S. cities.

The dock landing ship is a medium amphibious transport that is designed specifically to support and operate LCACs, though it is able to operate other amphibious assault vehicles in the United States inventory as well. Dock landing ships are normally deployed as a component of an expeditionary strike group's amphibious assault contingent, operating as a secondary launch platform for LCACs. All dock landing ships are named after cities or important places in U.S. and U.S. Naval history.[^cite_note-\:1-104]

The Navy operates 32 amphibious warfare ships, eight [*Wasp* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasp-class_amphibious_assault_ship) and two [*America* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America-class_amphibious_assault_ship) amphibious assault ships, four [*Harpers Ferry* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpers_Ferry-class_dock_landing_ship) and six [*Whidbey Island* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whidbey_Island-class_dock_landing_ship) dock landing ships, and 12 [*San Antonio* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio-class_amphibious_transport_dock) amphibious transport dock ships.

#### Cruisers

Main article: [List of cruisers of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cruisers_of_the_United_States_Navy)



[USS *Port Royal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Port_Royal_\(CG-73\)), a [*Ticonderoga*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticonderoga-class_cruiser) [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser)

[Cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser) are large surface combat vessels that conduct anti-air/anti-missile warfare, surface warfare, anti-submarine warfare, and strike operations independently or as members of a larger task force. Modern guided missile cruisers were developed out of a need to counter the [anti-ship missile](140725.md) threat facing the United States Navy. This led to the development of the [AN/SPY-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/SPY-1) phased array radar and the [RIM-67 Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-67_Standard) missile with the Aegis combat system coordinating the two. [*Ticonderoga*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticonderoga-class_cruiser) cruisers were the first to be equipped with Aegis and were put to use primarily as anti-air and anti-missile defense in a battle force protection role. Later developments of [vertical launch systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_launch_system) and the [Tomahawk missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGM-109_Tomahawk) gave cruisers additional long-range land and sea strike capability, making them capable of both offensive and defensive battle operations. The *Ticonderoga* class is the only active class of cruiser. All cruisers in this class are named after battles.[^cite_note-\:1-104]

#### Destroyers

Main article: [List of destroyers of the United States Navy](426565.md)



[USS *Jack H. Lucas*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Jack_H._Lucas), a [*Arleigh Burke*-class destroyer](197341.md)

[Destroyers](8779.md) are multi-mission medium surface ships capable of sustained performance in anti-air, anti-submarine, anti-ship, and offensive strike operations. Like cruisers, guided missile destroyers are primarily focused on surface strikes using Tomahawk missiles and fleet defense through Aegis and the Standard missile. Destroyers additionally specialize in anti-submarine warfare and are equipped with [VLA rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASROC) and [LAMPS Mk III Sea Hawk](202031.md) helicopters to deal with underwater threats. When deployed with a carrier strike group or expeditionary strike group, destroyers and their fellow Aegis-equipped cruisers are primarily tasked with defending the fleet while providing secondary strike capabilities. With very few exceptions, destroyers are named after U.S. Navy, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard heroes.[^cite_note-\:1-104]

The U.S. Navy currently has 75 destroyers, 73 [*Arleigh Burke*-class](197341.md) destroyers and two [*Zumwalt*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer) stealth destroyers, with a third (the [USS *Lyndon B. Johnson*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Lyndon_B._Johnson)) expected to enter service sometime in 2024.[^cite_note-106]

#### Frigates and littoral combat ships

Main articles: [List of frigates of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_frigates_of_the_United_States_Navy) and [Littoral combat ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship)



[USS *Independence*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Independence_\(LCS-2\)), a [littoral combat ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship)

Modern U.S. [frigates](11464.md) mainly perform anti-submarine warfare for carrier and expeditionary strike groups and provide armed escort for supply convoys and merchant shipping. They are designed to protect friendly ships against hostile submarines in low to medium threat environments, using torpedoes and LAMPS helicopters. Independently, frigates are able to conduct counterdrug missions and other maritime interception operations. As in the case of destroyers, frigates are named after U.S. Navy, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard heroes.

In late 2015, the U.S. Navy retired its most recent class of traditional frigates in favor of the littoral combat ship (LCS), relatively small vessels designed for near-shore operations that was expected to assume many of the duties the frigate had with the fleet. The LCS was "envisioned to be a networked, agile, stealthy surface combatant capable of defeating [anti-access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-access/area_denial) and [asymmetric threats](46863.md) in the [littorals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_zone)",[^cite_note-107] although their ability to perform these missions in practice has been called into question.[^cite_note-108] The Navy has announced it plans to reduce procurement of the LCS and retire early examples of the type.



[USS *Constitution*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constitution), firing a 17-gun salute in 2014

The Navy planned to purchase up to 20 of the [*Constellation*-class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation-class_frigate) (18 cancelled 2 under construction to be completed).[^cite_note-USNI_Constell_cancel-109] *Constellation* is based on the [FREMM multipurpose frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FREMM_multipurpose_frigate),[^cite_note-USNI_Constell_cancel-109] already in service with European navies.

The U.S. Navy has 23 littoral combat ships, eight [*Freedom*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom-class_littoral_combat_ship) and 15 [*Independence*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence-class_littoral_combat_ship) ships as of 2025.

#### Mine countermeasures ships

Main article: [List of mine warfare vessels of the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mine_warfare_vessels_of_the_United_States_Navy)

[Mine countermeasures vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_countermeasures_vessel) are a combination of [minehunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minehunter), a naval vessel that actively detects and destroys individual [naval mines](22102.md), and [minesweepers](217118.md), which clear mined areas as a whole, without prior detection of the mines. MCM vessels have mostly legacy names of previous US Navy ships, especially World War II-era minesweepers.

The Navy operates eight [*Avenger*-class mine countermeasures ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenger-class_mine_countermeasures_ship), with four expected to be retired in 2024.

#### Submarines

Main article: [Submarines in the United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarines_in_the_United_States_Navy)



[USS *Kentucky*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Kentucky_\(SSBN-737\)), an [*Ohio*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine) [ballistic missile submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine)

The U.S. Navy operates three types of submarines: [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine), [ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine) and [guided missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_submarine). All current and planned U.S. Navy submarines are nuclear-powered, as nuclear propulsion allows for a combination of stealth and long-duration, high-speed, sustained underwater movement.

Attack submarines typically operate as part of a [carrier battle group](7772.md), while [guided missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_submarine) generally operate independently and carry larger quantities of cruise missiles. Both types have several tactical missions, including sinking ships and other subs, launching [cruise missiles](6590.md), gathering intelligence, and assisting in special operations. Ballistic missile submarines operate independently with only one mission: to carry and, if called upon, to launch the [Trident nuclear missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_nuclear_missile).

The Navy operates 69 submarines, 29 [*Los Angeles* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles-class_submarine) attack submarines (with two more in reserve), 18 [*Ohio* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine) submarines with 14 configured as ballistic missile submarines and four configured as guided missile submarines, three [*Seawolf* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawolf-class_submarine) attack submarines, and 19 [*Virginia* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine) attack submarines.

#### Other

A special case is the [USS *Constitution*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constitution), commissioned in 1797 as one of the original six frigates of the United States Navy and which remains in commission at the Charlestown Navy Yard in Boston. She occasionally sails for commemorative events such as [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)).

The Navy operates a class of small tugboats, called [Barrier Boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_Boat).

### Aircraft

Main article: [List of active United States naval aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_United_States_naval_aircraft)

See also: [List of United States Navy aircraft squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_aircraft_squadrons) and [List of United States Navy aircraft designations (pre-1962)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_aircraft_designations_\(pre-1962\))



Four Navy [F/A-18E/F Super Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18E/F_Super_Hornet)

Carrier-based aircraft are able to strike air, sea, and land targets far from a carrier strike group while protecting friendly forces from enemy aircraft, ships, and submarines. In peacetime, aircraft's ability to project the threat of sustained attack from a mobile platform on the seas gives United States leaders significant diplomatic and crisis-management options. Aircraft additionally provide logistics support to maintain the navy's readiness and, through helicopters, supply platforms with which to conduct [search and rescue](172599.md), [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations), anti-submarine warfare (ASW), and anti-surface warfare, including the U.S. Navy's premier Maritime Strike and only organic ASW aircraft, the venerable Sikorsky MH-60R operated by [Helicopter Maritime Strike Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_Maritime_Strike_Wing).

The U.S. Navy began to research the use of aircraft at sea in the 1910s, with Lieutenant Theodore G. "Spuds" Ellyson becoming the first naval aviator on 28 January 1911, and commissioned its first aircraft carrier, [USS *Langley* (CV-1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Langley_\(CV-1\)), in 1922.[^cite_note-110] United States naval aviation fully came of age in World War II, when it became clear following the attack on Pearl Harbor, the Battle of the Coral Sea, and the Battle of Midway that aircraft carriers and the planes that they carried had replaced the battleship as the greatest weapon on the seas. Leading navy aircraft in World War II included the [Grumman F4F Wildcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F4F_Wildcat), the [Grumman F6F Hellcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F6F_Hellcat), the Chance [Vought F4U Corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_F4U_Corsair), the [Douglas SBD Dauntless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_SBD_Dauntless), and the [Grumman TBF Avenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_TBF_Avenger). Navy aircraft also played a significant role in conflicts during the following Cold War years, with the [F-4 Phantom II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-4_Phantom_II) and the [F-14 Tomcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-14_Tomcat) becoming military icons of the era. The navy's current primary fighter-attack airplane is the multi-mission [F/A-18E/F Super Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18E/F_Super_Hornet). The [F-35C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35_Lightning_II) entered service in 2019.[^cite_note-111] The Navy is also looking to eventually replace its F/A-18E/F Super Hornets with the [F/A-XX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-XX) program.

The Aircraft Investment Plan sees naval aviation growing from 30 percent of current aviation forces to half of all procurement funding over the next three decades.[^cite_note-112]

### Weapons

Main article: [List of United States Navy weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_weapons)



Aviation ordnancemen loading [GBU-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-12_Paveway_II) bombs in 2005

Current U.S. Navy shipboard weapons systems are almost entirely focused on missiles, both as a weapon and as a threat. In an offensive role, missiles are intended to strike targets at long distances with accuracy and precision. Because they are unmanned weapons, missiles allow for attacks on heavily defended targets without risk to human pilots. Land strikes are the domain of the BGM-109 Tomahawk, which was first deployed in the 1980s and is continually being updated to increase its capabilities. For anti-ship strikes, the navy's dedicated missile is the [Harpoon Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoon_Missile). To defend against enemy missile attack, the navy operates a number of systems that are all coordinated by the Aegis combat system. Medium-long range defense is provided by the Standard Missile 2, which has been deployed since the 1980s. The Standard missile doubles as the primary shipboard anti-aircraft weapon and is undergoing development for use in theater ballistic missile defense. Short range defense against missiles is provided by the [Phalanx CIWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_CIWS) and the more recently developed [RIM-162 Evolved Sea Sparrow Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESSM). In addition to missiles, the navy employs [Mark 46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_46_torpedo), [Mark 48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_48_torpedo), and [Mark 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_50_torpedo) [torpedoes](31424.md) and various types of naval mines.

Naval fixed-wing aircraft employ much of the same weapons as the [United States Air Force](32090.md) for both air-to-air and air-to-surface combat. Air engagements are handled by the heat-seeking [Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) and the radar guided [AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM) missiles along with the [M61 Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M61_Vulcan) cannon for close range dogfighting. For surface strikes, navy aircraft use a combination of missiles, smart bombs, and dumb bombs. On the list of available missiles are the [Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-65_Maverick), [SLAM-ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84H/K_SLAM-ER) and [JSOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-154_Joint_Standoff_Weapon). Smart bombs include the GPS-guided [JDAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JDAM) and the laser-guided [Paveway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paveway) series. Unguided munitions such as dumb bombs and [cluster bombs](79745.md) make up the rest of the weapons deployed by fixed-wing aircraft.

Rotary aircraft weapons are focused on anti-submarine warfare (ASW) and light to medium surface engagements. To combat submarines, helicopters use Mark 46 and Mark 50 torpedoes. Against small watercraft, they use [Hellfire](38151.md) and [Penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_missile) air to surface missiles. Helicopters also employ various types of mounted anti-personnel machine guns, including the [M60](491485.md), [M240](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M240_machine_gun), [GAU-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAU-16)/A, and [GAU-17/A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minigun).

Nuclear weapons in the U.S. Navy arsenal are deployed through ballistic missile submarines and aircraft. The *Ohio*-class submarine carries the latest iteration of the [Trident missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_missile), a three-stage, [submarine-launched ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine-launched_ballistic_missile) (SLBM) with [MIRV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIRV) capability; the current Trident II (D5) version is expected to be in service past 2020.[^cite_note-113] The navy's other nuclear weapon is the air-deployed [B61 nuclear bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B61_nuclear_bomb). The B61 is a thermonuclear device that can be dropped by strike aircraft such as the F/A-18 Hornet and Super Hornet at high speed from a large range of altitudes. It can be released through free-fall or parachute and can be set to detonate in the air or on the ground.

## Naval jack



U.S. naval jack



First navy jack

The current [naval jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_\(flag\)) of the United States is the [Union Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_of_the_United_States), a small blue flag emblazoned with the stars of the 50 states. The Union Jack was not flown for the duration of the war on terror, during which Secretary of the Navy [Gordon R. England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_R._England) directed all U.S. naval ships to fly the [First Navy Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Navy_Jack). While Secretary England directed the change on 31 May 2002, many ships chose to shift colors later that year in remembrance of the first anniversary of the [September 11, 2001, attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks). The Union Jack, however, remained in use with vessels of the U.S. Coast Guard and [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](37876.md). A jack of similar design to the Union Jack was used in 1794, with 13 stars arranged in a 3–2–3–2–3 pattern. When a ship is moored or anchored, the jack is flown from the [bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_\(watercraft\)) of the ship while the ensign is flown from the [stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern). When underway, the ensign is raised on the mainmast. Before the decision for all ships to fly the First Navy Jack, it was flown only on the oldest ship in the active American fleet, which is currently [USS *Blue Ridge*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Blue_Ridge_\(LCC-19\)). U.S. Navy ships and craft returned to flying the Union Jack effective 4 June 2019. The date for reintroduction of the jack commemorates the Battle of Midway, which began on 4 June 1942.[^cite_note-114]

## Naval post offices

During World War I the first U.S. government post offices were established aboard Navy ships, managed by a Navy postal clerk. Prior to this, mail from crew members was collected and at the first opportunity was dropped off at a port of call where it was processed at a US Post Office. Before the arrival of email and the internet, hand stamped mail was the only way Navy crew members at sea could communicate with their family, friends and others. Mail was considered almost as valuable to crew members as food and ammunition.[^cite_note-120] Sometimes mail from various crew members (referred to by historians and collectors as [postal history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_history)), is directly associated with naval history.[^cite_note-121] Letters and other correspondence sent by commanders, officers and crew members can include names, ranks, signatures, addresses, and ship's postmarks which can often confirm dates and locations of naval ships and crew members during various battles or other naval operations. As such, naval mail can serve as a source of information to naval historians and biographers. Among the more notable examples of Naval postal history include letters sent from the [USS *Arizona*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Arizona), before and on 7 December 1941.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]
