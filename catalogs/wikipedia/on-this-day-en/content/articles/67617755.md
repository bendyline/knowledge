**Randy Alan George** (born 1 November 1964) is a retired American [general](2965165.md) who served as the 41st [chief of staff of the United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_staff_of_the_United_States_Army) from 2023 to 2026. He also served as the 38th [vice chief of staff of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_chief_of_staff_of_the_United_States_Army) from 2022 to 2023, and the [senior military assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Military_Assistant_to_the_Secretary_of_Defense) to the [United States secretary of defense](43998.md), [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin), from 2021 to 2022.

Born and raised in [Iowa](26810748.md), George enlisted in the [United States Army](32087.md) in 1982, before entering the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) at [West Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point,_New_York) in 1984. He was commissioned in 1988 as an [Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Branch_\(United_States\)) officer and was deployed to [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) multiple times during his career. George served in the [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division) during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), commanded the [173rd Airborne Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/173rd_Airborne_Brigade) during the [Iraq War](5043324.md), and commanded the [4th Brigade Combat Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Brigade_Combat_Team,_4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) during the [war in Afghanistan](19666611.md). He then held staff positions on the [Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Staff_\(United_States\)) and the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff) before commanding the [4th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) from 2017 to 2019, during which time he deployed to Afghanistan again. George was later the commanding general of [I Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Corps_\(United_States\)) from 2020 to 2021.

As the Army chief of staff, he oversaw the implementation of several measures related to the [Army Transformation Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Transformation_Initiative).

## Early life and education

Born on 1 November 1964[^cite_note-1] and raised in [Alden, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alden,_Iowa), Randy Alan George is the son of Robert and Lorraine George.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Neighbors-3] He served as an enlisted soldier in the [United States Army](32087.md) from 1982 before beginning attendance at the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) at [West Point, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point,_New_York), in 1984.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] George graduated with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree in engineering in 1988.[^cite_note-Neighbors-3][^cite_note-4ID-6] He later received a master's degree in economics from the [Colorado School of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_School_of_Mines) and a master's in international security studies from the [Naval War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_War_College),[^cite_note-Army_bio-7] and is also a graduate of the [United States Army Command and General Staff College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Command_and_General_Staff_College).[^cite_note-Army-8]

## Army career

George commissioned from the U.S. Military Academy in 1988 as an [infantry officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Branch_\(United_States\)). He served as a lieutenant in the [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division) and deployed in support of [Desert Shield/Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm).[^cite_note-Army-8] He had the roles of platoon leader, company executive officer (Desert Shield/Storm), scout platoon leader (3d Battalion, [327th Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/327th_Infantry_Regiment_\(United_States\))), aide-de-camp and battalion S3-Air (3d Battalion, [187th Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/187th_Infantry_Regiment_\(United_States\))).[^cite_note-Army_bio-7]

Following the Armor Officer Advanced Course in 1993, George was stationed at [Fort Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Carson) where he was the assistant operations officer for 3rd Brigade, [4th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) and then commanded C Company and later Headquarters Company in 1st Battalion, [8th Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Infantry_Regiment_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-Army-8] After that assignment he attended the Colorado School of Mines, and later held a position at the Tactical Directorate, National Simulation Center, [Fort Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Leavenworth).[^cite_note-4ID-6]

After attending the [Army Command and General Staff College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Command_and_General_Staff_College) at Fort Leavenworth,[^cite_note-Army_bio-7] in 2001 George went to [Italy](14532.md) and served as the executive officer of 2nd Battalion, [503rd Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/503rd_Infantry_Regiment_\(United_States\)), [173rd Airborne Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/173rd_Airborne_Brigade). He was later executive officer and then deputy commander of the brigade, during which time he deployed in support of [Operation Iraqi Freedom](201936.md)[^cite_note-Army-8] in 2003, in [Kirkuk](199292.md), [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-Army_bio-7]

Returning to the 101st Airborne Division in 2004, George commanded 1st Battalion, 187th Infantry Regiment and deployed a second time to Iraq[^cite_note-Army-8] from 2005 to 2006.[^cite_note-Army_bio-7] He then went to United States [Naval War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_War_College) as an instructor of joint military operations and then as a student.[^cite_note-Army-8][^cite_note-4ID-6] He deployed again as part of the initiatives group for the commanding general, [Multi-National Corps-Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Corps_%E2%80%93_Iraq) in 2007,[^cite_note-Army-8] in [Baghdad](4492.md).[^cite_note-Army_bio-7]

In 2008 George returned to the 4th Infantry Division, where he commanded [4th Brigade Combat Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Brigade_Combat_Team,_4th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) and deployed to [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) in support of [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom)[^cite_note-Army-8] from 2009 to 2010.[^cite_note-Army_bio-7] Following command, he was a fellow on the [Council of Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Foreign_Relations), chief of the strategic policy division for the Pakistan-Afghanistan coordination cell on the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff#Joint_Staff), executive officer to the 33rd [Vice Chief of Staff of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Army), General [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin), and then executive assistant to the commander of [U.S. Central Command](338482.md),[^cite_note-Army_bio-7] the latter starting in March 2013.[^cite_note-4ID-6] He then returned to [Fort Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Carson) as the deputy commanding general (maneuver) of 4th Infantry Division[^cite_note-Army-8] in July 2014.[^cite_note-4ID-6][^cite_note-GOMO-9]

After two staff assignments as the director of force management for the Army (G-3/5/7) from May 2015 to June 2016, and deputy director for regional operations and force management on the Joint Staff (J-3) from June 2016 to August 2017, George took command of the 4th Infantry Division.[^cite_note-Army-8][^cite_note-GOMO-9] In this role he deployed again to Afghanistan[^cite_note-Army-8] for nine months, where he served as deputy chief of staff for operations of the [Resolute Support Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission).[^cite_note-Army_bio-7] After relinquishing command in October 2019, he was briefly a special assistant to the vice chief of staff of the Army.[^cite_note-GOMO-9]

George's most recent command was of [I Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Corps_\(United_States\)) at [Joint Base Lewis McChord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Base_Lewis_McChord), taking command in February 2020 and serving in that role during the [COVID-19 pandemic in the United States](63136490.md).[^cite_note-Army-8] In May 2021, George took up the post of [Senior Military Assistant to the Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Military_Assistant_to_the_Secretary_of_Defense) under Secretary Lloyd Austin.[^cite_note-10] On 5 August 2022, he assumed the duties of the vice chief of staff of the United States Army.[^cite_note-11]

### Chief of staff



George is sworn in as the 41st chief of staff of the Army on 21 September 2023.

He served in an acting capacity as the [chief of staff of the United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_staff_of_the_United_States_Army) from 4 August 2023.[^cite_note-12] George's nomination to be Army chief of staff was among those delayed by Senator [Tommy Tuberville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Tuberville)'s [hold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_hold) over opposition to the Defense Department's [abortion policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-13] Having been confirmed via standalone vote on 21 September 2023, George was sworn in on the same day via phone by [Secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Army) [Christine Wormuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Wormuth) while visiting soldiers of the [11th Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airborne_Division) in [Alaska](624.md).[^cite_note-Sword-14][^cite_note-15]

After becoming the chief of staff he said his top priorities were preparing the Army to fight future conflicts, strengthening the Army profession, and improving recruitment.[^cite_note-16] Initiatives undertaken for the former include improving the Army's ability to counter [drone warfare](38212231.md), increasing the range and accuracy of long-range weapon systems, and ensuring that the U.S. industrial base can meet the needs of the Army.[^cite_note-17] In 2024, he decided to cut 5% of the general officer positions in the Army by declining to fill 12 of the total of 219 positions in the next several years, which were deemed "non-essential."[^cite_note-18] At Army headquarters, it was decided to reduce its size by 1,000 personnel.[^cite_note-Breaking-19][^cite_note-Letter-20] George also led the Army out of one of the worst recruiting crises in its history in 2024.[^cite_note-NYT-2026-21]

On 1 May 2025, George and Army Secretary [Daniel Driscoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_P._Driscoll) announced the [Army Transformation Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Transformation_Initiative), a project to restructure the Army, reduce inefficiency, and quickly incorporate new technologies, as part of a larger effort consistent with a directive from Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md).[^cite_note-Letter-20][^cite_note-22] Described as the Army's biggest restructuring in a generation, among its changes are the creation of transformation-in-contact brigades and plans to merge several existing [Army commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_commands_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The first phase of the initiative took place during 2024, when three Army brigades started experimentation with new technology and structures as transformation-in-contact brigades.[^cite_note-25] This was intended to assist the Army's adoption of drones and other systems that have been widely used in the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md). George also ended the Army's acquisition of the [M10 Booker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M10_Booker), after it was determined to be too easy to destroy with drones, and accelerated the development of the [M1E3 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_Abrams#M1E3) and the acquisition of the [Infantry Squad Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1301_infantry_squad_vehicle).[^cite_note-NYT-2026-21]



George at the [Tomb of the Unknown Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier_\(Arlington_National_Cemetery\)) in Arlington, Virginia, in March 2024.

George was involved in the planning of the [U.S. Army 250th Anniversary Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_250th_Anniversary_Parade), held on 14 June 2025.[^cite_note-26] In October 2025, the Army combined [Training and Doctrine Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Training_and_Doctrine_Command) with [Futures Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Futures_Command), to create [Army Transformation and Training Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Transformation_and_Training_Command). George also announced that [Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Forces_Command) will be merged with [U.S. Army North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_North) and [U.S. Army South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_South) to form a Western Hemisphere Command before the end of the year, as an effort to reduce the size of headquarters.[^cite_note-Breaking-19]

In November 2025, George was part of a delegation of senior Army officials that were led by Secretary [Daniel Driscoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_P._Driscoll) to [Ukraine](31750.md), where they met with Ukrainian leaders to discuss the [peace negotiations in the Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_negotiations_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war).[^cite_note-27] On 5 December 2025 he spoke at the activation ceremony of the [United States Army Western Hemisphere Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Western_Hemisphere_Command), where he passed the colors to its first commanding general.[^cite_note-28] After the start of the [United States war against Iran](82533582.md) on 28 February 2026, George worked to get additional personnel and air defense assets to the Middle East.[^cite_note-29]

On 2 April 2026, Hegseth asked George to leave his post and retire a year and half before the completion of what is typically a four-year term. George's retirement was "effective immediately", according to a Pentagon spokesman.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] His official retirement ceremony was held on 24 April.[^cite_note-32] Hegseth's decision was reportedly due to George's close partnership with Army Secretary Driscoll and disagreements over the promotion of senior Army officers.[^cite_note-NYT-2026-21][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The vice chief, [Christopher LaNeve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_LaNeve), became acting chief of staff after George's removal.[^cite_note-35] Sources familiar with the matter told *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* that days before his firing, George was troubled by a discussion with LaNeve, who had relayed that Hegseth wanted George stop the military’s procedural investigation into an unauthorized helicopter flyby of [Kid Rock's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kid_Rock) home.[^cite_note-36]
