**Buryatia**,[^cite_note-10] officially the **Republic of Buryatia**,[^cite_note-11] is a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) of [Russia](25391.md) located in the [Russian Far East](173135.md). Formerly part of the [Siberian Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Federal_District), it has been administered as part of the [Far Eastern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District) since 2018. To its north lie [Irkutsk Oblast](79477.md) and [Lake Baikal](56865.md), the deepest lake in the world; [Zabaykalsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabaykalsky_Krai) to the east; [Tuva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuva) to the west and [Mongolia](19271.md) to the south.[^cite_note-12] Its [capital](181337.md) is the city of [Ulan-Ude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulan-Ude). It has an area of 351,300 square kilometers (135,600 sq mi) with a population of 978,588 ([2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2021\))).[^cite_note-2021Census-5] It is home to the indigenous [Buryats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryats).

### Mountains

Over 80% of the republic's territory is located in the mountainous region, including the [Baikal Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal_Mountains) on the northern shores of [Lake Baikal](56865.md), the [Ulan-Burgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulan-Burgas) east of the lake, and the [Selenga Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selenga_Highlands) in the south near the [Mongolia–Russia border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia%E2%80%93Russia_border).

### Natural resources

The republic's natural resources include [gold](12240.md), [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten), [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [uranium](31743.md), and more.

## History



Unusual blue [diopsidite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diopside) [skarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skarn) from the Dovyren Highlands, Buryatia

[Mongolian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) have lived in the area around Lake Baikal since the fifth century, with [Mongolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolic_peoples)-related [Slab Grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slab_Grave_Culture) cultural monuments found in the Baikal region.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-14] Over time, the Mongolic peoples of the region developed into distinct groups, one of which became the Buryats. Further divisions arose between those Buryats living on the western shore of Lake Baikal – which had better land for agriculture – and those in the east, who practised [nomadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) more regularly and continued to reside in moveable felt [yurts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yurt). As a result of the superior farmland, the western side of Lake Baikal was settled by European peasants during the time of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire); consequently, western Buryats were more exposed to, and influenced by, the culture, religions and economy of their European neighbours, whereas eastern Buryats maintained closer ties to other Mongolic peoples, Buddhism and Asian civilisations.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

The territory of Buryatia has been governed by the [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu) Empire (209 BC – 93 AD), the Mongolian [Xianbei state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianbei_state) (93–234), the [Rouran Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouran_Khaganate) (330–555), the [First Turkic Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Turkic_Khaganate) (552–603), the [Eastern Turkic Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Turkic_Khaganate) (682–744), the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) (647–784), the [Uyghur Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_Khaganate) (744–840), the [Yenisei Kyrgyz Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenisei_Kyrgyz_Khaganate) (840–1208), the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) (1206–1368) and the [Northern Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Yuan) (1368–1635).[^cite_note-HM-15] Medieval Mongol tribes such as the [Merkit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkit), [Bayads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayads), [Barga Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barga_Mongols) and [Tümeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumed) inhabited Buryatia.[^cite_note-HM-15]

### Imperial Russia

[Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) and other tsarist officials began moving eastward into the western Buryat lands in 1625, where they estimated that 30,000 Buryats were living in south‑eastern Siberia, collecting tribute from other small Siberian tribes.[^cite_note-\:1-13] The Buryats resisted incorporation into the Russian Empire's tribute system (*[yasak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasak)*), which demanded a yearly supply of [furs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade); it was not until the 1680s that the last of the eastern Buryat lands were forced to participate in the yasak system. In 1666, the fort of Udinskoye was founded. This area later became known as Verkhneudinsk – in 1934 it was renamed Ulan‑Ude, the present‑day capital of Buryatia.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

From 1727, the town of Kyakhta served as the border crossing for the [Kyakhta trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyakhta_trade) between Russia and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China).[^cite_note-EB1911-17] Kyakhta's founder, the [Serb](59512.md) [Sava Vladislavich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava_Vladislavich), established it as a trading post between Russia and the [Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty).[^cite_note-Founded-18] The reforms of [Mikhail Speransky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Speransky) in 1820 established indirect rule over Buryatia by codifying local clan leaders as official members of the "steppe [duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duma)", thereby incorporating them into the existing imperial government.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

Buddhism was recognised as an official religion of the Russian Empire by [Empress Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Russia) in 1741, and the first [Pandito Khambo Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandito_Khambo_Lama?action=edit\&redlink=1) – the spiritual leader of the Buryat Buddhists – was elected in 1764. The first person to serve in this role was [Damba Dorzha Zaiaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damba_Dorzha_Zaiaev?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1711–1776). At the time of the [Bolshevik Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), [Dashi-Dorzho Itigilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashi-Dorzho_Itigilov) served as the 12th Pandito Khambo [Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lama) of Eastern Siberia from 1911 to 1917.[^cite_note-\:0-16] Itigilov stepped down in 1917 at the time of the revolution and later encouraged his students to flee to Mongolia, though he refused to flee himself.

### Soviet Buryatia

National movements, including that of Buryatia, began to foment after the [February Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_Revolution) in 1917. From March 1917, the leading Buryat [intelligentsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsia) organized a number of conferences in cities such as [Petrograd](24320051.md), [Chita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chita,_Zabaykalsky_Krai), [Irkutsk](56872.md), and Verkhneudinsk (present-day Ulan-Ude) and invited representatives from Buryat administrative districts of the [Irkutsk](79477.md) and [Transbaikalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transbaikal) regions. The culmination of these conferences was the first All-Buryat Congress in 23–25 April 1917 in Chita, where activists advocated for a self-governing Buryat Autonomous Region, based on the models of [Poland](22936.md) and [Finland](10577.md), with an elected body, the Buryat National Duma, that all Buryats, men and women, over the age of 18 and without criminal convictions, would participate in. This Duma would elect a permanent executive body, the Buryat National committee, which would take on responsibilities such as organizing the elections, assembling the Buryat Duma, and publishing works in the Buryat language.[^cite_note-\:2-19] Among other topics discussed at the Congress were the establishment of an Education Council to create Buryat schools, trained educators, and curricula that included the history of the Buryats and Mongols and Buryat studies.

After the November Revolution in 1917, the Buryats bid for independence was complicated by the arrival of a [Japanese expeditionary force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_intervention_in_Siberia) into Buryatia in 1918.[^cite_note-\:2-19] The Buryat national leaders saw the Japanese as potential and critical allies in assisting the independence movement, but the cooperation ultimately failed due to the conflicting agendas. [The Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) advanced in Buryatia in 1920 and continued to [Outer Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Mongolia) in 1921. Attracted to the promises of [self-determination](29269.md) and territorial autonomy by the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks), and having lost the cooperation of the Japanese, the Buryat leaders embraced the idea of building a Buryat nation with the new Soviet state. In 1923, the [Buryat Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) ([Buryat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_language): Буряадай Автономито Совет Социалис Республика; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Бурятская Автономная Советская Социалистическая Республика) was created as a result of the merger of [State of Buryat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Buryat-Mongolia) and [Buryat Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_Oblast?action=edit\&redlink=1) and promised territorial autonomy. In 1929, a [revolt was suppressed in Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Buryat_Revolt), caused by collectivisation and repression of [Buddhism](3267529.md). In 1937, [Aga Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aga_Buryatia) and [Ust-Orda Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ust-Orda_Buryatia) were detached from the [Buryat-Mongolian ASSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat-Mongolian_ASSR) and merged with [Chita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chita_Oblast) and [Irkutsk Oblasts](79477.md), respectively. In 1958, the name "Mongol" was removed from the name of the republic and simply became the [Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic of Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) within the vast multi-ethnic, diverse Soviet Union.

The [Ivolginsky Datsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivolginsky_Datsan) was opened in 1945 as the only Buddhist spiritual centre of the USSR, home to the [Central Spiritual Board of Buddhists of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Spiritual_Board_of_Buddhists_of_the_USSR), the state-controlled [sangha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangha).

The Buryat intelligentsia were active throughout Buryatia and beyond, into [Tibet](31516.md) and Mongolia. At the turn of the 20th century, Buryats leaders, such as [Batu-dalai Ochirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu-dalai_Ochirov?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Mikhail Bogdanov, began actively writing political articles about the threat to Buryatia and Buryat existence from Russia. Despite their noted influence from 1900 to 1930, most of them were [purged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purges_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union), killed outright or sent to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment), in the 1930s.[^cite_note-20]

The leader of the Buryat ASSR from 1962 to 1984 was [Andrei Urupkheevich Modogoev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Urupkheevich_Modogoev?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-21] In the 1970s, Soviet authorities began two major industrial projects in Buryatia: the [Gusinoozerskii power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusinoozerskii_power_station?action=edit\&redlink=1) to the south of Ulan-Ude and the construction of the [Baikal–Amur Mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal%E2%80%93Amur_Mainline) railway in northern Buryatia. The construction of both projects, particularly the railway, required recruiting campaigns to bring workers from other parts of the country to Buryatia. Towns developed along the railroad, and the urban population in northern Buryatia doubled between 1979 and 1989.[^cite_note-\:1-13] In addition to the Russians who moved to Buryatia for work, Buryats from other parts of southern Siberia also migrated to the Buryat ASSR, particularly Ulan-Ude and other cities for jobs and educational opportunities. Prior to World War II, less than 10% of Buryats lived in urban areas, compared to almost half at the time of the fall of the Soviet Union. By 1989, one-third of the Buryat population of the Buryat ASSR was living in Ulan-Ude.

### Post-Soviet Buryatia

The Buryat ASSR declared its [sovereignty](28791.md) in 1990 and adopted the name *Republic of Buryatia* in 1992. However, it remained an autonomous republic within the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation). On 11 July 1995 Buryatia signed a power-sharing agreement with the federal government, granting it autonomy.[^cite_note-asymmetries-22] This agreement was abolished on 15 February 2002.[^cite_note-demokratiztsiya-23]

Following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), [autonomous republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_republic) such as Buryatia did not have the right to secede. However they retained considerable [autonomy](191145.md), with a separate legislature and president. However this autonomy has been curtailed following the 2004 law passed by [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) that decreed regional governors and presidents were to be appointed, rather than directly elected.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

## Politics



Modern Buryat home with musical instruments, scrolls, and weapons typical of Buryatia

The head of the republic is the [Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Republic_of_Buryatia) (formerly President), who is elected by popular vote for a four‑year term. Between 2004 and 2012, the head of Buryatia—like all other regional heads in Russia—was appointed directly by the [Russian president](239646.md).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

From 1991 to 2007, the post was held by [Leonid Vasilyevich Potapov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Potapov). He was first elected on 1 July 1994, re‑elected in 1998 with 63.25 per cent of the vote, and elected once more on 23 June 2002, securing over 67 per cent. Before the introduction of direct elections, Potapov served as Chairman of the Supreme Soviet of the Republic, at that time the highest office in the region.

The present Head of the Republic is [Alexey Tsydenov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Tsydenov), who was elected by popular vote on 10 September 2017. He had been appointed acting Head in February 2017 by the Russian president, [Vladimir Putin](32817.md).[^cite_note-26]

The republic's parliament is the [People's Khural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Khural_of_the_Republic_of_Buryatia), whose 66 deputies are elected every five years. The assembly is currently controlled by the country's ruling party, [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia), which holds 45 seats. [Vladimir Anatolyevich Pavlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Pavlov_\(politician,_born_1956\)) has served as Chairman of the People's Khural since September 2019.

The [Republic's Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_Buryatia?action=edit\&redlink=1) was adopted on 22 February 1994.

In the [2024 Russian presidential election](56748201.md), which independent observers and opposition figures described as rigged and fraudulent, President Vladimir Putin won 87.96 per cent of the vote in Buryatia.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

[Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) at a [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple) in [Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Russia)

Traditionally, Buryats adhered to belief systems that were based on the deification of nature, belief in spirits, and the possibility of their magic influence on the surroundings. They were led by [shamans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_shamanism), who systematized tribal beliefs and cults. From the second half of the 17th century, beliefs and cults in the shamanic form were displaced by [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism), which became widespread in ethnic Buryatia. By the end of the 19th century, the majority of Buryats were part of the Tibetan Buddhist tradition. A synthesis of Tibetan Buddhism and traditional beliefs that formed a system of ecological traditions has constituted a major attribute of Buryat culture.[^cite_note-40] In 2003, the Local Religious Organization of Shamans, Tengeri was officially registered as a religious organization in Buryatia.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

As of a 2012 survey[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-38] 27.4% of the population adheres to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), 19.8% to [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism), 2% to the [Slavic Native Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_Native_Faith), [Tengrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengrism) or Buryat [shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism), 4% declares to be [unaffiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Christianity) [Christian](5211.md) (excluding Protestants), 1% are Orthodox Christian believers without belonging to churches or are members of other [Orthodox churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), 1% are members of [Protestant churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism). In addition, 25% of the population declares to be "[spiritual but not religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_but_not_religious)", 13% to be [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), and 10.8% follows another religion or did not give an answer to the survey.[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-38]

[Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) and [Orthodox Christianity](10186.md) are the most widespread religions in the republic. Many Slavs, who constitute around 67% of the population, are Russian Orthodox. Since the breakup of the USSR in 1991, a small number have converted to various Protestant denominations or to [Rodnovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodnovery), also known as the Slavic native faith. There are also some [Catholics](606848.md) among the Slavs. Most of the [Germans](152735.md) (0.11% of the population) are also Orthodox, so are some other non-European groups like [Armenians](387816.md) (0.23%), [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians) (0.03%), and [Soyot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyot) (0.37%). Buryats constitute 30.04% of the total population.

Most urban Buryats are either Buddhist or Orthodox, while those in the rural areas often adhere to [Yellow shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_shamanism), a mixture of shamanism and Buddhism, or to [Black shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_shamanism).[^cite_note-41] There are also [Tengrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengrism) movements. [Siberian Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Tatars) are around 0.7% of the population. However, due to isolation from the main body of Tatars, many of them now are either non-religious or Orthodox. [Islam](6037917.md) is followed by immigrant groups like [Azeris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeris) and [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks), who constitute another 0.7% of the population.

### Education

The higher education institutions of the republic include [Buryat State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_State_University), [Buryat State Academy of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_State_Academy_of_Agriculture), [East Siberian State Academy of Arts and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Siberian_State_Academy_of_Arts_and_Culture?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [East Siberia State University of Technology and Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Siberia_State_University_of_Technology_and_Management).

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Buryatia)

The republic's economy is composed of agricultural and commercial products including [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable), [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber), [leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather), [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite), and [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile). [Fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing), [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting), [fur farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_farming), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_farming) and [cattle farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle-farming), [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), stock raising, [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), and [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing) are also important economic generators. The unemployment rate of Buryatia was 11% in 2020.[^cite_note-42]

GDP per person nominal in 2018 was 3,650 USD[^cite_note-43] and PPP in 2009 was 11,148 USD.[^cite_note-44]
