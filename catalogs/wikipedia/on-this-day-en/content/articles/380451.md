**Chittagong** ([/ˈtʃɪtəɡɒŋ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*CHIT-ə-gong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-dictionary1-7] [officially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_renamed_places_in_Bangladesh) **Chattogram**[^cite_note-dt2Apr2018-8] ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): চট্টগ্রাম, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Bengali): *Côṭṭôgrām*, IPA: [\[ˈt͡ʃɔʈːoɡram\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bengali) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5d/LL-Q9610\_%28ben%29-Yahya-%E0%A6%9A%E0%A6%9F%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9F%E0%A6%97%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE.wav/LL-Q9610\_%28ben%29-Yahya-%E0%A6%9A%E0%A6%9F%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9F%E0%A6%97%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5d/LL-Q9610_%28ben%29-Yahya-%E0%A6%9A%E0%A6%9F%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9F%E0%A6%97%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE.wav/LL-Q9610_%28ben%29-Yahya-%E0%A6%9A%E0%A6%9F%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9F%E0%A6%97%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, traditionally [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): চাটগাঁও, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Bengali): *Cāṭgão*; [Chittagonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagonian_language): চিটাং, romanized: *Sitang*), is the capital and largest city of the [Chittagong Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Division) and [Chittagong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_District). It is the [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Bangladesh) in [Bangladesh](3454.md) after the capital, Dhaka, and the largest city in the [Greater Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Chittagong) region, with a land area of 157 square kilometers.[^cite_note-9] Home to the [Port of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Chittagong), it is the busiest port in Bangladesh and the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md).[^cite_note-thedailystar.net-10] The city is also the business capital of Bangladesh. The city is located on the banks of the [Karnaphuli River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnaphuli_River) between the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts) and the Bay of Bengal. In 2022, the Chittagong District had a population of approximately 9.2 million according to a census conducted by the government of Bangladesh.[^cite_note-11] In 2022, the city area had a population of more than 5.6 million.[^cite_note-12] The city is an important export hub, with goods such as tea, jute, and petroleum products being shipped through its port.[^cite_note-13] The city is home to many large local businesses and plays an important role in the [Bangladeshi economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Bangladesh).

One of the world's oldest ports with a functional [natural harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_harbour) for centuries,[^cite_note-auto1-14] Chittagong appeared on ancient [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) maps, including on [Ptolemy's world map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy's_world_map). It was located on the southern branch of the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road). In the 9th century, merchants from the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) established a trading post in Chittagong.[^cite_note-bp-arabs-15][^cite_note-16] The port fell to the Muslim conquest of Bengal during the 14th century. It was the site of a [royal mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_\(facility\)) under the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate), [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate) and [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire).[^cite_note-17] Between the 15th and 17th centuries, Chittagong was also a centre of administrative, literary, commercial and maritime activities in [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan), a narrow strip of land along the eastern coast of the Bay of Bengal which was under strong Bengali influence for 350 years. During the 16th century, the port became a [Portuguese trading post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_settlement_in_Chittagong) and [João de Barros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_de_Barros) described it as "the most famous and wealthy city of the Kingdom of Bengal".[^cite_note-18] The Mughal Empire expelled the Portuguese and Arakanese in 1666.

The [Nawab of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab_of_Bengal) ceded the port to the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) in 1793. The Port of Chittagong was re-organized in 1887 and its busiest shipping links were with [British Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Burma). In 1928, Chittagong was declared a "Major Port" of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India). During [World War II](32927.md), Chittagong was a base for [Allied Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) engaged in the [Burma Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Campaign). The port city began to expand and industrialize during the 1940s, particularly after the [Partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_British_India). The city was the historic terminus of the [Assam Bengal Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Bengal_Railway) and [Pakistan Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Eastern_Railway). During the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) in 1971, Chittagong was the site of the [Bangladeshi declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_declaration_of_independence). The port city has benefited from the growth of [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry), logistics, and manufacturing in Bangladesh. Trade unionism was strong during the 1990s.

Chittagong accounts for 12% of Bangladesh's GDP, including 40% of industrial output, 80% of [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade), and 50% of [tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax) revenue. The port city is home to many of the oldest and largest companies in the country. The Port of Chittagong is one of the busiest ports in [South Asia](21566765.md). The [largest base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNS_Issa_Khan) of the [Bangladesh Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Navy) is located in Chittagong, along with an air base of the [Bangladesh Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Air_Force), garrisons of the [Bangladesh Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Army) and the main base of the [Bangladesh Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Coast_Guard). The eastern zone of the [Bangladesh Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Railway) is based in Chittagong. The [Chittagong Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Stock_Exchange) is one of the twin stock markets of Bangladesh with over 700 listed companies. The [Chittagong Tea Auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Tea_Auction) is a [commodity exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodities_exchange) dealing with [Bangladeshi tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_production_in_Bangladesh). The [CEPZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Export_Processing_Zone) and [KEPZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnaphuli_Export_Processing_Zone) are key industrial zones with foreign direct investments. The city is served by [Shah Amanat International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Amanat_International_Airport) for domestic and external flights. [Karnaphuli Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnaphuli_Tunnel), the first and only [underwater road tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_tunnel) of South Asia, is located in Chittagong. The city is the hometown of prominent economists, Nobel laureate [Muhammad Yunus](548105.md),[^cite_note-19] scientists, freedom fighters and entrepreneurs. Chittagong has a high degree of religious and ethnic diversity among Bangladeshi cities, despite having a great [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) majority. Minorities include [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus), [Christians](18337522.md), [Chakmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people), [Marmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marma_people), [Baruas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barua_people), [Tripuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri_people), [Garos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_people) and others.

## Etymology

Main article: [Names of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Chittagong)

The [etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) of *Chittagong* is uncertain.[^cite_note-gazetteer-20] The port city has been known by various names in history, including *Chatigaon*, *Chatigam*, *Chattagrama*, *Islamabad*, *Chattala*, *Chaityabhumi* and *Porto Grande De Bengala*.[^cite_note-21]

The Bengali word for Chittagong, *Chattogram (চট্টগ্রাম)*, has the suffix "*-gram*" (গ্রাম), meaning "village" in [Standard Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Bengali). The earliest records, before Islam reached the region, state that it was a place of chaitya or Buddhist monasteries. The city had a very large Buddhist population before Islam. The city was renamed *Islamabad (City of Islam)* during the Mughal era. The name continues to be used in the old city. In April 2018, the [Cabinet Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Division) of the [Government of Bangladesh](18420484.md) decided to change the city's name to Chattogram,[^cite_note-dt2Apr2018-8][^cite_note-22] based on its Bengali spelling and pronunciation; the move was criticized in the Bangladeshi media.[^cite_note-23]

One explanation credits the first Arab traders for *Shatt Al Ghangh* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): شط الغنغ) where *shatt* means "Delta" and *ghangh* stood for the Ganges, from that term *Chattala* evolved.[^cite_note-gazetteer-20][^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24][^cite_note-25] The [Arakanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) chronicle that a king named Tsu-la-taing Tsandaya (Sula Taing Chandra), after conquering Bengal, set up a stone pillar as a trophy/memorial at the place since called *Tst-ta-gaung* as the limit of conquest.[^cite_note-A_History_of_Chittagong-26]

## History

Main article: [History of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chittagong)



A Dutch map in 1638 showing Bengal, Chittagong and [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan)



[Dutch VOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) ships in Chittagong, 1702

[Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) fossils and tools unearthed in the region indicate that Chittagong has been inhabited since [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) times.[^cite_note-27] It is an ancient port city, with a recorded history dating back to the 4th century BC.[^cite_note-28] Its harbour was mentioned in [Ptolemy's world map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy's_world_map) in the 2nd century as one of the most impressive ports in the [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_world).[^cite_note-auto1-14] The region was part of the ancient Bengali [Samatata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samatata) and [Harikela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harikela) kingdoms. The [Chandra dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandra_dynasty) once dominated the area and was followed by the [Varman dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varman_dynasty) and [Deva dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_dynasty).

Chinese traveller [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) described the area as "a sleeping beauty rising from mist and water" in the 7th century.[^cite_note-29]

Many [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) missionaries settled in Chittagong and played an instrumental role in the [spread of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam).[^cite_note-\:0-30]

Sultan [Fakhruddin Mubarak Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhruddin_Mubarak_Shah) of [Sonargaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonargaon) conquered Chittagong in 1340,[^cite_note-31] making it a part of Sultanate of Bengal. It was the principal maritime gateway to the kingdom, which was reputed as one of the wealthiest states in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent). Medieval Chittagong was a hub for maritime trade with China, [Sumatra](28679.md), the [Maldives](19117.md), [Sri Lanka](26750.md), the Middle East, and East Africa. It was notable for its medieval trades in pearls,[^cite_note-Beyond_Price-32] silk, muslin, rice, bullion, horses, and gunpowder. The port was also a major [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) hub.

[Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) visited the port city in 1345.[^cite_note-33] [Niccolò de' Conti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_de'_Conti), from Venice, also visited around the same time as Battuta.[^cite_note-Buffetania-34] Chinese admiral [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He)'s [treasure fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_voyages) anchored in Chittagong during imperial missions to the Sultanate of Bengal.[^cite_note-ballads_of_Bengal-35][^cite_note-Eaton-36]

Dhaniya Manikya conquered Chittagong in 1513. Hossain Shah sent his noble commander Gorai Mallik to attack Tripura. Gorai Mallik recaptured the territories lost. But the following year Dhaniya Manikya again conquered Chittagong.[^cite_note-37] In 1516, Husain Shah decided to conquer Chittagong again. Husain assigned the charge of the land army for conquest of Chittagong to his general Paragal Khan; prince Nasrat, who was made crown prince of Bengal in 1515, was placed in overall command.[^cite_note-Muhammad_Abdur_Rahim_1957_48-38] On Nasrat's order, Paragal Khan advanced from his base on the Feni River. The territory up to the western bank of Kaladan river was placed under his governorship's administration. The hostilities probably ended in 1516, when Mrauk U recognized Bengali sovereignty over Chittagong and northern [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan).[^cite_note-বাংলাদেশের_ইতিহাস-39] As a result of the conflict, Mrauk U again became a vassal of the Bengal Sultanate.[^cite_note-বাংলাদেশের_ইতিহাস-39] Nasrat renamed Chittagong to Fatehabad, City of Victory. A locality of Chittagong named Nasirabad is named after him.[^cite_note-40] Bengali conquest of Chittagong is also corroborated by Portuguese adventurer Joao de Silvera who, landing in Chittagong in 1517, stated that Chittagong was a part of Bengal Sultanate.[^cite_note-Muhammad_Abdur_Rahim_1957_48-38]

The [Arakanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) ruled over Chittagong spanned from the late 16th century to 1666, marking a significant yet turbulent era in the region's history. The [Kingdom of Mrauk U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U), centered on the west coast of present-day Myanmar, expanded into south-eastern Bengal, with Chittagong becoming a strategic part of its domain. The Arakanese maintained their power through alliances with the Portuguese, who were instrumental in fortifying their control. Chittagong evolved into a centre of trade and piracy during this time, with Portuguese and Arakanese forces frequently raiding Mughal territories. The blending of Bengali, Buddhist, and Portuguese influences made the region a unique cultural and administrative frontier.[^cite_note-41]

The decline of Arakanese rule was triggered by political conflicts, including their involvement in the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) succession struggle. The assassination of Mughal prince [Shah Shuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Shuja_\(Mughal_prince\)) in Arakan strained relations with the Mughal Empire, prompting a decisive campaign led by [Subahdar Shaista Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaista_Khan) in 1666. The Mughals recaptured Chittagong, ending 70 years circa of Arakanese presence. This period left a lasting legacy on the region, highlighting the interplay of trade, politics, and cultural exchange between Bengal and Arakan.[^cite_note-42]

Chittagong featured prominently in the military history of the Bengal Sultanate, including during the [Reconquest of Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquest_of_Arakan) and the [Bengal Sultanate–Kingdom of Mrauk U War of 1512–1516](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate%E2%80%93Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U_War_of_1512%E2%80%931516).

A ship built in Chittagong near the coast of Hong Kong in 1890

During the 13th and 16th centuries, [Arabs](2185.md) and [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) heavily colonized the port city of Chittagong, initially arriving for trade and to spread [Islam](6037917.md). Most Arab settlers arrived from the trade route between [Iraq](7515928.md) and Chittagong and were perhaps the prime reason for the spread of Islam to [Bangladesh](3454.md).[^cite_note-bp-arabs-15] The first Persian settlers also arrived for trade and religious purposes, with the possible goal of [Persianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persianization) as well. Persians and other [Iranic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranic_peoples) have deeply affected the history of the Bengal Sultanate, with Persian being one of the main languages of the Muslim state, as well as also influencing the [Chittagonian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagonian_language) and writing scripts.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

Two decades after [Vasco Da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_Da_Gama)'s landing in [Calicut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calicut), the Bengal Sultanate permitted the Portuguese settlement in Chittagong to be established in 1528. It became the first European colonial enclave in Bengal. The Bengal Sultanate lost control of Chittagong in 1531 after Arakan declared independence and the established Kingdom of Mrauk U. This altered geopolitical landscape allowed the Portuguese unhindered control of Chittagong for over a century.[^cite_note-45]

Portuguese ships from Goa and [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Malacca) began frequenting the port city in the 16th century. The *[cartaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartaz)* system was introduced and required all ships in the area to purchase naval trading licenses from the Portuguese settlement.[^cite_note-46] [Slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery) and piracy flourished. The nearby island of [Sandwip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwip) was conquered in 1602. In 1615, the [Portuguese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Navy) defeated a joint [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) and Arakanese fleet near the coast of Chittagong.



Colonial architecture in Chittagong

In 1666, the Mughal government of Bengal led by viceroy [Shaista Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaista_Khan) moved to retake Chittagong from Portuguese and Arakanese control by launching the Mughal conquest of Chittagong. The Mughals attacked the Arakanese from the jungle with a 6,500-strong army, which was further supported by 288 Mughal naval ships blockading the Chittagong harbor.[^cite_note-\:0-30] After three days of battle, the [Arakanese](1918632.md) surrendered. The Mughals expelled the Portuguese from Chittagong. Mughal rule ushered a new era in the history of Chittagong territory to the southern bank of Kashyapnadi (Kaladan River). The port city was renamed Islamabad. The Grand Trunk Road connected it with [North India](1552939.md) and Central Asia. Economic growth increased due to an efficient system of land grants for clearing hinterlands for cultivation. The Mughals also contributed to the architecture of the area, including the building of Fort Ander and many mosques. Chittagong was integrated into the prosperous Bengali economy, which also included [Orissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa,_India) and [Bihar](442482.md).[^cite_note-Eaton-36][^cite_note-books.google.com.my-47] Shipbuilding increased dramatically under the Mughal rule, and the Ottoman Sultans had many Ottoman warships built in Chittagong during this period.[^cite_note-48]

In 1685, the British East India Company sent out an expedition under Admiral Nicholson with the instructions to seize and fortify Chittagong on behalf of the English; however, the expedition proved abortive. Two years later, the company's Court of Directors decided to make Chittagong the headquarters of their Bengal trade and sent out a fleet of ten or eleven ships to seize it under Captain Heath. However, after reaching Chittagong in early 1689, the fleet found the city too strongly held and abandoned their attempt at capturing it. The city was possessed by the Nawab of Bengal until 1793 when East India Company took complete control of the former Mughal province of Bengal.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

The [First Anglo-Burmese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Burmese_War) in 1823 threatened the British hold on Chittagong. There were several rebellions against British rule, notably during the [Indian rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rebellion_of_1857), when the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th companies of the 34th [Bengal Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Army) revolted and released all prisoners from the city's jail. In a backlash, the rebels were suppressed by the [Sylhet Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Princess_Mary's_Own_Gurkha_Rifles).[^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24]

[Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan) was annexed in 1826 and incorporated into the [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency).[^cite_note-arakanHistory-51] Agriculturalists from Chittagong played a key role in the development of the [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) economy in Arakan.[^cite_note-52] The economy of northern Arakan was integrated with the Chittagong economy. During this period, [Arakan Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Division) became one of the top rice exporters in the world.[^cite_note-Hartwig1863-53][^cite_note-54] Bengalis from Chittagong were vital to the success of Arakan's rice industry.

Railways were introduced in 1865, beginning with the [Eastern Bengal Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bengal_Railway) connecting Chittagong to [Dacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacca) and [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta). Chittagong became the main gateway to [Eastern Bengal and Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bengal_and_Assam).[^cite_note-55] In the 1890s, Chittagong became the terminus of [Assam Bengal Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Bengal_Railway). The hinterland of Chittagong Port covered the [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) and [jute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jute) producing regions of Assam and Bengal, as well as [Assam's oil industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_oil). Chittagong was also linked to the crucial [oil and gas industry in Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas_industry_in_Burma). Chittagong was a major center of trade with [British Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Burma). It hosted many prominent companies of the British Empire.

The [Chittagong armoury raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_armoury_raid) by Bengali revolutionaries in 1930 was a major event in British India's anti-colonial history.

### World War II



[Royal Air Force](25679.md) Thunderbolts lined up at Chittagong in 1944

During World War II, Chittagong became a frontline city in the [Southeast Asian Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_Asian_theatre_of_World_War_II). It was a critical air, naval and military base for Allied Forces during the [Burma Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Campaign) against Japan. The [Imperial Japanese Army Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army_Air_Force) carried out [air raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing) on Chittagong in April and May 1942, in the run-up to the aborted Japanese invasion of Bengal.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

After the [Battle of Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Imphal), the tide turned in favour of the Allied Forces. Units of the United States Army Air Forces' [4th Combat Cargo Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Combat_Cargo_Group) were stationed in Chittagong Airfield in [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Campaign_1944-1945).[^cite_note-Maurer,_Maurer_1983-58] Commonwealth forces included troops from [Britain](9316.md), [India](14533.md), [Australia](4689264.md), and [New Zealand](4913064.md). The war had major negative impacts on the city, including the growth of refugees and the [Great Famine of 1943](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_famine_of_1943).[^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24] Many wealthy Chittagonians profited from wartime commerce.

715 soldiers are buried at the [Chittagong War Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_War_Cemetery), which is maintained by the [Commonwealth War Graves Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_War_Graves_Commission). Allied soldiers constitute the bulk of burials in the cemetery. A few Japanese soldiers are also buried. Remembrance Day services are held each year at the cemetery, with diplomats from Commonwealth countries like the [UK](31717.md), [Bangladesh](3454.md), Australia, India and [Pakistan](23235.md), as well as the [United States](3434750.md) and [Japan](15573.md), usually in attendance.[^cite_note-59]

### Modern



Jamuna Bhaban on Sheikh Mujib Road was home to a [chamber of commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_commerce) for [British](31717.md) businesses.



Port of Chittagong in 1960

The [Partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_British_India) in 1947 made Chittagong the chief port of [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan). By March 1948, the Chittagong harbour became a bustling port for international shipping. The [Chittagong Tea Auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Tea_Auction) was set up in 1949. The port city had branches of the [Chartered Bank of India, Australia and China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_Bank_of_India,_Australia_and_China), [Burmah Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmah_Oil) (known locally as [Burmah Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Oil_Company)), and the [James Finlay shipping business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Finlay_Bangladesh). Wealthy Muslim families from British India and British Burma shifted their corporate headquarters to Chittagong. The [Ispahani family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ispahani_family) shifted the head office of [M. M. Ispahani Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._M._Ispahani_Limited) from Calcutta to Chittagong.[^cite_note-en.prothomalo.com-60] The Ispahanis also relocated the Eastern Federal Insurance Company from Calcutta to Chittagong.[^cite_note-en.prothomalo.com-60] The Ispahanis set up the Victory Jute Mills, the Chittagong Jute Manufacturing Company, and the Pahartali Textile Mills.[^cite_note-en.prothomalo.com-60] The Africawala brothers set up the first steel re-rolling mills in Chittagong in 1952, which eventually became [BSRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSRM).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] Banks, shipping companies and insurance firms proliferated the city. Many [British](31717.md)-owned businesses in East Pakistan were based in Chittagong. Britain's former flag carrier [BOAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Airways_Corporation) operated flights to the city. The [Agrabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrabad) area emerged as the [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) in the 1950s and 1960s, with many corporate offices. The Ispahani Building and Jamuna Bhaban are some of the corporate buildings from this period. The [Karnaphuli Paper Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnaphuli_Paper_Mills) were built in 1959. The project to build the [Eastern Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Refinery) was started in 1963;[^cite_note-63] and was partly funded by the last [Shah of Iran](13877.md). The Agrabad Chamber of Commerce was formed in 1963. It later became the Foreign Investors' Chamber of Commerce and Industry in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-64] The Chittagong Development Authority (CDA) was created by the government to promote urban planning; while wealthy families like the Ispahanis contributed to social welfare by setting up schools and hospitals.[^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24]

The lawyer and industrialist [A K Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Kashem_Khan), who set up [A K Khan & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_K_Khan_&_Company) in the aftermath of World War II, represented Chittagong in the federal cabinet of East and West Pakistan. However, East Pakistanis complained of a lack of investment in Chittagong in comparison to [Karachi](17123.md) in [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan), even though East Pakistan generated more exports and had a larger population. The [Awami League](404861.md) demanded that the country's naval headquarters be shifted from Karachi to Chittagong.[^cite_note-daily-sun.com-65]

During the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) in 1971, which was waged under the leadership of [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman), Chittagong witnessed heavy fighting between rebel Bengali military regiments and the Pakistan Army. It covered [Sector 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sectors_in_Bangladesh_Liberation_War) in the [Mukti Bahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini) chain of command. Major [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman) was the sector commander. The Bangladeshi Declaration of Independence was broadcast from [Kalurghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalurghat) Radio Station and transmitted internationally through foreign ships in Chittagong Port.[^cite_note-66] Ziaur Rahman and [M A Hannan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M_A_Hannan) announced the independence declaration from Chittagong. A K Khan drafted the English version of Zia's broadcast.[^cite_note-67] These radio broadcasts began the journey of [Swadhin Bangla Betar Kendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swadhin_Bangla_Betar_Kendra), which contributed heavily towards the Liberation. The Pakistani military, and supporting [Razakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razakar) militias, carried out widespread atrocities against civilians in the city. Mukti Bahini naval commandos drowned several Pakistani warships during Operation Jackpot in August 1971.[^cite_note-68] In December 1971, the [Bangladesh Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Air_Force) and the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) carried out the heavy bombing of facilities occupied by the Pakistani military. A [naval blockade](293054.md) was also enforced.[^cite_note-69]

After the war, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) offer to clear mines in Chittagong Port at free of cost, while [Sweden](5058739.md) offered to clear mines in [Mongla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Mongla) port.[^cite_note-70] 22 vessels of the [Soviet Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Pacific_Fleet) sailed from [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) to Chittagong in May 1972.[^cite_note-71] The process of clearing mines in the dense water harbor took nearly a year and claimed the life of Soviet marine Yuri V Redkin.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Chittagong soon regained its status as a major port, with cargo tonnage surpassing pre-war levels in 1973. In the immediate aftermath of 1971, many industries were nationalized. But in Chittagong, factories and business properties were given back to their private owners. The Ispahani family had to write only one letter in order to get back all their properties from the [Awami League](404861.md) government of Prime Minister Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.[^cite_note-en.prothomalo.com-60]

In [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) reforms launched by President Ziaur Rahman in the late 1970s, the city became home to the first [export processing zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export_processing_zone) in Bangladesh. Zia was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ziaur_Rahman) during an attempted military coup in Chittagong in 1981. The [1991 Bangladesh cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Bangladesh_cyclone) inflicted heavy damage on the city. The Japanese government financed the construction of several heavy industries and an international airport in the 1980s and 1990s. Bangladeshi [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector) investments increased since 1991, especially with the formation of the Chittagong Stock Exchange in 1995. A new airport opened in 2000. The port city has been the pivot of Bangladesh's [emerging economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_economy) in recent years, with the country's rising GDP growth rate. Chittagong has seen several infrastructure projects taken up by the government of Prime Minister [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md), including the Chittagong Elevated Expressway, the first underwater tunnel in South Asia, the expansion of its port, and new parks, power plants and flyovers.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

## Geography

### Topography



Mohammad Yusuf Chowdhury Road in the Tigerpass area, an example of the city's hilly landscape

Chittagong lies at [22°20′06″N 91°49′57″E  /  22.33500°N 91.83250°E  / 22.33500; 91.83250](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Chittagong\&params=22_20_06_N_91_49_57_E_type\:city_region\:BD). It straddles the coastal foothills of the Chittagong Hill Tracts in south-eastern Bangladesh. The [Karnaphuli River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnaphuli) runs along the southern banks of the city, including its central business district. The river enters the Bay of Bengal in an [estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) located 12 kilometres (7.5 mi) west of downtown Chittagong. Mount Sitakunda is the highest peak in Chittagong District, with an elevation of 351 metres (1,152 ft).[^cite_note-muhammadyunus.org-76] Within the city itself, the highest peak is Batali Hill at 85.3 metres (280 ft). Chittagong has many lakes that were created under the Mughal rule. In 1924, an engineering team of the Assam Bengal Railway established the [Foy's Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foy's_Lake).[^cite_note-muhammadyunus.org-76]

Major sediment outflows from the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) (or [Padma](1538264.md)) and [Brahmaputra rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River) form tidal flats around the city.[^cite_note-77]

### Ecological hinterland

The Chittagong Division is known for its rich [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity). Over 2000 of Bangladesh's 6000 flowering plants grow in the region.[^cite_note-78] Its hills and jungles are laden with [waterfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfalls), fast flowing river streams and elephant reserves. [St. Martin's Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Martin's_Island), within the Chittagong Division, is the only [coral island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_island) in the country. The fishing port of [Cox's Bazar](2271204.md) is home to one of the world's longest natural beaches. In the east, there are the three hill districts of [Bandarban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandarban_District), [Rangamati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangamati_District), and [Khagrachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khagrachari_District), home to the [highest mountains in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_Bangladesh). The region has numerous protected areas, including the [Teknaf Game Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teknaf_Game_Reserve) and the [Sitakunda Botanical Garden and Eco Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitakunda_Botanical_Garden_and_Eco_Park).[^cite_note-79]

[Patenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patenga) beach in the main seafront of Chittagong, located 14 kilometres (8.7 mi) west of the city.

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Chittagong has a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) (Am).[^cite_note-Peel-80]

Chittagong is vulnerable to [North Indian Ocean tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Indian_Ocean_tropical_cyclone). The deadliest tropical cyclone to strike Chittagong was the [1991 Bangladesh cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Bangladesh_cyclone), which killed 138,000 people and left as many as 10 million homeless.[^cite_note-NOAA-81]

## Government



Panorama of the [Chittagong Court Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Court_Building) on Court Hill (also known as "Porir Pahar" [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): পরীর পাহাড়, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Fairy hill')



The British-era [Central Railway Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Railway_Building) was the headquarters of the Assam Bengal Railway.



[Zia Memorial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_Memorial_Museum) was formerly the Circuit House

The [Chittagong City Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_City_Corporation) (CCC) is responsible for governing municipal areas in the Chittagong Metropolitan Area. It is headed by the mayor of Chittagong. The mayor and ward councillors are elected every five years. The mayor is [Shahadat Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahadat_Hossain_\(Chittagong_politician\)), as of December 2024.[^cite_note-86] The city corporation's mandate is limited to basic civic services, however, the CCC is credited for keeping Chittagong one of the cleaner and most eco-friendly cities in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Its principal sources of revenue are municipal taxes and conservancy charges.[^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24] The [Chittagong Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Development_Authority) is responsible for implementing the city's urban planning.

The [deputy commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_commissioner) and [district magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Commissioner_\(Bangladesh\)) are the chiefs of local administration as part of the Government of Bangladesh. Law enforcement is provided by the [Chittagong Metropolitan Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Metropolitan_Police) and the [Rapid Action Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Action_Battalion)-7. The district and sessions judges are the heads of the local [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) on behalf of the [Supreme Court of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh).[^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24] The Divisional Special Judge's Court is located in the colonial-era Chittagong Court Building.

### Military

Chittagong is a strategically important military port on the Bay of Bengal. The [Chittagong Naval Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNS_Issa_Khan) is the principal base of the Bangladesh Navy and the home port of most [Bangladeshi warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_ships_of_the_Bangladesh_Navy).[^cite_note-89] The [Bangladesh Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Naval_Academy) and the navy's elite special force- [Special Warfare Diving and Salvage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Warfare_Diving_and_Salvage) (SWADS) are also based in the city.[^cite_note-90] The [Bangladesh Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Army)'s 24th Infantry Division is based in [Chittagong Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Cantonment), and the Bangladesh Air Force maintains the BAF Zahurul Haq Air Base[^cite_note-91] in Chittagong.[^cite_note-92] The city is also home to the [Bangladesh Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Military_Academy), the premier training institute for the country's [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Armed_Forces).

### Diplomatic representation

In the 1860s, the American consulate-general in the [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency) included a consular agency in Chittagong.[^cite_note-93] Today, Chittagong hosts an assistant high commission of India and a consulate general of Russia. The city also has [honorary consulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_consul) of [Turkey](11125639.md), Japan, [Germany](11867.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Italy](14532.md), and the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

A substantial share of Bangladesh's national GDP is attributed to Chittagong. As of the early 2000s, the port city contributed 12% of the nation's economy.[^cite_note-CTGC-102] Chittagong generates for 40% of Bangladesh's [industrial output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector), 80% of its [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade) and 50% of its governmental [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue).[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-BBC-104] The [Chittagong Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Stock_Exchange) has more than 700 listed companies, with a [market capitalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalisation) of US\$32 billion in June 2015.[^cite_note-cse-101] The city is home to many of the country's oldest and largest corporations. The Port of Chittagong handled US\$60 billion in annual trade in 2011, ranking 3rd in South Asia after the [Port of Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Mumbai) and the [Port of Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Colombo).[^cite_note-thedailystar.net-10][^cite_note-BBC-104] The port is part of the Maritime [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road) that runs from the Chinese coast via the [Suez Canal](29323.md) to the [Mediterranean](19006.md) and on to the Upper Adriatic region of [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) with rail connections to [Central](5188.md) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

The group has been subjected to intense media scrutiny.

## Architecture



Abandoned colonial house of [Satya Saha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya_Saha), which was built in 1890

The [Anderkilla Shahi Jame Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderkilla_Shahi_Jame_Mosque) is a well-known [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) property in Chittagong. Anderkilla ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): আন্দরকিল্লা) means "Inner fort".[^cite_note-117] The mosque was built in 1667 by Umed Khan, the son of [Shaista Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaista_Khan), after the [Mughal conquest of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_conquest_of_Chittagong). The mosque is the only surviving part of a hilltop Mughal fort. A surviving remnant of the 17th century [Portuguese presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_settlement_in_Chittagong) is Darul Adalat in the premises of [Government Hazi Mohammad Mohsin College, Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Hazi_Mohammad_Mohsin_College,_Chittagong). The Kadam Mubarak Mosque in Jamal Khan was built in 1723 by a [faujdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faujdar) during the reign of the [Nawabs of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabs_of_Bengal).[^cite_note-118] During [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency), colonial officials lived in hilltop bungalows, which would feature a spacious [balcony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balcony) or [verandah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verandah), chimneys, fireplaces and big gardens. The [Firingi Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firingi_Bazar) has many colonial houses which belonged to rich local residents. The well-known buildings from the British colonial period include the Battali Railway Station, [Central Railway Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Railway_Building), [Chittagong Circuit House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_Memorial_Museum) and [Chittagong Court Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Court_Building).

The old Circuit House was originally built in the style of [Tudor Revival architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Revival_architecture). The Chittagong Court Building exhibits influence of [Neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) from the late 19th century. JM Sen Hall was a town hall built in 1920.[^cite_note-119] One of the grand old mansions of Chittagong is the PK Sen Bhaban.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] The First [Karnaphuli Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Amanat_Bridge), which was a steel bridge, was built in 1930.[^cite_note-122] The [Kalurghat Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalurghat_Bridge) was completed in 1931.[^cite_note-123] [Stripped Classicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stripped_Classicism) and elements of [art deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_deco) can be seen in [Agrabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrabad). [M. M. Ispahani Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._M._Ispahani_Limited) relocated its head office to Chittagong from [Calcutta](47905.md) after the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India);[^cite_note-124] the Ispahani building in Agrabad was influenced by the art deco style. Another building with 1930s classical and art deco elements is the headquarters of the [Jamuna Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamuna_Oil_Company). The building has a dome and modernist columns inspired by the style of the 1930s and 1940s.

## Culture

Further information: [Culture of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Bangladesh)



[Mezbani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezban) beef, a traditional dish of Chittagong



[Anderkilla Shahi Jame Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderkilla_Shahi_Jame_Mosque) built during the Mughal era

&#x20;An inhabitant of Chittagong is called *Chittagonian* in English.[^cite_note-archive.thedailystar.net-125] For centuries, the port city has been a [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot) for people from all over the world. Its historic trade networks have left a lasting impact on its language, culture, and cuisine. The [Chittagonian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagonian_language), although identified as a nonstandard dialect of Bengali, is considered to be a separate language by many linguists. The Chittagonian language has many Arabic, Persian, English and Portuguese loanwords.[^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24] The popular traditional feast of *[Mezban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezban)* features the serving of hot beef dish with white rice.[^cite_note-archive.thedailystar.net-125] Another dish named *kala-bhuna* of Chittagong, made with traditional spices, mustard oil, and beef through a special cooking style, is also renowned all over Bangladesh. The cultivation of pink [pearls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl) is a historic activity in Chittagong. Its Mughal-era name, *Islamabad* (City of Islam), continues to be used in the old city. The name was given due to the port city's history as a gateway for early Islamic missionaries in Bengal. Notable Islamic architecture in Chittagong can be seen in the historic Bengal Sultanate-era Hammadyar Mosque and the Mughal Fort of Anderkilla. Chittagong is known as the *Land of the Twelve Saints*[^cite_note-126] due to the prevalence of major Sufi Muslim shrines in the district. Historically, [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) played an important role in the spread of Islam in the region. Prominent [dargahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dargah) include the mausoleums of Shah Amanat, Badr Auliya, Miskin Shah, Garibullah Shah and the shrine of [Bayazid Bastami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayazid_Bastami) among many others. The Bastami shrine hosts a pond of [black softshell turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_softshell_turtle), a critically endangered species of freshwater turtle.



LRB Band founder [Ayub Bachchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Bachchu)&#x20;

&#x20;During the medieval period, many poets thrived in the region when it was part of the Bengal Sultanate and the Kingdom of Mrauk U. Under the patronage of Sultan [Alauddin Husain Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Husain_Shah)'s governor in Chittagong, Kabindra Parameshvar wrote his Pandabbijay, a Bengali adaptation of the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata).[^cite_note-127] [Daulat Qazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulat_Qazi) lived in the region during the 17th-century reign of the Kingdom of Mrauk U. Chittagong is home to several important [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) temples, including the [Chandranath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandranath_Temple) on the outskirts of the city, which is dedicated to the Hindu goddess [Sita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sita).[^cite_note-128] The city also hosts the country's largest [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) monastery and council of monks. The [Roman Catholic Diocese of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Chittagong) is the oldest catholic mission in Bengal.[^cite_note-129]



&#x20;[Shimul Khaled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimul_Khaled) (middle) is credited with starting the fashion show trend in Chittagong&#x20;

Major cultural organizations in the city include the [Theatre Institute Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Institute_Chittagong) and the [Chittagong Performing Arts Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilla_Shilpakala_Academy,_Chittagong). The city has a vibrant [contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_art) scene.

Being home to the pioneering rock bands in the country like [Souls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souls_\(band\))[^cite_note-130] and [LRB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Runs_Blind),[^cite_note-131] Chittagong is regarded as the "birthplace of [Bangladeshi rock music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_rock)".[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

Chittagong has a burgeoning fashion industry that blends traditional heritage with modern trends. The city’s contemporary fashion show culture was pioneered by designer **[Shimul Khaled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimul_Khaled)**, who is credited with introducing the first runway shows to the region.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

There is also the [Chattogram City Corporation Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattogram_City_Corporation_Public_Library).

Chittagong was a [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot) of ethnicities during the [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate) and [Mughal Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Bengal) periods. Muslim immigration started as early as the seventh century, and significant Muslim settlements occurred during the medieval period. Muslim traders, rulers, and preachers from Persia and Arabia were the early Muslim settlers, and their descendants are the majority of the current Muslim population of the city. The city has a relatively wealthy and economically influential [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Muslim) community, including [Ismailis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili) and [Twelver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver) Shias. The city also has many ethnic minorities, especially members of [indigenous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumma_people) from the frontier hills of Chittagong Division, including [Chakmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people), [Rakhines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) and [Tripuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri_people); as well as [Rohingya](1918632.md) refugees. The Bengali-speaking [Theravada Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada_Buddhists) of the area, known as *[Baruas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barua_people)*, are one of the oldest communities in Chittagong and one of the last remnants of Buddhism in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] Descendants of Portuguese settlers, often known as *Firingis*, also live in Chittagong, as well as [Catholics](606848.md), who largely live in the old Portuguese enclave of Paterghatta.[^cite_note-banglapedia.org-24] There is also a small Urdu-speaking [Bihari community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihari_people) living in the ethnic enclave known as *Bihari Colony*.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] Like other major urban centres in South Asia, Chittagong has experienced steady growth in its [informal settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_settlements) as a result of the increasing economic activities in the city and emigration from rural areas. According to a poverty reduction publication of the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), there were 1,814 [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slums) within the city corporation area, inhabited by about 1.8 million slum dwellers, the second highest in the country after the capital, [Dhaka](56656.md).[^cite_note-IMF-142] The slum dwellers often face eviction by the local authorities, charging them with illegal abode on government lands.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] In the early 1990s, Chittagong had a population of just over 1.5 million, of which there were an estimated 66,676 [squatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting_in_Bangladesh) living in 69 areas.[^cite_note-Chowdhury-145]

## Media and communications

Further information: [Media of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Bangladesh)

Various newspapers, including daily, opposition, and business newspapers, are based in Chittagong. Daily newspapers include [Dainik Azadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Azadi),[^cite_note-146] Peoples View,[^cite_note-147] [The Daily Suprobhat Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suprobhat_Bangladesh), [Daily Purbokone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Purbokone), Life, Karnafuli, Jyoti, Rashtrobarta and Azan. Furthermore, there are several weekly and monthly newspapers. These include [weeklies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_newspaper) such as Chattala, Jyoti, Sultan, Chattagram Darpan, and the monthlies such as Sanshodhani, Purobi, Mukulika, and Simanto. The only press council in Chittagong is the Chittagong Press Club. Government-owned [Bangladesh Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Television), with its [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTV_Chittagong) station, and [Bangladesh Betar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Betar) have transmission centres in the city. Privately owned [Ekushey Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekushey_Television) formerly broadcast on VHF channel 9 in Chittagong during its existence on terrestrial television.[^cite_note-148]

Chittagong has been featured in all aspects of Bangladeshi popular culture, including television, movies, journals, music, and books. Nearly all televisions and radios in Bangladesh have coverage in Chittagong. The southern zone of the [Bangladesh Power Development Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Power_Development_Board) is responsible for supplying electricity to city dwellers.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] The fire services are provided by the [Bangladesh Fire Service & Civil Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Fire_Service_&_Civil_Defence) department, under the Ministry of Home Affairs.[^cite_note-154] Total Electricity Consumption is approximately 1000 megawatts in the city proper. But in the whole Chittagong urban and city proper, it will be 1300 megawatts plus-minus. The power plant will be in production next year and its production power is 1320 megawatts and it creates Chittagong City as the energy production hub of Bangladesh

The water supply and sewage systems are managed by the Chittagong Water Supply and Sewerage Authority (Chittagong WASA).[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] Water is primarily drawn from Karnaphuli River and then purified in the Mohra Purification Plant.[^cite_note-157]

Chittagong has extensive [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) and [CDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_division_multiple_access) coverage, served by all the major mobile operators of the country, including [Grameenphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameenphone), [Banglalink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banglalink), [Citycell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citycell), [Robi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robi_\(company\)), [TeleTalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeleTalk) and [Airtel Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airtel_Bangladesh). However, landline telephone services are provided through the state-owned [Bangladesh Telegraph and Telephone Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Telegraph_and_Telephone_Board) (BTTB), as well as some private operators. BTTB also provides broadband Internet services, along with some private [Internet service providers](100245.md) (ISPs), including the [4G](486547.md) service providers [Banglalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banglalion)[^cite_note-158] and Qubee.[^cite_note-159]

## Education and research



[Saint Placid's High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Placid's_High_School) was established in 1853



[University of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chittagong)

See also: [Education in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Bangladesh) and [Rangunia Upazila § Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangunia_Upazila#Education)

The education system of Chittagong is similar to that of [rest of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Bangladesh), with four main forms of schooling. The general education system, conveyed in both Bangla and English versions, follows the curriculum prepared by the [National Curriculum and Textbook Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Curriculum_and_Textbook_Board), part of the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Bangladesh\)).[^cite_note-Schools-161] Students are required to take two major board examinations are \:the [Secondary School Certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_School_Certificate) (SSC) and the [Higher Secondary School Certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Secondary_School_Certificate) (HSC) before moving onto higher education. The [Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education, Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Intermediate_and_Secondary_Education,_Chittagong) is responsible for administering SSC and HSC examinations within the city.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] The [Madrasah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah) education system is primarily based on Islamic studies, though other subjects are also taught. Students are prepared according to the Dakhil and Alim examinations, which are controlled by the [Bangladesh Madrasah Education Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Madrasah_Education_Board) and are equivalent to SSC and HSC examinations of the general education system respectively.[^cite_note-164] There are also several private schools in the city, usually referred to as *English medium schools*,[^cite_note-Schools-161] which follow the [General Certificate of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Certificate_of_Education).

The [British Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Council) supervises the [O Levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_Level) and [A levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Level) examinations, conducted twice a year, through the [Cambridge International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_International_Examinations) and [Edexcel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edexcel) [examination boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examination_board).[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] The Technical and Vocational education system is governed by the Directorate of Technical Education (DTE) and follow the curriculum prepared by [Bangladesh Technical Education Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Technical_Education_Board) (BTEB).[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] [Chittagong College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_College), established in 1869, is the earliest modern institution for higher education in the city.[^cite_note-169] [Chittagong Veterinary and Animal Sciences University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Veterinary_and_Animal_Sciences_University) is the only public university located in Chittagong city. [Chittagong Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Medical_College) is the only government medical college in Chittagong.

[University of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chittagong) is located 22 kilometres (14 miles) north and [Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_University_of_Engineering_and_Technology) is located 25 kilometres (16 miles) north of the Chittagong city. The University of Chittagong, established in 1966 is one of the largest universities in Bangladesh. Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology, established in 1968, is one of the five public engineering universities in Bangladesh and the only engineering university in the Chittagong Division.

The city also hosts several other private universities and medical colleges. The [BGC Trust University Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGC_Trust_University_Bangladesh), [Chittagong Independent University (CIU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Independent_University_\(CIU\)), [Asian University for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_University_for_Women), [Port City International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_City_International_University), [East Delta University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Delta_University), [International Islamic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Islamic_University,_Chittagong), [Premier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_University,_Chittagong), [Southern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University,_Bangladesh), [University of Information Technology and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Information_Technology_and_Sciences) and the [University of Science & Technology Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Science_&_Technology_Chittagong) are among them.

## Transport

See also: [Transport in Bangladesh](68930.md)

Transport in Chittagong is similar to that of the capital, Dhaka. large avenues and roads are present throughout the metropolis. There are various bus systems and taxi services, as well as smaller 'baby' or 'CNG' taxis, which are tricycle-structured motor vehicles. Foreign and local [ridesharing companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridesharing_company) like [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) and [Pathao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathao) are operating in the city.[^cite_note-175] There are also traditional manual rickshaws, which are very common.

### Road

In the 2010s, the Chittagong Development Authority (CDA) undertook construction of numerous flyovers and road improvements aimed at easing the traffic congestion in Chittagong. The largest of these projects is the Chittagong Outer Ring Road,[^cite_note-176] which runs along the coast for 15.7 kilometres (9.8 mi) from Patenga to Sagorika Industrial Area. The four-lane [ring road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road) is meant to ease gridlock in Chittagong city, and the 33-foot (10 m) embankment on which it is built is intended to protect coastal areas from natural disasters.[^cite_note-177] When the project was approved in 2011, it was expected to be finished in 2014. Construction didn't start until 2015, and is ongoing as of 2025. The original cost estimate has risen almost fourfold, to Tk 33.24 billion (\$275M as of 2025).[^cite_note-178] The authority also began the construction of a 9.3 kilometres (5.8 mi) [underwater expressway tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnaphuli_Tunnel) through the Karnaphuli river to ensure better connectivity between the northern and southern parts of Chittagong. This tunnel will be the first of its kind in South Asia.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

The [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_\(Bangladesh\)) (Dhaka-Chittagong Highway), a major arterial national highway, is the only way to access the city by motor vehicle from most other parts of the country. It is considered a crowded and dangerous highway. This highway is also part of [AH41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH41) route of the [Asian Highway Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Highway_Network). It has been upgraded to four lanes.[^cite_note-182] The [N106](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N106_\(Bangladesh\)) (Chittagong-Rangamati Highway) is another major national highway that connects the Chittagong Hill Tracts with the [Oxygen Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen_Square).

### Rail

Chittagong can also be accessed by rail. It has a station on the [metre gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge), the eastern section of the Bangladesh Railway, whose headquarters are also located within the city. There are two main railway stations, on Station Road and in the [Pahartali Thana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahartali_Thana). Trains to Dhaka, [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet), [Comilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comilla), and [Bhairab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairab) are available from Chittagong. The [Chittagong Circular Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Circular_Railway) was introduced in 2013 to ease traffic congestion and to ensure better public transport service for commuters within the city. The railway includes high-speed [DEMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_multiple_unit#Diesel-electric) trains with a carrying capacity of 300 passengers. These DEMU trains also travel on the Chittagong-Laksham route which connects the city with Comilla.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

### Air



An [Antonov An 124](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An_124) parked in [Shah Amanat International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Amanat_International_Airport)

The [Shah Amanat International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Amanat_International_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **CGP**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **VGEG**), located at South [Patenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patenga), serves as Chittagong's only airport. It is the second busiest airport in Bangladesh. The airport is capable of annually handling 1.5 million passengers and 6,000 tonnes of cargo.[^cite_note-Monitor-185] Known as *Chittagong Airfield* during World War II, the airport was used as a supply point by the United States Army Air Forces' [Tenth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenth_Air_Force) during the [Burma Campaign 1944–45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Campaign_1944%E2%80%9345).[^cite_note-Maurer,_Maurer_1983-58] It officially became a Bangladeshi airport in 1972 after Bangladesh's liberation war.[^cite_note-Airport-186] International services fly to major cities of the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) as well as to Indian city of [Kolkata](47905.md).[^cite_note-route-187] At present, Middle Eastern airlines like [Air Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Arabia), [Flydubai](18432521.md), [Jazeera Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazeera_Airways), [Oman Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_Air) and [SalamAir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SalamAir) operate flights from the city to these destinations along with [airlines of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_of_Bangladesh).[^cite_note-route-187] All Bangladeshi airlines operate regular domestic flights to Dhaka. The airport was formerly known as [MA Hannan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._A._Hannan) International Airport but was renamed after a famous Sufi saint [Shah Amanat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Amanat) on 2 April 2005 by the Government.

## Sports



[Zohur Ahmed Chowdhury Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohur_Ahmed_Chowdhury_Stadium)



A [golf course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_course) in Chittagong

Chittagong has produced numerous cricketers, footballers, and athletes, who have performed at the national level. [Tamim Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamim_Iqbal), [Akram Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akram_Khan_\(cricketer\)), [Minhajul Abedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minhajul_Abedin), [Aftab Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftab_Ahmed_\(cricketer,_born_1985\)), [Nafees Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nafees_Iqbal), [Nazimuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazimuddin_\(cricketer\)), [Faisal Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_Hossain), [Tareq Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tareq_Aziz), [Mominul Haque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mominul_Haque), [Nayeem Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayeem_Hasan), [Mamunul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamunul_Islam), [Ashish Bhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashish_Bhadra), [Shahidul Alam Sohel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahidul_Alam_Sohel) are some of the most prominent figures among them. [Cricket](25675557.md) is the most popular sport in Chittagong, while [football](10568.md), tennis and [kabaddi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaddi) are also popular. Several stadiums are located in Chittagong with the main one being the multipurpose [MA Aziz Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MA_Aziz_Stadium), which has a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of 20,000 and hosts football matches in addition to cricket.[^cite_note-MA_Aziz_Stadium_1-188] MA Aziz Stadium was the stadium where Bangladesh achieved its first-ever [Test cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) victory, against Zimbabwe in 2005.[^cite_note-MA_Aziz_Stadium-189] The stadium now focuses only on football, and is currently the main football venue of the city. [Zohur Ahmed Chowdhury Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohur_Ahmed_Chowdhury_Stadium), is currently the main cricket venue of the city, which was awarded Test status in 2006, hosting both domestic and international cricket matches. The city hosted two group matches of the [2011 ICC Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup), both taking place in Zohur Ahmed Chowdhury Stadium.[^cite_note-Chittagong_divisional_stadium-190] It also co-hosted [2014 ICC World Twenty20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_ICC_World_Twenty20) along with Dhaka and Sylhet, [Zohur Ahmed Chowdhury Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohur_Ahmed_Chowdhury_Stadium) hosted 15 group stage matches. Other stadiums in Chittagong include the Women's Complex Ground. Major sporting clubs such as, [Mohammedan Sporting Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammedan_Sporting_Club_\(Chittagong\)) and [Abahani Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abahani_Limited_\(Chittagong\)) are also located in the city. Chittagong is also home to the [Bangladesh Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Premier_League) franchise, the [Chattogram Challengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattogram_Challengers).
