**Baranya** ([Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *Baranya vármegye*, pronounced [\[ˈbɒrɒɲɒ ˈvaːrmɛɟɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hungarian); [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Komitat Branau*; [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language): *Baranjska županija*) is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Hungary) (*vármegye*) in southern [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-4] It is part of the [Southern Transdanubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Transdanubia) statistical region and the historical [Baranya region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baranya_\(region\)), which was a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Hungary_\(before_1920\)) (*comitatus*) in the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) dating back to the 11th century. Its current status as one of the 19 counties of Hungary was established in 1950 as part of wider [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_People's_Republic) administrative territorial reform following [World War II](32927.md). Its [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) and largest city is [Pécs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9cs).

As of 2025, it has a population of 351,158. Of the 19 counties of Hungary (excluding [Budapest](36787.md)), it is ranked 10th, both in terms of geographic area and population.[^cite_note-population-2]

## Etymology

In [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), it is known as *Komitat Branau*, and in [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language) as *Baranjska županija (Baranja)*.

The county was probably named after its first *comes* 'Brana' or 'Braina'.

## Geography

This county has a total area of 4,429.60 km<sup>2</sup> (1,710 sq mi) – 4.76% of Hungary – which is divided in Upper Baranya and Lower Baranya.

It is bordered by [Somogy County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somogy_County) to the northwest, [Tolna County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolna_County) to the north, [Bács-Kiskun County](993023.md) and the [Danube](21209639.md) to the east, and the border with [Croatia](5573.md) (part of which is formed by the [Drava River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drava)) to the south.

The northern part of the county is a mountain area with large forests, the [Mecsek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecsek) Mountains. The central areas are shared between the [Baranya Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baranya_Hills?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Villány Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vill%C3%A1ny_Mountains). The very eastern and southern parts are flat.

The highest point in the county is [Zengő](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeng%C5%91) in the Mecsek Mountains, at 682 metres. This is also the highest point of the mountain range.

Baranya is rich in mineral and thermal water, and also in other resources. 98% of Hungary's [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) resources are found here.

### Climate

The [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) of Baranya is a mix of [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) and [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate), which makes it unique in Hungary, as the rest of the country is primarily continental. Its milder climate is due to its southern location and relative proximity to the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) in comparison to other parts of the country, leaving it with warmer winters. It has the highest average annual rainfall of the Hungarian counties and a high amount of sunshine hours.

## History

Main article: [Baranya County (former)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baranya_County_\(former\))

The area has been inhabited since ancient times. Before the Hungarian tribes conquered the area, it was inhabited by Slavs and Avars. [Stephen I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary) founded an episcopal seat here.

In 1526, the county was [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Hungary) by the [Ottomans](22278.md). It was taken by forces of the [Holy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_League_\(1684\)) in 1689 during the [Great Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Turkish_War); it was formally ceded by the Ottomans to the now [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Monarchy)-ruled [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_\(1526%E2%80%931867\)) in the 1699 [Treaty of Karlowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Karlowitz). Its medieval borders remained largely unchanged until 1919. According to the [Treaty of Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon), the southern part of the county (1,163 km<sup>2</sup>, 449 sq mi) passed to the newly formed [Kingdom of the Serbs, Croats and Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Serbs,_Croats_and_Slovenes) (later renamed Yugoslavia; now in present-day Croatia). In 1941 during World War 2 this southern area was re-annexed to Hungary following the [Axis invasion of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_invasion_of_Yugoslavia) but returned to Yugoslavia after the war.

In the [1950 reorganization of the counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_reorganization_of_the_counties_of_Hungary?action=edit\&redlink=1) the area around the town of [Szigetvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szigetv%C3%A1r) was transferred from [Somogy County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somogy_County). Baranya has the largest number of minorities in Hungary (more than twice the country average), providing home to 34% of the German minority, the so-called [Danube Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians), and 32% of the Southern Slav minorities in Hungary.

The *Stifolder* or *Stiffoller Shvove* are a Roman Catholic subgroup of the so-called [Danube Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians). Their ancestors once came c. 1717–1804 from the [Abbacy and later Prince-Bishopric of Fulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_Abbey_of_Fulda) and surroundings ([Roman Catholic Diocese of Fulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Fulda)), and settled in Baranya.[^cite_note-6] They retained their own German dialect and culture until the end of WW2. After WW2, the majority of Danube Swabians were expelled to [allied-occupied Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany) and [allied-occupied Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria), in accordance with the 1945 [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement).[^cite_note-7]

Only a few people can speak the old Stiffolerisch Schvovish dialect. Also a sausage (kolbász) is named after these people.[^cite_note-8]

### 2011 census

As of the [census of 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_Hungary?action=edit\&redlink=1), there were 386,441 residents, 160,040 households, and 105,646 families living in the county. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 87 inhabitants per square kilometres(227/sqmi). There were 167,453 housing units at an average density of 38per square kilometre (98/sqmi).

There were 160,040 [households](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household), of which 63.2% were one-family households, 1.4% were multi-family households, 32.1% were one-person households, and 3.4% were other non-family households. Elderly individuals living alone were 15.9% of all households. The average household size was 2.34.

There were 105,646 [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family), of which 44.1% were [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) or [consensual unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union) living together with children, 36.4% were couples without children, 16.7% were single females with children, and 2.8% were single males with children. The average family size was 2.82.

The age breakdown of the county was 20.1% under the age of 20, 7.0% between ages 20 and 24, 27.4% aged 25 to 44, 28.3% aged 45 to 64, and 17.2% aged 65 or older. The gender makeup of the county was 47.2% male and 52.8% female.

Religious adherence in the county was 46.8% [Roman Catholic](606848.md), 6.4% [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Church_in_Hungary) ([Calvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism)), 1.2% [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical-Lutheran_Church_in_Hungary) ([Lutheran](23371382.md)), 0.3% [Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Greek_Catholic_Church), 0.1% [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_Hungary), and 1.5% other religions. The [non-religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) were 16.2% and [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) were 1.5%, with 26.0% declining to answer.[^cite_note-Baranya_2011-12]

### 2001 census

As of the [census of 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_census_of_Hungary?action=edit\&redlink=1), there were 407,448 residents, 151,956 households, and 115,946 families living in the county. The population density was 238 inhabitants per square mile (92/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 156,632 housing units at an average density of 92 per square mile (35/km<sup>2</sup>).

There were 151,956 [households](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household), of which 68.5% were one-family households, 3.8% were multi-family households, 24.9% were one-person households, and 2.7% were other non-family households. Elderly individuals living alone were 13.1% of all households. The average household size was 2.60.

There were 115,946 [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family), of which 48.7% were [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) or [consensual unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union) living together with children, 35.4% were couples without children, 13.7% were single females with children, and 2.1% were single males with children. The average family size was 2.87.

The age breakdown of the county was 23.0% under the age of 20, 8.1% between ages 20 and 24, 28.0% aged 25 to 44, 26.0% aged 45 to 64, and 14.9% aged 65 or older. The gender makeup of the county was 47.5% male and 52.5% female.

Religious adherence in the county was 64.2% [Roman Catholic](606848.md), 8.7% [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Church_in_Hungary) ([Calvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism)), 1.5% [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical-Lutheran_Church_in_Hungary) ([Lutheran](23371382.md)), 0.5% [Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Greek_Catholic_Church), 0.1% [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_Hungary), and 0.8% other religions. The [non-religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) were 13.4%, with 10.8% declining to answer.[^cite_note-Baranya_2001-13][^cite_note-Baranya_2011-12]

## Municipalities

[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Baranya County has **1** [urban county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_with_county_rights), **13** [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_of_Hungary), **3** large villages and **284** villages.



Hosszúhetény - Temple from above

Like [Borsod-Abaúj-Zemplén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borsod-Aba%C3%BAj-Zempl%C3%A9n), Baranya is a county of extremes when it comes to regional structure. The county seat is one of the five largest cities (and three largest agglomerations) of Hungary, but more than 2/3 of the municipalities are small hamlets with a population under 500. Half of the county's population lives in the county seat or in its immediate vicinity, while 22% of the population lives in villages that have less than 1000 inhabitants.
