**Ovruch** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Овруч, IPA: [\[ˈɔu̯rʊtʃ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/37/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9E%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%83%D1%87.wav/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9E%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%83%D1%87.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/37/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9E%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%83%D1%87.wav/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9E%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%83%D1%87.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in [Korosten Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korosten_Raion), [Zhytomyr Oblast](1567596.md), northern [Ukraine](31750.md).

It was first mentioned as **Vruchiy** in 977. It was the capital city of the [Drevlians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drevlian) in the 10th century, later conquered by the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) in the 13th century, then later part of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). In the 16th century it became part of Poland, as a [royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city). After the [second partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_partition_of_Poland) in 1793 it became part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), and then part of [Ukraine](31750.md). Until 2020, it was the [administrative center](181337.md) of the former [Ovruch Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovruch_Raion), until it was merged into [Korosten Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korosten_Raion). It has a population of approximately 15,250 (2022 estimate),[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-2] and is home to the [Ovruch air base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovruch_\(air_base\)).

## Name

In addition to the [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) Овруч (*Ovruch*), in other languages the name of the city is [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Owrucz* and [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language): אוורוטש.

## History

### Middle Ages

Ovruch originated as an important town of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), first mentioned as **Vruchiy** in 977. Later after the sack of [Iskorosten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korosten) it became the capital city of the [Derevlians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derevlians).[^cite_note-3] During the 12th century Ovruch became a residence of [Rurik Rostislavich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurik_Rostislavich), who rebuilt the local wooden [Church of St. Basil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Basil's_Cathedral,_Ovruch?action=edit\&redlink=1) in stone. During Rurik's rule the city was fortified.[^cite_note-loc-4]

Saint [Hyacinth of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyacinth_of_Poland) evangelized in the town between 1222 and 1234.[^cite_note-sgk73-5] The area suffered during the [Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus') in 1240,[^cite_note-sgk73-5] and then it passed under Mongol suzerainty. In the 14th century it became part of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). It became one of many Lithuanian defensive strongholds in the region against possible Tatar invasions.[^cite_note-sgk73-5] In 1483 and 1506[^cite_note-loc-4] [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) destroyed the settlement.

### Modern period

From the 16th century the town was governed by [starosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starost), and it flourished and became a subregional center.[^cite_note-6]

According to the [Treaty of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lublin) (1569), Owrucz passed to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) within the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth).[^cite_note-sgk75-7] The town also served as the seat of county [sejmiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejmik).[^cite_note-sgk75-7] From 1614 until his death in 1616, the starost of Owrucz was [Michał Wiśniowiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C5%82_Wi%C5%9Bniowiecki), grandfather of future Polish King [Michał Korybut Wiśniowiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C5%82_Korybut_Wi%C5%9Bniowiecki).[^cite_note-sgk76-8] A [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) Monastery was founded, confirmed by Bishop Aleksander Sokołowski in 1638.[^cite_note-sgk76-8] In 1641, Polish King [Władysław IV Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_IV_Vasa) granted Owrucz city rights. It was a [royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) of Poland.[^cite_note-9]

After suffering from a major fire in 1578, Ovruch was once again damaged during the [Khmelnytsky Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytsky_Uprising) of 1648. In 1678 a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College) moved to Ovruch from nearby [Ksaveriv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksaveriv?action=edit\&redlink=1). During the 18th century a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) church was established at the local monastery. Following the disbandment of Jesuits in 1773 the building was transferred to [Basilian monks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilian_monks), who established a school there.[^cite_note-loc-4] After the [Second Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Partition_of_Poland) in 1793 it was annexed by the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).&#x20;

During the 18th and 19th century around half of Ovruch's population consisted of [Hasidic Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasidic_Jews), who established a [shtetl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shtetl).[^cite_note-loc-4]

Following the defeat of the [Polish Uprising of 1831](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising) the local Catholic church was transferred to the [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) community.[^cite_note-loc-4] In 1881, it had a population of 5,941.[^cite_note-10]

### 20th century



19th-century view of the city

In 1907-1911 the city's St. Basil's Cathedral, which had stood in ruins for several centuries, was reconstructed by a group of architects led by [Alexey Shchusev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Shchusev).[^cite_note-loc-4]

The city suffered from the man-made famine [Holodomor of 1932-1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor). In 2008, the [National Museum of the Holodomor-Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Holodomor-Genocide) published the National Book of Memory of the Victims of the Holodomor of 1932-1933 in Ukraine. Zhytomyr region - Zhytomyr.[^cite_note-11] According to historical records, more than 1517 people died during Holodomor in 1932-1933.

In 1918 Ovruch's Jewish quarter was destroyed as a result of a [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom) perpetrated by forces of [otaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otaman) Kozyr-Zirka.[^cite_note-loc-4]

The command of the Soviet [15th Rifle Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Rifle_Corps) was based in Ovruch shortly before the Soviet [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) at the start of [World War II](32927.md) in September 1939.[^cite_note-cg-12] The 15th Rifle Corps invaded towards [Dawidgródek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawidgr%C3%B3dek), [Kamień Koszyrski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamie%C5%84_Koszyrski) and [Włodawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82odawa).[^cite_note-cg-12] From 1941, the town was occupied by Germany. The German occupiers operated a Jewish [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) battalion in the town.[^cite_note-13] [Mordechai Schlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordechai_Schlein), a Jewish-Belarusian partisan, blew up a restaurant with about 200 German officers in it.[^cite_note-14]

In 1990 a women's monastery was reestablished on the base of St. Basil's Cathedral.[^cite_note-loc-4]

### 21 st century



Ruins of a house after the 2022 Russian invasion

During the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), on March 6, at 2\:32 a.m., enemy forces launched 3 bomb attacks on the residential sector of Ovruch. A total of 45 private houses were damaged, 5 of which were completely destroyed. There was also significant damage to 43 apartments in a multi-storey building, the premises of a children's and youth art center, a music school and a gym.[^cite_note-15]

Around 20\:30, Russian troops conducted 6 more air strikes. Three civilians were killed.[^cite_note-16] As a result, the building of the Ovruch Employment Center was completely destroyed. Also damaged were the premises of the Ovruch City Hospital, the Centers for administrative services, apartments in 8 multi-storey buildings, about 5 private houses, one of which was completely destroyed, and 2 more are in disrepair.[^cite_note-17]

## Architecture



St. Basil's Church

The only mark of the town's antiquity is St. Basil's Church, commissioned by [Rurik II of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurik_II_of_Kiev) from his court architect [Peter Miloneg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Miloneg?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the late 1190s. The church was built in Rurik's [votchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votchina) and was dedicated to his patron saint.

St. Basil's Church has four pillars, three apses and one dome. The western facade is flanked by two round towers, probably in imitation of the [Saint Sophia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sophia_Cathedral,_Kyiv) in [Kyiv](585629.md). The building is distinguished by elaborate brick facades, interlaced with bands of polished colored stone. The complicated design of pilasters points to a complex system of roofing and to a very high dome. The dome and vaults collapsed during the siege of Ovruch by [Gediminas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gediminas) in 1321. The ruins of the church survived until 1842, when they crumbled, with the exception of three apses and a portion of the northern wall with an arch. In 1782, a small [wooden church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_churches_in_Ukraine) was constructed inside of the original building.[^cite_note-ency-18]

In 1907 [Aleksey Shchusev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksey_Shchusev) was commissioned to restore the church to its presumed original form, incorporating the remains of Rurik's church into its edifice. Restoration work lasted for two years, and it won Schusev the prestigious title of the Academician of Architecture. More recently, the accuracy of his restoration has been questioned, as it didn't take into account the complicated system of vaulting and the considerable height of the drum. As a consequence of this oversight, the drum was restored according to a model typical of more archaic churches, rather than for the turn of the 13th century. Adjacent buildings of St. Basil's Convent were built on the model of medieval architecture of [Pskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov), simultaneously with the restoration of the main church. The restoration was finished in 1908, giving the building a pseudo-[Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_architecture) appearance.[^cite_note-ency-18]

Historically, Ovruch used to host two [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) monasteries owned by the Jesuits and [Dominicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order). Both have been destroyed, and the Catholic Church of St, Nicholas has since then been replaced by a [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church).[^cite_note-loc-4]

## Economy

### Medieval industrial centre

This section is an excerpt from [Money of Kievan Rus' § Ovruch slate spindle whorl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_of_Kievan_Rus'#Ovruch_slate_spindle_whorl).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Money_of_Kievan_Rus%27\&action=edit)]



Ovruch slate spindle whorl

A particularly valuable commodity, produced in the Volhynian city of Ovruch in the 10th–13th centuries, was the reddish-coloured slate [spindle whorl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spindle_whorl), carved out of pink and red slate stone ([pyrophyllite slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrophyllite)), which was mined near the town of Ovruch in the territory of present-day [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartin200775-19] Ovruch masters diligently repeated the most successful form of a clay spindle whorl - biconical (the weight as if consisted of two truncated cones connected by broad bases) The spindle whorl weighed on average about 16 g, the height was from 4 to 12 mm, the outer diameter was from 10 to 25 mm, the diameter of the hole for the spindle was 6–10 mm. If the spindle whorl turned out to be too narrow, it was wrapped with a thread so that it would not slip during rotation. Slate - soft stone; on the samples found by archaeologists, there are still scuffs from the threads.[^cite_note-20] The production of slate spindles in Ovruch was designed for wide sales. The merchants who bought the consignment of the [spindle whorls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spindle_whorl) made a considerable way with them, sold them in different lands. Ovruch spindle whorls are found by archaeologists not only in the territories of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), but in other regions. Production of spindle whorls was designed for a wide sale.[^cite_note-21] Ovruch's production and widespread sale of spindle whorls contributed significantly to the [Galician–Volhynian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia#Economy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartin200775-19] According to [A. V. Artsikhovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemiy_Artsikhovsky), "they are exactly the same in [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) and [Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir,_Russia), in [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliky_Novgorod) and [Ryazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan), even in [Cherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesus), in the [Crimea](163045.md) and in the [Bulgaria on the Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Bulgaria)." The Ovruch spindle whorls were so valued that the owners carved their personal tags on them, and after the spread of writing - carved their names. In the mid-13th century, the stone spindle whorls become clay again: the workshops of Ovruch were destroyed during the [Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus').[^cite_note-Money_of_Kievan_Rus'_\:2-22]

## Geography

Located in northwestern Ukraine, 50 kilometres (31 mi) south of the [Belarusian](3457.md) border, Ovruch is part of the geographical region of [Polesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polesia). It is located 48 kilometres (30 mi) from [Korosten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korosten), 133 kilometres (83 mi) from [Zhytomyr](1025103.md), and 92 kilometres (57 mi) from [Mazyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazyr), in Belarus; and it is 100 kilometres (62 mi) from the ghost town of [Pripyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pripyat), near the [Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Nuclear_Power_Plant).
