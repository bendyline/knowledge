**Shiraz**[^cite_note-5] is the [fifth-most-populous city](17288411.md) of [Iran](14653.md)[^cite_note-6] and the capital of [Fars province](448049.md), which is historically known by the exonym [Persia proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persis).[^cite_note-Fars_Province_Structure-7][^cite_note-Sykes-8] As of the 2016 national census, the population of the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) was 1,565,572 people, and its built-up area with [Sadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadra,_Fars) was home to almost 1,800,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-9] A census in 2021 showed an increase in the city's population to 1,995,500 people.[^cite_note-10] Shiraz is located in [southwestern Iran](36042664.md) on the *rudkhaneye khoshk* (lit. 'dry river') seasonal river. The city has a moderate climate and has been a regional trade center for over a thousand years.

The earliest reference to the city, as *Tiraziš*, is on [Elamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam) [clay tablets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_tablet) dated to 2000 BCE.[^cite_note-11] The modern city was founded by the [Sasanian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_dynasty) and restored by the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) in 693 CE and grew prominent under the successive [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples) [Saffarid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffarid_dynasty) and [Buyid dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid_dynasty) in the 9th and 10th–11th centuries, respectively. In the 13th century, Shiraz became a leading center of the arts and letters, due to the encouragement of its ruler and the presence of multiple [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) scholars and artists. Two famous poets of Iran, [Hafez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez) and [Saadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_Shirazi), are from Shiraz, whose tombs are located on the north side of the current city boundaries.

Shiraz is one of the top tourist cities in [Iran](14653.md), visited by people from around the world, and is known as the city of [poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_poetry), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_literature), and flowers.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] It is also considered by some Iranians to be the city of gardens due to the presence of a number of gardens and [fruit trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_tree) that can be seen throughout the city, such as [Eram Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eram_Garden). Shiraz has historically had major [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Iran) and [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Christianity_in_Iran) communities. The crafts of Shiraz consist of inlaid [mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic) work of triangular design; silverware; [pile carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knotted-pile_carpet)-weaving and weaving of [kilim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilim), called *gilim* and *jajim* in the villages and among the [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe).[^cite_note-14] Dominant industries in the city include the production of [cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement), sugar, fertilizers, textile products, wood products, metalwork, and [rugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_carpet). Shiraz also has a [major oil refinery](28205267.md) and is a major centre for Iran's electronic industries: 53 percent of Iran's electronic investment has been centred in Shiraz.[^cite_note-15] The city is home to Iran's first [solar powerplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_plants).[^cite_note-16]

## Etymology



Shiraz in a photo by [Canadian astronaut](328618.md) [Chris Hadfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hadfield) from the [ISS](15043.md) on 20 March 2013 (1392 [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz))

[^cite_note-17]

Several tablets from the [Persepolis treasury archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_Administrative_Archives), written in the [Elamite language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elamite_language), mention major workshops in *T/Ši-ra-iz-iz-iš* (read *T/Širazziš*), which is the ancient form of the name *Shiraz*.[^cite_note-18] Phonetically, this is interpreted as /tiračis/ or /ćiračis/. This name became Old Persian /širājiš/; through regular sound change comes the [modern Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Persian) name *Shirāz*. The name Shiraz also appears on clay sealings found at a 2nd-century CE [Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) ruin, east of the city. According to the Iranian linguist Zana Salehrad, the name Shiraz may derive from an ancient term meaning "sunlit plain" or "bright land", reflecting the region’s fertile terrain and favorable climate. This etymology suggests that the city’s name originally conveyed its natural abundance and strategic significance in southwestern Iran.[^cite_note-19]

## History

Main articles: [History of Shiraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Shiraz) and [Timeline of Shiraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Shiraz)

### Pre-Islamic era

Though there is no definitive record of its existence prior to the late 7th century CE, a few archaeological finds dating from 1933 and beyond indicate that the site or vicinity of Shiraz was likely settled in the pre-Islamic era as early as the 6th century BCE.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] A number of [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and [Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire)-era remains have been discovered around the city, including reliefs at [Barm-e Delak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barm-e_Delak) to the east and [Guyim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyim,_Shiraz) to the northwest, and ruins of Sasanian fortresses at [Qasr-e Abu Nasr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasre_Abunasr) to the east and Fahandezh.[^cite_note-Limbert4-5-21] The latter is identified with the fortress of Shahmobad mentioned as being in Shiraz by the 10th-century geographical work, *[Hudud al-'alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudud_al-'alam)*.[^cite_note-Limbert4-5-21] The Sasanian and early Islamic-era clay seals found at [Qasr-e Abunasr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasr-e_Abunasr) mention the name "Shiraz" alongside the name of the Sasanian administrative district of the area, [Ardashir-Khwarrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir-Khwarrah).[^cite_note-Limbert5-22] According to the diplomat and academic [John Limbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Limbert), this indicates that the name "Shiraz" is traced back to the Elamite "Shirrazish" and that both refer to a settlement that existed at the site of Qasr-e Abunasr.[^cite_note-Limbert5-22]

Interpretations of what type of settlement ancient Shiraz was vary. According to Berney and Ring, the lack of references to Shiraz in early Persian sources suggests the city could not have been more than a way-station in the plain in which it lies.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] On the other hand, according to [Abdolmajed Arfaee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Majid_Arfaei), Achaemenid-era Shiraz must have been one of the most important settlements in the area.[^cite_note-Arfaee_2008-23] He bases this on its frequent appearance in the [Persepolis Administrative Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_Administrative_Archives) (84 different tablets) as well as the number of workers present – in groups as large as 490.[^cite_note-Arfaee_2008-23] Most textual references to Shiraz involve rations for workers; it is never mentioned as a travel destination.[^cite_note-Arfaee_2008-23] Arfaee says that Sasanian Shiraz was relatively insignificant before its re-foundation in the early Islamic period.[^cite_note-Arfaee_2008-23] According to John Limbert,[^cite_note-24] however, Shiraz prospered between the 6th and 8th centuries CE and was possibly the administrative center for the Shiraz plain until the modern city of Shiraz was founded.[^cite_note-25]

### Early Islamic era

Shiraz was founded or restored in 693 by [Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Yusuf_al-Thaqafi), the brother of the [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) viceroy of the eastern half of the [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate), [al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hajjaj_ibn_Yusuf), or the latter's kinsman [Muhammad ibn Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Qasim).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Limbert4-27] The Arab Muslim army had conquered the wider region of [Fars](448049.md), where the site of Shiraz is located, in several expeditions launched from their garrison town of [Basra](57580.md) between 640 and 653, and specifically captured the immediate area around Shiraz early on, in 641. This area did not possess any cities, though there were a number of forts which were forced to pay tribute to the Arabs.[^cite_note-Limbert6-28] The Sasanians held firm in [Istakhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istakhr), their capital in Fars, until the Arabs captured it in a heavy battle in 653, during which the plain of Shiraz had been utilized as an Arab campground.[^cite_note-Limbert6-28] Because of Istakhr's deep association with the Sasanian Empire and the [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrian) religion, the [Arabs](2185.md) sought to establish in nearby Shiraz a rival cultural and administrative center.[^cite_note-Limbert6-28] Thus, during its initial founding in 693, the city was planned to be much larger than [Isfahan](223149.md).[^cite_note-Limbert6-28] However, the initial ambitions were not realized and Shiraz remained a "provincial backwater" in the shadow of Istakhr until at least the late 9th century, according to Limbert.[^cite_note-Limbert6-28] This is partly attributed to the reticence of the largely Zoroastrian population of Fars to inhabit the Islamic Arab city.[^cite_note-Limbert6-28] As the population gradually shifted to Islam from Zoroastrianism and Istakhr concurrently declined, Shiraz grew into the practical center of Fars.[^cite_note-Limbert6-28]

According to Muslim traditional sources, Shiraz was used as a hideout by three of the brothers of the [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Muslim) imam [Ali al-Ridha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_al-Ridha) following the latter's death in 817/18 and later by one of the brothers' sons, Ali ibn Hamza ibn Musa, until he was found and executed by the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) authorities in c. 835.[^cite_note-29] As Abbasid authority waned during this period, regional dynasties emerged with considerable autonomy.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] In the late 9th century, the Iranian Muslim [Saffarid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffarid_dynasty) under [Ya'qub ibn al-Layth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya'qub_ibn_al-Layth_al-Saffar) made Shiraz the capital of their autonomous state, which encompassed most of modern-day Iran.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20][^cite_note-Limbert6-28][^cite_note-Lambton473-30] In 894, Ya'qub's brother and successor, [Amr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amr_ibn_al-Layth), founded the city's first [congregational mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational_mosque), today known as the [Atigh Jame' Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atigh_Jame'_Mosque).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-Lambton473-30]

The Iranian [Buyid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid_dynasty) under [Imad al-Dawla Ali ibn Buya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imad_al-Dawla) ousted the Saffarids in 933 and his nephew and successor, ['Adud al-Dawla Fana Khusraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Adud_al-Dawla), took over and ruled Fars between 949 and 983, and added Iraq, the seat of the Abbasid Caliphate, to his Shiraz-based domains in 977;[^cite_note-Lambton473-30] the Abbasids thenceforth became a puppet state of the Shiraz-based dynasty.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] Shiraz developed into the largest and most prosperous city of Fars and an important economic and cultural center of the [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate).[^cite_note-Lambton473-30][^cite_note-Limbert9-32] Adud al-Dawla had a large library, a hospital and several mosques, [bazaars](556904.md), [caravanserais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai), palaces and gardens built in the city, while south of it he erected a fortified camp for his troops, known as Kard Fana Khusraw, in 974.[^cite_note-Lambton473-30][^cite_note-Limbert9-32] One of the congregational mosques built by Adud al-Dawla has survived until the present day.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] Two Zoroastrian [fire temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_temple) also existed in Shiraz,[^cite_note-Lambton473-30] catering to the Persians who had not converted to Islam.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] One of Adud al-Dawla's palaces stretched out for nearly three miles and consisted of 360 rooms.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20]

Under the Buyids, Shiraz was divided into twelve quarters and had eight gates.[^cite_note-Limbert9-32] It owed its economic prosperity to the booming agricultural trade of Fars.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] The city largely consumed the agricultural products of the province, including [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), [linen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linen), [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool), cotton, [collyrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collyrium), rose, violet and palm-blossom water.[^cite_note-Limbert9-32] It was also a market for rug weavers and painters to sell their pricey products, a testament to the residents' wealth.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] At the time, wine, grains, gold and silver were exported from the Persian port cities of [Siraf](8388522.md) and Najairam.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20] Adud al-Dawla patronized scientific, medical and Islamic religious research in Shiraz.[^cite_note-BerneyandRing644-20]

[Benjamin of Tudela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_of_Tudela) reported that Shiraz had 10,000 Jews in 1170.[^cite_note-33]

The city was spared destruction by the invading [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Empire), when its local ruler offered tributes and submission to [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan). Shiraz was again spared by [Tamerlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamerlane), when in 1382 the local monarch, Shah Shoja agreed to submit to the invader.[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34] In the 13th century, Shiraz became a leading center of the arts and letters, thanks to the encouragement of its ruler and the presence of a number of Persian scholars and artists . For this reason the city was named by classical geographers *Dar al-'Elm*, [the House of Knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Knowledge).[^cite_note-35] Among the Iranian poets, mystics and philosophers born in Shiraz were the poets [Sa'di](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_Shirazi)[^cite_note-saadi-iranchamber-36] and [Hafez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez),[^cite_note-hafez-iranica-37] the mystic [Ruzbehan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruzbihan_Baqli), and the philosopher [Mulla Sadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulla_Sadra).[^cite_note-mullasadra-38] Thus Shiraz has been nicknamed "The Athens of Iran".[^cite_note-39] As early as the 11th century, several hundred thousand people inhabited Shiraz.[^cite_note-40] In the 14th century, Shiraz had 60,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-41] During the 16th century, it had a population of 200,000 people, which by the mid-18th century had decreased to only 55,000.

### Safavids and later dynasties



An illustration of Shiraz by French traveler [Jean Chardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chardin) in 1670s while he was travelling through the Safavid Empire

In 1504, Shiraz was captured by the forces of [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I), the founder of the [Safavid Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty). Throughout the [Safavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran) (1501–1722), Shiraz remained a provincial capital, and [Emam Qoli Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Quli_Khan_\(governor\)), the governor of Fars under [Abbas the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_the_Great), constructed a number of palaces and ornate buildings in the same style as those built during the same period in [Isfahan](223149.md), the capital of the Empire.[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34] After the fall of the Safavids, Shiraz suffered a period of decline, worsened by the raids of the [Afghans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people) and the rebellion of its governor against [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah); the latter sent troops to suppress the revolt. The city was besieged for many months and eventually sacked. At the time of Nader Shah's murder in 1747, most of the historical buildings of the city were damaged or ruined, and its population fell to 50,000, one-quarter of that during the 16th century.[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34]

Shiraz soon returned to prosperity under the rule of [Karim Khan Zand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Khan_Zand), who made it his capital in 1762. Employing more than 12,000 workers, he constructed a royal district with a fortress, multiple administrative buildings, a mosque, and one of the finest covered bazaars in Iran.[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34] He had a moat built around the city, constructed an irrigation and drainage system, and rebuilt the city walls.[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34] However, Karim Khan's heirs failed to secure his gains. When [Agha Mohammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Mohammad_Khan), the founder of the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty), eventually came to power, he wreaked his revenge on Shiraz by destroying the city's fortifications and moving the national capital to [Tehran](57654.md).[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34] The [Lak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laks_\(Kurdistan\)) quarter of Shiraz, established during the Zand period, was destroyed by Agha Mohammad Khan.[^cite_note-44] Although lowered to the rank of a provincial capital, Shiraz maintained a level of prosperity as a result of the continuing importance of the trade route to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). Its governorship was a royal prerogative throughout the [Qajar era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran).[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34] Many of the famous gardens, buildings and residences built during this time contribute to the city's present skyline.

Shiraz is the birthplace of the co-founder of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), the [Báb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A1b) (Siyyid 'Ali-Muhammad, 1819–1850). In this city, on the evening of 22 May 1844, he first declared his mission as the bearer of a new divine revelation.[^cite_note-babism-browne-45] For this reason, Shiraz is a holy city for Baháʼís, and the city, particularly [the house of the Báb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A1b's_house), was identified as a place of [pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_pilgrimage).[^cite_note-smith2-46] Due to the [hostile climate towards Baháʼís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%ADs) in Iran, the house has been the target of repeated attacks; the house was destroyed in 1979, to be paved over two years later and made into a public square.[^cite_note-smith2-46]

In 1910, a [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom) in the Jewish quarter started after [false rumours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1910_Shiraz_blood_libel) that the Jews had ritually murdered a Muslim girl. In the course of the riots, 12 Jews were murdered and about 50 were injured, and the 6,000 Jews of Shiraz were robbed of all their possessions.[^cite_note-Littman12-47]

During the [Pahlavi era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran), Shiraz became the center of attention again. Important landmarks like the tombs of poets such as [Sa'di](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_\(poet\))[^cite_note-saadi-iranchamber-36] and [Hafez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez),[^cite_note-hafez-iranica-37] were constructed and presented to the public.

Lacking any great industrial, religious or strategic importance, Shiraz became an administrative center, although its population has nevertheless grown considerably since the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md).[^cite_note-48]

The city's municipality and other related institutions have initiated restoration and reconstruction projects.[^cite_note-Shiraz_history-34]

Some of the most recent projects have been the complete restoration of the [Arg of Karim Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arg_of_Karim_Khan) and of the Vakil Bath, as well as a comprehensive plan for the preservation of the old city quarters. ## Demographics

### Ethnicity

The majority of the population are [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians).[^cite_note-50] Most of the population of Shiraz are [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Muslim). Shiraz also was home to a 20,000-strong [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Jews), although most emigrated to the United States and [Israel](9282173.md) in the latter half of the 20th century.[^cite_note-51] Along with [Tehran](57654.md) and [Isfahan](223149.md), Shiraz is one of the handful of Iranian cities with a sizable Jewish population and more than one active synagogue. Shiraz also has a significant [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) population, the largest in the country after Tehran. There are currently two functioning churches in Shiraz, one [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) and the other [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St._Simon_the_Zealot).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

## Geography



Shiraz Garden Drives, in the north, has gardens predating the city's expansion.

The city of Shiraz, the capital of [Fars province](448049.md), is located at 52 degrees 32 minutes east longitude and 29 degrees 36 minutes north latitude, and is 919 kilometers from [Tehran](57654.md). In the first official census of Iran in 1335, the city of Shiraz with a population of 170,659 people was the sixth most populous city in Iran. Shiraz is located in the south of [Iran](14653.md) and the northwest of [Fars province](448049.md). It is built in a green plain at the foot of the [Zagros Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains) 1,500 metres (4,900 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). Shiraz is 800 kilometres (500 mi) south of [Tehran](57654.md).[^cite_note-autogenerated3-57]

A seasonal river, Dry River, flows through the northern part of the city and on into [Maharloo Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharloo_Lake).[^cite_note-58] As of 1920, the area had a large forest of [oak](39696.md) trees.[^cite_note-Sykes75-59]

### Gardens

Shiraz contains a considerable number of gardens. Due to population growth in the city, a number of these gardens may be lost to give way to new developments.[^cite_note-60] Although some measures have been taken by the Municipality to preserve these gardens, a number of illegal developments still endanger them.

### Climate



Sunset in Shiraz, with [Derock Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Derak) in the background

Shiraz's climate has distinct seasons, and is overall classed as a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSh*, bordering on *BSk*).[^cite_note-autogenerated3-57] [Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer) are hot, with a July average high of 38.7 °C (101.7 °F). [Winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter) are cool, with average low temperatures below freezing in January. Around 320 mm (13 in) of rain falls each year, almost entirely in the winter months, though in some cases as much as this has fallen in a single month (as in January 1965 and December 2004),[^cite_note-rainfall-61] whilst in the year from July 1965 to June 1966 as little as 82.9 millimetres (3.3 in) fell. The wettest year has been 1955/1956 with as much as 857.2 millimetres (33.75 in), though since 1959 the highest has been around 590 millimetres (23.2 in) in each of 1995/1996 and 2004/2005.[^cite_note-rainfall-61] Due to Shiraz' high elevation and low latitude, the [UV index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UV_index) is extremely high during summer which is further exacerbated by the high frequency of sunshine.

Despite being in a relatively dry climate, extreme weather is not a rare occasion. On 25 March 2019, flash floods from heavy rains has resulted in 19 deaths and over 200 injuries.

The highest record temperature was 43.4 °C (110.1 °F) on 3 July 2022[^cite_note-recordhighs-62] and the lowest record temperature was −14.0 °C (6.8 °F) on 5 January 1973.[^cite_note-recordlows-63]

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Iran](64704.md)

Shiraz is the economic center of southern Iran. The second half of the 19th century witnessed certain economic developments that greatly changed the economy of Shiraz. The opening of the Suez Canal in 1869 allowed the extensive import into southern Iran of inexpensive European factory-made goods, either directly from Europe or via India.[^cite_note-Bahai-70] Farmers in unprecedented numbers began planting cash crops such as opium poppy, tobacco, and cotton. A number of these export crops passed through Shiraz on their way to the Persian Gulf. Iranian long-distance merchants from Fars developed marketing networks for these commodities, establishing trading houses in Bombay, Calcutta, Port Said, Istanbul and even Hong Kong.[^cite_note-Bahai-70]

Shiraz's economic base is in its provincial products, which include grapes, citrus fruits, cotton and rice.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-71] Industries such as cement production, sugar, fertilizers, textile products, wood products, metalwork and rugs dominate.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-71] Shirāz also has a major oil refinery and is also a major center for Iran's electronic industries. 53% of Iran's electronic investment has been centered in Shiraz.[^cite_note-72]

The **Shiraz Special Economic Electronic Zone** or the **SEEZ** was established in 2000 with the purpose of boosting manufacturing in electronics and communications.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

The [Persian Gulf Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_Complex), located at the north end of the city, is the [largest mall in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_shopping_malls_in_the_world) in terms of the number of shops.[^cite_note-75]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Shiraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Shiraz)

Shiraz is known as the city of poets, gardens, wine, [nightingales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightingale) and flowers.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

The garden is an important part of Iranian culture . There are multiple old gardens in Shiraz such as the [Eram garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eram_garden) and the Afif abad garden. According to some people, Shiraz "disputes with [Xeres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xeres) \[or Jerez] in Spain the honour of being the birthplace of [sherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry)."[^cite_note-78] [Shirazi wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirazi_wine) originates from the city; however, under the current Islamic regime, liquor cannot be consumed except by religious minorities.[^cite_note-79]

Shiraz was the birthplace of the poet [Hafiz Shirazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Shirazi). Shiraz is a center for Iranian culture and has produced a number of famous poets. [Saadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_\(poet\)), a 12th- and 13th-century poet, was born in Shiraz. He left his native town at a young age for [Baghdad](4492.md) to study [Arabic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_literature) and [Islamic sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Science) at the [Nizamiyya of Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamiyya_of_Baghdad). When he reappeared in his native Shiraz, he was an elderly man. Shiraz, under Atabak Abubakr Sa'd ibn Zangy (1231–1260) was enjoying an era of relative tranquility. Saadi was not only welcomed to the city but he was highly respected by the ruler and enumerated among the greats of the province. He seems to have spent the rest of his life in Shiraz. A number of scientists also originate from Shiraz. [Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_al-Din_al-Shirazi), a 13th-century astronomer, mathematician, physician, physicist and scientist was from Shiraz. In his *The Limit of Accomplishment concerning Knowledge of the Heavens*, he also discussed the possibility of [heliocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliocentrism).[^cite_note-80]

## Higher education

Shiraz is home to a vibrant academic community. The [Shiraz University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University_of_Medical_Sciences) was the first university in Shiraz and was founded in 1946. Much older is the august Madrasa-e-Khan, or [Khan Theological School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Theological_School?action=edit\&redlink=1), with about 600 students; its tile-covered buildings date from 1627.[^cite_note-85]

Today [Shiraz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University) is the largest university in the province, and one of Iran's best academic centers. Other major universities in or nearby Shiraz are the [Islamic Azad University of Shiraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Azad_University_of_Shiraz),[^cite_note-86] [Shiraz University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University_of_Technology), and [Shiraz University of Applied Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University_of_Applied_Science_and_Technology?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-87]

The [Shiraz Regional Library of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_Regional_Library_of_Science_and_Technology) is a provincial library serving the public. Virtual University of Shiraz is one of the sub colleges of [Shiraz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University).

## Transportation

### Air



An [Iran Air](168635.md) [Airbus A320](164933.md) approaching Shiraz International Airport (2011)

[Shiraz International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_Shahid_Dastgheib_International_Airport), also known as [Shiraz Shahid Dastgheib International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_Shahid_Dastgheib_International_Airport), serves as the largest airport in southern [Iran](14653.md).

### Metro

Main article: [Shiraz Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_Metro)



[Shiraz Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_Metro)

Construction of a [metro system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_Metro) was started in 2001 by the Shiraz Urban Railway Organization.[^cite_note-88] The plan is to create six lines.[^cite_note-89] The length of the first Line is 22.4 km (13.9 mi), the length of the second line will be approximately 14 km (8.7 mi).[^cite_note-90]

The first three lines, when completed, will have 32 stations below ground, six above, and one special station connected to the [railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_railway_station). The first line was started in October 2014[^cite_note-91] between Shahid Dastgheib (airport) Metro Station and [Ehsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehsan_Metro_Station_\(Shiraz\)) stations.

### Bus



Shiraz has the [Iran](14653.md)'s third [Bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit).

The Shiraz and suburbs bus organization was established in 1966. In that year, the company purchased 10 buses from the Iran National Company using both cash and installments. The company had a staff of 10 drivers, 40 assistant drivers, 50 ticket sellers, and 10 repairmen and inspectors. The daily salary of each full-time driver (6 am to 10 pm) was set at 160 rials, while assistant drivers and ticket sellers were set at 83 rials. With the purchased buses, the company launched lines one, two, and three, which were welcomed by the people. Now Shiraz has 71 bus lines.[^cite_note-92]

Shiraz Train Station

Shiraz is connected with the rest of [Iran's railway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Railways). The trains arrive and leave from [Shiraz railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_railway_station), Iran's largest railway station according to surface area.[^cite_note-93]
