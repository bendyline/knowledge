The **United States Coast Guard** (**USCG**) is the [maritime security](8111405.md), [search and rescue](172599.md), and [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_law) [service branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md).[^cite_note-10] It is one of the country's eight [uniformed services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformed_services_of_the_United_States). The service is a maritime, military, multi-mission service unique among the [United States](3434750.md) military branches for having a maritime [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) mission with jurisdiction in both domestic and [international waters](394357.md) and a [federal](195149.md) [regulatory agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_agency) mission as part of its duties. It is the largest [coast guard](146790.md) in the world.&#x20;

The U.S. Coast Guard protects the United States' borders and economic and security interests abroad; and defends its sovereignty by safeguarding [sea lines of communication](1365004.md) and commerce across U.S. [territorial waters](496983.md) and its [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone). Like its [United States Navy](20518076.md) sibling, the U.S. Coast Guard maintains a global presence with permanently-assigned personnel throughout the world and forces routinely deploying to both [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_zone) and [blue-water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy) regions. As a humanitarian service, it provides emergency response and disaster management for a wide range of human-made and natural catastrophic incidents in the U.S. and throughout the world.[^cite_note-11]

The U.S. Coast Guard operates under the [U.S. Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) during peacetime. During times of war, it can be transferred in whole or in part to the [U.S. Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) under the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) by order of the [U.S. president](24113.md) or by act of [Congress](31756.md). Prior to its transfer to Homeland Security, it operated under the [Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Transportation) from 1967 to 2003 and the [Department of the Treasury](53667.md) from its inception until 1967.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] A congressional authority transfer to the Navy has only happened once: in 1917, during [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-NavyTransferWW1-14] As for [World War II](32927.md), the U.S. Coast Guard was transferred to the Navy by President [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) one month before the country's entry into the war.[^cite_note-NavyTransferWW2-15]

The U.S. Coast Guard was formed by a merger of the [U.S. Revenue Cutter Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Revenue_Cutter_Service) and the [U.S. Life-Saving Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Life-Saving_Service) on 28 January 1915, under the Department of the Treasury. The Revenue Cutter Service was created by Congress as the Revenue-Marine on 4 August 1790 at the request of [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton), and is therefore the oldest continuously operating naval service of the United States.[^cite_note-16] As secretary of the treasury, Hamilton headed the Revenue-Marine, whose original purpose was collecting [customs duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_duties) at U.S. seaports. By the 1860s, the service was known as the U.S. Revenue Cutter Service and the term Revenue-Marine gradually fell into disuse.[^cite_note-17]

In 1939, the [U.S. Lighthouse Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Lighthouse_Service) was also merged into the U.S. Coast Guard. As one of the country's six armed services, the U.S. Coast Guard and its predecessor have participated in every major U.S. war since 1790, from the [Quasi-War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-War) with France to the [Global War on Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_Terror).[^cite_note-USCG01-18][^cite_note-Reservist2012-19]

As of December 2021, the U.S. Coast Guard's authorized force strength is 44,500 active duty personnel[^cite_note-20] and 7,000 reservists.[^cite_note-21] The service's force strength also includes 8,577 full-time civilian federal employees and 21,000 uniformed auxiliarists of the [U.S. Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary).[^cite_note-22] The service maintains an extensive fleet of roughly 250 coastal and ocean-going [cutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_\(boat\)), patrol ships, buoy tenders, tugs, and icebreakers; as well as nearly 2,000 small boats and specialized craft. It also maintains an aviation division consisting of more than 200 helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft.[^cite_note-uscg.mil1-23] While the U.S. Coast Guard is the second smallest of the U.S. military service branches in terms of membership, the service by itself is the world's 12th largest naval force.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Mission

Main article: [Missions of the United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missions_of_the_United_States_Coast_Guard)



A USCG [HH-65 Dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HH-65_Dolphin) helicopter flies overhead as the Coast Guard Cutter *Resolute* steams near [Deepwater Horizon spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_Horizon_spill) site, July 4, 2010.

### Role

The Coast Guard carries out three basic roles, which are further subdivided into eleven statutory missions. The three roles are:

- [Maritime safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Sea_Patrol)

- [Maritime security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_security_\(USCG\))

- [Maritime stewardship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missions_of_the_United_States_Coast_Guard#Protection_of_natural_resources)

With a decentralized organization and much responsibility placed on even the most junior personnel, the Coast Guard is frequently lauded for its quick responsiveness and adaptability in a broad range of emergencies. In a 2005 article in *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine following [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina), the author wrote, "the Coast Guard's most valuable contribution to \[a military effort when catastrophe hits] may be as a model of flexibility, and most of all, spirit." Wil Milam, a [rescue swimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_swimmer) from [Alaska](624.md) told the magazine, "In the Navy, it was all about the mission. Practicing for war, training for war. In the Coast Guard, it was, take care of our people and the mission will take care of itself."[^cite_note-26]

### Missions

The eleven statutory missions as defined by law are divided into [homeland security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_security) missions and non-homeland security missions:[^cite_note-27]

#### Non-homeland security missions

- [Ice operations](483060.md), including the [International Ice Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Ice_Patrol)

- Living marine resources ([fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement))

- [Marine environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_environmental_protection)

- [Marine safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_safety_\(USCG\))

- [Aids to navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation_aid)

- [Search and rescue](172599.md)

#### Homeland security missions

- [Defense readiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEFCON)

- Maritime [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency)

- [Migrant interdiction](5044573.md)

- [Ports, waterways and coastal security (PWCS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Security)

- [Drug interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_Drugs)

### Search and rescue



USCG helicopter, [Rescue swimmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_swimmer) conduct search and rescue after [Hurricane Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma)

The U.S. Coast Guard Search and Rescue (CG-SAR) is one of the Coast Guard's best-known operations.[^cite_note-28] The *National Search and Rescue Plan* designates the Coast Guard as the federal agency responsible for maritime SAR operations, and the [United States Air Force](32090.md) as the federal agency responsible for inland SAR.[^cite_note-29] Both agencies maintain [rescue coordination centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Rescue_Coordination_Centers) to coordinate this effort, and have responsibility for both military and civilian search and rescue.[^cite_note-NSARC1-30] The two services jointly provide instructor staff for the National Search and Rescue School that trains SAR mission planners and coordinators. Previously located on Governors Island, New York, the school is now located at [Coast Guard Training Center Yorktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_Center_Yorktown) at Yorktown, Virginia.[^cite_note-31]

### National Response Center

Operated by the Coast Guard, the National Response Center (NRC) is the sole [U.S. Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Government) point of contact for reporting all [oil](158705.md), [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution), [radiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_contamination), [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution), and etiological [spills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) and discharges into the environment, anywhere in the United States and its territories.[^cite_note-32] In addition to gathering and distributing spill/incident information for [Federal On Scene Coordinators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_On_Scene_Coordinator) and serving as the communications and operations center for the National Response Team, the NRC maintains agreements with a variety of federal entities to make additional notifications regarding incidents meeting established trigger criteria. The NRC also takes Maritime Suspicious Activity and Security Breach Reports. Details on the NRC organization and specific responsibilities can be found in the National Oil and Hazardous Substances Pollution Contingency Plan.[^cite_note-33] [The Marine Information for Safety and Law Enforcement (MISLE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MISLE) database system is managed and used by the Coast Guard for tracking pollution and safety incidents in the nation's ports.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

### National Maritime Center

The National Maritime Center (NMC) is the merchant mariner credentialing authority for the USCG under the auspices of the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md). To ensure a safe, secure, and environmentally sound marine transportation system, the mission of the NMC is to issue credentials to fully qualified mariners in the United States maritime jurisdiction.[^cite_note-37]

### Authority as an armed service



A member of USCG [Maritime Security Response Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deployable_Specialized_Forces#Maritime_Security_Response_Team) West trains for a simulated terrorist threat aboard the motor vessel



[Maritime Safety and Security Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Safety_and_Security_Team) conduct operations in the Gulf of Aden Sept. 8, 2009 part of [Combined Task Force 151](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Task_Force_151), a multinational task force established to conduct counterpiracy operations off the coast of Somalia.

[Title 10 of the U.S. Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) says that "\[t]he term "armed forces" means the Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force, Space Force, and Coast Guard."[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] The Coast Guard is further defined by [Title 14 of the United States Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_14_of_the_United_States_Code): "The Coast Guard as established January 28, 1915, shall be a military service and a branch of the armed forces of the United States at all times. The Coast Guard shall be a service in the Department of Homeland Security, except when operating as a service in the Navy."[^cite_note-40] Coast Guard organization and operation is as set forth in [Title 33 of the Code of Federal Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_33_of_the_Code_of_Federal_Regulations).

On 25 November 2002, the [Homeland Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Act) was signed into law by U.S. President George W. Bush, designating the Coast Guard to be placed under the [U.S. Department of Homeland Security](58236.md). The transfer of administrative control from the U.S. Department of Transportation to the U.S. Department of Homeland Security was completed the following year, on 1 March 2003.[^cite_note-DHSTransfer2003-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

The U.S. Coast Guard reports directly to the civilian secretary of homeland security. However, under [14 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_14_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/14/3) as amended by section 211 of the Coast Guard and Maritime Transportation Act of 2006, upon the declaration of war and when [Congress](31756.md) so directs in the declaration, or when the [president](24113.md) directs, the Coast Guard operates under the Department of Defense as a service in the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy).[^cite_note-44]

As members of the military, coast guardsmen on active and reserve service are subject to the [Uniform Code of Military Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Code_of_Military_Justice) and receive the same pay and allowances as members of the same pay grades in the other uniformed services.[^cite_note-45]

The service has participated in every major U.S. conflict from 1790 through today, including landing troops on [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day) and on the Pacific Islands in [World War II](32927.md), in extensive patrols and shore bombardment during the [Vietnam War](32611.md), and multiple roles in [Operation Iraqi Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iraqi_Freedom). Maritime interception operations, coastal security, transportation security, and [law enforcement detachments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Enforcement_Detachments) have been its major roles in recent conflicts in [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-46]

On 17 October 2007, the Coast Guard joined with the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) and [U.S. Marine Corps](17349325.md) to adopt a new [maritime strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_strategy) called *[A Cooperative Strategy for 21st Century Seapower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Cooperative_Strategy_for_21st_Century_Seapower)* that raised the notion of prevention of war to the same philosophical level as the conduct of war.[^cite_note-Garamone-47] This new strategy charted a course for the Navy, Coast Guard and Marine Corps to work collectively with each other and international partners to prevent regional crises, man-made or natural, from occurring, or reacting quickly should one occur to avoid negative impacts to the United States. During the launch of the new U.S. maritime strategy at the International Seapower Symposium at the [U.S. Naval War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_War_College) in 2007, Coast Guard commandant admiral [Thad Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thad_Allen) said the new maritime strategy reinforced the time-honored missions the service has carried out in the United States since 1790. "It reinforces the Coast Guard maritime strategy of safety, security and stewardship, and it reflects not only the global reach of our maritime services but the need to integrate and synchronize and act with our coalition and international partners to not only win wars ... but to prevent wars," Allen said.[^cite_note-Garamone-47]

### Authority as a law enforcement agency

USCG Munro [Law Enforcement Detachments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Enforcement_Detachments) boarding team interdicts suspected drug smuggling vessel

[14 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_14_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/14/102) authorizes the Coast Guard to enforce U.S. federal laws.[^cite_note-48] This authority is further defined in [14 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_14_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 522](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/14/522), which gives law enforcement powers to all Coast Guard commissioned officers, warrant officers, and petty officers.[^cite_note-49] Unlike the other armed forces branches, which are prevented from acting in a law enforcement capacity by [18 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_18_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 1385](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1385), the [Posse Comitatus Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posse_Comitatus_Act), and [Department of Defense](7279897.md) policy, the Coast Guard is exempt from and not subject to the restrictions of the Posse Comitatus Act.[^cite_note-50]

Further law enforcement authority is given by [14 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_14_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 703](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/14/703) and [19 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_19_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 1401](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1401), which empower U.S. Coast Guard active and reserve commissioned officers, warrant officers, and petty officers as federal [customs officers](757040.md).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] This places them under [19 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_19_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 1589a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1589a), which grants customs officers general federal law enforcement authority, including the authority to:

> (1) carry a firearm;\
> &#x20;(2) execute and serve any order, warrant, subpoena, summons, or other process issued under the authority of the United States;\
> &#x20;(3) make an arrest without a warrant for any offense against the United States committed in the officer's presence or for a felony, cognizable under the laws of the United States committed outside the officer's presence if the officer has reasonable grounds to believe that the person to be arrested has committed or is committing a felony; and\
> &#x20;(4) perform any other law enforcement duty that the Secretary of Homeland Security may designate.

— 19 USC §1589a. Enforcement authority of customs officers[^cite_note-53]

The U.S. [Government Accountability Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) Report to the [House of Representatives](19468510.md), Committee on the Judiciary on its 2006 Survey of Federal Civilian Law Enforcement Functions and Authorities, identified the Coast Guard as one of 104 federal components that employed [law enforcement officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_officer).[^cite_note-54] The report also included a summary table of the authorities of the Coast Guard's 192 special agents and 3,780 maritime law enforcement boarding officers.[^cite_note-55]

Coast Guardsmen have the legal authority to carry their service-issued firearms on and off base. This is rarely done in practice, however; at many Coast Guard stations, commanders prefer to have all service-issued weapons in armories when not in use. Still, one court has held in the case of *People v. Booth* that Coast Guard boarding officers are qualified law enforcement officers authorized to carry personal firearms off-duty for self-defense.[^cite_note-56]

## History

Main article: [History of the United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Coast_Guard)



USCGC patrol boat forces North Vietnamese trawler aground after combat from direct fire ([Vietnam War](32611.md))

The Coast Guard traced its roots to the small fleet of vessels maintained by the [United States Department of the Treasury](53667.md) beginning in the 1790s to enforce tariffs (an important source of revenue for the new nation). [Secretary of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Treasury) [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton) lobbied Congress to fund the construction of ten [cutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_\(boat\)), which it did on 4 August 1790 (now celebrated as the Coast Guard's official birthday). Until the re-establishment of the Navy in 1798, these "revenue cutters" were the only naval force of the early United States. As such, the cutters and their crews frequently took on additional duties, including combating piracy, rescuing mariners in distress, ferrying government officials, and even carrying mail.[^cite_note-57] Initially not an organized federal agency at all, merely a "system of cutters," each ship operated under the direction of the customs officials in the port to which it was assigned. Several names, including "Revenue-Marine," were used as the service gradually became more organized. Eventually it was officially organized as the [United States Revenue Cutter Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Revenue_Cutter_Service). In addition to its regular law enforcement and customs duties, revenue cutters and their crews were used to support and supplement the Navy in various armed conflicts including the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War).[^cite_note-58]

A separate federal agency, the [U.S. Life-Saving Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Life-Saving_Service), developed alongside the Revenue-Marine. Prior to 1848, there were various charitable efforts at creating systems to provide assistance to shipwrecked mariners from shore-based stations, notably by the [Massachusetts Humane Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Humane_Society). The federal government began funding lifesaving stations in 1848 but funding was inconsistent and the system still relied on all-volunteer crews. In 1871, [Sumner Increase Kimball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumner_Increase_Kimball) was appointed chief of the Treasury Department's newly created Revenue Marine Division, and began the process of organizing the Revenue-Marine cutters into a centralized agency. Kimball also pushed for more funding lifesaving stations and eventually secured approval to create the Lifesaving Service as a separate federal agency, also within the Treasury Department, with fulltime paid crews.

In 1915 these two agencies, the Revenue Cutter Service and the Lifesaving Service, were merged to create the modern United States Coast Guard. The [Lighthouse Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Lighthouse_Service) and the [Bureau of Marine Inspection and Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Marine_Inspection_and_Navigation) were absorbed by the Coast Guard 1939 and 1942 respectively.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] In 1967, the Coast Guard moved from the [U.S. Department of the Treasury](53667.md) to the newly formed [U.S. Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Transportation), an arrangement that lasted until it was placed under the [U.S. Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) in 2003 as part of legislation designed to more efficiently protect American interests following the [terrorist attacks of 11 September 2001](5058690.md).[^cite_note-61]

In times of war, the Coast Guard or individual components of it can operate as a service of the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy). This arrangement has a broad historical basis, as the Coast Guard has been involved in wars as diverse as the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War), and the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), in which the cutter *Harriet Lane* fired the first naval shots attempting to relieve besieged [Fort Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sumter). The last time the Coast Guard operated as a whole within the Navy was in [World War II](32927.md), in all some 250,000 served in the Coast Guard during World War II.[^cite_note-62] A major fixture of the Coast Guard during WWII were the [Beach Patrol units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Beach_Patrol), responsible for patrolling the shorelines of the United States on-foot and horseback.

[Coast Guard Squadron One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Squadron_One), was a combat unit formed by the United States Coast Guard in 1965 for service during the [Vietnam War](32611.md). Placed under the operational control of the United States Navy, it was assigned duties in [Operation Market Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Market_Time). Its formation marked the first time since World War II that Coast Guard personnel were used extensively in a combat environment. The squadron operated divisions in three separate areas during the period of 1965 to 1970. Twenty-six [Point-class cutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-class_cutter) with their crews and a squadron support staff were assigned to the U.S. Navy with the mission of interdicting the movement of arms and supplies from the [South China Sea](74209.md) into [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam) by [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong) and [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam) [junk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_\(ship\)) and [trawler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_trawler) operators. The squadron also provided [81mm mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81mm_mortar) naval gunfire support to nearby friendly units operating along the South Vietnamese coastline and assisted the U.S. Navy during [Operation Sealords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sealords).[^cite_note-63]

[Coast Guard Squadron Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Squadron_Three?action=edit\&redlink=1), was a combat unit formed by the United States Coast Guard in 1967 for service during the [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-64] Placed under the operational control of the United States Navy and based in [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor). It consisted of five USCG High Endurance Cutters operating on revolving six-month deployments. A total of 35 High Endurance Cutters took part in operations from May 1967 to December 1971, most notably using their 5-inch guns to provide naval gunfire support missions.[^cite_note-Larzelere124-128-65]

Often units within the Coast Guard operate under [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) operational control while other Coast Guard units remain under the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md).[^cite_note-66]

### Deployable Operations Group/Deployable Specialized Forces

Main article: [Deployable Specialized Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deployable_Specialized_Forces)

The Deployable Operations Group (DOG) was a Coast Guard command established in July 2007. The DOG established a single command authority to rapidly provide the Coast Guard, Department of Homeland Security, Department of Defense, [Department of Justice](52563.md) and other interagency operational commanders adaptive force packages drawn from the Coast Guard's deployable specialized force units. The DOG was disestablished on 22 April 2013 and reorganized into Deployable Specialized Forces (DSF) units that were placed under the control of the Atlantic and Pacific Area Commanders.[^cite_note-67]

The planning for the unit began after the terrorist attacks of 11 September 2001, and culminated with its formation on 20 July 2007. Its missions included maritime law enforcement, [anti-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-terrorism), [port security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_security), pollution response, and [diving operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_diving).[^cite_note-68]

There were over 25 specialized units within the Deployable Operations Group including the [Maritime Security Response Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Security_Response_Team), [Maritime Safety and Security Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Safety_and_Security_Team), Law Enforcement Detachments, [Port Security Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Security_Unit), the [National Strike Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Strike_Force), and Regional Dive Lockers. The DOG also managed Coast Guard personnel assigned to the Navy Expeditionary Combat Command and was involved in the selection of Coast Guard candidates to attend [Navy BUD/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEAL_selection_and_training) and serve with [Navy SEAL Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs).[^cite_note-DOG-69]

### Shore establishments



The Douglas A. Munro Coast Guard Headquarters building in St. Elizabeths West Campus

Shore establishment commands exist to support and facilitate the mission of the sea and air assets and [Coastal defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_defense_and_fortification). U.S. Coast Guard Headquarters is located in Southeast Washington, D.C. Examples of other shore establishment types are [Coast Guard Sectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Sector) (which may include Coast Guard Bases), Surface Forces Logistics Center (SFLC),[^cite_note-77] [Coast Guard Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Coast_Guard_stations), [Coast Guard Air Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Air_Stations), and the [United States Coast Guard Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Yard). Training centers are included in the shore establishment commands. The military college for the USCG is called the [United States Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy)[^cite_note-78] which trains both new officers through a four year program and enlisted personnel joining the ranks of officers through a 17 week program called Officer Candidate School (OCS). Abbreviated TRACEN, the other Training Centers include [Training Center Cape May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Training_Center_Cape_May) for enlisted bootcamp,[^cite_note-79] [Training Center Petaluma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_Center_Petaluma)[^cite_note-80] and [Training Center Yorktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_Center_Yorktown)[^cite_note-81] for enlisted "A" schools and "C" schools, and [Coast Guard Aviation Technical Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Air_Station_Elizabeth_City)[^cite_note-82] and [Coast Guard Aviation Training Center Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Aviation_Training_Center)[^cite_note-83] for aviation enlisted "A" school, "C" schools, and pilot officer training. On 5 June 2026, the Coast Guard opened the Enlisted Training Center of Excellence at Coast Guard Training Center Birmingham-Southern.[^cite_note-84]

## Personnel

The Coast Guard has a total workforce of 87,569.[^cite_note-uscg.mil-85] The formal name for a uniformed member of the Coast Guard is "coast guardsman", irrespective of gender. "Coastie" is an informal term commonly used to refer to current or former Coast Guard personnel. In 2008, the term "guardian" was introduced as an alternative but was later dropped. Admiral [Robert J. Papp Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Papp_Jr.) stated that it was his belief that no Commandant had the authority to change what members of the Coast Guard are called as the term coast guardsman is found in Title 14 USC, which established the Coast Guard in 1915.[^cite_note-PappMessage-86][^cite_note-88] "Team Coast Guard" refers to the four components of the Coast Guard as a whole: Regular, Reserve, Auxiliary, and Coast Guard civilian employees.

### Commissioned officers

Main article: [United States Coast Guard officer rank insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_officer_rank_insignia)

Commissioned officers in the Coast Guard hold [pay grades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_grade) ranging from O-1 to O-10 and have the same rank structure as the Navy.[^cite_note-ranks-89][^cite_note-paygrades-90] Officers holding the rank of ensign (O-1) through lieutenant commander (O-4) are considered junior officers, commanders (O-5) and captains (O-6) are considered senior officers, and rear admirals (O-7) through admirals (O-10) are considered flag officers. The [commandant of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant_of_the_Coast_Guard) and the [vice commandant of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Commandant_of_the_Coast_Guard) are the only members of the Coast Guard authorized to hold the rank of admiral.[^cite_note-91]

The Coast Guard does not have medical officers or [chaplains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Chaplain) of its own. Instead, [chaplains from the U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_Chaplain_Corps), as well as officers from the [U.S. Public Health Service Commissioned Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Public_Health_Service_Commissioned_Corps) are assigned to the Coast Guard to perform chaplain-related functions and medical-related functions, respectively. These officers wear Coast Guard uniforms but replace the Coast Guard insignia with that of their own service.[^cite_note-92]

The Navy and Coast Guard share identical officer rank insignia except that Coast Guard officers wear a gold Coast Guard Shield in lieu of a line star or staff corps officer insignia.

### Warrant officers

Main article: [Warrant officer (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer_\(United_States\))

Highly qualified enlisted personnel in pay grades E-6 through E-9 with a minimum of eight years' experience can compete each year for appointment as warrant officers (WO). Successful candidates are chosen by a board and then commissioned as chief warrant officer two (CWO2) in one of twenty-one specialties. Over time, chief warrant officers may be promoted to chief warrant officer three (CWO3) and chief warrant officer four (CWO4). The ranks of warrant officer (WO1) and chief warrant officer five (CWO5) are not currently used in the Coast Guard. Chief warrant officers may also compete for the Chief Warrant Officer to Lieutenant Program. If selected, the warrant officer will be promoted to lieutenant (O-3E). The "E" designates over four years' active duty service as a warrant officer or enlisted member and entitles the member to a higher rate of pay than other lieutenants.

### Enlisted personnel

See also: [List of United States Coast Guard enlisted ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Coast_Guard_enlisted_ranks) and [List of United States Coast Guard ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Coast_Guard_ratings)

Enlisted members of the Coast Guard have pay grades from E-1 to E-9 and also follow the same rank structure as the Navy. Enlisted members in pay grades of E-4 and higher are considered *[petty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty_officer#United_States)* and follow career development paths very similar to those of Navy petty officers.[^cite_note-93]

Petty officers in pay grade E-7 and higher are *[chief petty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Petty_Officer#United_States)* and must attend the Coast Guard [Chief Petty Officer Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_Center_Petaluma#Chief_Petty_Officer_Academy), or an equivalent Department of Defense school, in order to be advanced to pay grade E-8. The basic themes of the school are:

- Professionalism

- Leadership

- Communications

- Systems thinking and lifelong learning

Enlisted rank insignia is also nearly identical to Navy enlisted insignia. The Coast Guard shield replacing the [petty officer's eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_enlisted_rates#E-4_to_E-6) on collar and cap devices for petty officers or enlisted rating insignia for seamen qualified as a ["designated striker"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_enlisted_rates#E-1_to_E-3). Group Rate marks (stripes) for junior enlisted members (E-3 and below) also follow Navy convention with white for seaman, red for fireman, and green for airman. In a departure from the Navy conventions, all petty officers E-6 and below wear red chevrons and all chief petty officers wear gold.[^cite_note-94]

### Training

#### Officer training



Training exercise at [Cape Disappointment State Park, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Disappointment_State_Park)&#x20;

The [U.S. Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy) is a four-year [service academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_service_academies) located in [New London, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_London,_Connecticut). Approximately 200 cadets graduate each year, receiving a [Bachelor of Science degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) and a commission as an [ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensign_\(rank\)) in the Coast Guard. Graduates are obligated to serve a minimum of five years on active duty. Most graduates are assigned to duty aboard Coast Guard cutters immediately after graduation, either as Deck Watch Officers (DWOs) or as Engineer Officers in Training (EOITs). Smaller numbers are assigned directly to flight training at [Naval Air Station Pensacola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Pensacola), Florida or to shore duty at Coast Guard [Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Sectors), District, or Area headquarters units.

In addition to the Academy, prospective officers, who already hold a college degree, may enter the Coast Guard through [Officer Candidate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_Candidate_School) (OCS), also located at the Coast Guard Academy. OCS is a 17-week course of instruction that prepares candidates to serve effectively as officers in the Coast Guard. In addition to indoctrinating students into a military lifestyle, OCS provides a wide range of highly technical information necessary to perform the duties of a Coast Guard officer.[^cite_note-96]

Graduates of OCS are usually commissioned as ensigns, but some with advanced graduate degrees may enter as [lieutenants (junior grade)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_\(junior_grade\)) or [lieutenants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant). Graduating OCS officers entering active duty are required to serve a minimum of three years, while graduating reserve officers are required to serve four years. Graduates may be assigned to a cutter, flight training, a staff job, or an operations ashore billet. OCS is the primary channel through which the Coast Guard enlisted grades ascend to the commissioned officer corps. Unlike the other military services, the Coast Guard does not have a [Reserve Officers' Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officers'_Training_Corps) (ROTC) program.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] However, the [Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary) offers the [Coast Guard Auxiliary University Programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary_University_Programs) (AUP), which provides college students with leadership training and experience in Coast Guard activities, though participation does not guarantee a commission.[^cite_note-99] The Coast Guard also operates the Select Reserve Direct Commission program, for prospective candidates interested serving as a Coast Guard Reserve Officer.[^cite_note-100]

Lawyers, engineers, intelligence officers, military aviators holding commissions in other branches of the U.S. Armed Forces requesting interservice transfers to the Coast Guard, graduates of maritime academies, and certain other individuals may also receive an officer's commission in the Coast Guard through the [Direct Commission Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Commission_Officer) (DCO) program. Depending on the specific program and the background of the individual, the course is three, four or five weeks long. The first week of the five-week course is an indoctrination week. The DCO program is designed to commission officers with highly specialized professional training or certain kinds of previous military experience.[^cite_note-101]

#### Recruit training



Recruit companies visiting [Arlington National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_National_Cemetery) for their one day of off-base liberty, which is their only break in an eight-week boot camp at the [Coast Guard Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Training_Center_Cape_May) in [Cape May, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_May,_New_Jersey)

Newly enlisted personnel are sent to eight weeks of [recruit training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training) at [Coast Guard Training Center Cape May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Training_Center_Cape_May) in [Cape May, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_May,_New_Jersey). New recruits arrive at [Sexton Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_W._Sexton) and remain there for three days of initial processing which includes haircuts, vaccinations, uniform issue, and other necessary entrance procedures. During this initial processing period, the new recruits are led by temporary company commanders. These temporary company commanders are tasked with teaching the new recruits how to march and preparing them to enter into their designated company. The temporary company commanders typically do not enforce any physical activity such as push ups or crunches. When the initial processing is complete, the new seaman recruits are introduced to their permanent company commanders who will remain with them until the end of training. There is typically a designated lead company commander and two support company commanders. The balance of the eight-week boot camp is spent in learning teamwork and developing physical skills. An introduction of how the Coast Guard operates with special emphasis on the Coast Guard's core values is an important part of the training.

The current nine Recruit Training Objectives are:

- [Self-discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-discipline)

- Military skills

- [Marksmanship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting)

- Vocational skills and academics

- Military bearing

- Physical fitness and wellness

- Water survival and swim qualifications

- [Esprit de corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esprit_de_corps)

- [Core values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard#Symbols) (Honor, Respect, and Devotion to Duty)

#### Service schools

Following graduation from recruit training, most members are sent to their first unit while they await orders to attend advanced training in [Class "A" Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_%22A%22_Schools). At "A" schools, Coast Guard enlisted personnel are trained in their chosen [rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Coast_Guard_ratings); rating is a Coast Guard and Navy term for enlisted skills synonymous with the Army's and Marine Corps' [military occupation codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_occupation_codes) (MOS) and Air Force's [Air Force Specialty Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Specialty_Code) (AFSC). Members who earned high [ASVAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASVAB) scores or who were otherwise guaranteed an "A" School of choice while enlisting may go directly to their "A" School upon graduation from Boot Camp.[^cite_note-103]

### Civilian personnel

The Coast Guard employs over 8,577 civilians in over two hundred different job types including [Coast Guard Investigative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Investigative_Service) [special agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_agents), lawyers, engineers, technicians, administrative personnel, tradesmen, and federal firefighters.[^cite_note-uscg.mil-85][^cite_note-104] Civilian employees work at various levels in the Coast Guard to support its various missions.[^cite_note-105]

### Boats



U.S. Coast Guard 45-foot (14 m) Response Boat Medium (RB-M)

The Coast Guard operates about 1,650 boats,[^cite_note-uscg.mil1-23] defined as any vessel less than 65 feet (20 m) long, which generally operate near shore and on inland waterways.

The Coast Guard boat fleet includes:

- [47-foot Motor Lifeboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47-foot_Motor_Lifeboat) (MLB): The Coast Guard's 47-foot (14 m) primary heavy-weather boat used for search and rescue as well as law enforcement and homeland security.

- [Response Boat – Medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Response_Boat_%E2%80%93_Medium) (RB-M): A new multi-mission 45-foot (14 m) vessel intended to replace the 41-foot (12 m) utility boat. 170 planned

- [Aids to Navigation Boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aids_to_Navigation_Boat) (TANB/BUSL/ATON/ANB): Various designs ranging from 26 to 55 feet (7.9 to 16.8 m) used to maintain aids to navigation.

The SPC-LE is 33 feet (10 m) long and capable of speeds in excess of 50 knots (93 km/h; 58 mph) and operations more than 30 miles (48 km) from shore.

- [25-foot Transportable Port Security Boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25-foot_Transportable_Port_Security_Boat) (TPSB): A 25-foot (7.6 m) well-armed boat used by [Port Security Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Security_Unit) for force protection.

- [Cutter Boat – Over the Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_Boat_%E2%80%93_Over_the_Horizon) (OTH): A 23-foot (7.0 m) rigid hull inflatable boat used by medium and high endurance cutters and specialized units.

### Aircraft



Every aircraft type in the U.S. Coast Guard fleet in June 2024. From left: [HC-130J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC-130J), [C-27J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alenia_C-27J_Spartan), [HC-144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_HC-144_Ocean_Sentry), [C-37B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-37B), [MH-60T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-60T), [MH-65E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_MH-65_Dolphin)

The Coast Guard operates approximately 201 [fixed and rotary wing aircraft](600659.md#Coast_Guard)[^cite_note-uscg.mil1-23] from 24 [Coast Guard Air Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Air_Stations) throughout the contiguous United States, Alaska, Hawaii, and Puerto Rico. Most of these air stations are tenant activities at civilian airports, several of which are former Air Force Bases and Naval Air Stations, although several are also independent military facilities. Coast Guard Air Stations are also located on active Naval Air Stations, [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard) bases, and Army Air Fields.

Coast Guard aviators receive Primary (fixed-wing) and Advanced (fixed or rotary-wing) flight training with their Navy and Marine Corps counterparts at [NAS Whiting Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Whiting_Field), Florida, and [NAS Corpus Christi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Corpus_Christi), Texas, and are considered [Naval Aviators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Aviator). After receiving [Naval Aviator Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Aviator_insignia), Coast Guard pilots, with the exception of those slated to fly the HC-130, report to [U.S. Coast Guard Aviation Training Center, Mobile, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Aviation_Training_Center) to receive 6–12 weeks of specialized training in the Coast Guard fleet aircraft they will operate. HC-130 pilots report to [Little Rock AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock_AFB), Arkansas, for joint C-130 training under the auspices of the [314th Airlift Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/314th_Airlift_Wing) of the [U.S. Air Force](32090.md).

Fixed-wing aircraft operate from Air Stations on long-duration missions. Helicopters operate from Air Stations and can deploy on a number of different cutters. Helicopters can rescue people or intercept vessels smuggling migrants or narcotics. Since the terrorist attacks of 11 September 2001, the Coast Guard has developed a more prominent role in national security and now has armed helicopters operating in high-risk areas for the purpose of maritime law enforcement and anti-terrorism.

The Coast Guard is now developing an [unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)](58900.md) program that will utilize the [MQ-9 Reaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-9_Guardian) platform for homeland security and search/rescue operations. To support this endeavor, the Coast Guard has partnered with the Navy and [U.S. Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) to study existing/emerging unmanned aerial system (UAS) capabilities within their respective organizations. As these systems mature, research and operational experience gleaned from this joint effort will enable the Coast Guard to develop its own cutter and land-based UAS capabilities.[^cite_note-117]

### Weapons



USCGC Legare fire the ship's MK-75 76mm cannon

#### Naval guns

Most Coast Guard Cutters have one or more naval gun systems installed, including:

- The [Oto Melara 76 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oto_Melara_76_mm), a radar-guided computer controlled gun system that is used on Medium Endurance Cutters. The 3-inch gun's high rate of fire and availability of specialized ammunition make it a multi-purpose gun capable of anti-shipping, anti-aircraft, ground support, and short-range anti-missile defense.

- The MK 110 57 mm gun, a radar-guided computer controlled variant of the [Bofors 57 mm gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bofors_57_mm_L/70_naval_artillery_gun). It is used on the [Legend-class cutter, also known as the National Security Cutter (NSC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend-class_cutter). It is a multi-purpose gun capable of anti-shipping, anti-aircraft, and short-range anti-missile defense. The stealth mount has a reduced radar profile. Also, the gun has a small radar mounted on the gun barrel to measure muzzle velocity for fire control purposes and can change ammunition types instantly due to a dual-feed system. It can also be operated/fired manually using a joystick and video camera (mounted on gun).

- The [Mk 38 Mod 0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M242_Bushmaster) weapons system consists of an M242 Bushmaster 25mm chain gun and the Mk 88 Mod 0 machine gun mount. A manned system, its gyro-stabilization compensates for the pitching deck. It provides ships with defensive and offensive gunfire capability for the engagement of a variety of surface targets. Designed primarily as a close-range defensive measure, it provides protection against patrol boats, floating mines, and various shore-based targets.

- The [Mk 38 Mod 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M242_Bushmaster) weapons system is a remotely operated Mk 38 with an electronic optical sight, laser range-finder, [FLIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FLIR), a more reliable feeding system, all of which enhance the weapon systems capabilities and accuracy.

- The [Phalanx CIWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_CIWS) (pronounced "sea-wiz") is a close-in weapon system for defense against aircraft and anti-ship missiles. it can also be used against a variety of surface targets. Consisting of a radar-guided 20 mm 6-barreled [M61 Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M61_Vulcan) cannon mounted on a swiveling base, it is used on the Coast Guard's National Security Cutters. This system can operate autonomously against airborne threats or may be manually operated with the use of electronic optical sight, laser range-finder and FLIR systems against surface targets.

- The [Sea PROTECTOR MK50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CROWS) is a remotely controlled gyro-stabilized [M2 .50 caliber heavy machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_Browning_machine_gun). The sight package includes a daylight video camera, a thermal camera and an eye-safe laser rangefinder operated by a joystick. It is also furnished with a fully integrated fire control system that provides ballistic correction. The Mk50s are used on only four [Marine Protector-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Protector-class_patrol_boat) Cutters, the [USCGC *Sea Fox* (WPB-87374)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Sea_Fox), [USCGC *Sea Devil* (WPB-87368)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Sea_Devil), [USCGC *Sea Dragon* (WPB-87367)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Sea_Dragon) and [USCGC *Sea Dog* (WPB-87373)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Sea_Dog_\(WPB-87373\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)

### "You have to go out, but you don't have to come back!"

This unofficial motto of the Coast Guard dates to an 1899 [United States Lifesaving Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Lifesaving_Service) regulation, which states in part: "In attempting a rescue, ... he will not desist from his efforts until by actual trial, the impossibility of effecting a rescue is demonstrated. The statement of the keeper that he did not try to use the boat because the sea or surf was too heavy will not be accepted, unless attempts to launch it were actually made and failed."[^cite_note-126]

### Coast Guard ensign



Ensign of the United States Coast Guard



Former Coast Guard ensign, used from 1915 to 1953

The Coast Guard [ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensign_\(flag\)) (flag) was first flown by the [Revenue Cutter Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_Cutter_Service) in 1799 to distinguish revenue cutters from merchant ships. A 1 August 1799 order issued by Secretary of the Treasury [Oliver Wolcott Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Wolcott_Jr.) specified that the Ensign would be "sixteen perpendicular stripes (for the number of states in the United States at the time), alternate red and white, the union of the ensign to be the arms of the United States in a dark blue on a white field."[^cite_note-127]

This ensign became familiar in American waters and served as the sign of authority for the Revenue Cutter Service until the early 20th century. The ensign was originally intended to be flown only on revenue cutters and boats connected with the Customs Service but over the years it was found flying atop custom houses as well, and the practice became a requirement in 1874. On 7 June 1910, President [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft) issued an executive order adding an emblem to (or "[defacing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defacement_\(flag\))") the ensign flown by the Revenue cutters to distinguish it from what is now called the Customs Ensign flown from the custom houses. The emblem was changed to the official seal of the Coast Guard in 1927.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

The purpose of the ensign is to allow ship captains to easily recognize those vessels having legal authority to stop and board them. It is flown only as a symbol of law enforcement authority and is never carried as a parade standard.[^cite_note-130]

### Coast Guard standard

The Coast Guard standard is used in parades and carries the battle honors of the Coast Guard. It was derived from the [jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_flags#Jacks) of the Coast Guard ensign which was flown by revenue cutters. The emblem is a blue eagle from the coat of arms of the United States on a white field. Above the eagle are the words "United States Coast Guard" below the eagle is the motto, "Semper Paratus" and the inscription "1790."[^cite_note-131]



The Service Mark was developed to distinguish Coast Guard cutters from other government and commercial ships.

### Service Mark ("Racing Stripe")

The Racing Stripe, officially known as the Service Mark, was designed in 1964 by the industrial design office of [Raymond Loewy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Loewy) Associates to give the Coast Guard a distinctive, modern image. Loewy had designed the colors for the [Air Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_One) fleet for [Jackie Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Kennedy). President [Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) was so impressed with his work, he suggested that the entire Federal Government needed his make-over and suggested that he start with the Coast Guard.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133] The stripes are canted at a 64-degree angle, coincidentally the year the Racing Stripe was designed.[^cite_note-134]

The racing stripe is borne by Coast Guard cutters, aircraft, and many boats. First used and placed into official usage as of 6 April 1967, it consists of a narrow blue stripe, a narrow white stripe between, and a broad CG red bar with the Coast Guard shield centered.[^cite_note-heraldry-9][^cite_note-135] Red-hulled icebreaker cutters and most HH-65/MH-65 helicopters (i.e., those with a red fuselage) bear a narrow blue stripe, a narrow empty stripe the color of the fuselage (an implied red stripe), and broad white bar, with the Coast Guard shield centered. Conversely, black-hulled cutters (such as buoy tenders and inland construction tenders) use the standard racing stripe. Auxiliary vessels maintained by the Coast Guard also carry the Racing Stripe, but in inverted colors (i.e., broad blue stripe with narrow white and CG red stripes) and the Auxiliary shield. Similar racing stripe designs have been adopted for the use of other coast guards and maritime authorities and many other law enforcement and rescue agencies.[^cite_note-136]

## Uniforms



Photo showing a variety of Coast Guard uniforms. From Left: Service Dress White, Tropical Blue, Service Dress Blue, Winter Dress Blue, Camouflage Utility Uniform, Operational Dress Uniform

Main article: [Uniforms of the United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniforms_of_the_United_States_Coast_Guard)

For most of the Coast Guard's history, its uniforms largely mirrored the style of U.S. Navy uniforms, distinguishable only by their insignia. In 1974, under the leadership of Admiral [Chester R. Bender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_R._Bender), the initial versions of the current Coast Guard Service Dress Blue and Tropical uniforms were introduced. This represented a major departure from many common conventions in naval and maritime uniforms. Notably, "Bender's Blues" was a common service dress uniform for all ranks, dispensing with the [sailor suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailor_suit) and [sailor cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailor_cap) formerly worn by enlisted members.[^cite_note-Regs-137] Rank insignia remained consistent with the naval pattern and some distinctly-nautical items such as the [pea coat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pea_coat), officer's sword, and dress white uniforms remained.[^cite_note-Regs-137]

Today, the Coast Guard's uniforms remain among the simplest of any branch of the armed forces, with fewer total uniforms and uniform variants than the other armed services. There are only three uniforms that typically serve as standard uniforms of the day—the Operational Dress Uniform, Tropical Blue, and Service Dress Blue (Bravo).[^cite_note-138]

## Coast Guard Reserve



A USCG [Port Security Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Security_Unit) boat patrolling in [Umm Qasr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Qasr), Iraq in 2003

Main article: [United States Coast Guard Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Reserve)

The [United States Coast Guard Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Reserve) is the reserve military force of the Coast Guard.[^cite_note-14USC704-139] The Coast Guard Reserve was founded on 19 February 1941. The Coast Guard has 8700 reservists[^cite_note-uscg.mil-85] who normally drill two days a month and an additional 12 days of active duty each year, although many perform additional drill and active duty periods, to include those mobilized to extended active duty. Coast Guard reservists possess the same training and qualifications as their active duty counterparts, and as such, can be found augmenting active duty Coast Guard units every day.

During the [Vietnam War](32611.md) and shortly thereafter, the Coast Guard considered abandoning the reserve program, but the force was instead reoriented into force augmentation, where its principal focus was not just reserve operations, but to add to the readiness and mission execution of every-day active duty personnel.[^cite_note-140]

Since 11 September 2001, reservists have been activated and served on tours of active duty, to include deployments to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and also as parts of Department of Defense combatant commands such as the [U.S. Northern](1685700.md) and [Central](338482.md) Commands. Coast Guard [Port Security Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Security_Unit) are entirely staffed with reservists, except for five to seven active duty personnel. Additionally, most of the staffing the Coast Guard provides to the [Navy Expeditionary Combat Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Expeditionary_Combat_Command) are reservists.[^cite_note-141]

The Reserve is managed by the Assistant Commandant for Reserve, Rear Admiral James M. Kelly, USCG.[^cite_note-142]

## Coast Guard Auxiliary



Badge of the [United States Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary)

Main article: [United States Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary)

The [United States Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary) is the uniformed, civilian volunteer component of the United States Coast Guard, created on 23 June 1939 by an act of Congress.[^cite_note-Title14-148] Although it is a civilian organization, it was originally named the "[United States Coast Guard Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Reserve)" and was later re-named the "United States Coast Guard Auxiliary" on 19 February 1941 when a [military reserve force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_force) for the Coast Guard was created. As part of "Team Coast Guard" (the term used to collectively describe all active, reserve, auxiliary, and civilian employees), the auxiliary carries out, or assists in, nearly all of the Coast Guard's noncombatant and non-law enforcement missions.[^cite_note-149] Auxiliarists are subject to direction from the commandant of the Coast Guard. As of 2022, there were approximately 26,000 members of the U.S. Coast Guard Auxiliary.[^cite_note-150]

Coast Guard policy has assigned many of its duties related to recreational boating safety to the Auxiliary, including public boating safety education and outreach. This includes offering boating skills courses, liaising with marine-related businesses at the local level, and providing voluntary Vessel Safety Checks (formerly called Courtesy Examinations) to the public.[^cite_note-151] Additionally, Auxiliarists use their own vessels, boats, and aircraft (once registered as Coast Guard facilities) to provide operational support to the Coast Guard by conducting safety patrols, assisting in search and rescue missions, inspecting [aids to navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aids_to_navigation), and performing other tasks on behalf of the Coast Guard.

Prior to 1997, Auxiliarists were largely limited to activities supporting recreational boating safety. In 1997, however, new legislation authorized the Auxiliary to participate in any and all Coast Guard missions except direct military and direct law enforcement.[^cite_note-152] Auxiliarists may directly augment active duty Coast Guard personnel in non-combat, non-law enforcement roles (e.g. radio communications watch stander, interpreter, cook, etc.) and may assist active duty personnel in inspecting commercial vessels and maintaining aids-to-navigation. Auxiliarists may support the law enforcement and homeland security missions of the Coast Guard but may not directly participate (make arrests, etc.), and Auxiliarists are not permitted to carry a weapon while serving in any Auxiliary capacity.[^cite_note-153]

## Medals and honors

Main article: [Awards and decorations of the United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awards_and_decorations_of_the_United_States_Coast_Guard)

See also: [Awards and decorations of the United States military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awards_and_decorations_of_the_United_States_military)

One Coast Guardsman, [Douglas Albert Munro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Albert_Munro), has earned the [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor), the highest military award of the United States.[^cite_note-Munro-154] Fifty-five coast guardsmen have earned the [Navy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Cross) and numerous men and women have earned the [Distinguished Flying Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Flying_Cross_\(USA\)).

The highest peacetime decoration awarded within the Coast Guard is the [Homeland Security Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Distinguished_Service_Medal); prior to the transfer of the Coast Guard to the Department of Homeland Security, the highest peacetime decoration was the [Department of Transportation Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transportation_Distinguished_Service_Medal). The highest unit award available is the [Presidential Unit Citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Unit_Citation_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-155]

In wartime, members of the Coast Guard are eligible to receive the Navy version of the Medal of Honor. A Coast Guard Medal of Honor is authorized but has not yet been developed or issued.[^cite_note-156]

In May 2006, at the Change of Command ceremony when Admiral [Thad Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thad_Allen) took over as Commandant, President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) awarded the entire Coast Guard, including the Coast Guard Auxiliary, the [Coast Guard Presidential Unit Citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Unit_Citation_\(US\)) with hurricane device, for its efforts during and after [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) and [Tropical Storm Rita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Rita).[^cite_note-157]

## Associations

### Coast Guard Aviation Association

Those who have piloted or flown in Coast Guard aircraft under official flight orders may join the [Coast Guard Aviation Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Aviation_Association) which was formerly known as the "Ancient Order of the Pterodactyl" ("Flying Since the World was Flat"). The Ancient Albatross Award is presented to the active duty USCG member who qualified as an aviator earlier than any other person who is still serving. Separate enlisted and officer awards are given.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

### Coast Guard CW Operators Association

The Coast Guard CW Operators Association (CGCWOA) is a membership organization comprising primarily former members of the United States Coast Guard who held the enlisted rating of Radioman (RM) or Telecommunications Specialist (TC), and who employed International Morse Code (CW) in their routine communications duties on Coast Guard cutters and at shore stations.[^cite_note-163]

### USCG Chief Petty Officers Association

Members of this organization unite to assist members and dependents in need, assist with Coast Guard recruiting efforts, support the aims and goals of the Coast Guard Chief Petty Officers Academy, keep informed on Coast Guard matters, and assemble for social amenities; and include Chief, Senior Chief, and Master Chief Petty Officers, active, reserve and retired. Membership is also open to all Chief Warrant Officers and Officers who have served as a Chief Petty Officer.[^cite_note-164]

### USCG Chief Warrant and Warrant Officers Association (CWOA)

Established in 1929, the Chief Warrant and Warrant Officers Association, United States Coast Guard (CWOA) represents Coast Guard warrant and chief warrant officers (active, reserve and retired) to the Congress, White House and the Department of Homeland Security. Additionally, the association communicates with the Coast Guard leadership on matters of concern to Coast Guard chief warrant officers.[^cite_note-165]

## Relationship to the Navy

Although the [Posse Comitatus Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posse_Comitatus_Act) prevents federal military personnel from acting in a law enforcement capacity, the Coast Guard is exempt from the law and fulfills a law enforcement role in naval operations. It provides [Law Enforcement Detachments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Enforcement_Detachments) (LEDETs) to Navy vessels, where they perform arrests and other law enforcement duties during Navy boarding and interdiction missions. In times of war, or when directed by the president, the Coast Guard operates as a service in the Navy and is subject to the orders of the secretary of the Navy until it is transferred back to the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md).[^cite_note-174] At other times, Coast Guard [Port Security Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Security_Unit) are sent overseas to guard the security of ports and other assets. The Coast Guard also jointly staffs the Navy's Naval Coastal Warfare Groups and Squadrons (the latter of which were known as Harbor Defense Commands until late-2004), which oversee defense efforts in foreign littoral combat and inshore areas. Additionally, Coast Guard and Navy vessels sometimes operate together in [search and rescue](172599.md) operations.
