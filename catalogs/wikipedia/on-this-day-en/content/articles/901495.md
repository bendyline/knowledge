**Mykolaiv** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Миколаїв [\[mɪkoˈlɑjiu̯\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/61/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%97%D0%B2.ogg/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%97%D0%B2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/61/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%97%D0%B2.ogg/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%97%D0%B2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Николаев, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Nikolayev* [\[nʲɪkɐˈla(j)ɪf\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)) is a port [city](4077166.md) and a [hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hromada) (municipality) in southern [Ukraine](31750.md). Mykolaiv is the [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) of [Mykolaiv Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_Raion) ([district](6719452.md)) and [Mykolaiv Oblast](494928.md) ([province](994124.md)).&#x20;

Located at the confluence of the [Inhul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhul) and [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug) rivers, the city serves as a transportation hub, containing a sea [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port), commercial port, and river port, as well as [highway](48519.md) and [railway junctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction_\(rail\)) and an [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport). Connected to the [Black Sea](3386.md), Mykolaiv is the location of an important bridge crossing. This city is one of the main [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) centers in the region. Aside from three shipyards within the city, there are a number of research centers specializing in shipbuilding, such as the [State Research and Design Shipbuilding Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Research_and_Design_Shipbuilding_Center), [Zoria-Mashproekt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoria-Mashproekt) and others. As of 2022, the city had a population of 470,011 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-2]

During the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), large parts of Mykolaiv were destroyed by Russian attacks.[^cite_note-3] Mykolaiv holds the honorary title "[Hero City of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_of_Ukraine)".

## Name

The city is known under two names, which differ in Ukrainian and Russian; there are several [transliterations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) of each name. The Ukrainian name of the city is Миколаїв, transliterated as *Mykolaiv*. The Russian name, Никола́ев, transliterates as *Nikolaev* or *Nikolayev*.

The city was founded in the course of the Russian conquests during the [Second Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1787%E2%80%931792\)) of 1787–1792. Founded by Prince [Grigory Potemkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Potemkin) as Nikolaev, it was the last of the many cities he established. On 27 August 1789,[^cite_note-4] Potemkin ordered its naming near the wharf at the mouth of the [Ingul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhul) river, on a high, cool and breezy spot where the Ingul river meets the [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug) river. To build the city he brought in peasants, soldiers, and Turkish prisoners; 2,500 were working there during 1789. The shipyards were built first (1788).[^cite_note-5]



Statue in Mykolaiv of [Saint Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas) after whom the city is named.

Potemkin named the city after [Saint Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas), the patron of seafarers, on whose day (6 December) he had obtained victory at the [siege of Ochakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ochakov_\(1788\))[^cite_note-6] in 1788. The name *Nikolaev* is known from the legal order (writ) Number 1065 by Prince Potemkin to [Mikhail Faleev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Faleev?action=edit\&redlink=1) dated 27 August 1789.[^cite_note-7]

In 1920, after the establishment of [Soviet power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_power), the [Odesa provincial council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa_Governorate) (of laborers and peasants' deputies) petitioned the Soviet Ukrainian government—the [All-Ukrainian Central Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Ukrainian_Central_Executive_Committee) (VUTSIK)—to rename the city of Mykolaiv to Vernoleninsk ("Faithful to Lenin"). As the city of Mykolaiv was a district center of the Odesan province, presumably, the petition would have been initiated by the [Odesa City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_City_Council), but documentary evidence of this so far has not been identified. On 15 April 1924 the Plenum of the Central Administrative-Territorial Commission of the VUTSIK considered and rejected the petition of the Odesan executive committee. Perhaps the members of the Soviet-Ukraine government thought that the name sounded too obsequious.[^cite_note-history.mk.ua-8]

Information regarding the alleged renaming of Mykolaiv was disseminated by German maps of the 1920s and 1930s, as well as in German encyclopedic publications in 1927 and 1932, which show Vernoleninsk on the USSR part of the European maps. The city was designated as Mykolaiv in publications of the same map in other languages.[^cite_note-history.mk.ua-8]

To distinguish Mykolaiv from the much smaller west Ukraine city of [Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv,_Lviv_Oblast) in [Lviv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Oblast), the latter is sometimes called "Mykolaiv on the Dniester" after the [major river](376633.md) that it is situated on, while the former is located on the [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug), another major river, and may also be called "Mykolaiv on the Bug".

## History

Main article: [History of Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mykolaiv)

### Early history



*View of the City of Mykolaiv*, painting by Fedor Alexeev, 1799

Archaeologists have found proof of ancient settlements on the territory of Mykolaiv.[^cite_note-9] In 2018, archaeologists discovered a sunken Ancient Greek ship near the Mykolaiv region, dating from the fifth century BC, the period of [Greek colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_colonization#Greek_colonies) of the Northern Black Sea. Researchers stated: "This Ancient Greek ship is one of the oldest known in the Northern Black Sea."[^cite_note-10]

The city has long had close associations with [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding).[^cite_note-11]

The area was populated throughout time by [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians), [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece), [Slavic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ancient_Slavic_peoples), hordes of nomads and free [Zaporozhian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Cossacks). However, the intensive settlement of the Mykolaiv peninsula started in the last quarter of the 18th century already after the capture of the northern Black Sea coast region by Russia from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).

The Russian Empire's [Black Sea Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) Headquarters was in Mykolaiv for more than 100 years until the [Imperial Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Navy) moved it to [Sevastopol](51587.md), near the southern tip of the [Crimean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Peninsula). During the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) (1853–1856), Mykolaiv became the main rear base to support Russia's efforts in the war. Most [businesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company) that were created in the city belonged to the military-industrial complex, and, consequently, Mykolaiv was closed to foreigners for many decades.[^cite_note-history-12] By the late 19th century, Mykolaiv's port ranked third in the Russian Empire (after [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) and [Odesa](55545.md)) in trade with foreign countries. Grain-export suppliers of the steppe region (of Ukraine and Southern Russia) were the greatest in the Russian Empire. Mykolaiv had become a great industrial center in the Southern Ukraine.[^cite_note-history-12]

Jews started to settle in Mykolaiv in the late 18th century.[^cite_note-13] By being in the area west of the Dnieper which was where Jews were legally allowed to reside (the [Pale of Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_of_Settlement)), Mykolaiv became a major Jewish centre of the Russian Empire in the 19th century. During the course of the 19th century, the Czarist governments largely banned Jews from living east of the [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River). In 1866 restrictions were lifted and the Jewish community of Mykolaiv developed rapidly but years later Jews suffered in the [pogroms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom) of May 1881 and April 1899.

### 20th century



Shipyard in Mykolaiv circa 1900

During the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence), the city was occupied in 1918 by [German troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Army) who were commanded by Field Marshal [Hermann von Eichhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_von_Eichhorn),[^cite_note-14] and passed to the [Ukrainian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State). It was subsequently occupied in 1919 by French and [Greek troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Army) who had been attached to the French-commanded [Armée d'Orient (1915–1919)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arm%C3%A9e_fran%C3%A7aise_d'Orient) in the course of the [Greek Ukrainian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Ukrainian_campaign?action=edit\&redlink=1) - which formed part of the [Southern Russia campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia_intervention) of 1918–1919 by the [Allies of World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) during the [Allied intervention in the Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_intervention_in_the_Russian_Civil_War).[^cite_note-15] In 1920, [Soviet power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Soviet_Union) was established.[^cite_note-history-12] Afterwards it was the seat of the [Mykolaiv Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_Governorate) of Ukraine,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] until it was merged with the [Odesa Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Governorate).[^cite_note-18]

In 1926 there were 21,786 Jews (about 20.8% of the total population) in Mykolaiv.[^cite_note-19]

In the course of [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) Mykolaiv was occupied on 16 August 1941 by German invaders. In September, German forces [massacred over 35,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaev_Massacre) non-combatants, most of them Jews, in the city and its region. In April 1942, the Stalag 364 [prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) was relocated from [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w) in [German-occupied Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) to Mykolaiv.[^cite_note-ushm-20] It was dissolved in December 1943.[^cite_note-ushm-20] During the occupation, an underground partisan sabotage group, the Mykolaiv Center, conducted [guerilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerilla) activities. On 28 March 1944 the city was liberated, in part because of Soviet Senior Lieutenant [Konstantin F. Olshansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Olshansky)'s marines and their daring raid during which the majority of his troops were killed.

In the post-war period Mykolaiv became one of the shipbuilding centers of the USSR, with three shipyards: [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Shipyard), [61 Kommunara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipyard_named_after_61_Communards), and [Okean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okean_Shipyard).



[Soviet-era apartment blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrushchevka) in Mykolaiv

The asteroid [8141 Nikolaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8141_Nikolaev) (1982 SO4) was discovered in 1982 by [Nikolai Chernykh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Chernykh) at the [Crimean Astrophysical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Astrophysical_Observatory) and was named in honor of the city.[^cite_note-21]

### 21st century

In March 2012, Mykolaiv gained international notoriety for lawlessness and [police corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_corruption) following the rape and [murder of Oksana Makar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Oksana_Makar).[^cite_note-22] Her three attackers were apprehended, but two were released because of family connections to local government officials. After a media outcry and public protests, all three attackers were charged with her murder.

During the [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) protests of 2013–2014, Mykolayiv was the scene of anti-[Yanukovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) protests. After the victory of Euromaidan, the situation calmed down somewhat until 7 April 2014, when some [pro-Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_pro-Russian_unrest_in_Ukraine) tried to take over the local administration building. Pro-Ukrainians stopped them from taking over the administration building and destroyed the pro-Russian camp not far from it, after which the situation in the city became calm.[^cite_note-23]

Until 2016, the main street of Mykolaiv was called Sovetskaya ("Soviet Street"). In 2016, it was renamed Soborna Street.[^cite_note-24]

Until 18 July 2020, Mykolaiv was incorporated as a [city of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)). It also served as the administrative center of Mykolaiv and [Vitovka Raions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitovka_Raion) even though it did not belong to any of these raions. In July 2020, as part of the administrative reform of Ukraine, which reduced the number of raions of Mykolaiv Oblast to four, the city of Mykolaiv was merged into Mykolaiv Raion.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]



Mykolaiv Regional State Administration after the [Russian rocket strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_government_building_missile_strike) on 29 March 2022

In February and March 2022, during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Russian military forces [attacked Mykolaiv and took the city into a semi-encirclement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mykolaiv).[^cite_note-27] [Ukrainian forces](42799055.md) barred Russian forces from the city, though Russian artillery [continued to shell it](74398144.md).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] By July, half of the pre-war population had left the city.[^cite_note-lemonde-31] The [Danish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Denmark) has invested nearly \$250 million to help the city with multiple issues such as construction, education, green energy and vocational training.[^cite_note-32]

Following a Russian strike against the main waterline connecting Mykolaiv with [Dnipro river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_river) on 12 April 2022, local inhabitants were left without access to centralized water supply for almost a month. Later, water for the needs of the city's population was being procured from local rivers. On 7 October 2025 [Oleksiy Kuleba](70796239.md), [Minister of Communities and Territorial Development of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Infrastructure_\(Ukraine\)), officially reported, that centralized supply of freshwater to the city has been resumed after the completion of a new water pipeline.[^cite_note-33]

## Administrative status

Mykolaiv is the [administrative center](181337.md) of [Mykolaiv Oblast](494928.md) ([region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)), as well as that of [Mykolaiv Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_Raion). It hosts the administration of [Mykolaiv urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_urban_hromada,_Mykolaiv_Oblast), one of the [hromadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hromada) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-admreform_2020_mykolaiv_mykolaiv-34]

## Geography



Bird's-eye view of the city center at the [river knee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knee_\(geography\)) of [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug) (Boh)

Mykolaiv is located in a primarily flat terrain area, the fertile, grain-producing steppe region of [southern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine). The nearest mountains to Mykolaiv are 300 km (186 mi) south, at the southern end of the [Crimean Peninsula](163045.md). The lack of any mountain barriers north of Mykolaiv means that cold Arctic winds can blow south, unimpeded by any terrain elevation, to Mykolaiv in winter.

Mykolaiv is on a peninsula along the [estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) of the [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug) river where it meets the [Inhul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhul) River, 65 km (40 mi) from the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-beautification-35] Both the Inhul River and the Southern Bug River follow very winding courses just before they join at the northeast corner of Mykolaiv. This has created several long and narrow peninsulas just north of Mykolaiv, and the main part of Mykolaiv is itself on a peninsula at a 180-degree bend in the Southern Bug River.

The area of the city is 260 km<sup>2</sup> (100 sq mi).[^cite_note-passport-36]

Mykolaiv is in the second time zone ([Eastern European Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_European_Time)).

### Climate

The city's climate is moderately continental- [cold semi arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) steppe climate with cold winters and warm to hot summers.[^cite_note-beautification-35][^cite_note-climate-41] Mykolaiv's average temperature is 10 °C (50 °F). The lowest average temperature is in January −1.9 °C (29 °F), the highest in July 23.1 °C (74 °F).[^cite_note-climate-41]

Mykolaiv has an average of 472 mm (19 in) of precipitation per year, with the lowest precipitation in October, and the most in July. Mykolaiv has mild snow cover every year.[^cite_note-climate-41]

Average relative humidity is 73% for the year; the lowest humidity is in August (60%); the highest in December (86%).[^cite_note-climate-41] The lowest cloud are seen in August; the highest are in December.[^cite_note-climate-41]

The prevailing winds come from the North; the least frequent source of wind is the Southeast. The maximum wind speed is in February, the lowest is from July through September. In January, the average wind speed is 4.1 m/s (meters per second); in July, the average is 3.1 m/s.[^cite_note-climate-41]

## Awards

The Soviet Government awarded Mykolaiv the [Order of the Red Banner of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Red_Banner_of_Labour) on 31 December 1970, for successfully fulfilling its assignments for the development of industrial production, in the USSR's five-year economic plan.

Mykolaiv is divided into four [urban districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_districts_of_Ukraine):

- **Tsentralnyi District** is located in the northwest of the city. It includes the historic center of Mykolaiv, Raketne Urochyshche, Temvod, Soliani, Pivnichnyi, Ternivka (with a separate village council), Matviivka, Varvarivka.

- **Zavodskyi District** is located in the west of the city. In this area, many industrial enterprises are concentrated. It also includes neighborhoods Lisky and Namyv, as well as towns of Velyka Korenykha and Mala Korenykha.

- **Inhulskyi District** (former Leninskyi District) is located in the east of Mykolaiv. Among other neighborhoods, it includes the PTZ, Novyi Vodopii, Staryi Vodopii. The district has a zoo, bus and railway stations.

- **Korabelnyi District** is located in the south of the city. It includes a broad beam, Bohoiavlenskyi, Balabanivka, Kulbakyne.

Mykolaiv adopted its current coat of arms on 26 September 1997. Its design came from the one adopted in 1883, by removing the symbol of [Kherson province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson_Governorate) to which Mykolaiv didn't belong anymore.[^cite_note-CityWSite1-61] Mykolaiv adopted its current flag on 2 July 1999 and its anthem on 11 September 2004.[^cite_note-CityWSite1-61]

## Economy



[Mykolayiv Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolayiv_Shipyard)



Modern [shopping mall](235723.md) in the Mykolaiv city center

Mykolaiv is a major shipbuilding center of Ukraine since the time of both the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (1721–1917) and the now defunct [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (1922–1991) and an important [river port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_port). The city has [three major shipyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_Shipyard_\(disambiguation\)) one of which is capable of building large [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) ships. Other important industries are [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering), [power engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_engineering), [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy) and food industry.

Mykolaiv was [closed to foreign visitors until the late 1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_city) due to the large number of secret Soviet Navy projects that took place in the city (as well as due to its military air base, turbine factory and military port). The majority of the Soviet Navy's surface ships, including its only aircraft carrier, [the aircraft carrie&#x72;*&#x20;Kuznetsov*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov), were built in Mykolaiv.

In May 2011, Ukrainian President [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) visited Mykolaiv and said that Ukraine was serious about reviving and further developing its shipbuilding industry in the Mykolaiv region.[^cite_note-62]

One of the largest industrial businesses in the city is the Mykolaiv Aluminia Factory (formerly part of [Rusal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusal) and currently owned by [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore)), which produces aluminium oxide (alumina), raw material for the production of aluminum.[^cite_note-63]

In addition to heavy industry, the city has a developed food processing industry, including a juice maker, [Sandora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandora) (part of [PepsiCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PepsiCo)), a dairy products maker, Laktalis-Mykolaiv, and a brewery, Yantar. [Nibulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nibulon) (Ukrainian: Нібулон), one of Ukraine's biggest agriculture companies specialized in the production and exportation of grain such as wheat, barley and corn is headquartered in Mykolaiv. The company has its own maritime fleet and shipyard, the [Nibulon Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nibulon_Shipyard), and also developed its own river fleet to transport grain to export terminals.[^cite_note-64]

In January 2017, the Mykolaiv Development Agency released a promo video of the city's investment potential.[^cite_note-65]

The Mykolaiv Armored Factory (owned by [Ukroboronprom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukroboronprom)) has been a large repair facility for Ukraine's military since the beginning of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md). Engineers at the plant designed an armored ambulance based on the [BTR-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-70) to be used by the Ukrainian Armed Forces.[^cite_note-66]

## Ukrainian military presence

Mykolaiv, being an important strategic city in southern Ukraine has a significant Ukrainian military presence, including the shipyards that build Ukraine's surface navy ships, the Mykolaiv [Ukrainian Navy](1590554.md) base, the "MARP" aircraft repair factory (Mykolaiv), and the Kulbakino army base (in the Mykolaiv Oblast, outside of the city of Mykolaiv).

Previously for many years after [World War II](32927.md) the city had been home to the 92nd Guards Motor Rifle Division, the former [92nd Guards Rifle Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/92nd_Guards_Rifle_Division). The [79th Airmobile Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_Airmobile_Brigade_\(Ukraine\)) is based in the city.[^cite_note-67]

Mykolaiv is one of Ukraine's most important transportation junctions. It is a major commercial river and sea port, and a major highway and rail junction.[^cite_note-68] Mykolaiv also has a dual-function passenger and freight airport, but passenger service at the airport is not significant, compared to Ukraine's major airports. In addition to the airport and sea and river port, Mykolaiv has two train stations, and an intercity bus station.

### Air

[Mykolaiv Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_Airport) (IATA code NLV) is one of the largest and most technically well equipped airports in the South of Ukraine, which serves the city. The airport, located northwest of Mykolaiv, is mainly used for air freight and only has limited passenger service. Russian airline [UTAir Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTAir_Aviation) offers flights from Mykolaiv to [Moscow](19004.md) ([Vnukovo – VKO airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vnukovo_International_Airport)). In addition, there are one-hour passenger flights from [Odesa](55545.md) (the nearest major airport) to Mykolaiv. Almost all airline passenger service in the South West of Ukraine (where Mykolaiv is located) is through Odesa International Airport: to reach Mykolaiv by airplane, tourists generally reach Odesa by plane, and then take a bus, taxi or train, for approximately 2 hours, to Mykolaiv. Odesa, the largest city in South West Ukraine, is 132 km (82 mi) from Mykolaiv.[^cite_note-69]

[Kulbakyno airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulbakino_Air_Base), also known as Mykolaiv, is a Class I military [air base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_base) located just to the southeast of the city center, in Kulbakyno. It primarily supports wings of [Sukhoi Su-24](382335.md), [Sukhoi Su-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-25), [Sukhoi Su-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-27), and [Mikoyan MiG-29](185653.md).[^cite_note-70]

### Bus travel long distance

Mykolaiv is an 8.5-hour bus ride from Kyiv's main bus station.[^cite_note-71] Ukrainian private national bus companies Gyunsel and Avtoluks operate overnight buses from Kyiv to Mykolaiv seven nights per week. The bus station in Mykolaiv is located at Prospekt (Avenue) Bohoyavlenskyi 21.

#### Bridges



Varvarivskyi Bridge



[Inhul River bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhul_Bridge) in Mykolaiv



Old pedestrian bridge over Inhul River

Mykolaiv, being located at the confluence of two major rivers, has two main bridges. The Varvarivskyi Bridge over [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug) is a [swing bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_bridge) with Europe's largest span (134 m, 440 ft).[^cite_note-KYIVDIPROTRANS-72] It is also the southernmost bridge over the Southern Bug. The bridge connects the North coast of Mykolaiv to its Tsentralnyi District, located on the West Bank of the river. The Odeske Highway crosses the bridge and then continues south-west to Odesa.

Another major bridge is the [Inhul Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhul_Bridge) crossing the [Inhul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhul) River. The bridge leads from the north coast of Mykolaiv, and goes north-northeast to the peninsula on the north side of the Inhul, just north of Mykolaiv. On the north Side of the Inhul River, the Heroyiv Stalingrada Highway crosses the bridge, streaming into Pushkinska Street on the other side.

### Rail

Overnight train travel in sleeper-berth passenger trains is a very common way to travel long distances in Ukraine. There are nightly trains from Kyiv's main passenger train station to Mykolaiv.[^cite_note-73]

In addition to Kyiv, trains from Mykolaiv regularly run to the two closest major cities to Mykolaiv: Odesa (south west of Mykolaiv); and Kherson (south of Mykolaiv).[^cite_note-rtpp-74] Direct trains to Moscow (26 hours), Kyiv (8-10), Lviv (18), Odesa (5), the Crimea (8 hours) depart every day. Train departures timetable. All trains have coach cars.

Mykolaiv's passenger train station is called [Mykolaiv – Passenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_railway_station). It at the intersection of Myru Avenue and Novozavodska Street 5 (in Ukraine street address numbers are placed after the street name).[^cite_note-rtpp-74] From the city square outside the railway station, buses depart to all other parts of Mykolaiv.

Mykolaiv's freight train station is called Mykolaiv-Gruzovoi (Freight). It is located at Pryvokzalna ploshcha (Square).[^cite_note-rtpp-74]

### Water-borne travel

Though a major Ukrainian commercial sea port, Mykolaiv has no regular passenger water-borne service. Water transport is offered by three sea ports and one river port, and also by several terminals. The port is linked with the sea by [Dnieper-Bug Estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper-Bug_Estuary) canal. The canal begins at the island [Berezan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berezan_Island) and extends 44 kilometers (27 miles) until it reaches the port of Mykolaiv. The canal consistes of 13 tracks, six of which reach [Dnieper Estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Estuary), and the rest along the river [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug). The width of the canal is 100 meters (330 feet). Its depth is 10.5 meters (34 feet).

Large ocean-going ships can reach Mykolaiv year round, via the Southern Bug River. The Southern Bug River, which flows into the Black Sea 65 km (40 mi) south of Mykolaiv, is 1.5 km (0.9 mi) wide in Mykolaiv. Mykolaiv's passenger river port is at Varvarovskii Spusk (Descent) 5.[^cite_note-rtpp-74]

### Local transportation



&#x20;[Tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_tram) in Mykolaiv

The main forms of city transport are fixed-route *[marshrutkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshrutka)*, buses, trolley buses, and [trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_tram).

#### Tram

Main article: [Mykolaiv tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_tram)

The length of Mykolaiv's tram lines is 73 kilometers (45 miles). From 1897 until 1925 Mykolaiv's trams were [pulled by horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_tram). Trams began to be powered by electricity in 1915, and this has continued through the present. At their inception, the tracks were 1,000 mm (3 ft 3+3⁄8 in) [metre gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge), but during the period from 1952 to 1972 they were switched to standard gauge.

#### Тrolleybus

The length of Mykolaiv's trolleybus lines is 59 kilometers (37 miles). Mykolaiv's trolleybuses have operated since 29 October 1967.

## Education



&#x20;[Admiral Makarov National University of Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Makarov_National_University_of_Shipbuilding)

There are several universities in Mykolaiv. The main universities are: [Admiral Makarov National University of Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Makarov_National_University_of_Shipbuilding) (leading shipbuilding university in Ukraine), [Petro Mohyla Black Sea State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Mohyla_Black_Sea_State_University), Sukhomlinsky National University of Mykolaiv, and [Mykolaiv State Agrarian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_State_Agrarian_University).

Sukhomlinsky National University of Mykolaiv is the oldest university in Mykolaiv. The idea of the university foundation arose in the 1860s, but it was realized only on 18 July 1913, when the Mykolaiv Teacher's Institute was founded. Nowadays there are 7,000 students studying at the university, 300 teachers working at 36 departments. Annually, the university graduates 1,000 specialists and 60-70 undergraduates.

There are 10 higher education institutions in Mykolaiv of level III or IV accreditation. 65 general education schools, lycees, gymnasium schools, 3 evening schools, and 12 private learning institutions are in the city.

In a survey in June–July 2017, adult respondents reported the following educational levels:[^cite_note-IRI2017-57]

- 5% primary or incomplete secondary education.

- 26% general secondary education.

- 31% vocational secondary education.

- 38% university education (including incomplete university education).

## Culture

There are three performing arts theaters in Mykolaiv: the Academic Ukrainian Theater of Drama and Musical Comedy, the Mykolaiv State Puppet Theater, and the Mykolaiv Academic Art Russian Drama Theater. In addition, the Mykolaiv Oblast Philharmonic performs in the city.

Mykolaiv has the following museums: [Mykolaiv Regional Museum of Local History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv_Regional_Museum_of_Local_History), [The Museum of Shipbuilding and Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Museum_of_Shipbuilding_and_Fleet), the Museum of the World War II Partisan Movement, [the V. V. Vereshchagin Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_V._V._Vereshchagin_Mykolaiv_Art_Museum).

Three movie theaters operate in Mykolaiv: Rodina (Motherland), Pioneer, Multiplex. Movies shown include the latest Hollywood films dubbed into Ukrainian and modern Russian films.

## Media

Publications include *Vecherniy Nikolayev* (*Evening Mykolaiv*), *Nikolaevsckie Novosti* (*Mykolaiv News*), and *Yuzhnaya Pravda* (*Southern Truth*). Many publications have an Internet version, but exist in an independent Internet publication.

Television programs that are broadcast in Mykolaiv include movies, news, dramas (some of which originated in other countries, such as Mexico and the US, and are dubbed), cartoons, and professional sporting events such as Ukrainian football (soccer). Mykolaiv has the following TV channels: 1+1; 2+2; 5 Kanal; ICTV; Inter; Real Estate TV; Tonis: K1: Kanal Ukraina; Kultura (Ukraina); CTB; TV: TET; and TRK.[^cite_note-76]

## Sports

Mykolaiv is represented within the Ukrainian [Bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy) and Rink-bandy Federation.[^cite_note-77]



Central City Stadium, Mykolaiv

[MFC Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MFC_Mykolaiv) (Municipal Football Club "Mykolaiv", Ukrainian: Муніципальний футбольний клуб "Миколаїв") is a Ukrainian football club. Created back in 1920, it is one of the oldest sports clubs in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). The club existed as a football team of the [Black Sea Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Shipyard). The club has been relegated three times from the [Ukrainian Premier League](2322171.md). MFС Mykolaiv's best achievement in the Ukrainian Premier League was the 13th place (in 1994–95). Since the 2022 Russian aggression, the club has suspended its participation in professional competitions. Mykolaiv's main football stadium is [Tsentralnyi Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsentralnyi_Stadion_\(Mykolaiv\)). There are several smaller stadiums located within or nearby Mykolaiv, among which is the [Stadion Parku Peremohy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Peremohy_Stadium) (Victory Park Stadium). As for other professional-level football clubs in the city, there were also [FC Vast Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Vast_Mykolaiv), [SC Enerhiya Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Enerhiya_Mykolaiv), [FC Sudnobudivnyk Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Sudnobudivnyk_Mykolaiv_\(2016\)). Besides those clubs, there are many smaller amateur-level clubs such as Varvarivka, [Torpedo Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Torpedo_Mykolaiv), and many others.

Mykolaiv's professional basketball team is [MBC Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBC_Mykolaiv). The team has won or finished second or third in several international tournaments since 1988, and won the Ukrainian Championship in 1992. MBC Mykolaiv is part of the Ukrainian Basketball SuperLeague, which is the top professional basketball league in Ukraine.&#x20;

The Mykolaiv students won two gold and bronze medals in the Ukrainian Academic Rowing Cup.[^cite_note-78]

Among other types of sports, Mykolaiv hosts an [American football](18951490.md) team, Mykolaiv Vikings, and there are many enthusiasts of sailing sports.
