The **High Court of Australia** is the [apex court](4913027.md) of the [Australian legal system](1948944.md).[^cite_note-2] It exercises [original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction) and [appellate jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_jurisdiction) on matters specified in the [Constitution of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia) and supplementary legislation.

The High Court was established following the passage of the *[Judiciary Act 1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_1903)* (Cth).[^cite_note-Judiciary_Act-3] Its authority [derives from chapter III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_III_court) of the Australian Constitution, which vests it (and other courts the Parliament creates) with the [judicial power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) of the Commonwealth.[^cite_note-4] Its internal processes are governed by the *High Court of Australia Act 1979* (Cth).[^cite_note-High_Court_of_Australia_Act-5]

The court consists of seven justices, including a [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Australia), currently [Stephen Gageler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Gageler). Justices of the High Court are appointed by the [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia) on the formal [advice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_\(constitutional_law\)) of the [attorney-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_of_Australia) following the approval of the [prime minister](24117.md) and [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Australia).[^cite_note-6] They are appointed permanently until their mandatory retirement at age 70, unless they retire earlier.

Typically, the court operates by receiving applications for appeal from parties in a process called [special leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_leave_in_the_High_Court_of_Australia). If a party's application is accepted, the court will proceed to a full hearing, usually with oral and written submissions from both parties. After conclusion of the hearing, the result is decided by the court. The special leave process does not apply in situations where the court elects to exercise its original jurisdiction; however, the court typically delegates its original jurisdiction to Australia's inferior courts.

The court has resided in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra) since 1980, following the construction of a purpose-built [High Court building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Australia_Building), located in the [Parliamentary Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Triangle) and overlooking [Lake Burley Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Burley_Griffin).[^cite_note-AHD-7]

Sittings of the court previously rotated between state capitals, particularly [Melbourne](17306237.md) and [Sydney](27862.md), and the court continues to regularly sit outside Canberra.

## Role

The High Court exercises both [original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction) and [appellate jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_jurisdiction).

Sir [Owen Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Dixon) said on his swearing in as Chief Justice of Australia in 1952:[^cite_note-Dixon_cj_speech-8]

> The High Court's jurisdiction is divided in its exercise between constitutional and federal cases which loom so largely in the public eye, and the great body of litigation between man and man, or even man and government, which has nothing to do with the Constitution, and which is the principal preoccupation of the court

The broad jurisdiction of the High Court means that it has an important role in Australia's legal system.[^cite_note-Barwick_keystone_intro-9]

### Original jurisdiction

Its original jurisdiction is determined by sections 75 and 76 of Australia's Constitution. Section 75 confers original jurisdiction in all matters:

1. arising under any treaty

2. affecting [consuls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_general) or other representatives of other countries

3. in which the Commonwealth, or a person suing or being sued on behalf of the Commonwealth, is a party

4. between States, or between residents of different States, or between a State and a resident of another State

5. in which a writ of [mandamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandamus) or [prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_\(writ\)) or an [injunction](39343.md) is sought against an officer of the Commonwealth.

Section 76 provides that Parliament may confer original jurisdiction in relation to matters:

1. arising under the constitution or involving its interpretation

2. arising under any laws made by the Parliament

3. of [admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_law) and maritime jurisdiction

4. relating to the same subject matter claimed under the laws of different states.

Constitutional matters, referred to in section 76(i), were conferred on the High Court by section 30 of the *[Judiciary Act 1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_1903)*.[^cite_note-Judiciary_Act-3] While the conferral of constitutional matters might be removed by amending the Judiciary Act, section 75(iii) (suing the Commonwealth) and section 75(iv) (conflicts between states) are broad enough that many constitutional matters would still be within original jurisdiction. The original constitutional jurisdiction of the High Court is now well established; the [Australian Law Reform Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Law_Reform_Commission) has described the reference to constitutional matters in section 76 rather than in section 75 as "an odd fact of history".[^cite_note-ALRC_92-10] The [1998 Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Constitutional_Convention_1998) recommended an amendment to the constitution to prevent the possibility of the jurisdiction being removed by Parliament.

The word "matter" in sections 75 and 76 has been understood to mean that the High Court is unable to give [advisory opinions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advisory_opinion).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Appellate jurisdiction

The court is empowered by section 73 of the Constitution to hear appeals from the supreme courts of the states and territories; as well as any court exercising federal jurisdiction.[^cite_note-13] It may also hear appeals of decisions made in an exercise of its own original jurisdiction.[^cite_note-14]

The High Court's appellate jurisdiction is limited by the *[Judiciary Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_1903)*, which requires special leave to be granted before the hearing of an appeal. [Special leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_leave_in_the_High_Court_of_Australia) may only be granted where a question of law is raised which is of public importance, involves a conflict between courts or "is in the interests of the administration of justice".

Since November 2023, the High Court has adopted the practice of deciding the majority of special leave applications on the basis of written submissions only. In adopting this practice, the High Court also made the decision to publish decisions in special leave applications on its public website rather than in open court.[^cite_note-15]

### Appeals to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council

Appeals to the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council) in London were a notable controversy when the Constitution was drafted. Section 74 of the Constitution, as it was put to voters, stated that there would be no appeals to the Privy Council in any matter involving the interpretation of the federal or state constitutions.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

However, section 74 as enacted by the Imperial Parliament instead only prohibited appeals on constitutional matters when they related to the respective powers of the states and the Commonwealth ("*inter se*" matters),[^cite_note-18] and even then allowed the High Court discretion to waive the prohibition by certifying cases as being approriate for appeal to the Privy Council. The High Court has used this discretion only once, in 1912,[^cite_note-19] and in 1985, in [*Kirmani v Captain Cook Cruises Pty Ltd* (No 2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirmani_v_Captain_Cook_Cruises_Pty_Ltd_\(No_2\)), it denied certification and further declared that the discretion was "obsolete", that "such limited purpose as it had has long since been spent", and that it was "impossible to suppose" that the Court would ever use it again.[^cite_note-kirmani_no2-20]

No certificate was required to appeal constitutional cases not involving *inter se* matters, such as in the interpretation of section 92 (relating to the freedom of inter-state commerce), and thus the Privy Council regularly heard appeals against High Court decisions.[^cite_note-21] In some cases the Council acknowledged that the Australian common law had developed differently from English law and thus did not apply its own principles.[^cite_note-uren-22][^cite_note-viro-23][^cite_note-Gleeson_s74-24] Other times it followed English authority, and overruled decisions of the High Court.

This arrangement led to tensions between the High Court and the Privy Council. In *Parker v The Queen* (1964), Chief Justice Sir [Owen Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Dixon) led a unanimous judgment rejecting the authority of the [House of Lords](13658.md) decision in *[DPP v Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homicide_in_English_law#Murder)*, writing, "I shall not depart from the law on this matter as we have long since laid it down in this Court and I think that Smith's case should not be used in Australia as authority at all."[^cite_note-parker-25] The Privy Council overturned this by enforcing the UK precedent upon the High Court the following year.[^cite_note-parker_appeal-26]

Thirteen High Court judges have heard cases as part of the Privy Council. [Sir Isaac Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Isaacs) is the only judge to have sat on an appeal from the High Court, in 1936 after his retirement as [Governor-General of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia).[^cite_note-Payne-27] [Sir Garfield Barwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfield_Barwick) insisted on an amendment to Privy Council procedure to allow dissent;[^cite_note-28] however, he exercised that capacity only once in an appeal from Guyana to the Privy Council.[^cite_note-29] The appeals mostly related to decisions from other Commonwealth countries, although they occasionally included appeals from the supreme court of an Australian state.[^cite_note-Brunton-30][^cite_note-Sydney_Council-31][^cite_note-Caratti-32][^cite_note-Peinkinna-33]

#### Abolition of Privy Council appeals

Section 74 allowed parliament to prevent appeals to the Privy Council. It did so in 1968 with the *[Privy Council (Limitation of Appeals) Act 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_\(Limitation_of_Appeals\)_Act_1968?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, which closed off all appeals to the Privy Council in matters involving federal legislation.[^cite_note-42] In 1975, the *[Privy Council (Appeals from the High Court) Act 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_\(Appeals_from_the_High_Court\)_Act_1975?action=edit\&redlink=1)* closed all routes of appeal from the High Court with the exception of those cases in which High Court issued a certificate of appeal.[^cite_note-43]

In 1986, with the passing of the *[Australia Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Act_1986)* by both the British and Commonwealth parliaments,[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] appeals to the Privy Council from state supreme courts were closed off, leaving the High Court as the only avenue of appeal.[^cite_note-46] In 2002, Chief Justice [Murray Gleeson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Gleeson) said that the "combined effect" of the legislation and the announcement in *Kirmani* "has been that section 74 has become a dead letter, and what remains of section 74 after the legislation limiting appeals to the Privy Council will have no further effect".[^cite_note-47]

### Appellate jurisdiction for Nauru

On 6 September 1976, Australia and [Nauru](21302.md), which was newly-independent from Australia, signed an agreement for the High Court to become Nauru's apex court.[^cite_note-48] It was empowered to hear appeals from the [Supreme Court of Nauru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Nauru) in both criminal and civil cases, but not constitutional matters.[^cite_note-49] There were a total of five appeals to the High Court under this agreement in the first 40 years of its operation. In 2017, however, this jumped to 13 appeals, most relating to asylum seekers.[^cite_note-Gans-50] At the time some legal commentators argued that this appellate jurisdiction sat awkwardly with the High Court's other responsibilities, and ought be renegotiated or repealed.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Anomalies included the need to apply Nauruan law and customary practice, and that special leave hearings were not required.[^cite_note-Gans-50]

Nauruan politicians[^cite_note-53] had said publicly that the Nauruan government was unhappy about these arrangements.[^cite_note-Guardian-54] Of particular concern was a decision of the High Court in October 2017, which quashed an increase in sentence imposed upon political protestors by the Supreme Court of Nauru.[^cite_note-Gans-50][^cite_note-CecilHCA-55] The High Court had remitted the case to the Supreme Court "differently constituted, for hearing according to law".[^cite_note-CecilHCA-55]

On Nauru's 50th anniversary of independence, [Baron Waqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_Waqa) declared to parliament that "\[s]everance of ties to Australia's highest court is a logical step towards full nationhood and an expression of confidence in Nauru's ability to determine its own destiny".[^cite_note-Gans-50] Justice Minister [David Adeang](785296.md) said that an additional reason for cutting ties was the cost of appeals to the High Court.[^cite_note-56] Nauru then exercised an option under its agreement with Australia to end its appellate arrangement with 90 days notice. The option was exercised on 12 December 2017 and the High Court's jurisdiction ended on 12 March 2018.[^cite_note-Guardian-54] The termination did not become publicly known until after the Supreme Court had reheard the case of the protesters and had again imposed increased sentences.[^cite_note-ABC-57] In 2022, Australia passed legislation which removed the possibility for reinstatement of the appeal pathway.[^cite_note-58]

## History

### Pre-establishment

Following [Earl Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_George_Grey,_3rd_Earl_Grey)'s 1846 proposal to federate the colonies, an 1849 report from the Privy Council suggested a national court be created.[^cite_note-Williams_hundred-59] In 1856, the [Governor of South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_South_Australia), [Richard MacDonnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Graves_MacDonnell), suggested to the [Government of South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_South_Australia) that they consider establishing a court to hear appeals from the Supreme Courts in each colony. In 1860 the [South Australian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_South_Australia) passed legislation encouraging MacDonnell to put the idea to the other colonies. However, only [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Victoria) considered the proposal.[^cite_note-Bennett_keystone-60]

At a [Melbourne](17306237.md) inter-colonial conference held in 1870, the idea of an inter-colonial court was again raised. A [royal commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission) was established in Victoria to investigate options for establishing such a court, and a draft bill was put forward. This draft bill, however, completely excluded appeals to the Privy Council, causing a reaction in London which prevented any serious attempt to implement the bill through the [British Imperial Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Imperial_Parliament).[^cite_note-Bennett_keystone-60]

Another draft bill was proposed in 1880 for the establishment of an Australasian court of appeal. The proposed court would consist of one judge from each of the colonial supreme courts, who would serve one-year terms.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-Bennett_keystone-60] However, the proposed court allowed for appeals to the Privy Council, which was disliked by some of the colonies, and the bill was abandoned.

#### Constitutional conventions



&#x20;[Sir Samuel Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Griffith), first [Chief Justice of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Australia)

The idea of a federal supreme court was raised during the [Constitutional Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(Australia\)) of the 1890s. A proposal for a supreme court of Australia was included in an 1891 draft. It was proposed to enable the court to hear appeals from the state supreme courts, with appeals to the Privy Council only occurring on assent from the [British monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_monarch). It was proposed that the Privy Council be prevented from hearing appeals on constitutional matters.

This draft was largely the work of [Sir Samuel Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Griffith),[^cite_note-Hull_centenary-62] then the [Premier of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_Queensland). The attorney-general of Tasmania [Andrew Inglis Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Inglis_Clark) also contributed to the constitution's judicial clauses. Clark's most significant contribution was to give the court its own constitutional authority, ensuring a [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers). The original formulation of Griffith, [Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Barton) and [Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kingston) provided only that the parliament could establish a court.[^cite_note-Williams_hundred-59]



&#x20;[Andrew Inglis Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Inglis_Clark), prominent contributor to the clauses about the High Court in the [Constitution of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia)

The draft was later amended at various conventions.[^cite_note-63] In Adelaide the court's proposed name was changed to be the "High Court of Australia".

Many people opposed the idea of the new court completely replacing the Privy Council. Commercial interests, particularly subsidiaries of British companies, preferred to operate under the unified jurisdiction of the British courts, and petitioned the conventions to that effect.[^cite_note-Hull_centenary-62] Others argued that Australian judges were of a poorer quality than British, and that the inevitable divergence in law that would occur without the oversight of the Privy Council would put the legal system at risk.[^cite_note-Williams_hundred-59]

Some politicians (e.g. [George Dibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Dibbs)) supported a retention of Privy Council supervision; whereas others, including [Alfred Deakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Deakin), supported the design of the court as it was.[^cite_note-Hull_centenary-62] Inglis Clark took the view that the possibility of divergence was a good thing, for the law could adapt appropriately to Australian circumstances.[^cite_note-Williams_hundred-59] Despite this debate, the draft's judicial sections remained largely unchanged.

After the draft had been approved by the electors of the colonies, it was taken to London in 1899 for the assent of the British Imperial Parliament. The issue of Privy Council appeals remained a sticking point however; with objections made by [Secretary of State for the Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Colonies), [Joseph Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Chamberlain), the [Chief Justice of South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_South_Australia), [Sir Samuel Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Way), and [Samuel Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Griffith), among others.[^cite_note-Gleeson_s74-24] In October 1899, Griffith made representations to Chamberlain soliciting suggestions from British ministers for alterations to the draft, and offered alterations of his own.[^cite_note-Gleeson_s74-24] Indeed, such was the effect of these and other representations that Chamberlain called for delegates from the colonies to come to London to assist with the approval process, with a view to their approving any alterations that the British government might see fit to make; delegates were sent, including Deakin, Barton and [Charles Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kingston), although they were under instructions that they would never agree to changes.[^cite_note-Gleeson_s74-24]

After intense lobbying both in Australia and in the United Kingdom, the Imperial Parliament finally approved the draft constitution. The draft as passed included an alteration to section 74, in a compromise between the two sides. It allowed for a general right of appeal from the High Court to the Privy Council, but the Parliament of Australia could make laws restricting this avenue. In addition, appeals in *inter se*[^cite_note-64] matters were not as of right, but had to be certified by the High Court.[^cite_note-Gleeson_s74-24]

#### Formation of the court



The first [Chief Justice of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Australia), [Sir Samuel Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Griffith), is administered the judicial oath at the first sitting of the High Court, in the [Banco Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Court) of the [Supreme Court of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Victoria), 6 October 1903.

The High Court was not immediately established after the Commonwealth of Australia came into being on 1 January 1901. Some [members of the first Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_the_Australian_House_of_Representatives,_1901%E2%80%931903), including [Sir John Quick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quick_\(politician\)), then one of the leading legal experts in Australia, opposed legislation to set up the court. Even [H. B. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._B._Higgins), who was himself later appointed to the court, objected to setting it up, on the grounds that it would be impotent while Privy Council appeals remained, and that in any event there was not enough work for a federal court to make it viable.[^cite_note-Hull_centenary-62]

The then [Attorney-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_of_Australia) Alfred Deakin introduced the *Judiciary Bill* to the [House of Representatives](61565.md) in 1902. Prior efforts had been continually delayed by opponents in the parliament, and the success of the bill is generally attributed to Deakin's passion and persistence.[^cite_note-Williams_hundred-59] Deakin proposed that the court be composed of five judges, specially selected to the court. Opponents instead proposed that the court should be made up of state supreme court justices, taking turns to sit on the High Court on a rotation basis, as had been mooted at the Constitutional Conventions a decade before.[^cite_note-mchugh_oxford_companion-65] Deakin eventually negotiated amendments with the [opposition](579561.md), reducing the number of judges from five to three, and eliminating financial benefits such as pensions.

&#x20;At one point, Deakin threatened to resign as Attorney-General due to the difficulties he faced.[^cite_note-Williams_hundred-59] In his three and a half hour [second reading](4062253.md) speech to the [House of Representatives](61565.md), Deakin said,[^cite_note-Deakin_keystone-66]

> The federation is constituted by distribution of powers, and it is this court which decides the orbit and boundary of every power... It is properly termed the keystone of the federal arch... The statute stands and will stand on the statute-book just as in the hour in which it was assented to. But the nation lives, grows and expands. Its circumstances change, its needs alter, and its problems present themselves with new faces. \[The High Court] enables the Constitution to grow and be adapted to the changeful necessities and circumstances of generation after generation that the High Court operates.

Deakin's friend, painter [Tom Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Roberts), who viewed the speech from the public gallery, declared it Deakin's "*magnum opus*".[^cite_note-Williams_hundred-59] The Judiciary Act 1903 was finally passed on 25 August 1903, and the first three justices, Chief Justice Sir Samuel Griffith and justices [Sir Edmund Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Barton) and [Richard O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_O'Connor_\(politician\)), were appointed on 5 October of that year. On 6 October, the court held its first sitting in the [Banco Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Court) in the [Supreme Court of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Victoria).

### Early years

&#x20;On 12 October 1906, the size of the High Court was increased to five justices, and Deakin appointed [H. B. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._B._Higgins) and [Isaac Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Isaacs) to the High Court. Following a court-packing attempt by the Labor Prime Minister [Andrew Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Fisher) In February 1913, the bench was increased again to a total to seven. [Charles Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Powers) and [Albert Bathurst Piddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Bathurst_Piddington) were appointed. These appointments generated an outcry, however, and Piddington resigned on 5 April 1913 after serving only one month as High Court justice.[^cite_note-history_of_the_high_court-67]



The court's home between 1928 and 1980, the purpose-built courtroom in [Little Bourke Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Bourke_Street), [Melbourne](17306237.md)

The High Court continued its Banco location in [Melbourne](17306237.md) until 1928, until a [dedicated courtroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_Court_Building,_Melbourne) was built in [Little Bourke Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Bourke_Street), next to the [Supreme Court of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Victoria). That space provided the court's Melbourne sitting place and housed the court's principal [registry](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/registry) until 1980.[^cite_note-prov_old_building-68] The court also sat regularly in Sydney, sharing space in the criminal courts of [Darlinghurst Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlinghurst_Courthouse), before a dedicated courtroom was constructed next door in 1923.[^cite_note-centenary_press_release-69]



The annexe to the criminal court in [Darlinghurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlinghurst_Courthouse), the court's home in Sydney

The court travelled to other cities across the country, where it would use facilities of the respective supreme courts. Deakin had envisaged that the court would sit in many different locations, so as to truly be a federal court. Shortly after the court's creation, Chief Justice Griffith established a schedule for sittings in state capitals: [Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart) in February, [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) in June, [Perth](24355.md) in September, and Adelaide in October. It has been said that Griffith established this schedule because those were the times of year he found the weather most pleasant in each city.

The tradition of special sittings remains to this day, although they are dependent on the court's caseload. There are annual sittings in Perth, Adelaide and Brisbane for up to a week each year, and sittings in Hobart occur once every few years. Sittings outside of these special occurrences are conducted in Canberra.

The court's operations were marked by various anomalies during [World War II](32927.md). The Chief Justice, [Sir John Latham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Latham_\(Australian_jurist\)), served from 1940 to 1941 as Australia's first ambassador to Japan; however, his activities in that role were limited by a pact Japan had entered with the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) prior to his arrival in [Tokyo](30057.md).[^cite_note-adb_latham-70] [Owen Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Dixon) was also absent for several years of his appointment, while serving as Australia's minister to the United States in [Washington](108956.md).[^cite_note-adb_Dixon-71] [Sir George Rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Rich) acted as chief justice during Latham's absence.

### Post-war period



The bench in 1952, shortly before Chief Justice Latham's retirement. Back, left to right, [Fullagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfred_Fullagar), [Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Webb_\(judge\)), [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Williams_\(judge\)) & [Kitto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Kitto). Front, left to right, [Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Dixon), [Latham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Latham_\(Australian_jurist\)) & [McTiernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_McTiernan)

From 1952, with the appointment of Sir Owen Dixon as chief justice, the court entered a period of stability. After World War II, the court's workload continued to grow, particularly from the 1960s onwards, putting pressures on the court.[^cite_note-HChistory-72] [Sir Garfield Barwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfield_Barwick), who was [attorney-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_of_Australia) from 1958 to 1964, and from then until 1981 chief justice, proposed that more federal courts be established, as permitted under the Constitution. In 1976 the [Federal Court of Australia](4688076.md) was established, with a general federal jurisdiction, and in more recent years the [Family Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Court_of_Australia) and [Federal Magistrates Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Magistrates_Court) have been set up to reduce the court's workload in specific areas.

In 1968, appeals to the Privy Council in matters involving federal legislation were barred. In 1986, with the passage of the [Australia Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Act_1986) direct appeals to the Privy Council from state Supreme Courts were also closed off.

The life tenure of High Court justices ended in 1977. A national referendum in May 1977 approved the *[Constitution Alteration (Retirement of Judges) 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Australian_referendum_\(Retirement_of_Judges\))*, which upon its commencement on 29 July 1977 amended section 72 of the Constitution so as require that all justices appointed from then on must retire on attaining the age of 70 years.[^cite_note-HChistory-72][^cite_note-73]

The *High Court of Australia Act 1979* (Cth), which commenced on 21 April 1980, gave the High Court power to administer its own affairs and prescribed the qualifications for, and method of appointment of, its Justices.[^cite_note-HChistory-72][^cite_note-High_Court_of_Australia_Act-5]

## Legal history

&#x20;Historical periods of the High Court are commonly denoted by reference to the chief justice of the time, akin to that of the [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md) under the [Chief Justice of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Canada). However, the chief justice is not always the most influential figure on the Court.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]



Chief Justice Griffith

### Griffith court: 1903–1919

The first court under Chief Justice Griffith laid the foundations of Australia's constitutional law. The court was conscious of its position as Australia's new court of appeal, and made efforts to establish its authority at the top of Australia's court hierarchy. In *[Deakin v Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deakin_v_Webb)* (1904)[^cite_note-deakin_webb-76] It criticised the [Victorian Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Victoria) for following a Privy Council decision about the [Constitution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada) instead of its own authority.[^cite_note-Bennett_keystone-60]

In its early years Griffith and other federalists on the bench were dominant. Their decisions were occasionally at odds with nationalist judges such as Sir [Isaac Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Isaacs) and [H. B. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._B._Higgins) in 1906. With the death of Justice [Richard Edward O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Edward_O'Connor), in 1912; the nationalists achieved majority and Griffith's influence began to decline.[^cite_note-companion_Griffith_court-77]

The early constitutional law decisions of the Griffith court was influenced by [US constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_constitutional_law).[^cite_note-78]

An important doctrine peculiar to the Griffith court was that of the [reserved state powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_State_powers).[^cite_note-79] Under this doctrine, the Commonwealth parliament's legislative powers were to be interpreted narrowly; so as to avoid intruding on areas of power traditionally exercised by the state Parliaments prior to federation.[^cite_note-union_label-80] [Anthony Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Mason_\(judge\)) has noted that this doctrine probably helped smooth the transition to a federal system of government and "by preserving a balance between the constituent elements of the Australian federation, probably conformed to community sentiment, which at that stage was by no means adjusted to the exercise of central power".[^cite_note-companion_Griffith_court-77]

&#x20;Griffith and Sir Edmund Barton were frequently consulted by governors-general, including on the exercise of the [reserve powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_powers).[^cite_note-81]



&#x20;[Sir Isaac Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Isaacs), Justice from 1906 and Chief Justice from 1930 to 1931

### Knox, Isaacs and Gavan Duffy courts: 1919–1935

#### Knox court

[Adrian Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Knox) became chief justice on 18 October 1919. Justice Edmund Barton died soon after, leaving no original members. During the Knox court, Justice Isaacs Isaacs had strong influence.[^cite_note-companion_Isaacs-82]

Under the Knox court the [Engineers case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamated_Society_of_Engineers_v_Adelaide_Steamship_Co_Ltd) was decided, ending the reserved state powers doctrine. The decision had lasting significance for the federal balance in Australia's political arrangements. Another significant decision was *[Roche v Kronheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roche_v_Kronheimer)*, in which the court relied upon the [defence power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_51\(vi\)_of_the_Australian_Constitution) to uphold federal legislation seeking to implement Australia's obligations under the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles).[^cite_note-83]

#### Isaacs court

[Sir Isaac Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Isaacs) was Chief Justice for only forty-two weeks; he left the court to be appointed [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia). He was ill for most of his term, and few significant cases were decided in this time.[^cite_note-companion_Isaacs-82]

#### Duffy court

Sir [Frank Gavan Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Gavan_Duffy) was Chief Justice for four years from 1931; but he was already 78 when appointed to the position. He was not influential, and only participated in 40% of the cases during his tenure. For the most part he gave short judgements, or joined in the judgements of his colleagues. His frequent absence resulted in many tied decisions which have no lasting value as [precedent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precedent).[^cite_note-companion_Gavan_Duffy_court-84]

### Latham court: 1935–1952



&#x20;[John Latham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Latham_\(judge\)), as Deputy Prime Minister prior to appointment

John Latham was elevated to Chief Justice in 1935. His tenure is most notable for the court's interpretation of wartime legislation, and the subsequent transition back to peace.[^cite_note-companion_Latham_court-88]

Most legislation was upheld as enabled by the [defence power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_51\(vi\)_of_the_Australian_Constitution).[^cite_note-89] The [Curtin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Curtin) [Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) government's legislation was rarely successfully challenged, with the court recognizing a necessity that the defence power permit the federal government to govern strongly.

Following the war, the court reined in the scope of the defence power. It struck down several key planks of the [Chifley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Chifley) Labor government's reconstruction program, notably an attempt to [nationalise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) the banks in the [Bank Nationalisation case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_New_South_Wales_v_Commonwealth) (1948),[^cite_note-bank_nationalisation-91] and an attempt to establish a comprehensive medical benefits scheme in the [First Pharmaceutical Benefits case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_\(Victoria\);_Ex_rel_Dale_v_Commonwealth) (1945).[^cite_note-pharma-92]

## Appointment process, composition, and working conditions

Main article: [List of Justices of the High Court of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Justices_of_the_High_Court_of_Australia)

### Appointment and tenure

High Court Justices are appointed by the [Governor-General in Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia).[^cite_note-141] The advice of the Council typically consists of the [advice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_\(constitutional_law\)) of the prime minister assisted by the [Attorney-General for Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_for_Australia). Advice from the attorney-general is legally required by implication, because since 1979 the attorney-general has been required by statute to consult the attorneys-general of the states (but not the territories).[^cite_note-142] Some reformers have advocated for states to have a determinative role.[^cite_note-companion_HCA_Act-143]

Originally, no particular qualifications for appointment to the High Court were required by the Constitution or by statute. The only constitutional requirement is that the appointee be under the compulsory retirement age of 70.[^cite_note-144] Further qualifications were introduced by statute in 1979: that an appointee be a judge of a federal, state or territory court; or have been an Australian legal practitioner for at least five years.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-companion_appointment-146] Unlike members of the [Parliament](302298.md), it is not necessary to be an [Australian Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Citizen) and a member of the Court may be a dual citizen.[^cite_note-147]

The appointment process has been relatively uncontroversial.[^cite_note-148] This has, however, been due in part to the opacity of the process. There is no procedure for application, the only definite criteria are the minimal criteria above, and nothing is publicly known until an appointee is announced. Appointment to federal courts was extensively formalised in 2007, except for the High Court, and those reforms were reversed by the next federal government. Some recent attorneys-general have stated that they were consulting widely—to include, for instance, Australian Women Lawyers, the National Association of Commonwealth Legal Centres and the heads of Australian law schools.[^cite_note-149] However, the nature of the attorney-general's consultations remains almost wholly discretionary.

Some appointments to the High Court have displayed clear political influence. Three early justices had been conservative politicians prior to their appointment as chief justice;[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] and Justices [H. V. Evatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._V._Evatt), [Edward McTiernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_McTiernan), and [Lionel Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Murphy) were all [Labor party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Party_\(Australia\)) politicians at some stage in their careers prior to being appointed to the High Court by a Labor prime minister.[^cite_note-152]

Members of the Court are required to retire when they reach the age of 70.[^cite_note-153] This requirement was introduced by constitutional [amendment in 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Australian_referendum_\(Retirement_of_judges\)).[^cite_note-154] Previously, there had been no retirement age and Sir [Edward McTiernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_McTiernan) had served for 46 years until being persuaded to retire at age 84. Retired members of the Court do not retain the title of 'Chief Justice' or 'Justice'.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

#### Initial composition



The first bench of the High Court: Barton, Griffith and O'Connor seated, with court officials in the background. Photo taken at the first sitting of the court on 6 October 1903.

The first High Court bench consisted of three justices: Samuel Griffith, Edmund Barton, and Richard O'Connor.[^cite_note-162]

According to the contemporary press, among those considered and overlooked were [Henry Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.B._Higgins), [Isaac Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Isaacs), [Andrew Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Inglis_Clark), [John Downer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Downer), [Josiah Symon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_Symon), and [George Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Wise_\(Australian_politician\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDixon_&_Williams_(eds)Ch_5_The_Griffith_Court_by_John_M_Williams-163]

Barton and O'Connor were both members of the federal parliament's government bench. Each appointee had participated in the drafting of the Constitution. All three have been described as relatively conservative justices for the time, and were strongly influenced by law of the United States in their constitutional jurisprudence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDixon_&_Williams_(eds)Ch_5_The_Griffith_Court_by_John_M_Williams-163]

#### Expansion of the court

In 1906, at the request of the Justices, two seats were added to the bench, with Isaacs and Higgins being appointed.

After O'Connor's death in 1912, an amendment was made to the *[Judiciary Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_1903)* expanding the bench to seven, which took place the following year.

Following Isaacs' retirement in 1931, his seat was left vacant, and an amendment to the *[Judiciary Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_1903)* reduced the number of seats to six. This, however, led to some decisions being split three-all.[^cite_note-164]

With the appointment of Justice [Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Webb_\(judge\)) in 1946, the court returned to seven seats, and has had a full bench of seven justices since.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDixon_&_Williams_(eds)pp._78–80,_118–119,_141–143,_160,161-165]

#### Historical and Current demographics

Only seven of the High Court's fifty-six justices have been women.



&#x20;[Mary Gaudron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Gaudron) (left) with Prime Minister [Julia Gillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Gillard), Governor-General [Quentin Bryce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Bryce), Attorney-General [Nicola Roxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Roxon) in 2011

The first female appointee to the bench was [Mary Gaudron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Gaudron) (who was a justice from 1987 to 2003), the second was [Susan Crennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Crennan) (who was a justice from 2005 to 2015), and the third [Virginia Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Bell_\(judge\)) from 2009 to 2021.&#x20;

As of October 2022, for the first time there is now a female majority of the justices on the current bench with justices [Susan Kiefel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Kiefel), [Michelle Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Gordon_\(judge\)), [Jacqueline Gleeson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Gleeson), and [Jayne Jagot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayne_Jagot) (as replacement for [Patrick Keane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Keane))[^cite_note-166]

In 2017, Justice Kiefel became the [first woman to be appointed Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_first_women_appointed_to_Australian_judicial_positions).[^cite_note-167]

[Michael Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kirby_\(judge\)) was the first openly gay justice of the Court. He was replaced by [Virginia Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Bell_\(judge\)), who was the first lesbian justice on the bench.[^cite_note-Bell2008-168]

Twenty-eight appointees have been residents of New South Wales, twenty-five of which graduated from [Sydney Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Law_School). Sixteen have come from Victoria, eight from Queensland, and four from Western Australia. No resident of South Australia, Tasmania, or any of the territories has ever been appointed to the bench.

The majority of justices have been of [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) backgrounds, with a smaller number of a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) background. Two Jewish members have been appointed, Sir [Isaac Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Isaacs) and [James Edelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Edelman),[^cite_note-169] making them the only members of the court to have a faith background other than [Christianity](5211.md). However, many justices have refrained from commenting publicly upon their religious views.

Almost all judges on the High Court have taken silk in some form prior to their appointment, in the form of appointment to [King's Counsel (KC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Counsel), Queen's Counsel (QC) or [Senior Counsel (SC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Counsel). The exceptions are [Starke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayden_Starke), [McTiernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_McTiernan), Webb, [Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Walsh), Kirby, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_French), [Edelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Edelman) and [Jagot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayne_Jagot).

Thirteen justices of the court previously served in a Parliament, however no previous parliamentarian has been appointed to the court since [Lionel Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Murphy) in 1975.

### Working conditions

Salaries are determined by the Remuneration Tribunal. The regular justices receive \$551,880, while the Chief justice receives \$608,150.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172] High Court judicial compensation is constitutionally protected from decrease during appointment.[^cite_note-173]

The court typically sits for two weeks for each calendar month of the year, excepting for January and July in which no sitting days are held.[^cite_note-174]

#### Judicial associates

Each judge engages associates for assistance in exercising their functions. The usual practice is to engage two associates simultaneously for a one-year term. Additionally, the chief justice is assisted by a legal research officer employed by the court library.

Associates have varying responsibilities; typically their work involves legal research, assistance in preparation for oral arguments, [tipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipstaff) in court during oral argument, editing judgments and assisting with extrajudicial functions, such as speech-writing.[^cite_note-175] Associates are typically recruited after having graduated from an Australian law school with grades at or near the top of their class.[^cite_note-associate-176] Hundreds of applications for associate positions are received by the High Court annually.[^cite_note-associate-176]

Many High Court associates have gone on to illustrious careers. Examples of former associates include [Nicola Roxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Roxon), [Adrienne Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrienne_Stone) and [George Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Williams_\(lawyer\)).

Three High Court justices served as associates prior to their elevation to the bench: [Aickin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Aickin) to [Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Owen_Dixon), [Gageler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Gageler) to [Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Anthony_Mason), and [Edelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Edelman) to [Toohey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Toohey_\(judge\)).[^cite_note-177]

## Facilities

### Building

Main article: [High Court of Australia Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Australia_Building)

The High Court of Australia building is located on the shore of [Lake Burley Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Burley_Griffin) in Canberra's [Parliamentary Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Triangle). The High Court was designed between 1972 and 1974 by the Australian architect [Christopher Kringas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Kringas) (1936–1975), a director of the firm Edwards Madigan Torzillo and Briggs. The building was constructed from 1975 to 1980. Its international architectural significance is recognised by the Union of International Architects register of Architectural Heritage of the 20th Century. It received the Australian Institute of Architects Canberra Medallion in 1980 and the award for Enduring Architecture in 2007. The High Court was added to the [Commonwealth Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Heritage_List) in 2004.[^cite_note-AHD-7]

### Online

The High Court makes itself generally available to the public through its own website.[^cite_note-178] Judgment alerts, available on the Court's website and by email with free subscription, provide subscribers with notice of upcoming judgments (normally a week beforehand) and, almost immediately after the delivery of a major judgment, with a brief summary of it (normally not more than one page). All of the court's judgments, as well as transcripts of its hearings since 2009 and other materials, are made available, free of charge, through the [Australasian Legal Information Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasian_Legal_Information_Institute). The court has recently established on its website an "eresources" page, containing for each case its name, keywords, mentions of relevant legislation and a link to the full judgment; these links go to the original text from 2000 onward, scanned texts from 1948 to 1999 and facsimiles from the *Commonwealth Law Reports* for their first 100 volumes (1903 to 1959); there are also facsimiles of some unreported judgments (1906–2002).[^cite_note-179] Since October 2013, audio-visual recordings of full-court hearings held in Canberra have been available on its website.[^cite_note-180]
