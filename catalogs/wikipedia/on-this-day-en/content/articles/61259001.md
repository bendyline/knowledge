**Independent politicians** contest elections without the support of a political party. They have played a continuous role in the [politics of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Republic_of_Ireland) since its independence in 1922.

## Provision for independents in electoral law

If a candidate is not the candidate of a [registered political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland), they may be nominated for elections to [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann) with the assent of 30 electors in the [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_constituencies),[^cite_note-1] for elections to the [European Parliament](9581.md) with the assent of 60 electors in the constituency,[^cite_note-2] and for local elections with the assent of 15 electors in the [local electoral area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_electoral_area).[^cite_note-3] They may choose to have the designation non-party next to their name on the ballot paper.[^cite_note-4]

In [Seanad elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seanad_%C3%89ireann) and [presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_presidential_election), candidates are not nominated by parties directly, and party labels do not appear on the ballot.

## Independents supporting governments

In the case of [minority governments](271434.md), where the party or parties forming the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ireland) do not have a majority in the Dáil, they will usually be dependent on independent TDs in [votes of confidence](208101.md) or to pass the [budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget). This can be by formal arrangement with the government.

## Independent government ministers

In the [first inter-party government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inter-party_government) (1948–1951), [James Dillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dillon_\(Fine_Gael_politician\)) served as [Minister for Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Agriculture,_Food_and_the_Marine). He was an independent TD, having left [Fine Gael](11252.md) in 1942 because he disagreed with the policy of [neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_neutrality) during [World War II](32927.md). He rejoined Fine Gael in 1953 and became leader in 1959.

In 2009, [Mary Harney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Harney) continued as [Minister for Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Health_\(Ireland\)) as an independent member of the government after the dissolution of the [Progressive Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Democrats), and served until 2011.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

After the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election), three independent TDs were appointed to a minority [Fine Gael–Independent government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_32nd_D%C3%A1il) on 6 May 2016: [Denis Naughten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Naughten) as [Minister for Communications, Climate Action and Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Climate,_Energy_and_the_Environment), [Shane Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Ross) as [Minister for Transport, Tourism and Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Transport_\(Ireland\)), and [Katherine Zappone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Zappone) as [Minister for Children and Youth Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Children,_Disability_and_Equality). Ross was a member of the [Independent Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Alliance_\(Ireland\)), and other members of the Independent Alliance were appointed as [Ministers of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_\(Ireland\)).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Naughten resigned as minister on 11 October 2018.[^cite_note-9] Ross and Zappone served until the appointment of a new government on 27 June 2020. After the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election), three independent TDs were appointed to a minority [Fine Gael–Independent government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_32nd_D%C3%A1il) on 6 May 2016: [Denis Naughten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Naughten) as [Minister for Communications, Climate Action and Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Climate,_Energy_and_the_Environment), [Shane Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Ross) as [Minister for Transport, Tourism and Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Transport_\(Ireland\)), and [Katherine Zappone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Zappone) as [Minister for Children and Youth Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Children,_Disability_and_Equality). Ross was a member of the [Independent Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Alliance_\(Ireland\)), and other members of the Independent Alliance were appointed as [Ministers of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_\(Ireland\)).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Naughten resigned as minister on 11 October 2018.[^cite_note-12] Ross and Zappone served until the appointment of a new government on 27 June 2020. After the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_general_election), five independent TDs were appointed as [ministers of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_\(Ireland\)) in a majority [Fianna Fáil–Fine Gael–Independent](78963261.md) government. [Noel Grealish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Grealish), [Seán Canney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Se%C3%A1n_Canney), [Kevin "Boxer" Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_%22Boxer%22_Moran) , [Michael Healy-Rae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Healy-Rae) and [Marian Harkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Harkin) were appointed to various junior ministries.[^cite_note-\:0-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Healy-Rae resigned as minister during a [confidence vote](208101.md) on 15 April 2026.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Local government

At the [2024 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_local_elections), independents won 178 of the 949 seats on [city and county councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland).

## European Parliament

Two independent MEPs represent Ireland in the [European Parliament](9581.md). [Luke 'Ming' Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_'Ming'_Flanagan) has been MEP for [Midlands–North-West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands%E2%80%93North-West) since [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Parliament_election_in_Ireland), and [Michael McNamara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_McNamara_\(politician\)) has been MEP for [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_\(European_Parliament_constituency\)) since [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Ireland).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## President of Ireland

The current [president of Ireland](24676.md) [Catherine Connolly](25296931.md) was elected in [2025](75903783.md) having been nominated by members of the [Oireachtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas) from [Sinn Féin](28175.md), [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)), [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrats_\(Ireland\)), [People Before Profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Before_Profit), [Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_\(Ireland\)), [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Ireland\)), [100% Redress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100%25_Redress), and independents.[^cite_note-22]
