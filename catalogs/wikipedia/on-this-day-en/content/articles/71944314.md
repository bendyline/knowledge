# Russian occupation of Crimea

Not to be confused with the disputed [Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), which took place a few weeks after the occupation began.

Since 2014, Russia has occupied the [Crimean Peninsula](163045.md), a part of [Ukraine](31750.md). On 27 February 2014, Russia sent [soldiers in unmarked uniforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_green_men_\(Russo-Ukrainian_War\)) to Crimea to take control of it, starting the [Russo-Ukrainian war](42085878.md). This military occupation paved the way for the [Russian annexation of Crimea](42228673.md) on 18 March 2014. While the Russian government deems Crimea to be [part of](701438.md) the Russian Federation, Ukraine and [most of the international community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Crimea) see it as an [occupied territory of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine).

The occupation began during Ukraine's [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity), which ousted pro-Russian president [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych). Russian special forces, disguised as "Crimean self-defense forces", [took control of Crimea's government buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_the_Crimean_Parliament), surrounded Ukrainian military bases, and blockaded the peninsula. Russia denied involvement at the time,[^cite_note-reuters_20140305-1] but Putin later admitted that they were Russian troops.[^cite_note-reuters_20150309-2] A pro-Russian government was installed and a [referendum on Crimea's status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Crimean_status_referendum) was held under occupation. According to the Russian-installed authorities, the result was in favour of joining Russia. Russia annexed Crimea on 18 March 2014, re-organizing it as a [Russian republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) and turning [Sevastopol](51587.md) into a [Russian federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Russia also claimed an [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) in the [Black Sea](3386.md) around Crimea. This zone is three times bigger than the peninsula and holds vast [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_in_Ukraine) and [oil reserves](77988708.md).[^cite_note-Broad_NYT-5] The [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) adopted [a resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_68/262) affirming the "territorial integrity of Ukraine within its internationally recognised borders", and the UN considers Crimea to be Russian-occupied.[^cite_note-un_1845102-6]

Since the beginning of the [Russian invasion of mainland Ukraine](79400268.md) in February 2022, Russia has used Crimea as a base from which to [attack mainland Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine_campaign). The Ukrainian military has responded with [attacks on Russian forces in Crimea](71954377.md). One of Russia's preconditions for ending the invasion has been the recognition of Russian claims to Crimea, while one of Ukraine's goals is to liberate the territory, by military means if necessary.[^cite_note-\:12-7][^cite_note-\:2b-8][^cite_note-\:0b-9][^cite_note-\:8-10][^cite_note-\:9-11][^cite_note-\:10-12]

The [United Nations Human Rights Office](22463.md) stated that Russia has committed serious human rights violations in occupied Crimea.[^cite_note-OHCHR_10_Years-13] This includes severely curbing the right to [freedom of speech](21401843.md), [assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_assembly_in_Russia) and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion_in_Russia); [arbitrary arrest and detention](13435705.md); and [forced disappearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_disappearance). Ethnic Ukrainians and [Crimean Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Ukraine) have suffered discrimination and repression.[^cite_note-OHCHR_10_Years-13] Residents who did not accept Russian citizenship lost their right to live and work in Crimea.[^cite_note-OHCHR_10_Years-13] Russia has also been accused of [neo-colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocolonialism#Russia) by enforced [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) and by settling large numbers of Russians in Crimea and pushing out Ukrainians and Crimean Tatars.[^cite_note-neweasterneurope_20210623_the-14]

## History

### Russian planning



Russian President Vladimir Putin and his Defense Minister Sergei Shoigu in March 2014

In early February 2014, as the [protests in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) seemed likely to collapse the government, the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) received a strategy paper outlining plans for the occupation and annexation of Crimea.[^cite_note-Kim-15] The documents said that the Ukrainian government and president [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) would not last.[^cite_note-Kim-15] They proposed that Russia should foster separatism in Crimea and other eastern provinces, and should begin work on taking control of them.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-mcclatchydc_24780370-17] On 21 February 2014, Ukrainian president Yanukovych secretly fled the capital, and the next day, parliament [voted to remove him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity#Removal_of_Yanukovych) from office. Russia's leadership was reportedly worried that its [Sevastopol Naval Base](59166849.md) in Crimea might be at risk under a new Ukrainian government that was committed to closer ties with the West. On 22–23 February 2014, Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) held an all-night meeting with security chiefs to discuss the crisis. At the end of that meeting, Putin said: "we must start working on returning Crimea to Russia".[^cite_note-bbc_31796226-18][^cite_note-theguardian_2015mar09_vladimir-19]

Russia took advantage of the uncertainty in Ukraine immediately after the ousting of Yanukovych.[^cite_note-rand_RR1498-20] On 26 February, Putin ordered the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md) to be "put on alert in the [Western Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Military_District) as well as units stationed with the 2nd Army [Central Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Military_District) Command involved in aerospace defence, airborne troops and long-range military transport". Russian Defence Minister [Sergei Shoigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shoigu) denied that this was related to events in Ukraine.[^cite_note-Alpert-21]

### Russian military takeover



Russian soldiers without markings in Simferopol, Crimea, 2 March 2014

Early on 27 February 2014, Russian special forces began to occupy Crimea, wearing unmarked uniforms to hide their origin.[^cite_note-CrimeanInvasion-22][^cite_note-DeBenedictis-23] They were referred to as ["little green men"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_green_men_\(Russo-Ukrainian_War\)). The soldiers had set out from the [Russian base in Sevastopol](59166849.md) and travelled to the capital [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol). There, they [seized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_the_Crimean_Parliament) the [Crimean parliament building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_of_the_Supreme_Council_of_Crimea) and the Council of Ministers building.[^cite_note-nytimes_20140228_world-24] [Russian flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Russia) were raised over them[^cite_note-cnn_20140227_world-25] and barricades were erected.[^cite_note-LentaBerkut-26] A military checkpoint, with a Russian flag and Russian military vehicles, was set up on the main highway between Sevastopol and Simferopol.[^cite_note-globe26-27]



Russian soldiers without markings occupying the Crimean parliament building, 1 March 2014

Hours after the takeover began, the Crimean parliament held an emergency session while the armed men occupied the building.[^cite_note-interfax27feb-28][^cite_note-reuters13mar-29] It voted to dismiss the Crimean government, and replace Prime Minister [Anatolii Mohyliov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolii_Mohyliov) with [Sergey Aksyonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Aksyonov); a member of the [Russian Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Unity) party, which received only 4% of votes in the last election.[^cite_note-reuters13mar-29] Under the [Constitution of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ukraine), the prime minister of Crimea is appointed by the Crimean parliament (Supreme Council of Crimea) in consultation with the president of Ukraine.[^cite_note-Yanu'snewPMC-30][^cite_note-31] Both Aksyonov and speaker [Vladimir Konstantinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Konstantinov_\(politician\)) stated that they viewed Viktor Yanukovych as the *de jure* president of Ukraine.[^cite_note-lenta_20140227_truepresident-32] The parliament also voted to hold a referendum on Crimea's political status, set for 25 May. Historian [Andrew Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wilson_\(historian\)) and journalist [Luke Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Harding) called this the "Crimean [coup](25914154.md)".[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-theguardian_2014feb28_vladimir-34]

The troops had cut all of the building's communications, and took MPs' phones as they entered.[^cite_note-interfax27feb-28][^cite_note-reuters13mar-29] No independent journalists were allowed inside the building while the votes were held.[^cite_note-reuters13mar-29] Some MPs said they were threatened and that votes were cast for them and other MPs, even though they were not in the chamber.[^cite_note-reuters13mar-29] [Interfax-Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interfax-Ukraine) reported that there was no way to know how many MPs were present, and whether they voted themselves or if someone else used their voting cards.[^cite_note-IUCR27214-35] The head of parliament's information department, Olha Sulnikova, phoned journalists from inside the building, telling them 61 of the registered 64 deputies had voted for the referendum resolution and 55 for the resolution to dismiss the government.[^cite_note-IUCR27214-35] These votes were immediately declared illegal by the Ukrainian interim government.[^cite_note-obozrevatel_20140301-36]

&#x20;Russian FSB colonel [Igor Girkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Girkin) (alias 'Strelkov'), one of the commanders of the soldiers, said in January 2015 that Crimean MPs were held at gunpoint and forced to vote in favor.[^cite_note-KP11MA2015-37] Girkin said:

> "Unfortunately I did not see any support from the \[Crimean] authorities in Simferopol where I was ... It was militants who collected deputies and forced them to vote. Yes, I was one of the commanders of those militants. I saw that from the inside".[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]



Armed men in *[Berkut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkut_\(special_police_force\))* uniform guarding a checkpoint at [Chonhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonhar), on the border of mainland Ukraine and Crimea, 10 March 2014

The same day, more unmarked troops set up [security checkpoints](8116691.md) on the [Isthmus of Perekop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Perekop) and the [Chonhar Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonhar_Peninsula), which separate Crimea from the Ukrainian mainland.[^cite_note-LentaBerkut-26][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-allcrimea_2014227_na-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-lenta_20140228_blockpost-43] They were helped by what appeared to be local *[Berkut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkut_\(special_police_force\))* riot police, as well as Russian troops from the [31st Separate Airborne Assault Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Guards_Air_Assault_Brigade) dressed in Berkut uniforms.[^cite_note-informnapalm_20150709-44] Within hours, Ukraine had been cut off from Crimea. Ukrainian TV channels became unavailable for Crimean viewers, some replaced with Russian stations. On 29 February, unmarked Russian special forces occupied Crimea's airports and communications centers.[^cite_note-forceAuth-45]

On 1 March 2014, Aksyonov said that he would exercise control of all Ukrainian military and security installations on the peninsula. He also asked Putin for "assistance in ensuring peace and tranquillity" in Crimea.[^cite_note-46] Putin promptly received authorisation from the [Federation Council of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_Council_of_Russia) for a [Russian military intervention in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_military_intervention_in_Ukraine_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)) until the "political-social situation in the country is normalized".[^cite_note-themoscowtimes_20140301_federation-47][^cite_note-Federation_2014-48] Ukraine's prime minister, [Arseniy Yatsenyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arseniy_Yatsenyuk), said that Russian military intervention would be the beginning of war.[^cite_note-forceAuth-45]

An emergency meeting of the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) was held on 2 March 2014 to discuss the crisis. Ukrainian ambassador [Yuriy Sergeyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuriy_Sergeyev) said that Russia was committing "an [act of aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_aggression) against the state of Ukraine". He stated that Russia had broken the [Budapest agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Memorandum) and called on the other signatories to provide the assistance they had agreed to.[^cite_note-reuters_idUSDEEA2100T-49] Russian ambassador [Vitaly Churkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Churkin) accused the US and EU of "encouraging" the protests that had led to the ousting of Ukrainian president Yanukovych. He denied that Russian forces had invaded Crimea. US Ambassador [Samantha Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Power) told the session that Russia had violated Ukraine's sovereignty and called for the "immediate deployment" international monitors from the UN and the [Organization for the Security and Cooperation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_the_Security_and_Cooperation_in_Europe) (OSCE).[^cite_note-bloomberg_20140301_ukraine-50]



The blockade of military units of the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md) during the capture of Crimea by Russia in February–March 2014

By 2 March, Russian troops were in full control of Crimea, having deployed from the naval base in Sevastopol and reinforced by troops, armour, and helicopters from mainland Russia.[^cite_note-thehindu_5739708-51][^cite_note-walker-the-guardian-2014-descend-52][^cite_note-bloomberg-news-2014-request-53] On 3 March they [blockaded the Southern Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Southern_Naval_Base).

On 4 March, the [Ukrainian General Staff](17313362.md) said there were Russian units of the [18th Motor Rifle Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Guards_Motor_Rifle_Brigade), [31st Air Assault Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Guards_Air_Assault_Brigade) and [22nd Spetsnaz Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Spetsnaz_Brigade) deployed and operating in Crimea, not only [Russian Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) personnel, which violated international agreements signed by Ukraine and Russia.[^cite_note-zsu_henshtab-54][^cite_note-zsu_interfax-55]

At a press conference on 4 March, president Putin said that Russia had no plans to annex Crimea.[^cite_note-de-56] He also said that it had no plans to invade Ukraine, but that it might intervene if Russians in Ukraine were threatened.[^cite_note-de-56] This was part of a pattern of public denials of the ongoing Russian military operation.[^cite_note-de-56]

Numerous media reports and statements by the Ukrainian and foreign governments noted the identity of the unmarked troops as Russian soldiers, but Russian officials [concealed](2601678.md) the identity of their forces, claiming they were local "self-defence" units over whom they had no authority.[^cite_note-reuters_20140305-1] As late as 17 April, Russian foreign minister [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md) said that there were no "excessive Russian troops" in Ukraine.[^cite_note-theguardian_2014apr17_ukraine-57] At the same press conference, Putin said of the peninsula that "only citizens themselves, in conditions of free expression of will and their security can determine their future".[^cite_note-interfax_362633-58] Putin later acknowledged that he had ordered "work to bring Crimea back into Russia" as early as February.[^cite_note-revea-59] He also acknowledged that in early March there were "secret opinion polls" held in Crimea, which, according to him, reported overwhelming popular support for Crimea's incorporation into Russia.[^cite_note-tass_1111359-60]



Medal of the [Russian Defense Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Defense_Ministry) ["For the return of Crimea"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_%22For_the_Return_of_Crimea%22) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): За возвращение Крыма), 20 February – 18 March 2014

Russia eventually admitted its troops' presence.[^cite_note-bbc_140417-61] Defence Minister [Sergei Shoigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shoigu) said the country's military actions in Crimea were undertaken by forces of the [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) and were justified by "threat to lives of Crimean civilians" and danger of "takeover of Russian military infrastructure by extremists".[^cite_note-tass_1097051-62] Ukraine complained that by increasing its troop presence in Crimea, Russia violated the agreement under which it headquartered its Black Sea Fleet in Sevastopol[^cite_note-ukrinform_317983-63] and violated the country's [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-blogs.telegraph.co.uk-64] The United States and United Kingdom accused Russia of breaking the terms of the [Budapest Memorandum on Security Assurances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Memorandum_on_Security_Assurances), by which Russia, the US, and the UK had reaffirmed their obligation to refrain from the threat or use of force against the [territorial integrity](31335.md) or political independence of Ukraine.[^cite_note-USStatement-65] The Russian government said the Budapest Memorandum[^cite_note-budapest-memorandum-66] did not apply due to "circumstances resulting from the action of internal political or socio-economic factors".[^cite_note-bbc_140520-67] In March 2015, retired Russian Admiral [Igor Kasatonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Kasatonov?action=edit\&redlink=1) stated that according to his information the Russian troop deployment in Crimea included six helicopter landings and three landings of an [IL-76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IL-76) with 500 people.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-currenttime_26899211-69]

### Annexation

Further information: [Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation)



Ukrainian checkpoint at [Kalanchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalanchak), entering Kherson Oblast from Russian-occupied Crimea.

On 16 March 2014, a [referendum status of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Crimean_status_referendum) was held by Russia, where, according to official Russian data, 96.77% of the inhabitants of the (Autonomous) Republic of Crimea and the city of [Sevastopol](51587.md) voted for the reunification of the respective territories with the Russian Federation. On 17 March, the [Verkhovna Rada of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhovna_Rada_of_Crimea) proclaimed the independence of the [Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)), and on 18 March, in the Georgievsky Hall of the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin), [President of Russia](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), together with the self-proclaimed Chairman of the Council of [Ministers of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Crimea) [Sergey Aksyonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Aksyonov), the Speaker of the Verkhovna Rada of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea [Vladimir Konstantinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Konstantinov_\(politician\)) and the self-proclaimed life of Sevastopol [Aleksei Chalyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Chaly), signed the Treaty on the Adoption of the Republic Crimea to Russia. On 21 March, the [Federation Council](1268578.md) adopted a law on the ratification of the Treaty of 18 March and a law on the formation of new [subjects of the federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_divisions_of_Russia) — the [Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)) and the [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of [Sevastopol](51587.md), securing the annexation of these regions by Russia.

On 27 March 2014, the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) supported the territorial integrity of Ukraine, recognizing [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea) and [Sevastopol](51587.md) as its integral parts. 100 UN member states out of 194 voted for the relevant resolution. Only 11 countries voted against ([Armenia](10918072.md), [Belarus](3457.md), [Bolivia](3462.md), [Cuba](5042481.md), [Nicaragua](21362.md), [North Korea](21255.md), Russia, [Sudan](27421.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Venezuela](32374.md) and [Zimbabwe](34399.md)), 58 abstained.[^cite_note-un_1113152-70] The forced annexation of Crimea is not recognized by Ukraine,[^cite_note-71] is not recognized by the [UN General Assembly](31957.md), [PACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe),[^cite_note-khpg_1397090149-72] [OSCE PA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Organization_for_Security_and_Co-operation_in_Europe), and also contradicts the decision of the [Venice Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Commission), while the Russian authorities interpret it as "the return of Crimea to Russia." According to the Law of Ukraine "On Ensuring the Rights and Freedoms of Citizens and the Legal Regime in the Temporarily Occupied Territory of Ukraine", the territory of the Crimean Peninsula is considered temporarily occupied territory as a result of Russian occupation.

### 2014–2021



Russian troops in Crimea, March 2014



Russian forces during Victory Day in Sevastopol, 2014

On 21 November 2015, segments of the four electricity transmission lines in Ukraine supplying electricity to Crimea were destroyed by Ukrainian activists, causing about 1.6 million people and 150 schools in Crimea to be without power and water supplies to some high buildings to fail. [Ukrinterenergo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrinterenergo) repaired the lines a few days later, as Russia was considering stopping supplies of coal to Ukraine in response.[^cite_note-osw-20151125-73][^cite_note-bbc-20151122-74]

On 8 August 2016, Ukraine reported that Russia had increased its military presence along the demarcation line.[^cite_note-newsweek_488283-75] In response to this military buildup Ukraine also deployed more troops and resources closer to the border with Crimea.[^cite_note-haaretz_736052-76] The Pentagon has downplayed a Russian invasion of Ukraine, calling Russian troops along the border a regular military exercise.[^cite_note-co_20160820_world-77] On 10 August, Russia claimed two servicemen were killed in clashes with Ukrainian commandos, and that Ukrainian servicemen had been captured with a total of 40 kg of explosives in their possession.[^cite_note-theguardian_2016aug10_russia-78] Ukraine denied that the incident took place.[^cite_note-co_20160810_russia-79]

Russian accounts claimed that Russian [FSB](385990.md) detained "Ukrainian saboteurs" and "terrorists" near [Armiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armiansk). The ensuing gunfight left one [FSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FSB_Border_Service_of_Russia) officer and a suspect dead. A number of individuals were detained, including Yevhen Panov, who is described by Russian sources as a Ukrainian military intelligence officer and leader of the sabotage group. The group was allegedly planning terror attacks on important infrastructure in Armiansk, Crimea.[^cite_note-\:1-80][^cite_note-\:2-81] Ukrainian media reported that Panov was a military volunteer fighting in the east of the country, however he has more recently been associated with a charitable organization. Russia also claimed that the alleged border infiltration was accompanied by "heavy fire" from Ukrainian territory, resulting in the death of a Russian soldier.[^cite_note-\:1-80][^cite_note-\:2-81] The Ukrainian government called the Russian accusations "cynical" and "senseless" and argued that since Crimea was Ukrainian territory, it was Russia which "has been generously financing and actively supporting terrorism on Ukrainian territory".[^cite_note-bloomberg_20160810_putin-82]

On 28 December 2018, Russia completed a high-tech security fence marking the de facto border between Crimea and Ukraine.[^cite_note-bbc_46699807-83]

#### Kerch Strait incident

Further information: [Kerch Strait incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait_incident)

On 25 November 2018, three ships of the [Ukrainian Navy](1590554.md) – two small armored artillery boats, *Berdyansk* and *Nikopol*, and the tug *Yany Kapu* – carried out a planned transition from the port of Odesa on the [Black Sea](3386.md) to the [port of Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Mariupol) on the [Sea of Azov](78019.md). The Ukrainian side informed in advance about the route in accordance with international standards to ensure the safety of navigation. In the area of the [Kerch Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait), they were stopped by a Russian tanker, which blocked the passage under the [Crimean Bridge](22563317.md) built by the occupying authorities. Contrary to the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea and the Treaty between Ukraine and the Russian Federation on Cooperation in the Use of the Sea of Azov and the Kerch Strait, the border ships of the Russian Federation (patrol border boats of the Sobol type, the Don PSKR, the Mongoose type boats, the Suzdalets MPK) committed aggressive actions against the ships of the Navy of the Armed Forces of Ukraine. The border ship "Don" rammed the Ukrainian tug, as a result of which the ship's main engine, plating and railing were damaged, and a life raft was lost. Dispatching service refused to ensure the right of freedom of navigation, guaranteed by international agreements.[^cite_note-84] The Russians captured the three Ukrainian ships and 24 sailors, 6 of whom were wounded. In Ukraine, on the same day, an urgent meeting of the [National Security and Defense Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_and_Defense_Council_of_Ukraine) was convened to discuss the introduction of [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Ukraine). The next day, 26 November, they approved the decision to introduce martial law for 30 days.[^cite_note-voanews_4673893-85]

### 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine

Further information: [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), [Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)](70149799.md), and [Crimea attacks (2022–present)](71954377.md)



Movement of Russian forces out of Crimea during the outbreak of the 2022 invasion

## Resistance to occupation

During the early stages of the occupation, cities like [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol), [Yalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta) and [Sevastopol](51587.md) became the scene of pro-Ukrainian protests of different sizes, most notably a mass protest on 26 February 2014, where tensions arose between approximately 10,000 pro-Ukrainian protesters and a crowd of roughly 5,000 pro-Russian demonstrators in front of the [regional administration building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_parliament) in [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol).[^cite_note-pravda_7014965-125][^cite_note-aljazeera_201439162353-126][^cite_note-newrepublic_116949-127][^cite_note-euronews_20140226_crimean-128]

Between 2014 and 2017, the peninsula became the scene of a low-intensity insurgency, during which pro-Ukrainian partisans mainly committed sabotage and arson attacks against infrastructure targets, but also acts of psychological warfare, as well as attacks on Russian security forces.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-gordonua_263076-130][^cite_note-131]

During [Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), militant pro-Ukrainian [partisan movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerilla) started to [organize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_resistance_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine) in Russian-occupied [Eastern](13065940.md) and [Southern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine). Since then, Crimean partisan cells have been involved in acts of [sabotage](29462.md), arson, [assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassinations), vandalism and [psychological warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_warfare) on the territory of the whole [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula), with the far north, the [Southern Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Coast_\(Crimea\)) and the [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol)-[Bakhchysarai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhchysarai) metropolitan area being ″hotspots″ of their activity.[^cite_note-132]

## Discrimination against Ukrainians and Crimean Tatars



A commemoration event in Kyiv, likening the [Deportation of the Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_the_Crimean_Tatars) in 1944 to the Russian occupation of Crimea since 2014

See also: [Republic of Crimea (Russia) § Human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)#Human_rights)

[United Nations special rapporteurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_special_rapporteur) have condemned the Russian occupation authorities for attempting "to erase local \[Ukrainian] culture, history, and language" and to forcibly replace it with Russian language and culture. Ukrainian monuments and places of worship have been razed, while Ukrainian history books and literature deemed to be "extremist" have been seized from public libraries and destroyed. Civil servants and teachers have been detained for their refusal to implement Russian policy.[^cite_note-ohchr_20230222-133] The [International Court of Justice](14918.md) in ruled in 2024 that steep and systematic decline in providing education in [Ukrainian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) in occupied Crimea was artificially enforced by the Russian Federation with intent to harm [Ukrainian](145144.md) ethnicity in the region, thus breaking the [Convention on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Racial_Discrimination) by restricting Ukrainian-language school classes.[^cite_note-bbc_68161532-134] Russia has been accused of [neocolonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocolonialism) and colonization in Crimea by enforced [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification), [passportization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passportization), and by settling Russian citizens on the peninsula and forcing out Ukrainians and [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars).[^cite_note-neweasterneurope_20210623_the-14]

In the fall of 2014, the Russian authorities conducted a [population census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Federal_District_Census_\(2014\)) in the occupied Crimea, according to which there were 344,500 Ukrainians on the peninsula (15.7%), and the native language was Ukrainian for only 3.3% of the Crimean population. According to sociologist [Iryna Bekeshkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iryna_Bekeshkina), this reduction in the number of Ukrainians was due to the change in identification of part of the Ukrainian population from Ukrainians to Russians.[^cite_note-krymr_26918423-135] After the occupation, many ethnic Ukrainians and Crimean Tatars began to leave Crimea for mainland Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:0-105] As of 15 August 2019, 40,733 displaced people from the occupied peninsula were officially registered on the mainland of Ukraine.[^cite_note-krymr_30117853-136] According to activists of the Crimean Tatar movement, several hundreds of thousands of Russians moved to Crimea with the assistance of the Russian authorities during the 4 years of occupation.[^cite_note-\:0-105] All this creates grounds for further fundamental changes in the ethnic structure of the peninsula.[^cite_note-ndiu-137]

After the occupation of Crimea, Ukrainians became one of the ethnic communities of the peninsula most discriminated against by the occupying Russian authorities. The occupying power persecutes Ukrainian public figures, carries out anti-Ukrainian propaganda, makes [xenophobic](34141.md) and [chauvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvinism) statements, persecutes Ukrainian religious communities, bans the activities of Ukrainian public and political organizations, restricts the use of the Ukrainian language and Ukrainian national symbols.[^cite_note-ndiu-137][^cite_note-\:13-138] Thus, as early as the first half of 2014, Ukrainian-language signs were replaced with Russian ones,[^cite_note-\:13-138] in April 2014, the [monument to Petro Sahaydachny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_Petro_Sahaydachny?action=edit\&redlink=1) and a commemorative sign in honor of the 10th anniversary of the [Ukrainian Navy](1590554.md) were dismantled in Sevastopol,[^cite_note-pravda_7023702-139] in August 2014, it was announced that 250 Ukrainian language and literature teachers would be retrained in Russian,[^cite_note-\:3-140] in September 2014, the Faculty of Ukrainian Language was liquidated at the [Tavri University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavrida_National_V.I._Vernadsky_University) (a department of Ukrainian philology was created instead),[^cite_note-investigator_136635-141] in November 2014, the [Crimean Academic Ukrainian Musical Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Academic_Ukrainian_Musical_Theater?action=edit\&redlink=1) was renamed the State Academic Musical Theater of the Republic of Crimea, and the only [Ukrainian school-gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_school-gymnasium_\(Simferopol\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Simferopol was transferred to the Russian language of instruction and renamed to Simferopol Academic Gymnasium,[^cite_note-krymr_26694622-142] in December 2014, the Ukrainian children's theater studio "Svitanok" closed due to pressure,[^cite_note-krymr_27457300-143] in March 2016, the [Lesia Ukrainka Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesia_Ukrainka_Museum_\(Yalta\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Yalta was closed for renovation,[^cite_note-radiosvoboda_27613859-144] in the same month, the Russian [FSB](385990.md) searched the premises of the Ukrainian society "Prosvita" in Sevastopol, where they seized more than 250 "extremist materials".[^cite_note-censor_382076-145] The power structures of the occupation authorities detained a number of Ukrainian figures, in particular, [Andriy Shchekun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shchekun_Andriy_Stepanovych?action=edit\&redlink=1), Anatoliy Kovalskyi and others, some of the detainees were tortured.[^cite_note-\:3-140] The Russian military seized most of the churches of the [Orthodox Church of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Ukraine) —as of October 2018, 38 out of 46 Ukrainian parishes in Crimea stopped working.[^cite_note-ukrinform_2555308-146] As of June 2019, only one OCU church remained in operation, but it was also looted by a court decision under the guise of repairs. According to Archbishop of Simferopol and Crimea [Klyment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klyment_\(Kush\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), these hostile actions of the Russian occupying power towards the Ukrainian Church are aimed at the complete destruction of Ukrainian identity and Ukrainians as a separate nation in Crimea.[^cite_note-5_196419-147]

In May 2015, the Ukrainian Cultural Center was established in Crimea, whose participants aimed to preserve Ukrainian culture and language on the peninsula.[^cite_note-\:5-148][^cite_note-\:6-149][^cite_note-dw_19141405-150] Members of the center were repeatedly detained by Russian law enforcement agencies for conducting events to celebrate Ukrainian commemorative dates, some of the participants were searched at home, several were held administratively liable by a Russian court, and they were persecuted for their views at the everyday level.[^cite_note-\:5-148][^cite_note-\:6-149][^cite_note-\:7-151] In August 2017, the center started publishing the newspaper "Krymsky Teren" in Ukrainian and Russian.[^cite_note-\:7-151]

In 2015, after the persecution of members of the UCC, the organization "Ukrainian Community of Crimea" was created, headed by Oleg Usyk, a member of "[United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia)", but it later ceased to exist. In 2018, a new legal public organization "Ukrainian Community of Crimea" was founded in Crimea, headed by Anastasia Hrydchyna, a member of the Young Guard of United Russia. This organization expresses support for the actions of the Russian authorities, denies the persecution of Ukrainians on the peninsula, participates in official events of the local occupation authorities, holds several congresses of the Ukrainian diaspora in Crimea, and created the Ukrainian-language website "Pereyaslavska rada 2.0". According to historian Andrii Ivanets and journalists of the [Krim.Realii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krim.Realii?action=edit\&redlink=1) project, in fact this organization does not care about the problems of Ukrainians in Crimea and was created as a means of information warfare.[^cite_note-krymr_30226056-152]

According to the Constitution of the [Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)) adopted in April 2014, Ukrainian became one of the official languages of this subject (along with Russian and Crimean Tatar).[^cite_note-153] Despite this, the sphere of use of the Ukrainian language is constantly shrinking. After the occupation of 2014, the study of the Ukrainian language on the peninsula was made optional, and its use in official records was stopped altogether.[^cite_note-radiosvoboda_28482897-154] The number of students with Ukrainian language of instruction continues to decrease. According to official Russian statistics, 12,892 students studied Ukrainian in the 2016/2017 academic year, and 6,400 students in the 2017/2018 academic year.[^cite_note-\:4-155] At the same time, according to Ukrainian and international observers, the real number of students with the Ukrainian language of instruction is much lower than officially declared. According to Crimean human rights group, during the occupation, the number of students studying Ukrainian decreased 31 times —from 13,589 students in 2013 to 371 students in 2016.[^cite_note-krymr_28444498-156] In April 2017, following Ukraine's appeal, the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) UN in [The Hague](30269.md) considered the case of Russia's violation of the [International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Racial_Discrimination) and adopted a decision according to which Russia should provide opportunities for teaching in the Ukrainian language on the peninsula.[^cite_note-krymr_28439724-157] According to the UN, in the 2017/2018 school year, there were 318 students in Crimea who studied in Ukrainian.[^cite_note-dw_45471374-158] Before the occupation, there were 7 Ukrainian schools in Crimea, but by 2018 all of them were translated into Russian.[^cite_note-\:4-155] According to official data of the Russian Ministry of Education of the Crimea, in the 2018/2019 school year in the Crimea, out of 200,700 children, only 249 (0.2%) studied in the Ukrainian language, there was one Ukrainian-language school and 5 schools with 8 classes with the Ukrainian language of instruction. According to the report of the Crimean human rights group in 2019, not a single school remained in Crimea with the Ukrainian language of instruction, and there were even fewer Ukrainian-language classes than officially claimed.[^cite_note-krymr_29842936-159]

## Analysis

Further information: [Environmental impact of the Russian occupation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_the_Russian_occupation_of_Crimea)

Ukrainian historians and politicians assumed a similar development of events back in 2008 during the [Russo-Georgian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Georgian_War). Experts pointed out that the Russian Federation only needed a pretext to start annexing the peninsula. The events at the [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) and the [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) became such an occasion.[^cite_note-bbc_141120-160]

In November 2014, [Andrey Illarionov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Illarionov), a former economical adviser to [President of Russia](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) and a dissident, claimed that talks of a Russian invasion began long before Yanukovych's appeal.[^cite_note-euromaidanpress_20141125_russian-161]

In January 2017, [Ilya Ponomarev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Ponomarev), a Russian politician and member of the State Duma of Russia (Fair Russia faction), claimed that the leadership of the annexation of Crimea was entrusted to Defense Minister Sergei Shoigu and Vladimir Putin's aide Vladislav Surkov.[^cite_note-162]

On 17 December 2019, the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Ukraine\)) has brought up that, in his opinion, Russia's actions in Crimea and Donbas fully fall under the definition of aggression in accordance with UN General Assembly Resolution 1976:[^cite_note-ukrinform_2839220-163] "Since 2014, Ukrainian government, has been constantly qualifying the internationally illegal actions of the Russian Federation in Crimea and Donbas as an act of aggression. We clearly and consistently proving that such actions of the Russian Federation fully fall within the definition of aggression in accordance with paragraphs a), b), c), d), e) and g) of Article 3 of the Annex to UN General Assembly Resolution 3314 (XXIX) "Definition *aggression*", adopted 45 years ago".[^cite_note-mfa_20191217-164]

On 11 December 2022, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* published an article reporting that the Western world, while supporting [Ukraine](31750.md), fears that any Ukrainian counteroffensive in Crimea could prompt [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) to take decisive action, potentially even using [nuclear weapons](21785.md). Some Western officials hope that an agreement on Ukraine's renunciation of Crimea in favor of Russia can become the basis for a diplomatic end to the war. A proponent of Ukraine, Rory Finnin, an associate professor at the Department of Ukrainian Studies at the [University of Cambridge](25978572.md), believes that a compromise regarding Crimea is unlikely:

> The idea that somehow Ukraine should just go back to the status quo post-2014 is foolish because all that will happen is another escalation. It is hard to imagine [Ukrainians](145144.md) being comfortable with giving up this territory, knowing this means the abandonment of millions of people. The moral and geopolitical stakes of such an abandonment are grave.[^cite_note-washingtonpost_20221211_crimea-165]
