**Bossier City** ([/ˈboʊʒər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BOH-zhər*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a city in [Bossier Parish, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish,_Louisiana), United States.[^cite_note-c2K-5] The population was 62,701 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census). Located on the eastern bank of the [Red River](194242.md), Bossier City is closely tied economically and socially to its sister city, [Shreveport](150473.md), on the western bank. It is the second-most populous city in the [Shreveport–Bossier City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport%E2%80%93Bossier_City_metropolitan_area). The parish operates its own community college, [Bossier Parish Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish_Community_College).

## History

### 19th century

In the 1830s, the area of Bossier City was the plantation Elysian Grove, which was purchased by James Cane and his second wife Mary Doal Cilley Bennett Cane.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

Cane had come to the area with his first wife Rebecca Bennett, and her brother, William Bennett, and his wife Mary Doal (née Cille)y Bennett. They ran a trading post across the river on what was then [Caddo Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo) territory, a portion called "Bennett's Bluff". The trading post partners became a one-seventh partner in the new Shreve Town, which eventually developed as Shreveport.

Like most plantations, Elysian Grove had frontage on the Red River for access to transportation for shipping cotton and other commodities. The [Texas Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Trail) crossed the Red River at this point. The trading post on the west side operated a ferry between what would become Shreveport and Bossier City. The plantation loading and unloading dock was later recorded as "Cane's Landing" in the old ferry log books.[^cite_note-7] For a very short time, Cane's Landing was known as Cane City.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

The Canes and Bennetts were among the earliest European-American settlers in the area. Mary D. C. Bennett gave birth to the first white baby of the area, William Smith Bennett Jr. He died at an early age.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In 1843, a section of land east of the Red River was divided from the Great Natchitoches District and Claiborne Parish areas and was called Bossier Parish. It was named in honor of [Pierre Evariste John Baptiste Bossier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Bossier), a former [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Creole_people) general, who became a [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) planter in Bossier Parish.[^cite_note-11] Of ethnic French descent, he was one of the first European settlers in the area after most of the Native Americans had been forcibly [removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Removal) by the federal government.

In the 1840s, the [Great Western Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Western_Migration?action=edit\&redlink=1) of Americans and immigrants from the East and Upper South began. The parish grew in population. Many early settlers passed through the region on their way to the [Western U.S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States). By 1850, more than 200 wagons a week passed through Bossier City, with many travelers from the Upper South intending to settle in Texas.[^cite_note-\:2-8] Some of these settlers stayed in Louisiana, attracted by the fertile soil and river valley. In 1850, the [U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_census) listed the population of Bossier City (?) at 6,962.

### American Civil War

During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), companies of [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army) soldiers shipped out of Cane's Landing aboard [steamboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat) for distant battlefields.[^cite_note-\:2-8] Mrs. Cane hosted hundreds of Confederate officers and troops who were heading off to war. Mrs. Cane's plantation was fortified to protect Shreveport by three batteries, with Fort Kirby Smith in the center. The others were Batteries Price, and Walker and Ewell.

Fort Smith protected the area from an eastern invasion.[^cite_note-\:2-8] The American Civil War reached Bossier Parish in 1861, and ended in Shreveport four years later, when the [Trans-Mississippi Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Mississippi_Department) surrendered. In the 20th century, [Bossier High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_High_School_\(777_Bearcat_Drive,_Bossier_City,_Louisiana\)) was constructed near the former site of the fort.

### Shed Road

Shed Road, the first all-weather [turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_roads_in_the_United_States#History,_funding_through_toll) in the [American South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_South), was constructed in the 1870s and operated from 1874 to 1886. It extended for 9 miles (14 km) from [Red Chute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Chute,_Louisiana) to the Red River. The elevated and covered roadway led to a [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_in_the_American_South) that was accessible by a ferry. The covered road made the transportation of goods easier before the arrival of the [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport).[^cite_note-\:1-6]

### Classification as a city

Anna B., granddaughter of James and Mary Cane, felt the area would prosper and began promoting the idea of a riverfront city. Anna B. and J. J. Stockwell sold lots in 1883. The area grew quickly, as did transportation through it. At the time, the unincorporated settlement was often called Cane City.

Around 1907, Cane City was incorporated by Louisiana [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Louisiana) [Newton C. Blanchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_C._Blanchard); the village was renamed as Bossier City. Blanchard appointed Ewald Max Hoyer, a Shreveport businessman, as the first Bossier City mayor. By that time, Bossier City had grown from an area around a square mile to a city containing more than 35 sq mi (91 km<sup>2</sup>). Continued growth led to Bossier City's being reclassified from village to [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town) by Governor [John M. Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Parker). Later, Governor [Earl Kemp Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Kemp_Long) issued a proclamation classifying Bossier City as a city.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

The "golden spike" commemorated the completion of the east–west [Vicksburg, Shreveport and Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicksburg,_Shreveport_and_Pacific_Railroad). It was driven at Bossier City on July 12, 1884, by Julia "Pansy" Rule. It was the first such spike to be driven by a woman. The north–south [Shreveport and Arkansas Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_and_Arkansas_Railroad?action=edit\&redlink=1) was completed on April 6, 1888. The [Louisiana–Arkansas Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana%E2%80%93Arkansas_Railroad?action=edit\&redlink=1) was completed on November 2, 1909.[^cite_note-12] The [Dixie Overland Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_80) from the East to the West Coast was built in 1918. These railroads and highways combined to make Bossier City a hub for future activity.

The discovery of crude oil, to the south, in 1908, thrust Bossier City into the nationwide oil boom. Bossier's central location to the rural oil fields made it a major player in the oil patch. Several international oil companies were located in the area. The advantages brought by [black gold](23195.md) fueled many civic, social and economic improvements.

A fire on June 23, 1925, consumed one-half of downtown Bossier City.[^cite_note-\:1-6] Local citizens were unable to battle the blaze. The loss spurred civic improvements, including a modern water system capable of fighting such fires, a new city hall, a modern fire alarm system, modern sidewalks, and the first city park.

In the 1930s, construction began on Barksdale Airfield (now [Barksdale Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barksdale_Air_Force_Base)). In 1929 the land on which the base is built was unincorporated property south of Bossier City. The city of Shreveport annexed the land and donated it to the federal government. Through the years, Bossier City expanded, eventually encompassing the area surrounding the base. The first unit assigned to Barksdale was the [20th Pursuit Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Pursuit_Group). Before [World War II](32927.md), Barksdale was a training school for the Army Air Corps.

During World War II, Barksdale trained pilots, navigators, and bombardiers. Later, the base became one of the key bases of the [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) in the new Air Force. Today Barksdale is the headquarters for the [8th Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Air_Force).[^cite_note-13]

In the 1890s, Cane City had a population of about 600. Bossier City in 2012 had an estimated population of over 64,000. First a cotton-exporting river landing, next a railroad town, then an airbase and oil-boom town, Bossier City has become known for its tourism and casino gambling.[^cite_note-14]

Three [casinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino) in the city have financed a number of municipal projects, many completed during the administration of the late Mayor [George Dement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Dement). Recent improvements include the [CenturyLink Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CenturyLink_Center_\(Bossier_City\)), [Louisiana Boardwalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Boardwalk), Benton Road Overpass, and the Arthur Ray Teague Parkway, located along the eastern side of the Red River. Dement also procured [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) service between Bossier City and [Dallas](53838.md), [Texas](29810.md).[^cite_note-15]

In 2005, Dement was succeeded as mayor by Lo Walker, his former administrative assistant and mayoral opponent from 1989, the first [Republican](32070.md) politician to hold the city's top executive position.[^cite_note-16]

### Growth and redevelopment

On April 20, 2017, in their joint "State of Bossier" address, hosted by the Bossier Chamber of Commerce, Mayor Lo Walker, and Bossier Parish Police Jury President Bob Brotherton described the growth of the city and parish as "outstanding." With a population of 69,000 in a 2015 study by [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University), Bossier City had become the sixth-largest city in the state and the fastest-growing one.

Walker said that the city and the [parish](23623.md) "work extremely close together, and our business and civic leaders and military make us an outstanding parish." The parish grew at 19%; the city grew at 10%. According to the Bossier Economic Development Foundation, the city could have reached 80,000 by 2019. Ongoing projects contributing to growth include the Walter O. Bigby Carriageway (the north parkway extension named for former [state representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_House_of_Representatives) and [judge](44719.md) [Walter O. Bigby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_O._Bigby)), Shed Road construction, and the South Bossier redevelopment districts.[^cite_note-17]

## Geography

The city lies primarily on the banks of the [Red River](194242.md), and has a largely flat topography in contrast with Shreveport's terrain. The northern city limits are noticeably more hilly than the rest of the city. Many small waterways flow through the city, such as Flat River and Red Chute Bayou, which provide drainage for many areas of the city.[^cite_note-18]

The city has a total area of 43.90 square miles (113.69 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 0.99 square miles (2.56 km<sup>2</sup>) are covered by water.[^cite_note-wwwcensusgov-19]

### Climate

Bossier shares most aspects of its climate with its sister city of [Shreveport](150473.md). The city has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*) with hot, humid summers and mild winters.

During the warmer months, the city is prone to severe thunderstorms that feature heavy rain, high winds, hail, and occasional tornadoes. The city has a slightly above-average rate of tornadoes when compared to the U.S. average. Due to the flat topography of the city and the prominence of smaller waterways that are prone to backwater flooding from the Red River, the city occasionally experiences severe flooding events. A notable occurrence of severe flooding occurred in March 2016 after torrential rains caused a rapid rise of many local waterways, displacing upwards of 3,500 people from their homes across the area.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Freezes and ice storms regularly occur during the winters.

### 2020 census

Per the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), 62,701 people, 26,677 households, and 17,524 families resided in the city.[^cite_note-\:4-31] According to 2019 census estimates per the [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), 24.2% of the population were 18 and older, and 13.8% were 65 and older; the 26,927 households from 2015 to 2019 had an average of 2.44 people per household. In 1890, Bossier City initially had a population of 202, which has steadily increased since.

In 2019, the racial and ethnic makeup of the city was 58.5% [non-Hispanic or Latino white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_or_Latino_whites), 27.9% [Black or African American](2154.md), 0.4% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 2.4% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.1% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and other [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_Americans), 2.5% [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans), and 8.6% [Hispanic and Latino American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) of any race.[^cite_note-\:3-32] At the 2020 census, the racial and ethnic makeup of the city was 50.79% non-Hispanic white, 30.45% Black or African American, 0.38% Native American, 2.36% Asian, 0.12% Pacific Islander, 5.22% two or more races, and 10.68% Hispanic and Latino American of any race; the 2020 census reflected nationwide trends of greater diversification and the growth of traditional minority populations in areas once predominantly non-Hispanic white.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

Of the 26,927 households estimated at 2019's American Community Survey, the owner-occupied housing rate was 52.8% and the median value of an owner-occupied housing unit was \$163,500. The median cost with a mortgage was \$1,182 versus \$363 without a mortgage, and the median gross rent was \$955. The median income for a household in the city was \$50,340, and the per capita income was \$26,755; an estimated 19.7% of the population lived at or below the poverty line. By 2020, its median household income was \$48,385 with a mean income of \$63,114.[^cite_note-35]

### Religion

The majority of Bossier City's population is [Christian](5211.md).[^cite_note-\:0-36] Neighboring Shreveport is more religiously diverse than Bossier. Of its population, 37.9% identified as [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists), primarily affiliated with the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention) and [National Baptist Convention, USA, Inc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baptist_Convention,_USA,_Inc.). Within the city, 6.7% were [Methodists](20119.md), primarily served by the [United Methodist Church](32304.md), 5.2% were [Catholics](606848.md) in the [Shreveport Diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Shreveport), 2.2% [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism), 0.9% [Latter Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement), 0.4% [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism), 0.4% [Lutheran](23371382.md), and 0.2% [Episcopalian or Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism). Within the Christian population of Bossier City, 7.7% claimed to be from another Christian group. Outside of Christendom, 0.3% of the city's residents were adherents to [Islam](6037917.md).[^cite_note-\:0-36] Less than 0.1% of Bossier's residents identified with [Judaism](15624.md), or eastern religions such as [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) or [Buddhism](3267529.md).[^cite_note-\:0-36]

## Media

As a sister city to Shreveport, the city of Bossier City is served by the *[Bossier Press-Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Press-Tribune)* and *[Shreveport Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_\(Shreveport\))*.[^cite_note-46] In addition, *The Forum*, *City Lights*, and *SB Magazine* are news magazines in the Shreveport–Bossier City area. The city shares the same television and radio markets with Shreveport and the [Texarkana metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texarkana_metropolitan_area) as part of the [Ark-La-Tex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark-La-Tex).

In mainstream media, "Bossier City" is a song by [David Allan Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Allan_Coe), in which he sings, "And it sure smells like snow in Bossier City..." [Johnny Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Rodriguez) recorded a song called "Achin' Bossier City Backyard Blues" in 1972. [Turnpike Troubadours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnpike_Troubadours) 2007 freshman album is entitled Bossier City, and includes the title track "Bossier City".

## Sports and entertainment



[Horseshoe Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_Bossier_City)



The [Red River](194242.md) from the Arthur Teague Parkway in Bossier City

From the 1930s to the 1970s, Bossier was regionally and even nationally known for its entertainment district known as [The Bossier Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bossier_Strip), which followed [U.S. Highway 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Highway_80) through the city. Nightclubs proliferated from the Texas Avenue Bridge to the Bossier-[Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster_Parish,_Louisiana) parish line. Prior to the 1940s, The Strip was as well known for such entertainment as [Las Vegas, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas,_Nevada).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

Bossier City and Shreveport share an all-women's flat track [roller derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roller_derby) team named the Twin City Knockers. The team is the newest competing sport in the area, founded in January 2010. Bouts are hosted at Hot Wheels skating rink in south Bossier.

The [Brookshire Grocery Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookshire_Grocery_Arena) (formerly CenturyTel Center) in Bossier City was the home of the [Bossier–Shreveport Battle Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier%E2%80%93Shreveport_Battle_Wings) of the [AF2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AF2), as well as the [Bossier-Shreveport Mudbugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier-Shreveport_Mudbugs) of the [Central Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Hockey_League). The arena has hosted top performers, including [Britney Spears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britney_Spears) and [Aerosmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosmith), as well as rodeos, ice shows, and children's entertainment.

The 2005 Red River Classic PRCA [Rodeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodeo) to be hosted at the CenturyTel Center was cancelled due to the arena's use as a shelter for [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) evacuees.[^cite_note-50]

The city hosts three [riverboat casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverboat_casino) gambling resorts along the east bank of the Red River: [Margaritaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaritaville_Resort_Casino), [Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_Bossier_City), and [Boomtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomtown_Bossier_City). Diamond Jacks previously operated in the area before closing, but Foundation Gaming has plans for reopening in 2024.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] [Horse racing](74711.md) and gambling on slot machines is also available at [Louisiana Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Downs), which opened in 1974.
