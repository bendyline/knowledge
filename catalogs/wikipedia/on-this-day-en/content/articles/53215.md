[South Africa](17416221.md) is administered under nine provinces.[^cite_note-1] On the eve of the [1994 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_South_African_general_election), South Africa's former homelands, known as [Bantustans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantustan), were reintegrated into the country, and the four provinces were increased to nine. The borders of [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_\(province\)) and the [Orange Free State](180208.md) were retained, while the [Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Province) and [Transvaal Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_\(province\)) were divided into three provinces each, plus [North West Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Province) which straddles the border of and contains territory from both of these former provinces. The twelfth, thirteenth and sixteenth amendments to the [Constitution of South Africa](441610.md) changed the borders of seven of the provinces.

## History



The provinces at the creation of the Union in 1910

See also: [List of administrators of former South African provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_administrators_of_former_South_African_provinces)

The [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa) was established in 1910 by combining four British colonies: [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony), [Natal Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_Colony), [Transvaal Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Colony), and [Orange River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_River_Colony). The last two were, before the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War), independent republics known as the [South African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Republic) and the [Orange Free State](180208.md). These colonies became the four original provinces of the Union: [Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Province), [Natal Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_Province), [Transvaal Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Province), and [Orange Free State Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Free_State_Province).



Provinces and homelands, as they were at the end of apartheid

Segregation of the [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) population started as early as 1913, with ownership of land by the black majority being restricted to certain areas totalling about 13% of the country. From the late 1950s, these areas were gradually consolidated into "[homelands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland)", also called "[bantustans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantustan)". Four of these homelands were established as quasi-independent nation states of the black population during the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) era. In 1976, the homeland of [Transkei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transkei) was the first to accept [independence](62450.md) from South Africa, and although this independence was never acknowledged by any other country, three other homelands –  [Bophuthatswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bophuthatswana) (1977), [Venda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_\(Bantustan\)) (1979) and [Ciskei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciskei) (1981) –  followed suit.

On 27 April 1994, the date of the [first non-racial elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_1994) and of the adoption of the [Interim Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Constitution_of_1993), all of these provinces and homelands were dissolved, and nine new provinces were established. The boundaries of these provinces were established in 1993 by a *Commission on the Demarcation/Delimitation of Regions* created by [CODESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiations_to_end_apartheid_in_South_Africa), and were broadly based on *planning regions* demarcated by the [Development Bank of Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Bank_of_Southern_Africa) in the 1980s,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-historyBantustansOxfordRe-3] and amalgamated from existing [magisterial districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate's_court_\(South_Africa\)), with some concessions to political parties that wished to consolidate their power bases, by transferring districts between the proposed provinces.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-kingdomVolkstaatNewSa-5] The definitions of the new provinces in terms of magisterial districts were found in Schedule 1 of the [Interim Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Constitution_\(South_Africa\)).

On 11 July 2003, the [11th amendment to the fifth constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_South_Africa) renamed the Northern Province to [Limpopo](365944.md). On 1 March 2006, the [12th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_South_Africa) and [13th amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_South_Africa) altered the boundaries of 7 provinces. On 3 April 2009 the [16th amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteenth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_South_Africa) altered the boundaries of the [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_\(South_African_province\)) and [Gauteng](309661.md) provinces.

## Government



Johannesburg City Hall, now the seat of the [Gauteng Provincial Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng_Provincial_Legislature)

Main article: [Provincial governments of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_governments_of_South_Africa)

South Africa's provinces are governed, in different ways, on a national, provincial and local level.[^cite_note-South_Africa_Gateway-6]

Nationally, there is the National Council of Provinces, one of the houses of Parliament. Then there is the provincial government and, below that, the administration of district and metropolitan municipalities.

### National Council of Provinces

Main article: [National Council of Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_Provinces)

South Africa has two houses of parliament: the National Assembly, and the National Council of Provinces.[^cite_note-South_Africa_Gateway-6] The second exists to ensure that the interests of each province are protected in the laws passed by the National Assembly.

Each one of South Africa's nine provinces sends 10 representatives to the National Council of Provinces. Six of these are permanent members of the council, and four are special delegates.

### Provincial government

Each province is governed by a [unicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameral_legislature). The size of the legislature is proportional to population, ranging from 30 members in the [Northern Cape](447008.md) to 80 in [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md). The legislatures are elected every five years by a system of [party-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation); by convention, they are all elected on the same day, at the same time as the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(South_Africa\)) election.[^cite_note-SAinfo-7]

The provincial legislature elects, from amongst its members, a [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_\(South_Africa\)), who is the head of the executive. The Premier chooses an [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_\(South_Africa\)) consisting of between five and ten members of the legislature, which is the cabinet of the provincial government.[^cite_note-SAinfo-7] The [Members of the Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Executive_Council) (MECs) are the provincial equivalent of [ministers](739237.md).

The powers of the provincial government are limited to specific topics listed in the [national constitution](441610.md). On some of these topics – for example, agriculture, education, health and public housing – the province's powers are shared with the national government, which can establish uniform standards and frameworks for the provincial governments to follow; on other topics the provincial government has exclusive power.[^cite_note-8]

The provinces do not have their own court systems, as the administration of justice is the responsibility of the national government.
