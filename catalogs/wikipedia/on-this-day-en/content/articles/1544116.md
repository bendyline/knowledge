An **air-to-surface missile** (**ASM**) or **air-to-ground missile** (**AGM**) is a [missile](19594.md) designed to be launched from [military aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aircraft) at targets on land or sea. There are also unpowered guided [glide bombs](233100.md) not considered missiles. The two most common propulsion systems for air-to-surface missiles are [rocket motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_motor), usually with shorter range, and slower, longer-range [jet engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine). Some [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-designed air-to-surface missiles are powered by [ramjets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramjet), giving them both long range and high speed.

[Guidance for air-to-surface missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_guidance) is typically via [laser guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_guidance), [infrared guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing), [optical guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_guidance) or via [satellite guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_guidance) signals. The type of guidance depends on the type of target. Ships, for example, may be detected via [passive radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_radar) or [active radar homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_radar_homing), which is less effective against multiple, small, fast-moving land targets.

There is some cross-over between air-to-surface missiles and [surface-to-surface missiles](1483499.md). For example, there was an air-launched version of the [Tomahawk missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)), superseded by the [AGM-86 ALCM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-86_ALCM). Other missiles used in both roles include the [Penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_\(missile\)) and [AGM-84 Harpoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84_Harpoon) [anti-ship missiles](140725.md). Many air-to-surface missiles can be used against both ships and land targets, although some must be modified to perform a different role; for example, the [AGM-84E Standoff Land Attack Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84E_Standoff_Land_Attack_Missile) is a land-attack version of the Harpoon.

A major advantage of air-to-surface missiles for ground attack by aircraft is the [standoff distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standoff_distance) they provide: missiles can be launched from a distance without coming within range of the target's air defences. Most air-to-surface missiles are [fire-and-forget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-and-forget) from a standoff distance, allowing the attacker to withdraw without approaching further after launch. Some missiles (typically [cruise missiles](6590.md) or anti-ship missiles) have long enough range to be launched over the horizon, finding the target autonomously.

Sub-categories of air-to-surface missiles include:

- air-launched [anti-tank guided missiles](37517.md) (typically launched from [helicopters](8286923.md))

- air-launched [cruise missiles](6590.md)

- air-launched [anti-ship missiles](140725.md)

- [anti-radiation missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-radiation_missile)

Typically, the higher and faster the launching aircraft is flying, the longer the reach of a particular missile is. For long-range missiles this difference can be relatively small, but short-range missiles (like the [AGM-65 Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-65_Maverick)) have a much longer range when launched at altitude.

There have been examples of [air-launched ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-launched_ballistic_missile) ([Air Launched ICBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Launched_ICBM), [GAM-87 Skybolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAM-87_Skybolt)), but they are rare. Sometimes air-to-surface missiles are divided into the categories of *tactical* and *strategic*. Typically missiles with chemical explosive or small nuclear warheads are classed as tactical, and large nuclear warheads as strategic.
