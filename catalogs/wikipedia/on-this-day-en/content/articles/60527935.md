**Laurent Marie Joseph Nuñez-Belda** (French pronunciation: [\[lɔʁɑ̃ nuɲɛs bɛlda\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); born 19 February 1964) is a French senior civil servant and politician who has served as [Minister of the Interior](871703.md) in the [second government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Lecornu_government) of Prime Minister [Sébastien Lecornu](54364868.md) since 12 October 2025.

He previously served as director-general of the [General Directorate for Internal Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Directorate_for_Internal_Security) (DGSI) from 2017 to 2018, Secretary of State to the Minister of the Interior in the [second government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Philippe_government) of [Édouard Philippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Philippe) from 2018 to 2020, head of the [National Centre for Intelligence and Counter-Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Centre_for_Counter_Terrorism) (CNRLT) from 2020 to 2022 and [Prefect of Police of Paris](1386763.md) from 2022 to 2025.

## Early life and education

Of Spanish descent, Nuñez was born in [Bourges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourges) in 1964.[^cite_note-1] His father was an architect; his mother was a teacher.[^cite_note-Le_Monde-2] He studied public management at the [University of Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tours), obtaining a *[diplôme d'études supérieures spécialisées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipl%C3%B4me_d'%C3%A9tudes_sup%C3%A9rieures_sp%C3%A9cialis%C3%A9es)* before joining the [French civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_civil_service) in 1989. In 1999, he graduated from the [École nationale d'administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_nationale_d'administration).[^cite_note-bio-3]

## Career

A civil servant at the Ministry of the Interior, Nuñez was named chief of staff (*directeur de cabinet*) of the [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect_\(France\)) of [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) in 2008. In 2010, he was appointed [subprefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprefect) of the [arrondissement of Bayonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement_of_Bayonne). In 2012, Nuñez became chief of staff at the [Paris Police Prefecture](1386763.md), and in 2015 he was appointed police prefect of [Bouches-du-Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-du-Rh%C3%B4ne).[^cite_note-bio-3]

As part of the reorganisation of the French intelligence services, Nuñez was appointed director-general at the DGSI in June 2017 by newly elected President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md), and succeeded [Patrick Calvar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Calvar). He pursued a new policy of "openness".[^cite_note-4]

In October 2018, he was appointed to the government as [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_\(France\)) to the [Minister of the Interior](871703.md), under Minister [Christophe Castaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Castaner).[^cite_note-5] He was especially responsible for the coordination of the intelligence services and the police.[^cite_note-6]

In July 2020, Nuñez was appointed Head of the [National Center of Intelligence and Counter-Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Centre_for_Counter_Terrorism) (CNRLT), succeeding [Pierre de Bousquet de Florian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_de_Bousquet_de_Florian).[^cite_note-7] In July 2022, he was named Paris police prefect.

In 2025, he was appointed Minister of the Interior, succeeding [Bruno Retailleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Retailleau), who left the government following policy disagreements. In 2026, Nuñez faced the [killing of Quentin Deranque](82434853.md) in Lyon, which he accused the [ultra-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-leftism) of orchestrating.[^cite_note-8]
