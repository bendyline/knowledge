**Public works** are a broad category of [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) projects, financed and [procured](8488190.md) by a [government](12229.md) body for employment, health and safety, and recreational uses in the greater [community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community). They include [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection) ([drinking water protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_water_protection), preservation and restoration of forests and wetlands, soil erosion reduction, [wildlife habitat preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_restoration)), public buildings ([hospitals](18994221.md), [municipal buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_and_town_halls), and [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School)), public services ([dams](51518.md), [electrical grid](20344155.md), [sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment), and [water supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_network) and [treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_purification)), [public spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_spaces) ([beaches](40630.md), [parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park), and [public squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_square)), [transport infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_infrastructure) ([airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport), [bridges](3397.md), [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal), [pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport), [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port), [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad), and [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road)) and other, usually long-term, physical [assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset) and [facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building). Though often interchangeable with [public capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_capital) and [public infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_infrastructure), public works does not necessarily carry an economic component, thereby being a broader term. Construction may be undertaken either by directly employed labour or by a private operator.

Public works has been encouraged since antiquity. The [Roman emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor) [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero) encouraged the construction of various infrastructure projects during widespread [deflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflation).[^cite_note-1]

## Overview&#x20;

Public works is a multi-dimensional concept in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics), touching on multiple arenas including: recreation (parks, beaches, trails), aesthetics (trees, green space), economy (goods and people movement, energy), law (police and courts), and neighborhood (community centers, social services buildings). It represents any [constructed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction) object that augments a nation's physical infrastructure.

Municipal infrastructure, [urban infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_infrastructure), and [rural development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_development) usually represent the same concept but imply either large cities or [developing nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_nation)' concerns respectively. The terms [public infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_infrastructure) or [critical infrastructure](521286.md) are at times used interchangeably. However, critical infrastructure includes public works (dams, waste water systems, bridges, etc.) as well as facilities like hospitals, banks, and telecommunications systems and views them from a [national security](240468.md) viewpoint and the impact on the community that the loss of such facilities would entail.



Public works in Reggio Emilia, Emilia-Romagna, Italy

Furthermore, the term public works has recently been expanded to include digital public infrastructure projects. For example, in the [United States](3434750.md), the first nationwide digital public works project is an effort to create an open source software platform for e-voting (created and managed by the Open Source Digital Voting Foundation).[^cite_note-2]

Reflecting increased concern with [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability), [urban ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_ecology) and [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life), efforts to move towards [sustainable municipal infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_municipal_infrastructure) are common in [developed nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_nation), especially in the [European Union](9317.md) and [Canada](5042916.md) (where the [FCM InfraGuide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FCM_InfraGuide) provides an officially mandated [best practice exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_practice_exchange) to move municipalities in that direction).

## Public works programmes

A public employment programme or public works programme is the provision of employment by the creation of predominantly [public goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_goods) at a prescribed wage for those unable to find alternative employment. This functions as a form of [social safety net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_safety_net). Public works programmes are activities which entail the payment of a wage (in cash or in kind) by the state, or by an Agent (or cash-for work/CFW). One particular form of public works, that of offering a short-term period of employment, has come to dominate practice, particularly in regions such as [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md). Applied in the short term, this is appropriate as a response to transient shocks and acute labour market crises.[^cite_note-3]

Investing in public works projects in order to stimulate the general economy has been a popular policy measure since the economic crisis of the 1930s. Spearheaded by U.S. Secretary of Labor [Frances Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Perkins), the first female Cabinet member in the United States, the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) resulted in the creation of programs such as the [Civilian Conservation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Conservation_Corps), [Public Works Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Works_Administration), and the [Works Progress Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_Progress_Administration), among others, all of which created public goods through labor and infrastructure investments.[^cite_note-4]

More recent examples are the 2008–2009 [Chinese economic stimulus program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_economic_stimulus_program), India's [National Infrastructure Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Infrastructure_Pipeline) of 2020, the [2008 European Union stimulus plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_European_Union_stimulus_plan), and the [American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Recovery_and_Reinvestment_Act_of_2009).

## Utility of investment

While it is argued that capital investment in public works can be used to reduce unemployment, opponents of internal improvement programs argue that such projects should be undertaken by the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector), not the [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector), because public works projects are often inefficient and costly to taxpayers. Further, some argue that public works, when used excessively by a government, are characteristic of [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) and other public or collectivist forms of government because of their 'tax and spend' policies to achieve long-term economic improvement. However, in the private sector, entrepreneurs bear their own losses  and so private-sector firms are generally unwilling to undertake projects that could result in losses or would not develop a revenue stream. Governments will invest in public works because of the overall benefit to society when there is a lack of private sector benefit (a project that does generate revenue) or the risk is too great for a private company to accept on its own.

According to research conducted at the [Aalborg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_University), 86% of public works projects end up with cost overruns. Some findings of the research were the following:

- Technically difficult projects were not more likely to exceed the budget than less difficult projects.

- Projects in which more people were directly and indirectly affected by the project turned out to be more susceptible to cost overruns.

- Project managers generally did not learn from similar projects attempted in the past.

Generally, contracts awarded by public tenders include a provision for unexpected expenses (cost overruns), which typically amount to 10% of the value of the contract. This money is spent during the course of the project only if the construction managers judge that it is necessary, and the expenditure must typically be justified in writing.
