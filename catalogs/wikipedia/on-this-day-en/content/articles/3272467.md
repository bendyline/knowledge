**DP World** is an Emirati [multinational](214491.md) [logistics company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics_company) based in [Dubai](211583.md), United Arab Emirates. It specialises in [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo) logistics, port terminal operations, maritime services and [free trade zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_zone). Formed in 2005 by the merger of Dubai Ports Authority and Dubai Ports International, DP World handles 70 million [containers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_Container) that are brought in by around 70,000 vessels annually. This equates to roughly 10% of global container traffic accounted for by their 82 marine and inland terminals present in over 40 countries. Until 2016, DP World was primarily a global port operator, but since then, it has acquired other companies up and down the [value chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_chain).

[Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem](7424329.md) became chairman of DP World in 2007. He has been credited with transforming DP World from a regional ports operator into a global logistics firm.[^cite_note-\:0-5] Sulayem resigned from DP World in 2026 after it came to light that Sulayem had an extensive relationship with disgraced child sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md).[^cite_note-\:0-5]

## History

### Early history

Dubai Ports International (DPI) was founded in 1999.[^cite_note-DPWHistory-6] Its first project was at [Jeddah](277718.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), collaborating with a local partner on the South Container Terminal (SCT). DPI then began operations at the ports of [Djibouti](17207794.md) in 2000, [Vizag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizag), India in 2002 and [Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba), Romania in 2003.[^cite_note-DPWHistory-6] In January 2005, DPI acquired CSX World Terminals (CSX WT).[^cite_note-CSX-7] In September 2005 Dubai Ports International officially merged with the Dubai Ports Authority to form DP World.[^cite_note-Merger-8] The rapid expansion through acquisition continued in March 2006 when DP World purchased the fourth largest ports operator in the world, [P\&O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O_\(company\)), for £3.9 billion.

P\&O operated major US port facilities in [New York](8210131.md), [New Jersey](21648.md), [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), [New Orleans](53842.md), and [Miami](53846.md). Before the deal was secured, the arrangement was reviewed by the [Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_Foreign_Investment_in_the_United_States) headed by the [US Treasury Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Treasury_Department) and including the Departments of [State](31975.md), [Commerce](70243.md), and [Homeland Security](58236.md). It was given the green light, but soon after, both [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Democratic_Party) and [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Republican_party) members of [Congress](31756.md) expressed concern over the potential negative impact the deal would have on port security.

On 22 February 2006, President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) threatened to [veto](74661.md) any legislation passed by Congress to block the deal, which would be the first time in his presidency he would exercise the privilege. In a statement to reporters, Bush claimed, "It would send a terrible signal to friends and allies not to let this transaction go through."[^cite_note-9] On 23 February 2006, DP World volunteered to postpone its takeover of significant operations at the seaports and on 9 March 2006, it said that it would transfer its operations of American ports to a "[US entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_entity)".[^cite_note-10]

The [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md) held a vote on 16 March 2006 on legislation that would have blocked the DP World deal, with 348 members voting for blocking the deal, and 71 voting against.[^cite_note-11] DP World later sold P\&O's American operations to [American International Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_International_Group)'s asset management division, Global Investment Group for an undisclosed sum.[^cite_note-wsj_20061211_dpw_aig-12]

In August 2006, DP World signed an agreement with the [Port Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Qasim) Authority, to invest in a new container terminal at Port Muhammad Qasim near [Karachi](17123.md) and announced that it was in discussions with the Pakistani Government about the development of a container terminal at [Gwadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar) in [Balochistan](8431743.md).[^cite_note-DP_World_News_Releases-13] DP World had been favourite to win the Gwadar concession, but withdrew from the bidding.[^cite_note-World_Cargo_News-14] [Gwadar Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar_Port) was subsequently awarded to PSA (Port of [Singapore](27318.md) Authority) and opened in March 2007.[^cite_note-15]

### 2006: US Port security

Main article: [Dubai Ports World controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Ports_World_controversy)

The ownership of various US ports by DP World (which had been acquired as part of the P\&O deal) was seen as controversial by many in the United States even though it was supported by the US president [George W. Bush](3414021.md); the US ports were sold shortly afterwards.

### 2022: UK mass redundancy

Main article: [P\&O sacking of 800 staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O_sacking_of_800_staff)

On 17 March 2022, P\&O Ferries sacked its entire British work force and abruptly suspended its operations, cancelling all sailings and offloading passengers and cargo. Eight hundred UK staff were told in a video call that their employment was "terminated with immediate effect due to redundancy", and that their work would in future be undertaken by staff contracted to a third-party supplier. A spokesman for the [National Union of Rail, Maritime and Transport Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Rail,_Maritime_and_Transport_Workers) said that there had been no consultation with the staff or [trade unions](17626.md).[^cite_note-16] Staff on some ferries refused to leave their vessels. Some replacement staff, unaware of the circumstances or location of their deployment, declined to work,[^cite_note-17] and a recruitment agency who supplied replacement staff said that they were unaware of the plans.[^cite_note-18]

The method of the expulsion, overseen by ex-military security guards, has been criticised by government and business leaders, and Mark Russell, a non-executive director of DP World, resigned afterwards in disagreement. P\&O Ferries had recently seen losses during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) as well as deficits in its pension fund. Industry sources have also cited its high overheads and competition from [Irish Ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Ferries) as contributory factors. Verity Slater, expert in maritime employment law at Stephens Scown, commented that P\&O may not have to follow UK employment laws, since some of its ships are not registered in Britain.[^cite_note-19]

### Recent history



[London Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Gateway) is a deepwater port and logistics park in the UK, wholly owned and operated by DP World

In June 2007 the company raised \$3.25 billion in Islamic and conventional bond sales to refinance existing debt and fund expansion[^cite_note-20] and issued 3.818 billion shares, representing 20% of the company on the [NASDAQ Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ_Dubai) [stock exchange](28168.md) in November 2007 in what was the Middle East's largest [initial public offering](63879.md) (IPO) which raised 4.96 billion dollars.[^cite_note-21]

By 2008 the company was handing 46.8 million [TEU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_unit) worldwide, up 8% on 2007, with expansion and development projects in India, China, the Middle East, and elsewhere. Capacity was expected to rise to around 95 million TEU over the next ten years.[^cite_note-DPWHistory-6]

In December 2009 [Moody's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's) downgraded DP World's financial status to [junk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-yield_debt) after the [Dubai 2009 debt standstill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_World).[^cite_note-22]

In the second quarter of 2010 DP World gave the go-ahead for construction of the £1.5 billion [London Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Gateway) port.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Work started in February 2010[^cite_note-25] with the port due to open in the fourth quarter of 2013. By April 2011, [Moody's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's) upgraded DP World's financial status to ['investment grade'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_grade#Investment_grade).[^cite_note-26] Since December 2010, DP World has undertaken a series of asset disposals, exiting markets where it does not have a significant presence and seeking to redeploy funds in fast-growing markets.[^cite_note-27]

On 20 February 2019, DP World announced it had repurchased P\&O Ferries from Dubai World in a £322m deal.[^cite_note-NIF-28]

In June 2019 DP World signed agreements with the [Russian Direct Investment Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Direct_Investment_Fund), [ROSATOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROSATOM), and [Norilsk Nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norilsk_Nickel) aiming to develop the [Northern Sea Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sea_Route).[^cite_note-s4s-29][^cite_note-30]



DP World CEO [Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem](7424329.md) and [Somaliland](51425.md)'s President [Muse Bihi Abdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_Bihi_Abdi) in July 2021

In March 2021, the company said that, due to the COVID-19 pandemic, it had witnessed a drop of its profits by 29% comparing to 2019.[^cite_note-31] In May of the same year, Abdulla Bin Damithan was appointed as CEO and managing director of UAE business.[^cite_note-32] In October, coinciding with the launch of [Expo 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2020), DP World unveiled its new corporate identity.[^cite_note-DPW_cites_new_corporate_branding-33] DP World is an official premier partner of Expo 2020.[^cite_note-34]

In March 2023, sold its shares in [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Le_Havre) terminals and consolidate its operations in [Marseille-Fos Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Fos_Port) by maintaining a 50\:50 ownership.

In November 2023, a cyberattack on DP World paralysed imports and exports in Australia for several days. DP World accounts for about 40% of Australia's imports and exports, leading to a 30,000-container backlog; additionally, data was stolen.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

On 14 October 2024, DP World announced a £1 billion expansion plan of the London Gateway port at the London investment summit, despite criticism from the UK ministers. The investment was previously under review, after [Louise Haigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Haigh) called DP World's subsidiary, P\&O Ferries, a "rogue operator" for sacking 800 seafarers in March 2022. She had also urged consumers to boycott the company. Offended by Haigh's comments, DP World had threatened to pull out of the investment summit. However, Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem later confirmed to attend the summit and for the investment to go ahead as planned.[^cite_note-38] On 27 October, Ukraine's [Oleksandr Merezhko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Merezhko) condemned DP World's cooperation with Moscow, referring to Russian involvement in Ukraine war. He urged the UK ministers to cut ties with the firm due to its controversial business deals with [Rosatom](1641369.md).[^cite_note-39]

In March 2025, works started on the expansion on London Gateway to make it the UK's largest container port.[^cite_note-40]

In May 2025, DP World announced a plan to invest US \$2.5 billion in major logistics and port infrastructure projects across India, Africa, South America, and Europe to enlarge supply chain capabilities.[^cite_note-41] In India, a \$510 million terminal at Tuna Tekra, Gujarat, featuring a 1.1 km quay and annual capacity of 2.19 million TEUs.[^cite_note-42] In Europe, investments include the ongoing expansion of London Gateway, and new developments in South America.[^cite_note-43]

In 2026, Chairman and CEO [Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem](7424329.md) announced his resignation from the company due to his relationship with [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md).[^cite_note-44] Multiple companies had announced that they would stop working with DP World amid reporting on Sulayem's extensive ties to Epstein.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

In July 2026, the [Maritime Union of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Union_of_Australia) called for a 28-hour working week without reduction in pay during negotiations over DP World's increasing use of artificial intelligence and automation at its Australian container terminals which, according to a study commissioned by the union, could affect up to 1,000 dock and maintenance jobs.[^cite_note-45]

## Corporate affairs

### Supply chain security

DP World has been certified as a partner in the [Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs-Trade_Partnership_Against_Terrorism) (C‑TPAT) initiative by [US Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) – to date, the only international port operator to have achieved this recognition. This certification is primarily based on DP World's commitment to the independently audited international [ISO 28000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/PAS_28000) security standards. DP World was the first global maritime terminal operator to achieve ISO 28000 [supply chain security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain_security) certification and is rolling out the standard to all its terminals.[^cite_note-C-TPATfreelib-48]

### Counter-piracy

In June 2012, DP World convened the second high-level, public-private counterpiracy conference, in Dubai.[^cite_note-49] The *Washington Post* reported "Diplomats and business leaders...are pushing for stronger partnerships between the public and private sectors in combating [piracy off the coast of Somalia](17015903.md)".[^cite_note-50]

The April 2011 conference was the first time companies rather than governments had contributed financial support to the UN counter-piracy trust fund.[^cite_note-51] The inaugural conference was attended by more than 65 governments and representatives of international organisations, including the United Nations[^cite_note-52] and more than 120 leaders from maritime industry related companies.

### Berbera operations



&#x20;New [DP World Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DP_World_Berbera_New_Port) Container Terminal Port.

In 2016 a US\$442 million agreement was reached between DP World and the government of [Somaliland](51425.md).[^cite_note-53] The deal involves enhancing and operating the regional trade and logistics hub at the [Port of Berbera](41240501.md).[^cite_note-Abaltiisbp-54] The project, which will be phased in, will also involve the setting up of a [free zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_economic_zone).

On 1 March 2018, [Ethiopia](187749.md) became a major shareholder following an agreement with DP World and the Somaliland Port Authority. DP World holds a 51% stake in the project, Somaliland 30% and Ethiopia the remaining 19%. As part of the agreement, the government of Ethiopia will invest in infrastructure to develop the Berbera Corridor as a trade gateway for the inland country, which is one of the fastest-growing countries in the world. There are also plans to construct an additional berth at the Port of Berbera, in line with the Berbera master plan which DP World has started implementing, while adding new equipment to further improve the efficiencies and productivity of the port.[^cite_note-55]

The agreement comes as part of a larger government-to-government [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) between the Government of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) and the Government of [Somaliland](51425.md) to further strengthen their strategic ties.[^cite_note-56] [Somalia](27358.md)'s attempts to obstruct and block the deal were frustrated and failed to stop the project from commencing.[^cite_note-57]

### DP World Dakar

On 3 January 2022, construction began on the US\$1.13 billion deep-water port at [Ndayane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndayane?action=edit\&redlink=1), around 50 km (31 miles) south of the Senegalese capital [Dakar](44251.md), when president [Macky Sall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macky_Sall) laid the foundation stone. DP World Dakar is a joint venture between DP World and the Port Authority of Dakar. The first phase of the project will include 840 metres of quay and a 5 kilometres marine channel designed to handle 366-metre vessels. The second phase will add 410 metres of container quay and further dredging to handle 400-metre vessels.[^cite_note-58]
