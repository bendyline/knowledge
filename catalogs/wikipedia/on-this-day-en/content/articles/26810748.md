**Iowa** ([/ˈaɪ.əwə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8d/En-us-Iowa.ogg/En-us-Iowa.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8d/En-us-Iowa.ogg/En-us-Iowa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*EYE-ə-wə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] is a landlocked [state](18618239.md) in the upper [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) region of the [United States](3434750.md). It borders the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) to the east and the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) and [Big Sioux River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sioux_River) to the west; [Wisconsin](33127.md) to the northeast, [Illinois](14849.md) to the east and southeast, [Missouri](19571.md) to the south, [Nebraska](21647.md) to the west, [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) to the northwest, and [Minnesota](19590.md) to the north. Iowa is the [26th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) in total area and the [31st most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) of the [50 U.S. states](12610470.md), with a population of 3.19 million.[^cite_note-12] The state's [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States), [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Iowa), and largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_statistical_areas) fully located within the state is [Des Moines](9163.md).

During the 18th and early 19th centuries, Iowa was a part of [French Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)) and [Spanish Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_Spain\)); its [state flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Iowa) is patterned after the [flag of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_France). After the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase), pioneers laid the foundation for an agriculture-based economy in the heart of the [Corn Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Belt).[^cite_note-13] In the latter half of the 20th century, Iowa's agricultural economy began to transition to a diversified economy of advanced manufacturing, processing, [financial services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_services), [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), [biotechnology](4502.md), and [green energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy) production.[^cite_note-iaindustries-14][^cite_note-energy.iastate.edu-15]

Politically, Iowa is notable for the [Iowa Caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_caucuses), an influential event in national politics, as well as its high levels of [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout_in_United_States_presidential_elections) and foundational leadership in civil rights including early adoption or support of [black suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_suffrage).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Etymology

Like [many other states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_and_territory_name_etymologies_of_the_United_States), Iowa takes its name from its predecessor, [Iowa Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Territory), whose name in turn is derived from the [Iowa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_River), and ultimately from the [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) of the indigenous [Ioway people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioway_people). The Ioway are a [Chiwere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwere_language)-speaking [Siouan Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux), who were once part of the [Ho-Chunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-Chunk) Confederation that inhabited the area now corresponding to several [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest) states. The Ioway were one of the many [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) nations whose territory comprised the future state of Iowa before the time of European colonization.[^cite_note-18]

Meanwhile, the [state nickname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_nickname), being called the "Hawkeye State" comes from [James Fennimore Cooper's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Fenimore_Cooper) second book in his [Leatherstocking Tales pentalogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherstocking_Tales), *[The Last of the Mohicans: A Narrative of 1757](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_of_the_Mohicans)* (published in 1826). Several influential people in Iowa at the time promoted the nickname, including Judge David Rorer and Newspaper editor James G. Edwards, who renamed his newspaper to *The Hawk-Eye and Iowa Patriot* and eventually to just the *Hawk-Eye*. The name received official recognition by Iowa Territory Governor [Robert Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lucas_\(governor\)) in 1838, when he and several members of his cabinet agreed upon its appropriateness.[^cite_note-19]

### Prehistory

Main articles: [Iowa archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_archaeology) and [American Indians of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indians_of_Iowa)



Excavation of the 3,800-year-old [Edgewater Park Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgewater_Park_Site)

When [Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) first arrived in what is now Iowa more than 13,000 years ago, they were hunters and gatherers living in a [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) glacial landscape. By the time European explorers and traders visited Iowa, Native Americans were largely settled farmers with complex economic, social, and political systems. This transformation happened gradually. During the [Archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_in_North_America) (10,500 to 2,800 years ago), Native Americans adapted to local environments and ecosystems, slowly becoming more sedentary as populations increased.[^cite_note-iaarch-20]

More than 3,000 years ago, during the [Late Archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Archaic_period), Native Americans in Iowa began utilizing domesticated plants. The subsequent [Woodland period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_period) saw an increased reliance on agriculture and social complexity, with increased use of mounds, ceramics, and specialized subsistence. During the Late Prehistoric period (beginning about AD 900) increased use of maize and social changes led to social flourishing and nucleated settlements.[^cite_note-iaarch-20]

The arrival of European trade goods and diseases in the Protohistoric period led to dramatic population shifts and economic and social upheaval, with the arrival of new tribes and early European explorers and traders. There were numerous native American tribes living in Iowa at the time of early European exploration. Tribes which were probably descendants of the prehistoric [Oneota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneota) include the [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux), [Ho-Chunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-Chunk), [Ioway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioway), and [Otoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otoe_\(tribe\)). Tribes which arrived in Iowa in the late prehistoric or protohistoric periods include the [Illiniwek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiniwek), [Meskwaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki), [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_\(tribe\)), and [Sauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_people).[^cite_note-iaarch-20]

### Early colonization and trade, 1673–1808

Main articles: [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France), [Louisiana (New France)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)), [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War), [Treaty of Paris (1763)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)), [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), [Louisiana (New Spain)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_Spain\)), [Treaty of Aranjuez (1801)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Aranjuez_\(1801\)), [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase), [District of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Louisiana), and [Louisiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Territory)



Iowa in 1718 with the modern state area highlighted

The first known European explorers to document Iowa were [Jacques Marquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Marquette) and [Louis Jolliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Jolliet) who traveled the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) in 1673 documenting several Indigenous villages on the Iowa side.[^cite_note-peterson2009-21][^cite_note-22] The area of Iowa was claimed for France and remained a French territory until 1763. The French, before their impending defeat in the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War), transferred ownership to their ally, Spain.[^cite_note-23] Spain practiced very loose control over the Iowa region, granting trading licenses to French and British traders, who established trading posts along the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and [Des Moines Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River).[^cite_note-peterson2009-21]

Iowa was part of a territory known as [*La Louisiane*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)) or [Louisiana](18130.md), and European traders were interested in lead and furs obtained by Indigenous people. The [Sauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_people) and [Meskwaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki) effectively controlled trade on the Mississippi in the late 18th century and early 19th century. Among the early traders on the Mississippi were [Julien Dubuque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Dubuque), [Robert de la Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9-Robert_Cavelier,_Sieur_de_La_Salle), and [Paul Marin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Marin_de_la_Malgue).[^cite_note-peterson2009-21] Along the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) at least five French and English trading houses were built before 1808.[^cite_note-carlson2009-24] In 1800, [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) took control of Louisiana from Spain in a [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Treaty_of_San_Ildefonso).[^cite_note-25]

After the 1803 [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase), Congress divided the new land into two parts—the [Territory of Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Orleans) and the [District of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Louisiana), with present-day Iowa falling in the latter. The [Indiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Territory), created in 1800, exercised jurisdiction over this portion of the District; [William Henry Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison) was its first governor. Much of Iowa was mapped by [Zebulon Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebulon_Pike) in 1805,[^cite_note-26] but it was not until the construction of [Fort Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Madison,_Iowa) in 1808 that the U.S. established tenuous military control over the region.[^cite_note-mckusick2009-27]

### War of 1812 and unstable U.S. control

Main article: [Missouri Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Territory)

[Fort Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Madison,_Iowa) was built to control trade and establish U.S. dominance over the Upper Mississippi, but it was poorly designed and disliked by the Sauk and Meskwaki, many of whom allied with the British, who had not abandoned claims to the territory.[^cite_note-mckusick2009-27][^cite_note-28] Fort Madison was defeated by British-supported Indigenous people in 1813 during the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), and [Fort Shelby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Shelby_\(Wisconsin\)) in [Prairie du Chien, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_du_Chien,_Wisconsin), also fell to the British. [Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_\(Sauk_leader\)) took part in the siege of Fort Madison.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Another small military outpost was established along the Mississippi River in present-day [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Iowa). This poorly situated stockade was similarly attacked by hundreds of Indigenous people in 1813, but was successfully defended and later abandoned until settlers returned to the area in the mid-1830s.[^cite_note-31]

After the war, the U.S. re-established control of the region through the construction of [Fort Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Armstrong,_Illinois), [Fort Snelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Snelling) in [Minnesota](19590.md), and [Fort Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Atkinson_\(Nebraska\)) in [Nebraska](21647.md).[^cite_note-iowaforts-32]

### Indian removal, 1814–1832

See also: [Indian removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_removal)

The United States encouraged settlement of the east side of the Mississippi and removal of Indians to the west.[^cite_note-33] A disputed [1804 treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_St._Louis_\(1804\)) between [Quashquame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quashquame) and [William Henry Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison) (then governor of the [Indiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Territory)) that surrendered much of [Illinois](14849.md) to the U.S. enraged many Sauk and led to the 1832 [Black Hawk War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_War).[^cite_note-Jung-34]

The [Sauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_people) and [Meskwaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki) were forced to sell some of their land in the [Mississippi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Valley) to the U.S. in 1832 under the [Black Hawk Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_Purchase) Treaty[^cite_note-35] and sold their remaining land in Iowa in 1842, most of them moving to a reservation in Kansas.[^cite_note-Jung-34] In 1837, some the [Potawatomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potawatomi) from Illinois were resettled in Iowa, while many Meskwaki later returned to Iowa and settled near [Tama, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama,_Iowa); the [Meskwaki Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki_Settlement,_Iowa) remains to this day.[^cite_note-36] In 1856 the Iowa Legislature passed an unprecedented act allowing the Meskwaki to purchase the land.[^cite_note-37] The federal government, in contrast, used treaties to force the [Ho-Chunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-Chunk) and the [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux) from Iowa by 1848[^cite_note-38] and 1858,[^cite_note-39] respectively. Western Iowa around modern [Council Bluffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Bluffs) was used as an Indian Reservation for members of the [Council of Three Fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Three_Fires).[^cite_note-40]

### U.S. settlement and statehood, 1832–1860

Main articles: [Michigan Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Territory), [Wisconsin Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Territory), [Organic act § List of organic acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_act#List_of_organic_acts), [Iowa Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Territory), [Admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union), and [List of U.S. states by date of admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union)



Iowa [Territorial Seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_Iowa#Iowa_territorial_seal)



[Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Iowa) along the Mississippi, 1848

The first American settlers officially moved to Iowa in June 1833.[^cite_note-Settlers-41] Primarily, they were families from [Ohio](22199.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md), [New York](8210131.md), [Indiana](21883857.md), [Kentucky](16846.md), and [Virginia](32432.md) who settled along the western banks of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River), founding the modern day cities of [Dubuque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubuque,_Iowa) and [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Iowa) near the site of Julien Dubuque's 1785–1810 lead mining operation.[^cite_note-Settlers-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] On July 4, 1838, the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) established the [Territory of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Iowa). President [Martin Van Buren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren) appointed [Robert Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lucas_\(governor\)) governor of the territory, which at the time had 22 counties and a population of 23,242.[^cite_note-44]

Almost immediately after Iowa achieved territorial status, a clamor arose for statehood. On December 28, 1846, Iowa became the 29th state in the Union when President [James K. Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_K._Polk) signed its admission bill into law. Once admitted to the Union, with the state's boundary issues resolved and most of its land purchased from Natives, Iowa set its direction to development and organized campaigns for settlers and investors, boasting the young frontier state's rich farmlands, fine citizens, free and open society, and good government.[^cite_note-45]

Iowa has a long tradition of state and county fairs. The first and second [Iowa State Fairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_Fair) were held in the more developed eastern part of the state at [Fairfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield,_Iowa). The first fair was held October 25–27, 1854, at a cost of around \$323. Thereafter, the fair moved to locations closer to the center of the state and in 1886 found a permanent home in Des Moines. The State Fair has been held annually since then, except for a few exceptions: 1898 due to the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) and the [World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Fair) being held in nearby [Omaha, Nebraska](46159.md); from 1942 to 1945, due to [World War II](32927.md), as the fairgrounds were being used as an army supply depot; and in 2020 due to the [COVID pandemic](63136490.md).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

### Civil War, 1861–1865

Main article: [Iowa in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_in_the_American_Civil_War)

Iowa supported the Union during the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), voting heavily for [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln), though there was an antiwar "[Copperhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copperheads_\(politics\))" movement in the state, caused partially by a drop in crop prices caused by the war.[^cite_note-48] There were no battles in the state, although the [Battle of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Athens_\(1861\)), Missouri, 1861, was fought just across the Des Moines River from [Croton, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croton,_Iowa), and shots from the battle landed in Iowa. Iowa sent large supplies of food to the armies and the eastern cities.[^cite_note-Iowa_Official_Register_page_315-49]

Much of Iowa's support for the Union can be attributed to [Samuel J. Kirkwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_J._Kirkwood), its first wartime governor. Of a total population of 675,000, about 116,000 men were subjected to military duty. Iowa contributed proportionately more soldiers to Civil War military service than did any other state, north or south, sending more than 75,000 volunteers to the armed forces, over one-sixth of whom were killed before the [Confederates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) surrendered at [Appomattox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Appomattox_Courthouse).[^cite_note-Iowa_Official_Register_page_315-49]

Most fought in the great campaigns in the [Mississippi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Valley) and in the [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_South).[^cite_note-50] Iowa troops fought at Wilson's Creek in [Missouri](19571.md), [Pea Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pea_Ridge) in [Arkansas](1930.md), Forts Henry and Donelson, Shiloh, Chattanooga, Chickamauga, Missionary Ridge, and Rossville Gap as well as Vicksburg, Iuka, and Corinth. They served with the Army of the Potomac in [Virginia](32432.md) and fought under Union General [Philip Sheridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sheridan) in the [Shenandoah Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenandoah_Valley). Many died and were buried at Andersonville. They marched on General [Nathaniel Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Banks)' ill-starred expedition to the Red River. Twenty-seven [Iowans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowans) have been awarded the [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor), the highest military decoration awarded by the United States government, which was first awarded in the Civil War.[^cite_note-51]

Iowa had several brigadier generals and four major generals—[Grenville Mellen Dodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenville_Mellen_Dodge), [Samuel R. Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_R._Curtis), [Francis J. Herron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_J._Herron), and [Frederick Steele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Steele)—and saw many of its generals go on to state and national prominence following the war.[^cite_note-Iowa_Official_Register_page_315-49]

### Agricultural expansion, 1865–1930

Following the Civil War, Iowa's population continued to grow dramatically, from 674,913 people in 1860[^cite_note-52] to 1,624,615 in 1880.[^cite_note-53] The American Civil War briefly brought higher profits.[^cite_note-ec-ag-54]

In 1917, the United States entered World War I and farmers as well as all Iowans experienced a wartime economy. For farmers, the change was significant. Since the beginning of the war in 1914, Iowa farmers had experienced economic prosperity, which lasted until the end of the war.[^cite_note-ec-ag-54] In the economic sector, Iowa also has undergone considerable change. Beginning with the first industries developed in the 1830s,[^cite_note-55] which were mainly for processing materials grown in the area,[^cite_note-56] Iowa has experienced a gradual increase in the number of business and manufacturing operations.

### Depression, World War II and manufacturing, 1930–1985

The transition from an agricultural economy to a mixed economy happened slowly. The [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) and World War II accelerated the shift away from [smallholder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallholding) farming to larger farms, and began a trend of urbanization. The period after World War II witnessed a particular increase in manufacturing operations.[^cite_note-57]

In 1975, Governor Robert D. Ray petitioned President Ford to allow Iowa to accept and resettle [Tai Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Dam) refugees fleeing the Indochina War.[^cite_note-58] An exception was required for this resettlement as State Dept policy at the time forbid resettlement of large groups of refugees in concentrated communities; an exception was ultimately granted and 1200 Tai Dam were resettled in Iowa. Since then Iowa has accepted thousands of refugees from Laos, Cambodia, Thailand, Bhutan, and Burma.[^cite_note-59]

The [farm crisis of the 1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_crisis_of_the_1980s) caused a major recession in Iowa, causing poverty not seen since the Depression.[^cite_note-60] The crisis spurred a major, decade-long population decline.[^cite_note-iwin.iwd.state.ia.us-61]

### Reemergence as a mixed economy, 1985–present

After bottoming out in the 1980s, Iowa's economy began to reduce its dependence on agriculture. By the early 21st century, it was characterized by a mix of manufacturing, biotechnology, finance and insurance services, and government services.[^cite_note-iowalifechanging.com-62] The population of Iowa has increased at a slower rate than the U.S. as a whole since at least the [1900 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_census),[^cite_note-Census-2021-63] though Iowa now has a predominantly urban population.[^cite_note-data.iowadatacenter.org-64] The Iowa Economic Development Authority, created in 2011 has replaced the Iowa Department of Economic Development and its annual reports are a source of economic information.[^cite_note-IEDA-65] Its premier business development initiative, the High Quality Jobs (HQJ) tax credit program, has been cited as an economic development tool, driving \$7.4 billion in investment to the state from 2004 to 2012.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Iowa)

### Boundaries

See also: [List of counties in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Iowa)



Topography of Iowa, with counties and major streams

Iowa is bordered by the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) on the east along with the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) and the [Big Sioux River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sioux_River) on the west. The northern boundary is a line along 43 degrees, 30 minutes north latitude.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] The southern border is the [Des Moines River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River) and a not-quite-straight line along approximately 40 degrees 35 minutes north, as decided by the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) in *[Missouri v. Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Missouri_v._State_of_Iowa_\(1849\))* (1849) after a standoff between [Missouri](19571.md) and Iowa known as the [Honey War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey_War).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

Iowa is the only state whose east and west borders are formed almost entirely by rivers.[^cite_note-72] [Carter Lake, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Lake,_Iowa), is the only city in the state located west of the Missouri River.[^cite_note-73]

Iowa has 99 [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)), but 100 [county seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) because [Lee County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_County,_Iowa) has two. The state capital, [Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines), is in [Polk County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polk_County,_Iowa).[^cite_note-74]

### Geology and terrain



[DeSoto Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeSoto_Lake_\(Iowa\)) at [DeSoto National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeSoto_National_Wildlife_Refuge)

Iowa's bedrock geology generally increases in age from west to east. In northwest Iowa, [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) bedrock can be 74 million years old; in eastern Iowa [Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian) bedrock dates to c. 500 million years ago.[^cite_note-75] The oldest radiometrically dated bedrock in the state is the 2.9 billion year old Otter Creek Layered Mafic Complex. [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) rock is exposed only in the northwest of the state.[^cite_note-76]

Iowa can be divided into eight [landforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landform) based on [glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaciation), [soils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil), [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography), and river drainage.[^cite_note-Prior91-77] [Loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) hills lie along the western border of the state, some of which are several hundred feet thick.[^cite_note-78] Northeast Iowa along the [Upper Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mississippi_River) is part of the [Driftless Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area), consisting of steep hills and valleys which appear as mountainous.[^cite_note-79]

Several natural lakes exist, most notably [Spirit Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Lake_\(Iowa\)), [West Okoboji Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Okoboji_Lake), and [East Okoboji Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Okoboji_Lake) in northwest Iowa (*see [Iowa Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Great_Lakes)*). To the east lies [Clear Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_Lake_\(Iowa\)). Man-made lakes include Lake Odessa,[^cite_note-Odessa-80] [Saylorville Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saylorville_Lake), [Lake Red Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Red_Rock_\(Des_Moines_River\)), [Coralville Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coralville_Lake), Lake MacBride, and Rathbun Lake. Before European settlement, 4 to 6 million acres of the state was covered with wetlands; about 95% of these wetlands have since been drained.[^cite_note-81]

### Ecology and environment

Main article: [Environment of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_of_Iowa)



Landforms of Iowa

Iowa's natural vegetation is [tallgrass prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallgrass_prairie) and [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) in upland areas, with dense forest and wetlands in flood plains and protected river valleys, and pothole wetlands in northern prairie areas.[^cite_note-Prior91-77] Most of Iowa is used for agriculture; crops cover 60% of the state, grasslands (mostly pasture and hay with some prairie and wetland) cover 30%, and forests cover 7%; urban areas and water cover another 1% each.[^cite_note-82]

The southern part of Iowa is categorized as the [Central forest-grasslands transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_forest-grasslands_transition) ecoregion.[^cite_note-83] The Northern, drier part of Iowa is categorized as part of the [Central tall grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_tall_grasslands).[^cite_note-84]

There is a dearth of natural areas in Iowa; less than 1% of the [tallgrass prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallgrass_prairie) that once covered most of Iowa remains intact; only about 5% of the state's prairie pothole wetlands remain, and most of the original forest has been lost.[^cite_note-85] As of 2005 Iowa ranked 49th of U.S. states in public land holdings.[^cite_note-86] Threatened or endangered animals in Iowa include the [interior least tern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_tern), [piping plover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piping_plover), [Indiana bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_bat), [pallid sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallid_sturgeon), the [Iowa Pleistocene land snail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discus_macclintocki), [Higgins' eye pearly mussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgins'_eye_pearly_mussel), and the [Topeka shiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka_shiner).[^cite_note-87] Endangered or threatened plants include [western prairie fringed orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_prairie_fringed_orchid), [eastern prairie fringed orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_prairie_fringed_orchid), [Mead's milkweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mead's_milkweed), [prairie bush clover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_bush_clover), and [northern wild monkshood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_wild_monkshood).[^cite_note-88]

The explosion in the number of [high-density livestock facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_animal_feeding_operation) in Iowa has led to increased rural water contamination and a decline in air quality.[^cite_note-ISU_1-89]

Other factors negatively affecting Iowa's environment include the extensive use of older [coal-fired power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal-fired_power_plants),[^cite_note-gazette_coal-90] fertilizer and pesticide runoff from crop production,[^cite_note-IPT_runoff-91] and diminishment of the [Jordan Aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Aquifer).[^cite_note-92]

The [2020–2023 North American drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932023_North_American_drought) has affected Iowa particularly: As of January 2024, Iowa was in its 187th consecutive week of at least moderate [drought](37335.md), the longest stretch since the 1950s. 96% of areas are affected by drought.[^cite_note-93]

### Climate

Main articles: [Climate of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Iowa) and [Climate change in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Iowa)



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Iowa, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](42778673.md)

Iowa has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) throughout the state ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfa*) with extremes of both heat and cold. The average annual temperature at Des Moines is 50 °F (10 °C); for some locations in the north, such as Mason City, the figure is about 45 °F (7 °C), while [Keokuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keokuk,_Iowa), on the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River), averages 52 °F (11 °C).[^cite_note-94] Snowfall is common, with [Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines) getting about 26 days of snowfall a year, and other places, such as [Shenandoah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenandoah,_Iowa), getting about 11 days of snowfall in a year.[^cite_note-95]

Spring ushers in the beginning of the [severe weather](11593538.md) season. As of 2008, Iowa averaged about 50 days of [thunderstorm](70807.md) activity per year.[^cite_note-Thunderstorm_Hazard-96] As of 2015, the 30-year annual average of tornadoes in Iowa was 47.[^cite_note-Annual_Average_Number_of_Tornadoes-97] In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late-May_2008_tornado_outbreak_sequence), twelve people were killed by tornadoes in Iowa, making it the deadliest year since [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1968_tornado_outbreak) and also the second most tornadoes in a year with 105, matching the total from 2001.[^cite_note-98]

Iowa summers are known for heat and humidity, with daytime temperatures sometimes near 90 °F (32 °C) and occasionally exceeding 100 °F (38 °C). Average winters in the state have been known to drop well below freezing, even dropping below −18 °F (−28 °C). As of 2018, Iowa's all-time hottest temperature of 118 °F (48 °C) was recorded at Keokuk on July 20, 1934, during a nationwide heat wave;[^cite_note-99] as of 2014, the all-time lowest temperature of −47 °F (−44 °C) was recorded in [Washta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washta) on January 12, 1912.[^cite_note-100]

#### Precipitation



Iowa annual rainfall, in inches; as of 2009

Iowa has had a relatively smooth gradient of varying [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) across the state; from 1961 to 1990, areas in the southeast of the state received an average of over 38 inches (97 cm) of rain annually, and the northwest of the state receiving less than 28 inches (71 cm).[^cite_note-108] The pattern of precipitation across Iowa is seasonal with more rain falling in the summer months. Virtually statewide, the driest month is January or February, while the wettest month is June due to frequent showers and thunderstorms, some of which produce hail, damaging winds or tornadoes. In Des Moines, roughly in the center of the state, over two-thirds of the 34.72 inches (88.2 cm) of rain falls from April through September, and about half the average annual precipitation falls from May through August peaking in June.[^cite_note-109]

### Settlements

See also: [List of cities in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Iowa)

Iowa's population is more urban than rural, with 61 percent living in urban areas in 2000, a trend that began in the early 20th century.[^cite_note-data.iowadatacenter.org-64] Urban counties in Iowa grew 8.5% from 2000 to 2008, while rural counties declined by 4.2%.[^cite_note-110] The shift from rural to urban has caused population increases in more urbanized counties such as [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_County,_Iowa), [Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County,_Iowa), [Linn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_County,_Iowa), [Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polk_County,_Iowa), and [Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_County,_Iowa), at the expense of more rural counties.[^cite_note-Census-2010-111]

Iowa, in common with other Midwestern states (especially [Kansas](16716.md), [Nebraska](21647.md), [North Dakota](21651.md), and [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota)), is feeling the brunt of [rural flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_flight), although the population of Iowa has been increasing since approximately 1990. Some smaller communities, such as [Denison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denison,_Iowa) and [Storm Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Lake,_Iowa), have mitigated this population loss through gains in immigrant laborers.[^cite_note-112]

Another demographic problem for Iowa is [brain drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_drain), in which educated young adults leave the state in search of better prospects in higher education or employment. During the 1990s, Iowa had the second highest exodus rate for single, educated young adults, second only to North Dakota.[^cite_note-113]

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) determined the population of Iowa was 3,190,369 on April 1, 2020, a 4.73% increase since the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Census).[^cite_note-2020_Census-117][^cite_note-118]

Of the residents of Iowa, 70.8% were born in Iowa, 23.6% were born in a different U.S. state, 0.6% were born in Puerto Rico, U.S. Island areas, or born abroad to American parent(s), and 5% were foreign born.[^cite_note-119]

[Immigration](15051.md) from outside the United States resulted in a net increase of 29,386 people, while migration within the country produced a net loss of 41,140 people. 6.5% of Iowa's population were reported as under the age of five, 22.6% under 18, and 14.7% were 65 or older. Males made up approximately 49.6% of the population.[^cite_note-120] The population density of the state is 52.7 people per square mile.[^cite_note-121] As of the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Census), the [center of population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population) of Iowa is in [Marshall County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_County,_Iowa), near [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne,_Iowa).[^cite_note-122] The top countries of origin for Iowa's immigrants in 2018 were [Mexico](3966054.md), [India](14533.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), [China](5405.md) and [Thailand](30128.md).[^cite_note-123]

According to a version of *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica)* published in 1999, Germans are the largest ethnic group in Iowa. Other major ethnic groups in Iowa include Irish and English. There are also Dutch communities in the state. The Dutch can be found in [Pella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pella,_Iowa), in the centre of the state, and in Orange City, in the northwest. There is a Norwegian community in Decorah in northeast Iowa; and there are Czech and Slovak communities in both Cedar Rapids and Iowa City. Smaller numbers of Greeks and Italians are scattered in Iowa's metropolitan areas. The majority of Hispanics in Iowa are of [Mexican origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans).

[African Americans](2154.md), who constitute around 4% of Iowa's population, did not live in the state in any appreciable numbers until the early 20th century. Many blacks worked in the coal-mining industry of southern Iowa. Other blacks migrated to Waterloo, Davenport, and Des Moines, where the black population remained substantial in the early 21st century.[^cite_note-124] The African-American population in Des Moines experienced a significant increase with the establishment of the Colored Officers Training Camp at Fort Des Moines in 1917. Following the conclusion of World War I in 1918, numerous African-American families made the decision to remain in Des Moines. This marked the inception of a thriving community that eventually became a residence for numerous African-American leaders.[^cite_note-125] In the [2020 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Census), 131,972 Iowa residents were identified as African American (of the total 3,190,369).[^cite_note-126] In 1 of the state's 99 counties, African Americans make up more than 10% of the population: [Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_County,_Iowa) (10.4%). African Americans in the seven counties of [Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polk_County,_Iowa) (35,623), [Linn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_County,_Iowa) (16,449), [Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_County,_Iowa) (14,117), Black Hawk (13,676), [Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County,_Iowa) (12,847), [Woodbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodbury_County,_Iowa) (5,197), and [Dubuque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubuque_County,_Iowa) (4,126) make up more than 77% of all African Americans in the state.[^cite_note-127]

There is one [federally recognized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federally_recognized_tribes_by_state) tribe in Iowa, the [Sac and Fox Tribe of the Mississippi in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sac_and_Fox_Tribe_of_the_Mississippi_in_Iowa), and in 2020, 14,486 identified as being Native American alone, and 41,472 did in combination with one or more other races.[^cite_note-128]

As of the 2010 census, the population of Iowa was 3,046,355. The gender makeup of the state was 49.5% male and 50.5% female. 23.9% of the population were under the age of 18; 61.2% were between the ages of 18 and 64; and 14.9% were 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2010-129]

### Language

See also: [Chiwere language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwere_language)

English is the most common language in Iowa, being the sole language spoken by 91.1% of the population. Less common languages include sign language and indigenous languages. About 2.5% of the general population use sign language as of 2017, while indigenous languages are spoken by about 0.5% of the population.[^cite_note-141] After English, Spanish is the second-most-common language, with 120,000 people in Iowa of Hispanic or Latino origin and 47,000 people born in Latin America.[^cite_note-142] The third-most-common language is German, spoken by 17,000 people in Iowa; two notable German dialects used in Iowa include [Amana German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amana_German) spoken around the [Amana Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amana_Colonies), and [Pennsylvania German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_German_language), spoken among the [Amish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish) in Iowa. The [Babel Proclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babel_Proclamation) of 1918 banned the speaking of German in public. Around [Pella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pella,_Iowa), residents of Dutch descent once spoke the [Pella Dutch dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pella_Dutch_dialect).

## Culture

### Attractions

Iowa hosts [RAGBRAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAGBRAI), the Register's Annual Great Bicycle Ride Across Iowa, which is a bike across the state river-to-river that attracts thousands of bicyclists and support personnel. It has crossed the state on various routes each year since 1973. Iowa is home to more than 70 wineries,[^cite_note-143] and hosts five regional wine tasting trails.[^cite_note-144] Many Iowa communities hold farmers' markets during warmer months; these are typically weekly events, but larger cities can host multiple markets.[^cite_note-145]

The Christina Reiman Butterfly Wing at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University), Ames

Des Moines is the largest city and metropolitan area[^cite_note-MSA-146] in Iowa and the state's political and economic center. It is home to the [Iowa State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_Capitol), the [State Historical Society of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Historical_Society_of_Iowa) Museum, [Drake University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_University), [Des Moines Art Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Art_Center), [Greater Des Moines Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Des_Moines_Botanical_Garden), [Principal Riverwalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Riverwalk), the [Iowa State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_Fair), [Terrace Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_Hill), and the [World Food Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Food_Prize). Other communities with vibrant historic downtown areas include [Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Iowa), [Indianola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianola,_Iowa), [Pella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pella,_Iowa), [Knoxville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville,_Iowa), [Marshalltown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshalltown,_Iowa), [Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry,_Iowa), and [Story City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Story_City,_Iowa).

#### Western Iowa



Loess Hills east of [Mondamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondamin,_Iowa)

[Sioux City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_City,_Iowa) is the largest city in western Iowa and is found on the convergence of the [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), [Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_River), and [Big Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sioux_River) Rivers. The [Sioux City Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_City_metropolitan_area) encompasses areas in three states: Iowa, [Nebraska](21647.md), and [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota). Sioux City boasts a revitalized downtown and includes attractions such as the [Hard Rock Hotel and Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Rock_Hotel_&_Casino_Sioux_City), [Sergeant Floyd Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_Floyd_Monument), [Sergeant Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_Floyd) River Museum, the [Tyson Events Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyson_Events_Center), [Southern Hills Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hills_Mall), the [Orpheum Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheum_Theater_\(Sioux_City\)), and more. The historic downtown area is also filled with multiple restaurants, bars, and other entertainment venues. Sioux City is home to two higher education institutions, [Morningside College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningside_University) and [Briar Cliff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briar_Cliff_University). [Le Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mars,_Iowa) is in the northeastern part of the [Sioux City Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_City_metropolitan_area) and is the self-proclaimed "Ice Cream Capital of the World". Le Mars is home to [Wells Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Enterprises), one of the largest ice cream manufacturers in the world. Attractions in Le Mars include the Wells Visitor Center and Ice Cream Parlor, Archie's Wayside (steak house), Bob's Drive Inn, [Tonsfeldt Round Barn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonsfeldt_Round_Barn), Plymouth County Fairgrounds, Plymouth County Museum, and [Plymouth County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_County_Courthouse_\(Iowa\)). Le Mars hosts multiple ice cream-themed community events each year.

The [Iowa Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Great_Lakes) is made up of multiple small towns, such as [Spirit Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Lake,_Iowa), [Arnolds Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnolds_Park,_Iowa), [Milford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milford,_Iowa), and [Okoboji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okoboji). ## Economy

See also: [Iowa locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_locations_by_per_capita_income)



Iowa [gross state products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) by industry, 2009

[^cite_note-181]

In 2025, Iowa's gross domestic product was \$277.1 billion and the state's per capita personal income was \$68,326.[^cite_note-182] On July 2, 2009, [Standard & Poor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_&_Poor's) rated Iowa's credit as AAA (the highest of its credit ratings, held by only 11 U.S. state governments).[^cite_note-183]

In 2016, the total employment of the state's population was 1,354,487, and the total number of employer establishments was 81,563.[^cite_note-184] In 2025, [small businesses](276495.md) made up 99.3% of all businesses in the state, employing 46.0% of Iowa's work force.[^cite_note-185]

CNBC's list of "Top States for Business in 2010" has recognized Iowa as the sixth best state in the nation. Scored in 10 individual categories, Iowa was ranked first when it came to the "Cost of Doing Business"; this includes all taxes, utility costs, and other costs associated with doing business. Iowa was also ranked 10th in "Economy", 12th in "Business Friendliness", 16th in "Education", 17th in both "Cost of Living" and "Quality of Life", 20th in "Workforce", 29th in "Technology and Innovation", 32nd in "Transportation" and the lowest ranking was 36th in "Access to Capital".[^cite_note-186]

While Iowa is often viewed as a farming state, agriculture is a relatively small portion of the state's diversified economy, with manufacturing, biotechnology, finance and insurance services, and government services contributing substantially to Iowa's economy.[^cite_note-iowalifechanging.com-62] This economic diversity has helped Iowa weather the [late 2000s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late-2000s_recession) better than most states, with unemployment substantially lower than the rest of the nation.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188]

As of May 2025, the state's unemployment rate was 3.6%.[^cite_note-189]

### Manufacturing

[Manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) is the largest sector of Iowa's economy, with \$20.8 billion (21%) of Iowa's 2003 gross state product. Major manufacturing sectors include [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), heavy machinery, and [agricultural chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrochemical). Sixteen percent of Iowa's workforce is dedicated to manufacturing.[^cite_note-iowalifechanging.com-62] Food processing is the largest component of manufacturing. Besides processed food, industrial outputs include machinery, electric equipment, chemical products, publishing, and primary metals. Companies with direct or indirect processing facilities in Iowa include [ConAgra Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConAgra_Foods), [Wells Blue Bunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Dairy), [Barilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barilla_Group), [Heinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._J._Heinz_Company), Tone's Spices, [General Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Mills), and [Quaker Oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker_Oats). Meatpacker [Tyson Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyson_Foods) has 11 locations, second only to its headquarter state Arkansas.[^cite_note-190]

Major non-food manufacturing firms with production facilities in Iowa include [3M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M),[^cite_note-191] [Arconic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arconic),[^cite_note-192] [Amana Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amana_Corporation),[^cite_note-193] [Emerson Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson_Process_Management),[^cite_note-194] [The HON Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_HON_Company),[^cite_note-195] [SSAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSAB),[^cite_note-196] [John Deere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deere),[^cite_note-197] [Lennox Manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lennox_International),[^cite_note-198] [Pella Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pella_\(company\)),[^cite_note-199] [Procter & Gamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procter_&_Gamble),[^cite_note-200] [Vermeer Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermeer_Company),[^cite_note-201] and [Winnebago Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnebago_Industries).[^cite_note-202]

### Agriculture



Harvesting [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) in [Jones County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jones_County,_Iowa)



Farm in rural Northwest Iowa



Central Iowa cornfield and dairy in June

Industrial-scale, commodity agriculture predominates in much of the state. Iowa's main conventional agricultural commodities are [hogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig), with about 22.6 million hogs in 8,000 facilities large enough to require manure management plans in March 2018, outnumbering Iowans by more than 7 to 1,[^cite_note-Jordan-203] [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean), [oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oat), cattle, eggs, and dairy products. Iowa is the nation's largest producer of [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol) and [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and some years is the largest grower of soybeans. In 2008, the 92,600 farms in Iowa produced 19% of the nation's corn, 17% of the soybeans, 30% of the hogs, and 14% of the eggs.[^cite_note-204] As of 2009 major Iowa agricultural product processors included [Archer Daniels Midland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archer_Daniels_Midland), [Cargill, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill,_Inc.), Diamond V Mills, and [Quaker Oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker_Oats).[^cite_note-gazette_grain-205]

During the 21st century Iowa has seen growth in the [organic farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_farming) sector. Iowa ranks fifth in the nation in total number of organic farms. In 2016, there were about 732 organic farms in the state, an increase of about 5% from the previous year, and 103,136 organic acres, an increase of 9,429 from the previous year.[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207] Iowa has also seen an increase in demand for local, sustainably-grown food. Northeast Iowa, part of the [Driftless Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area), has led the state in development of its regional [food system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_system) and grows and consumes more local food than any other region in Iowa.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209]

Iowa's Driftless Region is also home to the nationally recognized [Seed Savers Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_Savers_Exchange), a non-profit [seed bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_bank) housed at an 890-acre heritage farm near [Decorah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decorah,_Iowa), in the northeast corner of the state.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] The largest nongovernmental seed bank of its kind in the United States, Seed Savers Exchange safeguards more than 20,000 varieties of rare, heirloom seeds.[^cite_note-212]



Mural in [Mt. Ayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ayr,_Iowa) Post Office, "The Corn Parade" by Orr C. Fischer, which was commissioned as part of the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal)

[^cite_note-CornParade-213]

As of 2007, the direct production and sale of conventional agricultural commodities contributed only about 3.5% of Iowa's gross state product.[^cite_note-214] In 2002 the impact of the indirect role of agriculture in Iowa's economy, including agriculture-affiliated business, was calculated at 16.4% in terms of value added and 24.3% in terms of total output. This was lower than the economic impact of non-farm manufacturing, which accounted for 22.4% of total value added and 26.5% of total output.[^cite_note-215]

### Health insurance

As of 2014, there were 16 organizations offering [health insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance) products in Iowa, per the State of Iowa Insurance Division.[^cite_note-iid-216] Iowa was fourth out of ten states with the biggest drop in competition levels of health insurance between 2010 and 2011, per the 2013 annual report on the level of competition in the health insurance industry by the [American Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Medical_Association)[^cite_note-comp2013-217] using 2011 data from HealthLeaders-Interstudy, the most comprehensive source of data on enrollment in [health maintenance organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_maintenance_organization) (HMO), [preferred provider organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preferred_provider_organization) (PPO), [point-of-service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-of-service) (POS) and [consumer-driven health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer-driven_health_care) plans.[^cite_note-218] According to the AMA annual report from 2007 [Wellmark Blue Cross Blue Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellmark_Blue_Cross_Blue_Shield) had provided 71% of the state's health insurance.[^cite_note-219]

The Iowa Insurance Division "Annual report to the Iowa Governor and the Iowa Legislature" from November 2014 looked at the 95% of health insurers by premium, which are 10 companies. It found Wellmark Inc. to dominate the three health insurance markets it examined (individual, small group and large group) at 52–67%.[^cite_note-iidreport-220]<sup>: 2 </sup> Wellmark HealthPlan of Iowa and Wellmark Inc had the highest risk-based capital percentages of all 10 providers at 1158% and 1132%, respectively.[^cite_note-iidreport-220]<sup>: 31 </sup> Rising RBC is an indication of profits.[^cite_note-iidreport-220]<sup>: 31 </sup>

### Taxation

Tax is collected by the [Iowa Department of Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Department_of_Revenue).[^cite_note-237]

Iowa imposes taxes on net state income of individuals, estates, and trusts. There are nine [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) brackets, ranging from 0.36% to 8.98%, as well as four corporate income tax brackets ranging from 6% to 12%, giving Iowa the country's highest marginal corporate tax rate.[^cite_note-238] The state [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) rate is 6%, with non-prepared food having no tax.[^cite_note-239] Iowa has one local option sales tax that may be imposed by counties after an election.[^cite_note-240] Property tax is levied on the taxable value of real property. Iowa has more than 2,000 taxing authorities. Most property is taxed by more than one taxing authority. The tax rate differs in each locality and is a composite of county, city or rural township, [school district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) and special levies. Iowa allows its residents to deduct their [federal income taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_income_tax) from their state income taxes.[^cite_note-241]

## Education

### Primary and secondary schools

See also: [List of school districts in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Iowa) and [List of high schools in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Iowa)

Iowa was one of the leading states in the [high school movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_movement), and continues to be among the top educational performers today.[^cite_note-242]

The four-year graduation rate for high schoolers was 91.3% in 2017.[^cite_note-Grad_rate_2016-243] Iowa's schools are credited with the highest graduation rate in the nation as of 2019.[^cite_note-244] Iowa has 333 school districts,[^cite_note-Grad_rate_2016-243] 1,329 school buildings and has the 14th lowest student-to-teacher ratio of 14.2.[^cite_note-NEA-245] Teacher pay is ranked 22nd, with the average salary being \$55,647.[^cite_note-NEA-245]

As of 2015 transportation spending is a significant part of the budgets of rural school districts as many are geographically large and must transport students across vast distances. This reduces the amount of money spent on other aspects of the districts.[^cite_note-246]

The state's oldest functioning school building is located in [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Iowa) in the historic [Jackson County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_County_Courthouse_\(Bellevue,_Iowa\)) which has been in continuous use as a school since 1848.[^cite_note-247]

## Health

Main article: [Health in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Iowa)

The life expectancy of Iowa is 77.9 years in 2022[^cite_note-Centers-for-Disease-Control-and-Prevention-2024-251] The state's rural population are more susceptible to diseases related to [food insecurity](216361.md), due to a lack of healthy food sources.[^cite_note-Smith-2024-252] The leading cause of death in Iowa is heart disease, followed by cancer and fatalities relating to unintentional injuries.[^cite_note-Centers-for-Disease-Control-and-Prevention-2024-251]

Iowa ranks eleventh among states in terms of proportion of obesity, with 37% of the state being affected by it.[^cite_note-Lensing-2024-253][^cite_note-254] Around 9% of Iowans suffer from [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes), costing the state \$2 billion.[^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256]

### Political parties

Main article: [Political party strength in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_strength_in_Iowa)



[Samuel J. Kirkwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_J._Kirkwood), founder of the Iowa Republican Party, abolitionist, and Iowa's Civil War Governor

In Iowa, the term "political party" refers to political organizations which have received two percent or more of the votes cast for president or governor in the "last preceding general election". Iowa recognizes three political parties—the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Iowa), the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Democratic_Party), and the [Libertarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-262] The Libertarian Party obtained official political party status in 2017 as a result of presidential candidate [Gary Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Johnson) receiving 3.8% of the Iowa vote in the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-263] [Third parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_party_\(politics\)), officially termed "nonparty political organizations", can appear on the ballot as well. Four of these have had candidates on the ballot in Iowa since 2004 for various positions: the [Constitution Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Party_\(United_States\)), the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(United_States\)), the [Pirate Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pirate_Party), and the [Socialist Workers Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Workers_Party_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265]

### Voter trends

See also: [United States presidential elections in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_elections_in_Iowa)

As a result of the 2010 elections, each party controlled one house of the [Iowa General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_General_Assembly): the House had a Republican majority, while the Senate had a Democratic majority. As a result of the 2016 elections, Republicans gained control of the Senate. Incumbent Democratic governor [Chet Culver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Culver) was defeated in 2010 by Republican [Terry Branstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Branstad), who had served as governor from 1983 to 1999. On December 14, 2015, Branstad became the longest serving governor in U.S. history, serving (at that time) 20 years, 11 months, and 3 days; eclipsing [George Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_\(vice_president\)), who served 21 years until 1804.[^cite_note-266] Lieutenant Governor Kim Reynolds succeeded him on May 24, 2017, following Branstad's appointment as U.S. Ambassador to China.

In previous eras, Democratic and Republican parties had a balance in state politics and federal representation. By 2024, the two became dominated by the Republican Party. Factors include younger people leaving for more urbanized, more Democratic-leaning states, as well as homogenization of rural voters in the Midwest and in other regions.[^cite_note-267]

### Presidential caucus

Main article: [Iowa caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_caucuses)

The [Iowa caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_caucus), gatherings of voters to select delegates to the state conventions, is the first presidential caucus in the country. The caucuses, held in January or February of the election year, involve people gathering in homes or public places and choosing their candidates, rather than casting secret ballots as is done in a [presidential primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_primary) election.[^cite_note-269] Along with the [New Hampshire primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_primary) the following week, Iowa's caucuses have become the starting points for choosing the two major-party candidates for president.[^cite_note-270] The national and international media give Iowa and New Hampshire extensive attention, which gives Iowa voters leverage.[^cite_note-271] In 2007 presidential campaign spending was the seventh highest in the country.[^cite_note-272] In a 2020 study, Iowa was ranked as the 24th easiest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-273]

### Civil rights

#### Racial equality



The [Union Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Block_\(Mount_Pleasant,_Iowa\)) building in [Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pleasant,_Iowa), scene of early civil rights and women's rights activities

In the 19th century Iowa was among the earliest states to enact prohibitions against race discrimination, especially in education, but was slow to achieve full integration in the 20th century. In the first decision of the [Iowa Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Supreme_Court)—*In Re the Matter of Ralph*,[^cite_note-274] decided July 1839—the Court rejected slavery in a decision that found a slave named Ralph became free when he stepped on Iowa soil, 26 years before the end of the Civil War.[^cite_note-civil_rights-275] The state did away with racial barriers to marriage in 1851, more than 100 years before the U.S. Supreme Court would ban [miscegenation statutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-miscegenation_laws) nationwide.[^cite_note-ChicagoTrib-276]

The Iowa Supreme Court decided *Clark v. The board of directors*[^cite_note-277] in 1868, ruling that racially segregated "separate but equal" schools had no place in Iowa, 85 years before *[Brown v. Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Board_of_Education)*.[^cite_note-civil_rights-275] By 1875, a number of additional court rulings effectively ended segregation in Iowa schools.[^cite_note-278] Social and housing discrimination continued against Blacks at state universities until the 1950s.[^cite_note-279] The Court heard *Coger v. The North Western Union Packet Co.*[^cite_note-280] in 1873, ruling against racial discrimination in public accommodations 91 years before the U.S. Supreme Court reached the same decision.[^cite_note-civil_rights-275]

In 1884, the Iowa Civil Rights Act apparently outlawed discrimination by businesses, reading: "All persons within this state shall be entitled to the full and equal enjoyment of the accommodations, advantages, facilities, and privileges of inns, restaurants, chophouses, eating houses, lunch counters, and all other places where refreshments are served, public conveyances, barber shops, bathhouses, theaters, and all other places of amusement." However, the courts chose to narrowly apply this act, allowing de facto discrimination to continue.[^cite_note-281] Racial discrimination at public businesses was not deemed illegal until 1949, when the court ruled in *[State of Iowa v. Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Iowa_v._Katz)* that businesses had to serve customers regardless of race; the case began when [Edna Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edna_Griffin) was denied service at a Des Moines drugstore.[^cite_note-282] Full racial civil rights were codified under the Iowa Civil Rights Act of 1965.[^cite_note-283]

#### Women's rights

As with racial equality, Iowa was a vanguard in women's rights in the mid-19th century, but did not allow women the right to vote until the [Nineteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) was ratified in 1920, Iowa legislature being one of the ratifying votes.[^cite_note-nps-284] In 1847, the [University of Iowa](241119.md) became the first public university in the U.S. to admit men and women on an equal basis.[^cite_note-285] In 1869, Iowa became the first state in the union to admit women to the practice of law, with the Court ruling women may not be denied the right to practice law in Iowa and admitting [Arabella A. Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabella_A._Mansfield) to the practice of law.[^cite_note-civil_rights-275] Several attempts to grant full voting rights to Iowa women were defeated between 1870 and 1919. In 1894 women were given "partial suffrage", which allowed them to vote on issues, but not for candidates. It was not until the ratification of the Nineteenth Amendment that women had full suffrage in Iowa.[^cite_note-286] Although Iowa supported the Federal [Equal Rights Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Rights_Amendment), in 1980 and 1992 Iowa voters rejected an Equal Rights Amendment to the state constitution.[^cite_note-287] Iowa added the word "women" to the Iowa Constitution in 1998. After the amendment, it reads: "All men and women are, by nature, free and equal, and have certain inalienable rights—among which are those of enjoying and defending life and liberty, acquiring, possessing and protecting property, and pursuing and obtaining safety and happiness."[^cite_note-288]

In May 2018 Iowa signed into law one of the country's most restrictive abortion bans: the requirement that a doctor cannot perform an abortion if they can detect a fetal heartbeat, which in many cases would restrict abortions pregnancies less than six weeks old.[^cite_note-289] It was struck down in January 2019, when an Iowa state judge ruled that the "fetal heartbeat" law was unconstitutional.[^cite_note-290]

The state's law criminalizing same-sex sexual activity was repealed in June 1976, 27 years before *[Lawrence v. Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_v._Texas)*. In 2007, the Iowa Legislature added "sexual orientation" and "gender identity" to the protected classes listed in the Iowa Civil Rights Act.[^cite_note-291] The Iowa Legislature later struck "gender identity" from these protections in 2025, making Iowa the first U.S. state to remove a [protected group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_group) from a state [anti-discrimination law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-discrimination_law).[^cite_note-292]

On April 3, 2009, the Iowa Supreme Court decided *[Varnum v. Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varnum_v._Brien)*,[^cite_note-293] holding in a [unanimous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unanimous) decision,[^cite_note-294] the state's law forbidding [same-sex marriage](92656.md) was [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconstitutional). This made Iowa the third state in the U.S. and first in the Midwest to permit same-sex marriage.[^cite_note-295][^cite_note-296]

On March 22, 2023, Governor [Kim Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Reynolds) signed into law Iowa Senate File 538 (S.F. 538) with immediate effect. The law restricted access to [gender-affirming care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_health_care) for legal minors, including [hormone replacement therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-affirming_hormone_therapy) and [puberty blockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puberty_blockers).[^cite_note-297]
