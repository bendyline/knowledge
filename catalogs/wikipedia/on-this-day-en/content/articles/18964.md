**Madagascar**, officially the **Republic of Madagascar**,[^cite_note-19] is an [island country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) that includes the [island of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Madagascar) and numerous smaller peripheral islands. Lying off the southeastern coast of [Africa](5334607.md), it is the world's [fourth-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area), the [second-largest island country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_island_countries), and the [46th-largest country overall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area). Its [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Madagascar) is [Antananarivo](56597.md).&#x20;

Following the prehistoric breakup of the [supercontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercontinent) [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana), Madagascar split from Africa during the Early [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) period, around 180 million years ago, and from the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) approximately 90 million years ago. This isolation allowed native plants and animals to evolve in relative seclusion. As a result, Madagascar is a [biodiversity hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot) and one of the world's 17 [megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries), with over 90% of [its wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Madagascar) being [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism). The island has a subtropical to tropical maritime climate.&#x20;

Madagascar was first permanently settled during or before the mid-first millennium CE (roughly 500 to 700) by [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples), presumably arriving on [outrigger canoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outrigger_boat) from present-day [Indonesia](14579.md). These were joined around the ninth century by [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples) groups crossing the [Mozambique Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_Channel) from [East Africa](260987.md). Other groups continued to settle on Madagascar over time, each one making lasting contributions to Malagasy cultural life. Consequently, there are 18 or more classified [peoples of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_peoples), the most numerous being the [Merina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merina_people) of the central highlands.&#x20;

Until the late 18th century, the island of Madagascar was ruled by a fragmented assortment of shifting sociopolitical alliances. Beginning in the early 19th century, most of it was united and ruled as the [Kingdom of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merina_Kingdom) by [a series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Imerina_monarchs) of Merina [nobles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriana). The monarchy was ended in 1897 by France's [annexation of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Madagascar), from which the country gained independence in 1960. It has since undergone four major constitutional periods, termed republics, and has been governed as a constitutional democracy since 1992. Following [a political crisis and military coup in 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Malagasy_political_crisis), Madagascar underwent [a protracted transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Transitional_Authority) towards its fourth and current republic, with constitutional governance being restored in January 2014. In 2025, [a series of mass protests](81184334.md) resulted in [a military coup](81321149.md) and the installation of [Michael Randrianirina](81340595.md) as president of [a military junta](81351542.md).&#x20;

Madagascar is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN), the [African Union](10634933.md) (AU), the [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community) (SADC), and the *[Organisation Internationale de la Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie)*. [Malagasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_language) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) are both [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of the state. [Christianity](5211.md) is the country's predominant religion, with a significant minority still practicing [traditional faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_mythology).&#x20;

Madagascar is classified as a [least-developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries) by the UN. [Ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) and [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Madagascar), paired with greater investments in [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Madagascar), [health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Madagascar), and [private enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector), are key elements of its development strategy. Despite substantial economic growth since the early 2000s, income disparities have widened, and quality of life remains low for the majority of the population. As of 2021, 68.4% of the population was considered to be [multidimensionally poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_Poverty_Index).&#x20;

## Etymology

In the [Malagasy language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_language), the island of Madagascar is called *Madagasikara* (Malagasy pronunciation: [\[madaɡasʲˈkʲarə̥\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Malagasy)) and its people are referred to as *[Malagasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_people)*.[^cite_note-NatGeo1-20][^cite_note-MalagasyOrNot-21] The origin of the name is uncertain,[^cite_note-22] and is likely foreign, having been propagated in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) by Europeans.[^cite_note-Cousins2-23] If this is the case, it is unknown when the name was adopted by the inhabitants of the island. No single Malagasy-language name predating *Madagasikara* appears to have been used by the local population to refer to it, although some communities had their name for part or all of the lands they inhabited.[^cite_note-Room_2006,_p._2302-24]&#x20;

One hypothesis relates *Madagascar* to the word *[Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\))*, referring to the [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) origin of the [Malagasy people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_people) in modern-day [Indonesia](14579.md). In a map by [Muhammad al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi) dating from the year 1154, the island is named *Gesira Malai*, or "Malay island" in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic). The inversion of this name to *Malai Gesira*, as it was known by the [Greeks](42056.md), is thought to be the precursor of the island's modern name.[^cite_note-Clockers-2014-25] The name "Malay island" was later rendered in Latin as *Malichu*, an abbreviated form of *Malai Insula*, in the medieval [Hereford Mappa Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereford_Mappa_Mundi) as the name of Madagascar.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Clockers-2014-25]&#x20;

Another hypothesis is that *Madagascar* is a corrupted [transliteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) of [Mogadishu](60154.md), the capital of [Somalia](27358.md) and an important medieval [Indian Ocean](14580.md) port. This would have resulted from the 13th-century [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) explorer [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo)'s confusion of the two locations in his memoirs, in which he mentions the land of *Madageiscar* to the south of [Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra). This name would then have been popularized on [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) maps by Europeans.[^cite_note-Cousins2-23][^cite_note-Room_2006,_p._2302-24] One of the first documents written that might explain why Marco Polo called it *Madagascar* is in a 1609 book on Madagascar by [Jerome Megizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jerome_Megizer\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-London_Missionary_Society_Press-27][^cite_note-Sibree_1896-28] Megizer describes an event in which the kings of the sultanates of [Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Mogadishu) and [Adal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate) traveled to Madagascar with a fleet of around 25,000 men in order to invade the wealthy islands of [Taprobane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taprobana) and [Sumatra](28679.md). However, a tempest threw them off course and they landed on the coasts of Madagascar, conquering the island and signing a treaty with its inhabitants. They remained for eight months and erected eight pillars at different points of the island on which they engraved *Magadoxo*, a name which later, by corruption, became Madagascar.[^cite_note-London_Missionary_Society_Press-27][^cite_note-Sibree_1896-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-Warhafftige,_gründliche_und_aussfüh-30] [Jan Huyghen van Linschoten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Huyghen_van_Linschoten), a Dutch traveler who copied Portuguese works and maps, confirmed this event by saying, "Madagascar has its name from 'makdishu' (Mogadishu)" whose "shayk" invaded it.[^cite_note-London_Missionary_Society_Press-27][^cite_note-31]&#x20;

The name *Malagasikara*, or *Malagascar*, is also historically attested. An English state paper in 1699 records the arrival of 80 to 90 passengers from "Malagaskar" to what eventually became [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-32] An 1882 edition of the British newspaper *[The Graphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Graphic)* referred to "Malagascar" as the name of the island, stating that the word was etymologically of Malay origin and might be related to the name of [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_City).[^cite_note-33] In 1891, Saleh bin Osman, a [Zanzibari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar) traveler, referred to the island as "Malagaskar" when recounting his journeys, including part of the [Emin Pasha Relief Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emin_Pasha_Relief_Expedition).[^cite_note-34] In 1905, Charles Basset wrote in his doctoral thesis that *Malagasikara* was the way the island was referred to by its natives, who emphasized that they were *Malagasy*, and not *Madagasy*.[^cite_note-35]&#x20;

## History

Main article: [History of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Madagascar)

### Early period



[Malagasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_people) ancestry reflects a blend of Southeast Asian, Oceanian, and Bantu (Southeast African) roots.&#x20;

Traditionally, archeologists have estimated that the earliest settlers arrived in successive waves in [outrigger canoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outrigger_canoe) from [South Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan), possibly throughout the period between 350 BCE and 550 CE, whereas others are cautious about dates earlier than 250 CE. In either case, these dates make Madagascar one of the most recent major landmasses on Earth to be settled by humans, predating the settlement of Iceland and New Zealand.[^cite_note-36] It has been proposed that [Ma'anyan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%27anyan_people) were brought as laborers and slaves by [Javan](69336.md) and [Sumatran](28679.md)-[Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_\(ethnic_group\)) in their trading fleets to Madagascar.[^cite_note-Dewar199322-37][^cite_note-Burney200422-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Cox2-40] Dates of settlement of the island earlier than the mid-first millennium CE are not strongly supported;[^cite_note-Mitchell-2020b-41] however, there is scattered evidence for much earlier human visits and presence.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-Hixon-2024-46]&#x20;

Upon arrival, early settlers practiced [slash-and-burn agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash-and-burn_agriculture) to clear the coastal rainforests for cultivation. The first settlers encountered Madagascar's abundance of [megafauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_megafauna),[^cite_note-47] including 17 species of [giant lemurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfossil_lemur); the large flightless [elephant birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_bird) (including possibly the largest bird to ever exist, *[Aepyornis maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aepyornis_maximus)*); the [giant fossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptoprocta_spelea); and several species of [Malagasy hippopotamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_hippopotamus), which have since become extinct because of hunting and habitat destruction.[^cite_note-48] According to the *[General History of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_History_of_Africa)*, these first settlers, the *[tompontany](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tompontany\&action=edit\&redlink=1)* (masters of the soil/land), are thought to have been the [Kimosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kimosy\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in south-central Madagascar, the [Antevinany](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antevinany\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in the southeast, the [Antankoala](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antankoala\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Kajemby](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kajemby\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in the northwest, and the [Rasikajy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasikajy) in the northeast. Newer arrivals formed marriage alliances with the *_tompontany_*, facilitating their gradual assimilation.[^cite_note-Esoavelomandroso-2025-49]<sup>: 900 </sup>&#x20;

By 600 CE, groups of these early settlers had begun clearing the forests of the [Central Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Highlands_\(Madagascar\)).[^cite_note-Campbell-1993-50] According to the *General History of Africa*, by the 8th century the [Vazimba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vazimba) (a "way of life" rather than ethnic group)[^cite_note-Randrianja_2009-51]<sup>: 71 </sup> had absorbed or violently displaced the first settlers, and had come to refer to themselves as *_tompontany_*.[^cite_note-Esoavelomandroso-2025-49]<sup>: 900 </sup> Malagasy popular belief, however, regards the Vazimba as the island's first inhabitants.[^cite_note-Mythe2-52]&#x20;

Arab traders first reached the island between the 7th and 9th centuries,[^cite_note-Wink2-53] and introduced Islam and the [Arabic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script) (adapted as *[sorabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorabe)* for the Malagasy language).[^cite_note-LOC2-54] [Indian Ocean trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade) along Madagascar's Northwestern Coast came to be controlled by the [Antalaotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antalaotra\&action=edit\&redlink=1), Muslim [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language)-speakers who had migrated to the region around the 10th century and intermarried with the locals,[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 36–37 </sup> forming [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-states) such as [Mahilaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mahilaka\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Irodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Irodo_\(village\)\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Esoavelomandroso-2025-49]<sup>: 900 </sup>&#x20;

A wave of [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages)-speaking migrants from southeastern Africa arrived around the year 1000.[^cite_note-56] Around this time, [zebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebu) cattle from [South India](678583.md) were first brought, intermingling with [sanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanga_cattle) cattle found in East Africa.[^cite_note-57]&#x20;

By 1100, all regions of Madagascar were inhabited, although the total population remained small.[^cite_note-Randrianja_2009-51]<sup>: 48 </sup> Societies, driven by [*hasina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasina_\(Madagascar\)) organized, after which they competed with one another over the [island's estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Madagascar) and [bridgeheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgehead). Oral histories describe bloody clashes and earlier settlers were often pushed along the coast or inland.[^cite_note-Randrianja_2009-51]<sup>: 43, 52–53 </sup> An Arab geographer wrote in 1224 that the island consisted of a great many towns and kingdoms, with kings making war on each other.[^cite_note-Randrianja_2009-51]<sup>: 51–52 </sup> Assisted by climate change, the peoples gradually transformed the island from [dense forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_lowland_forests) to grassland for cultivation and zebu [pastoralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism).[^cite_note-Randrianja_2009-51]<sup>: 71 </sup>&#x20;

### Rise of early kingdoms and contact with Europeans

The period from 1500 to 1800 saw Madagascar's populations go from being mobile and unsettled to being organized largely into states.[^cite_note-58]<sup>: 70 </sup> On the northern coast, Mahilaka was abandoned and replaced by [Vohemar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vohemar) in the 15th century as one of the island's main trading ports,[^cite_note-Hixon-2024-46] accompanied by [Mazalagem Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazalagem_Nova) in the late 16th century. [Portuguese navigators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_maritime_exploration#Indian_Ocean_explorations_\(1497%E2%80%931542\)) reached Madagascar around 1500, and sacked the port city of [Sada](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sada,_Madagascar\&action=edit\&redlink=1) (part of the [Guingemaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guingemaro) kingdom) in 1506.[^cite_note-Randrianja_20092-59]<sup>: 75, 91 </sup> Over the following centuries, the [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_slave_trade) grew in importance as slaves were traded for firearms.[^cite_note-Randrianja_20093-60]<sup>: 102 </sup> In the late 17th century, Madagascar had an influx of pirates who had been expelled from the [Caribbean](18956035.md), some of whom participated in local wars and [married local women](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zanamalata\&action=edit\&redlink=1), although they were routed by the British navy in the 1720s.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-Randrianja_20093-60]<sup>: 105 </sup>[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 864 </sup>&#x20;

The origin of the Maroserana, the dynasty of the [Sakalava Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakalava_Empire), is uncertain, with [Sakalava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakalava_people) traditions[^cite_note-65] holding that they originated from overseas and migrated to southwest Madagascar.[^cite_note-Kent-1970-66]<sup>: 164 </sup> The proto-Sakalava are thought to have originated from [Sadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sadia_\(city\)\&action=edit\&redlink=1) (located at the mouth of the [Manambolo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manambolo_River)).[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 857, 869 </sup> Historian [Solofo Randrianja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solofo_Randrianja) considers the Maroserana to have lived in south-central Madagascar,[^cite_note-Randrianja_20092-59]<sup>: 94 </sup> whereas [Raymond Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Raymond_Kent\&action=edit\&redlink=1) thought they originated in the southwest and first came to power among the [Mahafaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahafaly).[^cite_note-Kent-1970-66]<sup>: 26 </sup>&#x20;

Mahafaly tradition has Olembetsitoto as the first Maroserana [sacred ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_king) in the 16th century, who was protected by an *[ombiasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ombiasy\&action=edit\&redlink=1)* (priest).[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 856, 867 </sup> Prior to 1600, only the north of the island was integrated into Indian Ocean trade; but in the mid-to late-16th century, European merchants (*vazaha*; "foreigners") began using the newly-named [St. Augustine Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Saint-Augustin) in the southwest as a stopping point and traded with the communities there. The British attempted to found a colony at the Bay in 1645 but were expelled by the Malagasy.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 38–39, 54, 61–62 </sup>&#x20;

Kent considered the Maroserana to have migrated and met the proto-Sakalava near the [Mangoky River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangoky_River), who all traditions agree were skilled warriors.[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 869 </sup> Sakalava traditions detail how a kingdom was founded along the [Morondava River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morondava_River) before 1600, called *[Menabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menabe)* ("very red") after the red soil. In the late 16th or early 17th century, [Andriamandazoala](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Andriamandazoala\&action=edit\&redlink=1) centralized the [Kingdom of Menabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Menabe\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-67] [Andriandahifotsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Andriandahifotsy\&action=edit\&redlink=1) expanded the state and monopolized coastal trade.[^cite_note-68] After initial hostility, he established relations with European merchants.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 77–79, 83–89 </sup>&#x20;

Around 1685, a succession dispute brought [Andriamanetriarivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Andriamanetriarivo\&action=edit\&redlink=1) to power in Menabe when he expelled his brother [Andriamandisoarivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Andriamandisoarivo\&action=edit\&redlink=1). Taking this opportunity to expand north with his followers, Andriamandisoarivo conquered port cities along the coast, capturing Mazalagem Nova and killing the Antalaotra sultan, establishing his commercial capital at [Majunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majunga) in the early-18th century and founding the [Kingdom of Boina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Boina).[^cite_note-69] How the Sakalava acquired territory in the south is disputed. Jane Hooper considers another expelled brother to have expanded south and founded [Toliara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toliara) in the [Fiherenana Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiherenana_River) as an [Andrevola](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Andrevola\&action=edit\&redlink=1) tributary,[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 89–91, 95–97 </sup> whereas Randrianja thought the Maroserana to have come to rule there during their initial migration.[^cite_note-Randrianja_20093-60]<sup>: 100 </sup> Kent considered a brother, [Andriamandresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Andriamandresy\&action=edit\&redlink=1), to have migrated east and founded the [Antesaka kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antesaka_kingdom\&action=edit\&redlink=1) on the southeast coast in accordance with [Antesaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antesaka) tradition.[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 884 </sup> Hooper writes that Andriandahifotsy re-established relations with his brothers as the Sakalava came to control Madagascar's west coast and dominate trade.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 98–99 </sup>&#x20;

In the southwest and south, conflict between the [Mahafaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mahafaly_kingdom\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Antandroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antandroy_kingdom\&action=edit\&redlink=1) kingdoms in the mid-17th century caused the death of two Antandroy kings and saw Mahafaly split in two: [Menarandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Menarandra\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Sakatovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Sakatovo\&action=edit\&redlink=1), with Menarandra soon splitting further to produce [Linta](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Linta\&action=edit\&redlink=1). In the early 18th century, a Menarandra king expanded east to conquer the Western Antandroy, though yet another split produced [Onilahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Onilahy\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 867–868 </sup> On the East Coast, when the [Antemoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antemoro_people) settled their lands they found Muslim settlers, the [Zafiraminia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafiraminia), already there since around 1500. A conflict between the two broke out,[^cite_note-71] and the Zafiraminia had come to rule the [Antemoro kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antemoro_kingdom\&action=edit\&redlink=1) by the mid-16th century.[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 851–853 </sup>&#x20;

In the southeast, the French founded [Fort Dauphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort-Dauphin_\(Madagascar\)) in 1642. They intervened in local conflicts and raided for cattle, provoking insecurity. The [Antanosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antanosy) attacked the colony, although they were defeated by [Flacourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tienne_de_Flacourt)'s forces. Another attack destroyed the colony and resulted in the [killing of French settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_Fort-Dauphin_\(Madagascar\)) who remained in 1674.[^cite_note-72]&#x20;

In the Central Highlands, Merina traditions hold that they encountered the Vazimba[^cite_note-73] when gradually settling the highlands from the southeast, thought to have been completed by the 15th century.[^cite_note-Campbell-1993-50][^cite_note-Randrianja_2009-51]<sup>: 70 </sup> After peacefully coexisting for several generations, the [Alasora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_of_Alasora) king [Andriamanelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriamanelo), son of a Vazimba queen[^cite_note-75] and a Merina man, launched a campaign to conquer the Vazimba.[^cite_note-Raison-Jourde-74]<sup>: 142 </sup>&#x20;

Of his successors, [Ralambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralambo) founded the [Merina Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merina_Kingdom),[^cite_note-76] and [Andrianjaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrianjaka) completed the expulsion and assimilation of the Vazimba by the early 17th century.[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 876 </sup> Traditions attribute the conquest of the Vazimba to the need to acquire more land for rice cultivation. Archeological research places the beginning of this expansion in the 14th century.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 49–50 </sup> In the mid-17th century a Mahafaly king invaded [Bara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bara_people) territory and appointed his relatives, the [Zafimanely](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zafimanely\&action=edit\&redlink=1), as rulers, who gained independence of [various kinglets](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bara_kingdoms\&action=edit\&redlink=1) after his death.[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 880 </sup> [Betsileo kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Betsileo_kingdoms\&action=edit\&redlink=1) such as [Arindrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arindrano\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Isandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Isandra) were likely founded in the mid-17th century, and the [Betsileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsileo_people) derive their name from King Besilau, who repelled a Sakalava-Menabe invasion in the 1670s.[^cite_note-77] By the 18th century, the Betsileo were the island's most proficient cultivators of rice.[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 874–875 </sup>&#x20;

By 1720, the Sakalava-Boina king [Toakafo](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Toakafo\&action=edit\&redlink=1) is considered to have been the most powerful ruler in Madagascar, and possibly ruled the entire northern third of the island.[^cite_note-Ellis-2007-78] In the 18th century, the French established various trading posts along the east coast in order to supply the [Mascarenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascarenes).[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 155–156 </sup> On the northeast coast, the Tsikoa tribe coalesced under a single ruler and invaded the *Antavaratra* (Northerners) c. 1710, intent on capturing their lucrative ports. [Ratsimilaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratsimilaho), a son of an [English pirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tew) and a Malagasy woman who had been Toakafo's chief minister,[^cite_note-Randrianja_20093-60]<sup>: 106 </sup> managed to unite the *_Antavaratra_*, repel the Tsikoa, and drive them south. Ratsimilaho founded the [Betsimisaraka Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Betsimisaraka_Confederation\&action=edit\&redlink=1), and by the 1730s was one of the most powerful kings in Madagascar, although the state disintegrated soon after his death in 1754.[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 882–883 </sup>&#x20;

The French attempted to set up trading posts on the northwest coast, resulting in conflict with the Sakalava in the 1770s that ended inconclusively.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 160–1 </sup> The Merina king [Andriamasinavalona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriamasinavalona) expanded the kingdom further and ruled much of the Central Highlands during his reign, though in the early 18th century he abdicated and divided the state between four of his sons. A fierce [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Merina_Civil_War\&action=edit\&redlink=1) ensued characterized by slave-raiding and -trading, and it was not until the 1790s that the kingdom was reunited by means of conquest and diplomacy by [Andrianampoinimerina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrianampoinimerina).[^cite_note-Kent-1992-64]<sup>: 877 </sup>[^cite_note-79]<sup>: 204 </sup>[^cite_note-Randrianja_20093-60]<sup>: 114–8 </sup>&#x20;

### 19th century and the Kingdom of Madagascar



King [Andrianampoinimerina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrianampoinimerina) (1787–1810)

After reuniting [Imerina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imerina) and seated at the [Rova of Antananarivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rova_of_Antananarivo), Andrianampoinimerina rapidly expanded his rule over neighboring principalities. He used the ports' reliance on the supply of slaves from the highlands to gain control over trade on the Eastern Coast.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 165 </sup>&#x20;

His ambition to bring the entire island under his control was largely achieved by his son and successor, King [Radama I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radama_I) (r. 1810–28).[^cite_note-Ade_Ajayi_1989,_pp._413–4222-80] The 18th century had seen rivalry between the French and the British over the wider Indian Ocean trade, of which Malagasy trade had proved decisive during the [Carnatic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnatic_Wars).[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 195 </sup> Radama expanded east to control [Toamasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toamasina) on the coast,[^cite_note-Mutibwa-1989-81]<sup>: 416 </sup> and concluded a treaty in 1817 with the British governor of [Mauritius](19201.md) to abolish the lucrative slave trade in return for British military and financial assistance, and recognition as the "King of Madagascar". Artisan missionary envoys from the [London Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Missionary_Society) began arriving in 1818. They established schools, transcribed the Malagasy language using the [Roman alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), and translated the Bible.[^cite_note-Ade_Ajayi_1989,_pp._413–4222-80] In response, the Sakalava and French found themselves as natural allies.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 198 </sup> Radama embarked on successive campaigns to conquer the island, subduing the east coast, dismantling [Iboina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iboina) and expelling its ruler, and achieving Menabe's nominal submission. By 1828 he controlled two-thirds of the island (excepting the Bara, Mahafaly, and [Antandroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antandroy)), although Merina rule was far from secure and widespread resistance continued.[^cite_note-Mutibwa-1989-81]<sup>: 420–2 </sup>&#x20;

Radama's successor, Queen [Ranavalona I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranavalona_I) (r. 1828–61), responded to increasing political and cultural encroachment on the part of Britain and France by issuing a royal edict prohibiting the practice of [Christianity in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Madagascar) and pressuring most foreigners to leave the territory. The queen made heavy use of the traditional practice of *[fanompoana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corv%C3%A9e)* (forced labor as tax payment) to complete public works projects and develop a standing army of between 20,000 and 30,000 Merina soldiers, whom she deployed to pacify outlying regions of the island and further expand the Merina Kingdom to encompass most of Madagascar.[^cite_note-Stats2-82]&#x20;

Residents of Madagascar could accuse one another of various crimes, including theft, Christianity, and especially witchcraft, for which the ordeal of *[tangena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangena)* (a [poison trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poison_trial)) was routinely obligatory. Between 1828 and 1861, the *tangena* ordeal caused about 3,000 deaths annually.[^cite_note-Stats2-82] The combination of regular warfare, disease, difficult forced labor, and harsh measures of justice resulted in a high mortality rate among soldiers and civilians alike during the queen's 33-year reign. The population of Madagascar is estimated to have declined from around 5 million to 2.5 million between 1833 and 1839.[^cite_note-Stats2-82]&#x20;

In 1839, Boina queen [Tsiomeko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsiomeko_of_Boina), having fled to the island of [Nosy Be](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nosy_Be), requested French assistance against Merina attacks. In accordance with France's conditions, she signed over Nosy Be and part of the mainland to the French,[^cite_note-Feeley-Harnik-1982-83] which they then declared as France's [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate).[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 241–5 </sup>&#x20;

Among those who continued to reside in Imerina were [Jean Laborde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Laborde), an entrepreneur who developed munitions and other industries on behalf of the monarchy, and [Joseph-François Lambert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph-Fran%C3%A7ois_Lambert), a French adventurer and slave trader, with whom then-Prince [Radama II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radama_II) signed a controversial trade agreement termed the [Lambert Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambert_Charter). Succeeding his mother, Radama II attempted to relax the queen's stringent policies but was [overthrown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radama_II#Assassination_plot) two years later by Prime Minister [Rainivoninahitriniony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainivoninahitriniony) and an alliance of *[Andriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriana)* (noble) and *[Hova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hova_\(Madagascar\))* (commoner) courtiers, who sought to end the monarch's absolute power.[^cite_note-LOC2-54]&#x20;

Following the coup, the courtiers offered Radama's queen, [Rasoherina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasoherina), the opportunity to rule, if she would accept a power-sharing arrangement with the prime minister: a new social contract that would be sealed by a political marriage between them.[^cite_note-84] Queen Rasoherina accepted, first marrying Rainivoninahitriniony, then later deposing him and marrying his brother, Prime Minister [Rainilaiarivony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainilaiarivony), who would go on to marry Queen [Ranavalona II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranavalona_II) and Queen [Ranavalona III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranavalona_III) in succession.[^cite_note-85]&#x20;

Over the course of Rainilaiarivony's 31-year tenure as prime minister, numerous policies were adopted to modernize and consolidate the central government's power.[^cite_note-TA9102-86] Schools were constructed throughout the island and attendance was made mandatory. Army organization was improved and British consultants were employed to train and professionalize soldiers.[^cite_note-FFO5222-87] [Polygamy](24475.md) was outlawed and Christianity, declared the official religion of the court in 1869, was adopted alongside traditional beliefs among a growing portion of the populace.[^cite_note-TA9102-86] Legal codes were reformed on the basis of British [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) and three European-style courts were established in the capital city.[^cite_note-FFO5222-87] In his joint role as commander-in-chief, Rainilaiarivony also successfully ensured the defense of Madagascar against several French colonial incursions.[^cite_note-FFO5222-87] The French devised plans in 1842 and the 1860s to land troops in Madagascar and assist the Sakalava, who continued to resist, in a war against Imerina; however, they did not materialize.[^cite_note-Hooper-2010-55]<sup>: 252 </sup>&#x20;

### French colonization and the colonial period

Main articles: [Malagasy Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_Protectorate) and [French Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Madagascar)

See also: [Madagascar in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_in_World_War_II)



French poster about the [Franco-Hova War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Hova_Wars)

In 1883 after diplomatic fallout, France began the [First Franco-Hova War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Franco-Hova_War) by bombing [Majunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majunga), during which Madagascar suffered under blockade and diplomatic isolation as it was ignored by the nominally allied British. In 1885, an ambiguous peace treaty was signed that allowed the two parties to interpret it to their benefit: Madagascar viewed it as affirming its sovereignty, France viewed it as establishing [protectorate status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate).[^cite_note-88]<sup>: 224–8 </sup> Although the British accepted the full formal imposition of a French protectorate on the island in 1890, French authority was not acknowledged by the government of Madagascar. To force capitulation, the French bombarded and occupied the harbor of Toamasina on the east coast and Majunga on the west coast in December 1894 and January 1895, respectively.[^cite_note-Disease2-89]&#x20;

A French military [flying column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_column) then marched toward [Antananarivo](56597.md), losing many men to malaria and other diseases. Reinforcements came from [Algeria](358.md) and [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md). Upon reaching the city in September 1895, the column bombarded the royal palace with heavy artillery, causing heavy casualties and leading Queen Ranavalona III to surrender.[^cite_note-90] Popular resistance to the French capture of Antananarivo—known as the [Menalamba rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menalamba_rebellion)—broke out in December 1895 and was not suppressed until the end of 1897.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] France annexed Madagascar in 1896 and declared the island a colony the following year, dissolving the Merina monarchy and sending the royal family into exile to the island country of [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) and Algeria.[^cite_note-93]&#x20;

The conquest was followed by 10 years of civil war, due to the Menalamba insurrection. The "pacification" carried out by the French administration lasted more than 15 years, in response to the rural guerrillas scattered throughout the country.[^cite_note-Tronchon-1947-94] The French [campaign against Menabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Franco-Menabe_war\&action=edit\&redlink=1) began with the [Ambiky massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ambiky_massacre\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1897, ending in 1902. The Antandroy and Mahafaly continued to oppose colonial rule, though yielded in 1904.[^cite_note-95]<sup>: 238–9 </sup> In total, the repression of this resistance to colonial conquest caused several tens of thousands of Malagasy victims.[^cite_note-Tronchon-1947-94]&#x20;

Under colonial rule, plantations were established for the production of a variety of export crops.[^cite_note-96] [Slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) was abolished in 1896, freeing approximately 500,000 slaves, though many remained in their former masters' homes as servants[^cite_note-97] or as sharecroppers. In many parts of the island strong discriminatory views against slave descendants are still held today.[^cite_note-98] Wide paved boulevards and gathering places were constructed in the capital city of Antananarivo[^cite_note-99] and the Rova palace compound was turned into a museum.[^cite_note-Frémigacci2-100] Additional schools were built, particularly in rural and coastal areas where the schools of the Merina had not reached. Education became mandatory between the ages of 6 and 13, focusing primarily on the French language and practical skills.[^cite_note-101]&#x20;



National monument in [Moramanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moramanga) commemorating the beginning of the [Malagasy Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_Uprising) on 29 March 1947. Between 11,000 and 90,000 Malagasy died during the uprising, which lasted nearly two years.

[^cite_note-102]

Huge mining and forestry concessions were granted to large companies. Native chiefs loyal to the French administration were also granted part of the land. Forced labor was introduced in favor of the French companies and peasants were encouraged, through taxation, to work for wages (especially in the colonial concessions), to the detriment of small individual farms. However, the colonial period was accompanied by movements fighting for independence: the Menalamba, the [Vy Vato Sakelika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vy_Vato_Sakelika), and the [Democratic Movement for Malagasy Renovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Movement_for_Malagasy_Renovation) (MDRM). In 1927, major demonstrations were organized in Antananarivo, notably on the initiative of the communist activist [François Vittori](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_Vittori\&action=edit\&redlink=1), who was imprisoned as a result. The 1930s saw the Malagasy anti-colonial movement gain further momentum. Malagasy trade unionism began to appear underground and the [Communist Party of the Madagascar region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Madagascar_region) was formed. But in 1939, all these organizations were dissolved by the administration of the colony, which opted for the [Vichy regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_regime). The MDRM was accused by the colonial regime of being at the root of the 1947 insurrection and was pursued by violent repression.[^cite_note-103]&#x20;

The Merina royal tradition of [taxes paid in the form of labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corv%C3%A9e) was continued under the French and used to construct a railway and roads linking key coastal cities to Antananarivo.[^cite_note-104]&#x20;

Malagasy troops fought for France in [World War I](4764461.md). In the 1930s, [Nazi](21212.md) political thinkers developed the [Madagascar Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_Plan) that had identified the island as a potential site for the deportation of Europe's Jews.[^cite_note-contemplation2-105] During [World War II](32927.md), the island was the site of the [Battle of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Madagascar) between the [Vichy French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) and an [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) expeditionary force.[^cite_note-106] The occupation of France during the Second World War tarnished the prestige of the colonial administration in Madagascar and galvanized the growing independence movement, leading to the [Malagasy Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_Uprising) of 1947.[^cite_note-107] This movement led the French to establish reformed institutions in 1956 under the *[Loi Cadre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loi_Cadre)* (Overseas Reform Act), and Madagascar moved peacefully towards independence.[^cite_note-Kitchen_1962,_p._2562-108] The [Malagasy Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_Republic) was proclaimed on 14 October 1958, as an [autonomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_entity) state within the [French Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Community). A period of provisional government ended with the adoption of a constitution in 1959 and full independence on 26 June 1960.[^cite_note-109]&#x20;

### Independent state

Main articles: [Malagasy Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_Republic), [Democratic Republic of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Madagascar), and [Third Republic of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Republic_of_Madagascar)



[Philibert Tsiranana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philibert_Tsiranana), first [president of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Madagascar) (1960–1972)

Since regaining independence, Madagascar has transitioned through four republics with corresponding revisions to its constitution. The [First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_Republic) (1960–72), under the leadership of French-appointed President [Philibert Tsiranana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philibert_Tsiranana), was characterized by a continuation of strong economic and political ties to France. Many high-level technical positions were filled by French expatriates; and French teachers, textbooks, and curricula continued to be used in schools around the country. Popular resentment over Tsiranana's tolerance for this "neo-colonial" arrangement inspired a [series of farmer and student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotaka) that overturned his administration in 1972.[^cite_note-LOC2-54]&#x20;

[Gabriel Ramanantsoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Ramanantsoa), a major general in the army, was appointed interim president and prime minister that same year but low public approval forced him to step down in 1975. Colonel [Richard Ratsimandrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Ratsimandrava), appointed to succeed him, was assassinated six days into his tenure. General [Gilles Andriamahazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_Andriamahazo) ruled after Ratsimandrava for four months before being replaced by another military appointee, Vice Admiral [Didier Ratsiraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Ratsiraka), who ushered in the Marxist–Leninist [Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Madagascar) that ran under his tenure from 1975 to 1993.[^cite_note-110]&#x20;

This period saw a political alignment with the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries and a shift toward economic insularity. These policies, coupled with economic pressures stemming from the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), resulted in the rapid collapse of Madagascar's economy and a sharp decline in living standards,[^cite_note-LOC2-54] and by 1979 the country had become completely bankrupt. The Ratsiraka administration accepted the conditions of transparency, anti-corruption measures, and free market policies imposed by the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), [World Bank](36752.md), and various bilateral donors in exchange for their bailout of the nation's broken economy.[^cite_note-ISS2-111]&#x20;

Ratsiraka's dwindling popularity in the late 1980s reached a critical point in 1991 when presidential guards opened fire on unarmed protesters during a rally. Within two months, a transitional government had been established under the leadership of [Albert Zafy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Zafy) (1993–96), who went on to win the 1992 presidential elections and inaugurate the [Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Republic_of_Madagascar) (1992–2010).[^cite_note-crisisgroup2-112] The new [Madagascar Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Madagascar) established a multi-party democracy and a separation of powers placing significant control in the hands of the National Assembly. The constitution also emphasized human rights, social and political freedoms, and free trade.[^cite_note-LOC2-54] Zafy's term, however, was marred by economic decline, allegations of corruption, and his introduction of legislation to give himself greater powers. Consequently, he was impeached in 1996 and an interim president, [Norbert Ratsirahonana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Ratsirahonana), was appointed for the three months prior to the next presidential election. Ratsiraka was then voted back into power on a platform of decentralization and economic reforms for a second term lasting from 1996 to 2001.[^cite_note-ISS2-111]&#x20;

The contested [2001 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_presidential_election,_2001) in which the then-mayor of Antananarivo, [Marc Ravalomanana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Ravalomanana), eventually emerged victorious, caused a seven-month standoff in 2002 between supporters of Ravalomanana and Ratsiraka. The negative economic impact of the political crisis was gradually overcome by Ravalomanana's progressive economic and political policies encouraging investments in education and ecotourism, facilitated foreign direct investment, and cultivated trading partnerships both regionally and internationally. The national GDP grew at an average rate of 7% per year under his administration. In the latter half of his second term, however, Ravalomanana was criticized by domestic and international observers who accused him of increasing authoritarianism and corruption.[^cite_note-ISS2-111]&#x20;

Opposition leader and then-mayor of Antananarivo, [Andry Rajoelina](21298487.md), led a [movement in early 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Malagasy_political_crisis) in which Ravalomanana was pushed from power in an unconstitutional process widely condemned as a *[coup d'état](25914154.md)*.[^cite_note-113] In March 2009, Rajoelina was declared by the Supreme Court as president of the [High Transitional Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Transitional_Authority), an interim governing body responsible for moving the country toward presidential elections. In 2010, a new constitution was [adopted by referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_constitutional_referendum,_2010), establishing a Fourth Republic, which sustained the democratic, multi-party structure established in the previous constitution.[^cite_note-crisisgroup2-112] [Hery Rajaonarimampianina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hery_Rajaonarimampianina) was declared the winner of the [2013 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_general_election,_2013), which the international community deemed fair and transparent.[^cite_note-114]&#x20;

In 2018 the first round of Malagasy's [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malagasy_presidential_election) was held on 7 November and the second on 10 December. Three former presidents and the most recent president were the main candidates. Rajoelina won the second round of the elections. Although Ravalomana lost that round, he did not accept the results because of allegations of fraud. Rajaonarimampianina received very modest support in the first round. In January 2019, the High Constitutional Court declared Rajoelina as the winner of the elections and the new president.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] In the June 2019 [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Malagasy_parliamentary_election), Rajoelina won an absolute majority of the seats of the National Assembly. It received 84 seats and the supporters of former president Ravalomana got only 16 seats of 151 seats of the National Assembly. Fifty-one seats of deputies were independent or represented small parties. Rajoelina could rule as a [strongman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongman_\(politics\)).[^cite_note-118]&#x20;

Mid-2021 marked the beginning of the [2021–2022 Madagascar famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_Madagascar_famine), which, due to a severe drought, caused hundreds of thousands of people to face [food insecurity](216361.md) and leaving over one million on the verge of [famine](51329.md).[^cite_note-119]&#x20;

In November 2023, Rajoelina was re-elected to another term with 58.95% of the vote in the first round of the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Malagasy_presidential_election) amid an opposition boycott and a controversy about his acquisition of French citizenship and subsequent eligibility. The turnout was 46.36%, the lowest in a presidential election in the country's history.[^cite_note-120] Power and water cuts, as well as general distrust of Rajoelina's administration, sparked a [series of protests](81184334.md) in 2025 culminating in his flight and impeachment with the military [taking control](81321149.md) of the country.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] On 17 October 2025, Colonel [Michael Randrianirina](81340595.md) was sworn in as Madagascar's new president to succeed Rajoelina.[^cite_note-123]&#x20;

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Madagascar) and [Geology of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Madagascar)



Land coverage (left) and topographical (right) maps of Madagascar

With 592,800 square kilometres (228,900 sq mi),[^cite_note-BGNote-10] Madagascar is the world's [46th-largest country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area),[^cite_note-cia-124] the [second-largest island country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_island_countries)[^cite_note-world-atlas-125] and the [fourth-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area).[^cite_note-BGNote-10] The country lies mostly between latitudes [12°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_parallel_south) and [26°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_parallel_south) and longitudes [43°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_meridian_east) and [51°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51st_meridian_east).[^cite_note-georeport-126] Neighboring islands include the [French territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_France) of [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) and the country of [Mauritius](19201.md) to the east, as well as the state of [Comoros](5403.md) and the French territory of [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) to the northwest. The nearest mainland state is [Mozambique](19301.md), located to the west.&#x20;

The prehistoric breakup of the [supercontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercontinent) [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana) resulted in the separation of [East Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Gondwana) (comprising Madagascar, Antarctica, Australia and the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent)) and [West Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Gondwana) (Africa and South America) during the [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) period, around 185 million years ago. The Indo-Madagascar landmass separated from Antarctica and Australia around 125 million years ago,[^cite_note-127] and Madagascar from the Indian landmass about 84 to 92 million years ago during the Late [Cretaceous Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous).[^cite_note-128] This long history of separation from other continents has allowed plants and animals on the island to evolve in relative isolation.&#x20;

Along the length of Madagascar's Eastern Coast runs a narrow and steep [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) containing much of the island's remaining tropical [lowland forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_lowland_forests). To the west of this ridge lies a [plateau](538005.md) in the center of the island ranging in altitude from 750 to 1,500 m (2,460 to 4,920 ft) above sea level. These [Central Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Highlands_\(Madagascar\)), traditionally the homeland of the Merina people and the location of their historic capital at Antananarivo, are the most densely populated part of the island, characterized by terraced, rice-growing valleys lying between grassy hills and patches of the [subhumid forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_subhumid_forests) that formerly covered the highland region. To the west of the highlands, the increasingly arid terrain gradually slopes down to the [Mozambique Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_Channel) and [mangrove swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_mangroves) along the coast.[^cite_note-endemicstats-129]&#x20;



Landscape in the [Central Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Highlands_\(Madagascar\)) region

Madagascar's highest peaks rise from three prominent highland [massifs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif). [Maromokotro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maromokotro) 2,876 m (9,436 ft) in the [Tsaratanana Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaratanana_Massif) is the island's highest point, followed by Boby Peak 2,658 m (8,720 ft) in the [Andringitra Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andringitra_Massif), and [Tsiafajavona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsiafajavona_Ankaratra) 2,643 m (8,671 ft) in the [Ankaratra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankaratra) Massif. To the east, the *[Canal des Pangalanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_des_Pangalanes)* is a chain of human-made and natural lakes connected by canals built by the French just inland from the east coast and running parallel to it for some 600 km (370 mi).[^cite_note-EBLand-130]&#x20;

The western and southern sides, which lie in the [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) of the Central Highlands, are home to [dry deciduous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_dry_deciduous_forests), [spiny forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_spiny_forests), and [deserts and xeric shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts_and_xeric_shrublands). Due to their lower population densities, Madagascar's dry deciduous forests have been better preserved than the Eastern Rainforests or the original woodlands of the Central Plateau. The Western Coast features many protected harbors, but [silting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silting) is a major problem caused by sediment from the high levels of inland erosion carried by rivers crossing the broad Western Plains.[^cite_note-EBLand-130]&#x20;

### Climate

See also: [Geography of Madagascar § Climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Madagascar#Climate)



[Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) map of Madagascar

The combination of southeastern [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds) and northwestern [monsoons](57630.md) produces a hot rainy season (November–April) with frequently destructive [cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone), and a relatively cooler dry season (May–October). Rain clouds originating over the Indian Ocean discharge much of their moisture over the island's Eastern Coast and the heavy precipitation supports the area's [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest) ecosystem. The Central Highlands are both drier and cooler. The west is drier still, and a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) prevails in the island's southwest and southern interior.[^cite_note-endemicstats-129]&#x20;

[Tropical cyclones](8282374.md) cause damage to infrastructure and local economies as well as loss of life.[^cite_note-LOC-131] In March 2004, [Cyclone Gafilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Gafilo) became the strongest cyclone ever recorded to hit the country. The storm killed 363 people,[^cite_note-132] left 214,260 homeless,[^cite_note-gafilo-133] and caused more than US\$250 million in damage.[^cite_note-IRIN-2004-134] In February 2022, [Cyclone Batsirai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Batsirai) killed 121 people,[^cite_note-Mitchell-2020a-135][^cite_note-136] weeks after [Cyclone Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Ana_\(2022\)) killed 55 and displaced 130,000.[^cite_note-137]&#x20;

A 2022 analysis found that in order to adapt to and avert the environmental consequences of [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Madagascar), Madagascar would have to spend 15% of its [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP).[^cite_note-138]&#x20;

### Biodiversity and conservation

Main articles: [Wildlife of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Madagascar), [Flora of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_of_Madagascar), [Fauna of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Madagascar), [Agriculture in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Madagascar), [Ecoregions of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregions_of_Madagascar), [List of World Heritage Sites in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Madagascar), [Deforestation in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Madagascar), [Illegal logging in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_logging_in_Madagascar), and [List of invasive species in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_invasive_species_in_Madagascar)



Comet orchid (*[Angraecum sesquipedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angraecum_sesquipedale)*). The flowers of this orchid have a very long spur and are pollinated by a species of [hawkmoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphingidae) with a proboscis of matching length.

As a result of the island's long isolation from neighboring continents, Madagascar is home to various [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) plants and animals found nowhere else on Earth.[^cite_note-CIHotSpot-139][^cite_note-tattersall-140] Approximately 90% of all plant and animal species found in Madagascar are endemic.[^cite_note-141] This distinctive ecology has led some ecologists to refer to Madagascar as the "eighth continent",[^cite_note-142] and the island has been classified by [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International) as a biodiversity hotspot.[^cite_note-143] It is classed as one of 17 [megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries). The country is home to seven terrestrial ecoregions: [lowland forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_lowland_forests), [subhumid forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_subhumid_forests), [dry deciduous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_dry_deciduous_forests), [ericoid thickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_ericoid_thickets), [spiny forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_spiny_forests), [succulent woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_succulent_woodlands), and [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_mangroves).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-144]&#x20;

More than 80% of Madagascar's 14,883 plant species are found nowhere else in the world, including five plant families.[^cite_note-endemism-145] The family *[Didiereaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didiereaceae)*, composed of four genera and 11 species, is limited to the spiny forests of southwestern Madagascar.[^cite_note-endemicstats-129] Four-fifths of the world's *[Pachypodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachypodium)* species are endemic to the island.[^cite_note-146] Three-fourths[^cite_note-B2011plant-147] of Madagascar's 860[^cite_note-endemism-145] [orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid) species are found there alone, as are six of the world's nine [baobab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adansonia) species.[^cite_note-148] The island is home to around 170 [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae) species, three times as many as on all of mainland Africa, of which 165 are endemic.[^cite_note-B2011plant-147]&#x20;

Many native plant species are used as herbal remedies for a variety of afflictions. For instance, the drugs [vinblastine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinblastine)[^cite_note-MoleculesReview-149][^cite_note-150] and [vincristine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincristine)[^cite_note-MoleculesReview-149][^cite_note-151] are [*vinca* alkaloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinca_alkaloid)[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]—which are used to treat [Hodgkin lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgkin_lymphoma),[^cite_note-154] [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia),[^cite_note-155] and other cancers—[^cite_note-156] were derived from the [Madagascar periwinkle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catharanthus).[^cite_note-periw-157][^cite_note-158] The traveler's palm, known locally as *[Ravenala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenala)*[^cite_note-159] and endemic to the Eastern Rainforests,[^cite_note-160] is highly iconic of Madagascar and featured in the [national emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_Madagascar) as well as the [Madagascar Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_Airlines) logo.[^cite_note-161]&#x20;

Two ring-tailed lemurs curled up together

The [ring-tailed lemur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring-tailed_lemur) is one of over 100 known species and subspecies of lemur found only in Madagascar.

[^cite_note-2009Mittermeier-162]

Like its flora, Madagascar's fauna are diverse and exhibit a high rate of endemism. [Lemurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemur) have been characterized as "Madagascar's flagship mammal species" by Conservation International.[^cite_note-CIHotSpot-139] In the absence of monkeys and other competitors, these [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate) have adapted to a wide range of habitats and diversified into numerous species. As of 2012, there were officially [103 species and subspecies of the lemur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lemur_species),[^cite_note-lemurextinction-163] 39 of which were described by zoologists between 2000 and 2008.[^cite_note-Mittermeier2008-164] Almost all of these are classified as rare, vulnerable, or endangered. Since the arrival of humans on Madagascar, at least 17 species of lemur have become extinct, all of which were larger than the surviving lemur species.[^cite_note-165]&#x20;

A number of other [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal), including the catlike [fossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossa_\(animal\)), are endemic to Madagascar. Over 300 species of birds have been recorded on the island, of which over 60%—including four families and 42 genera—are endemic.[^cite_note-CIHotSpot-139] The few families and genera of [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile) that have reached Madagascar have diversified into more than 260 species, with over 90% of these being endemic,[^cite_note-Okajima-166] including one endemic family.[^cite_note-CIHotSpot-139] The island is home to two-thirds of the world's [chameleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon) species,[^cite_note-Okajima-166] including the [smallest known](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookesia_nana).[^cite_note-167]&#x20;

Endemic fish of Madagascar include two families, 15 genera, and over 100 species, primarily inhabiting the island's freshwater lakes and rivers. Although [invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate) remain poorly studied in Madagascar, researchers have found high rates of endemism among the known species. All 651 species of [terrestrial snail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_snail) are endemic, for example, as are a majority of the island's butterflies, [scarab beetles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarabaeidae), [lacewings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroptera), spiders, and [dragonflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonfly).[^cite_note-CIHotSpot-139]&#x20;

Burning Malagasy rainforest

A vast, red soil gully caused by erosion

Tavy ([slash-and-burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash-and-burn_agriculture)) destruction of native forest habitat is widespread (top), causing massive erosion (bottom).

Madagascar's varied fauna and flora are endangered by human activity.[^cite_note-168] Since the arrival of humans around 2,350 years ago, Madagascar has lost more than 90% of its original forest.[^cite_note-169] This forest loss is largely fueled by *tavy* (fat), a traditional [slash-and-burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash-and-burn_agriculture) agricultural practice imported to Madagascar by the earliest settlers.[^cite_note-Gade_1996-170] Malagasy farmers embrace and perpetuate the practice not only for its practical benefits as an agricultural technique but also for its cultural associations with prosperity, health, and venerated ancestral custom (*fomba malagasy*).[^cite_note-171] As human population density rose on the island, deforestation accelerated beginning around 1,400 years ago.[^cite_note-Campbell-1993-50] By the 16th century, the Central Highlands had been largely cleared of their original forests.[^cite_note-Gade_1996-170] More recent contributors to the loss of forest cover include the growth in cattle herd size since their introduction around 1,000 years ago, continued reliance on charcoal as a fuel for cooking, and increased prominence of coffee as a [cash crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_crop) over the past century.[^cite_note-spittingwind-172]&#x20;

According to a conservative estimate, about 40% of the island's original forest cover was lost from the 1950s to 2000, with a thinning of remaining forest areas by 80%.[^cite_note-173] In addition to traditional agricultural practice, wildlife conservation is challenged by the illicit harvesting of protected forests, as well as the state-sanctioned harvesting of precious woods within national parks. Although banned by then-President Ravalomanana from 2000 to 2009, the collection of small quantities of precious timber from national parks was re-authorized in January 2009 and dramatically intensified under the administration of Rajoelina as a key source of state revenues to offset cuts in donor support following Ravalomanana's ousting.[^cite_note-CrossroadsMarcus-174]&#x20;

[Invasive species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_invasive_species_in_Madagascar) have likewise been introduced by human populations. Following the 2014 discovery in Madagascar of the [Asian common toad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duttaphrynus_melanostictus), a relative of a toad species that has severely harmed wildlife in Australia since the 1930s, researchers warned that it could "wreak havoc on the country's unique fauna."[^cite_note-175] [Habitat destruction](1707053.md) and hunting have threatened many of Madagascar's endemic species or driven them to extinction. The island's [elephant birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_bird), a family of endemic giant [ratites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratite), became extinct in the 17th century or earlier, most probably because of human hunting of adult birds and poaching of their large eggs for food.[^cite_note-Davies-176] Numerous [giant lemur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfossil_lemur) species vanished with the arrival of human settlers on the island, and others became extinct over the course of the centuries as a growing human population put greater pressures on lemur habitats and, among some populations, increased the rate of lemur hunting for food.[^cite_note-177]&#x20;

A July 2012 assessment found that the exploitation of natural resources since 2009 had had dire consequences for the island's wildlife: 90% of lemur species were found to be threatened with extinction, the highest proportion of any mammalian group, of which 23 species were classified as critically endangered. A 2023 study published in *[Nature Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Communications)* found that 120 of the 219 mammal species found only on Madagascar were threatened with extinction.[^cite_note-178]&#x20;

In 2003, Ravalomanana announced the Durban Vision, an initiative to more than triple the island's [protected natural areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_Madagascar) to over 60,000 km<sup>2</sup> (23,000 sq mi) or 10% of Madagascar's land surface. As of 2011, areas protected by the state included five Strict Nature Reserves (*Réserves Naturelles Intégrales*), 21 Wildlife Reserves (*Réserves Spéciales*), and 21 National Parks (*Parcs Nationaux*).[^cite_note-179] In 2007 six of the national parks—[Marojejy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marojejy_National_Park), [Masoala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoala_National_Park), [Ranomafana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranomafana_National_Park), [Zahamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahamena_National_Park), [Andohahela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andohahela_National_Park), and [Andringitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andringitra_National_Park)—were declared a joint [world heritage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Madagascar) under the name [Rainforests of the Atsinanana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforests_of_the_Atsinanana).[^cite_note-180] Local timber merchants have been harvesting scarce species of [rosewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosewood) trees from protected rainforests within Marojejy National Park and exporting the wood to China for the production of luxury furniture and musical instruments.[^cite_note-181]&#x20;

[Conservation International Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International_Madagascar) is Madagascar's country programme within Conservation International, with a central office in Antananarivo and regional branches in [Fianarantsoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianarantsoa) and Toamasina. Its work has included support for protected-area and landscape initiatives, including forest corridor management in eastern and southeastern Madagascar such as the Ambositra-Vondrozo Forest Corridor and the [Ankeniheny-Zahamena Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankeniheny-Zahamena_Corridor).[^cite_note-CIMadagascar-182][^cite_note-EIB-ESMP2016-183]&#x20;

## Government

### Structure

Main articles: [Government of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Madagascar) and [Cabinet of Madagascar](71507495.md)



[Antananarivo](56597.md), the political and economic capital of Madagascar

Madagascar is a [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) [multi-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_\(politics\)) republic, wherein the popularly elected president is the head of state. He selects a [prime minister](30863461.md), who recommends candidates to him to form his cabinet of ministers. According to the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Madagascar), executive power is exercised by the government, whereas legislative power is vested in the ministerial cabinet,[^cite_note-184] the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Madagascar), and the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Madagascar)—although in reality the two latter bodies have very little power or legislative role. The Constitution establishes independent executive, legislative, and judicial branches, and mandates that each president is limited to a maximum of three terms.[^cite_note-BGNote-10] The public directly elects the president and the 151 members of the National Assembly to five-year terms. All 18 members of the Senate also serve five-year terms, with 12 senators elected by local officials and six appointed by the president.[^cite_note-185]&#x20;

At the local level, the island's 23 regions are administered by a governor and regional council. Provinces are further subdivided into regions and communes. The judiciary is modeled on the French system, with a high constitutional court, high court of justice, supreme court, court of appeals, criminal tribunals, and tribunals of first instance.[^cite_note-justiceMada-186] The courts, which adhere to [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)), lack the capacity to quickly and transparently try the cases in the judicial system, which often forces defendants to pass lengthy pretrial detentions in unsanitary and overcrowded prisons.[^cite_note-mediainfo-187]&#x20;

Antananarivo, the administrative capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Madagascar) of Madagascar,[^cite_note-BGNote-10] is located in the highlands region, near the island's geographic center. King Andrianjaka founded Antananarivo as the capital of his [Imerina Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merina_Kingdom) around 1610 or 1625 upon the site of a captured Vazimba capital on the hilltop of [Analamanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_sacred_hills_of_Imerina#Hill_of_Analamanga).[^cite_note-vazimbadjp-188] As Merina dominance expanded over neighboring Malagasy peoples in the early 19th century to establish the Kingdom of Madagascar, Antananarivo became the administrative center for virtually the entire island. In 1896 the French colonizers of Madagascar adopted the Merina capital as their center of colonial administration, and that city remained Madagascar's capital after the country regained independence in 1960. In 2017, the capital's population was estimated at 1,391,433 inhabitants.[^cite_note-189] After Antananarivo, the country's next largest cities are [Antsirabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antsirabe), Toamasina, and [Mahajanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanga).[^cite_note-BGNote-10]&#x20;

### Politics

Main articles: [Politics of Madagascar](50877.md), [Foreign relations of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Madagascar), and [Human rights in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Madagascar)



[Michael Randrianirina](81340595.md), [President of Madagascar](30876601.md) and [President of the Council of the Presidency for the Re-Foundation](81351542.md).

Since Madagascar's independence from France in 1960, the island's political transitions have been marked by numerous popular protests, several disputed elections, an impeachment, two military coups, and an assassination. The country's recurrent political crises have often been prolonged, with detrimental effects on the local economy, international relations, and Malagasy living standards. For example, the eight-month standoff between incumbent Ratsiraka and challenger Ravalomanana following the 2001 presidential elections cost Madagascar millions of dollars in lost tourism and trade revenue as well as damage to infrastructure, such as bombed bridges and buildings damaged by arson.[^cite_note-190] [A series of protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Malagasy_protests) led by Andry Rajoelina against Ravalomanana in early 2009 became violent, with more than 170 people killed.[^cite_note-191]&#x20;

Modern politics in Madagascar are also colored by the history of the Merina subjugation of coastal communities under their rule in the 19th century. The consequent tension between the highland and coastal populations has periodically flared up into isolated events of violence.[^cite_note-ethnicstrife-192]&#x20;

Madagascar has historically been perceived as being on the margin of mainstream African affairs, despite its being a founding member of the [Organization of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_African_Unity), established in 1963 and dissolved in 2002 to be replaced by the [African Union](10634933.md). The country was not permitted to attend the first African Union summit because of a dispute over the results of the 2001 presidential election, but rejoined the Union in July 2003 after a 14-month hiatus.[^cite_note-193] Again it was suspended by the African Union in March 2009, however, following the unconstitutional transfer of executive power to Rajoelina.[^cite_note-194]&#x20;

Madagascar is a member of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) with a bilateral immunity agreement of protection for the [United States military](32212.md).[^cite_note-BGNote-10] Eleven countries have established embassies in Madagascar, including France, the United Kingdom, the United States, China, and India;[^cite_note-195] and Madagascar has [embassies in 16 other countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Madagascar).[^cite_note-MAEMg-Reps-196]&#x20;

Human rights in Madagascar are protected under the Constitution and the state is a signatory to numerous international agreements including the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) and the [Convention on the Rights of the Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_the_Rights_of_the_Child).[^cite_note-DOS-197] Religious, ethnic, and sexual minorities are protected under the law. Freedom of association and assembly are also guaranteed under the law, although in practice the denial of permits for public assembly has occasionally been used to impede political demonstrations.[^cite_note-CrossroadsMarcus-174][^cite_note-DOS-197] Torture by security forces is rare and state repression is low relative to other countries with comparably few legal safeguards, although arbitrary arrests and the corruption of military and police officers remain problems. Ravalomanana's 2004 creation of BIANCO, an anti-corruption bureau, resulted in reduced corruption among Antananarivo's lower-level bureaucrats in particular, although high-level officials have not been prosecuted by the bureau.[^cite_note-CrossroadsMarcus-174] Accusations of media [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship) have risen due to the alleged restrictions on the coverage of government opposition.[^cite_note-198] Some journalists have been arrested for allegedly spreading [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news).[^cite_note-199]&#x20;

### Military and law enforcement

Main articles: [Madagascar Armed Forces](50881.md) and [Law enforcement in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Madagascar)

The rise of centralized kingdoms among the Sakalava, Merina, and other ethnic groups produced the island's first standing armies by the 16th century, initially equipped with spears but later with muskets, cannons, and other firearms.[^cite_note-200] By the early 19th century, the Merina sovereigns of the Kingdom of Madagascar had brought much of the island under their control by mobilizing an army of trained and armed soldiers numbering as high as 30,000.[^cite_note-army-201] French attacks on coastal towns in the later part of the century prompted then-Prime Minister Rainilaiarivony to solicit British assistance to provide training to the Merina monarchy's army. Despite the training and leadership provided by British military advisers, the Malagasy army was unable to withstand French weaponry and was forced to surrender following an attack on the royal palace at Antananarivo. Madagascar was declared a colony of France in 1897.[^cite_note-202]&#x20;

The political independence and sovereignty of the Malagasy armed forces, comprising an army, navy, and air force, was restored with independence from France in 1960.[^cite_note-203] Since this time, the Malagasy military has never engaged in armed conflict with another state or within its own borders, but has occasionally intervened to restore order during periods of political unrest. Under the socialist Second Republic, Admiral Didier Ratsiraka instituted mandatory national armed or civil service for all young citizens regardless of sex, a policy that remained in effect from 1976 to 1991.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205] The armed forces are under the direction of the Minister of Defense and have remained largely neutral during times of political crisis, as during the protracted standoff between incumbent Ratsiraka and challenger Ravalomanana in the disputed 2001 presidential elections, when the military refused to intervene in favor of either candidate. This tradition was broken in 2009, when a segment of the army defected to the side of Rajoelina, then-mayor of Antananarivo, in support of his attempt to force President Ravalomanana from power.[^cite_note-CrossroadsMarcus-174]&#x20;

The Minister of Interior is responsible for the national police force, the paramilitary force (*gendarmerie*), and the secret police.[^cite_note-justiceMada-186] The police and gendarmerie are stationed and administered at the local level; however, in 2009 fewer than a third of all communes had access to the services of these security forces, with most lacking local-level headquarters for either corps.[^cite_note-INSTAT-206] Traditional community tribunals, called *dina*, are presided over by elders and other respected figures and remain a key means by which justice is served in rural areas where state presence is weak. Historically, security has been relatively high across the island.[^cite_note-CrossroadsMarcus-174] Violent crime rates are low, and criminal activities are predominantly crimes of opportunity such as pickpocketing and petty theft, although child prostitution, human trafficking, and production and sale of marijuana and other illegal drugs are increasing.[^cite_note-justiceMada-186] Budget cuts since 2009 had a severe impact on the national police force, producing a steep increase in criminal activity in the following years.[^cite_note-CrossroadsMarcus-174]&#x20;

### United Nations involvement

Madagascar became a [member state](31969.md) of the [United Nations](31769.md) on 20 September 1960, shortly after gaining its independence on 26 June 1960.[^cite_note-208] As of January 2017, 34 police officers from Madagascar were deployed in Haiti as part of the [United Nations Stabilization Mission in Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Stabilization_Mission_in_Haiti).[^cite_note-209]&#x20;

In 2015, the United Nations (UN) [World Food Programme](33582.md) started the Madagascar Country Programme with the two main goals of long-term development and reconstruction efforts and addressing of food insecurity issues in the country's southern regions[^cite_note-WFP-2015-210] by providing meals for specific schools in rural and urban priority areas and developing national school feeding policies to increase consistency of nourishment. Small and local farmers have also been assisted in increasing both the quantity and quality of their production, as well as improvement of their crop yield in unfavorable weather conditions.[^cite_note-WFP-2015-210]&#x20;

In 2017, Madagascar signed the UN [Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-211]&#x20;

## Economy

Main articles: [Economy of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Madagascar) and [Tourism in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Madagascar)



Historical change in per capita GDP of Madagascar since 1950



Nosy Iranja, one of the international tourism destinations in Madagascar

Madagascar's GDP in 2015 was estimated at US\$9.98 billion, with a per-capita GDP of \$411.82.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213] Approximately 69% of the population lived below the national poverty line threshold of one dollar per day;[^cite_note-214] and according to the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme), 68.4% of the population was [multidimensionally poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_Poverty_Index) as of 2021.[^cite_note-UNDP-2023-215]&#x20;

During 2011–15, the country's average growth rate was 2.6% but expected to have reached 4.1% in 2016, due to public works programs and growth of the service sector.[^cite_note-216] As of 2011, the agriculture sector constituted 29% of the GDP, with manufacturing at 15%. Madagascar's other sources of growth have been tourism, agriculture, and the extractive industries.[^cite_note-AEO2011-217] The fishing sector represents US\$800 million or 6% of GNP and directly employs 200,000 jobs.[^cite_note-218]&#x20;

Tourism focuses on the niche ecotourism market, capitalizing on Madagascar's unique biodiversity, unspoiled natural habitats, national parks, and lemur species.[^cite_note-tourismsector-219] An estimated 365,000 tourists visited Madagascar in 2008, but the sector declined during the political crisis in 2010 with only 180,000 tourists visiting.[^cite_note-AEO2011-217] It then grew steadily for a few years. In 2016, 293,000 tourists came to the island, representing an increase of 20% compared to 2015. The country had the goal of drawing 366,000 visitors in 2017 and 500,000 in 2018.[^cite_note-220]&#x20;

Madagascar was still a very poor country in 2018 and structural brakes remained on its development: corruption and the shackles of the public administration, lack of legal certainty, and backward land legislation. The economy, however, had grown since 2011, with GDP growth exceeding 4% per year.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] Almost all economic indicators were also growing: the GDP per capita was around \$1,600 (PPP) for 2017,[^cite_note-223] one of the lowest in the world, although growing since 2012; unemployment was also cut, which in 2016 was equal to 2.1%[^cite_note-224] with a work force of 13.4 million as of 2017.[^cite_note-225]&#x20;

In 2017, poverty affected 92% of Madagascar's population. The country ranked fourth globally in terms of chronic [malnutrition](258979.md), with nearly one in two children under the age of five experiencing stunted growth. Additionally, it was among the five countries where access to clean water was most limited. According to the NGO WaterAid, in 2021 approximately 12 million people lacked access to safe drinking water.[^cite_note-226]&#x20;

As of 2025, poverty affected approximately 80% of Madagascar's population, based on a daily income threshold of \$2.15 (2017 PPP),[^cite_note-227] and nearly 1.94 million people were projected to experience high levels of acute food insecurity during the lean season.[^cite_note-228] Access to clean water is a critical issue, as Madagascar remains a water-stressed country, with climate shocks and infrastructure gaps exacerbating the crisis.[^cite_note-229]&#x20;

### Natural resources and trade



Toy animals made from [raffia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffia_palm), a native palm

[^cite_note-230]

Madagascar's [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource) include a variety of [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) products. Agriculture (including the growing of [raffia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffia)), [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing), and [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry) are mainstays of the economy. In 2017 the top exports were vanilla (US\$894 million), [nickel metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) (US\$414 million), cloves (US\$288 million), knitted sweaters (US\$184 million, and [cobalt](24580536.md) (US\$143 million).[^cite_note-231]&#x20;

Madagascar is the world's principal supplier of vanilla, cloves,[^cite_note-232] and [ylang-ylang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cananga_odorata#Ylang-ylang_essential_oil).[^cite_note-gafilo-133] The island supplies 80% of the world's natural vanilla.[^cite_note-233] Other key agricultural resources include coffee, [lychees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychee), and [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp_and_prawn_as_food). Key mineral resources include various types of precious and semi-precious stones, and as of 2001 it provided half of the world's supply of [sapphires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire), which was discovered near [Ilakaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilakaka) in the late 1990s.[^cite_note-234]&#x20;

Madagascar has one of the world's largest reserves of [ilmenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilmenite) (titanium ore), as well as important reserves of [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite), coal, iron, cobalt, copper, and nickel.[^cite_note-EBLand-130] Several major projects are underway in the mining, oil, and gas sectors that are anticipated to give a significant boost to the Malagasy economy. These include such projects as ilmenite and [zircon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zircon) mining at the [Mandena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandena) mine by [Rio Tinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_Group),[^cite_note-RioTinto-235] extraction of nickel at the [Ambatovy mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambatovy_mine) near [Moramanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moramanga) and its processing near Toamasina by [Sherritt International](4516089.md),[^cite_note-236] and development of the giant onshore [heavy oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_crude_oil) deposits at [Tsimiroro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsimiroro) and [Bemolanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemolanga) by [Madagascar Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_Oil).[^cite_note-237]&#x20;

Exports formed 28% of the GDP in 2009.[^cite_note-BGNote-10] Most of the country's export revenue is derived from the textiles industry, fish and shellfish, vanilla, cloves, and other [foodstuffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food).[^cite_note-AEO2011-217] France is the nation's main trading partner, although the United States, Japan, and Germany also have strong economic ties with Madagascar.[^cite_note-EBLand-130] High-value cash crops for export such as lychees are more recent growth areas, with 18,000 tons sold abroad in 2023, of which 16,000 tons were exported to Europe.[^cite_note-238]&#x20;

The Madagascar-US Business Council was formed in May 2003 as a collaboration between the [US Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) and Malagasy artisan producers to support the export of local handicrafts to foreign markets.[^cite_note-handicrafts-239] Imports of items such as foodstuffs, fuel, vehicles, electronics, [capital goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(economics\)), and [consumer goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_good) consume an estimated 52% of the GDP. Madagascar obtains most of its imports from [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Madagascar_relations),[^cite_note-240] [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France%E2%80%93Madagascar_relations), Iran, Mauritius, and Hong Kong.[^cite_note-BGNote-10]&#x20;

### Infrastructure and media

Main articles: [Transport in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Madagascar), [Telecommunications in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Madagascar), and [List of newspapers in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Madagascar)



News stand in [Antananarivo](56597.md)

In 2010, Madagascar had approximately 7,617 km (4,730 mi) of paved [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road), 854 km (530 mi) of [railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport), and 432 km (270 mi) of navigable [waterways](174851.md).[^cite_note-Bradtp2-18] The majority of [roads in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_roads_in_Madagascar#National_roads) are unpaved, with many impassable in the rainy season. Largely paved [national routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_routes_of_Madagascar) connect the six largest regional towns to Antananarivo, with minor paved and unpaved routes providing access to other population centers in each district.[^cite_note-LOC-131] As of 2023, the [World Bank](36752.md) estimated that 17 million people in Madagascar's rural areas live more than 2 km away from an all-season road.[^cite_note-241]&#x20;

Construction of the [Antananarivo–Toamasina toll highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AR1_highway), the country's first [toll highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_highway), began in December 2022. The approximately US\$1 billion infrastructure project, which would connect Madagascar's capital to its largest seaport, was expected to take four years to complete.[^cite_note-Africanews-2022-242] Another project to create 348 km (216 mi) of roads and create better connections cost €235.5 million, including a €116 million grant from the [European Union](9317.md), a €110 million loan from the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank), and €4.8 million in finance from the Republic of Madagascar.[^cite_note-EIB-2023-243][^cite_note-244]&#x20;

There are several [rail lines in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_stations_in_Madagascar#Cities_served_by_rail). Antananarivo is connected to Toamasina, [Ambatondrazaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambatondrazaka), and Antsirabe by rail, and another rail line connects Fianarantsoa to [Manakara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manakara). In early 2026 a new passenger train service was launched in Antananarivo, to ease traffic in the capital.[^cite_note-Railway_Gazette-2026-245]&#x20;

The most important [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port#Seaport) in the country is located on the east coast at Toamasina. Ports at [Mahajanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanga) and [Antsiranana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antsiranana) are significantly less used because of their remoteness.[^cite_note-LOC-131] Madagascar's government hopes to expand the ports of Antsiranana in the north and [Taolagnaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort-Dauphin_\(Madagascar\)) in the south to connect them to improved road networks, as many imports are everyday necessities and the country also relies on export money.**[^cite_note-EIB-2023-243]**[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247] The island's newest port at Ehoala, constructed in 2008 and privately managed by [Rio Tinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_\(corporation\)), will come under state control upon completion of the company's mining project near [Tôlanaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B4lanaro) around 2038.[^cite_note-RioTinto-235]&#x20;

Madagascar Airlines services the island's many small regional [airports in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Madagascar), which offer the only practical means of access to many of the more remote regions during rainy season road washouts.[^cite_note-LOC-131]&#x20;



[Oxcarts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullock_cart) are an important medium of transport in many places, like [Ambatolampy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambatolampy).

[Running water](684928.md) and [electricity](9550.md) are supplied at the national level by a government service provider, [Jirama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirama), which is unable to service the entire population. As of 2009, only 6.8% of Madagascar's *fokontany* had access to water provided by Jirama and 9.5% had access to its electricity services.[^cite_note-INSTAT-206] Fifty-six percent of Madagascar's power is provided by [hydroelectric](381399.md) power plants, with the remaining 44% provided by [diesel engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine) generators.[^cite_note-248]&#x20;

Mobile telephone and Internet access are widespread in urban areas but remain limited in rural parts of the island. Approximately 30% of the districts are able to access the nation's several private telecommunications networks via mobile (cell) telephones or landlines.[^cite_note-INSTAT-206] As of 2023, 11% of the rural population had access to power.[^cite_note-249]&#x20;

Radio broadcasting remains the principal means by which the population accesses international, national, and local news, but only state radio broadcasts are transmitted across the entire island. Hundreds of public and private stations with local or regional range, however, provide alternatives to state broadcasting,[^cite_note-mediainfo-187] providing local and international programming throughout the country. Several [media outlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_outlets) are owned by political partisans or politicians themselves, including the media groups MBS (owned by Ravalomanana) and Viva (owned by Rajoelina),[^cite_note-CrossroadsMarcus-174] which contributes to political polarization in reporting. Madagascar's media have historically come under varying degrees of pressure to censor their criticism of the government, with reporters occasionally threatened or harassed and media outlets periodically forced to close.[^cite_note-mediainfo-187] Accusations of media censorship have increased since 2009 because of alleged intensification of restrictions on political criticism.[^cite_note-DOS-197]&#x20;

Internet access has grown dramatically in the 21st century. In December 2011, an estimated 352,000 Madagascar residents had access from home or in one of the nation's many Internet cafés ;[^cite_note-mediainfo-187] but by January 2022, 22.3% of the population (6.43 million people) had access, mostly through mobile phones.[^cite_note-250]&#x20;

## Demographics

Main articles: [Demographics of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Madagascar) and [List of cities in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Madagascar)



Ethnolinguistic map of Madagascar

Only two general censuses, in 1975 and 1993, have been carried out after independence. The most densely populated regions of the island are the Eastern Highlands and the Eastern Coast, contrasting most dramatically with the sparsely populated Western Plains.[^cite_note-LOC-131] [Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) has long influenced settlement on the island. Almost 60% of the nation's population live in rural areas.[^cite_note-251]&#x20;

In 2024, the population of Madagascar was estimated at 32 million, up from 2.2 million in 1900,[^cite_note-Division-2024-252][^cite_note-LOC-131] and the annual population growth rate approximately 2.4%.[^cite_note-Division-2024-252] As of 2024, approximately 39.3% of the population was younger than 15 years of age, 57.3% were between the ages of 15 and 64, and 3.4% were aged 65 and older.[^cite_note-Division-2024-252]&#x20;

### Ethnic groups

Main article: [Demographics of Madagascar § Ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Madagascar#Ethnicity)



[Antanosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antanosy) girl with dark blond hair

The [Malagasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_peoples) ethnic group forms over 90% of Madagascar's population and is typically divided into 18 ethnic subgroups.[^cite_note-BGNote-10] Recent [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) research revealed that the genetic makeup of the average Malagasy person constitutes an approximately equal blend of [Southeast Asian](28741.md) and [East African](260987.md) genes,[^cite_note-253][^cite_note-Tofanelli-254][^cite_note-Cox-255] although the genetics of some communities show a predominance of Southeast Asian or East African origins or some Arab, Indian, or European ancestry.[^cite_note-Adelaar-256]&#x20;

Southeast Asian features—specifically from the southern part of Borneo—are most predominant among the Merina of the Central Highlands,[^cite_note-ethnicstrife-192] who form the largest Malagasy ethnic subgroup at approximately 26% of the population, whereas certain communities among the Western Coastal peoples (collectively called *côtiers*) have relatively stronger East African features. The largest coastal ethnic subgroups are the [Betsimisaraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsimisaraka_people) (14.9%) and the [Tsimihety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsimihety) and [Sakalava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakalava) (6% each).[^cite_note-LOC-131]&#x20;

### Languages

Main articles: [Malagasy language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_language) and [Languages of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Madagascar)



Sorabe Malagasy Arabic script

The Constitution of 2007 recognized three official languages: Malagasy, French, and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^cite_note-259] A fourth Constitution, adopted in 2010 following [a constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Malagasy_constitutional_referendum),[^cite_note-4thRepublic-1] recognized only Malagasy and French.&#x20;

The Malagasy language is of [Malayo-Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayo-Polynesian_languages) origin and is generally spoken throughout the island. The numerous dialects of Malagasy, which are generally mutually intelligible,[^cite_note-260] can be clustered under three subgroups: Central-Eastern Malagasic, Northern Malagasic, and Southern Malagasic.[^cite_note-261]&#x20;

The Malagasy language derives from the Southeast [Barito languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barito_languages), with the [Maʼanyan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%CA%BCanyan_language) being its closest relative, incorporating numerous [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) and [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) loanwords.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263]&#x20;

French became the official language during the colonial period, when Madagascar came under the authority of France. In the first national Constitution of 1958, Malagasy and French were named the official languages of the Malagasy Republic. Madagascar is a [francophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophonie) country, and French is mostly spoken as a second language among the educated population and used for international communication.[^cite_note-LOC-131] Among the upper class in large cities, French is spoken as a native language.[^cite_note-264]&#x20;

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Madagascar)



[Our Lady of La Salette Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_La_Salette_Cathedral,_Antsirabe) in [Antsirabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antsirabe)



Faravohitra Catholic church in [Antananarivo](56597.md)

Christianity is the most widely professed religion in Madagascar. It is predominant in the Highlands.[^cite_note-\:0-265]&#x20;

According to the most recent national census completed in 1993, a majority of the population (52%) adhered to Indigenous beliefs, with Christianity the largest single religion at 41%, followed by Islam at 7%.[^cite_note-\:0-265] According to the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) in 2010, however, 85% of the population identified as [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Madagascar), just 4.5% exclusively practiced folk religions; Protestants comprise a plurality of Christians, followed by Catholics.[^cite_note-pew2-266] In contrast, the [Association of Religion Data Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Religion_Data_Archives), citing the [World Religion Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=World_Religion_Database\&action=edit\&redlink=1), estimates that in 2025 58.1% of the population was Christian, 2.1% Muslim, 39.2% of traditional faiths, and 0.6% [nonreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) or adhering to other faiths.[^cite_note-National_Profiles2-267]&#x20;

The inconsistency in religious data reflects the common practice of alternating between religious identities or [syncretizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism) different faith traditions. Christians integrate and combine their religious beliefs with the deeply rooted practice of honoring ancestors. For instance, they may bless their dead at church before proceeding with traditional burial rites or invite a [Christian minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_\(Christianity\)) to consecrate a *famadihana* reburial.[^cite_note-famadihana2-268]&#x20;

[Veneration of ancestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_the_dead) has led to the widespread tradition of tomb-building as well as the Highlands practice of the *[famadihana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famadihana)*, whereby a deceased family member's remains are exhumed and re-wrapped in fresh silk shrouds, before being replaced in the tomb. The *famadihana* is an occasion to celebrate the beloved ancestor's memory, reunite with family and community, and enjoy a festive atmosphere. Residents of surrounding villages are often invited to attend the party, where food and rum are typically served, and a *[hiragasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragasy)* troupe or other musical entertainment is commonly present.[^cite_note-famadihana22-269]&#x20;

Consideration for ancestors is also demonstrated through adherence to *[fady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fady_\(taboo\))*, taboos that are respected during and after the lifetime of the person who establishes them. It is widely believed that by showing respect for ancestors in these ways, they may intervene on behalf of the living. Conversely, misfortunes are often attributed to ancestors whose memory or wishes have been neglected. The sacrifice of zebu is a traditional method used to appease or honor the ancestors. In addition, the Malagasy traditionally believe in a creator god, called [Zanahary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanahary) or Andriamanitra.[^cite_note-Bradtbeliefs2-270]&#x20;

The Malagasy Council of Churches comprises the four oldest and most prominent [Christian denominations of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Madagascar) ([Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Madagascar), [Church of Jesus Christ in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Jesus_Christ_in_Madagascar), [Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_Lutheran_Church), and [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Province_of_the_Indian_Ocean)) and has been influential in Malagasy politics.[^cite_note-USSD-271]&#x20;

Islam was first brought to Madagascar in the Middle Ages by Arab and Somali Muslim traders, who established several Islamic schools along the Eastern Coast. Whereas the use of Arabic script and loan words and the adoption of Islamic astrology would spread across the island, Islam took hold in only a handful of Southeastern Coastal communities. They are largely concentrated in the northwestern provinces of [Mahajanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanga) and [Antsiranana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antsiranana). Muslims are divided between ethnic Malagasy and Indians, Pakistanis and Comorans.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-AMNE2-273]&#x20;

Hinduism was introduced to Madagascar through [Gujarati people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_people) immigrating from the [Saurashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurashtra_\(region\)) region of India in the late 19th century. Most Hindus in Madagascar speak Gujarati or Hindi at home, reflecting the faith's concentration among those of Indian ancestry.[^cite_note-id2-274]&#x20;

Rabbinic Judaism emerged on the island in the 21st century, as the common belief in a myth of Jewish origin for the Malagasy peoples inspired [Messianic Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messianic_Judaism) in Antananarivo to begin researching Judaism and studying the [Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah). In 2016, 121 members of the [Malagasy Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism_in_Madagascar) were formally converted to Orthodox Judaism.[^cite_note-275]&#x20;

### Health

Main article: [Healthcare in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Madagascar)

[Medical centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinic), [dispensaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispensary), and [hospitals](18994221.md) are found throughout the island, although concentrated in urban areas and particularly in Antananarivo. Access to medical care remains beyond the reach of many Malagasy, especially in the rural areas, and many people have recourse to traditional healers.[^cite_note-276]&#x20;

In addition to the high expense of medical care relative to the average Malagasy income, the prevalence of trained medical professionals remained extremely low. In 2010, Madagascar had a total of 3,150 doctors, 5,661 nurses, 385 community health workers, 175 pharmacists, and 57 dentists for a population of 22 million, and an average of three hospital beds per 10,000 people. Fifteen percent of government spending was directed toward the health sector in 2008. As of 2011, approximately 70% of spending on health was contributed by the government and 30% by international donors and other private sources.[^cite_note-WHO2011-277] The government also provided one basic health center per commune. Private health centers are concentrated within urban areas, particularly those of the Central Highlands.[^cite_note-INSTAT-206]&#x20;

Despite these barriers to access, health services have shown a trend toward improvement over the past 20 years. Child immunizations against such diseases as [hepatitis B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_B), [diphtheria](58937.md), and [measles](58911.md) increased an average of 60% during this period, indicating low but increasing availability of basic medical services and treatment.[^cite_note-278]&#x20;

The Malagasy fertility rate in 2009 was 4.6 children per woman, declining from 6.3 in 1990. Teen pregnancy rates of 14.8% in 2011, much higher than the African average, were a contributing factor to rapid population growth.[^cite_note-WHO2011-277] In 2010, the maternal mortality rate was 440 per 100,000 births, compared to 373.1 in 2008 and 484.4 in 1990, representing a decline in perinatal care following the 2009 coup. The infant mortality rate in 2011 was 41 per 1,000 births,[^cite_note-BGNote-10] with an under-five mortality rate at 61 per 1,000 births.[^cite_note-SOWMY-279] [Schistosomiasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schistosomiasis), [malaria](20423.md), and sexually transmitted diseases are common in Madagascar, although as of 2011 the [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS) infection rate remained low relative to many countries in mainland Africa, at 0.2% of the adult population; and the malaria mortality rate was also among Africa's lowest at 8.5 deaths per 100,000 people, in part because of the highest frequency use of insecticide-treated nets in Africa.[^cite_note-WHO2011-277] Adult life expectancy as of 2009 was 63 years for men and 67 years for women.&#x20;

Madagascar had [outbreaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st-century_Madagascar_plague_outbreaks) of the [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) and [pneumonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonic_plague) in 2017 (2,575 cases, 221 deaths) and earlier in 2014 (263 confirmed cases, 71 deaths).[^cite_note-280] In 2019, Madagascar had [a measles outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Madagascar_measles_outbreak), resulting in 118,000 cases and 1,688 deaths and was also affected the next year by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Madagascar). Undernourishment and hunger rates were at 42% in 2018.[^cite_note-281] According to the [United Nations](31769.md), more than one million people in southern Madagascar in 2021 were struggling to get enough to eat,[^cite_note-282] due to what could become the country's [first famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%93present_Madagascar_famine) caused by [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Madagascar).[^cite_note-283] [UNICEF](19698110.md) has supported an initiative in the country assisting parents to recognise the signs of severe malnutrition among their children.[^cite_note-284]&#x20;

### Education

Main article: [Education in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Madagascar)

Students working in groups in classroom as teacher observes

[Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Madagascar) access and quality were prioritized under [Ravalomanana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Ravalomanana).

Prior to the 19th century, all education in Madagascar was informal and typically served to teach practical skills as well as social and cultural values, including respect for ancestors and elders.[^cite_note-LOC-131] The first formal European-style school was established in 1818 at Toamasina by members of the London Missionary Society (LMS). The LMS was invited by King Radama I to expand its schools throughout Imerina to teach basic literacy and numeracy to aristocratic children. The schools were closed by Ranavalona I in 1835,[^cite_note-Ralibera-285] but reopened and expanded in the decades after her death.&#x20;

By the end of the 19th century, Madagascar had the most developed and modern school system in pre-colonial Sub-Saharan Africa. Access to schooling was expanded in coastal areas during the colonial period, with French language and basic work skills becoming the focus of the curriculum. During the post-colonial First Republic, a continued reliance on French nationals as teachers, and French as the language of instruction, displeased those desiring a complete separation from the former colonial power.[^cite_note-LOC-131] Consequently, under the socialist Second Republic, French instructors and other nationals were expelled, Malagasy was declared the language of instruction, and a large cadre of young Malagasy were rapidly trained to teach at remote rural schools under the mandatory two-year national service policy.[^cite_note-286]&#x20;

This policy, known as *malgachization* (Malagasyization), coincided with a severe economic downturn and a dramatic decline in the quality of education. Those schooled during this period generally failed to master the French language or many other subjects and struggled to find employment, forcing many to take low-paying jobs in the informal or black market that mired them in deepening poverty. Excepting Zafy's brief presidency from 1992 to 1996, Ratsiraka remained in power from 1975 to 2001 and failed to achieve significant improvements in education throughout his tenure.[^cite_note-287]&#x20;

Education was prioritized under the Ravalomanana administration (2002–09), free and compulsory from ages 6 to 13.[^cite_note-WBeducation-288] The primary schooling cycle was five years, followed by four at the lower secondary level and three at the upper secondary level.[^cite_note-LOC-131] During Ravalomanana's first term, thousands of new primary schools and additional classrooms were constructed, older buildings renovated, and tens of thousands of new primary teachers recruited and trained. Primary school fees were eliminated, and kits containing basic school supplies distributed to primary students.[^cite_note-WBeducation-288]&#x20;

Government school construction initiatives have ensured at least one primary school per *fokontany* and one lower secondary school within each commune. At least one upper secondary school is located in each of the larger urban centers.[^cite_note-INSTAT-206] The three branches of the national public university are located at Antananarivo, Mahajanga, and Fianarantsoa. These are complemented by public teacher-training colleges and several private universities and technical colleges.[^cite_note-LOC-131]&#x20;

As a result of increased educational access, enrollment rates more than doubled between 1996 and 2006. However, education quality was weak, producing high rates of grade repetition and dropout.[^cite_note-WBeducation-288] Education policy in Ravalomanana's second term focused on quality issues, including an increase in minimum education standards for the recruitment of primary teachers from a middle-school-leaving certificate (BEPC) to a high-school-leaving certificate (BAC), and a reformed teacher training program to support the transition from traditional didactic instruction to student-centered teaching methods to boost student learning and classroom participation.[^cite_note-289] Public expenditure on education was 2.8% of GDP in 2014. The literacy rate was estimated at 75% in 2021.[^cite_note-290]&#x20;

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Madagascar)



Performance of [Antandroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antandroy) dance

All the many ethnic subgroups in Madagascar adhere to their own set of beliefs, practices, and ways of life that have historically contributed to their unique identities. However, a number of core cultural features are common throughout the island, creating a strongly unified Malagasy cultural identity. In addition to a common language and shared traditional religious beliefs around a creator god and veneration of ancestors, the traditional Malagasy worldview is shaped by values that emphasize *fihavanana* (solidarity), *vintana* (destiny), *tody* (karma), and *[hasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasina_\(Madagascar\))*, a sacred life force that traditional communities believe imbues and thereby legitimates authority figures within the community or family. Other cultural elements commonly found throughout the island include the practice of male circumcision; strong kinship ties; widespread belief in the power of magic, diviners, astrology and witch doctors; and a traditional division of social classes into nobles, commoners, and slaves.[^cite_note-LOC-131][^cite_note-Bradtbeliefs-291]&#x20;

Although social castes are no longer legally recognized, ancestral caste affiliation often continues to affect social status, economic opportunity, and roles within the community.[^cite_note-292] Malagasy people traditionally consult *Mpanandro* ("Makers of the Days") to identify the most auspicious days for important events such as weddings or *famadihana*, according to a traditional astrological system introduced by Arabs. Similarly, the nobles of many Malagasy communities in the pre-colonial period would commonly employ advisers known as the *ombiasy* (from *olona-be-hasina*, "man of much virtue") of the southeastern Antemoro ethnic group, who trace their ancestry back to early Somali settlers.[^cite_note-293]&#x20;

The diverse origins of Malagasy culture are evident in its tangible expressions. The most emblematic instrument of Madagascar, the *[valiha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valiha)*, is a [bamboo](39029.md) [tube zither](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tube_zither) carried to the country by early settlers from southern Borneo, very similar in form to those found in Indonesia and the Philippines today.[^cite_note-Blench,_R._1982_pp._81-93-294] Traditional [houses in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Madagascar) are likewise similar to those of southern Borneo in terms of symbolism and construction, featuring a rectangular layout with a peaked roof and central support pillar.[^cite_note-295] Reflecting widespread veneration of the ancestors, tombs are culturally significant in many regions and tend to be built of more durable material, typically stone, and display more elaborate decoration than the houses of the living.[^cite_note-Acquier,_Jean-Louis.-296] The production and weaving of silk can be traced back to the island's earliest settlers, and Madagascar's national dress, the woven *[lamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamba_\(garment\))*, has evolved into a varied and refined art.[^cite_note-297]&#x20;

[Southeast Asian cultural influence](28741.md#Culture) is also evident in [Malagasy cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_cuisine), in which [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is consumed at every meal, typically accompanied by one of a variety of flavorful [vegetable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable) or [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat) dishes.[^cite_note-Antal-298] African influence is reflected in the sacred importance of zebu cattle and their embodiment of their owner's wealth, traditions originating on the African mainland. [Cattle rustling](1553326.md), originally a rite of passage for young men in the plains areas of Madagascar where the largest herds of cattle are, has become a dangerous and sometimes deadly criminal enterprise, as herdsmen in the Southwest attempt to defend their cattle with traditional spears against increasingly armed professional rustlers.[^cite_note-Campbell-1993-50]&#x20;

### Arts

Main article: [Music of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Madagascar)



[Hiragasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragasy) dancer

A wide variety of oral and written [literature of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_of_Madagascar) has developed. One of its foremost artistic traditions is its oratory, as expressed in the forms of *[hainteny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainteny)* (poetry), *kabary* (public discourse), and *ohabolana* (proverbs).[^cite_note-299][^cite_note-Madatana-300] An epic poem exemplifying these traditions, the *[Ibonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibonia)*, has been handed down over the centuries in several different forms across the island, and offers insight into the diverse mythologies and beliefs of traditional Malagasy communities.[^cite_note-301] This tradition was continued in the 20th century by such artists as [Jean-Joseph Rabearivelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Joseph_Rabearivelo), who is considered Africa's first modern poet,[^cite_note-302] and [Elie Rajaonarison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Rajaonarison), an exemplar of the new wave of Malagasy poetry.[^cite_note-303]&#x20;

Madagascar has also developed a rich [musical heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Madagascar), embodied in dozens of regional musical genres such as the coastal *[salegy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salegy)* or highland *[hiragasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragasy)* that enliven village gatherings, local dance floors, and national airwaves.[^cite_note-Randrianary-304] Madagascar also has a growing culture of classical music fostered through youth academies, organizations, and orchestras that promote youth involvement in this type of music.[^cite_note-305]&#x20;

The plastic arts are also widespread throughout the island. In addition to the tradition of silk weaving and lamba production, the weaving of raffia, and other local plant materials has been used to create a wide array of practical items such as floor mats, baskets, purses, and hats.[^cite_note-handicrafts-239] Wood carving is a highly developed art form, with distinct regional styles evident in the decoration of balcony railings and other architectural elements. Sculptors create a variety of furniture and household goods, *aloalo* funerary posts, and wooden sculptures, many of which are produced for the tourist market.[^cite_note-Terrible-306] The decorative and functional woodworking traditions of the [Zafimaniry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafimaniry) people of the Central Highlands was inscribed on [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s [Intangible Cultural Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists) in 2008.[^cite_note-307]&#x20;

Among the [Antemoro people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antemoro_people), the production of paper embedded with flowers and other decorative natural materials is a long-established tradition that the community has begun to market to ecotourists.[^cite_note-Terrible-306] Embroidery and [drawn thread work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawn_thread_work) are done by hand to produce clothing, as well as tablecloths and other home textiles for sale in local crafts markets.[^cite_note-handicrafts-239] Malagasy artists such as [Madame Zo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Zo) have incorporated textile traditions of Madagascar directly into their work.[^cite_note-Artnet_News-308] A small but growing number of fine art galleries in Antananarivo and several other urban areas offer paintings by local artists, and annual art events, such as the Hosotra open-air exhibition in the capital, contribute to the continuing development of fine arts in Madagascar.[^cite_note-309]&#x20;

### Sport and recreational activities



*[Moraingy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraingy)* is a traditional martial art of Madagascar.

A number of traditional pastimes have emerged in Madagascar. *[Moraingy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraingy)*, a type of [hand-to-hand combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand-to-hand_combat), is a popular spectator sport in coastal regions. Although it is traditionally practiced by men, women have recently begun to participate.[^cite_note-310] *Savika* or *[tolon-omby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolon-omby)*, the wrestling of zebu cattle, is also practiced in many regions.[^cite_note-lambatradition-311]&#x20;

In addition to sports, a wide variety of games are played. Among the most emblematic is *[fanorona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanorona)*, a [board game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_game) widespread throughout the Highland regions. According to folk legend, the succession of King Andrianjaka after his father Ralambo was partially the result of the obsession that Andrianjaka's older brother may have had with playing *fanorona* to the detriment of his other responsibilities.[^cite_note-iarivo-312]&#x20;

Western recreational activities were introduced to Madagascar over the past two centuries. [Rugby union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_Madagascar) is considered the national sport of Madagascar.[^cite_note-313] [Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Madagascar) is also popular. Madagascar has produced a world champion in *[pétanque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9tanque)*, a French game similar to [lawn bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowls), which is widely played in urban areas and throughout the Highlands.[^cite_note-314] School athletics programs typically include soccer, [track and field](194904.md), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [boxing](4243.md), women's basketball, and women's tennis. Madagascar sent its first competitors to the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics) in 1964, and has also competed in the [African Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Games).[^cite_note-EBLand-130] [Scouting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouting) is represented in Madagascar by [its own local federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firaisan%27ny_Skotisma_eto_Madagasikara) consisting of three Scouting clubs. Membership in 2011 was estimated at 14,905.[^cite_note-Census_2010-315]&#x20;

Because of its advanced sports facilities, Antananarivo gained the hosting rights for several of Africa's top international basketball events, including the [2011 FIBA Africa Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIBA_Africa_Championship),[^cite_note-316] the [2009 FIBA Africa Championship for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIBA_Africa_Championship_for_Women),[^cite_note-317] the [2013 FIBA Africa Under-16 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIBA_Africa_Under-16_Championship),[^cite_note-318] the [2014 FIBA Africa Under-18 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_Africa_Under-18_Championship),[^cite_note-319] and the [2015 FIBA Africa Under-16 Championship for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIBA_Africa_Under-16_Championship_for_Women).[^cite_note-320] At the [2019 African Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3x3_basketball_at_the_2019_African_Games), [Madagascar's national 3x3 basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar%27s_national_3x3_basketball_team) won the gold medal.[^cite_note-321]&#x20;

### Cuisine

Main article: [Malagasy cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_cuisine)

Malagasy cuisine reflects the diverse influences of [Southeast Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_cuisines#Southeast_Asian_cuisine), [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_cuisine), [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanian_cuisine), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine), and [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_cuisine) culinary traditions. The complexity of Malagasy meals can range from the simple traditional preparations introduced by the earliest settlers to the refined festival dishes prepared for the island's 19th-century monarchs.&#x20;

Throughout almost the entire country, Madagascar's contemporary cuisine typically consists of a base of rice (*vary*) served with an accompaniment (*laoka*). The many varieties of *laoka* may be vegetarian or include animal proteins, typically featuring a sauce flavored with such ingredients as ginger, onion, garlic, tomato, vanilla, coconut milk, salt, curry powder, and green peppercorns, or less commonly, other spices or herbs. In parts of the arid south and west, pastoral families may replace rice with [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), or [curds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curd) made from fermented zebu milk. A wide variety of sweet and savory [fritters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritter) as well as other street foods are available across the island, as are diverse tropical and temperate-climate fruits.&#x20;

Locally produced beverages include fruit juices, coffee, herbal teas, and alcoholic drinks such as rum, wine, and beer.[^cite_note-Antal-298] [Three Horses Beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Horses_Beer) is the most popular beer on the island[^cite_note-THBpilsner-322] and is considered emblematic of Madagascar.[^cite_note-promos-323]&#x20;
