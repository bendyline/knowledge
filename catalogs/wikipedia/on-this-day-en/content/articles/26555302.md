The **governor of Alaska** ([Iñupiaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C3%B1upiaq_language): *Alaaskam kavanaa*) is the [head of government](37570.md) of [Alaska](624.md). The governor is the chief executive of the state and is the holder of the highest office in the executive branch of the government as well as being the commander in chief of the [Alaska's state forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Department_of_Military_and_Veterans_Affairs).

Twelve people have served as governor of the State of Alaska over 14 distinct terms, though Alaska had over 30 civilian and military governors during its long history as a [United States territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_territory). Only two governors, [William A. Egan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_A._Egan) and [Bill Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Walker_\(American_politician\)), were born in Alaska. Two people, Egan and [Wally Hickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Hickel), have been elected to multiple non-consecutive terms as governor. Hickel is also noted for a rare [third party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_party_\(United_States\)) win in American politics, having been elected to a term in 1990 representing the [Alaskan Independence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaskan_Independence_Party). The longest-serving governor of the state was Egan, who was elected three times and served nearly 12 years. The longest-serving territorial governor was [Ernest Gruening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Gruening), who served over 13 years.

The current governor is Republican [Mike Dunleavy](40933016.md), who took office on December 3, 2018.

## Governors before statehood

Alaska was [purchased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Purchase) by the United States from the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) in 1867, with formal transfer occurring on October 18, 1867,[^cite_note-2] which is now celebrated as [Alaska Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Day). Before then, it was known as [Russian America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_America) or Russian Alaska, controlled by the [governors and general managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-American_Company#Chief_managers) of the [Russian-American Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-American_Company).

The vast region was initially designated the [Department of Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Alaska), under the jurisdiction of the [Department of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War) until 1877, when the Army was withdrawn from Alaska. The [Department of the Treasury](53667.md) then took control, with the Collector of Customs as the highest ranking federal official in the territory. In 1879, the [Navy](20518076.md) was given jurisdiction over the department.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGates200786-3]

Some believe the first American administrator of Alaska was [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) immigrant [Włodzimierz Krzyżanowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82odzimierz_Krzy%C5%BCanowski). However, the *[Anchorage Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchorage_Daily_News)* was unable to find any conclusive information to support this claim.[^cite_note-4]

## Governors of the State of Alaska

Alaska was [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) on January 3, 1959.

The [state constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Alaska) provides for the election of a governor and [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Alaska) every four years on the same [ticket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticket_\(election\)), with their terms commencing on the first Monday in the December following the election. Governors are allowed to succeed themselves once, having to wait four years after their second term in a row before being allowed to run again. Should the office of governor become vacant, the lieutenant governor assumes the office of governor. The original constitution of 1956 created the office of secretary of state, which was functionally identical to a lieutenant governor, and was renamed to "lieutenant governor" in 1970.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKallenbach197741–43-115]
