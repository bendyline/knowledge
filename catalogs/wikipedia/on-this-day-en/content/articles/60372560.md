Pipeline construction shown in Alberta

**[Pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport) in Canada** are important components of [energy infrastructure in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Canada) as the majority of [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_in_Canada) and [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_proven_oil_reserves) are located in landlocked [Alberta](717.md) and need to be transported to ports or terminals to access larger markets.

## Background

Early pipelines were built in [Ontario](22218.md) during the 1850s to transport crude oil from nearby wells. However, Canada's modern pipeline system began following the discovery of [Leduc No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leduc_No._1) in [Alberta](717.md) in 1947.[^cite_note-1] Since then, Alberta's economy has been energy reliant, with oil and natural gas production expanded after major pipelines were constructed in the 1950s to connect Western Canadian energy resources with domestic and international markets. The majority of Canada's natural gas production is exported to the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-2] In the 21st century, pipelines in Canada became politicized, especially during the [premiership of Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Justin_Trudeau), whose government's environmental policies proved unpopular in Alberta. While some pipeline projects were cancelled because of environmental concerns, legal challenges, market conditions, and Indigenous rights issues, the Trans Mountain Expansion was completed in 2024 after the federal government purchased the project in 2018. Pipelines remain a vital part of Canada's economic development, while environmental protection and Indigenous consultation have received increased importance. During the [premiership of Mark Carney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Mark_Carney), the construction of oil pipelines has been given increased importance due to the need for energy independence due to the [2025–2026 United States trade war with Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_trade_war_with_Canada) and the [2026 Alberta independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Alberta_independence_referendum).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Regulation and ownership

### Regulation

The Canadian federal government regulates around 10% (by length) of pipelines through the [Canada Energy Regulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Energy_Regulator).[^cite_note-5] The Regulator has precedence over provincial regulation when pipelines cross provincial or international boundaries.
