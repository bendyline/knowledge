**Zhytomyr** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Житомир [\[ʒɪˈtɔmɪr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/6f/Uk-%D0%96%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80.ogg/Uk-%D0%96%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/6f/Uk-%D0%96%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80.ogg/Uk-%D0%96%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; see [below](#Names) for other names) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in the north of the western half of [Ukraine](31750.md). It is the [administrative center](181337.md) of [Zhytomyr Oblast](1567596.md) ([province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)), as well as the administrative center of the surrounding [Zhytomyr urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhytomyr_urban_hromada) ([commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hromada)) and [Zhytomyr Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhytomyr_Raion) ([district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion)). Moreover, Zhytomyr consists of two [urban districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_districts_of_Ukraine): Bohunskyi District and Koroliovskyi District (named in honour of [Sergey Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Korolyov)). Zhytomyr occupies an area of 65 square kilometers (25 sq mi). Its population is 261,624 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-2]

Zhytomyr is a major Ukrainian transport hub as the city lies on a historic route linking the city of [Kyiv](585629.md) with the west through [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_Belarus). Today it links [Warsaw](32908.md) with Kyiv, [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk) with [Izmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izmail), and several major cities of Ukraine. Zhytomyr was also the location of [Ozerne airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozerne_\(air_base\)), a key [Cold War](325329.md) strategic aircraft base 11 kilometers (6.8 mi) southeast of the city.

Important economic activities of Zhytomyr include lumber milling, food processing, granite quarrying, metalworking, and the manufacture of musical instruments.[^cite_note-3]

Zhytomyr Oblast is the main center of the [Polish minority in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Ukraine), and in the city itself there is a Latin Catholic cathedral and large [Roman Catholic](606848.md) Polish cemetery, founded in 1800. It is regarded as the third biggest Polish cemetery outside Poland, after the [Lychakivskiy Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychakivskiy_Cemetery) in [Lviv](49968.md) and [Rasos Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasos_Cemetery) in [Vilnius](32597.md).

### Early history



Medieval [kurgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan)

Legend holds that Zhytomyr was established about 884 by Zhytomyr, prince of a Slavic tribe of [Drevlians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drevlians). This date, 884, is cut into a large stone of the ice age times, standing on the hill where Zhytomyr was founded. Zhytomyr was one of the prominent cities of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'). The first records of the town date from 1240, when it was sacked by the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) hordes of [Batu Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Khan).

In 1320 Zhytomyr was captured by the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). It was granted [Magdeburg city rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg_rights) in 1444 by [Casimir IV Jagiellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon).[^cite_note-byt-4] As a border outpost, during the 15th century the city was seriously damaged by [Tatar raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean%E2%80%93Nogai_slave_raids_in_Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-ency-5] After the [Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin) (1569) the city was incorporated into the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) and became an important center of local administration, seat of the [starosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starosta), and capital of Żytomierz County. Here, [sejmiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejmik) of [Kiev Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Voivodeship) took place. In 1572, the town had 142 buildings, a manor house of the starosta and a castle. Following the privilege of King [Sigismund III Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund_III_Vasa), Zhytomyr had the right for two fairs a year. During [Khmelnytsky Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytsky_Uprising) (1648) Zhytomyr was destroyed by [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Cossacks) troops.[^cite_note-ency-5] As a result of the revolt, it was incorporated into the [Cossack Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate). In 1667, following the [Treaty of Andrusovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Andrusovo), Zhytomyr became the capital of the [Kiev Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Voivodeship). It was a [royal city of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Poland).[^cite_note-6]

During the 18th century Zhytomyr became an important centre of [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Ukraine) culture, serving as a centre of a [Roman Catholic](606848.md) [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric).[^cite_note-ency-5] In 1724, a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) school and monastery were opened here. By 1765, Zhytomyr had five churches, including 3 Roman Catholic and 2 Orthodox, and 285 houses. [Bernardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardine) monks were also active in the city.[^cite_note-ency-5] After the [Second Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Partition_of_Poland) in 1793 it passed to [Imperial Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia) and became the capital of the [Volhynian Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynian_Governorate). In 1804 Zhytomyr was named capital of the Volhynian Governorate. In 1808, a Polish theater was founded in the city.[^cite_note-byt-4] In 1837 the city was deprived of its self-government according to Magdeburg Law.[^cite_note-tyzh-7] The Polish [January Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising) of 1863 was supported by the townspeople.[^cite_note-byt-4]

### Early 20th century



Kyivska street looking West toward St. Michael's Cathedral. Photo early 1900s.

After the [February Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_Revolution) of 1917, the [city duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_duma) of Zhytomyr supported the [Provisional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government) and opposed the [Kornilov coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kornilov_affair). On 8 November the duma condemned the [October coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), and on 28 November recognized [Central Rada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Rada) as the only legal authority in Ukraine.[^cite_note-tyzh-7]

In January 1918 the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) attempted to capture power in Zhytomyr, but were defeated.[^cite_note-tyzh-7] During a period of [Ukrainian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence), between late January and early March 1918, the city served as the national capital of [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic). During that period, laws on the introduction of the national currency, [hryvnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hryvnia), on Ukrainian citizenship and on the administrative-territorial divisions of Ukraine were adopted in the city.[^cite_note-tyzh-7] On 6 March 1918, the city became the seat of Bolokhiv land, a newly established [zemlia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_Ukraine_\(1918\)) of the Ukrainian People's Republic, which was disbanded on 29 April 1918 by [Hetman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman_of_all_Ukraine) of Ukraine [Pavlo Skoropadsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Skoropadsky), who brought back old [governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_\(Russia\)) divisions of the Russian Empire.

Between December 1918 and 1920 Zhytomyr was contested between Ukrainian and Bolshevik troops, as well as other powers, changing hands over 15 times.[^cite_note-tyzh-7] In August 1919 a battle between forces of [Ukrainian Galician Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Galician_Army) and the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) took place in the city.[^cite_note-ency-5]

### Soviet rule before WWII

On 12 June 1920 Zhytomyr occupied by [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) troops of [Grigory Kotovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Kotovsky). As a result, for the following 70 years the city was a part of the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).[^cite_note-tyzh-7] It was the capital of the [Volhynia Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia_Governorate).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Due to one of [Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin)'s 5-year plans, the city suffered from the man-made famine [Holodomor of 1932-1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor). In 2008, the National Museum of the Holodomor Genocide published the National Book of Memory of the Victims of the Holodomor of 1932–1933 in Ukraine. Zhytomyr region – Zhytomyr.[^cite_note-10] The book has 1116 pages and consists of three sections. According to historical records, more than 8015 people died during Holodomor in 1932–1933.

In 1936, part of the Polish population was expelled by the Soviets to Kazakhstan.[^cite_note-11] During the [Polish Operation of the NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Operation_of_the_NKVD), the local Polish population was persecuted with mass deportations and executions.[^cite_note-12]

### During WWII



Zhytomyr during German occupation in World War II

&#x20;During [World War II](32927.md) Zhytomyr and the surrounding territory was for two and a half years (first from [9 July 1941](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) to 12 November 1943, and again from 19 November 1943 to [31 December 1943](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhitomir%E2%80%93Berdichev_Offensive)) under [Nazi German](21212.md) occupation and was [Heinrich Himmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Himmler)'s Ukrainian headquarters. The Nazi regime in what they called the "Zhytomyr General District" became what historian [Wendy Lower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy_Lower) describes as

> a laboratory for… Himmler's resettlement activists… the elimination of the Jews and German colonization of the East—transformed the landscape and devastated the population to an extent that was not experienced in other parts of Nazi-occupied Europe besides Poland. \[While]… \[u]ltimately, the exigencies of the war effort and mounting partisan warfare behind the lines prevented Nazi leaders from fully developing and realizing their colonial aims in Ukraine… In addition to the immediate destruction of all Jewish communities, Himmler insisted that the Ukrainian civilian population be brought to a 'minimum.'[^cite_note-Lower-13]

The Germans operated a Jewish [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) battalion in the city.[^cite_note-14]

Before the invasion, roughly 30,000 Jews lived in Zhytomyr, accounting for about one-third of the city's residents. More than 5,000 Jews were murdered between July and September 1941.[^cite_note-15] In August 1941, the Nazis established a [Jewish ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_ghettos_in_Europe) in Zhitomir by enclosing several streets within a small, overcrowded area. A census conducted on September 5, 1941, recorded 4,820 Jews living in the ghetto.[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-17]

During July and August 1941, German security forces and police units carried out systematic mass murders of Jews in Zhitomir. By the end of August 1941, approximately 2,000 Jews had been murdered in Zhitomir.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

After the September 1941 massacre, the remaining Jews in Zhitomir were confined to a forced labor camp, where doctors, craftsmen, and skilled workers were kept alive temporarily for labor, including construction projects for the German military. Throughout 1942, German forces and Ukrainian police carried out successive executions of the camp's prisoners, including 237 Jewish laborers on August 19 and additional mass shootings in October and November. By 1943, the remaining Jewish artisans were also murdered.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

At the same time, Jewish Soviet prisoners of war held at [POW Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner-of-war_camp) No. 358 were systematically separated from other prisoners and executed by SD forces in nearby woods. An estimated 1,200–1,400 Jewish POWs were killed during the camp's early months.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

Of the approximately 7,000 Jews who were in Zhitomir when the Germans occupied the city in July 1941, only about 20 are known to have survived until the city's liberation.[^cite_note-\:0-16] On November 12, 1943, Soviet forces captured Zhytomyr.[^cite_note-18]

### After WWII

During 1942-1949 Zhytomyr region was a territory of mild activity of UPA North branch of the [Ukrainian Insurgent Army](1045217.md) (UPA), whose members fought for the independence of Ukraine against [Nazi Germany](21212.md) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). After USSR defeated the Germans, Zhytomyr fell under Soviet rule and once again became a part of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic.&#x20;

Between 1960s and 1980s Zhytomyr was one of the centres of [dissident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_dissidents) movement, with notable Ukrainian activists including [Viacheslav Chornovil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacheslav_Chornovil), [Yevhen Sverstiuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevhen_Sverstiuk), [Ivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Svitlychnyi) and [Nadia Svitlychna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Svitlychna), [Alla Horska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alla_Horska) and [Anatoliy Shevchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Shevchuk), being active in the city. Many of them were put under [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) supervision, imprisoned and put into [labour camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_camp). In order to suppress dissent, Soviet authorities organized provocations aimed at souring the relations between local Ukrainian, Polish and Jewish communities.[^cite_note-tyzh-7]

Starting from 1989, pro-democracy demonstrations under [Ukrainian national flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ukraine) were taking place in the city, despite pressure from KGB. On 21 January 1990 many local inhabitants took place in the celebration of [Unity Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_Zluky#Commemoration). On 13 June 1990 Zhytomyr became Soviet Ukraine's fourth city after [Ternopil](512798.md), [Ivano-Frankivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk) and [Lviv](49968.md) to hoist the national flag of Ukraine over its city council.[^cite_note-tyzh-7] On 24 August 1991 Ukrainian parliament announced [Declaration of Independence of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine). From 1991, Zhytomyr has been part of the independent and sovereign [Ukraine](31750.md).

### 2022 Russian invasion



School in Zhytomyr after a Russian airstrike on 4 March 2022

During the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Zhytomyr and the surrounding area were subjected to several Russian air and missile strikes, such as the 2 March airstrike which damaged residential buildings, a thermal electricity plant, and two hospitals, killing at least two and injuring more than a dozen.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

### Roman Catholics



[Saint Sophia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sophia_Cathedral,_Zhytomyr)

Zhytomyr had been a Latin Catholic bishopric since 1321, until The See was suppressed in 1789 in favor of the [Diocese of Lutsk and Zytomierz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Lutsk_and_Zytomierz), until that was split up again in 1925, when it was restored as the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Zhytomyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Zhytomyr); that was formally suppressed in 1998 to establish the [Diocese of Kyïv–Žytomyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Ky%C3%AFv%E2%80%93%C5%BDytomyr), but actually the city retains the episcopal see in its Cathedral of the [Holy Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Wisdom), while [Kyiv](585629.md) (although first in the title and the national capital) only has a [co-cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-cathedral).[^cite_note-29]

The Zhytomyr cemetery was opened in 1800. At first, it served Polish nobility from [Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia), such as the Czeczel and the Woronicz families. Later, other Catholics were buried here, including Germans, Ukrainians and Russians.



Polish Cemetery

In 1840, the Chapel of St. Stanislaus was built (now in ruins), and the cemetery was divided into nine districts, named after different saints. In the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the complex was devastated, now it is under the process of renovation.

Among most famous people buried here are:&#x20;

- Bronislaw Matyjewicz-Maciejewicz, one of the first Polish air pilots

- Karol Niedzialkowski – bishop of Lutsk and Zhytomir in the late 19th century

- [Apolinary Wnukowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apolinary_Wnukowski) – Roman Catholic archbishop and scholar

- Juliusz Zarębski – Polish composer

- parents of [Ignacy Jan Paderewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski)

- the family of [Stanisław Moniuszko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Moniuszko)

### Jews in Zhytomyr



Zhytomyr Jewish Institute building

Zhytomyr apparently had few [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) at the time of the [Khmelnytsky Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytsky_Uprising) (1648), but by the time it became part of [Russia](25391.md) in 1778, it had a large Jewish community, and was a center of the [Hasidic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasidic_Judaism) movement. Jews formed nearly one-third of the 1861 population (13,299 of 40,564); thirty years later, they had somewhat outpaced the general growth of the city, with 24,062 Jews in a total population of 69,785. By 1891 there were three large [synagogues](48559.md) and 46 smaller *[batei midrash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_midrash)*. The proportion of Jews was much lower in the surrounding district of Zhytomyr than in the city itself; at the turn of the century (circa 1900) there were 22,636 Jews in a total population of 281,378.

In [Imperial Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia), Zhytomyr held the same status as the official Jewish center of southern part of the [Pale of Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_of_Settlement) as [Vilnius](32597.md) held in the north.[^cite_note-Kehila_Zhitomir-30] The printing of [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) books was permitted only in these two cities during the monopoly of Hebrew printing from 1845 to 1862, and both were chosen as the seats of the two [rabbinical](51273.md) schools which were established by the government in 1848 in pursuance of its plans to force secular education on the [Jews of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia_and_the_Soviet_Union) in accordance with the program of the Teutonized Russian [Haskalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskalah) movement.[^cite_note-Kehila_Zhitomir-30] The rabbinical school of Zhytomyr was considered the more Jewish, or rather the less Russianized, of the two (*[Ha-Meliẓ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ha-Meli%E1%BA%93)*, 1868, No. 40, cited in *Jewish Encyclopedia*). Its first head master was [Jacob Eichenbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Eichenbaum), who was succeeded by [Hayyim Selig Slonimski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayyim_Selig_Slonimski) in 1862. The latter remained at the head of the school until it was closed (together with the one at Vilnius) in 1873 because of its failure to provide rabbis with a secular education who would be acceptable to the Jewish communities. [Suchastover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suchastover?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Gottlober](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottlober), [Lerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lerner_\(rabbi\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Zweifel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zweifel_\(rabbi\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) were among the best-known teachers of the rabbinical school at Zhytomyr, while [Abraham Goldfaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Goldfaden), [Salomon Mandelkern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salomon_Mandelkern), and [Abraham Jacob Paperna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Jacob_Paperna) were among the students who later became famous in the Jewish world.

The Jewish community of Zhytomyr suffered [pogroms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom):

1. On 7–8 May 1905, when the section of the city known as "Podol" was devastated, and 20 were killed within the city.

2. On 7–10 January 1919, 15 young Jewish neighbors were killed when they came to defend, and the [Christian](5211.md) student Nicholas Blinov, also attempting to defend, likewise died. Ten young Jews from nearby [Chudnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chudnov) were also killed while on their way to aid the Jews of Zhytomyr.

   [^cite_note-bh.org.il-31]

3. Beginning on 22 March 1919, according to witnesses, the 317 deaths were fewer than might have been, due to both Christian sheltering efforts and the return of the [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) troops within a few days.

   [^cite_note-32]

The Jewish community of the region was largely destroyed in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust). The killing began as early as October 1941 when a majority of the city's Jews were driven from the town to destinations unknown, leaving about only 5,000 Jews.[^cite_note-33] In the four months beginning with [Himmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Himmler)'s 25 July 1942 orders, "all of Ukraine's [shtetls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shtetl) and [ghettos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghetto#Second_World_War) lay in ruins; around 3,000[^cite_note-34] Jewish men, women, and children were murdered by stationary and mobile [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel)-police units with local Ukrainian auxiliaries."[^cite_note-Lower-13]

Today, the Zhytomyr Jewish community numbers about 5,000. The community is a part of the "Union of Jewish Communities in Ukraine" and the city and district's rabbinate. Rabbi Shlomo Vilhelm, who came to the city as a [Chabad emissary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad_emissary) in 1994, serves as rabbi. Other Jewish institutions are also active in the city, including the [Joint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_Joint_Distribution_Committee) and its humanitarian branch "Chesed" and the [Jewish Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Agency).

The community has an ancient [synagogue](48559.md) in the city center which has a [mikveh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikveh). [Chabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad) operates in the city various educational institutions which have residence in a village next to the city.

Famous composers [Borys Lyatoshynsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borys_Lyatoshynsky) and [Sviatoslav Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_Richter) were born in Zhytomyr.

## Geography



Teteriv River in Zhytomyr

Zhytomyr lies in a unique natural setting; all sides of the city are surrounded by ancient [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) through which flow the [Teteriv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teteriv_River), Kamianka, Kroshenka and Putiatynka [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River). The Teteriv river generally forms the southern boundary of Zhytomyr, though there are also some small areas of Zhytomyr city territory below the southern bank of the river. The city is rich in [parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks) and [public squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_square).

Zhytomyr is set out on a mostly radial type of [street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street) net with the centre at the main public square of the city, named *Sobornyi Maidan* (which means *Cathedral Square*). A building containing [courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court) and some other institutions is in the west of the square. Before 1991, this building contained Zhytomyr Oblast Committee of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPSU). Just behind the building (that is to the west of Sobornyi Square) is a small quiet park, bearing the name of *Zamkova Gora* (*Castle Mountain*) and containing a monument-type boulder with an inscription stating that this is a place where Zhytomyr was founded. This historical centre of Zhytomyr is in the south part of the city. The old part of Zhytomyr is on three rocky hills over the river Kamianka: Okhrimova, Zamkova, and Petrovska.

The old town is surrounded by new housing estates, the names of which are often borrowed from the former suburban villages or reflect the longstanding occupations common in these places. The main streets connecting Sobornyi Maidan with the outskirts of Zhytomyr are Kyivska Street or [Kyiv](585629.md) Street (going to northeast, to the [railway station](26161590.md) and also to the main [bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station) of the city), Velyka [Berdychivska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdychiv) Street (going to [southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_direction)), [Lech Kaczyński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Kaczy%C5%84ski) Street (going southwest; its further continuation is [Chudnivska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chudniv) Street going to [beaches](40630.md) and a forest-type park near the river of Teteriv), and Peremohy Street (going north).

The best-known street in the central part of Zhytomyr is Mykhailivska (named after [St. Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael)'s [Church](6325.md) at the northern end of the street).[^cite_note-37] The street is about 500 [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) to the east of Sobornyi Maidan and runs approximately from north to south, connecting some points at the above-mentioned Kyivska Street and Velyka Berdychivska. Mykhailivska Street is for [pedestrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian) traffic: [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle) are forbidden, with the exception of some slow-moving ones. A puppet theatre is nestled in the middle of the street, while the building of the Zhytomyr City Council is at its southern end. Several small coffee houses and cafés have sprung up here recently, frequented by locals from all walks of life and of all ages. If one crosses Velyka Berdychivska Street from the southern end of Mykhailivska Street, then one finds oneself at [Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Korolyov) Square containing the building of the Zhytomyr Oblast Council. Crossing Kyivska Street from the northern end of Mykhailivska Street, one can continue to go along Pokrovska Street, another important long avenue of Zhytomyr (going north).

The best-known park of Zhytomyr is named after [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin), in the south of the city, at the left (northern) bank of the Teteriv River. It was formerly owned by the [Baron de Chaudoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilien_Chaudoir).

## Economy



Zhytomyr central department store

Zhytomyr is an important economic center in the region. Enterprises in the city include glass, metal fabrication, electronic devices, screens, fabrics, furniture, shoes and others. In addition, there is a large pharmaceutical factory in Zhytomyr. Since 1944, a confectionery factory (ALC "ZhL") has operated in Zhytomyr; the enterprise is one of the leaders of the Ukrainian confectionery market.[^cite_note-40]

The city is home to the Zhytomyr Armoured Vehicle and Tank Factory. The factory has been one of the main repair facilities in Ukraine since the start of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), running on 3 shifts. In September 2014 it was announced that the Ministry of Defence of Ukraine had placed a ₴280 million order with the factory.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

### Public city transport

Common kinds of [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) shuttling within Zhytomyr are [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus), [trams](30733.md), [buses](4146.md), and [minibuses](1094925.md). There are also electric [trams](30733.md), but currently on one route only. Earlier there were several tram routes in Zhytomyr, but all excepting one were canceled during a period of domination of the opinion that a tram is a bad kind of [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport). Trams began to shuttle in Zhytomyr in 1899, has been one of the first cities in [Ukraine](31750.md) to introduce tram service. Trolleybuses appear in Zhytomyr in 1962. The total length of Zhytomyr city electric transport routes (trolleybuses and trams) is 275 km. Zhytomyr is the first city in Ukraine to implement e-ticket system in all municipal public transport.
