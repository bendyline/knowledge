**Dnipropetrovsk Oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Дніпропетровська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Dnipropetrovska oblast*), also commonly known as **Dnipropetrovshchyna** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Дніпропетровщина), is an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) (province) in southeastern [Ukraine](31750.md), the most important industrial region of the country. It was created on 27 February 1932. Dnipropetrovsk Oblast has a population of about 3,096,485 (2022 estimate),[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-4] approximately 80% or 4/5th of the population living on [administrative centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) like [Dnipro](989795.md), [Kryvyi Rih](624764.md), [Kamianske](1261408.md), [Nikopol](1958336.md) and [Pavlohrad](3889613.md). The [Dnieper River](380138.md) runs through the oblast.

## Geography

Most of Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, including [Dnipro Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_Raion), is located in [eastern Ukraine](13065940.md), though some parts are in [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ukraine) and [southern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine), such as [Kamianske Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamianske_Raion) and [Nikopol Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikopol_Raion), respectively. The area of the oblast (31,974 km<sup>2</sup>) comprises about 5.3% of the total area of the country. Its longitude from north to south is 130 km, from east to west — 300 km. The oblast borders the [Poltava](1567650.md) and [Kharkiv](494901.md) oblasts on the north, the [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) on the east, the [Zaporizhzhia](494936.md) and [Kherson](1273370.md) oblasts on the south, and the [Mykolaiv](494928.md) and [Kirovohrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirovohrad_Oblast) oblasts on the west. Historically, it is located in the region of [Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_\(region\)).

The [Black Sea Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Lowland) covers about half of the territory of the oblast, where it lies only within the west bank of the Dnieper. In [Terny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternivskyi_District), a [Ternivsky meteorite crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternovka_crater) is located. It is 11 km (6.8 mi) in diameter and its age is estimated at 280 ± 10 million years ([Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian)). The crater is not exposed at the surface.[^cite_note-7] The [Dnieper Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Upland) contains a number of minerals including [iron](145352.md), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite), [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite), [brown coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_coal), and [kaolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaolin). [Kryvbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvbas) is an important economic region, specializing in iron ore mining and the steel industry. It is arguably the main iron ore region of [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). Named after the city of [Kryvyi Rih](624764.md), the mining base of the region occupies the southwestern part of the Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, as well as the small neighboring parts of the [Kirovohrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirovohrad_Oblast) and [Kherson Oblasts](1273370.md).[^cite_note-8]

The region possesses major deposits of iron ore and some other metallurgical ores. To exploit them, several large mining companies were founded here in the middle of the 20th century. Most of them are located in Kryvyi Rih itself, which is the longest city in Europe (roughly 67 km (42 mi) in a straight line from one end to another).

### Geology

Much of the Dnipropetrovsk oblast is located within the boundaries of the [Ukrainian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Shield) and only the northern regions and the extreme eastern part of the territory are confined to the south-eastern side of the Dnipro-Donets depression.

In the geological structure of the region, the breeds come from the archaea, the [Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic), the [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic), the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) and the [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic).

## History



1648 map of [Beauplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Le_Vasseur_de_Beauplan), with [Dzikie Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Fields) (the Wild Fields) identified in upper portion of the map.

In the 6th to 8th centuries AD the first settlements of Slavs appeared on the banks of the Dnieper within the region. During the period of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') (9th to 12th centuries AD) the Dnieper River functioned as one of the main trade corridors of medieval Eastern Europe, part of the route ["from the Varangians to the Greeks"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks), which connected the Baltic Sea region with the [Crimea](163045.md) and with the capital of [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium), [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople). The Dnieper also served as a major route for transporting the armies of Kyiv princes on their way to the Byzantine coastal cities in the early 9th and late 9th centuries.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

At the beginning of the 15th century, [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) tribes inhabiting the right bank of the Dnieper were driven away by the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). However, by the mid-15th century, the [Nogai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogais) (who lived north of the [Sea of Azov](78019.md)) and the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate) invaded these lands. The Grand Duchy of Lithuania and the Crimean Khanate agreed to a border along the Dnieper, and farther east along the [Samara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_\(Dnieper\)), i.e. through what is today the city of Dnipro. At this time, there appeared a new force, the [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) — armed free men not subject to any feudal lord — who soon came to dominate the region. They later became known as [Zaporozhian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Cossacks), from [Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_\(region\)), the lands south of [Naddniprianshchyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Ukraine) (Zaporizhzhia translates to "the Land Beyond the [Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_rapids)"). This period of raids and fighting caused considerable devastation and [depopulation](720599.md) in the [Pontic steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic%E2%80%93Caspian_steppe); the area became known as the "Wilderness" or the "[Wild Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Fields)".

In 1635, the [Polish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) built the [Kodak fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodak_Fortress) above the [Dnieper Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Rapids) at *Kodaky*, partly as a result of rivalry in the region between Poland, [Turkey](22278.md) and the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate),[^cite_note-Go2Kiev-11] and partly to maintain control over Cossack activity (i.e. to suppress the Cossack raiders and to prevent peasants moving out of the area).[^cite_note-SerhiiPlokhy-12] On the night of 3 or 4 August 1635, the Cossacks of [Ivan Sulyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Sulyma) captured the fort by surprise, burning it down and butchering the garrison of about 200 West European mercenaries under Jean Marion.[^cite_note-SerhiiPlokhy-12] The fort, rebuilt by French engineer [Guillaume Le Vasseur de Beauplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Le_Vasseur_de_Beauplan)[^cite_note-13] for the Polish government in 1638, had a mercenary garrison.[^cite_note-SerhiiPlokhy-12] *Kodak* was captured by [Zaporozhian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Host) on 1 October 1648, and was garrisoned by the Cossacks until its demolition in accordance with the [Treaty of the Pruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Pruth) in 1711.[^cite_note-Kodak_Pruth-14]

Under the [Treaty of Pereyaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Pereyaslav) of 1654, the territory came within the sphere of influence of the Moscow-based [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia). In 1774 [Prince Grigori Potemkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigori_Aleksandrovich_Potemkin) was appointed governor of [Novorossiysk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Governorate), and after the [destruction of the Zaporozhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidation_of_the_Zaporozhian_Sich) in 1775, he started founding cities in the region and encouraging foreign settlers. The city of *Yekaterinoslav* (present-day Dnipro) was founded in 1776, not in its current location, but at the confluence of the [River Samara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_River_\(Dnieper\)) with the River Kil'chen' at [Loshakivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loshakivka?action=edit\&redlink=1), north of the Dnieper. On 8 May 1775, after the end of the [Russian-Turkish War of 1768 to 1774](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1768%E2%80%931774\)), Russian authorities opened a [postal station and track](624764.md) which linked [Kremenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk) city, the [Kinburn foreland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinburn_peninsula) and [Ochakiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochakiv), all locations of the Imperial Russian Army.

In December 1796, Emperor [Paul I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_I_of_Russia) re-established the Novorossiysk Governorate, mostly with land from the former [Yekaterinoslav Viceroyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Viceroyalty). In 1802, this province was divided into the [Nikolayev Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolayev_Governorate) (known as the Kherson Governorate from 1803), [Yekaterinoslav Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Governorate), and the [Taurida Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Governorate). The capital of the Yekaterinoslav Governorate was the city of [Yekaterinoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav) (modern Dnipro). It was located within the former lands of the [Zaporizhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhian_Sich). The governorate bordered to the north with the [Kharkov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov_Governorate) and [Poltava Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Governorate), to the west and southwest with the [Kherson Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson_Governorate), to the south with the [Taurida Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Governorate) and [Sea of Azov](78019.md), and to the east with the [Don Host Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Host_Oblast).

[Olexander Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olexander_Paul) (1832–1890) discovered iron ore and initiated smelting,[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] and this became the core of a developing a mining district.[^cite_note-17] In 1874 Emperor [Alexander II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_II_of_Russia) initiated the founding project of a railway,[^cite_note-18] running 505 kilometres (314 mi). This enabled transportation directly to the nearest factories and greatly sped up the development of the region.



Yekaterinoslav, modern [Dnipro](989795.md), in 1910

On 1 August 1925, the Yekaterinoslav Governorate administration was discontinued, and in 1926 the city of Yekaterinoslav (Katerynoslav) was renamed Dnipropetrovsk after Ukrainian Soviet [leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader) [Grigory Petrovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Petrovsky).[^cite_note-19] Before the introduction of oblasts in 1932, the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) comprised 40 [okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okrugs), which had replaced the former Russian Imperial *guberniya* (governorate) subdivisions. In 1932 the territory of the Ukrainian SSR was re-organized into oblasts. The first oblasts were [Vinnytsia Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Oblast), [Kyiv Oblast](494884.md), [Odesa Oblast](61438266.md), [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), and Dnipropetrovsk Oblast. Soon after that, in the summer of 1932, [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) was formed out of eastern parts of Kharkiv and Dnipropetrovsk oblasts.

During the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor) in the 1930s, more than 200 [collective farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_farm) in Dnipropetrovsk Oblast were put on ["Blackboards"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackboards_\(Soviet_policies\)) which implied a complete blockade of trade and food-aid to villages under-performing in fulfilling grain-procurement quotas; a number representing more than half of all such "Blackboards" throughout all of the Ukrainian SSR.[^cite_note-blackboard-20]

During the [1991 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), 90.36% of votes in Dnipropetrovsk Oblast favored the [Declaration of Independence of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine). A survey conducted in December 2014 by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology found 2.2% of the oblast's population supported their region joining Russia, 89.9% did not support the idea, and the rest were undecided or did not respond.[^cite_note-DT150103-21]

The city of Dnipropetrovsk was renamed "Dnipro" in May 2016 as part of the [decommunization laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine) enacted a year earlier.[^cite_note-22] Dnipropetrovsk Oblast could not be renamed at the time because it is mentioned by name in the [Constitution of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ukraine) and accordingly, could only be renamed through a [constitutional amendment](261586.md).[^cite_note-23] In April 2018 a group of over a hundred deputies formally initiated a proposal in the [Ukrainian Parliament](844993.md) to change the name to *Sicheslav Oblast* ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Січеславська область); in February 2019, the Verkhovna Rada voted to officially amend the Constitution, thus granting state sanction to the name change.[^cite_note-24] Later that year the Constitutional Court officially approved the change. The oblast's administrative centre and largest city, [Dnipro](989795.md), had the unofficial name "Sicheslav" (commemorating the [Zaporizhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhian_Sich)) in 1918–21 during the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence).[^cite_note-25] Since then, the renaming process has stalled (as of December 2025), for reasons such as the 2019 [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ukrainian_presidential_election) and [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ukrainian_parliamentary_election) elections, the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Ukraine) and the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) (2022 onwards) with the supplementing [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

During the Russian invasion, the cities of [Dnipro](989795.md), [Kryvyi Rih](624764.md), and [Nikopol](1958336.md), among other locations in the region, were bombed by Russia.[^cite_note-28] It was also reported that Russian troops were pushed from areas near Dnipropetrovsk Oblast and Kherson Oblast, near the border.[^cite_note-29] One village bordering Kherson Oblast, [Hannivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannivka,_Kryvyi_Rih_Raion,_Dnipropetrovsk_Oblast), may have been occupied[^cite_note-30] and liberated by Ukrainian forces by May 2022.[^cite_note-31] Between 2022 and 2024, there was no further ground fighting and the oblast had remained completely under Ukrainian control. In June 2025, the [Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War) confirmed that Russian troops entered Dnipropetrovsk Oblast.[^cite_note-32]

## Transport



[Kryvyi Rih Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Metro)

There are eight over-Dnieper bridges and dozens of grade-separated intersections. Several new intersections are under construction. [European route E105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E105) cross Left-bank Dnipro from North to South. [Highway M04 (Ukraine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_M04_\(Ukraine\)) and [Highway M18 (Ukraine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_M18_\(Ukraine\)) cross River Dnieper and [Dnipro](989795.md) from West to East, entering Kryvyi Rih. Overall, roads are in poor technical condition and maintained inadequately.

[Cisdnieper Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Dnipro_Railway) (NDZ), headquartered in Dnipro, is a component part of the [Ukrzaliznytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrzaliznytsia) (UZ) company. CDR's route map includes all the railroads in the Dnipropetrovsk, [Zaporizhzhia](494936.md), [Kharkiv](494901.md), [Kherson](1273370.md) oblasts and the [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea).

As of 2008, Cisdnieper rail system included 3,275 km (2,035 mi) of track, of which 93,3% were electrified. The CDR consists of five sections (directions), the Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia, Kryvyi Rih, and Crimea directions. There are 244 railway stations in the NDR system. More than a dozen *elektrichka* stops are located within the city allowing residents of different neighborhoods to use the suburban trains.

The cities of Dnipro and Kryvyi Rih are served by a local sales-tax-funded bus, tram, metro and trolleybus systems.

[Dnipro International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_International_Airport) and [Kryvyi Rih International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_International_Airport) are the only international airports. The airport of Dnipro serves as one of the hubs for [Dniproavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniproavia). The airport has non-stop service to over 20 destinations throughout Ukraine and [Turkey](11125639.md), as well as to [Vienna](55866.md) and [Tel Aviv](31453.md). Kryvyi Rih International Airport provides limited commercial air service.

## Environment



River Dnieper in [Dnipro](989795.md)



Pryorilskyi Landscape Reserve

The oblast is situated in the [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) region. Forests in the oblast occupy about 3.9% of the oblast's total territory. The average temperature in the winter balances from −3 to −5 °C and in the summer from 22 to 24 °C. The average annual rainfall is 400–490 mm. During the summer, Dnipropetrovsk oblast is very warm (average day temperature in July is 24 to 28 °C (75 to 82 °F), even hot sometimes 34 to 38 °C (93 to 100 °F). Temperatures as high as 36 °C (97 °F) have been recorded in May. Winter is not so cold (average day temperature in January is −3 to 0 °C (27 to 32 °F), but when there is no snow and the wind blows hard, it feels extremely cold. A mix of snow and rain happens usually in December.

The tender climate, mineral sources, and the curative mud allow opportunities for rest and cure within the region. Here there are 21 health-centers and medicated pensions, 10 rest homes, recreation departments and rest camps for children.

The Dnipropetrovsk Oblast has a wide range of [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) with over 1700 kinds of vegetation and 7500 kinds of animals (including the [elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk), [wild boar](52255.md), dappled [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer), [roe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe), [hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare), [fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox), [wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf), etc.). There are 114 parks and nature objects, including 15 state reserves; 3 nature memorials, 24 local parks; 7 landscape parks; 3 park tracts, which altogether make up approximately 260 square kilometres.

217 rivers flow within the area, including 55 rivers which are longer than 25 km, the major one being the [Dnieper](380138.md), which crosses through the center of the oblast. Also flowing through the region are two major reservoirs, the [Kamianske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamianske_Reservoir) and [Dnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Reservoir), while the former [Kakhovka Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Reservoir) was drained in 2023 following the [destruction of the Kakhovka Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Kakhovka_Dam) and the subsequent restoration of the [Great Meadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Meadow,_Ukraine). A major channel in the region is the [Dnipro — Kryvyi Rih Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_%E2%80%93_Kryvyi_Rih_Canal).

## Economy

The Dnipropetrovsk Oblast has a high industry potential. There are 712 basic industrial organizations, including 20 different types of economic activity with about 473,4 thousand workers. The area also produces about 16.9% of the total industry production of Ukraine. This places the Dnipropetrovsk Oblast second in Ukraine (after the neighbouring [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md)).

Dnipro is a major industrial centre of Ukraine. It has several facilities devoted to heavy industry that produce a wide range of products, including [cast-iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast-iron), rolled metal, pipes, [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery), different mining combines, [agricultural equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_equipment), [tractors](152692.md), [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus), refrigerators, different chemicals and many others.  The most famous and the oldest (founded in the 19th century) is the Metallurgical Plant named after Petrovsky. The city also has big food processing and light industry factories. Many sewing and dress-making factories work for [France](5843419.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Germany](11867.md) and [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) , using the most advanced technologies, materials and design. Dnipro also formerly dominated in the [aerospace industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_industry) since the 1950s: engineering department [Yuzhnoye Design Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzhnoye_Design_Bureau) and construction at [Pivdenmash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PA_Pivdenmash).

[Pivdenmash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PA_Pivdenmash), the former [Yuzhmash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzhmash), is a manufacturer of space [rockets](26301.md), [agricultural equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_equipment), [buses](4146.md), [trolley buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolley_bus), [trams](30733.md), [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine), and [satellites](27683.md) that was inherited from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). It is a large state-owned company located in [Dnipro](989795.md).

[Dniproavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniproavia), an airline, has its head office on the grounds of [Dnipropetrovsk International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipropetrovsk_International_Airport).[^cite_note-36] The region possesses major deposits of iron ore and some other metallurgical ores. To exploit them, several large mining companies were founded here in the middle of the 20th century. Most of them are located in Kryvyi Rih itself, which is the longest city in Europe. Steel companies of the region (except Mittal Steel-owned Kryvorizhstal) are controlled by either the [Privat Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privat_Group) or the [SCM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCM_Holdings). From the 1990s until 2004, these once united and state-owned industries went through a hard and scandal-ridden process of privatization. Being a business oligarch entity, Privat Group controls some prominent Ukrainian media, maintains close relations with politicians and sponsors professional sports. Key businesses of the group (including the [PrivatBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PrivatBank) itself) are based in Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, which is regarded as its "homeland". Group's founding owners are natives of Dnipropetrovsk and made their entire career there.

[ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal_Kryvyi_Rih), owned by [ArcelorMittal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal) since 2005 is the largest private company by revenue in Ukraine,[^cite_note-37] producing over 7 million tonnes of crude steel, and mined over 17 million tonnes of iron ore. As of 2011, the company employed about 37,000 people. 4 Iron Ore Enrichment Works of [Metinvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metinvest) are a large contributors to the UA's [balance of payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_payments). The third giant — [Evraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evraz) mining company.

The oblast is named after the Communist leader of [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) [Grigory Petrovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Petrovsky) and is thus to be renamed

Historically, Dnieper Ukraine comprised territory that roughly corresponded to the area of Ukraine within the expanding [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). Ukrainians sometimes call it Great Ukraine (Velyka Ukraina). Historically, this region is tightly entwined with the history of Ukraine and is considered the heart of the country.

[Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) (67,0%) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) (31,9%) language are both used, with Russian being more common in cities, while Ukrainian is the dominant language in rural communities. These details result in a significant difference across different survey results, as even a small restating of a question switches responses of a significant group of people.

[Petrykivka painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrykivka_painting), originating from the village of [Petrykivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrykivka), is known for its distinctive features such as patterns, unusual technique and white background. It was included to the [UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_of_Ukraine).

The Poles tried to establish order in that area, and commissioned [French](5843419.md) military cartographer and engineer [William le Vasseur de Beauplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_le_Vasseur_de_Beauplan) to construct it. The quarry was closed in 1994, but at that time two-thirds of the fortress had been destroyed. Today the site is just ruins, but it is a popular tourist attraction.

The event is described in the memoirs of comte de Ségur. Prince [Grigory Potemkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Potemkin) envisioned the church as one of the spiritual centres of [New Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Russia). [Ivan Starov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Starov) submitted to Potemkin his designs for a Roman-style basilica, but construction was postponed until the end of the [Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War).

A Cossack with a musket was an emblem of the [Zaporizhian Host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhian_Host) and later the state emblem of the Hetmanate and the [Ukrainian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State). The origin of the emblem is uncertain, while its first records date back to 1592. On the initiative of [Pyotr Rumyantsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Rumyantsev) the emblem was phased out and replaced by the Russian double-headed eagle in 1767.

A Cossack with a rifle was restored by the [Hetman of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman_of_Ukraine) [Pavlo Skoropadsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Skoropadsky) in 1918. However, later the emblem disappeared again until in 2005 it reappeared on the proposed Great Seal of Ukraine. In 2002 was adopted the flag and identical coat of arms of the Oblast, which consists of cossack with musket and nine yellow eight-pointed stars. Stars represent coat of arms of [Yekaterinoslav Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Governorate) which also consisted of imperial monogram of [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great).

&#x20;The official plants are [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [acanthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acanthus_\(plant\)) and [oak](39696.md). The motto of the oblast is [Per aspera ad astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_astra).
