**Davao City**, officially the **City of Davao**,[^cite_note-17] is a [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_the_Philippines#Legal_classification) in the [Davao Region](296162.md), Philippines. The city has a total land area of 2,443.61 km<sup>2</sup> (943.48 sq mi), making it the [largest city in the Philippines in terms of land area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_municipalities_in_the_Philippines). It is the [third-most-populous city in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_municipalities_in_the_Philippines) after [Quezon City](284223.md) and [Manila](184334.md) respectively, and the most populous city in [Mindanao](20546.md), in Davao Region, and outside of Metro Manila.[^cite_note-PSA15–11-18] According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 1,848,947 people.[^cite_note-2020Popcen-11]

It is the largest city in the province of [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur) both in population and land area wherein it is geographically situated and grouped under the province by the [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority), but the city is governed and administered independently from it. The city is divided into three congressional districts, which are subdivided into 11 administrative districts with a total of 182 [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay).

Davao City is the regional center of Davao Region and also the center of [Metro Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Davao), the [second-most-populous metropolitan area in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_the_Philippines). The city serves as the main trade, commerce and industry hub of Mindanao, and the regional center of Davao Region. The region of Davao is home to [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo), the highest mountain in the Philippines, which is highly visible in most parts of Davao City. The city is also nicknamed the "[Durian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durian) Capital of the Philippines".

## Etymology

The region's name is derived from its [Bagobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagobo) origins, who are indigenous to the area. The word *davao* came from the phonetic blending of three Bagobo subgroups' names for the [Davao River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_River), a major waterway emptying into [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf) near the city. The Obos, who inhabit the hinterlands of the region, called the river *Davah* (with a gentle vowel ending, although later pronunciation is with a hard *v* or *b*); the Clatta (or Giangan/Diangan) called it *Dawaw*, and the Tagabawas called it *Dabo*. To the Obos, *davah* also means "a place beyond the high grounds" (alluding to settlements at the mouth of the river surrounded by high, rolling hills).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

## History

### Precolonial era

The area of what is now Davao City was once a lush forest inhabited by [Lumadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad) peoples such as the [Bagobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagobo_people)[^cite_note-nccabagobo-21] and [Matigsalugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matigsalug_people),[^cite_note-22] alongside other ethnic groups such as the [Aeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeta_people), [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people) and the [Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_people).[^cite_note-karlgaspar-23] [Davao River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_River) was then called Tagloc River by the Bagobos, Maguindanaons and Tausugs who then inhabited a settlement near the mouth of the river to the sea around what is now Bolton Riverside due immediately southwest of the city plaza.[^cite_note-24] In 1543, Spanish explorers led by [Ruy Lopez de Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_Lopez_de_Villalobos) sailing around Mindanao deliberately avoided the area around [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf), then called Gulf of Tagloc, due to the danger posed by fleets of Moro warships operating in the area while surveying the southeastern coast of Mindanao for possible colonization,[^cite_note-reubencanoy-25] and as a result the Davao Gulf area remained virtually untouched by European explorers for the next three centuries.

### Maguindanao era

A [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people) Datu under the name [Datu Bago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Bago) was rewarded the territory of the surroundings of Davao Gulf by the [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) of [Maguindanao Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_Sultanate) for joining the [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish-Moro_conflict) against the Spanish in the late 1700s. From his ancestral home in [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao), he moved to the area in 1800 and, having convinced Bagobos and other native groups in the area to his side, conquered the entire Davao Gulf area.[^cite_note-macariotiu-26] Having consolidated his position, he founded the fortress of *Pinagurasan* in what is now the site of Bangkerohan Public Market[^cite_note-27] in 1830 which served as his capital.[^cite_note-sunstardatubago-28] From being a fortification and base of operations from which Datu Bago could gather and rally his forces, the settlement of Pinagurasan eventually grew into a small city extending from present-day Generoso Bridge in Bangkerohan to Quezon Boulevard more than a kilometer down south,[^cite_note-macariotiu-26]<sup>: 172–176 </sup> as Maguindanaons and Bagobos alike among other nearby tribes in the area flocked into the settlement, eventually becoming the main trade entrepot in the Davao Gulf area.[^cite_note-karlgaspar-23] With his immense overlordship of Davao Gulf, Datu Bago was eventually crowned Sultan by his subjects at his capital Pinagurasan in 1843, effectively making his realm virtually independent from the Sultanate of Maguindanao, becoming a Sultanate with sovereignty over Davao Gulf in equal standing with the Muslim kingdoms of [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao) and [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sultanate).[^cite_note-sunstardatubago-28]

### Spanish era

Although the Spaniards began to explore the Davao Gulf area as early as the 16th century, Spanish influence was negligible in the Davao region until 1842, when the Spanish Governor-General of the Philippines [Narciso Clavería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narciso_Claver%C3%ADa_y_Zald%C3%BAa,_1st_Count_of_Manila) ordered the colonization of the [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf) region, including what is now Davao City, for the Spanish Crown. This came after the [loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) of their [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_America) in the [Americas](29833.md) from the 1820s to 1830s which gravely reduced their sources of revenue to the point that the royal government in [Madrid](41188263.md) could no longer continue to properly provide financial support to what remained of its worldwide colonies. Thus, it became more urgent for local officials in the colonies, including the Philippines, to find ways and means of expanding the revenues in running the colonies, primarily in terms of tribute extracted from the natives. It meant that for the first time, the Spanish colonial government in the Philippines was compelled to embark on a full-scale conquest of Mindanao in the hopes of increasing its coffers.[^cite_note-karlgaspar-23]

Davao Gulf seemed to be a tempting target among Spanish military circles based in Manila for its thriving maritime trade taking place there.[^cite_note-karlgaspar-23] Their initial forays began with their incursion on the village of [Sigaboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_Generoso,_Davao_Oriental) in 1842, from which the local Spanish officials who recently landed there immediately demanded heavy tribute on the natives who then asked for Datu Bago's help in expelling the Spaniards, which he responded swiftly by sending a combined naval and land force in the area to defeat and drive out the Spanish force there.[^cite_note-sunstardatubago-28] The Spanish, seeing Datu Bago as a mere pirate and brigand, did not take the threat seriously for years despite his numerous victories against them until the burning of the Spanish trading vessel *San Rufo*, which carried a letter of friendship from Sultan Iskandar Qudaratullah Muhammad Zamal al-Azam of Maguindanao to Governor-General Claveria, and its massacre of all its crew by seaborne corsairs under orders from Datu Bago himself in 1846.[^cite_note-karlgaspar-23][^cite_note-sunstardatubago-28] Incensed with the incident, the Spanish secured the consent from the Sultan of Maguindanao who finally disowned the Moros of Davao Gulf by using the incident as pretext for justification to conquer the area. Thus, the official Spanish colonization of Davao Gulf finally began in earnest in April 1848 when an expedition of 70 men and women led by José Cruz de Oyanguren of [Vergara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergara), Spain, landed on the estuary of the Davao River the same month, intent on conquering Pinagurasan, the capital of Datu Bago's domain, in the hopes of permanently ending the menace posed on Spanish vessels by Moro raiders in Davao Gulf.[^cite_note-29]

Being the strongest chieftain in the region, Datu Bago imposed heavy tribute on the [Mandaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaya) tribes nearby, therefore also making him the most loathed chieftain in the region. Cruz de Uyanguren had orders from the higher authorities in [Manila](184334.md) to colonize the Davao Gulf region, which included the Bagobo settlement on the northern riverbank; in return, he asked for the position of the governor of the conquered area and the monopoly of its commerce for ten years. At this juncture, a Mandaya chieftain named Datu Daupan, who then ruled [Samal Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samal_Island), came to him, seeking for an alliance against Datu Bago.[^cite_note-30] The two chieftains were archrivals, and Cruz de Uyanguren took advantage of it, initiating an alliance between Spain and the Mandayas of Samal Island. Intent on taking the settlement for Spain, he and his men accordingly assaulted it, but the Bagobo natives fiercely resisted the attacks, which resulted in his Samal Mandaya allies to desert. Thus, a three-month-long inconclusive battle for the possession of the settlement ensued which was only decided when an infantry company which sailed its way by warships from [Zamboanga](25167165.md) came in as reinforcements, thus ensuring the takeover of the settlement and its surroundings by the Spaniards while the defeated Bagobos fled further inland[^cite_note-31] while Datu Bago and his followers fled north to [Hijo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagum) where he would die two years later.[^cite_note-macariotiu-26]

After Cruz de Oyanguren defeated Bago and conquered Pinagurasan, he founded the town of *Nueva Vergara*, the future Davao, in the mangrove swamps of what is now Bolton Riverside on June 29, 1848,[^cite_note-32] in honor of his home in Spain and became its first [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor). Pinagurasan was then incorporated into the new town. Almost two years later on February 29, 1850, the province of *[Nueva Guipúzcoa](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva%20Guip%C3%BAzcoa)* was established via a royal decree, with the newly founded town as the capital,[^cite_note-33] once again to honor his homeland in Spain. When he was the governor of the province, however, his plans of fostering a positive economic sway on the region backfired, which resulted in his eventual replacement under orders of the colonial government.

The province of Nueva Guipuzcoa was dissolved on July 30, 1860, as it became the Politico-Military Commandery of Davao.[^cite_note-34] By the clamor of its natives, a petition was given to the Spanish government to rename Nueva Vergara into Davao, the latter being the name used by the natives since its founding. It was eventually accepted in 1867, and Nueva Vergara was given its present name Davao.[^cite_note-35]

The Spanish control of the town was unstable at best, as its [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad) and [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) natives routinely resisted the attempts of the Spanish authorities to forcibly resettle them and convert them into Christianity.[^cite_note-36] Despite all these, such were all done with the goal of making the governance of the area easier, dividing the Christians—both settlers and native converts—and the Muslim Moros into several religion-based communities within the town.

### During the Philippine Revolution

As the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution), having been fought for two years, neared its end in 1898, the expected departure of the Spanish authorities in Davao became apparent, although they took no part in the war at all, owing to the lack of revolutionary figures in the area save for a negligible pro-Filipino separatist rebel movement in the town of [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz,_Davao_del_Sur) in the south.[^cite_note-37] When the war finally ended, as the Spanish authorities finally left the town, two Davaoeño locals by the names of Pedro Layog and Jose M. Lerma represented the town and the region at the [Malolos Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos_Congress) of 1898, therefore indicating Davao as a part of the nascent [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic).[^cite_note-38]

The period of Filipino revolutionary control of Davao did not last long, however, as the Americans landed at the town later the same year. There was no record of locals offering any sort of resistance to the Americans.

### American period



Aerial view of Davao, 1935

As the Americans began their administration of the town in 1900, economic opportunities quickly arose as huge swathes of its areas, mainly lush forests and fertile grasslands, were declared open for agricultural investment. As a result, foreign businessmen, especially Japanese entrepreneurs, started settling the region, staking their claims on the vast lands of Davao and turning them into huge coconut and banana plantations.[^cite_note-39] In just a short period, Davao changed from a small and sparsely inhabited town into a bustling economic center serving the [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf) region, heavily populated alongside natives by tens of thousands of settlers and economic migrants from [Luzon](69375.md), [Visayas](72418.md) and Japan. The [Port of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Davao) was established and opened the same year to facilitate the international export of agricultural products from Davao.



[Davao City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City_Hall) was established in 1926 as the Municipal Hall when it was still a town.

Davao was incorporated as a part of [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province) from 1903 to 1914.[^cite_note-40] When the province was dissolved in 1914, it led to the establishment of [Davao Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(province\)), with Davao as its provincial capital. What is now the city's Legislative Council Building served as the provincial capitol.[^cite_note-41] It was built in 1926, the same year the Davao Municipal Hall, now the City Hall, was constructed.[^cite_note-42]

Japan-town, Davao City (circa 1930s)

Because of the rapidly increasing progress of the town, on March 16, 1936, congressman Romualdo Quimpo from Davao filed *Bill 609* (passed as *Commonwealth Act 51*), creating the City of Davao from the town of Davao and the municipal district of [Guianga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Davao_City). The bill called for the appointment of local officials by the president. By that time, the new city was already mostly populated with Japanese businessmen and settlers who then became its locals.[^cite_note-macariotiu-26] Davao was inaugurated as a [charter city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_city) on October 16, 1936, by President [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon);[^cite_note-TheCorpusJuris-CA51-43] the charter came into effect on March 1, 1937. It was one of the first two towns in [Mindanao](20546.md) to be converted into a city, the other being [Zamboanga](25167165.md).

#### Second World War

Main articles: [Battle of Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mindanao) and [Battle of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Davao)

On December 8, 1941, Japanese planes bombed the harbor,[^cite_note-44] and from December 20 they [landed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Davao) and began an occupation of the city which lasted until 1945. Davao was among the earliest to be occupied by Japanese forces, and the city was immediately fortified as a bastion of Japanese defense. Under the brutal Japanese regime, girls, teenagers and young adults were kidnapped by Japanese soldiers and were forced into becoming sex slaves under the oppressive "[comfort women](302767.md)" system, where the victims were routinely gang-raped and killed. Korean and Taiwanese nationals were also brought by the Japanese to Davao and were forced into becoming sex slaves.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

The city was subjected to extensive bombing by forces led by [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur) before American forces landed in [Leyte](194750.md) in October 1944. The [Battle of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mindanao) towards the end of [World War II](32927.md) was one of the longest and bloodiest battles during the Philippine Liberation, and brought tremendous destruction to the city, setting back the economic and physical strides made before the Japanese occupation.

### Postwar growth

See also: [History of the Philippines (1946–1965)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1946%E2%80%931965\))



Old seal of the city, [NHCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Commission_of_the_Philippines) version

Davao regained its status as the agricultural and economic hub of Mindanao after the war ended in 1945. Wood products such as plywood and timber, and more agricultural products being produced within the city, such as copra and other varieties of banana, became available for export. Some Japanese locals—80% percent of the city's population prior to the war's end—assimilated with the Filipino population, while others were expelled from the country by the Filipino locals, due to recent enmity.[^cite_note-48]

Davao was peaceful and increasingly progressive in the postwar period, including the 1950s and the mid-1960s. Ethnic tensions were minimal, and there was essentially no presence of secessionists groups in Mindanao.[^cite_note-MiclatinArguillas-49]

In 1967, the [Province of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(province\)) was divided into three provinces: [Davao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Norte), [Davao Oriental](296986.md) and [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur). The city of Davao became part of Davao del Sur;[^cite_note-TheCorpusJuris-RA4867-50] no longer the provincial capital, it became a commercial center of southern Mindanao. This period also saw the first ever election of an indigenous person to the office of Mayor of Davao City, when Elias Lopez, a full-blooded Bagobo, won the mayoral elections of 1967.[^cite_note-51]

### Social unrest, martial law and the 1980s

See also: [Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos), [Soledad Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledad_Duterte), and [Antonio L. Mabutas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_L._Mabutas)

By the late 1960s, Davao had become the regional capital of southern Mindanao; with the reorganization, it became the regional capital of the Davao Region (Region XI) and a highly urbanized city in the province of Davao del Sur.

Things began to take a turn for the worse late into [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos)'s first presidential term, when news about the [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre) ignited a furor in the Moro community, and ethnic tensions encouraged with the formation of secessionist movements.[^cite_note-52] An economic crisis in late 1969 led to social unrest, and violent crackdowns on protests led to the radicalization of many students throughout the country.[^cite_note-53] With no way to express their grievances about government abuses after the declaration of martial law in 1972, many of them joined the [New People's Army](1262096.md) (NPA), bringing the [Communist rebellion in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_rebellion_in_the_Philippines) to Davao and the rest of Mindanao for the first time.[^cite_note-MiclatinArguillas-49] The movement would continue to grow over the next decade, but was continuing to struggle by the early 1980s, convincing prominent party thinker and idealogue [Edgar Jopson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Jopson) to be based there, until he was killed after a raid in Skyline Subdivision in September 1982.[^cite_note-54]

The years immediately after Jopson's death saw an unmanageable increase in the Communist Party's ranks, however.[^cite_note-MadlosWrong-55] The [1983 Philippine economic nosedive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Philippine_economic_nosedive) and the [Assassination of Ninoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ninoy_Aquino) a few months later saw the New People's Army grow to seven fronts (Battalion sized units),[^cite_note-56] which coincided with a decision to experiment with strategies like urban insurrectionism.[^cite_note-MadlosWrong-55] Severe violence began with assassinations of corrupt officials and policemen, but mid-1984 and August 1985 saw 16 journalists killed where only six had been killed in the decade between 1975 and 1984.[^cite_note-Branigin1985MurderCapital-57] Agdao, the poor Barangay from which of the NPA got much of its support, began to be known as "Nicaragdao." Foreign press began labelling Davao the Philippines' "Murder Capital" and "Killing Fields,"[^cite_note-ChapmanPost-58] while the NPA's experiments with urban insurrectionism led all of Mindanao to be labeled "the laboratory of the revolution."[^cite_note-ICG2011-59] In order to keep its large number of cadres supplied, the NPA depended heavily on local citizens for supplies, increasing the incidences of abusive behavior, and reducing support for their cause.[^cite_note-ChapmanPost-58][^cite_note-60]

In 1984, rightwing vigilantes[^cite_note-61] with the support of [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) Davao City Metropolitan Command commander Lt. Colonel Franco Calida,[^cite_note-62] formed the armed group "Alsa Masa" (People's Rise) to counter them.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] Their presence, coinciding infighting within the NPA triggered by a hunt for deep penetration agents, was perceived as reducing NPA presence in Davao, but at the cost of the Alsa Masa itself committing human rights violations.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

Most Davao residents remained staunchly against violence from either extreme. Early examples had included the Roman Catholic Archbishop of Davao [Antonio L. Mabutas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_L._Mabutas), who was among the first religious leaders to peacefully speak out against the [Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship).[^cite_note-67] However, these peaceful citizens lacked the political clout to influence the situation much before 1983.[^cite_note-JubairinArguillas-68] One stabilizing element was the designation of then-Colonel [Rodolfo Biazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Biazon) as commander of the 3rd Marine Brigade assigned to Davao. In what the international press dubbed "the most sophisticated approach" to addressing the insurgency,[^cite_note-CSMBiazon-69] Biazon eschewed the aggressive stance preferred by the Philippine Constabulary and instead focused on outreach and community engagement—visiting schools and communities, and assuring the public that any Marine would be disciplined for any abuses committed.[^cite_note-CSMBiazon-69]

Ordinary citizens began to have more of a voice for change after the economic crisis of 1983, the [assassination of Ninoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Benigno_Aquino_Jr.),[^cite_note-JubairinArguillas-68] and the murder of prominent Davao City journalist [Alex Orcullo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Orcullo) at a checkpoint in [Barangay Tigatto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Davao_City) on October 19, 1984.[^cite_note-MiclatinArguillas-49] These seminal events prompted prominent city figures like [Soledad Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledad_Duterte)[^cite_note-\:1-70][^cite_note-\:2-71] to organize a protest group called the "Yellow Friday Movement",[^cite_note-72] which slowly gained support until 1986, when Marcos was finally ousted and forced into exile.[^cite_note-\:1-70][^cite_note-\:2-71]

### After the People Power revolution

Because the local leaders of the time were closely associated with Marcos, they were removed by the 1986 revolutionary government which took power after Marcos's ouster.[^cite_note-JubairinArguillas-68] President [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) then appointed Soledad Duterte's son, [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md), as temporary Vice Mayor of Davao.[^cite_note-81] Rodrigo Duterte later ran for Mayor of Davao City and won, taking the top city office from 1988 to 1998, from 2001 to 2010, and yet again from 2013 to 2016, after which [he became](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Rodrigo_Duterte) [President of the Philippines](271126.md).[^cite_note-82]

## Geography

Davao City is approximately 946 kilometers (588 mi) southeast of Manila over land, and 971 kilometers (524 nmi) by sea. The city is located in southeastern Mindanao, on the northwestern shore of [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf), opposite [Samal Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samal,_Davao_del_Norte). [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo) and [Mount Talomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apo%E2%80%93Talomo_Mountain_range) can be seen from here.

### Barangay

Main article: [Barangays of Davao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangays_of_Davao_City)

Davao City politically subdivided into 11 districts and 182 [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay). Each barangay consists of [puroks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purok) while some have [sitios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitios).

### Topography



Mouth of the [Davao River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_River) in Talomo District



[Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo) is the tallest mountain in the Philippines.

Davao City's land, totaling about 2,443.61 km<sup>2</sup> (943.48 sq mi), is hilly in the west (the Marilog district) and slopes down to the southeastern shore. [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo), the highest peak in the Philippines, is located at the city's southwestern tip. Mount Apo National Park (the mountain and its surrounding vicinity), was inaugurated by [President](271126.md) [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon) (in *Proclamation 59* of May 8, 1936) to protect the flora and fauna of the surrounding mountain range.[^cite_note-GovPH-Proc59-83]

The [Davao River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_River) is the city's primary drainage channel. Draining an area of over 1,700 km<sup>2</sup> (660 sq mi), the 160-kilometer (99 mi) river begins in the town of [San Fernando, Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Bukidnon). The mouth of the river is located at Barangay Bucana at Talomo District.

### Climate

Davao has a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Af*), with little seasonal variation in temperature. The areological mechanism of the [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone) occurs more often than that of the [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_wind) and because it experiences rare cyclones the climate is not purely equatorial but subequatorial. Average monthly temperatures are always above 26 °C (78.8 °F), and average monthly rainfall is above 77 millimeters (3.03 in). This gives the city a tropical climate, without a true [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season); while there is significant rainfall in winter, the largest rainfall occurs during the summer months (see climate chart, below).

### Flora and fauna

Mount Apo is home to many bird species, 111 of which are [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) to the area. It is also home to one of the world's largest eagles, the [critically endangered](7451605.md) [Philippine eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_eagle), the country's national bird. The Philippine Eagle Foundation is based near the city.[^cite_note-87] Plant species include the orchid *[waling-waling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waling-waling)*, also known as the "Queen of Philippine Flowers" as well as one of the country's [national flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_the_Philippines), which are also endemic to the area. Fruits such as [mangosteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangosteen) (known as the "queen of fruits") and [durian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durian) (known as the "king of fruits"), grow abundantly on Mount Apo.[^cite_note-88]

### Geology

Despite Davao City's location in the Asian portion of the Pacific [Ring of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire), the city has suffered few earthquakes and most have been minor. [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo), 40 km (25 mi) southwest from the city proper, is a [dormant volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormant_volcano).

As of the 2024 census, the city has a total population of 1,848,947 people.[^cite_note-2020Popcen-11] [Metro Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Davao), with the city as its center, had about 2.77 million inhabitants in 2015, making it the [third-most-populous metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Metropolitan_areas) in the Philippines and the most-populous city in Mindanao. ### Ethnicities

A Matigsalug woman in a red decorated tribal blouse making tribal bracelets

A [Matigsalug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matigsalug) woman from Davao

Residents of Davao City and the whole corresponding Davao Region are colloquially known as *[Davaoeños](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davaoe%C3%B1o_people)*. Nearly all local Davaoeños, mostly descended from migrant settlers from [Visayas](72418.md) and sometimes [Luzon](69375.md) in recent centuries and decades, are [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans) (an overwhelming majority are [Cebuanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people), with the rest being [Ilonggos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people) ([Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people) speakers)) and the respective [mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) from those groups, while others of different ethnicities—especially the indigenous people, who are collectively categorized as [Lumads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad)—make up the remainder of the local population. The Davao City residents of non-Visayan ethnicity are mostly [Tagalogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people), [Kapampangans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people), [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) and their respective [mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) from those groups, which are also descendants of migrant settlers from [Luzon](69375.md), particularly [Metro Manila](72340.md), [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon), [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon), [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region), the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region) and [Cagayan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley). The [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) ethnic groups of the city include the [Maguindanaons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people), [Sangils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangil_people), [Kalagans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_people), [Maranaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_people), [Iranuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun), [Tausugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taus%C5%ABg_people) and [Sama-Bajaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau_peoples). There are also historical and recent migrant [Chinese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) and [Japanese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_settlement_in_the_Philippines) coming from other parts of the Philippines that have made sizable communities in Davao. Modern more recent migrants also include [Indonesians](4885606.md), [Malaysians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysians), [Chinese people](372983.md) (both from [China](5405.md) and [Taiwan](25734.md)), [Japanese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people) and [Koreans](3440060.md#Davao), that have recently settled and made small communities in Davao City. Non-Asian foreigners, such as Americans and Europeans, are also present in the city in small numbers.[^cite_note-94]

[Philippine Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Hokkien) and [Japanese](15606.md) can also be heard privately spoken by [Chinese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) and [Japanese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_in_the_Philippines) among their fellows in Davao, whereas [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) ([Standard Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese)) and [Japanese](15606.md) is also taught in Chinese class and Japanese class of [Chinese Filipino schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_school) and [Japanese Nikkei Filipino schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_school).

A linguistic phenomenon has developed in the city whereby locals significantly mix [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) terms and grammar into their [Cebuano Bisaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) speech because of varying reasons such as, the normalcy of the older generations of Cebuano-speaking families speaking Tagalog to their children at home, some migrant settlers to the city are also from [Luzon](69375.md) where Tagalog is mainstream, and the modern Filipino [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) is also mostly in [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language) ([Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language)) and Filipino [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_\(education\)) is also taught in schools in Davao, while [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) is spoken in other everyday settings, making [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) a secondary casual [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca).

### Religion



The city's San Pedro Cathedral

The majority of Davao City's inhabitants are [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholics) [Christians](18337522.md), forming 78% of the population. The next largest religious affiliation is [Islam](6037917.md) at 4%.[^cite_note-95] Other Christian groups, such as the [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md), [Jesus Miracle Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Miracle_Crusade), [Pentecostal Missionary Church of Christ (4th Watch)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal_Missionary_Church_of_Christ_\(4th_Watch\)) and [Kingdom of Jesus Christ](47225546.md), form eighteen percent of the city's religious background.[^cite_note-96] The [Seventh-day Adventist Church](28632.md), the [United Church of Christ in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ_in_the_Philippines), the [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church) and the [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist) constitute the city's other Christian denominations. Some of the other faiths of the city are [Sikhism](27964.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Judaism](15624.md), [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) and irreligion.

The [Restorationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restorationism_\(Christian_primitivism\)) Church [Kingdom of Jesus Christ](47225546.md)[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] had its origins in the city. [Apollo Quiboloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Quiboloy), who claims to be the "Appointed Son of God", is the leader of the movement.

The [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Davao) is the main metropolitan see of the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) in southern Mindanao. It comprises the city of Davao, the [Island Garden City of Samal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Garden_City_of_Samal) and the municipality of [Talaingod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talaingod,_Davao_del_Norte) in [Davao del Norte province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Norte); under its jurisdiction are the three [suffragan dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan_diocese) of [Digos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digos), [Tagum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagum) and [Mati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mati,_Davao_Oriental) (the capital cities of the three Davao provinces). [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) [Romulo Valles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulo_Valles) of the Archdiocese of Davao, appointed on February 11, 2012, by [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI), took office on May 22, 2012, at San Pedro Cathedral. [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter), locally known as *San Pedro*, is the patron saint of the city.

### Industry

Agriculture remains the largest economic sector comprising banana, [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) and coconut plantations in the city. It is the island's leading exporter of fruits such as [mangoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), [pomeloes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomelo), bananas, coconut products, pineapples, [papayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), [mangosteens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangosteen) and [cacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean).

The chocolate industry is the newest development in the city. Malagos Chocolate, developed here by Malagos Agriventures Corp., is now the country's leading artisan chocolate recognized worldwide. On the other hand, Seed Core Enterprises is the country's biggest exporter of [cacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean) to [Barry Callebaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Callebaut).[^cite_note-111] Durian which is locally grown and harvested in the city, is also a notable export, although banana is the largest fruit export in the city. Local corporations like Lorenzo Group, Anflo Group, AMS Group, Sarangani Agricultural Corp. and Vizcaya Plantations Inc. have operations and headquarters here. Multinational companies like [Dole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dole_Food_Company), Sumifru/Sumitomo and [Del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Monte_Foods) have their regional headquarters here also.[^cite_note-112] [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf) provides livelihood for many fishermen. Some of the fish products include yellow fin tuna, brackish water milkfish, mudfish, shrimp and crab.[^cite_note-113] Most of the fish catches are discharged in the fishing port in Barangay Toril, which are then sold in the numerous markets within the city.

The city also serves as the main trade, commerce and industry hub of Mindanao and is also one of the financial hubs of Mindanao. [Phoenix Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Petroleum) is a multinational oil company based in Davao City and is the first company in the Philippines-based outside [Metro Manila](72340.md) to be in the [PSE Composite Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSE_Composite_Index). Several industrial plants such as those of Coca-Cola Bottlers, Phil., Pepsi-Cola Products, Phil., Interbev Phil Inc. and RC Cola Phil., companies are located in the city. There is also a number of fruit packaging-exporting facilities and food manufacturing plants as well as industrial construction plants such as Holcim Philippines, Union Galvasteel Corporation and SteelAsia. The SteelAsia plant is now the largest and most modern steel rolling mill production facility in the country, completed in December 2014[^cite_note-114] and was purposely built to increase the national steel production and to reduce the construction costs in Mindanao.

## Culture and heritage

### Foreign influence



Davao City's Chinatown is said to be the Philippines' biggest in terms of land area.

As with most cities in the Philippines, Christianity is widespread as a result of Spanish colonialism. Christian churches and chapels dot the city's landscape. A small number of temples, mosques and other religions' places of worship may also be found around the city.

A notable tradition brought by the Spanish still celebrated today in Davao City is the celebration of the feast day of each of the [barrios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrio) (villages) patron saints with a [festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival) (*fiesta*). These are celebrated through song and dance.

The biggest celebration native to the city is the [Kadayawan Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadayawan_Festival) in early to mid August which, in pre-colonial times was a celebration of the harvest. Today, it serves to commemorate the cultures of the indigenous tribes that inhabit the area surrounding Davao City. Many tribes people visit the city during this time. Festivities include native Mindanaoan street dances, motorcades featuring various clubs and social awareness groups based in the city and art exhibits in various locations featuring local artists and artisans.



*Indak-Indak sa Kadayawan* or the Street dancing competition, part of [Kadayawan Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadayawan_Festival) celebration

The [Davao Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Chinatown) is the primary residence of the Chinese community in the city. It has its own seaport, the Santa Ana Wharf, which is also a part of [Davao International Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Davao).

[Japanese cultural influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_culture), like the Chinese, is also prominent in the city.[^cite_note-128] The Japanese community was concentrated in [Barangay Mintal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mintal,_Davao_City?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the District of Tugbok, Davao City. In fact, a Japanese cemetery and Japanese shrine is located there. Evidence of Japanese influence are still visible in Mintal. There are various Japanese-owned businesses in the city as well. Davao City is also home to Philippine Nikkei Jin Kai International School, a Japanese-administered educational institution.

Several foreign communities reside in the city, including Indonesians, Malaysians, Koreans and Indians. There are [ESL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESL) schools for foreigners, and export-oriented industrial parks to entice Japanese and (South) Korean firms to set up shop in the city. However, there has been some cultural conflict over the integration of Koreans in the city, with then-city mayor Rodrigo Duterte complaining about their habit of smoking in public places.[^cite_note-129]

### Heritage

There are a number of cultural-heritage sites in the city, including the Davao Museum (in Insular Village, Lanang), the Mindanao Folk Arts Museum (Philippine Women's College, Juna Subdivision, Matina), Davaoeño Historical Society Museum (at Magallanes and Claveria Streets) and the Philippine-Japan Museum (Matsuo Compound, Calinan). Japanese historical sites include the Japanese Tunnel (used by Japanese forces during World War II), the 20th-century Japanese cemetery (now located inside Mintal Public Cemetery in Brgy. Mintal) and the Furukawa Fiber Plant (used by Yoshizo Furukawa as an abacá and banana plantation).[^cite_note-130]

The cuisine of Davao City features skewered and grilled meat dishes, but the most common dish served in the city is [kinilaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinilaw), made from [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna), [mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackerel), or [swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordfish) with [cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucumber) (and sometimes [radishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radish)) and [chili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper) marinated in vinegar. *Sinuglaw*, a portmanteau of *sinugba* (grilled) and *kinilaw* in the Cebuano language, is also a term for a dish in which diced, grilled pork belly is mixed with *kinilaw*.

Fruit dishes, snacks and desserts are also popular, most made from durian and bananas. [Durian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durian) also made appearance on Davao's culinary scene.

## Tourism



Land of Promise, located atop a hill at The Gap Farming Resort

Wikivoyage logo

Wikivoyage has a travel guide for ***[Davao City](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Davao%20City)***.

The [Philippine eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_eagle), the country's national bird and considered the largest eagle in the world, is endemic to Davao.[^cite_note-131] The orchid *[waling-waling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euanthe_sanderiana)* and fruits such as durians, marang, rambutans, pomeloes and mangosteens are popular and generally cheaper in the city. Tourist destinations in the city include the Philippine Eagle Foundation and Nature Center, [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo), Gap Farming Resort, the Davao Crocodile Park, Malagos Garden Resort, Eden Nature Park and [People's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Park_\(Davao_City\)) in the city center which is popular for its sculptures of indigenous people and dancing fountain. [Samal Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samal,_Davao_del_Norte), a part of [Metro Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Davao), is an island city situated immediately off the city's coast in Davao Gulf, popularly known for its scenic beaches.

Two major annual festivals are held in the city: the *Araw ng Dabaw* (Day of Davao) on March 1 (The city's incorporation day) and the [Kadayawan Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadayawan_Festival)[^cite_note-133] in August.[^cite_note-134] Also celebrated in the entire month of December, *Pasko Fiesta sa Davao* is an integration of festive and competitive Christmas activities showcasing colorful lightings and array of decorations in barangays, public parks, roads and buildings, and a series of competitive performances. Since 2024, a new festival is being held every June, the *Duaw Davao* (Visit Davao) showcases the city's diverse attractions, and vibrant music festivities, which ignites Davao's tourism spirit. The festival is revolved around four pillars: Davao TOURismo, [Fiesta sa San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul), Arts and [Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Month), and Sports and Lifestyle.[^cite_note-135] Originally celebrated in late June, it was expanded through the whole month of June the next year.[^cite_note-136] Formerly, the [Torotot Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City_Torotot_Festival)[^cite_note-colina-137] was held annually every New Year's Eve. First organized in the last day of 2013 during the 2014 New Year's Eve, it was organized as a recompense for the city firecracker-pyrotechnics ban; it includes a number of people simultaneously blowing party horns, locally known as *torotots*. It recorded a number of 7,568 people[^cite_note-tacio-138] participating in the first event, aiming to break the world record set by Japan for the most people simultaneously blowing party horns. Currently, it has been discontinued as the city's new year celebration is merged into *Pasko Fiesta sa Davao*, with the City Tourism themselves clarifying that the Torotot Festival was organized by a private company and there were no plans to host the said festival.[^cite_note-139]

During 2011, there were 1.075 million recorded tourist arrivals in the city, totaled from 81,081 foreign travelers, 983,315 local citizens and 10,604 balikbayans/overseas Filipino workers. Estimated tourist receipts were recorded at 12.81 billion pesos while estimated economic benefits were 28.19 billion pesos.[^cite_note-ner-110][^cite_note-140]

### &#x20;Land

Popular modes of public transportation in the city are multicabs, [jeepneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepneys), [tricycles](34555677.md), buses and taxis. Multicabs and jeepneys ply 82 designated passenger-vehicle routes around the clock. Tricycles ply routes beyond the main streets of the city. Taxis have several routes in and around Davao City. In mountainous areas, the *habal-habal* passenger motorcycle is the main mode of transportation

The city has the first taxis in the Philippines to accept payments from [BancNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BancNet) and [MegaLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MegaLink) ATM and debit cards.[^cite_note-141] The black taxis are linked to the [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) (GPS), and dispatching is done by computer.[^cite_note-142]

The city offers a wide bus network to cities and provinces in Mindanao and as far as [Pasay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasay) in Luzon, and [Tagbilaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagbilaran), [Ormoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormoc) and [Tacloban](746499.md) in the Visayas. The city is accessible by bus from several points in Mindanao such as [Kidapawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidapawan), [General Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos), [Digos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digos), [Koronadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koronadal), [Tagum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagum), [Tandag](877402.md), [Bislig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislig), [Mati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mati,_Davao_Oriental), [Monkayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkayo), [Malita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malita,_Davao_Occidental), [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan) and [Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City).

Construction and improvement of roads and bridges in the city are underway. The city's third major road, the Buhangin Underpass, was completed in the first quarter of 2003. The Traffic Management and Computerization Scheme was implemented, considered one of the most modern in the country.[^cite_note-143]

A 28-kilometer (17 mi) monorail project, named the [Davao People Mover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_People_Mover), has been endorsed by the City Government to the Department of Transportation (DOTr) and Philippine National Railways (PNR).[^cite_note-144]

A road project, dubbed [Davao City Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City_Bypass_Road), was expected to be completed in 2022. It aims to reduce travel time from 1 hour and 44 minutes via Pan-Philippine Highway and Diversion Road to 49 minutes via Davao By-Pass road.[^cite_note-145] However, it was postponed until 2028.[^cite_note-146]

The [Davao City Coastal Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City_Coastal_Road) is intended to provide an alternative route to the Pan-Philippine Highway in the southern part of the city, which has been experiencing heavy traffic congestion. It will also serve as a costal shore protection and breakwater.[^cite_note-147]

#### Mindanao Railway Phase 1 (Tagum-Davao-Digos section)

The first phase of the [Mindanao Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao_Railway) is a 100-kilometer (62 mi) railway line connecting the cities of Davao Region: [Tagum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagum), Davao City and [Digos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digos).[^cite_note-148] The project is set to have 8 stations and a daily ridership of 130,000, but it has not yet received funding from other countries or lenders. The project was supposed to be financed by the [Chinese](5405.md) [official development assistance](1316305.md), but it was taken away in 2023.[^cite_note-149]

#### Davao Public Transport Modernization Project

See also: [Davao Public Transport Modernization Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Public_Transport_Modernization_Project)

Formerly known as the High Priority Bus System (HPBS), it includes the development of improved public transport operations that will cover the full urban area of the city. The project will overhaul the outdated services that provide a poor level of service in terms of coverage area, hours of operation, and passenger comfort and facilities. The project will introduce a city-wide bus-based public transport operating system, with improved and modern franchising mechanisms that ensure supply meets demand. This will be supported through traffic management improvements to prioritize bus services along a core network of around 110 kilometers (km).[^cite_note-150]

The project involves delivering a modern, high-priority bus system for Metro Davao, wherein interconnected bus services will be prioritized along various inter-city routes. It will include 1,000 buses and 1,000 bus stops spread out in Davao City. Of the 1,000 buses, 300 will be electric articulated buses, 500 will be regular 12-meter buses, and 200 will be the smaller nine-meter buses. It has an allocated budget of ₱80 billion.[^cite_note-151]

### Sea

Large ship approaching a wharf

The [Port of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Davao) is the busiest port in Mindanao.

Davao is connected to other major cities of the Philippines by [roll-on/roll-off](1457484.md) inter-island ferries. The city is served by domestic passenger ferries at Sasa International Seaport and Santa Ana Wharf, the international seaports of the [Port of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Davao), the busiest port in Mindanao.[^cite_note-Port_of_Davao-152] The port is capable of servicing inter-island and international shipments. It is located in Davao Gulf and has two approaches, one at Pakiputan Strait between Davao and western [Samal Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samal_Island).

The Davao City and General Santos to Bitung, Indonesia sea connectivity route has also had just been started very recently. This route will enable traders from Mindanao to easily export goods and commodities like food and beverage, electronics and garments, beauty products, fertilizer, construction materials, agricultural inputs, tin cans and packaging materials up to North Sulawesi in Indonesia. The roll-on-roll-off (Ro-Ro) shipping service which will serve the route is Asian Marine Transport, a Philippine-registered shipping firm and operator of the Super Shuttle Ferry, Super Shuttle Roro and Shuttle Fast Ferry vessels.

White air-traffic control tower

Located north of the city center, [Francisco Bangoy International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Bangoy_International_Airport) is the main airport serving the city and the region. ## Utilities

[Davao Light and Power Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Light_and_Power_Company), an Aboitiz company which is the third-largest electric utility company in the country, serves the city's electricity needs. It had its own gas-operating power plant at Bajada district and 300-megawatt coal-fired power plant in Toril district.

Davao City Water District is the main water supplier in the city. It obtains its water supply from the mountain springs in the city's western portion as well as from underground or surface water sources.[^cite_note-155] The city's water supply runs through the company's production wells, sumps and reservoirs within the city. Its biggest water supply system is located at Barangay Dumoy.

## Healthcare

There are 31 hospitals and tertiary centers in the city like the United Davao Specialists Hospital and Medical Center, Inc. (Unidav), Davao Doctors Hospital, San Pedro Hospital, Brokenshire Medical Center, Ricardo Limso Medical Center, Davao Medical School Foundation Hospital (DMSF Hospital), Metro Davao Medical and Research Center, Adventist Hospital, MMG Hospital, CHDC Hospital and the Southern Philippines Medical Center. In addition, [CURE International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CURE_International), a non-profit organization that operates charitable hospitals and programs worldwide, operates the [Tebow CURE Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CURE_International#Tebow_CURE_Hospital_\(CURE_Philippines\)), which is an orthopedic specialty hospital located in Davao City. It provides elective surgeries to both children and adults. Their primary charitable mission is to heal disabled children with conditions such as clubfoot, bowed legs, other bone deformities, untreated burns and cleft lip.[^cite_note-156] Ecoland Medical and Wellness Center (EcoMed), one of the first DOH-licensed private primary care facilities in Mindanao, is located along Quimpo Boulevard in the southern part of the city. EcoMed offers primary healthcare and diagnostic services (laboratory, radiology and pharmacy), a 24-hour outpatient department (that includes a birthing facility and minor operating room) that can handle and treat minor and primary health emergencies, and a range of outpatient healthcare services through its renal dialysis unit, doctor's clinics (covering a range of specializations), and its ambulatory surgical unit.

Davao City is also noted and have been praised by the World Health Organization for its smoke-free policy since 2002,[^cite_note-auto1-157] the first in the Philippines.[^cite_note-158]

## Law and order

Law and order is maintained by the [Philippine National Police](1313055.md) and a special military group, Task Force (TF) Davao. TF Davao is formed to protect the city from terrorist attacks and other crime and is affiliated with the [Philippine Army](6032559.md) and headed by an army [colonel](199023.md).[^cite_note-159]

A [curfew](297681.md) on minors is enforced. All businesses, especially [bars](272207.md) and [discos](19321330.md), are mandated by a city ordinance to stop selling alcoholic drinks at 1\:00 am (final approval last July 24, 2013). [Motorcyclists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcyclist) without helmets and motorists with defective lights are not legally allowed to enter (or drive in) the city.[^cite_note-160] Executive Order No. 39 imposes the reduction of speed limits for all kinds of motor vehicles within the territorial jurisdiction of Davao City.[^cite_note-161]

Former President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md), mayor of Davao for 22 years, has been credited with making Davao one of the world's safest cities.[^cite_note-162] Rights groups, however, claim that he cites this as justification for his [national drug policy](51032591.md).[^cite_note-163] Raw crime data from the Philippine National Police for the years 2010 to 2015 shows that the city had the highest murder rate in the Philippines,[^cite_note-164] and the second-highest number of rapes.[^cite_note-g-165]

In February 2018, the Davao City Council officially declared Senator [Antonio Trillanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Trillanes) "*[persona non grata](521241.md)*" after Trillanes portrayed the city as the most dangerous in the Philippines and likening Dabawenyos to North Koreans who are easily brainwashed by President Rodrigo Duterte.[^cite_note-166] "Safest city" rankings are often cited from [Numbeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbeo), a [crowd-sourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowd-sourced) survey website; as of February 2018, Davao had dropped to 275th out of 330 cities in Numbeo's "crime index".[^cite_note-n1-167][^cite_note-n2-168][^cite_note-n3-169]

The Public Safety and Security Command Center (PSSCC), the first in the Philippines, is located in Sandawa, Matina. It is headquarters for [911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-1-1_\(Philippines\)) and the center for the 170 closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras installed in different strategic areas as of today covering access roads and populated downtown areas, and also including outside the Francisco Bangoy International Airport and six in different bridges to monitor the rise of water level in the city's rivers. The center also controls traffic signals in the city.[^cite_note-170]

In Davao City, by city ordinance, police ensure that prostitutes have a valid health card, but do not arrest them, as prostitution is considered a health issue for the women involved and is not a police matter.[^cite_note-171] Jeanette Ampog, the executive director of [Talikala](https://web.archive.org/web/20170101091334/http://talikala.net/), a Davao-based [NGO](46539.md) that helps prostitutes, said in October 2016 that child prostitution had sharply increased over the past two years. She said that children were cheaper and more marketable.[^cite_note-172] Nevertheless, the city was awarded "Most Child-Friendly City for Highly Urbanized Category" in 2013, 2014 and 2017. The city also won the same award in 1998 and 1999.[^cite_note-173] Also in 2017, the city was awarded with the "Best Disaster Risk Reduction and Management".[^cite_note-174]

The city's *Executive Order No. 04, Series of 2013* imposes an order on creating the implementation of rules and regulations for the new comprehensive anti-smoking ordinance no. 0367–12, Series of 2012.[^cite_note-175] Davao City's [Firecracker Ban](418188.md#Firecracker_ban) was also implemented with ordinance No. 060-02/1406-02, Series of 2002.[^cite_note-cnnphdvo-176]

From 2015[^cite_note-177] to 2017, Davao City was among the local government units awarded with a "Seal of Good Local Governance" by the [Department of the Interior and Local Government](4718691.md).[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

### Davao Death Squad

Main article: [Davao Death Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Death_Squad)

The controversial [Davao Death Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Death_Squad) is a [vigilante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilante) group supposedly active in Davao City. The group is allegedly responsible for [summary executions](1227309.md) of [street children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_children_in_the_Philippines) and individuals suspected of petty crimes and dealing in drugs in Davao.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-180] It has been estimated that the group is responsible for the killing or disappearance of between 1,020 and 1,040 people between 1998 and 2008.[^cite_note-New_York_Times-181]\[^cite_note-[www.news.com.au-182](http://www.news.com.au-182/)] An investigation in 2009 was later discontinued.[^cite_note-183] Another investigation by the [Office of the Ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Ombudsman_of_the_Philippines) was opened and closed in 2019, stating that they found no evidence that the alleged group exists.[^cite_note-Reuters-DeathSquads-184]

## Sports



The stadium and track-and-field area of [Davao City Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City%E2%80%93UP_Sports_Complex).



*[Sabong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabong)* or cockfight in Davao City.

Sports facilities in the city include the Davao City Recreation Center (Almendras Gymnasium), Tionko Football Field (near Agro College and the Davao River) and the gymnasiums of Ateneo de Davao University, Philippine Women's College of Davao's Rosa Santos Munda Events Center (RSM Events Center), the University of Southeastern Philippines, Holy Cross of Davao College, the University of Mindanao and Mintal Comprehensive High School. The main sports center of Davao City and also the largest is the [Davao City Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City%E2%80%93UP_Sports_Complex), which hosted the [2019 National Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Palarong_Pambansa).

There are locally based sports teams in the city. [Davao Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_F.A.), working under the [Philippine Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Football_Federation), represents the city and Davao Region for national [football](10568.md) events. Locally based basketball teams such as *Goldstar Davao* and *Duterte Agilas* work for the Mindanao Visayas Basketball Association. Collegiate varsity teams based in the tertiary institutions inside the city also compete in national competitions. Despite being based outside of the city or province, the [Davao Occidental Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Occidental_Tigers) of the [Maharlika Pilipinas Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika_Pilipinas_Basketball_League) (MPBL) normally play their home games in Davao City.

[Sabong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabong) or cockfighting events are also held in cockfighting arenas within the city. In June 2015, the city held the country's largest cockfighting event, the sixth Annual Thunderbird Challenge.[^cite_note-185]

## Foreign relations

The influx of foreign visitors and the presence of [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriates) and [migrants](4599312.md) in the city have prompted the governments of Japan, China, Malaysia and Indonesia to open Consulates-General in the city, while Palau and the United States have consular offices. Honorary consulates of the Czech Republic,[^cite_note-186] Mexico,[^cite_note-187] Austria,[^cite_note-188] Spain,[^cite_note-189] Timor Leste,[^cite_note-190] Denmark,[^cite_note-191] and South Korea were also recently established. The United States Embassy in the Philippines opened a virtual consulate, where inquiries regarding [visas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_\(document\)), foreign-relations concerns and travel to the United States can be made by [e-mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mail) and [chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_chat). The virtual consulate is maintained in coordination with [Ateneo de Davao University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateneo_de_Davao_University), [University of Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mindanao), [University of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Immaculate_Conception), [Holy Cross of Davao College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Cross_of_Davao_College) and AMA Computer College.
