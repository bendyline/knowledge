**Lublin**[^cite_note-7] is [the eighth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Poland) in [Poland](22936.md)[^cite_note-TERYT-8] and the second-largest city of historical [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland). It is the capital and the centre of [Lublin Voivodeship](344869.md) with a population of 336,339 (December 2021).[^cite_note-population-2] Lublin is the largest Polish city east of the [Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula) River, located 153 km (95 mi) southeast of [Warsaw](32908.md).

One of the events that greatly contributed to the city's development was the [Polish–Lithuanian Union of Krewo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Krewo) in 1385. Lublin thrived as a centre of trade and commerce due to its strategic location on the route between [Vilnius](32597.md) and [Kraków](16815.md); the inhabitants had the privilege of free trade in the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). The Lublin [Parliament](28167.md) session of 1569 led to the creation of a [real union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin) between the [Crown of the Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, thus creating the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth). Lublin witnessed the early stages of the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) in the 16th century. A [Calvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinist) congregation was founded and groups of radical [Polish Brethren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Brethren) (called [Arians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianism) by their detractors) appeared in the city, making it an important global centre of [Unitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarianism).[^cite_note-9]

Until the [partitions at the end of the 18th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland), Lublin was an important [royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city) of the Kingdom of Poland. Its delegates, alike [nobles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta), had the right to participate in the [royal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_elections_in_Poland). In 1578, Lublin was chosen as the seat of the [Crown Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Tribunal), the highest [appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal) [court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court) in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, and for centuries, the city has been flourishing as a centre of culture and higher learning.

In 2011, the analytical [Financial Times Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times_Group) found Lublin to be one of the best cities for business in Poland.[^cite_note-10] The [Foreign Direct Investment ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) placed Lublin second among larger Polish cities in the [cost-effectiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost-effectiveness_analysis) category. Lublin is noted for its green spaces and a high [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living);[^cite_note-11] the city has been selected as the 2023 [European Youth Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Capital) and 2029 [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Its historical [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Old_Town) is one of Poland's national [monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Historical_Monuments_\(Poland\)) (*[Pomnik historii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomnik_historii)*) tracked by the [National Heritage Board of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narodowy_Instytut_Dziedzictwa).[^cite_note-14]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Lublin)



[Kraków Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w_Gate_in_Lublin) in the Old Town is among the city's most recognisable landmarks.

Archaeological finds indicate a long presence of cultures in the area. A complex of settlements started to develop on the future site of Lublin and in its environs in the sixth to seventh centuries. Remains of settlements dating back to the sixth century were discovered in the centre of today's Lublin on Czwartek ("Thursday") Hill.

The [early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages) were marked by an intensified settlement of people, particularly in the areas along river valleys. The settlements were centred around the stronghold on Old Town Hill, which was likely one of the main centres of the [Lendians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lendians), a [Lechitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechites) tribe. When the tribal [stronghold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stronghold) was destroyed in the 10th century, the centre shifted to the northeast, to a new stronghold above Czechówka valley and, after the mid-12th century, to Castle Hill.

At least two churches are presumed to have existed in Lublin in the early medieval period. One of them was most probably erected on Czwartek Hill during the rule of [Casimir the Restorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_the_Restorer) in the 11th century.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] The castle became the seat of a [castellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castellan), first mentioned in historical sources from 1224, but was quite possibly present from the start of the 12th or even 10th century. The oldest historical document mentioning Lublin dates from 1198, so the name must have come into general use some time earlier.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15]

The location of Lublin at the eastern borders of the Polish lands gave it military significance. During the first half of the 13th century, Lublin was a target of attacks by [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols), [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars), [Ruthenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenians), and [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians), which resulted in its destruction.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] It was also ruled by [Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia) between 1289 and 1302.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] Lublin was founded as a town by [Władysław I the Elbow-high](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_I_the_Elbow-high) or between 1258 and 1279 during the rule of the prince [Bolesław V the Chaste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_V_the_Chaste).[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] [Casimir III the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_III_the_Great), appreciating the site's strategic importance, built a masonry castle in 1341 and encircled the city with defensive walls.[^cite_note-lublin.eu-16] From 1326, if not earlier, the stronghold on Castle Hill included a chapel in honor of the Holy Trinity. A stone church dating to 1335–1370 exists to this day.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15]

### Jagiellonian Poland



[Neogothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neogothic) façade of [Lublin Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Castle)



[Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Castle) courtyard with a fortified [keep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep)

In 1392, the city received an important trade privilege from the king [Władysław II Jagiełło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_II_Jagie%C5%82%C5%82o). With the coming of peace between Poland and Lithuania, it developed into a trade centre, handling a large portion of commerce between the countries. In 1474, the area around Lublin was carved out of [Sandomierz Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandomierz_Voivodeship) and combined to form the [Lublin Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Voivodeship_\(1474%E2%80%931795\)), the third voivodeship of Lesser Poland.

During the 15th and 16th centuries, the town grew rapidly. The largest trade fairs of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) were held in Lublin. In the 16th century, the parliaments (*[Sejm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland)*) of the Kingdom of Poland were held in Lublin several times. On 26 June 1569, one of the most important proclaimed the [Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin), which united Poland and [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). Lublin as one of the most influential cities[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] of the state enjoyed voting rights during the [royal elections in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_elections_in_Poland).

Some of the artists and writers of the 16th century [Polish renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_renaissance) lived and worked in Lublin, including [Sebastian Klonowic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Klonowic) and [Jan Kochanowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kochanowski), who died in the city in 1584. In 1578, the [Crown Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Tribunal), the highest court of the [Lesser Poland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Province,_Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland), was established in Lublin.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15]

Since the second half of the 16th century, [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation) movements devolved in Lublin, and a large congregation of [Polish Brethren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Brethren) was present in the city. One of Poland's most important Jewish communities was established in Lublin around this time.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] Jews established a widely respecte&#x64;*&#x20;yeshiva*, Jewish hospital, synagogue, [cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Jewish_Cemetery,_Lublin), and education centre (*kahal*) and built the Grodzka Gate (known as the Jewish Gate) in the historic district. Jews were a vital part of the city's life until the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust), during which they were relocated by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) to the infamous [Lublin Ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Ghetto) and ultimately murdered.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15]



*[Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin_\(painting\))*, painting by [Jan Matejko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Matejko) at the [National Museum of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum,_Lublin)

The yeshiva became a centre of learning of [Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud) and [Kabbalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah), leading the city to be called "the Jewish [Oxford](31797.md)".[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] In 1567, the *[rosh yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosh_yeshiva)* (headmaster) received the title of rector from the king along with rights and privileges equal to those of the heads of Polish universities.

The city declined due to the disastrous [Deluge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deluge_\(history\)), when it was invaded by [Russo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia)-[Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) forces in 1655, and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) in 1656.

### 19th and early 20th century

After the [Third Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland) in 1795, Lublin was located in the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy), then following the [Austro-Polish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Polish_War) of 1809 it was part of the short-lived Polish [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw), and then in 1815 it became part of the [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland) in the [Russian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Partition) of Poland.



A 19th-century drawing of the Lublin's Old Town by Adam Lerue

At the beginning of the 19th century, new squares, streets, and public buildings were built. In 1877, a railway connection to Warsaw and [Kovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovel) and [Lublin Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Station) were constructed, spurring industrial development. Lublin's population grew from 28,900 in 1873 to 50,150 in 1897 (including 24,000 Jews).[^cite_note-17]

Russian rule ended in 1915, when the city was occupied by German and Austro-Hungarian armies. After the defeat of the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) in 1918, the [Provisional People's Government of the Republic of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_People's_Government_of_the_Republic_of_Poland)—the first government of independent Poland—operated in Lublin for a short time. In the interwar years, the city continued to modernise and its population grew; important industrial enterprises were established, including the first aviation factory in Poland, the [Plage i Laśkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plage_i_La%C5%9Bkiewicz) works, later nationalised as the [LWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LWS_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)) factory. The [Catholic University of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Lublin) was founded in 1918.

In 1921, Roman Catholics constituted 58.9% of the city's population, with Jews at 39.5%. In 1931, 63.7% of the inhabitants were Roman Catholic and 34.7% Jewish.[^cite_note-18]

On 20 July 1931 [a violent tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_Lublin_tornado) carved a path of destruction through the city, destroying dozens of structures in downtown and killing six people. This tornado is officially rated F4 on the [Fujita scale](165206.md); however, the Polish Weather Service estimated winds at 246 to 324 mph (396 to 521 km/h), potentially ranking it as an F5.[^cite_note-ESWD-19]

### World War II



German and Soviet troops in Lublin during the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) in September 1939

In early September 1939, during the joint German-Soviet [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), which started [World War II](32927.md), the Polish government evacuated a portion of the Polish [gold reserve](1056205.md) from Warsaw to Lublin, and then further east to [Łuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81uck),[^cite_note-20] before the city was [occupied by Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)), and found itself in the newly formed [General Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government) territory. The Polish population became a target of severe Nazi persecutions focusing on [intelligentsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsia) and Polish Jews. In November 1939, during the *[Intelligenzaktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligenzaktion)*, the Germans carried out mass arrests of hundreds of Poles, including teachers, judges, lawyers, engineers, priests, lecturers of the local theological seminary, and lecturers and students of the [Catholic University of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Lublin),[^cite_note-mw247-21] which the occupiers closed down.[^cite_note-mw247-21] Arrested Poles were held in a prison established in the Lublin Castle, and many were afterwards deported to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps).[^cite_note-mw247-21]

On 23–25 December 1939, the Germans carried out massacres of 31 Poles in several locations in Lublin.[^cite_note-mw248-22] Among the victims were lawyers, professors, school principals, [starosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starost) of [Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_County) and [Lubartów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubart%C3%B3w_County) counties and other well-known and respected citizens of the region.[^cite_note-mw248-22] In January and February 1940, the occupiers arrested 23 [Capuchin friars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin) and 43 [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) friars.[^cite_note-23] Persecution of Polish intelligentsia was continued with the *[AB-Aktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_AB-Aktion_in_Poland)*. On 24 June 1940, the Germans carried out mass arrests of over 800 Poles in Lublin, who were then imprisoned in the castle, along with dozens of Poles who were arrested at the same time in other towns in the region, including [Biała Podlaska](389586.md), [Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m), [Puławy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu%C5%82awy).[^cite_note-mw264-24] Many of the prisoners were then deported to the [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp) and [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp) concentration camps, while around 500 Poles were murdered in [five large massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executions_in_Rury_Jezuickie) carried out in the present-day district of [Rury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rury,_Lublin) in 1940.[^cite_note-25] Among the victims of the massacres were both men and women: doctors, engineers, local officials, lawyers, judges, activists, military officers, parliamentarians, [Polish resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) members, policemen, teachers and school and university students.[^cite_note-mw264-24]



Monument and cemetery in [Rury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rury,_Lublin) where the Germans [massacred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executions_in_Rury_Jezuickie) around 500 Poles in 1940

An attempt to "[Germanise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanisation_in_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\))" the city led to an influx of the ethnic *[Volksdeutsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksdeutsche)*, increasing the number of German minority from 10–15% in 1939 to 20–25%. Near Lublin, the so-called "reservation" for the Jews was built based on the idea of racial segregation known as the "[Nisko or Lublin Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nisko_Plan)".[^cite_note-MajerMuseum2003-26]

The Germans established and operated a [Baudienst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baudienst) [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) camp for [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) in Lublin.[^cite_note-27] Many Poles from or associated with Lublin, including 94 lecturers, alumni and students of the Catholic University of Lublin were murdered by the Soviets in the large [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre) in April–May 1940.[^cite_note-28]

The Jewish population was forced into the newly established [Lublin Ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Ghetto). The city served as headquarters for [Operation Reinhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Reinhardt), the main German effort to exterminate all Jews in occupied Poland. The majority of the ghetto inmates, about 26,000 people, were deported to the [Bełżec extermination camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be%C5%82%C5%BCec_extermination_camp) between 17 March and 11 April 1942. The remainder were moved to facilities around the [Majdanek concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek_concentration_camp) established at the outskirts of the city. Almost all of Lublin's Jews were murdered during the [Holocaust in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_in_Poland). The secret [Polish Council to Aid Jews "Żegota"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBegota), established by the [Polish resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) operated in the city.[^cite_note-29] There are also known cases of local Polish men and women, who were captured and sent to either forced labour or concentration camps by the Germans for [sheltering and aiding Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_of_Jews_by_Poles_during_the_Holocaust).[^cite_note-30] Poles who saved Jews in other places in the region were also temporarily imprisoned in the local castle, before being sent to the Auschwitz concentration camp.[^cite_note-31]



The site of the former [Majdanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek) concentration camp, located on the outskirts of Lublin

After the war, some survivors emerged from hiding with the [Christian rescuers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Righteous_Among_the_Nations) or returned from the Soviet Union, and re-established a small Jewish community in the city, but their numbers were insignificant. Most survivors left Poland for Israel, the United States and other countries.[^cite_note-Ścieżki-32]

In the first years of the occupation, many [expelled Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Poles_by_Nazi_Germany) from [Gdańsk](12099.md) and German-annexed Pomerania were deported to Lublin,[^cite_note-33] and later on, in 1943, around 9,000 [expelled Poles from the nearby Zamojszczyzna region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing_of_Zamojszczyzna_by_Nazi_Germany) were brought to Lublin and imprisoned in the Majdanek concentration camp and in a transit camp at Krochmalna Street; many were afterwards deported to forced labour in Germany.[^cite_note-maj-34] In August 1943, thanks to efforts of the Polish *Rada Główna Opiekuńcza* charity organisation, around 2,200 people were released from those two camps.[^cite_note-maj-34] Many of the released people, including hundreds of [kidnapped Polish children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_of_children_by_Nazi_Germany), were extremely exhausted or sick, and were taken to local hospitals,[^cite_note-maj-34] which quickly became overcrowded.[^cite_note-mgrs-35] Many exhausted children died soon.[^cite_note-mgrs-35] Lublin pharmacists and residents organized help for the children, and after leaving the hospital, the people were taken in by the inhabitants of the surrounding villages, which resulted in an [epidemic typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic_typhus) outbreak, which caused many deaths among the population.[^cite_note-mgrs-35]

On 24 July 1944, the city was taken by the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) and became the temporary headquarters of the [Soviet-controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_Poland) communist [Polish Committee of National Liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Committee_of_National_Liberation) established by Joseph Stalin, which was to serve as the basis for a puppet government. The Soviets carried out arrests of Polish resistance members, including the regional delegate of the [Polish government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_government-in-exile), Władysław Cholewa, and the commander of the regional branch of the [Home Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Army), Colonel Kazimierz Tumidajski, who was eventually killed in Russian captivity in 1947.[^cite_note-36] The capital of new Poland was moved to Warsaw in January 1945 after the Soviet westward offensive.

### Post-war period

In the postwar years, Lublin continued to grow, tripling its population and greatly expanding its area. A considerable scientific and research base was established around the newly founded [Maria Curie-Skłodowska University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Curie-Sk%C5%82odowska_University). A large automotive factory, [Fabryka Samochodów Ciężarowych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabryka_Samochod%C3%B3w_Ci%C4%99%C5%BCarowych) (FSC), was built in the city. In 2017, the city was awarded the [Europe Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Prize) by the [Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) for having made exceptional efforts to spread the ideal of European unity.[^cite_note-37]

## Geography



Zemborzyce Lake

Lublin lies in eastern [Poland](22936.md) on the [Lublin Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Upland). The [Bystrzyca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bystrzyca_\(Wieprz_tributary\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) river, a tributary of the [Wieprz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wieprz) river, passes through the city and divides it into two parts: western, with a more varied relief with [loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) gorges, and eastern, which is flat. In the southern part of the city there is an artificial Zemborzyce Lake created by damming Bystrzyca.

The area of the city is 147 km2. The highest point lies at a height of 235.0 m (771.0 ft) and the lowest point at a height of 163.6 m (537 ft).

Lublin has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfb*) with cold, damp winters and warm summers.

## Administration



Districts of Lublin

Lublin is the capital of the province called [Lublin Voivodeship](344869.md), a province ([voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland)) created in 1999. The city is a separate urban [gmina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina) and city county ([powiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powiat)).

### Municipal government

Lublin is governed by the municipal legislature known as the [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council) (Rada Miasta) and the city's mayor (Prezydent Miasta). The city council is made up of 31 councillors directly elected by the city's inhabitants. The remit of the council and president extends to all areas of municipal policy and development planning, up to and including the development of local infrastructure, transport, and planning permission. The city's current mayor is [Krzysztof Żuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_%C5%BBuk), who has served in this position since 2010.[^cite_note-49]

### Symbols

Lublin has an official flag, a 5\:8 rectangle divided into three horizontal stripes: white (top), green (narrow, middle), and red (bottom). In the central part, there is the coat of arms of Lublin. It is also allowed to hang the flag in the form of a vertical ribbon: then white should be on the left side or near the spar.

### Districts

Lublin is divided into 27 administrative divisions (dzielnica):[^cite_note-50] Abramowice, Bronowice, Czechów Południowy, Czechów Północny, Czuby Południowe, Czuby Północne, Dziesiąta, Felin, Głusk, Hajdów-Zadębie, Kalinowszczyzna, Konstantynów, Kośminek, Ponikwoda, Rury, Sławin, Sławinek, Stare Miasto, Szerokie, Śródmieście, Tatary, Węglin Południowy, Węglin Północny, Wieniawa, Wrotków, Za Cukrownią, and Zemborzyce.

## Economy and infrastructure

The Lublin region is a part of eastern Poland, which has benefited less from the economic transformation after 1989 than regions of Poland located closer to [Western Europe](33800.md). Despite the fact that Lublin is one of the closest neighbour cities for Warsaw, the investment inflow in services from the Polish capital has secured a steady growth due to relatively fast connection, while external investments are progressing, enabling nearby satellite municipality [Świdnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awidnik) for large-scale industrial investments.



Polish MPs in the [PZL-Świdnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZL-%C5%9Awidnik) helicopter factory



Perła – [Browary Lubelskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browary_Lubelskie)

Lublin is a regional centre of IT companies. Asseco Business Solutions S.A., eLeader Sp z o.o., CompuGroup Medical Polska Sp. z o.o., Abak-Soft Sp. z o.o. and others have their headquarters here. Other companies (for example [Comarch S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarch), Britenet Sp. z o.o., Simple S.A., [Asseco Poland S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asseco_Poland)) outsourced to Lublin, to take advantage of the educated specialists. There is a visible growth in professionals eager to work in Lublin, due to reasons like quality of life, culture management, the environment, improving connection to Warsaw, levels of education, or financial, because of usually higher operating margins of global organisations present in the area.

The large car factory [Fabryka Samochodów Ciężarowych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabryka_Samochod%C3%B3w_Ci%C4%99%C5%BCarowych) (FSC) was acquired by the [South Korean](27019.md) [Daewoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daewoo) conglomerate in the early 1990s. With Daewoo's financial troubles in 1998 related to the [Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_financial_crisis), the production at FSC practically collapsed and the factory entered bankruptcy.[^cite_note-56] Efforts to restart its van production succeeded when the engine supplier bought the company to keep its prime market. With the decline of Lublin as a regional industrial centre, the city's economy has been reoriented toward service industries. Currently, the largest employer is the [Maria Curie-Sklodowska University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Curie-Sklodowska_University).

The price of land and investment costs are lower than in western Poland. However, the Lublin area has to be one of the main beneficiaries of the EU development funds.[^cite_note-57] Jerzy Kwiecinski, the deputy secretary of state in the Ministry for Regional Development at the Conference of the Ministry for Regional Development (Poland in the European Union — new possibilities for foreign investors) said:

> In the immediate financial outlook, between 2007 and 2013, we will be the largest beneficiaries of the EU — every fifth Euro will be spent in Poland. In total, we will have at our disposal 120 billion EUR, assigned exclusively for post-development activities. This sum will be an enormous boost for our country.[^cite_note-58]&#x20;

In September 2007, the prime minister signed a bill creating a special economic investment zone in Lublin that offers tax incentives. It is part of "Park Mielec" — the European Economic Development area.[^cite_note-59] At least 13 large companies had declared their wish to invest here, e.g., Carrefour, Comarch, Safo, Asseco, Aliplast, Herbapol, [Modern-Expo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern-Expo?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Perła Browary Lubelskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browary_Lubelskie).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] At the same time, the energy conglomerate, Polska Grupa Energetyczna, which will build Poland's first nuclear power station, is to have its main offices in Lublin.

Modern shopping centers built in Lublin like Tarasy Zamkowe (Castle Terraces), Lublin Plaza, Galeria Olimp, Galeria Gala, the largest shopping mall in the city, covering 33,500 square meters of area. Similar investments are planned for the near future such as Park Felin (Felicity) and a new underground gallery ("Alchemy") between and beneath Świętoduska and Lubartowska Streets.[^cite_note-62]

## Culture and tourism

Lublin is the largest city in eastern Poland and serves as an important regional cultural capital. Since then, many important international events have taken place here, involving international artists, researchers and politicians.

### Architecture

Lublin's Old Town shares several traditions with Lesser Poland, mostly the dialect and historic architecture that brings a unique ambience comparable with [Kraków](16815.md). It is, however, a distinct experience, which benefits from artistic renovation, that progressed somehow slower and more modestly focusing more on quality and less on commercialization. Historic buildings, including ruined townhouses that await for new owners, create a unique atmosphere of the renaissance city. Lublin's Old Town has cobbled, narrow streets and mostly medieval layout and design. Many venues around Old Town enjoy an architecture applicable for restaurants, art hotels, pubs galleries, and clubs. Apart from entertainment, the area houses small businesses and prestigious offices.

There are several historic churches in the Old Town, including the [Holy Trinity Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_of_the_Holy_Trinity,_Lublin_Castle) in [Lublin Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Castle) with the frescos, that are a mixture of Roman Catholic motifs with eastern Byzantine styles, reinforcing how the city connects the west with the east. Other important churches are the Late Gothic Virgin Mary Victorious Church, Renaissance Dominican Basilica and Bernardine church as well as [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_in_Poland) [St. John the Baptist Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John_the_Baptist_Cathedral,_Lublin).

Monuments of the 20th-century architecture include the [Chachmei Lublin Yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachmei_Lublin_Yeshiva) and its [synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachmei_Lublin_Yeshiva_Synagogue), socialist-realist [Zamkowy Square](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plac%20Zamkowy%20w%20Lublinie) and brutalist [Słowacki housing estate](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiedle%20S%C5%82owackiego%20\(Lublin\)) by Oskar Hansen.

### The arts

#### Museum



National Museum in Lublin

The premier museum in the city is the [National Museum of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum,_Lublin), one of the oldest and largest museums of Eastern Poland. It is located in the Lublin castle and contains some castle's interiors, like the [Holy Trinity Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_of_the_Holy_Trinity,_Lublin_Castle) with its frescoes in the Byzantine style. The museum permanent collection include also many Polish and foreign painting from the 17th to 20th century, as well as the gallery of paintings by [Tamara de Lempicka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_de_Lempicka).

Other museums include also the Museum of the History of the City of Lublin, the Museum of the Eastern Territories of the Old Polish Republic, the Józef Czechowicz Museum, the Under the clock Martyrdom Museum and the Museum of Housing Estates on the Słowacki Housing Estate.

Important museum is also the [Majdanek State Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek_State_Museum) in the former Majdanek Nazi concentration and extermination camp. In 2011 it was visited by 121,404 visitors.[^cite_note-majdanek.com-66]

#### Cinema

Lublin is a city with a proactive approach towards filmmaking industry. The city is featured in some notable films, and that include [Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award)-winning *[The Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reader)* which was partially filmed at the Nazi [Majdanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek) concentration camp.[^cite_note-67]

In 2008, Lublin collaborated with Ukrainian [Lviv](49968.md), to film and distribute promotional materials which painted both cities as attractive to the filmmaking industry. Films were handed out between filmmakers present at [Cannes Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Festival).[^cite_note-68] This was sponsored by the [European Union](9317.md). There are numerous movie theatres in Lublin including a few multiplexes, i.e. Cinema City and Multikino chains, also smaller venues like Cinema Bajka, Cinema Chatka Żaka, Cinema Perla, Cinema Grazyna and Cinema Medyk.

The Lublin Film Fund has been active since 2009, actively caring for cultivation of cinematographic talents in Lublin and promoting the city by provision of financial and organizational support. Numerous feature films have been partially financed by the fund, including Kamienie na Szaniec, Panie Dulskie, Volta and award-winning Carte Blanche.[^cite_note-69]

### Food and music

In the Old Town and the immediate surrounding, over 100 unique restaurants, fine-dining venues, cafes, pubs, clubs and other catering outlets are located. In the latter half of the 2010s, the robust international community gathered around Lublin's Medical University has impacted the growth of restaurants offering various world cuisines.

Catering to students, who account for 35% of the population, the city offers a music and nightclub scene.[^cite_note-70] Lublin has many theatres and museums and a professional orchestra, the Lublin Philharmonic.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

### City of festivals

Lublin aims to be known as the Polish Capital of Festivals.[^cite_note-Rozwalka-15] Most years, Lublin increases the number of festivals held in the city. The most significant of them include:

- Carnaval Sztukmistrzów – held in last days of July, is the largest new circus festival in Poland. Name of the Carnival is inspired by the character of [The Magician of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magician_of_Lublin_\(novel\)), from a novel by [Isaac Bashevis Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Bashevis_Singer).

  [^cite_note-75]

- [Urban Highline Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Highline_Festival_Lublin) – held in last days of July

  [^cite_note-76]

- [Night of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_Culture) (Noc Kultury) – usually held on the first Saturday night of June. Consisting of hundreds of events spanning the city, it is a cultural manifestation of Lublin's potential. Admission to all events is free.

  [^cite_note-77]

- OpenCity Festival – outdoor performances festival. International artists and performers create art installations in public places in Lublin.

  [^cite_note-78]

- [Night of Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Night_of_Museums) – Lublin's culture institutions become open to visitors at night.

- The Jagiellonian Fair (Jarmark Jagielloński) – held in late August, is a chance to meet numerous artists, artisans, and craftspeople from Central and Eastern Europe.

- St Nicolas Day International Festival of Folk Music (Mikołajki Folkowe)

  [^cite_note-79]

  &#x20;– the oldest folk music festival in Poland, held in the first decade of December

  [^cite_note-80]

- East Of Culture – Different Sounds Art'n'Music Festival (Wschód Kultury – Inne Brzmienia Art'n'Music Festival), held in late June, is a meeting of world-class artists that represent various nationalities and practise diverse music styles.

  [^cite_note-81]

- Lubelskie Dni Kultury Studenckiej – Lublin's Days of Student Culture – an annual students' holiday. Usually celebrated for about three weeks between May and June. Due to the city's large student population, the festival in Lublin is the longest in Poland.

- Lublin. Miasto Poezji – Poetry Festival organised by *Ośrodek "*&#x42;rama Grodzka – Teatr N&#x4E;*"* and Polish Literature Institute of [Catholic University in Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II_Catholic_University_of_Lublin).

- Noc z Czechowiczem – *A Night with Czechowicz* – a walking tour, inspired by the "Poem on the City of Lublin" written by [Józef Czechowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Czechowicz). It is held on the first full moon in July, and is organised by Ośrodek "Brama Grodzka – Teatr NN".

- Najstarsze Pieśni Europy – *The oldest songs of Europe* – Festival of Muzyka Kresów Foundation

- Future Shorts – World Short Film Label

  &#x20;– held in November since 2008, is a showcase of contemporary Ukrainian culture and a space for Polish-Ukrainian intercultural dialogue.

- "Falkon" – Fantasy and Science Fiction Festival – Ogólnopolski Festiwal Fantastyki Falkon – held in November, is one of the biggest fantasy conventions in Poland.

### European Capital of Culture

In 2007, Lublin joined the group of Polish cities as candidates for the title of [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture). Lublin was shortlisted, but ultimately [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw) was chosen. Lublin is a pilot city of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and the [European Commission](9974.md) Intercultural cities programme.

## International relations

Lublin is a pilot city of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and the EU Intercultural cities programme.[^cite_note-86] In 2017, Lublin was awarded [the Europe Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Europe_Prize) by the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe).[^cite_note-87] In 2023, Lublin was selected as the [European Youth Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Capital)[^cite_note-88] by international jury of the [European Youth Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Forum). Lublin is a signatory of the [European charter for equality of women and men in local life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_charter_for_equality_of_women_and_men_in_local_life).[^cite_note-89]

In 2023, following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the city of Lublin was honoured by President of Ukraine [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) who granted it the title of "City-Rescuer" in recognition of its humanitarian and financial assistance to Ukraine and the country's [war refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_refugee).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]
