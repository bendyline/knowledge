**Ralph Everard Gonsalves** (born 8 August 1946) is a [Vincentian](27228.md) politician who served as [prime minister](466897.md) of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines from 2001 to 2025, and as the leader of the [Unity Labour Party](1836153.md) (ULP) since 2001.[^cite_note-caribbeanelectionsralph-1] He was the longest continuously serving prime minister since St. Vincent's independence in 1979, with almost 25 years in office. Gonsalves has been [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament) (MP) for the [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) of [North Central Windward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_Windward) since [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Vincentian_general_election).

He became deputy leader upon the formation of the ULP in 1994 and party leader in 1998.[^cite_note-caribbeanelectionsulp-2] Gonsalves became prime minister after his party won a [majority government](465640.md) in the [2001 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Vincentian_general_election). He was the first prime minister from the newly constructed ULP, following a merger of the [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines_Labour_Party) and the [Movement for National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_National_Unity).[^cite_note-caribbeanelectionsulp-2]

With Gonsalves as leader, the ULP won a majority in the popular vote in every general election from 1998 through 2015, though it failed to secure the majority of parliamentary seats in the [1998 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Vincentian_general_election). In 2020, the ULP won the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Vincentian_general_election), but did not win the popular vote.[^cite_note-3] On 7 November 2020, Gonsalves was sworn in for his fifth term as prime minister.[^cite_note-4] In the [2025 general election](76606789.md), his party was defeated by [Godwin Friday](52936025.md)'s [New Democratic Party](1836145.md), with Gonsalves being the only member of the ULP to win re-election.

Gonsalves was the longest-serving democratically-elected state leader since Samoan PM [Tuilaʻepa Saʻilele Malielegaoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuila%CA%BBepa_Sa%CA%BBilele_Malielegaoi) was replaced in 2021, until his defeat in 2025.

## Early life and education

Gonsalves, known affectionately as "Comrade Ralph", was born in [Colonarie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonarie), [Saint Vincent, British Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_\(Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines\)) to his father, Alban Gonsalves, a farmer and small businessman (now deceased), and his mother, Theresa Francis, a small businesswoman. His ancestors came to [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](27228.md) in 1845 as indentured servants from the [Portuguese](23033.md) island of [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira).[^cite_note-caribbeanelectionsralph-1]

Gonsalves attended Colonarie Roman Catholic School, and later the St. Vincent Grammar School. He then enrolled at the [University of the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_West_Indies), where he completed a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics). He later returned there to earn a [master's degree](170346.md) in [government](12229.md), which he completed in 1971. In 1974, he completed a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in government at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester). Gonsalves was [called to the bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_to_the_bar) at [Gray's Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray's_Inn) in [London](17867.md) in 1981.[^cite_note-5] He also attended [Makerere University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerere_University) in Uganda according to his address at the United Nations during the Africa Day event on 25 May 2019.

## Early political career

Gonsalves became involved in politics at university, as president of the University of West Indies' Guild of Undergraduates and Debating Society. In 1968, he led a student protest of the deportation of historian and intellectual [Walter Rodney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Rodney) by the Jamaican government.[^cite_note-caricom-6]

In 1994, Gonsalves became the deputy leader of the [Unity Labour Party](1836153.md). After the resignation of [Vincent Beache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Beache), Gonsalves became leader of the party in 1998.[^cite_note-caribbeanelectionsulp-2] He was appointed [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines\)) from December 1999 to January 2001.[^cite_note-parlhist-7]

## Prime Minister (2001–2025)



Gonsalves in 2004

Gonsalves was appointed [Prime Minister of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines) after he led the Unity Labour Party to victory in the [2001 Vincentian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Vincentian_general_election). He led the party to victory in a record four subsequent elections: [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Vincentian_general_election), [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Vincentian_general_election), [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Vincentian_general_election), and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Vincentian_general_election).[^cite_note-caribbean3602010-8][^cite_note-9] Gonsalves and the ULP were defeated in a landslide the [2025 Vincentian general election](76606789.md) by the [New Democratic Party](1836145.md) led by [Godwin Friday](52936025.md). The ULP lost eight of its nine seats in the [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines), with Gonsalves being the sole member of the party to retain his seat, North Central Windward.[^cite_note-10]

### Domestic policy

In 2009 Gonsalves and the ULP led a referendum campaign[^cite_note-caribbean360referendum-11] in favour of constitutional reform that would have abolished the country's [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy), replacing [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II)[^cite_note-constitution-12] with a non-executive [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_\(government_title\)). The [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Vincentian_constitutional_referendum) was defeated, with 55.64% of voters rejecting the changes.[^cite_note-antilleanreferendum-13] Gonsalves has persisted in his calls for the establishment of a presidency, proposing another referendum in 2022 to replace the monarchy while also voicing support to rename places in the country named after colonial figures such as [Victoria Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Park_\(Kingstown\)).[^cite_note-14]

&#x20;From 2001 to 2017, Gonsalves also served as [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines). He handed over the position to his son, [Camillo Gonsalves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Gonsalves) in a 2017 cabinet reshuffle.[^cite_note-camillo-15]



Gonsalves at a [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM) meeting in 2016 with President of Chile [Michelle Bachelet](986814.md) and Chilean Foreign Minister [Heraldo Muñoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldo_Mu%C3%B1oz)

Gonsalves supports [capital punishment](5902.md).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Foreign policy



Gonsalves with [United States secretary of state](32293.md) [Marco Rubio](5502549.md), in 2025

On 3 July 2020, Gonsalves was elected Chairman of the [Caribbean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community) succeeding [Mia Mottley](15337866.md).[^cite_note-caricom-6] His 6-month term ended on 1 January 2021, and he was succeeded by [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) Prime Minister, [Keith Rowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Rowley).[^cite_note-auto-18][^cite_note-auto1-19][^cite_note-20]

Gonsalves attended the [coronation of Charles III and Camilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Charles_III_and_Camilla) at [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey) along with Governor-General [Susan Dougan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Dougan) on 6 May 2023. Gonsalves met with the King and other leaders of the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) the day prior.[^cite_note-21]

Gonsalves helped to organize a meeting in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines between Venezuelan President [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md) and Guyanese President [Irfaan Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irfaan_Ali) to discuss the [2023 Guyana-Venezuela crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Guyana-Venezuela_crisis) between the two nations. Gonsalves called on Maduro and Ali "to de-escalate the situation" and engage in "appropriate dialogue". Also invited to the meeting was Brazilian President [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md).[^cite_note-22]

## Personal life

Gonsalves has been married twice; currently he is married to Eloise Harris. He has two sons by his first marriage, [Camillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Gonsalves) and Adam; one son by his second wife, Storm; and two daughters, Isis and Soleil. Camillo followed his father into politics, and is currently serving as [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines).[^cite_note-camillo-15]

Gonsalves practices law before the [Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Caribbean_Supreme_Court).[^cite_note-caribbeanelectionsralph-1] He has written and published on a range of matters including the [Caribbean](18956035.md), [Africa](5334607.md), [trade unionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_unionism), comparative political economy, and developmental issues generally.

On 5 August 2021, at a protest [against mandatory vaccination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-vaccine_movement) from [COVID-19](63030231.md) organized by trade unions representing nurses, police and other workers,[^cite_note-23] Gonsalves was attacked with a projectile near the entrance to Parliament. He sustained visible injuries to his head in the attack and was rushed to the hospital.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]
