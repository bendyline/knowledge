&#x20; The **Massachusetts Institute of Technology** (**MIT**) is a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university) [research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university) in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts), United States. Founded in 1861 to advance "useful knowledge", the university has played a significant role in the development of many areas of technology and science.

[William Barton Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barton_Rogers) founded MIT to accelerate [American industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_and_industrial_history_of_the_United_States) through scientific knowledge. Initially funded by a [federal land grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_universities), the institute adopted a German polytechnic model emphasizing laboratory instruction in [applied science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_science) and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), and moved from Boston's Back Bay to its current campus in Cambridge in 1916. Early growth came through research contracts with private industry, though the institute remained financially constrained and focused primarily on practical engineering education into the 1930s.

MIT's transformation as a research enterprise began during [World War II](32927.md), when projects like the [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory) made it the nation's largest non-industrial R\&D contractor. Graduate enrollment and research funding grew rapidly in the postwar decades as faculty members such as [Vannevar Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannevar_Bush) helped shape federal support for basic science. In the late twentieth century, MIT became closely associated with [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [artificial intelligence](1164.md), [biotechnology](4502.md), [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software_movement) software development, and ["big science" initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_science) like the [Apollo Guidance Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Guidance_Computer) and the [LIGO project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO). Engineering remains its largest school, though MIT has also developed prominent programs in basic science, economics, management, architecture, and humanities.

MIT has an urban campus that extends more than a mile (1.6 km) along the [Charles River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_River). Academic buildings are connected by an extensive corridor system. MIT's off-campus operations include [Lincoln Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Lincoln_Laboratory) and [Haystack Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haystack_Observatory), as well as affiliated laboratories such as the [Broad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Institute) and [Whitehead Institutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead_Institute). Undergraduate life is known for [hands-on research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_Research_Opportunities_Program) and [elaborate pranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacks_at_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). Tuition is generally not charged to undergraduates from families with incomes below \$200,000, and most graduate students are funded by research.&#x20;

As of October 2024, [105 Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_by_university_affiliation),[^cite_note-10] 26 [Turing Award](31093.md) winners, and 8 [Fields Medalists](10859.md) have been affiliated with MIT as alumni, faculty members, or researchers.[^cite_note-11] Alumni and faculty have founded many notable companies and served in senior government positions in the United States and abroad.

## History

Main article: [History of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)

### Foundation and vision

> \[...] a school of industrial science aiding the advancement, development and practical application of science in connection with arts, agriculture, manufactures, and commerce \[...][^cite_note-12]

— Massachusetts General Court, *Acts of 1861, Chapter 183*

In 1859, a proposal was submitted to the [Massachusetts General Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Court) to use newly filled lands in [Back Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Bay,_Boston,_Massachusetts), Boston for a "[Conservatory of Art and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatoire_national_des_arts_et_m%C3%A9tiers)", but the proposal failed.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] A charter for the [incorporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporation_\(business\)) of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, proposed by [William Barton Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barton_Rogers), was signed by [John Albion Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Albion_Andrew), the [governor of Massachusetts](252510.md), on April 10, 1861.[^cite_note-15]

Rogers, a [geologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology) who had recently arrived in Boston from the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia),[^cite_note-\:02-16] wanted to establish an institution to address rapid scientific and technological advances.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] He did not wish to found a [professional school](56551.md), but a combination with elements of both professional and [liberal education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_education),[^cite_note-Lewis_Report-19] proposing that:

> The true and only practicable object of a polytechnic school is, as I conceive, the teaching, not of the minute details and manipulations of the arts, which can be done only in the workshop, but the inculcation of those scientific principles which form the basis and explanation of them, and along with this, a full and methodical review of all their leading processes and operations in connection with physical laws.[^cite_note-20]

The Rogers Plan reflected the [German research university model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_European_research_universities#European_university_models_in_the_19th_and_20th_centuries), emphasizing an independent faculty engaged in research, as well as instruction oriented around seminars and laboratories.\[^cite_note-Angulo\_[https://archive.org/details/williambartonrog00angu/page/155_155–156-21](https://archive.org/details/williambartonrog00angu/page/155_155%E2%80%93156-21)][^cite_note-22]

### Early developments



Original [Rogers Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Building_\(MIT\)) in [Back Bay, Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Bay,_Boston), 1872

Two days after MIT was chartered, the [first battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Sumter) of the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) broke out. After a long delay through the war years, MIT's first classes were held in the Mercantile Building in Boston in 1865.[^cite_note-23] The new institute was founded as part of the [Morrill Land-Grant Colleges Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrill_Land-Grant_Colleges_Act) to fund institutions "to promote the liberal and practical education of the industrial classes" and was a land-grant school.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStrattonMannix2005251–276-24][^cite_note-LoC-25] In 1863, under the same act, the Commonwealth of Massachusetts founded the [Massachusetts Agricultural College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst#History), which later developed into the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst). In 1866, the proceeds from land sales went toward new buildings in the Back Bay.[^cite_note-BostonTech1-26]



"Boston Tech" students testing [dynamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamo)

MIT was informally called "Boston Tech".[^cite_note-BostonTech1-26] The institute adopted the [European polytechnic university model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_European_research_universities) and emphasized laboratory instruction from an early date.\[^cite_note-Angulo\_[https://archive.org/details/williambartonrog00angu/page/155_155–156-21](https://archive.org/details/williambartonrog00angu/page/155_155%E2%80%93156-21)] Despite chronic financial problems, the institute saw growth in the last two decades of the 19th century under President [Francis Amasa Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Amasa_Walker).[^cite_note-Dunbar1-27] Programs in electrical, chemical, marine, and sanitary engineering were introduced,[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-Munroe1923a-29] new buildings were built, and the size of the student body increased to more than one thousand.[^cite_note-Dunbar1-27]

The curriculum drifted to a vocational emphasis, with less focus on theoretical science.[^cite_note-30] The fledgling school still suffered from chronic financial shortages which diverted the attention of the MIT leadership. During these "Boston Tech" years, MIT faculty and alumni rebuffed [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) president (and former MIT faculty) [Charles W. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_W._Eliot)'s repeated attempts to merge MIT with Harvard College's [Lawrence Scientific School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Scientific_School).[^cite_note-31] There would be at least six attempts to absorb MIT into Harvard.[^cite_note-Alexander-32] In its cramped Back Bay location, MIT could not afford to expand its overcrowded facilities, driving a desperate search for a new campus and funding. Eventually, the MIT Corporation approved a formal agreement to merge with Harvard and move to then-remote [Allston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allston), over the vehement objections of MIT faculty, students, and alumni.[^cite_note-Alexander-32] The merger plan collapsed in 1905 when the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court ruled that MIT could not sell its Back Bay land.[^cite_note-33]



"New Technology" campus in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts), opened in 1916.

In 1912, MIT acquired its current campus by purchasing a one-mile (1.6 km) tract of [filled lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation) along the Cambridge side of the Charles River.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] The [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) "New Technology" campus was designed by [William W. Bosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_W._Bosworth)[^cite_note-36] and had been funded largely by anonymous donations from a mysterious "Mr. Smith", starting in 1912. In January 1920, the donor was revealed to be the industrialist [George Eastman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Eastman), an inventor of film production methods and founder of [Eastman Kodak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastman_Kodak).[^cite_note-38] In 1916, with the first academic buildings complete, the MIT administration and the MIT charter crossed the [Charles River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_River) on the ceremonial barge *Bucentaur* built for the occasion.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Industry dependence and reform

Unlike the [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) universities, MIT drew an unusually large share of its students from families of moderate means and depended heavily on [tuition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuition_payments) rather than [endowment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_endowment) income for its operating budget.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger199313–14-41] The "Technology Plan," launched by President [Richard Maclaurin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_C._Maclaurin) in 1919, sought to deepen industry patronage. Under the plan, corporations paid MIT an annual retaining fee in exchange for access to faculty, library resources, and technical services.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer201065–66-42] By the late 1920s, more than a third of the teaching staff were engaged in research, testing, or consulting for industry, with MIT handling an expanding volume of corporate contracts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer201069–70-43] An industry orientation meant that salaries and faculty research funds lagged behind those at other American research universities. Heavy work on industry problems limited basic research and diminished interest from [foundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_\(nonprofit\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger1993179–181-44][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992157-45] By the late 1920s MIT was regarded by elite universities as a "mere engineering school servicing industry".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992156–157-46]



[Vannevar Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannevar_Bush) (l) and [Karl Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Compton) (r) led reforms to funding and curriculum

A mandate for reform came from board members tied to industry research: [Gerard Swope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Swope), president of [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric), and [Frank B. Jewett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_B._Jewett), head of [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories). Both argued that practice-oriented training was obsolete and industry needed engineers grounded in fundamental science.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer201070–71-47][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992157–158-48] In 1930 they recruited the physicist [Karl Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Taylor_Compton) to carry out a broad program of reform.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992157-45]

As president, Compton overhauled the science departments first, recruiting a cohort of research-oriented faculty.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992159–160-49] In 1932, he reorganized MIT into schools of engineering, science, and architecture, created a formal [graduate school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_school), and appointed [Vannevar Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannevar_Bush) as vice president and dean of engineering.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992163-50] To relieve dependence on industry, the pair centralized all industrial contracts, established a patent licensing program, and curtailed faculty consulting.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992163–165-51][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger1993181–182-52] Compton also tripled philanthropic support for research and campaigned for federal government support of university science.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992159-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKevles1995252–257-54]

The reforms were uneven. Physics, chemistry, and electrical engineering advanced rapidly, but much of the engineering school did virtually no research well into the 1930s. Faculty resisted changes to shop practice and consulting arrangements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992165–166-55] Even so, by the mid-1930s, MIT had been admitted to the [Association of American Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Universities), the organization of the nation's top research universities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992156-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer201075-57] The institutional changes of this decade positioned MIT to take a leading role in wartime research after 1940.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992170–171-58]

### Defense research

Further information: [MIT Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory)



Navy recruits training on [Rad Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory) radar systems

In June 1940, Vannevar Bush, who had left MIT's administration to lead the [Carnegie Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institution) in Washington, persuaded President Roosevelt to create the [National Defense Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Research_Committee) (NDRC) to mobilize civilian science for defense.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie19936–7-59] The NDRC's first major project was a laboratory for microwave radar research. After other proposed sites fell through, Bush and other science administrators turned to Compton, who agreed to host the project at MIT.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger19939–10-60][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie199321–22-61]

The [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory), as it was called to conceal its purpose, opened in 1940 and grew from a staff of thirty to roughly 4,000 and rivaled the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project) in scale: the NDRC division under which it operated expended some \$1.5 billion on radar systems.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger19939-62][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie199322-63] The Rad Lab contract was the first and largest wartime research agreement between the federal government and a university; its terms became a model for postwar government–university contracts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger19939–10-60][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurchard1948215–221-64][^cite_note-67] By war's end MIT had received \$117 million (\$2.1 billion in 2025) in government R\&D contracts, more than any industrial contractor and roughly a third of all NDRC spending on university research.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie19937-68][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKevles1995342-69]



[Margaret Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Hamilton_\(software_engineer\)) wrote guidance code for the Apollo moon landings

The Rad Lab closed in 1945, but opened a new era of large military research contracts at MIT. New interdepartmental laboratories took shape: the [Research Laboratory of Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Laboratory_of_Electronics) (1946) inherited the Rad Lab's facilities and an Army–Navy contract for basic research in microwaves and electronics; the [Laboratory for Nuclear Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_for_Nuclear_Science?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1946) opened with Navy support; [Lincoln Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Lincoln_Laboratory) (1951) was created to develop a [continental air-defense radar network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-Automatic_Ground_Environment) for the Air Force.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger199366–67-70][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie199315–16,_38–39,_144–145-71] [Charles Stark Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stark_Draper)'s wartime gunsight laboratory, renamed the [Instrumentation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper_Laboratory), expanded into inertial guidance for [ballistic missiles](38025.md) and computerized guidance for the [Apollo lunar mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Guidance_Computer).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie199390–94-72] The new laboratories became the primary training ground for graduate students in science and engineering and spawned dozens of firms along the [Route 128](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_128_\(Massachusetts\)) corridor.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie199315–16-73] [^cite_note-75]

The cumulative effect transformed MIT. Between the early 1930s and the mid-1950s, the faculty doubled and the graduate student body quintupled.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992179-76] Federal funding, negligible before the war, reached \$38 million by 1944, and by 1957 research expenditures represented 72 percent of MIT's operating budget.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992174-77][^cite_note-Canizares2007-78] The Department of Defense was the dominant sponsor for much of this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie199315–16-73][^cite_note-Canizares2007-78] By 1962 the physicist [Alvin Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Weinberg), coiner of the term "[big science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_science)," said it was difficult "to tell whether MIT is a university with many government research laboratories appended to it or a cluster of government research laboratories with a very good educational institution attached to it."[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaiser2010104–105-80]

Most defense-funded work on campus was basic and unclassified.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie199315–16-73][^cite_note-Canizares2007-78] The major exception was the Instrumentation Laboratory, which carried projects from conception through deployment, an anomaly in university research.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger1993248–249-81] Its work on guidance systems for American [Poseidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UGM-73_Poseidon) [MIRV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIRV) warheads drew particular criticism as destabilizing the Cold War arms race.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie1993233–235-82]



"Doc" Draper and picketers at the Instrumentation Lab

Opposition to the [Vietnam War](32611.md) brought these tensions to a head. On March 4, 1969, students and faculty organized a research stoppage to protest the military applications of science.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENelkin197255–58-83][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBridger2015164–165-84][^cite_note-85] President [Howard Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Wesley_Johnson) convened a review panel on the Instrumentation Lab and Lincoln Lab, chaired by Sloan School dean William Pounds, even as demonstrations continued into the fall. The Pounds Panel reaffirmed the place of the labs at MIT and recommended diversification and oversight, not military divestiture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie1993235–237-86][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENelkin197279–80-87] In May 1970, Johnson announced that the Instrumentation Laboratory would be separated from MIT, and it became the independent [Draper Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper_Laboratory) in 1973.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeslie1993239–243-88][^cite_note-92] MIT established a policy that on-campus research must be open and publishable, and classified work was consolidated at [Lincoln Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Lincoln_Laboratory)'s off-campus site.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger1993249–250-93][^cite_note-95]

### Postwar educational reform

The Institute's new scale and resources raised questions about its educational direction. By 1946, research dwarfed the academic budget.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992175–176-96] The number of graduate students rose from roughly 700 in 1940 to 2,700 by 1959; the ratio of graduate to undergraduate students shifted from about 1\:3 before the war to nearly 1\:1.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992179-76] Federal research contracts, which supported research assistants, drove much of this growth. By the late 1950s, MIT had "virtually become a graduate school with a strong undergraduate school."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992179-76]

At Vice President [James Killian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Killian)'s urging in 1947, MIT faculty formed a Committee on Educational Survey chaired by chemical engineer [Warren K. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_K._Lewis).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKillian198579–80-97][^cite_note-98] After two years of study, the Lewis Committee's landmark report reaffirmed the founding principle that the Institute should integrate useful training and liberal education, and warned that a preoccupation with research was coming at the expense of undergraduate teaching.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis_Committee194915–22-99][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger199365–66-100] It called for MIT students to build creative and intellectual autonomy rather than command of routine procedures.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis_Committee194915–17-101][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeiger199365–66-100]

In 1950, the Corporation approved the committee's recommendation for a [School of Humanities and Social Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_School_of_Humanities,_Arts,_and_Social_Sciences) to stand on equal footing with the existing schools.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis_Committee194943–44-102][^cite_note-103] The new school offered general education for undergraduates alongside graduate programs in political science, economics, linguistics, and [science and technology studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_studies).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKillian1985221–222-104]

Reform in engineering met greater resistance. Professors hired to advance applied science in the 1930s were opposed by older faculty attached to practical instruction and shop-centered training.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992178–179-105] In mechanical engineering, [Richard Söderberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_S%C3%B6derberg) and reformers dismantled machinery laboratories. [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_S._Brown) gave higher priority to modern physics within electrical engineering and instituted new programs in engineering science across departments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992179-76][^cite_note-107] These changes, part of a national movement to put engineering education on scientific footing, reshaped the MIT undergraduate experience in a single generation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELécuyer1992180-108]

New educational experiments sought to improve undergraduate training. In 1957, [Edwin H. Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_H._Land), the inventor of instant photography, gave a lecture arguing that students should engage in original research from their arrival on campus, working with faculty rather than waiting years to reach the research frontier.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKillian1985174–177-109] In 1969, the new [Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_Research_Opportunities_Program) (UROP), directed by physicist [Margaret MacVicar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_MacVicar), allowed undergraduates to participate in research projects across the Institute. This was widely adopted at other schools and was later identified by [Clark Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Kerr) as one of the few genuine university reforms from the 1960s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKerr2001127-110]

### Recent history

#### Life sciences

From the 1970s onward, MIT's reduced portfolio of on-campus defense research was matched by a rise in federal health research.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharp2006-112] The [Center for Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_Institute_for_Integrative_Cancer_Research), opened in 1974 in a converted candy factory (E17) on Ames Street, marked a turning point. Founded by [Salvador Luria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Luria) with a [National Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Institute) grant and staffed by a cohort recruited with [David Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Baltimore), including [Phillip Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Allen_Sharp), [Nancy Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Hopkins_\(scientist\)), and [Robert Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Weinberg_\(biologist\)), the center quickly became one of the strongest groups in cancer biology in the country.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDurant2010148–149-113][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._7-114][^cite_note-116]

After a contentious public debate over [recombinant DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recombinant_DNA) research was resolved by a Cambridge city ordinance in 1977, MIT expanded biological research through a series of independent but affiliated research institutes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDurant2010149–158-117] The [Whitehead Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead_Institute) (1982) added sixteen investigators to the biology faculty. Mathematician [Eric Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Lander), working from the Whitehead, established a genome center in 1990 that became a major contributor to the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharp2006-112][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDurant2010157-118] The [Broad Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Institute) (2003), a joint enterprise with Harvard, grew out of that effort into one of the largest genomic research operations in the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharp2006-112][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._7-114] The [McGovern Institute for Brain Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGovern_Institute_for_Brain_Research) (2001) and the [Picower Institute for Learning and Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picower_Institute_for_Learning_and_Memory) (2002) extended the model into the neurosciences.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharp2006-112] New buildings for biology, neuroscience, genomics, and cancer research rose on the northeast campus. In 1998, a new Department of Biological Engineering was created at the interface of molecular biology and engineering.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharp2006-112]

Investments in the life sciences induced a biotechnology [industry cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_theory) around Kendall Square. Homegrown firms such as [Biogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogen) and [Genzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genzyme) first expanded in the area. In 2002, [Novartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis) relocated its research headquarters to Cambridge, a decision that drew virtually every major pharmaceutical company to follow over the next decade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._16-119] In 2004, the appointment of neuroscientist [Susan Hockfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Hockfield) as MIT's sixteenth president, the first life scientist to lead MIT, reflected the programs' maturity.[^cite_note-120] In 2006, President Hockfield launched the [MIT Energy Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Energy_Initiative) to investigate challenges posed by increasing [global energy consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_resources_and_consumption).[^cite_note-121]

#### Computation



The 1985 [MIT Media Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Media_Lab) building, designed by [I.M. Pei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.M._Pei), houses researchers developing novel uses of computer technology.

Programs that emerged from defense projects—[Whirlwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Whirlwind), the Research Laboratory of Electronics, and the [SAGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAGE_\(computer\)) air-defense system—gave rise in the 1960s to digital computing laboratories. [Project MAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_MAC), launched in 1963 with [ARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) funding, drew researchers from scattered departments into a single effort around [time-sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-sharing) and [artificial intelligence](1164.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._8-122][^cite_note-LeeMcCarthy1992-123] Project MAC was reorganized in 1976 as the [Laboratory for Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Laboratory_for_Computer_Science) (LCS).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWildesLindgren1985ch._22-124] A culture of student programmers grew out of the [Tech Model Railroad Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tech_Model_Railroad_Club), whose members were more drawn to the electrical switching systems beneath the layout than to the trains themselves.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._8-122][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaymond2001ch._1-125] These groups became the nucleus of the [AI Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Artificial_Intelligence_Laboratory), regarded as the birthplace of [hacker culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker_culture).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaymond2001ch._1-125] When commercial pressures began pulling researchers into spinoff companies in the early 1980s, [Richard Stallman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Stallman) responded by launching the [GNU Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Project) (1983) and the [Free Software Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Software_Foundation) (1985), establishing a framework for [free software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software) that shaped the later [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) movement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaymond2001chs._1,_6-126]

In 1983, MIT launched [Project Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Athena), an eight-year partnership with [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) and the [Digital Equipment Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Equipment_Corporation) that placed networked workstations across campus and produced widely adopted infrastructure, including the [Kerberos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerberos_\(protocol\)) authentication protocol and the [X Window System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Window_System).[^cite_note-Frederick2018-127] In 1985, [Nicholas Negroponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Negroponte) and former MIT president [Jerome Wiesner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Wiesner) founded the [Media Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Media_Lab), which focused on integration of computing with communication, design, and the arts, drawing researchers from the AI Lab.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._14-128] Its industry sponsorship model helped draw technology firms to establish research outposts in nearby [Kendall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_Square).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._14-128]

In 1994, [Tim Berners-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee) established the [World Wide Web Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web_Consortium) (W3C) at the Laboratory for Computer Science to develop open standards for the web.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._14-128] In 2003, the AI Laboratory and LCS merged to form the [Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Computer_Science_and_Artificial_Intelligence_Laboratory) (CSAIL), now the largest research laboratory at the Institute.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuderi2022ch._14-128] Open-access ideas running from the AI Lab found new expression when MIT launched [OpenCourseWare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_OpenCourseWare) in 2002, a project whose architects explicitly drew on the open-source principle that knowledge systems should be freely accessible.[^cite_note-Vest2001-129][^cite_note-Abelson2008-130] In 2018, MIT announced the creation of the [Schwarzman College of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Schwarzman_College_of_Computing), a billion-dollar initiative to integrate artificial intelligence research and education across the Institute.[^cite_note-Gershgorn-131]

#### Institutional life

In 1991, the [Department of Justice](52563.md) sued MIT and the eight [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) universities, alleging that their practice of jointly setting need-based financial aid for commonly admitted students violated [antitrust law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Antitrust_Act). The other schools signed [consent decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consent_decree) and MIT contested the suit alone under President [Charles Vest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_M._Vest).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeyser201153–54-132][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBambergerCarlton2000-133][^cite_note-134] A federal appeals court ruled in MIT's favor in 1993, and Congress subsequently passed legislation permitting need-based aid coordination among universities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBambergerCarlton2000-133]

In 1999, a committee of women faculty in the School of Science, led by biologist Nancy Hopkins, published a report documenting that senior women faculty received less laboratory space, lower salaries, and fewer institutional resources than male colleagues of comparable rank.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZernike2023335–339-135][^cite_note-136] President Vest publicly acknowledged the findings, writing that he now understood the reality of gender discrimination at MIT to be "the greater part of the balance" than the perception of it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZernike2023339-137] The report prompted policy changes across MIT's schools, spurred similar investigations at nine other universities, and was credited with advancing gender parity in academic science nationally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZernike2023epilogue-138]

Three days after the [Boston Marathon bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon_bombing) of April 2013, [MIT Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_Police_Department) officer [Sean Collier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Collier) was fatally shot by the bombers on campus, setting off a manhunt that shut down much of the Boston metropolitan area.[^cite_note-139] His memorial service drew more than 10,000 people.[^cite_note-Bidgood-140]

## Campus

Main article: [Campus of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_of_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)



MIT's central campus from above the [Harvard Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Bridge). Left of center is the Great Dome, with the [Stata Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stata_Center) and [Kendall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_Square) behind.

MIT's 166-acre (67.2 ha) campus in the city of [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) spans approximately a mile along the north side of the [Charles River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_River) basin.[^cite_note-Campus-6] The campus is divided roughly in half by [Massachusetts Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Avenue_\(metropolitan_Boston\)), with most dormitories and student life facilities to the west and most academic buildings to the east. The bridge closest to MIT is the [Harvard Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Bridge), which is known for being marked off in a [non-standard unit of length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_humorous_units_of_measurement) – the [smoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoot).[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

The [Kendall/MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall/MIT_station) [subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_subway) station is located on the northeastern edge of the campus, in [Kendall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_Square). Since the 1960s, MIT and other firms have intensively developed high-rise educational, retail, residential, startup incubator, and office space around the station. The Cambridge neighborhoods surrounding MIT are a mixture of modern offices for high-tech firms, old industry buildings, and low-rise residential neighborhoods.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-BO-144] The [MIT Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Museum) has moved immediately adjacent to a Kendall Square subway entrance, joining the [List Visual Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_Visual_Arts_Center) on the eastern end of the campus.[^cite_note-New_MIT_Museum_Location-145]

Each building at MIT [has a number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_of_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology#Campus_organization) (possibly preceded by a *W*, *N*, *E*, or *NW*) designation, and most have a name as well. Typically, academic and office buildings are referred to primarily by number while residence halls are referred to by name. The organization of building numbers roughly corresponds to the order in which the buildings were built and their location relative (north, west, and east) to the original center cluster of Maclaurin buildings.[^cite_note-Numbering_system-146] Many of the buildings are connected above ground as well as through an extensive network of tunnels, providing protection from the Cambridge weather as well as a venue for [roof and tunnel hacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof_and_tunnel_hacking).[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

The campus' primary energy source is [natural gas](22131.md), supplied by a power plant constructed for the original Cambridge campus. Since the 1990s, the Institute has retrofit existing buildings to improve their energy efficiency, retooled its campus power plant for [cogeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cogeneration), and jointly financed a 60-megawatt solar power plant in North Carolina to offset carbon use.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

### Research facilities

[MIT's on-campus nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Nuclear_Research_Reactor) is one of the oldest operating [nuclear reactors](22151.md) in the United States, and one of three university research reactors operating above 5 megawatts.[^cite_note-151] MIT allows students to be trained as reactor operators, and the facility was historically used for experimental cancer treatment.[^cite_note-Talusan2011-152] The siting of the reactor's containment building in a densely populated area has attracted periodic public scrutiny, but MIT maintains that it is well-secured and routinely inspected.[^cite_note-Talusan2011-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]

MIT Nano, also known as Building 12, is the campus' central facility for nanoscale research. Its 100,000 sq ft (9,300 m<sup>2</sup>) [cleanroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleanroom) and research space, visible through glass panels, is the largest research facility of its kind in the United States.[^cite_note-155] At US\$400 million to construct, it is also one of the costliest buildings on campus. The facility also provides nanoimaging capabilities with vibration damped imaging and metrology suites sitting atop a 5×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> lb (2,300,000 kg) slab of concrete underground.[^cite_note-156]

Other notable campus facilities include a pressurized [wind tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_tunnel) for testing [aerodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamics) research, a [towing tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_model_basin) for testing ship and ocean structure designs, and previously [Alcator C-Mod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcator_C-Mod), which was the largest fusion device operated by any university.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] MIT's campus-wide wireless network was completed in the fall of 2005 and consists of nearly 3,000 access points covering 9.4×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> sq ft (870,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of campus.[^cite_note-159]

### Architecture



Bosworth's Building 10 and Great Dome overlooking Killian Court

MIT has a history of commissioning innovative buildings.[^cite_note-Starchitecture-160][^cite_note-161] The first buildings for the Cambridge campus, completed in 1916 and designed by [William Welles Bosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Welles_Bosworth), were the first non-industrial buildings built from reinforced concrete in the United States.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-Jarzombek1-164] Bosworth's idea—industrial efficiency inside, classical aesthetics outside—was influenced by the [City Beautiful movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Beautiful_movement) of the early 1900s. His design features the [Pantheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome)-esque Great Dome overlooking Killian Court, where [graduation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation) ceremonies are held each year.[^cite_note-Jarzombek1-164] The friezes of the limestone-clad buildings around Killian Court are engraved with the names of important scientists and philosophers.[^cite_note-167] The [Infinite Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Corridor) runs the east-west length of Bosworth's buildings, beginning at Lobby 7 despite a name suggesting it has no beginning.[^cite_note-BO-144]



The [Stata Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stata_Center) houses [CSAIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSAIL), [LIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Laboratory_for_Information_and_Decision_Systems), linguistics, and philosophy.

Later buildings, many connected to the Bosworth's original buildings, range from utilitarian to high design. The demolished [Building 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_20), constructed cheaply with little architectural effort for the Rad Lab, has been acclaimed for their research utility.[^cite_note-168] After World War II, MIT commissioned many of its new buildings from high-profile architects. Among the post-war [modernist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_architecture) on campus is [Alvar Aalto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvar_Aalto)'s Baker House (1947), [Eero Saarinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eero_Saarinen)'s [MIT Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Chapel) and [Kresge Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kresge_Auditorium) (1955), and [I.M. Pei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.M._Pei)'s four research buildings: [Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Building_\(MIT\)), Dreyfus, Landau, and [Wiesner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesner_Building).[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

More recent buildings like [Frank Gehry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Gehry)'s [Stata Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stata_Center) (2004), [Steven Holl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Holl)'s [Simmons Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_MIT_undergraduate_dormitories#Simmons_Hall) (2002), [Charles Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Correa)'s Building 46 (2005), and [Fumihiko Maki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumihiko_Maki)'s Media Lab Extension (2009) stand out among the Boston area's traditional architecture as examples of contemporary campus "[starchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starchitect)".[^cite_note-Starchitecture-160][^cite_note-172] These high-end buildings have not always been well received;[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] in 2010, *[The Princeton Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princeton_Review)* included MIT in a list of twenty schools whose campuses are "tiny, unsightly, or both".[^cite_note-175]

### Housing

Main article: [Housing at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_at_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)

See also: [List of Massachusetts Institute of Technology fraternities, sororities, and ILGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_fraternities,_sororities,_and_ILGs)



[Simmons Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_at_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology#Simmons_Hall), an undergraduate dormitory.

Undergraduates are guaranteed four-year housing in one of MIT's 11 undergraduate dormitories.[^cite_note-176] Those living on campus can receive support and mentoring from live-in graduate students and faculty.[^cite_note-177] Because housing assignments are made based on the preferences of the students themselves, diverse social atmospheres can be sustained in different living groups; for example, according to the *Yale Daily News* staff's *The Insider's Guide to the Colleges, 2010*, "The split between East Campus and West Campus is a significant characteristic of MIT. East Campus has gained a reputation as a thriving counterculture."[^cite_note-178] MIT also has five dormitories for single graduate students and two apartment buildings on campus for married student families.[^cite_note-179]

MIT has an active Greek and [co-op housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_housing_cooperative) system, including thirty-six [fraternities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternity), [sororities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorority), and independent living groups ([FSILGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FSILG)).[^cite_note-180] As of 2015, 98% of all undergraduates lived in MIT-affiliated housing; 54% of the men participated in fraternities and 20% of the women were involved in sororities.[^cite_note-CDS-181] Most FSILGs are located across the river in [Back Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Bay) near where MIT was founded, and there is also a cluster of fraternities on MIT's West Campus that face the Charles River Basin.[^cite_note-182] After the 1997 alcohol-related death of Scott Krueger, a new pledge at the [Phi Gamma Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Gamma_Delta) fraternity, MIT required all freshmen to live in the dormitory system starting in 2002.[^cite_note-183] Because FSILGs had previously housed as many as 300 freshmen off-campus, the new policy could not be implemented until [Simmons Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_MIT_undergraduate_dormitories#Simmons_Hall) opened in that year.[^cite_note-184]

In 2013–2014, MIT abruptly closed and then demolished undergrad dorm Bexley Hall, citing extensive water damage that made repairs infeasible. In 2017, MIT shut down Senior House after a century of service as an undergrad dorm. That year, MIT administrators released data showing just 60% of Senior House residents had graduated in four years. Campus-wide, the four-year graduation rate is 84% (the cumulative graduation rate is significantly higher).[^cite_note-185]

### Off-campus real estate

MIT has substantial [commercial real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_real_estate) holdings in Cambridge on which it pays [property taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax), plus an additional voluntary [payment in lieu of taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_in_lieu_of_taxes) (PILOT) on academic buildings which are legally tax-exempt. As of 2025, it is the largest taxpayer in the city, contributing 14% of the city's local tax revenues.[^cite_note-UnderstandingTaxes-186] Holdings include [Technology Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_Square_\(Cambridge,_Massachusetts\)), parts of [Kendall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_Square), [University Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Park_\(Cambridge\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), and many properties in [Cambridgeport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeport,_Cambridge) and [Area 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_4,_Cambridge) neighboring the main campus.[^cite_note-187] The land is used for investment purposes and held for potential long-term expansion.[^cite_note-188]

## Organization and administration



Lobby 7 at 77 [Massachusetts Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Avenue_\(metropolitan_Boston\)) is regarded as the main entrance to campus.

MIT is a state-chartered [nonprofit corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_corporation) governed by a privately appointed [board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_directors) known as the **MIT Corporation**.[^cite_note-Mead-189] The Corporation has 60–80 members at any time, some with fixed terms, some with life appointments, and eight who serve *[ex officio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio_member)*.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-Mead-189][^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] The Corporation approves the budget, new programs, degrees and faculty appointments, and elects a president to manage the university and preside for the Institute's faculty.[^cite_note-Mead-189][^cite_note-BO-144] The current president is [Sally Kornbluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Kornbluth), a cell biologist and former provost at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), who became MIT's eighteenth president in January 2023.[^cite_note-193]

MIT has five schools ([Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_School_of_Science), [Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_School_of_Engineering), [Architecture and Planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_School_of_Architecture_and_Planning), [Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Sloan_School_of_Management), and [Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_School_of_Humanities,_Arts,_and_Social_Sciences)) and one college ([Schwarzman College of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Schwarzman_College_of_Computing)); the institute does not operate a law school or a medical school.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-Schools-196][^cite_note-197] Faculty committees control MIT's curriculum and many areas of research, student life, and administrative affairs.[^cite_note-198] The chair of each of MIT's academic departments reports to the dean of that department's school, who in turn reports to the Provost.[^cite_note-199] Academic departments also report to "Visiting Committees," specialized bodies of Corporation members and outside experts who evaluate the performance, activities, and needs of each department.

MIT's [endowment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_endowment), real estate, and other financial assets are managed by the MIT Investment Management Company (MITIMCo), a subsidiary of the MIT Corporation created in 2004.[^cite_note-200] A minor revenue source for much of the Institute's history, the endowment's role in MIT operations has grown due to strong investment returns since the 1990s, making it [one of the largest U.S. university endowments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_the_United_States_by_endowment_size).[^cite_note-201] Among its holdings are a majority of shares in the audio equipment manufacturer [Bose Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose_Corporation), as well as a commercial real estate portfolio in [Kendall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_Square).[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]

MIT is a large, highly residential, research university with a majority of enrollments in graduate and professional programs.[^cite_note-Carnegie-206] The university has been [accredited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_accreditation) by the [New England Association of Schools and Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Association_of_Schools_and_Colleges) since 1929.[^cite_note-207] MIT operates on a [4–1–4 academic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_term#Collegiate_calendars) with the fall semester beginning after [Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day_\(United_States\)) and ending in mid-December, a 4-week "Independent Activities Period" in the month of January, and the spring semester commencing in early February and ceasing in late May.[^cite_note-208]

MIT students refer to both their majors and classes using numbers or acronyms alone.[^cite_note-209] Departments and their corresponding majors are numbered in the approximate order of their foundation; for example, Civil and Environmental Engineering is Course 1, while Linguistics and Philosophy is Course 24.[^cite_note-Butcher-210] Students majoring in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science (EECS), the most popular department, collectively identify themselves as "Course 6".[^cite_note-211] The most popular major, Computer Science and Engineering, is known as "Course 6-3".[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213] MIT students use a combination of the department's course number and the number assigned to the class to identify their subjects; for instance, the introductory calculus-based [classical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_mechanics) course is simply "8.01" (pronounced *eight-oh-one*) at MIT.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-217]

### Undergraduate program

MIT enrolls about 4,500 undergraduates in about 53 majors, conferring only [bachelor of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_science) degrees.[^cite_note-IRmajors-222] The four-year residential program tries to hold classroom time in balance with "[learning by doing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_by_doing)," an aim the institute compresses into its motto, *mens et manus*, or "mind and hand".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStrattonMannix2005-223] Engineering claims the largest share of undergraduates, ahead of science majors, with management, the humanities and social sciences, and architecture and planning enrolling smaller shares.[^cite_note-IRmajors-222] As of 2025, the largest undergraduate degree programs were in Computer Science and Engineering (Course 6–3), Mechanical Engineering (Course 2), Artificial Intelligence and Decision Making (Course 6–4), Electrical Engineering and Computer Science (Course 6–2), and Mathematics (Course 18).[^cite_note-Enrollment_Statistics-5]

Admission is among the most selective in the United States: in recent cycles MIT has admitted 4–5 percent of applicants and very few transfers.[^cite_note-Carnegie-206][^cite_note-CDS2024-25-224][^cite_note-AdmitStats-225] The share of admitted students who enroll, its yield, has run above 85 percent in the 2020s, ahead of schools such as Stanford and Harvard.[^cite_note-TechYield2025-226] MIT is one of a few American universities that meets the full financial need for every admitted student, domestic and international.[^cite_note-sfs-affordable-227][^cite_note-need_blind-228] Beginning in 2025 it charged no tuition to families earning under \$200,000.[^cite_note-Petri2024-229]

Every undergraduate completes a shared core, the General Institute Requirements, which currently require two terms each of calculus and physics, a term each of chemistry and biology, a laboratory subject, and eight subjects in the humanities, arts, and social sciences.[^cite_note-GIR-230] Two of those subjects, with two more in the student's major, must be "communication-intensive".[^cite_note-CommReq-231] One hurdle survives from an older era: to graduate, students must pass a swimming test.[^cite_note-232]



The [Infinite Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Corridor) is the primary passageway through campus.

Teaching leans on lectures, smaller recitations, weekly problem sets (called "p-sets"), and frequent exams. Students students and administrators have likened the pace of instruction to "drinking from a fire hose".[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] To aid with adjustment, students' first-term subjects appear on the transcript only if passed.[^cite_note-235] Freshmen may choose to join alternative learning communities, such as [Experimental Study Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_Study_Group), [Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concourse_Program_at_MIT), or Terrascope.[^cite_note-Freshman_Year-236]

In keeping with *mens et manus*, MIT's curriculum promotes "hands-on" work.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] The [Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_Research_Opportunities_Program), founded in 1969 and since copied widely, places most undergraduates in faculty research for credit, pay, or on a volunteer basis; some leave with publications, patents, or startups.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240]

Historically, MIT's intensity can breed gamesmanship. In *[The Hidden Curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hidden_Curriculum)* (1970), dean Benson Snyder argued that thriving at MIT often meant working out which official requirements to ignore in favor of unwritten norms, and that the resulting maneuvering, including the student-compiled "[course bibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology#Course_%22Bibles%22)" of old problem sets and answers, could crowd out real learning.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnyder1970-241][^cite_note-242]

### Graduate programs

MIT's graduate programs overlap with the undergraduate program, and many courses are taken by qualified students at both levels. Most departments administer a doctoral program, and MIT offers advanced degrees in the humanities, social sciences, and [engineering and science fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEM_fields). The majority of graduate students enroll in research doctorates programs, and more than 90% of doctoral students are supported by fellowships, research assistantships (RAs), or teaching assistantships (TAs), which usually cover tuition costs and provide stipend support.[^cite_note-243] The institute offers a smaller number of tuition-funded [professional degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_degree) programs, the largest of which is the [Master of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) (MBA) in the Sloan School of Management.[^cite_note-Carnegie-206] Because students are brought to work on funded research, graduate enrollment levels depend heavily on outside research funding sources.

Admission to graduate programs is decentralized. MIT has no graduate school, and departments directly admit applicants to degree programs.[^cite_note-244] Departments offers advanced academic degrees such as the Doctor of Philosophy ([PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD)), and [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) (DSc), Master of Science (MS), and various Engineer's Degrees. Many graduating undergraduates choose to complete a continuing one-year Masters of Engineering (MEng) program, most frequently in computer science.[^cite_note-Hir-245] MIT also hosts several joint graduate programs such as an [MD-PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD-PhD) with [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School) and a joint program in [oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography) with [Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole_Oceanographic_Institution).[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248][^cite_note-\:2-249]

### Collaborations



[Eero Saarinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eero_Saarinen)'s [Kresge Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kresge_Auditorium) (1955) is a classic example of [post-war architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-century_modern).

The university historically pioneered research and training collaborations between academia, industry and government.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268] In 1946, President Compton, Harvard Business School professor [Georges Doriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Doriot), and Massachusetts Investor Trust chairman Merrill Grisswold founded [American Research and Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Research_and_Development_Corporation), the first American [venture-capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture-capital) firm.[^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270] In 1948, Compton established the MIT Industrial Liaison Program.[^cite_note-271] Throughout the late 1980s and early 1990s, American politicians and business leaders accused MIT and other universities of contributing to a [declining economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_1980s_recession) by [transferring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_transfer) taxpayer-funded research and technology to international – especially [Japanese](15578.md) – firms that were competing with struggling American businesses.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273] On the other hand, MIT's extensive collaboration with the federal government on research projects has led to several MIT leaders serving as [presidential scientific advisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Science_Advisory_Committee) since 1940.[^cite_note-275] MIT established a Washington Office in 1991 to continue effective [lobbying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying) for research funding and national [science policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_policy).[^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277]



Walker Memorial is a monument to MIT's fourth president, [Francis Amasa Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Amasa_Walker).

MIT's proximity[^cite_note-278] to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) ("the other school up the [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_River)") has led to a substantial number of research collaborations such as the [Harvard–MIT Division of Health Sciences and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard%E2%80%93MIT_Division_of_Health_Sciences_and_Technology) and the [Broad Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Institute).[^cite_note-EdPart-279] In addition, students at the two schools can [cross-register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-registration) for credits toward their own school's degrees without any additional fees.[^cite_note-EdPart-279] A cross-registration program between MIT and [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) has also existed since 1969, and in 2002 the [Cambridge–MIT Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge%E2%80%93MIT_Institute) launched an undergraduate exchange program between MIT and the [University of Cambridge](25978572.md).[^cite_note-EdPart-279] MIT also has a long-term partnership with [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London), for both student exchanges and research collaboration.[^cite_note-280][^cite_note-281] More modest cross-registration programs have been established with [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University), [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University), [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University), [Massachusetts College of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_College_of_Art), and the [School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_the_Museum_of_Fine_Arts,_Boston).[^cite_note-EdPart-279]

MIT maintains substantial research and faculty ties with independent research organizations in the Boston area, such as the [Charles Stark Draper Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stark_Draper_Laboratory), the [Whitehead Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead_Institute) for Biomedical Research, and the [Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole_Oceanographic_Institution).[^cite_note-\:2-249] Ongoing international research and educational collaborations include the [Amsterdam Institute for Advanced Metropolitan Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Institute_for_Advanced_Metropolitan_Solutions?action=edit\&redlink=1) (AMS Institute),[^cite_note-282] Singapore-MIT Alliance, MIT-[Politecnico di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politecnico_di_Milano),[^cite_note-EdPart-279][^cite_note-283] MIT-[Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zaragoza) International Logistics Program, and projects in other countries through the MIT International Science and Technology Initiatives (MISTI) program.[^cite_note-EdPart-279][^cite_note-284]

The mass-market magazine *[Technology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_Review)* is published by MIT through a subsidiary company, as is a special edition that also serves as an [alumni magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumni_magazine).[^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286] The [MIT Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Press) is a major [university press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_press), publishing over 200 books and 30 journals annually, emphasizing science and technology as well as arts, architecture, new media, current events, and social issues.[^cite_note-287]

MIT Microphotonics Center and [PhotonDelta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhotonDelta?action=edit\&redlink=1) founded the global roadmap for integrated photonics: Integrated Photonics Systems Roadmap – International (IPSR-I). The first edition has been published in 2020. The roadmap is an amalgamation of two previously independent roadmaps: the IPSR roadmap of MIT Microphotonics Center and AIM Photonics in the United States, and the WTMF (World Technology Mapping Forum) of PhotonDelta in Europe.[^cite_note-288] In 2022, Open Philanthropy donated \$13,277,348 to MIT to study potential risks from AI.[^cite_note-289]

### Libraries, collections, and museums

See also: [Massachusetts Institute of Technology Libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_Libraries) and [Campus of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology § Artwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_of_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology#Artwork)

The MIT library system consists of five subject libraries: Barker (Engineering), Dewey (Economics), Hayden (Humanities and Science), Lewis (Music), and Rotch (Arts and Architecture). There are also various specialized libraries and archives. The libraries contain more than 2.9 million printed volumes, 2.4 million microforms, 49,000 print or electronic journal subscriptions, and 670 reference databases. The past decade has seen a trend of increased focus on digital over print resources in the libraries.[^cite_note-290] Notable collections include the Lewis Music Library with an emphasis on 20th and 21st-century music and electronic music,[^cite_note-291] the [List Visual Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_Visual_Arts_Center)'s rotating exhibitions of contemporary art,[^cite_note-292] and the Compton Gallery's cross-disciplinary exhibitions.[^cite_note-293] MIT allocates a percentage of the budget for all new construction and renovation to commission and support its extensive public art and outdoor sculpture collection.[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295] For example, the display boats, known as the [Hart Nautical Collection](https://mitmuseum.mit.edu/more/hart-nautical-gallery), are located on the second-floor hallways of Building 5 (part of the main Maclaurin Buildings) at MIT in Cambridge, MA. The collection features numerous ship models, marine art, and boat plans that are on permanent display for visitors.

The [MIT Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Museum) was founded in 1971 and collects, preserves, and exhibits artifacts significant to the culture and [history of MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_MIT). The museum now engages in significant educational outreach programs for the general public, including the annual [Cambridge Science Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Museum#Cambridge_Science_Festival), the first celebration of this kind in the United States. Since 2005, its official mission has been, "to engage the wider community with MIT's science, technology and other areas of scholarship in ways that will best serve the nation and the world in the 21st century".[^cite_note-MITMuseum-296]

### Research

MIT is [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Classification_of_Institutions_of_Higher_Education) among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity";[^cite_note-Carnegie-206] research expenditures totaled \$952 million in 2017.[^cite_note-297] The federal government was the largest source of sponsored research, with the [Department of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services) granting \$255.9 million, [Department of Defense](7279897.md) \$97.5 million, [Department of Energy](62866.md) \$65.8 million, [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) \$61.4 million, and [NASA](18426568.md) \$27.4 million.[^cite_note-Research-298] MIT employs approximately 1300 researchers in addition to faculty.[^cite_note-MITFac-299] In 2011, MIT faculty and researchers disclosed 632 inventions, were issued 153 patents, earned \$85.4 million in cash income, and received \$69.6 million in royalties.[^cite_note-300] Through programs like the Deshpande Center, MIT faculty leverage their research and discoveries into multi-million-dollar commercial ventures.[^cite_note-301]

In electronics, [magnetic-core memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic-core_memory), [radar](25676.md), [single-electron transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-electron_transistor), and [inertial guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_guidance) controls were invented or substantially developed by MIT researchers.[^cite_note-302][^cite_note-RLE_History-303] [Harold Eugene Edgerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Eugene_Edgerton) was a pioneer in [high-speed photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_photography) and [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar).[^cite_note-304][^cite_note-305] [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon) [introduced Boolean logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switching_circuit_theory) to circuit design, providing foundations for digital systems.[^cite_note-306] In the domain of computer science, MIT faculty and researchers made fundamental contributions to [cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener), [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Minsky), [computer languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Weizenbaum), [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Winston), [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_Brooks), and [cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Rivest).[^cite_note-RLE_History-303][^cite_note-307] At least nine [Turing Award](31093.md) laureates and seven recipients of the [Draper Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper_Prize) in engineering have been or are currently associated with MIT.[^cite_note-TuringAward-308][^cite_note-309]

Current and previous physics faculty have won eight [Nobel Prizes](52497.md),[^cite_note-IR_Nobel-310] four [ICTP Dirac Medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_\(ICTP\)),[^cite_note-311] and three [Wolf Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) predominantly for their contributions to subatomic and [quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum) theory.[^cite_note-312] Members of the chemistry department have been awarded three [Nobel Prizes](25416893.md) and one Wolf Prize for the discovery of novel syntheses and methods.[^cite_note-IR_Nobel-310] MIT biologists have been awarded six [Nobel Prizes](52502.md) for their contributions to genetics, immunology, oncology, and molecular biology.[^cite_note-IR_Nobel-310] Professor [Eric Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Lander) was one of the principal leaders of the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project).[^cite_note-313][^cite_note-314] [Positronium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positronium) atoms,[^cite_note-315] synthetic [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin),[^cite_note-316] [synthetic self-replicating molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Rebek),[^cite_note-317] and the genetic bases for [Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyotrophic_lateral_sclerosis) (also known as ALS or Lou Gehrig's disease) and [Huntington's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington's_disease) were first discovered at MIT.[^cite_note-MIT_Firsts-318] [Jerome Lettvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Lettvin) transformed the study of cognitive science with his paper "What the frog's eye tells the frog's brain".[^cite_note-319] Researchers developed a system to convert MRI scans into 3D printed physical models.[^cite_note-320]

Beginning in 1980, [Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO) (LIGO) was designed and constructed by a team of scientists from [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), MIT, and industrial contractors, and funded by the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation). It was designed to open the field of [gravitational-wave astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational-wave_astronomy) through the detection of [gravitational waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_wave) predicted by [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity).[^cite_note-321] Gravitational waves were [detected for the first time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_observation_of_gravitational_waves) by the LIGO detector in 2015. For contributions to the LIGO detector and the observation of gravitational waves, two Caltech physicists, [Kip Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne) and [Barry Barish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Barish), and MIT physicist [Rainer Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_Weiss) won the [Nobel Prize in physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_physics) in 2017.[^cite_note-322] Weiss, who is also an MIT graduate, designed the laser interferometric technique, which served as the essential blueprint for the LIGO.[^cite_note-323]

In the domain of humanities, arts, and social sciences, as of October 2019 MIT economists have been awarded seven [Nobel Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Economics) and nine [John Bates Clark Medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal).[^cite_note-IR_Nobel-310][^cite_note-324] Linguists [Noam Chomsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky) and [Morris Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Halle) authored seminal texts on [generative grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_grammar) and [phonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonology).[^cite_note-325][^cite_note-326] The [MIT Media Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Media_Lab), founded in 1985 within the [School of Architecture and Planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_School_of_Architecture_and_Planning) and known for its unconventional research,[^cite_note-327][^cite_note-328] has been home to influential researchers such as [constructivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_\(learning_theory\)) educator and [Logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo_\(programming_language\)) creator [Seymour Papert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Papert).[^cite_note-329]

Allegations of [research misconduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_misconduct) or improprieties at MIT have received press coverage. In 1986, Professor [David Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Baltimore), a [Nobel Laureate in medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physiology_or_Medicine), became embroiled in decade-long research investigation over a colleague's alleged data falsification.[^cite_note-Saltus1990-330][^cite_note-Boffey1988-331] The government's final investigation identified paper errors, no evidence of misconduct, and resulted an overhaul of federal research misconduct procedures.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKevles1998-332] In 2000, Professor [Ted Postol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Postol) alleged research misconduct involving a [ballistic missile defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_defense) study in the Lincoln Laboratory and accused the MIT administration of failing to fully investigate.[^cite_note-333] A Department of Defense investigation did not substantiate the allegations.[^cite_note-334][^cite_note-335] Associate Professor [Luk Van Parijs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luk_Van_Parijs) was dismissed in 2005 following allegations of scientific misconduct and found guilty of the same by the [United States Office of Research Integrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Research_Integrity) in 2009.[^cite_note-336][^cite_note-337]

In 2019, [Clarivate Analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarivate_Analytics) named 54 members of MIT's faculty to its list of "Highly Cited Researchers". That number places MIT eighth among the world's universities.[^cite_note-338]

## Student life

Main articles: [Traditions and student activities at MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditions_and_student_activities_at_MIT) and [MIT class ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_class_ring)



[ROTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officers'_Training_Corps) students celebrate [Veterans Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Day) at MIT in 2019.

The faculty and student body place a high value on [meritocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy) and on technical proficiency.[^cite_note-371][^cite_note-372] MIT has never awarded an [honorary degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree),[^cite_note-373] nor does it award [athletic scholarships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_scholarship),[^cite_note-374] or [Latin honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors)[^cite_note-375] upon graduation. However, MIT has twice awarded honorary professorships: to [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) in 1949 and [Salman Rushdie](28722.md) in 1993.[^cite_note-376]

Many [upperclass](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/upperclassman) students and alumni wear a large, heavy, distinctive [class ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_ring) known as the "[Brass Rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass_Rat)".[^cite_note-Brass_Rat-377][^cite_note-BrassRat2013-378] Originally created in 1929, the ring's official name is the "Standard Technology Ring".[^cite_note-379] The undergraduate ring design (a separate graduate student version exists as well) varies slightly from year to year to reflect the unique character of the MIT experience for that class, but always features a three-piece design, with the MIT seal and the class year each appearing on a separate face, flanking a large rectangular bezel bearing an image of a [beaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Beaver).[^cite_note-Brass_Rat-377] The [initialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initialism) [IHTFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHTFP), representing the informal school motto "I Hate This Fucking Place" and jocularly [euphemized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemized) as "I Have Truly Found Paradise", "Institute Has The Finest Professors", "Institute of Hacks, TomFoolery and Pranks", "It's Hard to Fondle Penguins", and other variations, has occasionally been featured on the ring given its historical prominence in student culture.[^cite_note-380]

### Caltech Rivalry

Main article: [Caltech–MIT rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech%E2%80%93MIT_rivalry)

MIT also shares a well-known [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech%E2%80%93MIT_rivalry) with the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (Caltech), stemming from both institutions' reputations as two of the highest ranked and most highly recognized science and engineering schools in the world.[^cite_note-381] The rivalry is an unusual college rivalry given its focus on academics and pranks instead of sports, and due to the geographic distance between the two (their campuses are separated by about 2580 miles and are on [opposite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) [coasts](89126.md) of the United States). In 2005, Caltech students pranked MIT's Campus Preview Weekend by distributing t-shirts that read "MIT" on the front, and "...because not everyone can go to Caltech" on the back.[^cite_note-calvsmit-382][^cite_note-383][^cite_note-mit2005-384] Additionally, the word Massachusetts in the "Massachusetts Institute of Technology" engraving on the exterior of the Lobby 7 dome was covered with a banner so that it read "That Other Institute of Technology". In 2006, MIT retaliated by posing as contractors and stealing the 1.7-ton, 130-year-old [Fleming cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_System_at_the_California_Institute_of_Technology#Fleming_cannon), a Caltech landmark. The cannon was relocated to Cambridge, where it was displayed in front of the [Green Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Building_\(MIT\)) during the 2006 Campus Preview Weekend.[^cite_note-385][^cite_note-386] In September 2010, MIT students unsuccessfully tried to place a life-sized model of the [TARDIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TARDIS) time machine from the *[Doctor Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Who)* (1963–present) television series on top of Baxter Hall at Caltech. A few months later, Caltech students collaborated to help MIT students place the TARDIS on top of their originally planned destination.[^cite_note-387] The rivalry has continued, most recently in 2014, when a group of Caltech students gave out mugs sporting the MIT logo on the front and the words "The Institute of Technology" on the back. When heated, the mugs turned orange and read, "Caltech, The Hotter Institute of Technology".[^cite_note-388]

### Activities

Main article: [Traditions and student activities at MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditions_and_student_activities_at_MIT)

See also: [Hacks at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacks_at_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)

See also: [List of Massachusetts Institute of Technology fraternities and sororities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_fraternities_and_sororities)



The start of the [MIT Mystery Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mystery_Hunt) in 2007

MIT has over 500 recognized student activity groups,[^cite_note-389] including a [campus radio station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMBR), *[The Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tech_\(newspaper\))* student newspaper, an annual [entrepreneurship competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_\$100K_Entrepreneurship_Competition), a [crime club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Crime_Club), and weekly screenings of popular films by the [Lecture Series Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_life_and_culture_at_MIT#Lecture_Series_Committee). Less traditional activities include the "world's largest open-shelf [collection of science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Science_Fiction_Society)" in English, a [model railroad club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMRC), and a vibrant [folk dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tech_Squares) scene. Students, faculty, and staff are involved in over 50 educational outreach and public service programs through the [MIT Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Museum), Edgerton Center, and MIT Public Service Center.[^cite_note-390]

Fraternities and sororities provide a base of activities in addition to housing. Approximately 1,000 undergrads, 48% of men and 30% of women, participate in one of several dozen Greek Life men's, women's and co-ed chapters on the campus.[^cite_note-391]

The [Independent Activities Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_life_and_culture_at_MIT#Independent_Activities_Period) is a four-week-long "term" offering hundreds of optional classes, lectures, demonstrations, and other activities throughout the month of January between the Fall and Spring semesters. Some of the most popular recurring IAP activities are Autonomous Robot Design (course 6.270), Robocraft Programming (6.370), and MasLab [competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditions_and_student_activities_at_MIT#Competitions),[^cite_note-Discover-392] the annual ["mystery hunt"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mystery_Hunt),[^cite_note-Globe-393] and [Charm School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditions_and_student_activities_at_MIT#Independent_Activities_Period).[^cite_note-CharmSchool-394][^cite_note-395] More than 250 students pursue [externships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externships) annually at companies in the US and abroad.[^cite_note-396][^cite_note-397]

Many MIT students also engage in "hacking", which encompasses both the [physical exploration of areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof_and_tunnel_hacking) that are generally off-limits (such as rooftops and steam tunnels), as well as [elaborate practical jokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacks_at_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^cite_note-398][^cite_note-399] Examples of high-profile hacks have included the [abduction of Caltech's cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech's_rival),[^cite_note-400] reconstructing a [Wright Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Flyer) atop the Great Dome,[^cite_note-401] and adorning the [John Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harvard) statue with the [Master Chief's Mjölnir Helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_Chief_\(Halo\)).[^cite_note-402]

### Athletics

Main article: [MIT Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Engineers)



The [Zesiger Sports and Fitness Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zesiger_Sports_and_Fitness_Center) houses a two-story fitness center as well as swimming and diving pools.

MIT sponsors 31 varsity sports, reduced from 41 in 2009, and has one of the three broadest NCAA Division III athletic programs.[^cite_note-403][^cite_note-404] Nearly 20% of undergraduates play at least one varsity sport.[^cite_note-AthleteFAQ-405] Applying athletes are considered by the academic standards applied to all applicants, though coaches may advocate for their admission.[^cite_note-AthleteFAQ-405][^cite_note-SchillSoriero-406]

MIT participates in the [NCAA's](60706.md) [Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_III_\(NCAA\)) and the [New England Women's and Men's Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Women's_and_Men's_Athletic_Conference), with women's crew competing at the Division I level in the [Patriot League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_League). MIT's intercollegiate sports teams, called the Engineers, have won 22 Team National Championships and 42 Individual National Championships. Teams' overall success has varied; in 2013, the [MIT mens football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Engineers_football) broke a 124-year streak without back-to-back winning seasons, then went unbeaten the following year.[^cite_note-London-407]

MIT is the all-time leader in [Academic All-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_All-America) selections (468 as of August 2025), ahead of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and the [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](323058.md) across all NCAA divisions.[^cite_note-408] MIT Athletes won 13 [Elite 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_90_Award) awards and ranks first among NCAA Division III programs, and third among all divisions.[^cite_note-409]

## People

Further information: [List of Nobel laureates affiliated with Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_affiliated_with_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and [List of astronauts educated at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_astronauts_educated_at_the_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)

MIT affiliates have received major academic and professional honors across a range of fields. As of October 2024, 105 Nobel laureates, 26 Turing Award winners, and 8 Fields Medalists have been affiliated with MIT as alumni, faculty, or researchers. The institute also counts 58 National Medal of Science recipients, 29 National Medal of Technology and Innovation recipients, and 84 MacArthur Fellows among its affiliates.[^cite_note-410]

In public service, 49 MIT-affiliated astronauts have flown in space,[^cite_note-411] 16 have served as Chief Scientist of the U.S. Air Force, and 8 alumni have served as foreign heads of state. Many alumni have also held senior positions in the U.S. federal government, including members of the Cabinet, the Federal Reserve, and the leadership of national defense and intelligence agencies. Alumni and faculty have founded or led many notable companies, particularly in technology, defense, and biotechnology.

MIT is one of nine U.S. colleges that is both [need-blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need-blind_admission) and full-need for all undergraduate applicants, including international students.[^cite_note-MITSFS-416] All financial aid is based on demonstrated need, MIT does not offer merit or athletic scholarships.[^cite_note-MITSFS-416] Beginning with the 2025–2026 academic year, tuition is not charged to students from families with incomes below \$200,000 with typical assets.[^cite_note-WBUR2024-417][^cite_note-MITSFS-416] At times, annual increases led to a student tradition (dating back to the 1960s) of tongue-in-cheek "tuition riots."[^cite_note-Tuition_Riot-418]

The admissions process does not give preference to [children of alumni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy_preferences).[^cite_note-NYTChetty2023-419][^cite_note-Petersen2012-420] A 2023 study by economists [Raj Chetty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raj_Chetty), David Deming, and John Friedman found that legacy preferences, athletic recruitment, and non-academic ratings were the primary drivers of admissions advantages for wealthy applicants at twelve highly selective U.S. colleges. At MIT, students from the wealthiest families were no more likely to attend than other applicants with comparable test scores.[^cite_note-Chetty2023-421][^cite_note-NYTChetty2023-419]

In August 2024, after the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) overruled race-based [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action_in_the_United_States) in *[Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_Fair_Admissions_v._Harvard)* (2023), the university reported that for the class of 2028, Black and Latino student enrollment decreased from previous averages to 5 and 11 percent, respectively, while [Asian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans) enrollment increased to 47 percent.[^cite_note-422][^cite_note-423]

MIT has been nominally [co-educational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-education) since admitting [Ellen Swallow Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Swallow_Richards) in 1870. Richards also became the first female member of MIT's faculty, specializing in [sanitary chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_health).[^cite_note-Bowden-424][^cite_note-CHFBio-425] Female students remained a small minority prior to the completion of the first wing of a women's dormitory, [McCormick Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Dexter_McCormick), in 1963.[^cite_note-426][^cite_note-427][^cite_note-428] Between 1993 and 2009 the proportion of women rose from 34 percent to 45 percent of undergraduates and from 20 percent to 31 percent of graduate students.[^cite_note-Enrollments-215][^cite_note-429] As of 2009, women outnumbered men in Biology, Brain & Cognitive Sciences, Architecture, Urban Planning, and Biological Engineering.[^cite_note-Enrollments-215][^cite_note-Women_Enrollments-430]

A 2000 panel featuring Institute Professors Emeriti and Nobel Laureates (from left to right) [Franco Modigliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Modigliani), [Paul Samuelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Samuelson), and [Robert Solow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Solow)

MIT's faculty count reached 1,000 in 1983 and has stayed near that level in the years since.[^cite_note-FNL2006-431][^cite_note-MITFactFacStaff-4] Faculty are responsible for lecturing classes, for conducting original research, for advising both graduate and undergraduate students, and for sitting on academic committees. As of May 2026, MIT identifies 41 faculty members, past or present, as Nobel Prize recipients, the majority in [Economics](23535306.md) or [Physics](52497.md).[^cite_note-Faculty_Awards-432] Faculty and teaching staff included 55 [Guggenheim Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow), 27 [MacArthur Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellow), and 5 [John Bates Clark medalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal).[^cite_note-Faculty_Awards-432] Faculty members who have made extraordinary contributions to their research field as well as the MIT community are granted appointments as [Institute Professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_Professor) for the remainder of their tenures.&#x20;

MIT faculty members have often been recruited to lead other colleges and universities. Founding faculty-member [Charles W. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_W._Eliot) became president of Harvard University in 1869, a post he would hold for 40 years, during which he had influence both on American higher education and on secondary education[^cite_note-433]. [George Ellery Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ellery_Hale) played a central role in the development of the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (Caltech),[^cite_note-434] and other faculty members have been key founders of the nearby [Olin College of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olin_College_of_Engineering).

MIT faculty have served as the president's science adviser four times, beginning with the first appointment to the role: [James Killian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rhyne_Killian), then MIT's president, who advised Eisenhower from 1957 to 1959.[^cite_note-killian-osa-435] He was followed by the electrical engineer [Jerome Wiesner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Wiesner) under Kennedy (1961–1964),[^cite_note-wiesner-mit-436] the geophysicist [Frank Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Press) under Carter (1977–1981),[^cite_note-aps-advisers-437] and the biologist [Eric Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Lander) under Biden (2021–2022).[^cite_note-lander-ostp-438][^cite_note-441] Other faculty have led federal agencies or held cabinet posts, including [Ernest Moniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Moniz) as [secretary of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Energy) (2013–2017),[^cite_note-moniz-doe-442] [Hans Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Mark) (1979–1981)[^cite_note-mark-af-443] and [Sheila Widnall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Widnall) (1993–1997)[^cite_note-widnall-af-444][^cite_note-widnall-70th-445] as [secretary of the Air Force](305210.md), [Marcia McNutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcia_McNutt) as president of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (2016–2026),[^cite_note-nas-leadership-446] and [Xavier de Souza Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_de_Souza_Briggs) as associate director of the [White House Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget).[^cite_note-briggs-omb-447]

MIT is the second-largest employer in the city of Cambridge.[^cite_note-Top25Employers-448] Based on feedback from employees, MIT was ranked No. 7 as a place to work, among US colleges and universities as of March 2013.[^cite_note-Glassdoor-449] Surveys cited a "smart", "creative", "friendly" environment, noting that the [work-life balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work-life_balance) tilts towards a "strong work ethic" but complaining about "low pay" compared to an industry position.[^cite_note-GlassdoorMIT-450]

### Notable alumni

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of Massachusetts Institute of Technology alumni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_alumni) and [List of Nobel laureates affiliated with Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_affiliated_with_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).

Many of MIT's over 120,000 alumni have achieved considerable success in scientific research, public service, education, and [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_sector). As of October 2020, 41 MIT alumni have won Nobel Prizes, 48 have been selected as [Rhodes Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholar),[^cite_note-451] 61 have been selected as [Marshall Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Scholar),[^cite_note-IRAwardsHonors-452] and 3 have been selected as [Mitchell Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Scholarship).[^cite_note-\:3-453]

Alumni in United States politics and public service include former [chairman of the Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Federal_Reserve) [Ben Bernanke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bernanke), former [MA-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts's_1st_congressional_district) representative [John Olver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Olver), former [CA-13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_13th_congressional_district) representative [Pete Stark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Stark), [KY-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky's_4th_congressional_district) representative [Thomas Massie](28005891.md), California senator [Alex Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Padilla), and former [National Economic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Economic_Council) chairman [Lawrence H. Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_H._Summers).[^cite_note-454]

MIT alumni have founded or co-founded many notable companies, such as [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Noyce), [McDonnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Smith_McDonnell) [Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Wills_Douglas,_Sr.), [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Howard_Green), [3Com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Metcalfe), [Qualcomm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Viterbi), [Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amar_Bose), [Raytheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannevar_Bush), [Apotex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apotex), [Koch Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_C._Koch), [Rockwell International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Rockwell), [Genentech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Swanson), [Dropbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Houston), and [Campbell Soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Thompson_Dorrance). According to the British newspaper *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, "a survey of living MIT alumni found that they have formed 25,800 companies, employing more than three million people including about a quarter of the workforce of Silicon Valley. Those firms collectively generate global revenues of about \$1.9 trillion (£1.2 trillion) a year". If the companies founded by MIT alumni were a country, they would have the 11th-highest [GDP](12594.md) of any country in the world.[^cite_note-Entrepreneur-455][^cite_note-Kauffman-456][^cite_note-457]

More than one third of the [United States' crewed spaceflights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NASA_missions#Human_spaceflight) have included [MIT-educated astronauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_alumni#Alumni_Astronauts), a contribution exceeding that of any university excluding the [United States service academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_service_academies).[^cite_note-458] Of the [12 people who have set foot on the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Apollo_astronauts) as of 2019, four graduated from MIT (among them [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) [Lunar Module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Lunar_Module) Pilot [Buzz Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Aldrin)). Alumnus and former faculty member [Qian Xuesen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qian_Xuesen) led the [Chinese nuclear-weapons program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_China) and became instrumental in Chinese rocket-program.[^cite_note-459]

Noted alumni in other fields include health care policy analyst and journalist [Avik Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avik_Roy), children's book author [Hugh Lofting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Lofting),[^cite_note-460][^cite_note-461] sculptor [Daniel Chester French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Chester_French), guitarist [Tom Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Scholz) of the band [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_\(band\)), the British *[BBC](19344654.md)* and *[ITN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITN)* correspondent and political advisor [David Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walter_\(British_journalist_and_politician\)), *[The New York Times](30680.md)* columnist and Nobel Prize-winning economist [Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman), *[The Bell Curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bell_Curve)* author [Charles Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Murray_\(political_scientist\)), and [United States Supreme Court building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Supreme_Court_building) architect [Cass Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cass_Gilbert).[^cite_note-WDL-462] Other distinguished alumni include economist Esther Duflo,[^cite_note-463][^cite_note-464] who received the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](23535306.md) in 2019 for her experimental approach to poverty alleviation; physicist [Mildred Dresselhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildred_Dresselhaus), a pioneer in carbon science and recipient of the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom)[^cite_note-465][^cite_note-466]; physicist and science policy leader [Shirley Ann Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Ann_Jackson), former chair of the [U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Regulatory_Commission) and president of [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute)[^cite_note-467][^cite_note-468]; and astronaut [Eileen Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Collins), the first woman to pilot and command a [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) mission.[^cite_note-469][^cite_note-470]
