Railways in Peru

**Rail transport in Peru** has a varied history. Peruvian rail transport has never formed a true network, primarily comprising separate lines running inland from the coast and built according to freight need rather than passenger need.

Many Peruvian railroad lines owe their origins to contracts granted to [United States](3434750.md) entrepreneurs [Henry Meiggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Meiggs)[^cite_note-1] and [W. R. Grace and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._R._Grace_and_Company)[^cite_note-2] but the mountainous nature of [Peru](170691.md) made expansion slow and much of the surviving mileage is of twentieth-century origin. It was also challenging to operate, especially in the age of the [steam locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive).[^cite_note-Fawcett-3]

Also [Ernest Malinowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Malinowski), Polish engineer in exile distinguished himself in the Central Trans-Andean Railway project which runs from [Callao](294857.md) to [Huancayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancayo).

In the latter part of the 1880s, the principal public railways, the Central and Southern, with others, passed to the control of the [Peruvian Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Corporation), registered in [London](17867.md) and controlled by Americans [Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_P._Grace) and [William R. Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Russell_Grace).[^cite_note-4] In 1972 they were [nationalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) as [Empresa Nacional de Ferrocarriles del Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empresa_Nacional_de_Ferrocarriles_del_Peru) (ENAFER), but this survived as an operator only until 1999 when most surviving lines were [privatized](24661.md). Regular passenger traffic now operates over only a small proportion of the mileage.

The [Tacna-Arica Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacna-Arica_Railway) crosses the boundary with [Chile](5489.md), running twice daily, once in the morning and once in the late afternoon. The Southern Railway provides connection with [Bolivia](3462.md) by ship across [Lake Titicaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Titicaca).

## Principal lines



[Lima](85423.md) - [Morococha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morococha) - [Abra Anticona (Ticlio)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticlio) - [La Oroya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Oroya) - [Huancayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancayo) passenger line

The Central Railway, **[Ferrocarril Central del Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarril_Central_del_Per%C3%BA)** (FCC), incorporates the first railway in Peru opened on May 17, 1851, linking the [Pacific](23070.md) port of [Callao](294857.md) and the capital [Lima](85423.md) (13.7 km (8.5 mi) of [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge)).[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5] This was expanded to form the Callao, Lima & Oroya Railway, opened to Chicla by 1878, the original contractor being Henry Meiggs and engineer being [Ernest Malinowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Malinowski) assisted by [Edward Jan Habich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jan_Habich). The line reached [La Oroya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Oroya) by 1893 and [Huancayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancayo) (346 km (215 mi)) in 1908.[^cite_note-Binns-6] It is the second highest railway in the world (following opening of the [Qingzang railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzang_railway) in Tibet), with the Galera summit tunnel under Mount Meiggs at 4,783 m (15,692 ft) and [Galera station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galera_railway_station) at 4,777 m (15,673 ft) above sea level, requiring constructional feats including many [switchbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zig_zag_\(railway\)) and steel bridges. Since 1999 it has been operated as the [Ferrocarril Central Andino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarril_Central_Andino) (FCCA) (with its associated maintenance company Ferrovias Central Andina (FVCA)) by the [Pittsburgh](25101.md)-registered [Railroad Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_Development_Corporation).[^cite_note-7] There is no regular passenger traffic but excursions are operated from the Lima [Desamparados station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desamparados_station).[^cite_note-8] In April 1955 the Central Railway opened a spur line from La Cima on the [Morococha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morococha_District) branch (4,818 m (15,807 ft) above sea level) to Volcán Mine, reaching an (at the time) world record altitude of 4,830 m (15,850 ft). Both branch and spur have since closed to traffic.[^cite_note-9]



Contemporary [Railcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railcar) manufactured by [EIKON International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EIKON_International) with final destination to the [Cusco - Machu Picchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusco_-_Machu_Picchu) line

[^cite_note-10]

The Central is extended by the **[Ferrocarril Huancayo - Huancavelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarril_Huancayo_-_Huancavelica)** which was authorised in 1904 (engineer: Charles Weber) but work was interrupted during the [World War I](4764461.md) and it was not opened throughout (148 km (92 mi) of [3 ft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_ft_gauge_railways) (914 mm) gauge) until 1926.[^cite_note-Koch-11] Work was continued but never completed on extending the line to the Pacific coast. After a period under provincial government control it was agreed in June 2006 by the Peruvian government that FCCA should go ahead with [converting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_gauge_conversion) the line to 1,435 mm (4 ft 8+1⁄2 in) [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) (as had in fact been intended prior to 1919). Estimated to take 16 months, the US\$33m project was to be funded jointly by the government and [CAF – Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_%E2%80%93_Development_Bank_of_Latin_America_and_the_Caribbean).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] This project was finished by October 2010.[^cite_note-14]

Also connecting with the Central, at La Oroya, is the **Cerro de Pasco** railway opened in standard gauge form in 1904 to serve ore mining in the [Cerro de Pasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_de_Pasco) district.[^cite_note-Binns-6] It was thoroughly North American in all its operations[^cite_note-Fawcett-3] and, although primarily a mineral line, did run a passenger operation, latterly known as the “Flamingo” from the consist purchased from the [Florida East Coast Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_East_Coast_Railway).[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5] The owning company was nationalised as Centromín in 1974 and operation of the railway was taken over by FCCA.[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5] 80 km (50 mi) of 3 ft (914 mm) gauge was completed of a Tambo del Sol-Pachitea line intended eventually to extend to the head of [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) navigation on the [Ucayali River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ucayali_River) at [Pucallpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pucallpa); this aspiration was abandoned by the government in 1957.[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5]

The Southern Railway, **[Ferrocarriles del Sur del Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarriles_del_Sur_del_Per%C3%BA)** (FCS), another Meiggs concession, was completed from [Arequipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arequipa) to [Puno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puno) in 1876 and to the coast at [Matarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matarani). The railway also operated steamers (including the *[Yavari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavari_\(ship\))*) and [train ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_ferry#Peru) on [Lake Titicaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Titicaca) connecting with [Guaqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaqui) in [Bolivia](3462.md). Although work on the [Juliaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliaca)–[Cuzco](72735.md) section was begun in 1872 it was not completed through until 1908. The summit of this section is reached at La Raya (4,313 m (14,150 ft) above sea level). Since 1999 it has been operated by [PeruRail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeruRail), an affiliate of the [Belmond Ltd.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmond_Ltd.) group, whose tourist trains form the only passenger services.[^cite_note-15]

From Cuzco, the 3 ft (914 mm) gauge **[Ferrocarril Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarril_Santa_Ana)** (Ferrocarril Cuzco á Santa Ana) (engineer: Mauro Valderrama) was authorised in 1907, originally at 2 ft 6 in (762 mm) gauge, but the first section was not opened until the early 1920s. It was extended to [Aguas Calientes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguas_Calientes,_Peru) (113 km (70 mi)) in 1928, passing to government control in 1931. Although further extended in stages through to [Quillabamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quillabamba) (reached in 1978), landslides (attributed to effects of [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o-Southern_Oscillation)) caused it to be abandoned beyond [Hidroelectrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_Station_\(train_station\)) in 1998. It is now operated by PeruRail and [Inca Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Rail), and forms the only means of access for visitors to [Machu Picchu](80019.md).[^cite_note-Koch-11][^cite_note-16] At the beginning of 2010 it was cut by landslides.[^cite_note-17]

The isolated **[Ferrocarril Tacna á Arica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacna%E2%80%93Arica_railway)** was completed in 1856. Following the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) it and the surrounding territory passed to [Chile](5489.md); after a settlement in 1929 the [Tacna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacna) end of the line was returned to Peru while the port of [Arica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arica,_Chile) remained in Chilean hands. The British concession for the line reverted to the Peruvian government during [World War II](32927.md). The line remained open for both passengers and freight for several decades, with a museum collection at Tacna station.[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5] The line closed in May 2012; in June 2014 the Peruvian government sought bids for redeveloping the line.[^cite_note-Tacna_–_Arica_reopening_studies-18] Finally, in 2016 the line was reopened, offering two services daily.[^cite_note-19]

### Tren de la Costa

A regional rail line, referred to as [Tren de la Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tren_de_la_Costa_\(Peru\)) is planned, paralleling the Pan American Highway between the cities of Sullana and Ica, via Lima.[^cite_note-20]

## Other lines

The newest railway in Peru is a standard gauge line opened in 1959 by the **[Southern Peru Copper Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Copper_Corporation)** from its opencast mine at Toquepala to the port of [Ilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilo,_Peru) (187 km or 116 mi) with a later branch largely in tunnel to its workings at Cuajone.[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5]

There were a number of other lines, all now closed, mostly for mineral or agricultural traffic, running inland from the coast north of Lima[^cite_note-Whetham_1-21] and in [Pisco Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisco_Province).[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5] There were also lines serving [nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrate) deposits in the [Tarapacá Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapac%C3%A1_Region), ceded to Chile in 1883.[^cite_note-Whetham_2-5]

Some railway exhibits, including a working 500 mm (19+3⁄4 in) gauge pleasure line, are to be seen in the Parque de la Amistad in the [Surco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Surco) district of Lima.

## Metro



&#x20;[Lima Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metro) line 1

[Lima](85423.md) has a [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) service called [Lima Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metro) or *Tren Eléctrico*. The line 1 is operating now with 39 km and 26 stations, a second line is under construction. A fast bus system called *metropolitano* complements this system.[^cite_note-22]

A light railway [Metro Wanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Wanka) was partially constructed in the central Andean city of [Huancayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancayo) but the project eventually failed.
