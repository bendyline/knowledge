New Zealand compared to the [East Coast of the United States](89126.md). New Zealand is slightly longer than [California](5407.md) and is roughly the size of [Colorado](5399.md) or [Oregon](26811621.md).

[New Zealand](4913064.md) is a [major non-NATO ally](3812445.md) of the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-1]

United States and New Zealand share comparable histories; both nations grew from [British colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonies) and both have indigenous populations of [East Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_languages) descent ([Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people) and [Native Hawaiians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians)). Both nations are known for their proximity to the Pacific Ocean, and the New Zealand-United States relationship is a key factor of [Asia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia%E2%80%93Pacific) geopolitics. Both states have also been part of a [Western alliance](21208200.md) of states in various wars. Together with three other [Anglophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone) countries, they comprise the [Five Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Eyes) espionage and intelligence alliance.

### Polynesian and pre-statehood relations

Cross-cultural similarities between the [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people) (*tangata māori*) of precolonial New Zealand and native Hawaiians (*kanaka māoli*) date back thousands of years. According to oral history, the Māori homeland is [Hawaiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiki), a semi-mythical island from which the name of the [state of Hawaii](13270.md) is derived.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-pollex-4] The Māori language and the Hawaiian language are closely related [Polynesian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_languages), to the extent that the Hawaiians' endonym [*Kānaka Maoli*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) itself is [cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate) to *Tangata Māori* of the former[^cite_note-5] both deriving from Proto-Polynesian \**ma(a)qoli*, which has the reconstructed meaning "true, real, genuine".[^cite_note-pollex2-6][^cite_note-Eastern_Polynesian_Languages-7]

The United States established consular representation in New Zealand in 1838 to represent and protect American shipping and whaling interests, appointing [James Reddy Clendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Reddy_Clendon) as consul, resident at [Okiato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okiato) in the [Bay of Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Islands).[^cite_note-8] In 1840, New Zealand became part of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) with the signing of the [Treaty of Waitangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Waitangi). Although it gradually grew more independent, for its first hundred years, New Zealand followed the United Kingdom's lead on foreign policy. In declaring war on Germany on 3 September 1939, Prime Minister [Michael Joseph Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Joseph_Savage) proclaimed, "Where she goes, we go; where she stands, we stand".

While Hawaii was still an [independent kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Kingdom), many Hawaiian sailors in the 1850s settled at [Papakōwhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papak%C5%8Dwhai) not far from Wellington, where they were referred to with the exonym *Wahu* (after the island [Oʻahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%CA%BBahu)).[^cite_note-9] On the other hand, Māori New Zealanders quickly established a community at [Manoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoa); they at one point gained the audience with [Kamehameha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Kamehameha) and [Kalākaua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Kal%C4%81kaua) nobility in 1920.[^cite_note-10]

### Conflicts fought alongside the United States

Further information: [Military history of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_New_Zealand) and [Military history of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_States)

New Zealand has fought in a number of conflicts on the same side as the United States, including [World War I](4764461.md), [World War II](32927.md), the [Korean War](16772.md), the [Vietnam War](32611.md), the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) and the [Afghanistan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)); it also sent a unit of army engineers to help rebuild Iraqi infrastructure during the [Iraq War](5043324.md).

### World War II

See also: [Battle of Kolombangara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kolombangara)



Tanks and landing equipment in New Zealand for the US Guadalcanal operation

During the Pacific theatre of WWII (1941–1945), significant numbers of US military personnel were deployed to New Zealand to prepare for crucial battles such as [Guadalcanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_Campaign) and [Tarawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tarawa);[^cite_note-autogenerated2-11] there were between 15,000 and 45,000 US servicemen stationed in New Zealand at any one time between June 1942 and mid-1944.[^cite_note-12] The habits and spending power of these troops had various influences on New Zealand's culture.

Though relations were largely positive, some points of tension developed. For example, the [1st Marine Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Marine_Division_\(United_States\)), was tasked with loading reconfiguration from administrative to combat configuration during a strike by Wellington dock workers.[^cite_note-13] The [Battle of Manners Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manners_Street) also occurred in Wellington involving American servicemen and New Zealand servicemen and civilians outside the Allied Services Club in Manners Street after American servicemen in the Services Club began forcibly stopping [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people) soldiers from also using the Club because of their skin colour. Many New Zealand soldiers in the area, both white ([Pākehā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81keh%C4%81)) and Māori, combined in opposition. The battle resulted in a possible two American deaths and 1 New Zealand Serviceman being arrested, as well as a cover up.

After the war New Zealand joined with [Australia](4689264.md) and the United States in the [ANZUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZUS) security treaty in 1951.

### Korean War 1950–1953

Main article: [New Zealand in the Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_in_the_Korean_War)

New Zealand was among those who responded to the [United Nations](31769.md) call for help in Korea. New Zealand joined 15 other nations, including the United Kingdom and the United States, in the anti-communist war. The Korean War was also significant, as it marked New Zealand's first move towards association with the United States' stand against communism.[^cite_note-14]

New Zealand contributed six frigates, several smaller craft, and a 1044 strong volunteer force (known as K-FORCE) to the [Korean War](16772.md). The ships were under the command of a British flag officer[^cite_note-15] and formed part of the [US Navy](20518076.md) screening force during the [Battle of Inchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Inchon), performing shore raids and inland bombardment. The last New Zealand soldiers did not leave until 1957 and a single liaison officer remained until 1971. A total of 3,794 New Zealand soldiers served in K-FORCE and 1300 in the [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Navy) deployment. 33 were killed in action, 79 wounded and one soldier was taken prisoner. That prisoner was held in North Korea for eighteen months and repatriated after the armistices.

### ANZUS Treaty

Main article: [ANZUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZUS)



This World War II poster was intended to remind an American audience of New Zealand's efforts in the Pacific theatre of war. It was part of a series of posters issued by the New Zealand Legation in Washington DC

[^cite_note-16]

The Australia, New Zealand, United States Security Treaty ([ANZUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZUS) or ANZUS Treaty) is the military alliance which binds Australia and New Zealand and, separately, Australia and the United States to cooperate on defence matters in the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) area, though today the treaty is understood to relate to defence operations. Initially the 1951 ANZUS Treaty was a fully mutual [collective security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_security) alliance between Australia, New Zealand and the United States, but this is no longer the case as the United States suspended its treaty obligations to New Zealand following the refusal to allow an American destroyer, the [USS *Buchanan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Buchanan_\(DDG-14\)), into a New Zealand port in February 1985. A 1984 policy of a [New Zealand nuclear-free zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_nuclear-free_zone) meant that any ship thought to be carrying nuclear weapons was banned from New Zealand's ports, which meant all American naval vessels were essentially denied access due to the American policy to 'neither confirm nor deny' the presence of nuclear weapons.[^cite_note-\:0-17]

In suspending obligations to New Zealand under the ANZUS treaty, the [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Ronald_Reagan_administration#New_Zealand) cut major military and diplomatic ties between Wellington and Washington, downgrading New Zealand from 'ally', to 'friend'.[^cite_note-18] This included removing New Zealand from military exercises and war games in the area, and limiting the intelligence sharing to New Zealand.[^cite_note-19] New Zealand has not removed itself from ANZUS, arguing that allowing nuclear weapons into New Zealand was not part of the ANZUS treaty, and that New Zealand's position is not a pacifist or Anti-American decision,[^cite_note-20] and would increase its conventional military cooperation with the US.[^cite_note-21] The Americans felt personally betrayed by the New Zealanders and would not accept the anti-nuclear stance,[^cite_note-22] stating that New Zealand will be welcome back in ANZUS if and when New Zealand accepted all US ship visits.[^cite_note-\:0-17] The New Zealand government passed the [New Zealand Nuclear Free Zone, Disarmament, and Arms Control Act 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Nuclear_Free_Zone,_Disarmament,_and_Arms_Control_Act_1987).[^cite_note-23] This Act formalized the previous policy of New Zealand being a [nuclear-free zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear-free_zone) and banned all nuclear-powered ships or nuclear weapons from entering New Zealand waters or air space.[^cite_note-24]

New Zealand's relationship with the United States further suffered when French agents [sank the *Rainbow Warrior*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Rainbow_Warrior) while it was docked in Auckland Harbour in July 1985. The United States, as well as other western countries aside from Australia, failed to condemn the attack which was seen in New Zealand as [state-sponsored terrorism](317645.md) by the French. This inaction furthered the breach between the two countries, with the US State Department finally stating that it "deplored such acts, wherever they may occur" in September 1985, a few days after the French admission of guilt.[^cite_note-25]

In 1996, the United States under President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) reinstated New Zealand's status from a 'friend' to an 'ally' by designating New Zealand as a [Major non-NATO ally](3812445.md).[^cite_note-26]

Although the ANZUS treaty has never been officially called on by the United States, New Zealand has fought alongside the United States in multiple conflicts since it was signed, notably the [Korean War](16772.md), [Vietnam War](32611.md), [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), and [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md).

### Vietnam War



Members of 161 Battery, Royal New Zealand Artillery, carry out a fire mission.

Main article: [New Zealand in the Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_in_the_Vietnam_War)

New Zealand's involvement in the [Vietnam War](32611.md) was highly controversial, sparking widespread protest at home from anti-Vietnam War movements modelled on their American counterparts.[^cite_note-27] This conflict was also the first in which New Zealand did not fight alongside the United Kingdom, instead following the loyalties of the ANZUS Pact (Australia also fought in the war).[^cite_note-28]

New Zealand's initial response was carefully considered, and characterised by Prime Minister [Keith Holyoake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Holyoake)'s cautiousness towards the entire Vietnam question. New Zealand non-military economic assistance continued from 1966 and averaged US\$347,500 annually. This funding went to mobile health teams to support refugee camps, the training of village vocational experts, to medical and teaching equipment for [Huế University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu%E1%BA%BF_University), equipment for a technical high school and a contribution toward the construction of a science building at the University of Saigon. Private civilian funding was also donated for 80 Vietnamese students to take scholarships in New Zealand.

The government preferred minimal involvement, with other [Southeast Asian](28741.md) deployments already straining the New Zealand armed forces. From 1961, New Zealand came under pressure from the United States to contribute military and economic assistance to South Vietnam, but refused.

American pressure continued for New Zealand to contribute military assistance,[^cite_note-29] as the United States would be deploying combat units (as opposed to merely advisors) itself soon, as would Australia. Holyoake justified New Zealand's lack of assistance by pointing to its military contribution as an ally of [Malaysia](3607937.md), which was [ confronted in arms by Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Malaysia_confrontation), but eventually the government decided to contribute. It was seen as in the nation's best interests to do so—failure to contribute even a token force to the effort in Vietnam would have undermined New Zealand's position in ANZUS and could have had an adverse effect on the alliance itself. New Zealand had also established its post-Second World War security agenda around countering communism in South-East Asia and of sustaining a strategy of forward defence, and so needed to be seen to be acting upon these principles. On 27 May 1965 Holyoake announced the government's decision to send 161 Battery, [Royal Regiment of New Zealand Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Regiment_of_New_Zealand_Artillery) to South Vietnam in a combat role. The Engineers were replaced by the battery in July 1965. The battery served under the U.S. [173rd Airborne Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/173rd_Airborne_Brigade) until the formation of the [1st Australian Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Australian_Task_Force) in 1966.



Soldiers from Victor Coy, RNZIR patrol with Australian APCs during *Operation Coburg*, February 1968



President Lyndon B Johnson and Prime Minister Keith Holyoake disembarking an RNZAF DC6 during the President's visit in 1966.

In 1966, when the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation came to an end, Australia and New Zealand were pressured by the United States to expand their involvement in the Vietnam War. The 1st Australian Task Force (1ATF) was formed with additional Australian infantry and artillery, supported by Australian tanks, cavalry, air support, logistics, intelligence, engineering, and [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces). The task force was given the province of [Phuoc Tuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuoc_Tuy) as its tactical area of operations. New Zealand reluctantly increased its commitment by sending two companies of [RNZIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Infantry_Regiment) troops in 1967. The companies were integrated into [2nd Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Battalion,_Royal_Australian_Regiment) in March 1968, forming the 2RAR/NZ (ANZAC) Battalion, with New Zealand personnel assuming various positions in the battalion, including that of second in command. A [NZSAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Special_Air_Service) troop was sent in 1968 and worked on operations with the Australian SAS. The New Zealand rifle companies were deployed on infantry operations with various Australian regiments and in independent operations in addition to their battalion operations, with each member serving a 12-month tour of duty thereafter.

The RNZA artillery battery continued to support Australian, New Zealand, and American forces throughout the entire war. RNZAF pilots joined [No. 9 Squadron RAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._9_Squadron_RAAF) in 1968 and from December 1968 more than a dozen RNZAF [forward air controllers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_air_control) served with the [Seventh Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Air_Force), [United States Air Force](32090.md).[^cite_note-30]

As American focus shifted to President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon)'s '[Vietnamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamization)'—a policy of slow disengagement from the war, by gradually building up the [Army of the Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Republic_of_Vietnam) so that it could fight the war on its own—New Zealand withdrew one of the infantry companies at the end of 1970, replacing it with a training team in January 1971. Numbering 25 men from all branches of service, the New Zealand team assisted the [United States Army](32087.md) Training Team in Chi Lang. In December 1971 all Australian and New Zealand combat forces left Vietnam after turning over the 1ATF base to the Army of the Republic of Vietnam. In February 1972 a second NZ training team, 18 strong, was deployed to Vietnam and was based at [Dong Ba Thin Base Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Ba_Thin_Base_Camp), near Cam Ranh Bay. It assisted with the training of [Cambodian](334751.md) infantry battalions. This team also provided first aid instruction and specialist medical instruction at Dong Ba Thin's 50-bed hospital. The two New Zealand training teams were withdrawn from South Vietnam in December 1972.[^cite_note-31]



[Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) fighters, [Royal New Zealand Air Force](190903.md) and [United States Navy](20518076.md) patrol aircraft in Australia during Exercise Sandgroper in 1982

Like veterans from many of the other allied nations, as well as Vietnamese civilians, New Zealand veterans of the war claimed that they (and their children and grandchildren) had suffered serious harm as a result of exposure to [Agent Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange), a [herbicidal warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbicidal_warfare) programme used by the British military during the [Malayan Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency) and the U.S. military during the Vietnam War. In 1984, Agent Orange manufacturers paid New Zealand, Australian and Canadian veterans in an out-of-court settlement,<sup>[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand%E2%80%93United_States_relations#endnote_AgentOrangeSettlement](12792048.md#endnote_AgentOrangeSettlement)</sup> and in 2004 Prime Minister [Helen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Clark)'s government apologised to Vietnam War veterans who were exposed to Agent Orange or other toxic defoliants,<sup>[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand%E2%80%93United_States_relations#endnote_GovtApology](12792048.md#endnote_GovtApology)</sup> following a health select committee's inquiry into the use of Agent Orange on New Zealand servicemen and its effects.<sup>[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand%E2%80%93United_States_relations#endnote_Report](12792048.md#endnote_Report)</sup> In 2005, the New Zealand government confirmed that it supplied Agent Orange chemicals to the United States military during the conflict. Since the early 1960s, and up until 1987, it manufactured the 2,4,5T herbicide at a plant in [New Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Plymouth), which was then shipped to U.S. military bases in South East Asia.

### Middle East (1982–present)



New Zealand Prime Minister [Helen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Clark) and [United States Secretary of Defense](43998.md) [Robert Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gates) at [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), 21 March 2007.



[United States Secretary of Defense](43998.md) [Robert Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gates) and [New Zealand Minister of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(New_Zealand\)) [Phil Goff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Goff) at [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), 11 May 2007.

New Zealand has assisted the United States and [Britain](31717.md) in many of their military activities in the [Middle East](19323.md). However New Zealand forces have fought only in Afghanistan; in other countries New Zealand support has been in the form of support and engineering. During the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) two New Zealand frigates joined the British [Royal Navy](26061.md) in monitoring merchant shipping in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). and in 1991, New Zealand contributed three transport aircraft and a medical team to assist [coalition forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition#Military) in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War).

New Zealand's heaviest military involvement in the Middle East in recent decades has been in Afghanistan following the [United States](3434750.md)-led invasion of that country after the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md). A Squadron of [New Zealand Special Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Special_Air_Service) (NZSAS) personnel were dispatched, and in March 2002 they took part in [Operation Anaconda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Anaconda) against about 500 to 1000 [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) and [Taliban](30635.md) forces in the [Shah-i-Kot Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah-i-Kot_Valley) and Arma Mountains southeast of Zorma, Afghanistan. New Zealand has also supplied two transport aircraft and a 122-strong tri-service Provincial Reconstruction Team, which has been located in [Bamyan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamyan_Province) since 2003.[^cite_note-32]

### Afghanistan (2001–2021)

The [New Zealand Special Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Special_Air_Service) (NZSAS) began operations assisting in the [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md) in late 2001. Three six-month rotations of between 40 and 65 soldiers from the SAS served in Afghanistan during [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) before the unit was withdrawn in November 2005. On 17 June 2004, two SAS soldiers were wounded in a predawn gun-battle in central Afghanistan. According to a New Zealand government fact sheet released in July 2007, SAS soldiers routinely patrolled enemy territory for three weeks or more at a time, often on foot, after being inserted by helicopter. There were "casualties on both sides" during gun battles.[^cite_note-33]

In December 2004, the [Presidential Unit Citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Unit_Citation_\(United_States\)) was awarded to those units that comprised the Combined Joint Special Operations Task Force-SOUTH/Task Force K-BAR between 17 October 2001 and 30 March 2002 for "extraordinary heroism" in action. One of these units was the NZSAS. The citation said SAS helped "neutralise" Taliban and al-Qaeda in "extremely high risk missions, including search and rescue, special reconnaissance, sensitive site exploitation, direct action missions, destruction of multiple cave and tunnel complexes, identification and destruction of several known al-Qaeda training camps, explosions of thousands of pounds of enemy ordnance." "They established benchmark standards of professionalism, tenacity, courage, tactical brilliance and operational excellence while demonstrating superb esprit de corps and maintaining the highest measures of combat readiness." In August 2009, the [John Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand) government decided that NZSAS forces would be sent back to Afghanistan.[^cite_note-34]

In April 2013, the last remaining New Zealand troops, the [Provincial Reconstruction Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Reconstruction_Team_\(New_Zealand\)), withdrew from Afghanistan.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] As of 2017, a contingent of 10 [New Zealand Defence Force](21359.md) personnel remained in Afghanistan to provide mentorship and support at the Afghan National Army Officer Academy in Kabul, in addition to support personnel.[^cite_note-37]

### Iraq (2003–2011)

Main article: [Iraq War](5043324.md)

In accordance with [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) [Resolution 1483](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1483) New Zealand contributed a small engineering and support force to assist in post-war reconstruction and provision of humanitarian aid. The engineers returned home in October 2004 and New Zealand is still represented in [Iraq](7515928.md) by liaison and staff officers working with coalition forces.

### Hurricane Katrina

Main article: [International response to Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_response_to_Hurricane_Katrina)

In response to [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) in the U.S. in 2005, [Prime Minister](106321.md) [Helen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Clark) sent condolences and an offer of help to [United States President](24113.md) [George W. Bush](3414021.md). [Foreign Affairs Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(New_Zealand\)) [Phil Goff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Goff) sent a message of sympathy to [Secretary of State](32293.md) [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice).[^cite_note-38] The [Government of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_Zealand) offered to send an [urban search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_search_and_rescue) team, a [disaster victim identification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Victim_Identification_System) team or post-[disaster recovery](1455589.md#Recovery) personnel, and gave \$2 million to the [Red Cross](15487.md) for aid and disaster relief. A senior member of the [Ministry of Civil Defence and Emergency Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Civil_Defence_&_Emergency_Management_\(New_Zealand\)), John Titmus went to [Denton, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denton,_Texas), to lead an official [United Nations Disaster Assessment and Coordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Disaster_Assessment_and_Coordination) (UNDAC) team to assess the damage from the hurricane.[^cite_note-39]

## Political relations

### Fifth Labour Government, 1999–2008

Following the 9/11 attacks, Prime Minister Clark expressed condolences with the victims of 9/11 and contributed New Zealand military forces to the US-led [War in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)) in October 2001.[^cite_note-CRS_Report-40] While New Zealand did not participate in the 2003 invasion of Iraq, it still contributed a small engineering and support force to assist coalition forces in post-war reconstruction and the provision of humanitarian work.[^cite_note-41] [Diplomatic cables leaked in 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak) suggested New Zealand had only done so in order to keep valuable Oil for Food contracts.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

New Zealand was also involved in the [Proliferation Security Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proliferation_Security_Initiative) (PSI), which was launched by President Bush on 31 May 2003 as part of a US-led global effort which aimed to stop trafficking of [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction) (WMD), their delivery systems, and related materials to and from [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)) and [non-state actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-state_actor) of proliferation concern.[^cite_note-44] New Zealand's participation in the PSI led to the improvement of defense ties with the United States, including increased participation in joint military exercises. In 2008, the Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice visited Prime Minister Helen Clark, and described New Zealand as a "friend and an ally." She also signalled that the US–NZ relationship had moved beyond the ANZUS dispute. The strengthening of [US–NZ bilateral relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US%E2%80%93NZ_bilateral_relations) would be continued by the [Barack Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_administration), and Clark's successor: the [National Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand) of [John Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Key).[^cite_note-CRS_Report-40]

In May 2006, US [Assistant Secretary of State for East Asian and Pacific Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Secretary_of_State_for_East_Asian_and_Pacific_Affairs), [Christopher R. Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_R._Hill), described the New Zealand anti-nuclear issue as "a relic", and signalled that the US wanted a closer defence relationship with New Zealand. He also praised New Zealand's involvement in [Afghanistan](737.md) and reconstruction in [Iraq](7515928.md). "Rather than trying to change each other's minds on the nuclear issue, which is a bit of a relic, I think we should focus on things we can make work"[^cite_note-45]



New Zealand Prime Minister [Helen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Clark) and United States President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) meet at the [White House](33057.md) in 2007

Relations with the [George W. Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) improved and became increasingly closer especially after the [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_of_New_Zealand) [Prime Minister](106321.md) [Helen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Clark) visited the White House on 22 March 2007.[^cite_note-46] They ended the difficult relationship that had escalated in 1986.[^cite_note-CRS_Report-40] After Helen Clark's visit to Washington and talks with President Bush, The *[New Zealand Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Herald)* reported, on 23 March 2007, that the United States "no longer seeks to change" New Zealand's anti-nuclear policy, and that this constituted "a turning point in the US-NZ relationship".[^cite_note-NZ_Herald_10430398-47]

In July 2008, [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice), the [United States Secretary of State](32293.md), visited New Zealand, which she referred to as "a friend and an ally". The *New Zealand Herald* reported that the use of the word "ally" was unexpected, as United States officials had avoided it since the ANZUS crisis. Rice stated that the relationship between the two countries was a "deepening" one, "by no means \[...] harnessed to or constrained by the past", which prompted *the Herald* to write of a "thaw in US-NZ relations".[^cite_note-NZ_Herald_10523634-48][^cite_note-NZ_Herald_10523751-49] Secretary Condoleezza Rice stated that "US and New Zealand have moved on. If there are remaining issues to be addressed then we should address them".[^cite_note-NZ_Herald_10523625-50] She went on to say that: "New Zealand and the United States, Kiwis and Americans, have a long history of partnership. It is one that is grounded in common interests, but it is elevated by common ideals. And it is always defined by the warmth and the respect of two nations, but more importantly, of two peoples who are bound together by countless ties of friendship and family and shared experience."[^cite_note-51]

## Economic relations and trade



An [Air New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_New_Zealand) [Boeing 777-300ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777) at [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md). Aircraft and travel services are a major source of trade between New Zealand and the United States.

The United States is New Zealand's third-largest individual trading partner (behind China and Australia), while New Zealand is the United States' 48th-largest partner.[^cite_note-\:1-68] In 2018, bilateral trade between the two countries was valued at US\$13.9 billion[^cite_note-\:1-68] or NZ\$18.6 billion.[^cite_note-GSTC_StatNZ-69] New Zealand's main exports to the United States are meat, travel services, wine, dairy products, and machinery. The United States' main exports to New Zealand are machinery, vehicles and parts, travel services, aircraft, and medical equipment.[^cite_note-GSTC_StatNZ-69] The US is a major source of tourists coming to New Zealand.

In addition to trade, there is a high level of corporate and individual investment between the two countries; in March 2012, the United States had a total of \$44 billion invested in New Zealand.[^cite_note-70]

### Proposed free trade agreement

Further information: [Free trade agreements of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreements_of_New_Zealand)

The government of New Zealand has indicated its desire for a [free trade agreement](10883.md) (FTA) between the United States and New Zealand.[^cite_note-71] Such an agreement would presumably be pursued alongside, or together with, an FTA between the United States and Australia, as New Zealand and Australia have had their own FTA for almost twenty years and their economies are now closely integrated.[^cite_note-72] Fifty House members wrote to President Bush in January 2003 advocating the initiation of negotiations, as did 19 Senators in March 2003. However, Administration officials had enumerated several political and security impediments to a potential FTA, including New Zealand's longstanding refusal to allow nuclear-powered ships into its harbors and its refusal to support the United States in the Iraq War.[^cite_note-73]

New Zealand's economy is small compared with that of the United States, so the economic impact of an FTA would be modest for the United States and considerably larger for New Zealand. However, US merchandise exports to New Zealand would rise by about 25 percent and virtually every US sector would benefit. The inclusion of Australia would increase the magnitude of these results substantially; US exports would rise by about \$3 billion. The adjustment costs for the United States would be minimal: production in the most impacted sector, dairy products, would decline by only 0.5 percent and any adverse effect on jobs would be very small. It would also contribute toward the accomplishment of [APEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation)'s goals of achieving "free and open trade and investment in the (Asia Pacific) region by 2010."

The Friends of New Zealand Congressional Caucus member numbers stood at 62 in 2007.[^cite_note-74] Congressional support is vital for the US free trade agenda. New Zealand enjoyed strong support in the [United States Congress](31756.md) – both in the [House of Representatives](19468510.md) and the [Senate](24909346.md):

- Several letters to the President signed by Congressmen and women from both sides of the House recommended negotiations with New Zealand

- Leading senators Baucus, Grassley and [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) advocated a negotiation with New Zealand

- Friends of New Zealand Caucus was established in the Congress in February 2005 led by Representatives Kolbe (R-Arizona) and Tauscher (D-California).

- Congressional support is enhanced by the absence of any difficulty New Zealand might pose in terms of non-trade issues such as environment or labour.

  [^cite_note-council_fta-75]

On 4 February 2008, U.S. Trade Representative [Susan Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Schwab) announced that the United States would join negotiations with four [Asia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific) countries: [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), [Chile](5489.md), New Zealand and [Singapore](27318.md) to be known as the "P-4". These nations had already negotiated an FTA called the [Trans-Pacific Strategic Economic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Pacific_Strategic_Economic_Partnership), and the United States expressed its intent to become involved in the "vitally important emerging Asia-Pacific region." A number of U.S. organisations supported the negotiations including, but not limited to: the [United States Chamber of Commerce](1448498.md), [National Association of Manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Manufacturers), [National Foreign Trade Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Foreign_Trade_Council), Emergency Committee for American Trade and Coalition of Service Industries.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

On 23 September 2008, Schwab announced that the United States was to begin negotiations with the P-4 countries,[^cite_note-79] with the first round of talks scheduled for March 2009. New Zealand [Prime Minister](106321.md) [Helen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Clark) stated, "I think the value to New Zealand of the United States coming into a transpacific agreement as a partner would be of the same value as we would hope to get from a bilateral FTA. It's very, very big news." The decision to continue talks would be up to the new administration following the [2008 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election). On the potential for opposition from the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) Helen Clark said, "I believe that to Democrats, New Zealand offers very few problems because we are very keen on environment and labour agreements as part of an overall approach to an FTA".[^cite_note-NZ_Herald_10533623-80][^cite_note-Stuff.co.nz_639521-81]

After the inauguration of President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) in 2009, talks about an FTA between the two nations were postponed. Prime Minister John Key said that "New Zealand will continue to advocate very strongly for a trade deal."[^cite_note-TVNZ_2524837-82] At the [APEC meeting in Singapore in 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC_Singapore_2009), Obama announced that the U.S. would participate in the [Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Pacific_Partnership) trade agreement.[^cite_note-83] The United States signed the agreement in 2016, but before they had ratified it, Donald Trump became president and withdrew the United States from it. The other 11 countries went on to sign a revised version of the agreement, the [Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_and_Progressive_Agreement_for_Trans-Pacific_Partnership), without the United States.

### Trump tariffs

In early April 2025, the [second Trump Administration](78298108.md) imposed a ten percent tariff on New Zealand exports to the United States as part of President [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s global [Liberation Day tariffs](79617151.md). In 2024, New Zealand had exported NZ\$9 billion worth of goods to the United States; including NZ\$2.6 billion worth of meat exports and NZ\$883 million worth of dairy exports. [Stuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_\(website\)) estimated that the tariffs could cost New Zealand exports NZ\$900 million.[^cite_note-84] In response, [Minister of Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Trade_\(New_Zealand\)) [Todd McClay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_McClay) confirmed that New Zealand would not impose retaliatory tariffs but would seek to resolve differences with the United States.[^cite_note-85]

On 1 August 2025, Trump signed a second executive order imposing new tariffs rates on several international partners including New Zealand. US tariffs on New Zealand exports to the United States were raised to 15 percent. Trade Minister McClay expressed disappointment with the tariffs hike and said that New Zealand would engage with the US government to seek changes to the tariffs rate.[^cite_note-86] On 23 August, [NZ Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZ_Post) suspended several postal services to the United States and [its unincorporated territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area#Insular_areas) in response to rising costs associated with the new 15 percent tariff rate.[^cite_note-87] On 28 August, NZ Post introduced a new business parcel service to the US and its insular territories to cope with the 15 percent tariff on NZ exports.[^cite_note-88]

On 14 November 2025, Trump announced that he would lift tariffs on over 200 food products including [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef), [offal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offal) and [kiwifruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiwifruit) in response to rising American grocery prices. These foodstuffs account for 25 percent of New Zealand's exports to the United States and are worth NZ\$2.21 billion (US\$1.25 billion) annually. On 16 November, Trade Minister McClay welcomed the relaxation of US tariffs on certain New Zealand agricultural and horticultural products, and expressed hope that tariffs would be lifted on more New Zealand exports to the United States.[^cite_note-89]

In February 2026, the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) struck down most of the Liberation a day tariffs. In response, the Trump Administration imposed a temporary 10% rate on global imports, which lasted for 150 days until mid-July 2026. On 24 July, the United States imposed a 12.5 tariff on New Zealand and 59 other countries, citing an investigation into the alleged presence of forced labour in their supply chains. The [United States Trade Representative](211349.md) [Jamieson Greer](78457418.md) cited a long-standing American forced labour import ban and called on US trade partners to do the same. In response, Trade Minister McClay described the US tariffs as "very disappointing" and disputed the accuracy of the American report into the presence of forced labour in the New Zealand economy.[^cite_note-90]

## Defence relations

### Washington Declaration

The Washington Declaration between the United States and New Zealand, signed on 19 June 2012 at [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), established a framework for strengthening and building the basis for defense cooperation.[^cite_note-91] The agreement was signed by New Zealand Defence Minister [Jonathan Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Coleman_\(politician\)) and US Secretary of Defense [Leon Panetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Panetta).[^cite_note-92] While non-binding and not renewing ANZUS treaty obligations between the US and New Zealand, the Washington Declaration established the basis for increased defense cooperation between the two states.[^cite_note-93]

### Military cooperation



Commander Timothy LaBenz, USN (right), commanding officer of the USS Sampson (DDG 102), and Kaikōura Mayor Winston Gray, survey earthquake damage in a US MH-60R Sea Hawk helicopter (18 Nov. 2016).

On 21 September 2012, while on a visit to New Zealand, US Secretary of Defense Leon Panetta announced that the United States was lifting the 26-year-old ban on visits by New Zealand warships to US Department of Defense and US Coast Guard bases around the world[^cite_note-94] "These changes make it easier for our militaries to engage in discussions on security issues and to hold co-operative engagements that increase our capacity to tackle common challenges. \[We will work together despite] differences of opinion in some limited areas." At the same time, however, New Zealand had not changed its stance as a nuclear-free zone.[^cite_note-95]

On 29 October 2013, in a joint statement at the Pentagon, New Zealand Defence Minister [Jonathan Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Coleman_\(politician\)) and US Secretary of Defense [Chuck Hagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Hagel) confirmed the two countries would resume bilateral military cooperation. The announcement followed a successful meeting of Pacific Army Chiefs, co-chaired by New Zealand and the US. New Zealand was set to take part in an international anti-piracy mission in the Gulf of Aden, and participate in an upcoming Exercise Rim of the Pacific ([RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_RIMPAC)).[^cite_note-96]

The [Royal New Zealand Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Navy) (RNZN) invited the [United States Navy](20518076.md) to send a vessel to participate in the RNZN's 75th Birthday Celebrations in [Auckland](18660332.md) over the weekend of 19–21 November 2016. The guided-missile destroyer [USS *Sampson*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Sampson_\(DDG-102\)) became the first US warship to visit New Zealand in 33 years. New Zealand Prime Minister [John Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Key) granted approval for the ship's visit under the New Zealand Nuclear Free Zone, Disarmament, and Arms Control Act 1987, which requires that the Prime Minister has to be satisfied that any visiting ship is not nuclear armed or powered.[^cite_note-97] Following the [7.8 magnitude Kaikōura earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Kaik%C5%8Dura_earthquake) on 14 November 2016 the *Sampson* and other naval ships from Australia, Canada, Japan and Singapore were diverted to proceed directly to [Kaikōura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaik%C5%8Dura) to provide humanitarian assistance.[^cite_note-98]

In mid May 2025, [Radio New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_New_Zealand) reported that the [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) led by [Brigadier General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_General) [Kristin Panzenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristin_Panzenhagen) was holding talks with the New Zealand Government to launch hundreds of satellites from New Zealand. These satellites would support the US's nuclear command-control-and-communications (NC3) system. Panzenhagen also confirmed that the US was holding similar talks with European allies including the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Sweden](5058739.md).[^cite_note-99]

Between 5 and 6 August 2025, a [Royal New Zealand Air Force](190903.md) (RNZAF) C-130J Hercules evacuated three personnel of the United States [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) from [McMurdo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMurdo_Station). The mission occurred amidst frigid and dark mid-winter conditions.[^cite_note-100] The United States Embassy's Chargé d'Affaires Melissa Sweeney thanked the RNZAF for evacuating the three Americans, who received medical treatment in Christchurch.[^cite_note-101]

### New Zealand and the UKUSA community

New Zealand is one of five countries who share intelligence under the [UKUSA Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKUSA_Agreement). New Zealand has two (known) listening posts run by the [Government Communications Security Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Communications_Security_Bureau) (GCSB) as part of the [ECHELON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echelon_\(signals_intelligence\)) spy network. The partnership gives "a direct line into the inner circles of power in London and Washington,"[^cite_note-NZ_Herald_10366146-102] gives New Zealand a distinct partnership with the United States not just on economic policies but domestic security agreements and operations as well, and is a familiar platform for further deals involving both countries.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-103] The UKUSA community allows member countries to cooperate in multilateral military exercises, more recently focussing on terrorism after 9/11.

On 31 July 2025, [Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation](5861065.md) [Kash Patel](62202727.md) opened a new [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI) attaché office in Wellington to boost bilateral law enforcement cooperation. Prior to that, New Zealand was the only Five Eyes country which lacked an FBI attaché presence.[^cite_note-104]

### Strategic Alliance Cyber Crime Working Group



Map showing the Strategic Alliance Cyber Crime Working Group member countries and lead agencies

The Strategic Alliance Cyber Crime Working Group is a new initiative by Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and headed by the United States as a "formal partnership between these nations dedicated to tackling larger global crime issues, particularly organized crime". The cooperation consists of "five countries from three continents banding together to fight cyber crime in a synergistic way by sharing intelligence, swapping tools and best practices, and strengthening and even synchronizing their respective laws." This means that there will be increased information sharing between the [New Zealand Police](406756.md) and the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) on matters relating to serious fraud or cyber crime.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-103]

### National Technology and Industrial Base

In May 2024, [RNZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNZ) reported that 18 months earlier the [United States Congress](31756.md) had added New Zealand to the US [National Technology and Industrial Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technology_and_Industrial_Base?action=edit\&redlink=1) (NTIB), a military law that boosts the United States' military-industrial ties with close allies. The other NTIB partners are Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia. While the New Zealand Government was not involved in New Zealand's inclusion, top intelligence and defence officials welcomed it. NTIB is not linked to the [AUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUKUS) agreement.[^cite_note-105]

## Sports relations

## United States – New Zealand Council

Main article: [United States – New Zealand Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_%E2%80%93_New_Zealand_Council)

The United States – New Zealand Council is a non-profit, nonpartisan organisation in [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC). Dating back to 1986, it has played a prominent role in US and New Zealand bilateral relations. The council provides information on a range of economic, political, and security issues affecting the two countries and on their increasing collaboration, historical links and shared values, outlooks, and approaches on economic, political, and legal systems. The council spreads awareness in both the business community and in Congress of the importance of the Asia-Pacific region.

As well as working with the New Zealand United States Council to organise the widely lauded Partnership Forums, the US-NZ Council periodically honours distinguished individuals with the Torchbearer Award for promoting bilateral exchanges. Past recipients have included Assistant Secretary of State for East Asian and Pacific Affairs, Christopher Hill; three-term NZ Prime Minister and Ambassador to the United States, Jim Bolger; California Congressman, Calvin Dooley; NZ Prime Minister and Director of WTO, Mike Moore; Agriculture Secretary and US Trade Representative, [Clayton Yeutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton_Yeutter).

The [Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Pacific_Partnership) agreement was a focus of the council, but President Donald Trump withdrew the United States from the partnership in 2017 before it could be ratified.

## New Zealand United States Council



Sir [Michael Hardie Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hardie_Boys), [Governor-General of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_New_Zealand) and [President of the United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States_of_America), [Bill Clinton](3356.md) in 1999

Founded in 2001, the New Zealand United States Council, headquartered in [Auckland](18660332.md), is committed to fostering and developing a strong and mutually beneficial relationship between New Zealand and the United States. The council is an advocate for the expansion of trade and [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic) links between the two countries, including a possible [free trade agreement](2533562.md).[^cite_note-council-124]

The council works closely with its counterpart in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), the US-NZ Council, with business groups in New Zealand and with government agencies, especially the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade](5982589.md) and the New Zealand Embassy in Washington.[^cite_note-council-124] The council has been working for an improvement in NZ-US relations with New Zealand MPs (Members of Parliament) and their American counterparts in Congress, through discussions about political and domestic issues involving either countries. Their work has not been in vain: [United States Secretary of State](32293.md) [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice) began regular communication with [New Zealand's Foreign Affairs Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(New_Zealand\)) [Winston Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Peters) about issues such as nuclear tests in [North Korea](21255.md), and other issues of politics, trade and business affairs of both New Zealand and the United States.[^cite_note-125]

The two countries share much in common:

- Both are former colonies of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).

- Apart from their common language and status as fully developed new world economies, both countries’ soldiers have fought together in the two world wars and New Zealand supported US interests in every regional conflict in the 20th century and lately in the war against terrorism.

- Their cultures are relatively aligned and they continue to stand together on many of the same issues, such as the need to spread democracy and human rights around the globe, and the rule of international law.

- Even though ANZUS is no longer a strong link between the two countries, they worked very closely in [SEATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia_Treaty_Organization) during 1954–77.
