**Ōiso** (大磯町, *Ōiso-machi*) is a [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_towns_in_Japan) located in [Kanagawa Prefecture](61757.md), Japan. As of 1 January 2023, the town had an estimated population of 31,262 and a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 1820 persons per km².[^cite_note-Ōiso-hp-1] The total area of the town is 17.18 square kilometres (6.63 sq mi).

## Geography

Ōiso is located on the coastline of central Kanagawa Prefecture, facing [Sagami Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_Bay) of the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). The area is generally hilly, rising to Mount Koma (168 metres) in the northwest of the centre of town. The coastline of Ōiso is sandy and is regarded as the western end of the [Shōnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dnan) area. Ōiso remains a popular beach resort and holiday spot for residents of Tokyo.

### Climate

Ōiso has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Köppen *Cfa*) characterized by warm summers and cool winters with light to no snowfall. The average annual temperature in Ōiso is 15.8 °C. The average annual rainfall is 2144 mm with September as the wettest month. The temperatures are highest on average in August, at around 25.7 °C, and lowest in January, at around 5.2 °C.[^cite_note-2]

## History

Ōiso is the ancient centre of [Sagami Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_Province). The exact location of the [Nara period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_period) provincial government of Sagami Province is unknown, but tradition and the place name "Kōzu" place its probable location within the boundaries of present-day Ōiso.

As a minor coastal settlement, Ōiso was under the control of the [later Hōjō clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_H%C5%8Dj%C5%8D_clan) of [Odawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odawara,_Kanagawa) during the [Sengoku period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_period). In the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period), it was nominally part of [Odawara Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odawara_Domain), and developed as [Ōiso-juku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ciso-juku), a [post town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shukuba) on the [Tōkaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D_\(road\)) connecting [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo) with [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto). After the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration) and with the establishment of the district system in 1878, it came under the control of Yurugi District (淘綾郡, *Yurugi-gun*). Ōiso became a town on 1 April 1889 with the creation of the modern municipalities system. Blessed with a temperate climate, and with convenient access to [Tokyo](30057.md) due to the [Tōkaidō Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D_Main_Line) railway, it was favoured as a seaside health resort by politicians and literary figures during the [Meiji period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_period) after a glowing report on its location was written by noted physician [Matsumoto Jun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsumoto_Jun_\(physician\)). [Prime Minister](24833.md) [Itō Hirobumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%C5%8D_Hirobumi), [Yamagata Aritomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamagata_Aritomo), [Saionji Kinmochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saionji_Kinmochi) and [Ōkuma Shigenobu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckuma_Shigenobu), Foreign Minister [Mutsu Munemitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutsu_Munemitsu), writer [Shimazaki Toson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toson_Shimazaki) and *[zaibatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaibatsu)* founder [Yasuda Zenjirō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuda_Zenjir%C5%8D) had summer residences in Ōiso. This popularity continued into the postwar era, and Prime Minister [Shigeru Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Yoshida) also had a residence in Ōiso to which he retired after leaving politics. The former Itō, Yoshida and Shimazaki residences have been preserved as memorial museums.

Ōiso merged with neighbouring town Kokufu on 1 December 1954.

## Government

Ōiso has a [mayor-council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-council) form of government with a directly elected mayor and a [unicameral](204304.md) town council of 14 members. Ōiso, together with neighboring Ninomiya, contributes one member to the Kanagawa Prefectural Assembly. In terms of national politics, the town is part of [Kanagawa 15th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanagawa_15th_district) of the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_Japan) of the [Diet of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_of_Japan).

## Economy

The local economy of Ōiso is based primarily on seasonal tourism, agriculture and [commercial fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_fishing). The town is also a [bedroom community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedroom_community) for both [Tokyo](30057.md)/[Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama) and [Odawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odawara)

## Education

Ōiso has one public elementary schools and one public middle schools operated by the town government. The town has one public high school operated by the Kanagawa Prefectural Board of Education. There is also one private elementary school, middle school and high school. The private [Seisa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seisa_University) has a campus at Ōiso.
