**Jamieson Lee Greer** (born August 16, 1980) is an American trade attorney and former [Air Force](32090.md) officer who has served as the [United States trade representative](211349.md) since February 2025. Greer has additionally served as the acting [special counsel of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Special_Counsel) since March 2025. He served as the acting director of the [United States Office of Government Ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Government_Ethics) from March to August 2025.

Greer attended [Brigham Young University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University), where he was a [Mormon missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_missionary). After graduating with a bachelor's degree in international studies, he obtained a joint master's degree in global business law from [Sciences Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po) and [Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_1_Panth%C3%A9on-Sorbonne_University), as well as a [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) from the [University of Virginia School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia_School_of_Law). Greer served in the [United States Air Force Judge Advocate General's Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Judge_Advocate_General's_Corps) and was the chief of military justice at [Joint Base Balad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balad_Air_Base) in Iraq. After Greer's military service, [Robert Lighthizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lighthizer) hired him at [Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skadden,_Arps,_Slate,_Meagher_&_Flom) in 2012. Greer worked in trade law for the firm and later for [Kirkland & Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkland_&_Ellis).

In June 2017, Lighthizer, the United States trade representative, hired Greer as his chief of staff. Greer worked with Lighthizer on several trade policies within the [Trump administration](52231341.md), including initiating [a trade war](57055272.md) with China and signing the [United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement](58620732.md). He represented the United States on Lighthizer's behalf in various events. Greer resigned from his position in May 2020 to work in private practice, becoming a partner at [King & Spalding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_&_Spalding). In November 2024, President-elect [Donald Trump](4848272.md) named Greer as his nominee for United States trade representative. Greer appeared before the [Senate Committee on Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Finance) in February 2025, and he was confirmed by the [Senate](24909346.md) that month. Greer has been described as an "architect" of Trump's [tariff policy](78448911.md) by *[The New York Times](30680.md)*.

## Early life and education (1980–2007)

Jamieson Lee Greer was born on August 16, 1980.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Greer was raised in [Paradise, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise,_California), the fourth of five children.[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] Greer attended Paradise Intermediate School and [Paradise High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_High_School), graduating in 1998.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-ChicoRole-5] In the summers and during the school year, Greer worked as a dishwasher and at [McDonald's](2480627.md).[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] He is a practicing [Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons).[^cite_note-IrishEuropean-6]

After high school, Greer attended [Brigham Young University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University).[^cite_note-ChicoRole-5] He was a [Mormon missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_missionary) in Belgium, France, and Luxembourg for two years, visiting [Kosovo](17391.md) and [Rwanda](25645.md). Through his missionary service, he learned to speak fluent French.[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] In 2004, he married Marlo Marie Zirker, whom he had met at Brigham Young;[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] they have five children.[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] He graduated from Brigham Young with a bachelor's degree in international studies.[^cite_note-DeseretNominates-8] Greer later obtained a joint master's degree in global business law from [Sciences Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po) and [Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_1_Panth%C3%A9on-Sorbonne_University) in 2006, and a [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) from the [University of Virginia School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia_School_of_Law).[^cite_note-IrishEuropean-6][^cite_note-DeseretNominates-8] He was an intern for a Czech judge at the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) for several months in 2007.[^cite_note-IrishEuropean-6]

## Military service (2003–2012)

While attending Brigham Young University, Greer joined the military. He led a training at [Ellsworth Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellsworth_Air_Force_Base) in 2003.[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] Greer served in the [United States Air Force Judge Advocate General's Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Judge_Advocate_General's_Corps) and was stationed in [Kansas](16716.md), Turkey, and Iraq, including [Joint Base Balad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balad_Air_Base).[^cite_note-DeseretNominates-8][^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] In Iraq, he was the chief of military justice.[^cite_note-ChicoRole-5]

## Career

### Legal work (2012–2017)

After Greer's military service ended in 2012, [Robert Lighthizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lighthizer) hired him at [Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skadden,_Arps,_Slate,_Meagher_&_Flom).[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] Greer worked in the firm's international trade group defending [U.S. Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Steel) and other companies in trade cases.[^cite_note-PoliticoSteel-9][^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] He later practiced in the international trade and national security group at [Kirkland & Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkland_&_Ellis).[^cite_note-PoliticoSteel-9]

### Chief of staff to the United States trade representative (2017–2020)

In June 2017, Lighthizer, the [United States trade representative](211349.md), named Greer as his chief of staff.[^cite_note-PoliticoSteel-9] Greer additionally served as the Office of the United States Trade Representative's chief operating officer. Lighthizer dispatched Greer to the [G20](2206608.md) summits on steel excess capacity in 2017 and 2018, as well as meetings with the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), South Korea, and Mexico.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] Greer worked with Lighthizer to initiate [a trade war](57055272.md) with China and to end the [North American Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Free_Trade_Agreement), establishing the [United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement](58620732.md).[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3]

### Post-government work (2020–2024)

In March 2020, *[Politico](9172097.md)* reported that Greer had been interviewing for jobs in private practice.[^cite_note-12] In May, he resigned as Lighthizer's chief of staff to become a partner at [King & Spalding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_&_Spalding).[^cite_note-13] Greer worked for the steel manufacturer [Cleveland-Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland-Cliffs), agricultural firms [Simplot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplot) and the National Milk Producers Federation, the oil and gas company [Talos Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talos_Energy), and several chemical producers, including [BASF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASF).[^cite_note-NYTimesReorder-14]

## United States Trade Representative (2025–present)

### Nomination and confirmation



Greer is ceremonially sworn in by vice president [JD Vance](53396477.md) in April 2025.

In October 2024, *Politico* reported that [Robert Lighthizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lighthizer) was expected to have an important role in selecting [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s staff if he won the [2024 presidential election](59568546.md).[^cite_note-15] He was involved in trade and economic personnel and policy planning for Trump's [second presidential transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_presidential_transition_of_Donald_Trump).[^cite_note-16] After Trump's victory, Greer was among several candidates considered to serve as the United States trade representative.[^cite_note-17] Greer was endorsed for [United States trade representative](211349.md) by Lighthizer, who did not want to serve in the position again. [Jared Kushner](6191053.md), Trump's son-in-law, supported the decision.[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] On November 26, Trump named Greer as his nominee to serve as the United States trade representative.[^cite_note-18]

The decision came as Trump stated that his nominee for [secretary of commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Commerce), [Howard Lutnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Lutnick), would have "direct responsibility" over his [tariff](78448911.md) and [trade policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_policy_of_the_second_Trump_administration#Trade_and_tariffs).[^cite_note-19] Lutnick and Greer worked together to set Trump's trade and tariff agenda within [his first one hundred days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_100_days_of_the_second_Trump_presidency).[^cite_note-20] The unlikely alliance between Lutnick and Greer occurred amid the [Trump administration](78298108.md)'s initial divisions between protectionists—including Greer—and cautious businessmen, including his nominee for secretary of the treasury, [Scott Bessent](38532044.md).[^cite_note-21] Prior to his swearing in, Trump relied on Lutnick and Greer to renegotiate the United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement and tasked them with imposing global reciprocal tariffs.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

Greer appeared before the [Senate Committee on Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Finance) on February 6, 2025. He told the committee that the United States "should be a country of producers" and that he would restructure its approach to international trade.[^cite_note-NYTimesReorder-14] On February 12, the Senate Committee on Finance voted to advance Greer's nomination in a 15–12 vote largely along party lines. [Rhode Island](25410.md) senator [Sheldon Whitehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Whitehouse) was the only [Democrat](5043544.md) to vote in favor of the nomination.[^cite_note-24] The ascension of [Peter Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Navarro) in determining Trump's trade policy provided urgency to confirm Greer.[^cite_note-25] The [United States Senate](24909346.md) confirmed his nomination in a 56–43 vote on February 26.[^cite_note-26] He became the trade representative that day.[^cite_note-27]

### Tenure

Greer was an "architect" of the second Trump administration's trade policies, according to *[The New York Times](30680.md)*. Greer has supported Trump's tariff policy, seeking to implement it legally and negotiating trade agreements.[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3] was involved in discussions to impose the [Liberation Day tariffs](79617151.md).[^cite_note-28] After the tariffs were imposed in April 2025, Trump sent Greer to negotiate lower tariff rates with Japan.[^cite_note-29] That month, he appeared before the Senate Committee on Finance. He faced questioning from [Republicans](32070.md) over Trump's tariff policy, particularly the Liberation Day tariffs.[^cite_note-30] As he was testifying, Trump paused his tariffs, surprising Greer. According to *The New York Times*, Trump established a strong relationship with Greer and tasked him to negotiate other agreements, including investment and security deals with Saudi Arabia, Cambodia, and Thailand.[^cite_note-NYTimesArchitect-3]

### Acting positions

On March 26, 2025, Greer was appointed as the acting director of the [Office of Government Ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Government_Ethics) and as the acting [special counsel of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Special_Counsel).[^cite_note-31] Charles Baldis, a former Senate staffer, managed the Office of Special Counsel on Greer's behalf.[^cite_note-32] In August, Trump named [Eric Ueland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Ueland) to succeed Greer as the acting director of the Office of Government Ethics.[^cite_note-33]

## Views

In August 2017, Greer told the *[Chico Enterprise-Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_Enterprise-Record)* that he agreed with President [Donald Trump's trade policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_policy_of_the_first_Trump_administration#Trade_and_tariffs).[^cite_note-ChicoRole-5] He has accused countries, including China, of implementing unfair trade policies against the United States.[^cite_note-34] He has described [trade deficits](4810.md) as a major issue.[^cite_note-35] Greer has derisively described [allied shoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendshoring) as a lighter implementation of globalization.[^cite_note-36] He opposes [digital service taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_of_digital_goods).[^cite_note-37]

Greer supports implementing stricter economic policies towards China, including the aggressive enforcement of the trade deal that resulted from the [China–United States trade war](57055272.md) and using export controls and sanctions against China.[^cite_note-38] He supports trade deals with countries such as the United Kingdom, Kenya, the Philippines, and India to counter China, as well as restoring the U.S. manufacturing base.[^cite_note-39] Greer praised the Phase One trade deal signed between Trump and Chinese vice premier [Liu He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_He_\(politician\)) in January 2020.[^cite_note-40] He has called for broadening tariffs on Chinese products to encompass other countries that manufacture products from Chinese companies.[^cite_note-41]

On August 14, 2026, Greer criticized Canada for retaliating against the [tariffs the United States imposed on goods from Canada in 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_trade_war_with_Canada). He pointed out that the U.S. will not accept any form of retaliation from Canada or any other country. He further stated that if a country retaliates against the U.S., the U.S. will "take action".[^cite_note-42]
