# Pilgrimage

For other uses, see [Pilgrimage (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrimage_\(disambiguation\)) and [Pilgrim (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim_\(disambiguation\)).



*Pilgrim* by [Gheorghe Tattarescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Tattarescu)

A **pilgrimage** is a [journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel) to a holy place, which can lead to a personal transformation, after which the pilgrim returns to their daily life.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] A **pilgrim** (from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *peregrinus*) is a traveler (literally, one who has come from afar) who is on a journey to a holy place. Typically, this is a physical journey (often on foot) to some place of special significance to the adherent of a particular religious belief system.

## Background

Further information: [Religious tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_tourism)



Five Members of the Utrecht Brotherhood of Jerusalem Pilgrims

Pilgrimages frequently involve a journey or search of [moral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality) or [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality) significance. Typically, it is a journey to a [shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine) or other location of importance to a person's [beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief) and [faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith), although sometimes it can be a metaphorical journey into someone's own beliefs.

Many religions attach spiritual importance to particular places: the place of birth or death of founders or saints, or to the place of their "calling" or spiritual awakening, or of their connection (visual or verbal) with the divine, to locations where miracles were performed or witnessed, or locations where a deity is said to live or be "housed", or any site that is seen to have special spiritual powers. Such sites may be commemorated with shrines or temples that devotees are encouraged to visit for their own spiritual benefit: to be healed or have questions answered or to achieve some other spiritual benefit.

A person who makes such a journey is called a pilgrim. As a common human experience, pilgrimage has been proposed as a [Jungian archetype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungian_archetype) by [Wallace Clift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Clift) and [Jean Dalby Clift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Dalby_Clift).[^cite_note-4] Some research has shown that people who engage in pilgrimage walks enjoy biological, psychological, social, and spiritual therapeutic benefits.[^cite_note-5]

People embark upon pilgrimages for a wide variety of personal reasons rather than any single motive. Ian Reader notes that these reasons are both spiritual and pragmatic. For example, while some pilgrims may seek spiritual transformation or enlightenment, others may be motivated by the desire for some sort of benefit in their own lives or on behalf of deceased kin. Such desires and motivations may include those to do with welfare, healing, prosperity, or purification.[^cite_note-6]

The [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land) acts as a focal point for the pilgrimages of the [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religion) of [Judaism](15624.md), [Christianity](5211.md), and [Islam](6037917.md). According to a [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) study in 2011, these [pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim) visit the Holy Land to touch and see physical manifestations of their [faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith), confirm their beliefs in the holy context with collective excitation, and connect personally to the Holy Land.[^cite_note-7]

## History



Ancient Greek *lekythos* showing a pilgrim from 450–425 BC

Although the origins of the word 'pilgrimage' are associated with Christianity, the practise of pilgrimage predates it.[^cite_note-8] Pilgrims and the making of [pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrimages) are common in many [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), including the faiths of [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) in the [Mithraic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithraism), [India](14533.md), [China](5405.md), and [Japan](15573.md). The [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) customs of consulting the [gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deity) at local [oracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle), such as those at [Dodona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodona) or [Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi), both in [Greece](12108.md), are widely known. In [Greece](12108.md), pilgrimages could either be personal or state-sponsored.[^cite_note-9] The [Eleusinian mysteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleusinian_Mysteries) included a pilgrimage. The procession to [Eleusis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleusis) began at the [Athenian](1216.md) cemetery [Kerameikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerameikos) and from there the participants walked to Eleusis, along the [Sacred Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Way) ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἱερὰ Ὁδός, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek): *Hierá Hodós*).[^cite_note-10]

In the early period of [Hebrew history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ancient_Israel_and_Judah), pilgrims traveled to [Shiloh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiloh_\(Biblical_city\)), [Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_\(biblical_city\)), [Bethel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethel), and eventually [Jerusalem](16043.md) (see also [Three Pilgrimage Festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pilgrimage_Festivals), a practice followed by other [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religion)). These festivals, including Passover, Tabernacles, and [Shavuot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shavuot), often involved journeys that reflected a physical and spiritual movement, similar to the concept of "[tirtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirtha_\(Hinduism\)) yātrā" in Hinduism, where "tirtha" means "ford" or "crossing", and "yatra" signifies a journey or procession.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] While many pilgrims travel toward a specific location, a physical destination is not always a necessity. One group of pilgrims in early [Celtic Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Christianity) were the *Peregrinari Pro Christ*, (Pilgrims for Christ), or "white martyrs", who left their homes to wander in the world.[^cite_note-13] This form of pilgrimage, akin to the concept of "[hajj](23487999.md)" in Islam, which means "procession", was an [ascetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asceticism) religious practice, as the pilgrim left the security of home and the [clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan) for an unknown destination, trusting completely in [Divine Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Providence).[^cite_note-14] These travels often resulted in the founding of new [abbeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey) and the spread of Christianity among the pagan population in [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Roman_Britain) and in continental Europe.

In the Middle Ages, Christian pilgrimage became a relevant economic sector, initially through facilities at popular pilgrimage destinations, but later also through organised group trips for pilgrims throughout the Mediterranean region.[^cite_note-15]

The ceremonial center [Chavín de Huántar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn_de_Hu%C3%A1ntar) served as a gathering place for people of the pre-[Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca) culture [Chavín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn_culture) to come together, to attend and participate in rituals, consult an oracle, worship or enter a cult, and collect ideas.[^cite_note-16]

Pilgrimage experienced a new change with the improvement of infrastructure from the 19th century onwards.[^cite_note-17]

## Bahá'í Faith

Main article: [Bahá'í pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1'%C3%AD_pilgrimage)

[Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1'u'll%C3%A1h) decreed pilgrimage to two places in the [Kitáb-i-Aqdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit%C3%A1b-i-Aqdas): the [House of Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bah%C3%A1'u'll%C3%A1h,_Baghdad) in [Baghdad, Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad,_Iraq), and the House of the [Báb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A1b) in [Shiraz, Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz,_Iran). Later, [ʻAbdu'l-Bahá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BBAbdu'l-Bah%C3%A1) designated the [Shrine of Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine_of_Bah%C3%A1'u'll%C3%A1h) at Bahji, Israel as a site of pilgrimage.[^cite_note-oneworld-18] The designated sites for pilgrimage are currently not accessible to the majority of Bahá'ís, as they are in Iraq and Iran respectively, and thus when Bahá'ís currently refer to pilgrimage, it refers to a nine-day pilgrimage which consists of visiting the holy places at the [Bahá'í World Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1'%C3%AD_World_Centre) in northwest Israel in [Haifa](55606.md), [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel), and [Bahjí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansion_of_Bahj%C3%AD).[^cite_note-oneworld-18]

## Buddhism

Main article: [Buddhist pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_pilgrimage)



Tibetan pilgrim, [Rewalsar Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewalsar_Lake), [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), India

Places of pilgrimage in the [Buddhist](3267529.md) world include those associated with the life of the historical [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha): his supposed birthplace and childhood home ([Lumbini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumbini) and [Kapilavastu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapilavastu_\(ancient_city\)) in [Nepal](171166.md)) and place of enlightenment ([Bodh Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodh_Gaya) in northern [India](14533.md)), other places he is believed to have visited and the place of his death (or Parinirvana), [Kushinagar](33686220.md), India. Others include the many temples and monasteries with relics of the Buddha or Buddhist saints such as the [Temple of the Tooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_the_Tooth) in [Sri Lanka](26750.md) and the numerous sites associated with teachers and patriarchs of the various traditions.

In [India](14533.md) and [Nepal](171166.md), there are four places of pilgrimage which are tied to the life of [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha):

- [Lumbini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumbini): Buddha's birthplace (in Nepal)

- [Bodh Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodh_Gaya): place of [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_\(Buddhism\)) (in the current [Mahabodhi Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabodhi_Temple), Bihar, India)

- [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath): (formally Isipathana, Uttar pradesh, [India](14533.md)) where he delivered his first sermon ([Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta)), and the Buddha taught about the [Middle Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Way), the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths) and [Noble Eightfold Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Eightfold_Path)

- [Kusinara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusinara): (now [Kusinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusinagar), [India](14533.md)) where he attained *[mahaparinirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahaparinirvana)* (died)

Other pilgrimage places in India and Nepal connected Gautama Buddha's life are: [Savatthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savatthi), [Pataliputta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patna), [Nalanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda), [Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaya,_India), [Vesali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesali), [Sankasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankasia), [Kapilavastu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapilavastu_\(ancient_city\)), [Kosambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosambi,_India), [Rajagaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajagaha).



[Tibetans](31516.md) on a pilgrimage to [Lhasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa), doing full-body [prostrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostration_\(Buddhism\)), often for the entire length of the journey

## Christianity

Main article: [Christian pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage)



[Church of the Holy Sepulchre](7810.md) in [Jerusalem](16043.md) according to tradition is the site where [Jesus](1095706.md) was [crucified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus) and [resurrected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus)



The [Sanctuary of Our Lady of Fátima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_of_F%C3%A1tima) is one of the largest pilgrimage sites ([Marian shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrines_to_the_Virgin_Mary)) in the world.



Modern Orthodox pilgrim in [Kyiv Pechersk Lavra](478837.md), Ukraine

In the spiritual literature of [Christianity](5211.md), the concept of pilgrim and pilgrimage may refer to the experience of life in [the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_\(theology\)) (considered as a period of exile) or to the inner path of the spiritual aspirant from a state of wretchedness to a state of beatitude.[^cite_note-19]

Christian pilgrimage was first made to sites connected with the birth, life, crucifixion and resurrection of [Jesus](1095706.md). Aside from the early example of [Origen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origen) in the third century, surviving descriptions of Christian pilgrimages to the [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land) date from the 4th century, when pilgrimage was encouraged by church fathers including [Saint Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome), and established by [Saint Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_\(empress\)), the mother of [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I_and_Christianity).[^cite_note-20]

Beginning in 1894, Christian ministers under the direction of [Charles Taze Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Taze_Russell) were appointed to travel to and work with local [Bible Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Students_movement#International_Bible_Students_Association) congregations for a few days at a time; within a few years appointments were extended internationally, formally designated as "pilgrims", and scheduled for twice-yearly, week-long visits at each local congregation.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] [International Bible Students Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Students_movement#International_Bible_Students_Association) (IBSA) pilgrims were excellent speakers, and their local talks were typically well-publicized and well-attended.[^cite_note-23] Prominent Bible Students [A. H. Macmillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._H._Macmillan) and [J. F. Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._F._Rutherford) were both appointed pilgrims before they joined the board of directors of the [Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watch_Tower_Bible_and_Tract_Society_of_Pennsylvania); the IBSA later adopted the name [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) and renamed pilgrims as *[traveling overseers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_overseer)*.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

&#x20;The purpose of Christian pilgrimage was summarized by [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) in this way:

> To go on pilgrimage is not simply to visit a place to admire its treasures of nature, art or history. To go on pilgrimage really means to step out of ourselves in order to encounter God where he has revealed himself, where his grace has shone with particular splendour and produced rich fruits of conversion and holiness among those who believe. Above all, Christians go on pilgrimage to the Holy Land, to the places associated with the Lord's passion, death and resurrection. They go to Rome, the city of the martyrdom of Peter and Paul, and also to Compostela, which, associated with the memory of Saint James, has welcomed pilgrims from throughout the world who desire to strengthen their spirit with the Apostle's witness of faith and love.[^cite_note-26]

Pilgrimages were, and are, also made to [Rome](25458.md) and other sites associated with the [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_apostles), [saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint) and [Christian martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_martyrs), as well as to places where there have been [apparitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_apparitions) of the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary). A popular pilgrimage journey is along the [Way of St. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Way_of_St._James) to the [Santiago de Compostela Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela_Cathedral), in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)), Spain, where the [shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine) of the apostle [James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee) is located. A combined pilgrimage was held every seven years in the three nearby towns of [Maastricht](20125.md), [Aachen](1520.md) and [Kornelimünster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kornelim%C3%BCnster_Abbey) where many important relics could be seen (see: [Pilgrimage of the Relics, Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrimage_of_the_Relics,_Maastricht)). Marian pilgrimages remain very popular in [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage#Latin_America).

The Catholic priest Frank Fahey writes that a pilgrim is "always in danger of becoming a tourist" and vice versa, and describes pilgrimages as journeys containing "faith expectancy", a search for wholeness, that are often solitary and employing silence to create an [internal sacred space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation).[^cite_note-Fahey_2002-27]

## Islam

Main articles: [Hajj](23487999.md) and [Umrah](1591421.md)

See also: [Holiest sites in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiest_sites_in_Islam), [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca), [Medina](36636.md), and [Jerusalem](16043.md)



[Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) pilgrims [circumambulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawaf) the black cube of the *[Kaaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaaba)* in the [Al-Haram Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Haram_Mosque)

The *[Ḥajj](23487999.md)* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حَـجّ, main pilgrimage to Mecca) is one of the [five pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_pillars_of_Islam) and a [mandatory religious duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fard) for [Muslims](19541.md) that must be carried out at least once in their lifetime by all adult Muslims who are physically and financially capable of undertaking the journey, and can support their family during their absence.[^cite_note-Marshall86-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The Hajj is one of the largest annual gatherings of people in the world.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-time_2013-38] Since 2014, two or three million people have participated in the *Hajj* annually.[^cite_note-largest-39] The mosques in Mecca and Medina were closed in February 2020 because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Saudi_Arabia) and the *hajj* was permitted for only a very limited number of Saudi nationals and foreigners living in Saudi Arabia starting on 29 July.[^cite_note-40]

Another important place for Muslims is the city of Medina, the second holiest site in Islam, in Saudi Arabia, the final resting place of Muhammad in [Al-Masjid an-Nabawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Masjid_an-Nabawi) (The Mosque of the Prophet).[^cite_note-Syed-41]

The *[Ihram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihram)* (white robe of pilgrimage) is meant to show equality of all Muslim pilgrims in the eyes of Allah. 'A white has no superiority over a black, nor a black over a white. Nor does an Arab have superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab - except through piety' - statement of the Prophet Muhammad.

### Ziyarat

Main article: [Ziyarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyarat)

A different form of pilgrimage is [ziyarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyarat) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): زِيَارَة *ziyārah*, "visit"; [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): زیارت, *ziyārat*). Ziyarat generally refers to the act of visiting holy places such as tombs or shrines, often associated with the Prophet Muhammad, his family, companions, and other revered figures like legal scholars and Sufi saints. Ziyarat is a voluntary act of pilgrimage practiced by both Sunni and Shia Muslims.[^cite_note-ALSYED-42][^cite_note-EncyIslam-43] Unlike Hajj, which is obligatory for Muslims who are physically and financially able,[^cite_note-Britannica-44] or Umrah, which is highly recommended but not mandatory,[^cite_note-IslamicStudies-45] Ziyarat involves visits to a variety of sacred and historically significant locations beyond Mecca. These include mosques, tombs, battlefields, mountains, caves, and other places where important spiritual or historical events in Islamic history took place.[^cite_note-CambridgeCore-46] It holds deep spiritual significance for millions of Muslims around the world.[^cite_note-Springer-47]

One notable example is the [Grand Magal of Touba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Magal_of_Touba), 200 kilometres (120 mi) east of [Dakar](44251.md), Senegal. About four million pilgrims participate annually to celebrate the life and teachings of [Cheikh Amadou Bamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheikh_Amadou_Bamba?action=edit\&redlink=1), the founder of the [Mouride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouride) brotherhood, who established the order in 1883. The pilgrimage begins on the 18th of [Safar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safar), the second month of the Islamic calendar.[^cite_note-48]

While ziyarat is viewed as permissible and spiritually enriching by most Sunni and Shia traditions, some fundamentalist movements, such as Salafism and Wahhabism, discourage or oppose it. These movements are characterized by a strict, literalist interpretation of Islam and opposition to practices they consider innovations, such as shrine visitation.[^cite_note-EncyBritannica-49][^cite_note-EncyIslam-43]

Ziyarat also includes the Ziyarat al-Imam, which refers specifically to the pilgrimage to the shrines of the Shia Imams, especially revered figures like Imam Ali and [Imam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayn_ibn_Ali). The [Arba'in pilgrimage](48029038.md) is the world's largest pilgrimage and largest annual public gathering in the world, where millions of Shia Muslims travel to Karbala to commemorate the martyrdom of Imam Hussein during the 40-day mourning period after Ashura.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### Shia

Main articles: [Arba'in pilgrimage](48029038.md), [Arba'in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arba'in), [Ashura](488563.md), and [Imam Reza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Reza)



[Arba'een pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arba'een_Pilgrimage) in [Mehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehran,_Ilam)

[*Al-Arba‘īn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arba'in) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ٱلْأَرْبَـعِـيْـن, "The Forty"), *Chehelom* ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): چهلم, [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): چہلم, "the fortieth \[day]") or *Qirkhī*, *Imāmīn Qirkhī* ([Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language): *İmamın qırxı* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): إمامین قیرخی), "the fortieth of Imam") is a [Shia](26961.md) Muslim religious observance that occurs [forty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40_\(number\)) days after the [Day of Ashura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Ashura). It commemorates the [martyrdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrdom) of [Husayn ibn Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayn_ibn_Ali), the grandson of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad), which falls on the 20th or 21st day of the month of [Safar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safar). [Imam](15240.md) Husayn ibn Ali and 72 companions were killed by [Yazid I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazid_I)'s army in the [Battle of Karbala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Karbala) in 61 AH (680 [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era)). Arba'een or forty days is also the usual length of mourning after the death of a family member or loved one in many Muslim traditions. Arba'een is one of the largest pilgrimage gatherings on Earth, in which up to 31 million people go to the city of [Karbala](210957.md) in [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

The second largest holy city in the world, [Mashhad](406512.md), Iran, attracts more than 20 million tourists and pilgrims every year, many of whom come to pay homage to [Imam Reza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Reza) (the eighth [Shi'ite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'ite) Imam). It has been a magnet for travelers since medieval times.[^cite_note-Imam_Reza-56][^cite_note-largest-39]

## Judaism

See also: [Temple in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_in_Jerusalem), [Jerusalem in Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_in_Judaism), and [Three Pilgrimage Festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pilgrimage_Festivals)



[Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) at the [Western Wall](75225.md) in [Jerusalem](16043.md) during the [Ottoman](22278.md) period, 1867

While [Solomon's Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon's_Temple) stood, Jerusalem was the centre of the Jewish religious life and the site of the [Three Pilgrimage Festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pilgrimage_Festivals) of [Passover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover), [Shavuot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shavuot) and [Sukkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukkot), and all adult men who were able were required to visit and offer sacrifices (*[korbanot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korbanot)*) at the Temple. After the destruction of the Temple, the obligation to visit Jerusalem and to make sacrifices no longer applied. The obligation was restored with the [rebuilding of the Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple), but following its destruction in 70 CE, the obligation to make a pilgrimage to Jerusalem and offer sacrifices again went into abeyance.[^cite_note-57]

The western retaining wall of the [Temple Mount](65719.md), known as the [Western Wall](75225.md) or "Wailing" Wall, is the remaining part of [Second Jewish Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Jewish_Temple) in the [Old City of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Jerusalem\)) is the most sacred and visited site for Jews. Pilgrimage to this area was off-limits to Jews from 1948 to 1967, when East Jerusalem was under [Jordanian](7515964.md) control.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

There are numerous lesser Jewish pilgrimage destinations, mainly tombs of *[tzadikim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzadik)*, throughout [Israel](9282173.md) and [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) and all over the world, including: [Hebron](38577.md); [Bethlehem](4312.md); [Mount Meron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Meron); [Netivot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netivot); [Uman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uman), [Ukraine](31750.md); [Silistra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silistra), [Bulgaria](3415.md); [Damanhur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damanhur), [Egypt](8087628.md); and many others.[^cite_note-60]

Many rabbis claim that even today, after the destruction of the Temple, there is a mitzvah to make a pilgrimage on holidays.[^cite_note-61]

## Sikhism

Eventually, however, [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar) and [Harmandir Sahib (the Golden Temple)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmandir_Sahib) became the spiritual and cultural centre of the Sikh faith, and if a Sikh goes on pilgrimage it is usually to this place.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

The [Panj Takht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panj_Takht) (Punjabi: ਪੰਜ ਤਖ਼ਤ) are the five revered [gurdwaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwaras) in India that are considered the thrones or seats of authority of Sikhism and are traditionally considered a pilgrimage.[^cite_note-66]

## Zoroastrianism



The [Yazd Atash Behram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazd_Atash_Behram) in Iran is an [Atash Bahram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atash_Bahram), the highest grade of [fire temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_temple) in Zoroastrianism

[Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism) have as their main pilgrimage destinations the city of [Yazd](451385.md) and the temples of [Pir-e Sabz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chak_Chak,_Yazd) and [Pir-e Naraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir-e_Naraki?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Iran](14653.md), as well as the cities of [Navsari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navsari) and [Udvada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udvada) in India.

In [Iran](14653.md), there are pilgrimage destinations called *[pirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_\(Zoroastrianism\))* in several provinces, although the most familiar ones are in the province of [Yazd](451385.md).[^cite_note-69] In addition to the traditional Yazdi shrines, new sites may be in the process of becoming pilgrimage destinations. The ruins are the ruins of ancient [fire temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_temples). One such site is the ruin of the [Sassanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanian) era [Azargoshnasp fire temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azargoshnasp_fire_temple) in Iran's Azarbaijan Province. Other sites are the ruins of [fire temples at Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_fire_temple), south of the capital [Tehran](57654.md), and the Firouzabad ruins sixty kilometres south of [Shiraz](39622.md) in the province of [Pars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C4%81rs_Province).

[Atash Behram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atash_Behram) ("Fire of victory") is the highest grade of [fire temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_temple) in [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism). It has 16 different "kinds of fire", that is, fires gathered from 16 different sources.[^cite_note-70] Currently there are 9 Atash Behram, one in Yazd, Iran and the rest in Western [India](14533.md). They have become a pilgrimage destination.[^cite_note-AtashBehram-71]

In India the cathedral fire temple that houses the [Iranshah Atash Behram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udvada_Atash_Behram), located in the small town of [Udvada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udvada) in the west coast province of [Gujarat](53707.md), is a pilgrimage destination.[^cite_note-AtashBehram-71]

## Cultural pilgrimage



Japanese Prime Minister [Junichiro Koizumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junichiro_Koizumi), like many fans of [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley), visited [Graceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graceland).

A modern phenomenon is the cultural pilgrimage which, while involving a personal journey, is secular in nature. Destinations for such pilgrims can include historic sites of national or cultural importance, and can be defined as places "of cultural significance: an artist's home, the location of a pivotal event or an iconic destination".[^cite_note-star-72] An example might be a devotee of [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles) visiting [Liverpool](18081.md) in England or [anime fans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otaku) visiting [Akihabara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akihabara) in Tokyo.[^cite_note-73] Destinations for cultural pilgrims include [Auschwitz concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp), [Gettysburg Battlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Battlefield) or the [Ernest Hemingway House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway_House).[^cite_note-star-72] Cultural pilgrims may also travel on religious pilgrimage routes, such as the [Way of St. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Way_of_St._James), with the perspective of making it a historic or architectural tour rather than – or as well as – a religious experience.[^cite_note-74]

Under communist regimes, devout secular pilgrims visited locations such as the [Mausoleum of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Lenin), the [Mausoleum of Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Mao_Zedong) and the [Birthplace of Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx_House). Such visits were sometimes state-sponsored. Sites such as these continue to attract visitors. The distinction between religious, cultural or political pilgrimage and tourism is not necessarily always clear or rigid. Pilgrimage could also refer symbolically to journeys, largely on foot, to places where the concerned person(s) expect(s) to find spiritual and/or personal salvation.[^cite_note-75]

## In culture

Some prominent literary characters who were pilgrims include:

- [Chaucer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer) *[The Canterbury Tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canterbury_Tales)* recounts tales told by Christian pilgrims on their way to [Canterbury Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral) and the shrine of [Thomas Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Becket).

- In the epic poem [Divine Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Comedy), [Dante Alighieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri) portrays himself as a pilgrim traveling through the afterlife realms of Hell, Purgatory, and Paradise.

- [John Bunyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bunyan) depicted multiple pilgrims (e.g., Christian – the protagonist, Faithful, Talkative, Christiana, Mercy, Old Honest, Mr. Fearing, Mr. Feeble-Mind, Mr. Ready-to-Halt, and Mr. Valiant) as well as false pilgrims (e.g., Formalist, Hypocrisy, and Mr. By-Ends) in his Christian allegory, *[The Pilgrim's Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pilgrim's_Progress)* (1678)

- Wilfred of Ivanhoe, a [palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmer_\(Pilgrim\)) (medieval Christian from Europe who makes a pilgrimage to Jerusalem) and the titular character of [Sir Walter Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Walter_Scott)'s book *[Ivanhoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanhoe)*

- A palmer plays a significant role representing Reason in Book II of [Edmund Spenser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Spenser)'s epic poem *[The Faerie Queene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Faerie_Queene)*
