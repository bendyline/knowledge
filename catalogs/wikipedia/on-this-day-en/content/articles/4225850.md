**Lualaba Province** (*Mkoa wa Lualaba*, in [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language)) is a [province of the Democratic Republic of the Congo](3984115.md) created in the [2015 repartitioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_the_DR_Congo#New_provinces) when the former [Katanga province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province) was split up into four new provinces—Lualaba, [Haut-Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-Katanga), [Haut-Lomami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-Lomami), and [Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_Province).[^cite_note-2] Lualaba was formed from the [Lualaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lualaba_District) and [Kolwezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolwezi_District) districts. Kolwezi was a hybrid city/district which was separated from its two territories and the city proper became the capital of the new province. The 2020 population was estimated to be 3,183,300.[^cite_note-3]

Map of Lualaba showing tree-cover loss years, with forest remaining since 2000 in green and loss years shaded yellow through purple.

Tree-cover loss year in Lualaba, 2001-2024, from the [Global Forest Change dataset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Forest_Change_dataset).

Along with [Haut-Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-Katanga_Province), Lualaba is in the [Copperbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copperbelt) of Central Africa. The [Congo](76762.md) is only behind [Chile](5489.md), [Peru](170691.md), and [China](5405.md) in the largest amount of copper produced globally.

## History

Lualaba Province was separated from Katanga Province on 30 June 1963. Then, on 24 April 1966, it was united with [Katanga Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Oriental) to form Sud-Katanga Province, which was later merged back into Katanga. The President of Lualaba, from 1965 the governor, was [Dominique Diur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Diur) who held office from 23 September 1963 until 24 April 1966.
