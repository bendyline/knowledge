The **Twentieth Dynasty of Egypt** (notated **Dynasty XX**, alternatively **20th Dynasty** or **Dynasty 20**) is the third and last dynasty of the [Ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) [New Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt) period, lasting from 1189 BC to 1077 BC. The 19th and 20th Dynasties together constitute an era known as the **Ramesside period** owing to the predominance of rulers with the given name "Ramesses". This dynasty is generally considered to mark the beginning of the decline of Ancient Egypt, coinciding with the [Bronze Age Collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age_Collapse) which led to the end of the New Kingdom and the onset of the [Third Intermediate Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Intermediate_Period_of_Egypt). During the period of the Twentieth Dynasty, Ancient Egypt faced the crisis of invasions by the [Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples). Though the dynasty was able to successfully defend Egypt, it left the kingdom seriously crippled.

## History

After the death of the last pharaoh of the [19th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Dynasty), Queen [Twosret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twosret), Egypt entered into a [period of civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_19th_Dynasty). Because of lost historical records, the cause of the civil war is unknown. The war was ended with the accession to the throne by [Setnakhte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setnakhte), who founded the 20th Dynasty of Egypt.

From the reign of [Setnakhte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setnakhte) and his son [Ramesses III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_III), Egypt faced the crisis caused by the invading of the [Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples). These invasions formed part of a series of linked crises in numerous Mediterranean civilizations. Together, these crises are often referred to as the [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse).&#x20;

The [Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples) caused considerable damage to the people of Egypt, visible in the historical record. One inscription reads:

> "All at once the lands were removed and scattered in the fray. No land could resist their arms, from Hatti, Kode, [Carchemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carchemish), [Arzawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arzawa), and [Alashiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alashiya) on – being cut off at one time. A camp was set up in [Amurru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amurru_kingdom). They desolated its people and its land was like that which had never existed. They were coming forward toward Egypt, while the flame was prepared for them."

— Inscription from the second pylon of the temple of [Ramesses III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_III) at [Medinet Habu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medinet_Habu)[^cite_note-1]

Not only Egypt was affected by the Sea People invasions. The empire of the [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites), a long-standing rival to Egypt, collapsed, never to rise again. (In the inscription quoted above, the Hittites are called "Hatti".)

With the victory in the [Battle of Djahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Djahy) and the [Battle of the Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Delta) during Year 8 of Ramesses III's reign, Egypt successfully repelled the invading [Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples), protecting Egypt from ruin like other Bronze Age civilizations. During the Twentieth Dynasty, many of the temples were built to display the power of Egypt. However, they also indicate the political ascendancy of the priesthood over the pharaoh.

The Twentieth Dynasty declined because of drastic climate change, infighting in the royal family, and growing power of the priesthood and nobility. Following the death of [Ramesses XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_XI), the last pharaoh of the Twentieth Dynasty, a period of chaos ensued. This was ended by [Smendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smendes), a member of the Egyptian nobility, who became the first Pharaoh of the [21st Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-first_Dynasty_of_Egypt).

### Background

Main article: [End of the 19th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_19th_Dynasty)

Upon the death of the last pharaoh of the 19th Dynasty, Queen [Twosret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twosret), Egypt descended into a period of civil war, as attested by the Elephantine [stela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stela) built by [Setnakhte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setnakhte). The circumstances of Twosret's demise are uncertain, as she may have died peacefully during her reign or been overthrown by Setnakhte, who was likely already middle aged at the time.[^cite_note-2]

### 20th Dynasty

A consistent theme of this dynasty was the loss of pharaonic power to the [High Priests of Amun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Priests_of_Amun). [Horemheb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horemheb), a pharaoh of the [18th Dynasty](28830228.md), had restored the traditional [Ancient Egyptian religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_religion) and the priesthood of [Amun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amun) after their abandonment by [Akhenaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhenaten). With the High Priests now acting as intermediaries between the gods and the people, rather than the pharaoh, the position of pharaoh no longer commanded the same kind of power as it had in the past.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

#### Setnakhte

Main article: [Setnakhte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setnakhte)

Setnakhte stabilized the situation in Egypt, and may have driven off an attempted invasion by the [Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples). He ruled for about 3-4 years before being succeeded by his son Ramesses III.

#### Ramesses III

Main article: [Ramesses III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_III)

In Year 5 of his reign, Ramesses defeated a Libyan invasion of Egypt by the [Libu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libu), [Meshwesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshwesh) and [Seped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seped?action=edit\&redlink=1) people through [Marmarica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmarica), who had previously unsuccessfully invaded during the reign of [Merneptah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merneptah).[^cite_note-4]

Ramesses III is most famous for decisively defeating a confederacy of the Sea Peoples, including the [Denyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denyen), [Tjekker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tjeker), [Peleset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philistines), [Shardana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherden) and [Weshesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weshesh) in the [Battle of Djahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Djahy) and the [Battle of the Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Delta) during Year 8 of his reign. Within the [Papyrus Harris I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus_Harris_I), which attests these events in detail, Ramesses is said to have settled the defeated Sea Peoples in "strongholds", most likely located in [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan), as his subjects.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-5]

In Year 11 of Ramesses' reign, another coalition of Libyan invaders was defeated in Egypt.

Between regnal Year 12 and Year 29, a systematic program of reorganization of the varied cults of the [Ancient Egyptian religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_religion) was undertaken, by creating and funding new cults and restoring temples.

In Year 29 of Ramesses' reign, the first recorded labor [strike](155134.md) in human history took place, after food rations for the favored and elite royal tomb builders and artisans in the village of Set Maat (now known as [Deir el-Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_el-Medina)), could not be provisioned.[^cite_note-6]

The reign of Ramesses III is also known for a [harem conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harem_conspiracy) in which Queen [Tiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiye_\(20th_dynasty\)), one of his lesser wives, was implicated in an assassination attempt against the king, with the goal of putting her son [Pentawer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentawer) on the throne. The coup was unsuccessful. The king died from the attempt on his life; however, it was his legitimate heir and son [Ramesses IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_IV) who succeeded him to the throne, who thereafter arrested and put approximately 30 conspirators to death.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

#### Ramesses IV

Main article: [Ramesses IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_IV)

At the start of his reign Ramesses IV started an enormous building program on the scale of [Ramesses the Great's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_II) own projects. He doubled the number of work gangs at Set Maat to a total of 120 men and dispatched numerous expeditions to the stone quarries of [Wadi Hammamat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Hammamat) and the turquoise mines of the [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula). One of the largest expeditions included 8,368 men, of which some 2,000 were soldiers.[^cite_note-9] Ramesses expanded his father's [Temple of Khonsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Khonsu) at [Karnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnak) and possibly began his own mortuary temple at a site near the [Temple of Hatshepsut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortuary_Temple_of_Hatshepsut). Another smaller temple is associated with Ramesses north of [Medinet Habu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medinet_Habu_\(temple\)).

Ramesses IV saw issues with the provision of food rations to his workmen, similar to the situation under his father. [Ramessesnakht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramessesnakht), the High Priest of Amun at the time, began to accompany state officials as they went to pay the workmen their rations, suggesting that, at least in part, it was the Temple of Amun and not the Egyptian state that was responsible for their wages.

He also produced the Papyrus Harris I, the longest known [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus) from Ancient Egypt, measuring in at 41 meters long with 1,500 lines of text to celebrate the achievements of his father.

#### Ramesses V

Main article: [Ramesses V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_V)

Ramesses V reigned for no more than 4 years, dying of smallpox in 1143 BC. The Turin Papyrus Cat. 2044 attests that during his reign the workmen of Set Maat were forced to periodically stop working on Ramesses' [KV9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV9) tomb out of "fear of the enemy", suggesting increasing instability in Egypt and an inability to defend the country from what are presumed to be Libyan raiding parties.[^cite_note-10]

The [Wilbour Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbour_Papyrus) is thought to date from Ramesses V's reign. The document reveals that most of the land in Egypt by that point was controlled by the Temple of Amun, and that the Temple had complete control over Egypt's finances.[^cite_note-11]

#### Ramesses VI

Main article: [Ramesses VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_VI)

Ramesses VI is best known for his tomb which, when built, inadvertently buried the tomb of pharaoh [Tutankhamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutankhamun) underneath, keeping it safe from grave robbing until its [discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_the_tomb_of_Tutankhamun) by [Howard Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Carter) in 1922.

#### Ramesses VII

Main article: [Ramesses VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_VII)

Ramesses VII's only monument is his tomb, [KV1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV1).

#### Ramesses VIII

Main article: [Ramesses VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_VIII)

Almost nothing is known about Ramesses VIII's reign, which lasted for a single year. He is only attested at Medinet Habu and through a few plaques. The only monument from his reign is his modest tomb, which was used for [Mentuherkhepeshef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentuherkhepeshef_\(son_of_Ramesses_IX\)), son of Ramesses IX, rather than Ramesses VIII himself.

#### Ramesses IX

Main article: [Ramesses IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_IX)

During Year 16 and Year 17 of Ramesses IX's reign famous tomb robbery trials took place, as attested by the [Abbott Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_Papyrus). A careful examination by a [vizierial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizier_\(Ancient_Egypt\)) commission was undertaken of ten royal tombs, four tombs of the [Chantresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_\(Christianity\)) of the Estate of the [Divine Adoratrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Adoratrice_of_Amun), and finally the tombs of the citizens of [Thebes](65811.md). Many of these were found to have been broken into, like the tomb of Pharaoh [Sobekemsaf II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobekemsaf_II), whose [mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummy) had been stolen.[^cite_note-12]

Ramesses IX's [cartouche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartouche) has been found at [Gezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezer) in [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan), suggesting that Egypt at this time still had some degree of influence in the region.[^cite_note-13]

Most of the building projects during Ramesses IX's reign were at [Heliopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolis_\(ancient_Egypt\)).[^cite_note-14]

#### Ramesses X

Main article: [Ramesses X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_X)

Ramesses X's reign is poorly documented. The Necropolis Journal of Set Maat records the general idleness of the workmen at this time, due, at least in part, to the danger of Libyan raiders.[^cite_note-15]

#### Ramesses XI

Main article: [Ramesses XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_XI)

Ramesses XI was the last pharaoh of the 20th Dynasty. During his reign the position grew so weak that in the south the [High Priests of Amun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Priest_of_Amun) at [Thebes](65811.md) became the de facto rulers of [Upper Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Egypt), while [Smendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smendes) controlled [Lower Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Egypt) even before Ramesses XI's death. Smendes would eventually found the [Twenty-First dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-first_dynasty_of_Egypt) at [Tanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanis).[^cite_note-16]

### Decline

As happened under the earlier [Nineteenth Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_dynasty_of_Egypt), this dynasty struggled under the effects of the bickering between the heirs of Ramesses III. For instance, three different sons of Ramesses III are known to have assumed power as [Ramesses IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_IV), [Ramesses VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_VI) and [Ramesses VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_VIII) respectively. However, at this time Egypt was also increasingly beset by a series of droughts, below-normal flooding levels of the [Nile](21244.md), famine, civil unrest and official corruption – all of which would limit the managerial abilities of any king.

## Sea Peoples in Egypt

Main article: [Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples)

The late 13th century BC was a time of uncertainty and conflict for peoples and polities of the Aegean and Eastern Mediterranean due to the invasion by Sea Peoples, which was a contributing factor in the [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse).[^cite_note-Auto82-16-17][^cite_note-18] While there is not much information left to show us why the Sea Peoples began the large scale invasion, the written evidence shows the weakening of central administrations, erosion of political powers, and food shortage might be the reasons.[^cite_note-19]

From [Ramses III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_III)'s mortuary temple at [Medinet Habou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medinet_Habu) depicting a chaotic scene of boats and warriors entwined in battle in the Nile delta, it showing that Sea Peoples were seaborne foes from different origins.[^cite_note-20] They launched a combined land-sea invasion that destabilized the already weakened power base of empires and kingdoms of the old world, and attempted to enter or control the Egyptian territory.[^cite_note-Auto82-16-17]

While with the victory in the Battle of Djahy and the Battle of the Delta during Year 8 of Ramesses III's reign, Egypt successfully repelled the invading forces of Sea Peoples, the damage that caused the collapse of the Eastern Mediterranean world also damaged the trade routes of Egypt, as most of their trading partners had been destroyed by Sea Peoples.

  Ramesses IX was the grandson of Ramesses III, nephew of Ramesses IV and VI, and a son of Mentuherkhepeshef, who never became a pharaoh.
