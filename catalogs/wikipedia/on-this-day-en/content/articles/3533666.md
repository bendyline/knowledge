A ***nakamal*** is a traditional meeting place in [Vanuatu](32443.md). It is used for gatherings, ceremonies and the drinking of [kava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kava).

A nakamal is found in every significant Vanuatu community, but the design of the nakamal and the traditions surrounding it vary between areas.

## Etymology

The term *nakamal* is a [Bislama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislama) word, borrowed from certain [Oceanic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_languages) spoken in Vanuatu, such as [North Efate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Efate_language). In those languages, such a form can be parsed as *na kamal(i)*, combining the common [article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_\(grammar\)) *na* and a noun *kamal* or *kamali*. Ultimately, it descends from a [Proto-Oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Oceanic_language) and [Proto-Malayo-Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Malayo-Polynesian_language) [etymon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymon) *\*kamaliR*, meaning "men's house".[^cite_note-histories-1][^cite_note-2] Cognates in non-Oceanic languages include [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) and [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) *kamalig* “granary”.

## Traditional nakamals

In north and central Vanuatu, the nakamal generally takes the form of a large building, assembled from traditional materials with the help of the entire community, under the direction of a particular chief. Entry to the nakamal is often restricted to men, and the building may be used as a sleeping and living area for unmarried men and boys and for male visitors to the village. Significantly, most nakamals lack a lockable door, indicating that all friendly visitors are welcome, although there may be a low barrier across the entrance to keep out animals.

In front of a nakamal there is often a flattened clearing, or *nasara*, used for dances and outdoor gatherings.

In southern Vanuatu, a nakamal may be a large, sheltered outdoor space, such as under a banyan tree.

In Vanuatu's capital [Port Vila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Vila), the assembly building of the national council of chiefs ([Malvatumauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvatumauri)) is designed in the form of a traditional nakamal.

## *Nakamal* as *kava* bars

The *nakamal*’s most prominent function nowadays is as a place for the preparation and drinking of *[kava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kava)*. In urban Vanuatu, and in neighbouring [New Caledonia](21342.md), the term *nakamal* may be used for a kava bar where the drink is sold, although in rural Vanuatu a traditional nakamal (where *kava* preparation is a communal activity and money does not usually change hands) is distinct from a kava bar.

An urban *nakamal* or *kava* bar at which *kava* is available for sale is advertised by a coloured light displayed at the entrance.
