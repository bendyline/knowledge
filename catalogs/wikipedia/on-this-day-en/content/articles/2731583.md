**Adolf Hitler**[^cite_note-1] (20 April 1889 – 30 April 1945) was an Austrian-born  German politician who was dictator of [Germany in the Nazi era](21212.md) from 1933 until [his suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Adolf_Hitler) in 1945. [He rose to power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler's_rise_to_power) as the leader of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party),[^cite_note-2] becoming the [chancellor of Germany](20890626.md#Nazi_Germany_\(1933%E2%80%931945\)) in 1933 and then taking the title of *[Führer und Reichskanzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrer_und_Reichskanzler)* in 1934.[^cite_note-5] Germany's [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) on 1 September 1939 under his leadership marked the outbreak of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). Throughout the ensuing conflict, Hitler was closely involved in the direction of German military operations and was central to the perpetration of the [genocide](12441.md) of about six million Jews in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) as well as the deaths of [millions of other victims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victims_of_Nazi_Germany).

Hitler was born in [Braunau am Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunau_am_Inn) in [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) and moved to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in 1913. He was decorated during his service in the German Army in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), receiving the [Iron Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Cross). In 1919, he joined the [German Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Workers'_Party) (DAP), the precursor of the Nazi Party, and in 1921, was appointed the leader of the Nazi Party. In 1923, he attempted to seize governmental power in [a failed coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch) in Munich and was sentenced to five years in prison, serving just over a year. While there, he dictated the first volume of his autobiography and political manifesto *[Mein Kampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mein_Kampf)* ('my struggle'). After his early release in 1924, he gained popular support by attacking the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) as well as promoting [pan-Germanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Germanism), [antisemitism](1078.md), and [anti-communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism) with [charismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charismatic_authority) oratory and [Nazi propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany). He frequently denounced [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) as being part of an [international Jewish conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Jewish_conspiracy).&#x20;

Following [elections to the Reichstag in November 1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_1932_German_federal_election), the Nazi Party held the most seats in the *[Reichstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_\(Weimar_Republic\))* ('parliament'), but not a majority. Subsequently, former chancellor [Franz von Papen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_von_Papen) and other conservative politicians convinced *[Reichspräsident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Germany_\(1919%E2%80%931945\))* ('president') [Paul von Hindenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_von_Hindenburg) to appoint Hitler as [chancellor](20890626.md) on 30 January 1933. Shortly thereafter, on 23 March, the *Reichstag* passed the [Enabling Act of 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1933), which ultimately began the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic)'s transformation into Nazi Germany. Upon Hindenburg's death on 2 August 1934, Hitler replaced him as [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and thereafter, Germany became a [totalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian) dictatorship. Domestically, Hitler began to implement numerous [racist policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_policies_of_the_Third_Reich), which [marginalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws) and [scapegoated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scapegoating) German Jews. His first six years in power resulted in rapid economic recovery from the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), the [abrogation](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/abrogation) of restrictions imposed on Germany after the First World War, and the [annexation](18950961.md) of territories inhabited by millions of ethnic Germans, which won him significant popularity.&#x20;

One of Hitler's key goals was *[Lebensraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum)* ('living space') for the German people in Eastern Europe. His aggressive, [expansionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansionism) foreign policy is considered the primary [cause of World War II in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_World_War_II). On 1 September 1939, Hitler oversaw the German invasion of Poland, thereby causing Britain and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) to [declare war on Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declarations_of_war_during_World_War_II). After ordering [an invasion of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) in June 1941, he [declared war on the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_declaration_of_war_against_the_United_States) in December of the same year. By the end of 1941, German forces and the European [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) occupied most of Europe and [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_campaign). These gains were gradually reversed after 1941 until the [Allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) defeated the German military in 1945. On 29 April 1945, Hitler married his longtime partner, [Eva Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Braun), in the *[Führerbunker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrerbunker)* ('leader's bunker') in Berlin. They committed suicide the next day to avoid capture by the Soviet [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army).&#x20;

The historian and biographer [Ian Kershaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Kershaw) described Hitler as "the embodiment of modern political evil".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000bxvii-6] Under Hitler's leadership and [racist ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_racial_theories), the Nazi regime was responsible for the genocide of an estimated six million Jews and millions of other victims, whom he and his followers deemed *[Untermenschen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untermensch)* ('subhumans') or socially undesirable. Hitler and the Nazis were also responsible for the deliberate killing of an estimated 19.3 million civilians and prisoners of war. In addition, 28.7 million soldiers and civilians died as a result of military action in the European [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_\(warfare\)). In absolute terms, the number of [civilians killed during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties) was unprecedented in warfare, and the casualties make it the [deadliest conflict in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wars_by_death_toll).

## Ancestry

Further information: [Hitler family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_family)

Hitler's father, [Alois Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Hitler), was the [illegitimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimacy_\(family_law\)) child of [Maria Schicklgruber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Schicklgruber).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock199924-7] The baptismal register did not show the name of his father, and Alois initially bore his mother's surname, *Schicklgruber*. In 1842, [Johann Georg Hiedler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_Hiedler) married Alois's mother. Alois was brought up in the family of Hiedler's brother, [Johann Nepomuk Hiedler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Nepomuk_Hiedler).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaser19734-8] Alois worked as a civil servant from 1855 until his retirement in 1895.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw19995,_14-9] In 1876, Alois was made legitimate and his baptismal record annotated by a priest to register Johann Georg Hiedler as Alois's father (recorded as *Georg Hitler*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaser197315-10][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw19995-11] Alois then assumed the surname *Hitler*,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw19995-11] also spelled *Hiedler*, *Hüttler*, and *_Huettler_*. The name is probably based on the German word *Hütte* (lit. 'hut'), and has the meaning 'one who lives in a hut'.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJetzinger197632-12]

The Nazi official [Hans Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Frank) suggested that Alois's mother had been employed as a housekeeper by a Jewish family in [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz), and that the family's 19-year-old son Leopold Frankenberger had fathered Alois, a claim that came to be known as the *[Frankenberger thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenberger_thesis)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenbaum199921-13] Nobody named Frankenberger was registered in Graz during that period, and no record has been produced of a Leopold Frankenberger's existence,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann201050-14] so historians dismiss the claim that Alois's father was Jewish.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToland1992246–247-15][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw19998–9-16] In 2025, blood from the sofa in Hitler's study was used by [Turi King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turi_King) of the University of Bath for [DNA analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_analysis). The blood was confirmed to be Hitler's by comparing it to that of a male-line relative, and the DNA analysis also showed that Hitler did not have Jewish ancestry through his father. "If that was the case, we wouldn't have got the DNA match with him," King said.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHunt2025-17][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOltermann2025-18] "That DNA match not only confirmed that this is Hitler's DNA but also confirms that the story of human Jewish ancestry through his father is just simply not true."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHunt2025-17][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOltermann2025-18]

## Early life

### Childhood and education



Hitler, c. 1889–1890

Adolf Hitler was born on 20 April 1889 in [Braunau am Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunau_am_Inn), a town in [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (present-day Austria), close to the border with Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer19606-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock199923-20] He was the fourth of six children born to Alois Hitler and his third wife, [Klara Pölzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klara_P%C3%B6lzl). Three of Hitler's siblings (Gustav, Ida, and Otto) died in infancy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw20084-21] Also living in the household were Alois's children from his second marriage: Alois Jr. (born 1882) and [Angela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Hitler) (born 1883).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToland19766-22] In 1892, the family moved to [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau), Germany, following Alois's promotion to the customs administration in Passau. Hitler was three at the time. Alois was promoted and transferred to [Linz](55856.md), Austria, on 1 April 1893, but the rest of the family remained in Passau.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosmus200433-23] The family returned to Austria and settled in [Leonding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonding) on 9 May 1894,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosmus200435-24] and in June 1895, Alois retired to Hafeld, near [Lambach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambach), where he farmed and kept bees. Hitler attended *[Volksschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksschule)* (a state-funded primary school) in nearby [Fischlham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischlham).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKubizek200692-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler19996-26]

The move to Hafeld coincided with the onset of intense father–son conflicts caused by six-year-old Hitler's refusal to conform to the strict discipline of his school.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFromm1977493–498-27] Alois tried to browbeat his son into obedience, while Adolf did his best to be the opposite of whatever his father wanted.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann201010–11-28] Alois would also beat his son, although his mother tried to protect him from regular beatings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDiver2005-29]



Hitler's father, [Alois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Hitler), c. 1900



Hitler's mother, [Klara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klara_Hitler), 1870s

Alois Hitler's farming efforts at Hafeld were unsuccessful, and in 1897, the family moved to Lambach. The eight-year-old Hitler took singing lessons, sang in the church choir, and even considered becoming a priest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196010–11-30] In 1898, the family returned permanently to Leonding. Hitler was deeply affected by the death of his younger brother Edmund in 1900 from [measles](58911.md). Hitler transformed from a confident, outgoing, and conscientious student to a morose, detached boy who frequently clashed with his father and teachers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayne199022-31] [Paula Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Hitler) recalled that Adolf was a teenage bully who would often slap her.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDiver2005-29]

Alois had made a successful career in the customs bureau and wanted his son to follow in his footsteps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw20089-32] Hitler later dramatised an episode from this period when his father took him to visit a customs office, depicting it as an event that gave rise to an unforgiving antagonism between father and son, who were both strong-willed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler19998-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeller201033–34-34][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFest197732-35] Ignoring his son's desire to attend a classical high school and become an artist, Alois sent Hitler to the *[Realschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realschule)* in Linz in September 1900.[^cite_note-Realschule-36][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw20088-37] Hitler rebelled against this decision, and in *Mein Kampf* states that he intentionally performed poorly in school, hoping that once his father saw "what little progress I was making at the technical school he would let me devote myself to my dream".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler199910-38]

Like many Austrian Germans, Hitler began to develop [German nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nationalist) ideas from a young age.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003163–164-39] He expressed loyalty only to Germany, despising the declining [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) and its rule over an ethnically diverse empire.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBendersky200026-40][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERyschka200835-41] Hitler and his friends used the greeting *Heil*, and sang the "[Deutschlandlied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutschlandlied)" instead of the [Austrian Imperial anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gott_erhalte_Franz_den_Kaiser#Austria-Hungary).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann201013-42] After Alois's sudden death on 3 January 1903, Hitler's performance at school deteriorated, and his mother allowed him to leave.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200810-43] He enrolled at the *Realschule* in [Steyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steyr) in September 1904, where his behaviour and performance improved.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199919-44] In 1905, after passing a repeat of the final exam, Hitler left the school without any ambitions for further education or clear plans for a career.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199920-45]

### Early adulthood in Vienna and Munich

See also: [Paintings by Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paintings_by_Adolf_Hitler)



The house in [Leonding, Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonding), where Hitler spent his early adolescence

In 1907, Hitler left Linz to live and study fine art in [Vienna](55866.md), financed by an orphan's benefits and support from his mother. He applied for admission to the [Academy of Fine Arts Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Fine_Arts_Vienna) but was rejected twice.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler199920-46][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock196230–31-47] The [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_\(academia\)) suggested Hitler should apply to the School of Architecture, but he lacked the necessary academic credentials because he had not finished secondary school.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock196231-48]

On 21 December 1907, his mother died of breast cancer at the age of 47; Hitler was 18 at the time. In 1909, Hitler ran out of money and was forced to live a [bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemianism) life in homeless shelters and the [Meldemannstraße dormitory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meldemannstra%C3%9Fe_dormitory).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock199930–33-49][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann2010157-50] He earned money as a casual labourer and by painting and selling [watercolours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolours) of Vienna's sights.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler199920-46] During his time in Vienna, he pursued a growing passion for architecture and music, attending ten performances of *[Lohengrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohengrin_\(opera\))*, his favourite of [Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner)'s operas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199941,_42-51]

In Vienna, Hitler was first exposed to racist rhetoric.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196026-52] Populists such as mayor [Karl Lueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Lueger) exploited the city's prevalent [antisemitic](1078.md) sentiment, occasionally also espousing German nationalist notions for political benefit. German nationalism was especially widespread in the [Mariahilf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariahilf) district, where Hitler then lived.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann2010243–246-53] [Georg Ritter von Schönerer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Ritter_von_Sch%C3%B6nerer) became a major influence on Hitler,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENicholls2000236,_237,_274-54] and he developed an admiration for [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann2010250-55]

Hitler read newspapers and pamphlets that promoted prejudice and warned of an influx of Eastern European Jews,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann2010341–345-56] as well as writings by figures such as [Houston Stewart Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Stewart_Chamberlain), [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin), [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche), [Gustave Le Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Le_Bon), and [Arthur Schopenhauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Schopenhauer).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann2010233-57] During his years in Vienna he also developed strong [anti-Slavic sentiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Slavic_sentiment).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritannica:_Nazism-58][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPinkus200527-59]

The origin and development of Hitler's antisemitism remain a matter of debate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199960–67-60] His friend [August Kubizek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Kubizek) claimed that Hitler was a "confirmed antisemite" before he left Linz.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196025-61] The historian [Brigitte Hamann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Hamann) describes Kubizek's claim as "problematical", because it was made retroactively in 1953.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann201058-62] While Hitler states in *Mein Kampf* that he first became an antisemite in Vienna,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler199952-63] [Reinhold Hanisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_Hanisch), who helped him to sell his paintings, disagrees.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToland199245-64][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199955,_63-65][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann2010174-66] Hitler also sold some of his art through a Jewish businessman named [Samuel Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Morgenstern) while living in Vienna. Many of Morgenstern's clients were Jewish; for example, a lawyer named Josef Feingold purchased several of Hitler's paintings of Vienna's historical architecture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHamann2010356-67] The historian [Richard J. Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Evans) states that "historians now generally agree that his notorious, murderous antisemitism emerged well after Germany's defeat in [World War I](4764461.md), as a product of the paranoid ['stab-in-the-back' explanation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stab-in-the-back_myth) for the catastrophe".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2011-68]

Hitler received the final part of his father's estate in May 1913 and moved to [Munich](19058.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196027-69] When he was conscripted into the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeber201013-70] he journeyed to [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg) on 5 February 1914 for medical assessment. After he was deemed unfit for service, he returned to Munich.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199986-71] Hitler later claimed that he did not wish to serve the [Habsburg Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) because of the mixture of races in its army and his belief that the collapse of Austria-Hungary was imminent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199949-72]

### World War I

Main article: [Military career of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_career_of_Adolf_Hitler)



Hitler (far right, seated) with [Bavarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Army) comrades from the Bavarian Reserve Infantry Regiment 16 (c. 1914–18)

In August 1914, at the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md), Hitler was living in Munich and voluntarily enlisted in the [Bavarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Army).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199990-73] According to a 1924 report by the Bavarian authorities, allowing Hitler to serve was most likely an administrative error, because as an Austrian citizen, he should have been returned to Austria.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199990-73]

Posted to the [Bavarian Reserve Infantry Regiment 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Bavarian_Reserve_Division) (1st Company of the List Regiment),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199990-73][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeber201012–13-74] he served as a dispatch [runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runner_\(soldier\)) on the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\)) in France and Belgium,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200853-75] spending nearly half his time at the regimental headquarters in [Fournes-en-Weppes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fournes-en-Weppes), well behind the front lines.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200854-76][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeber2010100-77] In 1914, he was present at the [First Battle of Ypres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Ypres)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196030-78] and in that year was decorated for bravery, receiving the [Iron Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Cross), Second Class.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196030-78] During the war, he was saved by his commanding officer, [Fritz Wiedemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Wiedemann), who pulled Hitler out of the rubble of a collapsed building while under heavy fire.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPearsonAllen1941-79]

During his service at headquarters, Hitler pursued his artistic interests, drawing [cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon) and providing instructions for an army newspaper. During the [Battle of the Somme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme) in October 1916, he was wounded in the left thigh when a shell exploded in the dispatch runners' dugout.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196030-78][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200857-80] Hitler spent almost two months recovering in hospital at [Beelitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beelitz), returning to his regiment on 5 March 1917.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200858-81] He was present at the [Battle of Arras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Arras_\(1917\)) of 1917 and the [Battle of Passchendaele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Passchendaele).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196030-78] He received the black [Wound Badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wound_Badge) on 18 May 1918.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteiner1976392-82] Three months later, in August 1918, on a recommendation by Lieutenant [Hugo Gutmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Gutmann), his Jewish superior, Hitler received the Iron Cross, First Class, a decoration rarely awarded at Hitler's *[Gefreiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gefreiter)* rank.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200859-83][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeber2010a-84] On 15 October 1918, he was temporarily blinded in a [mustard gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_gas) attack and was hospitalised in [Pasewalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasewalk).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200859,_60-85] While there, Hitler learned of Germany's defeat, and, by his own account, suffered a second bout of blindness after receiving this news.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw199997,_102-86]

Hitler described his role in World War I as "the greatest of all experiences" and was praised by his commanding officers for his bravery.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeegan1987238–240-87] His wartime experience reinforced his German patriotism, and he was shocked by Germany's capitulation in November 1918.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock196260-88] His displeasure with the collapse of the war effort began to shape his ideology.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200861,_62-89] Like other German nationalists, he believed the *Dolchstoßlegende* ([stab-in-the-back myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stab-in-the-back_myth)), which claimed that the German army, "undefeated in the field", had been "stabbed in the back" on the [home front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_front) by civilian leaders, Jews, [Marxists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxists), and those who signed the [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_11_November_1918) that ended the fighting—later dubbed the "November criminals".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200861–63-90]

The [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) stipulated that Germany had to relinquish several of its territories and [demilitarise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demilitarise) the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland). The treaty imposed economic sanctions and levied heavy reparations on the country. Many Germans saw the treaty as an unjust humiliation. They especially objected to [Article 231](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_231), which they interpreted as declaring Germany responsible for the war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200896-91] The Versailles Treaty and the economic, social, and political conditions in Germany after the war were later exploited by Hitler for political gain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200880,_90,_92-92]

## Entry into politics

Main article: [Political views of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_views_of_Adolf_Hitler)



Hitler's [German Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Workers'_Party) (DAP) membership card

After the war, Hitler returned to Munich.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock199961-93] Without formal education or career prospects, he remained in the army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999109-94] In July 1919, he was appointed *Verbindungsmann* (intelligence agent) of an *Aufklärungskommando* (reconnaissance unit) of the *[Reichswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichswehr)*, assigned to influence other soldiers and to infiltrate the [German Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Workers'_Party) (DAP). At a DAP meeting on 12 September 1919, Party chairman [Anton Drexler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Drexler) was impressed by Hitler's oratorical skills. He gave him a copy of his pamphlet *My Political Awakening*, which contained antisemitic, nationalist, [anti-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalist), and anti-Marxist ideas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200882-95] On the orders of his army superiors, Hitler applied to join the party,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003170-96] and within a week was accepted as party member 555 (the party began counting membership at 500 to give the impression they were a much larger party).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200875,_76-97][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMitcham199667-98]

Hitler made his earliest known written statement about the [Jewish question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_question) in a 16 September 1919 letter to Adolf Gemlich (now known as the [Gemlich letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemlich_letter)). In the letter, Hitler argues that the aim of the government "must unshakably be the removal of the Jews altogether".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999125–126-99] At the DAP, Hitler met [Dietrich Eckart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietrich_Eckart), one of the party's founders and a member of the occult [Thule Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_Society).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFest197021-100] Eckart became Hitler's mentor, exchanging ideas with him and introducing him to a wide range of Munich society.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200894,_95,_100-101] To increase its appeal, the DAP changed its name to the *Nationalsozialistische Deutsche Arbeiterpartei* (National Socialist German Workers' Party (NSDAP), now known as the "[Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party)").[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200887-102] Hitler designed the party's banner of a [swastika](45943.md) in a white circle on a red background.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200888-103]

Hitler was discharged from the army on 31 March 1920, and began working full-time for the party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200893-104] The party headquarters was in Munich, a centre for anti-government German nationalists determined to eliminate Marxism and undermine the Weimar Republic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200881-105]

[Friedrich Reck-Malleczewen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Reck-Malleczewen) commented in his 1947 book *[Diary of a Man in Despair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_a_Man_in_Despair)*:

> &#x20;He had come to a house where he had never been before, wearing gaiters, a floppy, wide-brimmed hat, and carrying a riding whip. ... Eventually, he managed to launch into a speech. He talked on and on, endlessly. He preached. He went on at us like a division chaplain in the Army. We did not in the least contradict him, or venture to differ in any way, but he began to bellow at us. The servants thought we were being attacked and rushed in to defend us. When he had gone, we sat silently, confused and not at all amused. There was a feeling of dismay, as when on a train you suddenly find you are sharing a compartment with a psychotic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReck-Malleczewen201317-106]

In February 1921, already highly effective at [crowd manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowd_manipulation), Hitler spoke to a crowd of over 6,000.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200889-107] To publicise the meeting, two truckloads of party supporters drove around Munich waving swastika flags and distributing leaflets. Hitler soon gained notoriety for his rowdy [polemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polemic) speeches against the Treaty of Versailles, rival politicians, and especially against Marxists and Jews.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200889–92-108]

In June 1921, while Hitler and Eckart were on a fundraising trip to Berlin, a mutiny broke out within the Nazi Party in Munich. Members of its executive committee wanted to merge with the Nuremberg-based [German Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Socialist_Party) (DSP).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008100,_101-109] Hitler returned to Munich on 11 July and angrily tendered his resignation. The committee members realised that the resignation of their leading public figure and speaker would mean the end of the party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008102-110] Hitler announced he would rejoin on the condition that he would replace Drexler as party chairman, and that the party headquarters would remain in Munich.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008103-111] The committee agreed, and he rejoined the party on 26 July as member 3,680. Hitler continued to face some opposition within the Nazi Party. Opponents of Hitler in the leadership had [Hermann Esser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Esser) expelled from the party, and they printed 3,000 copies of a pamphlet attacking Hitler as a traitor to the party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008103-111][^cite_note-libel_suit-112] In the following days, Hitler spoke to several large audiences and defended himself and Esser, to thunderous applause. His strategy proved successful, and at a special party congress on 29 July, he was granted absolute power as party chairman, succeeding Drexler, by a vote of 533 to 1.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw200883,_103-113]

Hitler's vitriolic beer hall speeches began attracting regular audiences. A [demagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demagogue),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000bxv-114] he became adept at using populist themes, including the use of [scapegoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scapegoat), who were blamed for his listeners' economic hardships.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999376-115][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrauenfeld1937-116][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoebbels1936-117] Hitler used personal magnetism and an understanding of [crowd psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowd_psychology) to his advantage while engaged in public speaking.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008105–106-118][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999377-119] Historians have noted the hypnotic effect of his rhetoric on large audiences, and of his eyes in small groups.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKressel2002121-120] [Alfons Heck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfons_Heck), a former member of the [Hitler Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_Youth), recalled:

> We erupted into a frenzy of nationalistic pride that bordered on hysteria. For minutes on end, we shouted at the top of our lungs, with tears streaming down our faces: *Sieg Heil, Sieg Heil, Sieg Heil!* From that moment on, I belonged to Adolf Hitler body and soul.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeck200123-121]

Early followers included [Rudolf Hess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Hess), the former air force ace [Hermann Göring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_G%C3%B6ring), and the army captain [Ernst Röhm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_R%C3%B6hm). Röhm became head of the Nazis' paramilitary organisation, the *[Sturmabteilung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung)* (SA, "Stormtroopers"), which protected meetings and attacked political opponents. A critical influence on Hitler's thinking during this period was the *[Aufbau Vereinigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aufbau_Vereinigung)*,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKellogg2005275-122] a conspiratorial group of [White Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_%C3%A9migr%C3%A9) exiles and early Nazis. The group, financed with funds channelled from wealthy industrialists, introduced Hitler to the idea of a Jewish conspiracy, linking international finance with [Bolshevism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKellogg2005203-123]

The programme of the Nazi Party was laid out in their [25-point programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25-point_programme) on 24 February 1920. This did not represent a coherent ideology, but was a conglomeration of received ideas which had currency in the *[völkisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%B6lkisch)* [pan-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Germanic) movement, such as [ultranationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalism), opposition to the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles), distrust of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism), as well as some [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) ideas. For Hitler, the most important aspect of it was its strong [antisemitic](1078.md) stance. He also perceived the programme as primarily a basis for propaganda and for attracting people to the party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBracher1970115–116-124]

### Beer Hall Putsch and Landsberg Prison

Main article: [Beer Hall Putsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch)



The [dust jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_jacket) of *[Mein Kampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mein_Kampf)*'s 1926–28 edition, which Hitler authored in 1925

In 1923, Hitler enlisted the help of World War I General [Erich Ludendorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Ludendorff) for an attempted coup known as the Beer Hall Putsch. The Nazi Party used [Italian Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Fascism) as a model for their appearance and policies. Hitler wanted to emulate [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini)'s [March on Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_on_Rome) in 1922 by staging his own coup in Bavaria, to be followed by a challenge to the government in Berlin. Hitler and Ludendorff sought the support of *Staatskommissar* (State Commissioner) [Gustav Ritter von Kahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Ritter_von_Kahr), Bavaria's *de facto* ruler. However, Kahr, along with Police Chief [Hans Ritter von Seisser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Ritter_von_Seisser) and Reichswehr General [Otto von Lossow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Lossow), wanted to install a nationalist dictatorship without Hitler.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008126-125]

On 8 November 1923, Hitler and the SA stormed a public meeting of 3,000 people organised by Kahr in the [Bürgerbräukeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCrgerbr%C3%A4ukeller), a beer hall in Munich. Interrupting Kahr's speech, he announced that the national revolution had begun and declared the formation of a new government with Ludendorff.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008128-126] Retiring to a back room, Hitler, with his pistol drawn, demanded and subsequently received the support of Kahr, Seisser, and Lossow.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008128-126] Hitler's forces initially succeeded in occupying the local Reichswehr and police headquarters, but Kahr and his cohorts quickly withdrew their support. Neither the Army nor the state police joined forces with Hitler.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008129-127] The next day, Hitler and his followers marched from the beer hall to the [Bavarian War Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_War_Ministry) to overthrow the Bavarian government, but police dispersed them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008130–131-128] In the failed coup, 16 Nazi Party members and four police officers were killed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196073–74-129]

Hitler fled to the home of [Ernst Hanfstaengl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Hanfstaengl) and by some accounts contemplated suicide.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008132-130] He was depressed but calm when arrested on 11 November 1923 for [high treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_treason).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008131-131] His trial began on 26 February 1924,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003195-132] and [Alfred Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Rosenberg) became the temporary leader of the Nazi Party. On 1 April, Hitler was sentenced to five years' *[Festungshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festungshaft)* ('fortress confinement') at [Landsberg Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsberg_Prison).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003196-133] There, he received friendly treatment from the guards and was allowed mail from supporters and regular visits by party comrades. Pardoned by the Bavarian Supreme Court, he was released from jail on 20 December 1924, against the state prosecutor's objections.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999239-134] Including time on remand, Hitler served just over one year in prison.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962121-135]

While at Landsberg, Hitler dictated most of the first volume of *Mein Kampf* ('my struggle'; originally titled *Four and a Half Years of Struggle against Lies, Stupidity, and Cowardice*) at first to his chauffeur, [Emil Maurice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Maurice), and then to his deputy, Rudolf Hess.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962121-135][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008147-136] The book, dedicated to Thule Society member Dietrich Eckart, was an autobiography and exposition of his ideology. The book laid out Hitler's plans for territorial expansion as well as transforming German society into a dictatorship based on race. Throughout the book, Jews are equated with "germs" and presented as the "international poisoners" of society.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008148–150-137] According to Hitler's ideology, the only solution was their extermination. While Hitler did not describe exactly how this was to be accomplished, his "inherent genocidal thrust is undeniable", according to [Ian Kershaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Kershaw).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008148–150-137]

Published in two volumes in 1925 and 1926, *Mein Kampf* sold 228,000 copies between 1925 and 1932. One million copies were sold in 1933, Hitler's first year in office.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer196080–81-138] Shortly before Hitler was eligible for parole, the Bavarian government attempted to have him deported to Austria.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999237-139] The Austrian federal chancellor rejected the request on the specious grounds that his service in the German Army made his Austrian citizenship void.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999238-140] In response, Hitler formally renounced his Austrian citizenship on 7 April 1925.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999238-140]

### Rebuilding the Nazi Party

At the time of Hitler's release from prison, politics in Germany had become less combative, and the economy had improved, limiting Hitler's opportunities for political agitation. As a result of the failed Beer Hall Putsch, the Nazi Party and its affiliated organisations were banned in Bavaria. In a meeting with the prime minister of Bavaria, [Heinrich Held](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Held), on 4 January 1925, Hitler agreed to respect the state's authority and promised that he would seek political power only through the democratic process. The meeting paved the way for the ban on the Nazi Party to be lifted on 16 February.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008158,_161,_162-141]

However, after an inflammatory speech he gave on 27 February, Hitler was barred from public speaking by the Bavarian authorities, a ban that remained in place until 1927.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008162,_166-142][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960129-143] To advance his political ambitions despite the ban, Hitler appointed [Gregor Strasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Strasser), [Otto Strasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Strasser), and [Joseph Goebbels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Goebbels) to organise and enlarge the Nazi Party in northern Germany. Gregor Strasser steered a more independent political course, emphasising the socialist elements of the party's programme.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008166,_167-144]

The stock market in the United States [crashed on 24 October 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_crash_of_1929). The impact in Germany was dire: millions became unemployed, and several major banks collapsed. Hitler and the Nazi Party prepared to take advantage of the emergency to gain support for their party. They promised to repudiate the Versailles Treaty, strengthen the economy, and provide jobs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960136–137-145]

Hitler discussed the name of the Nazi Party in October 1923:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitlerJäckel19801023-146]

> "Why," I asked Hitler, "do you call yourself a National Socialist, since your party program is the very antithesis of that commonly accredited to socialism?" "Socialism," he retorted, putting down his cup of tea, pugnaciously, "is the science of dealing with the common weal. Communism is not Socialism. Marxism is not Socialism. The Marxians have stolen the term and confused its meaning. I shall take Socialism away from the Socialists. Socialism is an ancient Aryan, Germanic institution. ... We demand the fulfilment of the just claims of the productive classes by the state based on race solidarity. To us, state and race are one."

### Brüning administration

The Great Depression provided a political opportunity for Hitler. Germans were ambivalent about the [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic), which faced challenges from [right-](221220.md) and [left-wing extremists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics). The moderate political parties were increasingly unable to stem the tide of extremism, and the [German referendum of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_German_referendum) helped to elevate Nazi ideology.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKolb1988105-148] The elections of September 1930 resulted in the break-up of a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition) and its replacement with a minority cabinet. Its leader, chancellor [Heinrich Brüning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Br%C3%BCning) of the [Centre Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Party_\(Germany\)), governed through [emergency decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_powers) from President Paul von Hindenburg. Governance by decree became the new norm, paving the way for authoritarian forms of government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalperin1965403_''et._seq''-149] The Nazi Party rose from obscurity to win 18.3 per cent of the vote and 107 parliamentary seats in the 1930 election, becoming the second-largest party in parliament.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalperin1965434–446_''et._seq''-150]

Hitler made a prominent appearance at the trial of two Reichswehr officers, Lieutenants Richard Scheringer and [Hanns Ludin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanns_Ludin), in late 1930. Both were charged with membership in the Nazi Party, at that time illegal for Reichswehr personnel.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWheeler-Bennett1967218-151] The prosecution argued that the Nazi Party was extremist, prompting defence lawyer Hans Frank to call on Hitler to testify.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWheeler-Bennett1967216-152] On 25 September 1930, Hitler testified that his party would pursue political power solely through democratic elections,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWheeler-Bennett1967218–219-153] which won him many supporters in the officer corps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWheeler-Bennett1967222-154]

Brüning's austerity measures brought little economic improvement and were extremely unpopular.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalperin1965449_''et._seq''-155] Hitler exploited this by targeting his political messages specifically at people who had been affected by the inflation of the 1920s and the Depression, such as farmers, war veterans, and the middle class.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalperin1965434–436,_471-156]

Although Hitler had terminated his Austrian citizenship in 1925, he did not acquire German citizenship for almost seven years. This meant that he was [stateless](789786.md), legally unable to run for public office, and still faced the risk of deportation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960130-157] On 25 February 1932, the interior minister of [Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Brunswick), [Dietrich Klagges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietrich_Klagges), who was a member of the Nazi Party, appointed Hitler as administrator for the state's delegation to the [Reichsrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsrat_\(Germany\)) in Berlin, making Hitler a citizen of Brunswick,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinrichs2007-158] and thus of Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalperin1965476-159]

Hitler ran against Hindenburg in the [1932 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_German_presidential_election). A speech to the Industry Club in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) on 27 January 1932 won him support from many of Germany's most powerful industrialists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalperin1965468–471-160] Hindenburg had support from various nationalist, monarchist, Catholic, and [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism) parties, and some [Social Democrats](60585.md). Hitler used the campaign slogan "*Hitler über Deutschland*" ("Hitler over Germany"), a reference to his political ambitions and his campaigning by aircraft.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962201-161] He was one of the first politicians to use aircraft travel for campaigning and used it effectively.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoffman1989-162][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008227-163] Hitler came in second in both rounds of the election, garnering more than 35 per cent of the vote in the final election. Although he lost to Hindenburg, this election established Hitler as a strong force in German politics.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalperin1965477–479-164]

### Appointment as chancellor



Hitler, at a window of the [Reich Chancellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Chancellery), receives an ovation on the evening of his inauguration as [chancellor](20890626.md), 30 January 1933

The absence of an effective government prompted two influential politicians, Franz von Papen and [Alfred Hugenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hugenberg), along with several other industrialists and businessmen, to write a letter to Hindenburg. The signers urged Hindenburg to appoint Hitler as leader of a government "independent from parliamentary parties", which could turn into a movement that would "enrapture millions of people".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchröder2019-165][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Fox_News''2003-166]

Hindenburg reluctantly agreed to appoint Hitler as chancellor after two further parliamentary elections—in July and November 1932—had not resulted in the formation of a majority government. Hitler headed a short-lived coalition government formed by the Nazi Party (which had the most seats in the Reichstag) and Hugenberg's party, the [German National People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_People's_Party) (DNVP). On 30 January 1933, the new cabinet was sworn in during a brief ceremony in Hindenburg's office. The Nazi Party gained three posts: Hitler was named chancellor, [Wilhelm Frick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Frick) Minister of the Interior, and Hermann Göring Minister of the Interior for Prussia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960184-167] Hitler had insisted on the ministerial positions as a way to gain control over the police in much of Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003307-168]

### Reichstag fire and March elections

Main article: [Reichstag fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_fire)

As chancellor, Hitler worked against attempts by the Nazi Party's opponents to build a majority government. Because of the political stalemate, he asked Hindenburg to again dissolve the Reichstag, and elections were scheduled for early March. On 27 February 1933, the [Reichstag building was set on fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_fire). Göring blamed a communist plot, as the Dutch communist [Marinus van der Lubbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinus_van_der_Lubbe) was found in incriminating circumstances inside the burning building.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962262-169] Until the 1960s, some historians, including [William L. Shirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_L._Shirer) and [Alan Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Bullock), thought the Nazi Party was responsible;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960192-170][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999262-171] now the view of most historians is van der Lubbe started the fire alone.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHett2014255–259-172]

At Hitler's urging, Hindenburg responded by signing the [Reichstag Fire Decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_Fire_Decree) of 28 February, drafted by the Nazis, which suspended basic rights and allowed detention without trial. The decree was permitted under [Article 48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_48_of_the_Weimar_Constitution) of the Weimar Constitution, which gave the president the power to take emergency measures to protect public safety and order.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960194,_274-173] Activities of the [German Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Germany) (KPD) were suppressed, and 4,000 KPD members were arrested.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960194-174]

In addition to political campaigning, the Nazi Party engaged in paramilitary violence and the spread of anti-communist propaganda, in the days preceding [the election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_1933_German_federal_election). On election day, 6 March 1933, the Nazis’ share of the vote increased to 44%, and the party acquired the largest number of seats in parliament. Hitler's party failed to secure an absolute majority, necessitating another coalition with the DNVP.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962265-175]

### Day of Potsdam and the Enabling Act

Main article: [Enabling Act of 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1933)

On 21 March 1933, the new *Reichstag* was constituted with an opening ceremony at the [Garrison Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison_Church,_Potsdam) in [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam). This "Day of Potsdam" was held to demonstrate unity between the Nazi movement and the old [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) elite and military. Hitler appeared in a [morning coat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_coat) and humbly greeted Hindenburg.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''City_of_Potsdam''2004-176][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960196–197-177]

To achieve full political control despite not having an absolute majority in parliament, Hitler's government brought the *Ermächtigungsgesetz* (Enabling Act) to a vote in the newly elected *Reichstag*. The Act—officially titled the *Gesetz zur Behebung der Not von Volk und Reich* ("Law to Remedy the Distress of People and Reich")—gave Hitler's cabinet the power to enact laws without the consent of the Reichstag for four years. These laws could (with certain exceptions) deviate from the constitution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960198-178]

Since it would affect the constitution, the Enabling Act required a two-thirds majority to pass. Leaving nothing to chance, the Nazis used the provisions of the Reichstag Fire Decree to arrest all 81 Communist deputies (in spite of their virulent campaign against the party, the Nazis had allowed the KPD to contest the election)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003335-179] and prevent several Social Democrats from attending.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960196-180]

On 23 March 1933, the *Reichstag* assembled at the [Kroll Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kroll_Opera_House) under turbulent circumstances. Ranks of SA men served as guards inside the building, while large groups outside, opposing the proposed legislation, shouted slogans and threats towards the arriving members of parliament.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999269-181] After Hitler verbally promised Centre party leader [Ludwig Kaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Kaas) that Hindenburg would retain his power of veto, Kaas announced the Centre Party would support the Enabling Act. The Act was passed by a vote of 444–94, with all parties except the Social Democrats voting in favour. The Enabling Act, along with the Reichstag Fire Decree, transformed Hitler's government into a *de facto* legal dictatorship.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960199-182]

### Dictatorship



Portrait by [Heinrich Knirr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Knirr) (1937)

> At the risk of appearing to talk nonsense, I tell you that the National Socialist movement will go on for 1,000 years! ... Don't forget how people laughed at me 15 years ago when I declared that one day I would govern Germany. They laugh now, just as foolishly, when I declare that I shall remain in power\![^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Time''1934-183]

— Adolf Hitler to a British correspondent in Berlin, June 1934

Having achieved full control over the legislative and executive branches of government, Hitler and his allies began to suppress the remaining opposition. The Social Democratic Party was made illegal, and its assets were seized.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960201-184] While many [trade union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_unions_in_Germany) delegates were in Berlin for May Day activities, SA stormtroopers occupied union offices around the country. On 2 May 1933, all trade unions were forced to dissolve, and their leaders were arrested. Some were sent to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960202-185] The [German Labour Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Labour_Front) was formed as an umbrella organisation to represent all workers, administrators, and company owners, thus reflecting the concept of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) in the spirit of Hitler's *[Volksgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksgemeinschaft)* ("people's community").[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003350–374-186]

By the end of June, the other parties had been intimidated into disbanding. This included the Nazis' nominal coalition partner, the DNVP; with the SA's help, Hitler forced its leader, Hugenberg, to resign on 29 June. On 14 July 1933, the Nazi Party was declared the only legal political party in Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2003350–374-186][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960201-184] The demands of the SA for more political and military power caused anxiety among military, industrial, and political leaders. In response, Hitler purged the entire SA leadership in the [Night of the Long Knives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_the_Long_Knives), which took place from 30 June to 2 July 1934.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008309–314-187] Hitler targeted Ernst Röhm and other SA leaders who, along with several Hitler's political adversaries (such as Gregor Strasser and former chancellor [Kurt von Schleicher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_von_Schleicher)), were rounded up, arrested, and shot.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETames20084–5-188] While the international community and some Germans were shocked by the killings, many in Germany believed Hitler was restoring order.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008313–315-189]

Hindenburg died on 2 August 1934. On the previous day, the cabinet had enacted the [Law Concerning the Head of State of the German Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Concerning_the_Head_of_State_of_the_German_Reich).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery200563-4] This law stated that upon Hindenburg's death, the office of president would be abolished, and its powers merged with those of the chancellor. Hitler thus became head of state as well as head of government and was formally named as *Führer und Reichskanzler* (Leader and Chancellor of the Reich),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960226–227-3] although *Reichskanzler* was eventually dropped.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans200544-190] With this action, Hitler eliminated the last legal remedy by which he could be removed from office.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960229-191]

As head of state, Hitler became commander-in-chief of the armed forces. Immediately after Hindenburg's death, at the instigation of the leadership of the *Reichswehr*, the traditional loyalty oath of soldiers was altered to [affirm loyalty to Hitler personally, by name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_oath), rather than to the office of commander-in-chief (which was later renamed to supreme commander) or to Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962309-192] On 19 August, the merger of the presidency with the chancellorship was approved by 88 per cent of the electorate voting in a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_German_referendum).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2005110-193]

In early 1938, Hitler used blackmail to consolidate his hold over the military by instigating the [Blomberg–Fritsch affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blomberg%E2%80%93Fritsch_affair). Hitler forced his War Minister, Field Marshal [Werner von Blomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_von_Blomberg), to resign by using a police dossier that showed that Blomberg's new wife had a record for prostitution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008392,_393-194][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960312-195] Army commander Colonel-General [Werner von Fritsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_von_Fritsch) was removed after the *[Schutzstaffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel)* (SS) produced allegations that he had engaged in a homosexual relationship.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008393–397-196] Both men had fallen into disfavour because they objected to Hitler's demand to make the *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* ready for war as early as 1938.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960308-197] Hitler assumed Blomberg's title of Commander-in-Chief, thus taking personal command of the armed forces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960318-198] He replaced the Ministry of War with the *[Oberkommando der Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkommando_der_Wehrmacht)* (OKW), headed by General [Wilhelm Keitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Keitel). On the same day, 16 generals were stripped of their commands, and 44 more were transferred; all were suspected of not being sufficiently pro-Nazi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960318–319-199] By early February 1938, 12 more generals had been removed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008397–398-200]

Hitler took care to give his dictatorship the appearance of legality. Many of his decrees were explicitly based on the Reichstag Fire Decree and hence on Article 48 of the Weimar Constitution. The *Reichstag* renewed the Enabling Act twice, each time for four years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960274-201] While elections to the *Reichstag* were still held (in 1933, 1936, and 1938), voters were presented with a single list of Nazis and pro-Nazi "guests" which received well over 90 per cent of the vote.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERead2004344-202] These sham elections were held in far-from-secret conditions; the Nazis threatened severe reprisals against anyone who did not vote or who voted against.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2005109–111-203]

## Nazi Germany

Main article: [Nazi Germany](21212.md)

### Economy and culture

Main article: [Economy of Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Nazi_Germany)



Hitler and his staff doing a [Nazi salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_salute) towards the teams during the opening ceremonies of the XI Olympic Games in August 1936

In August 1934, Hitler appointed *Reichsbank* President [Hjalmar Schacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Schacht) as Minister of Economics, and in the following year, as Plenipotentiary for War Economy in charge of preparing the economy for war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcNab200954-204] Reconstruction and rearmament were financed through [Mefo bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mefo_bills), printing money, and seizing the assets of people arrested as [enemies of the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemies_of_the_state), including Jews.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960259–260-205] The number of unemployed fell from six million in 1932 to fewer than one million in 1936.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960258-206] Hitler oversaw one of the largest infrastructure improvement campaigns in German history, leading to the construction of dams, [*autobahns*](1764276.md), railroads, and other civil works. Wages were slightly lower in the mid- to late 1930s compared with wages during the Weimar Republic, while the cost of living increased by 25 per cent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960262-207] The average workweek increased during the shift to a war economy; by 1939, the average German was working between 47 and 50 hours a week.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcNab200954–57-208]

Hitler's government sponsored [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_architecture) on an immense scale. [Albert Speer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Speer), instrumental in implementing Hitler's classicist reinterpretation of German culture, was placed in charge of the [proposed architectural renovations of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welthauptstadt_Germania).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971118–119-209] Despite a threatened [multi-nation boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics#Boycott_debate), Germany hosted the 1936 Olympic Games. Hitler [officiated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_have_opened_the_Olympic_Games) at the opening ceremonies and attended events at both the [Winter Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Winter_Olympics) in [Garmisch-Partenkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmisch-Partenkirchen) and the [Summer Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) in Berlin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2005570–572-210]

### Rearmament and new alliances

Main articles: [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers), [Tripartite Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Pact), and [German re-armament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_re-armament)

In a meeting with German military leaders on 3 February 1933, Hitler spoke of "conquest for *Lebensraum* in the East and its ruthless Germanisation" as his ultimate foreign policy objectives.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg197026–27-211] In March, Prince Bernhard Wilhelm von Bülow, secretary at the [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Foreign_Office) (*Auswärtiges Amt*), issued a statement of major foreign policy aims: *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)* with Austria, the restoration of Germany's national borders of 1914, rejection of military restrictions under the Treaty of Versailles, the return of the former German colonies in Africa, and a German zone of influence in Eastern Europe. Hitler found Bülow's goals to be too modest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999490–491-212] In speeches during this period, he stressed what he termed the peaceful goals of his policies and a willingness to work within international agreements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999492,_555–556,_586–587-213] At the first meeting of his cabinet in 1933, Hitler prioritised military spending over unemployment relief.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarr197223-214]

Germany withdrew from the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) and the [World Disarmament Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Disarmament_Conference) in October 1933.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008297-215] In January 1935, over 90 per cent of the people of the [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland), then under League of Nations administration, [voted to unite with Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Saar_status_referendum).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960283-216] That March, Hitler announced an expansion of the Wehrmacht to 600,000 members—six times the number permitted by the Versailles Treaty—including development of an air force (*[Luftwaffe](17885.md)*) and an increase in the size of the navy (*[Kriegsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriegsmarine)*). Britain, France, Italy, and the League of Nations condemned these violations of the Treaty but did nothing to stop them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990601–602-217][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartin2008-218] The [Anglo-German Naval Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-German_Naval_Agreement) (AGNA) of 18 June allowed German tonnage to increase to 35 per cent of that of the [Royal Navy](26061.md). Hitler called the signing of the AGNA "the happiest day of his life", believing that the agreement marked the beginning of the Anglo-German alliance he had predicted in *Mein Kampf*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHildebrand197339-219] France and Italy were not consulted before the signing, directly undermining the League of Nations and setting the Treaty of Versailles on the path towards irrelevance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoberts1975-220]

Germany [reoccupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remilitarization_of_the_Rhineland) the demilitarised zone in the Rhineland in March 1936, in violation of the Versailles Treaty. Hitler also sent troops to Spain to support [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) and his [Nationalist faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_faction_\(Spanish_Civil_War\)) during the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) after receiving an appeal for help in July 1936. At the same time, Hitler continued his efforts to create an Anglo-German alliance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990630–631-221] In August 1936, in response to a growing economic crisis caused by his rearmament efforts, Hitler ordered Göring to implement a [Four Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Year_Plan) to prepare Germany for war within the next four years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery1999b-222] The plan envisaged an all-out struggle between "[Judaeo-Bolshevism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judeo-Bolshevism)" and German Nazism, which in Hitler's view required a committed effort of rearmament regardless of the economic costs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarr197256–57-223]

In October 1936, Count [Galeazzo Ciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeazzo_Ciano), foreign minister of Mussolini's government, visited Germany, where he signed a [Nine-Point Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-German_protocol_of_23_October_1936) as an expression of *rapprochement* and had a personal meeting with Hitler. On 1 November, Mussolini declared an "axis" between Germany and Italy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoeschel201869–70-224] On 25 November, Germany signed the [Anti-Comintern Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Comintern_Pact) with [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan). Britain, China, Italy, and Poland were also invited to join the Anti-Comintern Pact, but only Italy signed in 1937. Hitler abandoned his plan of an Anglo-German alliance, blaming "inadequate" British leadership.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990642-225] At a meeting in the [Reich Chancellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Chancellery) with his foreign ministers and military chiefs that November, Hitler restated his intention of acquiring *Lebensraum* for the German people. He ordered preparations for war in the East to begin as early as 1938 and no later than 1943. In the event of his death, the conference minutes, recorded as the [Hossbach Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossbach_Memorandum), were to be regarded as his "political testament".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAigner1985264-226] He felt that a severe decline in living standards in Germany as a result of the economic crisis could only be stopped by military aggression aimed at seizing Austria and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990636–637-227][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarr197273–78-228] Hitler urged quick action before Britain and France gained a permanent lead in the [arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_race).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990636–637-227] In early 1938, in the wake of the [Blomberg–Fritsch affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blomberg%E2%80%93Fritsch_affair), Hitler asserted control of the military-foreign policy apparatus, dismissing Neurath as foreign minister and appointing himself as War Minister.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery1999b-222] From early 1938 onwards, Hitler was carrying out a foreign policy ultimately aimed at war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990638-229]

## World War II

### Early diplomatic successes

#### Alliance with Japan

See also: [Germany–Japan relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Japan_relations)

In February 1938, on the advice of his newly appointed foreign minister, the strongly pro-Japanese [Joachim von Ribbentrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_von_Ribbentrop), Hitler ended the [Sino-German alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-German_cooperation_until_1941) with the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%9349\)) to instead enter into an alliance with the more modern and powerful [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan). Hitler announced German recognition of [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo), the Japanese puppet state in [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria), and renounced German claims to their former colonies in the Pacific held by Japan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloch1992178–179-230] Hitler ordered an end to arms shipments to China and recalled all German officers working with the Chinese Army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloch1992178–179-230] In retaliation, Chinese General [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) cancelled all Sino-German economic agreements, depriving the Germans of many Chinese raw materials.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlating201121-231]

#### Austria and Czechoslovakia

On 12 March 1938, Hitler announced the unification of Austria with Germany in the *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEButlerYoung1989159-232][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962434-233] Hitler then turned his attention to the [ethnic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_German) population of the [Sudetenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetenland) region of Czechoslovakia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery2005425-234] On 28–29 March 1938, Hitler held a series of secret meetings in Berlin with [Konrad Henlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Henlein) of the [Sudeten German Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudeten_German_Party), the largest of the ethnic German parties of the Sudetenland. The men agreed that Henlein would demand increased autonomy for [Sudeten Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudeten_Germans) from the Czechoslovak government, thus providing a pretext for German military action against Czechoslovakia. In April 1938, Henlein told the [foreign minister](25144043.md) of [Hungary](13275.md) that "whatever the Czech government might offer, he would always raise still higher demands ... he wanted to sabotage an understanding by any means because this was the only method to blow up Czechoslovakia quickly".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980334–335-235] In private, Hitler considered the Sudeten issue unimportant; his real intention was a war of conquest against Czechoslovakia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980338–340-236]

In April, Hitler ordered the OKW to prepare for *[Fall Grün](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Gr%C3%BCn_\(Czechoslovakia\))* (Case Green), the code name for an invasion of Czechoslovakia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980366-237] As a result of intense French and British diplomatic pressure, on 5 September, Czechoslovak president [Edvard Beneš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Bene%C5%A1) unveiled the "Fourth Plan" for constitutional reorganisation of his country, which agreed to most of Henlein's demands for Sudeten autonomy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980418–419-238] Henlein's party responded to Beneš's offer by instigating a series of violent clashes with the Czechoslovak police that led to the declaration of martial law in certain Sudeten districts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKee1988149–150-239][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980419-240]

Germany was dependent on imported oil; a confrontation with Britain over the Czechoslovak dispute could curtail Germany's oil supplies. This forced Hitler to call off *Fall Grün*, originally planned for 1 October 1938.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurray1984256–260-241] On 29 September, Hitler, [Neville Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain), [Édouard Daladier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Daladier), and Mussolini attended a one-day conference in Munich that led to the [Munich Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement), which handed over the Sudetenland districts to Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962469-242][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery1999a207-243]

Chamberlain was satisfied with the Munich conference, calling the outcome "[peace for our time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_for_our_time)", while Hitler was angered about the missed opportunity for war in 1938;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKee1988202–203-244][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980462–463-245] he expressed his disappointment in a speech on 9 October in [Saarbrücken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarbr%C3%BCcken).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990672-246] In Hitler's view, the British-brokered peace, although favourable to the ostensible German demands, was a diplomatic defeat which spurred his intent of limiting British power to pave the way for the eastern expansion of Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990671,_682–683-247][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERothwell200190–91-248] As a result of the summit, Hitler was selected *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine's [Man of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year) for 1938.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Time''1939-249] In late 1938 and early 1939, the continuing economic crisis caused by rearmament forced Hitler to make major defence cuts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurray1984268-250] In his "Export or die" [speech of 30 January 1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_January_1939_Reichstag_speech), he called for an economic offensive to increase German foreign exchange holdings to pay for raw materials such as high-grade iron needed for military weapons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurray1984268-250]

On 14 March 1939, under threat from Hungary, [Slovakia declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_Republic_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) and received protection from Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2005682-251] The next day, in violation of the Munich Agreement and possibly as a result of the deepening economic crisis requiring additional assets,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurray1984268–269-252] Hitler ordered the Wehrmacht to [invade the Czech rump state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Czechoslovakia_\(1938%E2%80%931945\)), and from [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle) he proclaimed the territory a [German protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960448-253]

### Start of World War II

See also: [Causes of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_World_War_II)

In private discussions in 1939, Hitler declared Britain the main enemy to be defeated and that Poland's obliteration was a necessary prelude for that goal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980562-254] The eastern flank would be secured, and land would be added to Germany's *Lebensraum*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980579–581-255] Offended by the British "guarantee" on 31 March 1939 of Polish independence, he said, "I shall brew them a devil's drink".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaiolo1998178-256] In a speech in [Wilhelmshaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmshaven) for the launch of the battleship [*Tirpitz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_battleship_Tirpitz) on 1 April, he threatened to denounce the [Anglo-German Naval Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-German_Naval_Agreement) if the British continued to guarantee Polish independence, which he perceived as an "encirclement" policy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaiolo1998178-256] Poland was to either become a German [satellite state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_state), or it would be neutralised to secure the Reich's eastern flank and prevent a possible British blockade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990688–690-257]

Hitler initially favoured the idea of a satellite state, but upon its rejection by the Polish government, he decided to invade and made this the main foreign policy goal of 1939.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980537–539,_557–560-258] On 3 April, Hitler ordered the military to prepare for *[Fall Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Weiss_\(1939\))* ("Case White"), the plan for invading Poland on 25 August.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980537–539,_557–560-258] In a Reichstag speech on 28 April, he renounced both the Anglo-German Naval Agreement and the [German–Polish Non-Aggression Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German%E2%80%93Polish_Non-Aggression_Pact).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980558-259] Historians such as [William Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Carr_\(historian\)), [Gerhard Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Weinberg), and [Ian Kershaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Kershaw) have argued that one reason for Hitler's rush to war was his fear of an early death. He had repeatedly claimed that he must lead Germany into war before he got too old, as his successors might lack his strength of will.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarr197276–77-260][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000b36–37,_92-261][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg2010792-262] Hitler was concerned that a military attack against Poland could result in a premature war with Britain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMesserschmidt1990688–690-257][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson1985212-263] Hitler's foreign minister and former Ambassador to London, Joachim von Ribbentrop, assured him that neither Britain nor France would honour its commitments to Poland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloch1992228-264][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOveryWheatcroft198956-265] Accordingly, on 22 August 1939, Hitler ordered a military mobilisation against Poland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008497-266]

This plan required tacit Soviet support,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson1963181–187-267] and the [non-aggression pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-aggression_pact) (the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact)) between Germany and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), led by [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin), included a secret agreement to partition Poland between the two countries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2005693-268] Contrary to Ribbentrop's prediction that Britain would sever Anglo-Polish ties, Britain and Poland signed the Anglo-Polish alliance on 25 August 1939. This, along with news from Italy that Mussolini would not honour the [Pact of Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pact_of_Steel), prompted Hitler to postpone the attack on Poland from 25 August to 1 September.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloch1992252–253-269] Hitler unsuccessfully tried to manoeuvre the British into neutrality by offering them a non-aggression guarantee on 25 August; he then instructed Ribbentrop to present a last-minute peace plan with an impossibly short time limit in an effort to blame the imminent war on British and Polish inaction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg199585–94-270][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloch1992255–257-271]

On 1 September 1939, Germany [invaded western Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) under the pretext of having been denied claims to the [Free City of Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Danzig) and the right to extraterritorial roads across the [Polish Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Corridor), which Germany had ceded under the Versailles Treaty.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1980561–562,_583–584-272] In response, [Britain and France declared war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_and_French_declaration_of_war_on_Germany) on Germany on 3 September, surprising Hitler and prompting him to angrily ask Ribbentrop, "Now what?"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloch1992260-273] Britain and France did not act on their declarations immediately, and on 17 September, Soviet forces invaded eastern Poland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHakim1995-274]



Hitler reviews troops on the march during the [Invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), September 1939

The fall of Poland was followed by what contemporary journalists dubbed the "[Phoney War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoney_War)" or *Sitzkrieg* ("sitting war"). Hitler instructed the two newly appointed [Gauleiters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauleiter) of north-western Poland, [Albert Forster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Forster) of [Reichsgau Danzig-West Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsgau_Danzig-West_Prussia) and [Arthur Greiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Greiser) of [Reichsgau Wartheland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsgau_Wartheland), to [Germanise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanise) their areas, with "no questions asked" about how this was accomplished.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERees1997141–145-275] In Forster's area, ethnic Poles merely had to sign forms stating that they had German blood.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008527-276] In contrast, Greiser agreed with Himmler and carried out an [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) campaign towards Poles. Greiser soon complained that Forster was allowing thousands of Poles to be accepted as "racial" Germans and thus endangered German "racial purity".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERees1997141–145-275] Hitler refrained from getting involved. This inaction has been cited as an example of the theory of "working towards the Führer", in which Hitler issued vague instructions and expected his subordinates to develop policies independently.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERees1997141–145-275][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWelch200188–89-277]

Another dispute pitched one side represented by [Heinrich Himmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Himmler) and Greiser, who championed ethnic cleansing in Poland, against another represented by Göring and Hans Frank ([governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government) of [occupied Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\))), who called for turning Poland into the "granary" of the Reich. On 12 February 1940, the dispute was initially settled in favour of the Göring–Frank view, which ended the economically disruptive mass expulsions. On 15 May 1940, Himmler issued a memo entitled "Some Thoughts on the Treatment of Alien Population in the East", calling for the expulsion of the entire Jewish population of Europe into Africa and the reduction of the Polish population to a "leaderless class of labourers". Hitler called Himmler's memo "good and correct", and, ignoring Göring and Frank, implemented the Himmler–Greiser policy in Poland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERees1997148–149-278]

On 9 April, German forces [invaded Denmark and Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Weser%C3%BCbung). On the same day, Hitler proclaimed the birth of the [Greater Germanic Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Germanic_Reich), his vision of a united empire of Germanic nations of Europe in which the Dutch, Flemish, and Scandinavians were joined into a "racially pure" polity under German leadership.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWinkler200774-279] In May 1940, Germany [attacked France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France), and conquered [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Luxembourg_in_World_War_II), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Netherlands), and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Belgium). These victories prompted Mussolini to have Italy join forces with Hitler on 10 June. France and Germany signed an [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_22_June_1940) on 22 June.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960696–730-280] Kershaw notes that Hitler's popularity within Germany—and German support for the war—reached its peak when he returned to Berlin on 6 July from his tour of Paris.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008562-281] Following the unexpected swift victory, Hitler promoted 12 generals to the rank of [field marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_marshal) during the [1940 Field Marshal Ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Field_Marshal_Ceremony).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeighton20087–9-282][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis199394-283]

Britain, whose troops were forced to evacuate France by sea from [Dunkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkirk_evacuation),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960731–737-284] continued to fight alongside other British [dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) in the [Battle of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic). Hitler made peace overtures to the new British prime minister, [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill), and upon their rejection, he ordered a series of aerial attacks on [Royal Air Force](25679.md) airbases and radar stations in [southeast England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_England). On 7 September, the systematic nightly bombing of London began. The German Luftwaffe failed to defeat the Royal Air Force in what became known as the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960774–782-285] By the end of September, Hitler realised that air superiority for the invasion of Britain (in [Operation Sea Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sea_Lion)) could not be achieved, and ordered the operation postponed. The [nightly air raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blitz) on British cities intensified and continued for months, including London, [Plymouth](23508.md), and [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008563,_569,_570-286]

On 27 September 1940, the [Tripartite Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Pact) was signed in Berlin by [Saburō Kurusu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabur%C5%8D_Kurusu) of [Imperial Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japan), Hitler, and Italian foreign minister Ciano,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008580-287] and later expanded to include Hungary, Romania, and [Bulgaria](3415.md), thus yielding the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers). Hitler's attempt to integrate the Soviet Union into the anti-British bloc failed after inconclusive talks between Hitler and [Molotov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Molotov) in Berlin in November, and he ordered preparations for the invasion of the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoberts200658–60-288]

In early 1941, German forces were deployed to North Africa, the [Balkans](4829.md), and the Middle East. In February, [German forces arrived in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sonnenblume) to bolster the Italian presence. In April, Hitler launched the [invasion of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Yugoslavia), quickly followed by the [invasion of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Greece).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008604–605-289] In May, German forces were sent to support [Iraqi forces fighting against the British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War) and to [invade Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Crete).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKurowski2005141–142-290] On 28 November, Hitler met in Berlin with [Amin al-Husseini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amin_al-Husseini), the [Grand Mufti of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mufti_of_Jerusalem).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEApter2008-291] Hitler framed opposition to a [Jewish homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people) as part of Germany's broader "struggle against the Jews."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPorat2015-292]

### Path to defeat



Hitler [announcing the declaration of war against the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_declaration_of_war_against_the_United_States) to the *Reichstag* on 11 December 1941

On 22 June 1941, contravening the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact) of 1939, over three million Axis troops attacked the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMineau20041-293] This offensive (codenamed [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa)) was intended to destroy the Soviet Union and seize its natural resources for subsequent aggression against the Western powers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlantz20019-294][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoch1988-295] The action was also part of the overall plan to obtain more living space for German people; and Hitler thought a successful invasion would force Britain to negotiate a surrender.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2008162–163-296] The invasion conquered a huge area, including the [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_region) republics, [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), and West [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). By early August, Axis troops had advanced 500 km (310 miles) and won the [Battle of Smolensk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Smolensk_\(1941\)). Hitler ordered [Army Group Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_Centre) to temporarily halt its advance to Moscow and divert its Panzer groups to aid in the [encirclement of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad) and [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kiev_\(1941\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStolfi1982-297] His generals disagreed with this change, having advanced within 400 km (250 miles) of Moscow, and his decision caused a crisis among the military leadership.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilt1981-298][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2008202-299] The pause provided the Red Army with an opportunity to mobilise fresh reserves; the historian Russel Stolfi considers it to be one of the major factors that caused the failure of the Moscow offensive, which was resumed in October 1941 and [ended disastrously in December](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStolfi1982-297] During this crisis, Hitler appointed himself as head of the *[Oberkommando des Heeres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkommando_des_Heeres)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2008210-300]

On 7 December 1941, Japan [attacked the American fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) based at [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor), Hawaii. Four days later, Hitler [declared war against the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_declaration_of_war_against_the_United_States).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960900–901-301] On 18 December 1941, Himmler asked Hitler, "What to do with the Jews of Russia?", to which Hitler replied, *"als Partisanen auszurotten"* ("exterminate them as partisans").[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBauer20005-302] The Israeli historian [Yehuda Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehuda_Bauer) has commented that the remark is probably as close as historians will ever get to a definitive order from Hitler for the genocide carried out during the Holocaust.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBauer20005-302]

In late 1942, German forces were defeated in the [Second Battle of El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960921-303] thwarting Hitler's plans to seize the [Suez Canal](29323.md) and the Middle East. Overconfident in his own military expertise following the earlier victories in 1940, Hitler became distrustful of his Army High Command and began to interfere in military and tactical planning, with damaging consequences.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000b417-304] In December 1942 and January 1943, Hitler's repeated refusal to allow their withdrawal at the [Battle of Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad) led to the near-destruction of the [6th Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Army_\(Wehrmacht\)). Over 200,000 Axis soldiers were killed, and 235,000 were taken prisoner.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2008419–420-305] Thereafter came a decisive strategic defeat at the [Battle of Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer19601006-306] Hitler's military judgement became increasingly erratic, and Germany's military and economic position deteriorated, as did Hitler's health.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''BBC_News''1999-307]



The destroyed map room at the [Wolf's Lair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf's_Lair), Hitler's eastern command post, after the [20 July plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_July_plot)

Following the [Allied invasion of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Sicily) in 1943, [Mussolini was removed from power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Fascist_regime_in_Italy) by King [Victor Emmanuel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_III) after a vote of no confidence of the [Grand Council of Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Council_of_Fascism). Marshal [Pietro Badoglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Badoglio), placed in charge of the government, soon [surrendered to the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Cassibile).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960996–1000-308] Throughout 1943 and 1944, the Soviet Union steadily forced Hitler's armies into retreat along the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)). On 6 June 1944, the Western Allied armies landed in northern France in one of the largest [amphibious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) operations in history, [Operation Overlord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Overlord).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer19601036-309] Many German officers concluded that defeat was inevitable and that continuing under Hitler's leadership would result in the [complete destruction of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_war).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971513–514-310]

Between 1939 and 1945, there were numerous plans to [assassinate Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_attempts_on_Adolf_Hitler), some of which proceeded to significant degrees.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008544–547,_821–822,_827–828-311] The most well-known and significant, the [20 July plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_July_plot) of 1944, came from within Germany and was at least partly driven by the increasing prospect of a German defeat in the war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008816–818-312] Part of [Operation Valkyrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Valkyrie), the plot involved [Claus von Stauffenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_von_Stauffenberg) planting a bomb in one of [Hitler's headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrer_Headquarters), the [Wolf's Lair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf's_Lair) at [Rastenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastenburg). Hitler narrowly survived because the staff officer [Heinz Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Brandt) moved the briefcase containing the bomb behind a leg of the heavy conference table, which deflected much of the blast. Later, Hitler ordered reprisals, resulting in the execution of more than 4,900 people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer19601048–1072-313] Hitler was put on the [United Nations War Crimes Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_War_Crimes_Commission)'s first list of [war criminals](48626.md) in December 1944, after determining that Hitler could be held criminally responsible for the acts of the Nazis in occupied countries. By March 1945, at least seven indictments had been filed against him.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlesch2017158-314]

### Defeat and death

Main article: [Death of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Adolf_Hitler)



Front page of the US Armed Forces newspaper, *[Stars and Stripes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stars_and_Stripes_\(newspaper\))*, 2 May 1945, announcing Hitler's death. It erroneously states that Hitler died on 1 May; he died on 30 April.

By late 1944, both the Red Army and the [Western Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Allies) were advancing into Germany. Recognising the strength and determination of the Red Army, Hitler decided to use his remaining mobile reserves against the American and British armies, which he perceived as far weaker.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeinberg1964-315] On 16 December, he launched the [Ardennes Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes_Offensive) to incite disunity among the Western Allies and perhaps convince them to join his fight against the Soviets.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrandell1987-316] After some temporary successes, the offensive failed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962778-317] With much of Germany in ruins in January 1945, Hitler spoke on the radio: "However grave as the crisis may be at this moment, it will, despite everything, be mastered by our unalterable will."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReesKershaw2012-318] On 19 March, Hitler commented that the needs of the German population could now be disregarded, because they "had proven to be the weaker, and the future belongs solely to the stronger eastern nation. In any case only those who are inferior will remain after this struggle, for the good have already been killed".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971557-319] The same day, Hitler [ordered the destruction of all German industrial infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero_Decree) before it could fall into Allied hands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962774–775-320] Minister for Armaments [Albert Speer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Speer) was entrusted with executing this [scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth) policy, but he secretly disobeyed the order.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962774–775-320][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESereny1996497–498-321] Hitler's hope to negotiate peace with the United States and Britain was encouraged by the death of US president [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) on 12 April 1945, but contrary to his expectations, this caused no rift among the Allies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrandell1987-316][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962753,_763,_780–781-322]

On 20 April, his 56th birthday, Hitler made his last trip from the *Führerbunker* to the surface. In the ruined garden of the Reich Chancellery, he awarded Iron Crosses to boy soldiers of the [Hitler Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_Youth), who were now fighting the Red Army at the front near Berlin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeevor2002251-323] By 21 April, [Georgy Zhukov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Zhukov)'s [1st Belorussian Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Belorussian_Front) had broken through the defences of General [Gotthard Heinrici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Heinrici)'s [Army Group Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_Vistula) during the [Battle of the Seelow Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Seelow_Heights) and advanced to the outskirts of Berlin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeevor2002255–256-324] In denial about the dire situation, Hitler placed his hopes on the undermanned and under-equipped *Armeeabteilung Steiner* ([Army Detachment Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Detachment_Steiner)), commanded by [Felix Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Steiner). Hitler ordered Steiner to attack the northern flank of the [salient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salients,_re-entrants_and_pockets), while the German [Ninth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Army_\(Wehrmacht\)) was ordered to attack northward in a [pincer attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pincer_attack).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELe_Tissier201045-325]

During a military conference on 22 April, Hitler enquired about Steiner's offensive. He was informed that the attack had not been launched and that the Soviets had entered Berlin. Hitler ordered everyone but Wilhelm Keitel, [Alfred Jodl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Jodl), [Hans Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Krebs_\(Wehrmacht_general\)), and [Wilhelm Burgdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Burgdorf) to leave the room,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDollinger1995231-326] then launched into a tirade against the perceived treachery and incompetence of his generals, culminating in his declaration—for the first time—that "everything is lost".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJones1989-327] He announced that he would stay in Berlin until the end and then shoot himself.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeevor2002275-328]

By 23 April, the Red Army had surrounded Berlin,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZiemke196992-329] and Goebbels made a proclamation urging its citizens to defend the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDollinger1995231-326] That same day, Göring sent a telegram from [Berchtesgaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berchtesgaden), arguing that as Hitler was isolated in Berlin, Göring should assume leadership of Germany. Göring set a deadline, after which he would consider Hitler incapacitated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962787-330] Hitler responded by having Göring arrested, and in his [last will and testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_will_and_testament_of_Adolf_Hitler) of 29 April, he removed Göring from all government positions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962787,_795-331][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEButlerYoung1989227–228-332] On 28 April, Hitler discovered that Himmler, who had left Berlin on 20 April, was attempting to negotiate a surrender to the Western Allies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008923–925,_943-333][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962791-334] He considered this treason and ordered Himmler's arrest. He also ordered the execution of [Hermann Fegelein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Fegelein), Himmler's SS representative at Hitler's headquarters in Berlin, for desertion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962792,_795-335]

After midnight on the night of 28–29 April, Hitler married Eva Braun in a small civil ceremony in the *Führerbunker*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeevor2002343-336][^cite_note-will_and_marriage-337] Later that afternoon, Hitler was informed that [Mussolini had been executed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Benito_Mussolini) by the [Italian resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement) on the previous day; this is believed to have increased his determination to avoid capture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962798-338][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2008723–724-339] On 30 April, Soviet troops were within five hundred metres of the Reich Chancellery when Hitler shot himself in the head and Braun bit into a [cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanide) capsule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELinge2009199-340][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoachimsthaler1999160–182-341] In accordance with Hitler's wishes, their corpses were carried outside to the garden behind the Reich Chancellery, where they were placed in a bomb crater, doused with petrol, and set on fire as the Red Army shelling continued.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELinge2009200-342][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962799–800-343][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoachimsthaler1999217–220,_224–225-344] Grand Admiral [Karl Dönitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_D%C3%B6nitz) and Goebbels assumed Hitler's roles as head of state and chancellor respectively.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008949–950-345] On the evening of 1 May, Goebbels and his wife, [Magda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magda_Goebbels), committed suicide in the Reich Chancellery garden, after having poisoned their six children with cyanide.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer19601136-346]

[Berlin surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin) on 2 May. The remains of the Goebbels family, General [Hans Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Krebs_\(Wehrmacht_general\)) (who had committed suicide that day), General [Wilhelm Burgdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Burgdorf), and Hitler's dog [Blondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blondi) were repeatedly buried and exhumed by the Soviets.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVinogradov2005111,_333-347][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBezymenski196897–99-348] Hitler's and Braun's remains were alleged to have been moved as well, but this is most likely Soviet [disinformation](142690.md). There is no evidence that any identifiable remains of Hitler or Braun—with the exception of dental bridges—were ever found by them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoachimsthaler1999215–225-349][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFest2004163–164-350][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000b1110-351] While news of Hitler's death spread quickly, a [death certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_certificate) was not issued until 1956, after a lengthy investigation to collect testimony from 42 witnesses. Hitler's death was entered as an [assumption of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presumption_of_death) based on this testimony.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoachimsthaler19998–13-352]

## The Holocaust

Main articles: [The Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) and [Final Solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Solution)

> [If the international Jewish financiers in and outside Europe should succeed in plunging the nations once more into a world war, then the result will not be the Bolshevisation of the earth, and thus the victory of Jewry, but the annihilation of the Jewish race in Europe!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler's_prophecy)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarrus200037-353]

— Adolf Hitler, [30 January 1939 Reichstag speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_January_1939_Reichstag_speech)



A mass grave at [Bergen-Belsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen-Belsen) after the camp's liberation, April 1945

The Holocaust and Germany's war in the East were based on Hitler's long-standing view that the Jews were the enemy of the German people, and that *[Lebensraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum)* was needed for Germany's expansion. He focused on Eastern Europe for this expansion, aiming to defeat Poland and the Soviet Union and then removing or killing the Jews and [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGellately1996-354] The *[Generalplan Ost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalplan_Ost)* (General Plan East) called for deporting the population of occupied Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union to West Siberia, for use as slave labour or to be murdered;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnyder2010416-355] the conquered territories were to be colonised by German or "Germanised" settlers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteinberg1995-356] The goal was to implement this plan after the conquest of the Soviet Union, but when this failed, Hitler moved the plans forward.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnyder2010416-355][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008683-357] By January 1942, he had decided that the Jews, Slavs, and other deportees considered undesirable should be killed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960965-358][^cite_note-recent_scholarship-359]

The genocide was organised and executed by [Heinrich Himmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Himmler) and [Reinhard Heydrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Heydrich). The records of the [Wannsee Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wannsee_Conference), held on 20 January 1942 and led by Heydrich, with 15 senior Nazi officials participating, provide the clearest evidence of systematic planning for the Holocaust. On 22 February, Hitler was recorded saying, "we shall regain our health only by eliminating the Jews".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaimark200281-360] Similarly, at a meeting in July 1941 with leading functionaries of the Eastern territories, Hitler said that the easiest way to quickly [pacify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacification_\(military_action\)) the areas would be best achieved by "shooting everyone who even looks odd".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELongerich2005116-361] Although no direct order from Hitler authorising the mass killings has surfaced,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMegargee2007146-362] his public speeches and the diaries of Nazi officials demonstrate that he conceived and authorised the extermination of European Jewry.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELongerich2003chapter_15-363][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELongerich2003chapter_17-364] During the war, Hitler repeatedly stated his [prophecy of 1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler's_prophecy) was being fulfilled, namely, that a world war would bring about the annihilation of the Jewish race.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000b459–462-365] Hitler approved the *[Einsatzgruppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzgruppen)*—killing squads that followed the German army through Poland, the Baltic, and the Soviet Union[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008670–675-366]—and was well informed about their activities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELongerich2003chapter_15-363][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMegargee2007144-367] By summer 1942, [Auschwitz concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp) was expanded to accommodate large numbers of deportees for murder or [enslavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008687-368] Scores of other concentration camps and satellite camps were set up throughout Europe, with [several camps devoted exclusively to extermination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extermination_camp).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2008366map-369]

Between 1939 and 1945, the *[Schutzstaffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel)* (SS), assisted by [collaborationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany_and_Fascist_Italy) governments and recruits from occupied countries, were responsible for the deaths of at least 11 million non-combatants,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERummel1994112\[httpwwwhawaiiedupowerkillsNAZISTAB11GIF_table\]-370][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnyder2010416-355] including the murders of about six million Jews (representing two-thirds of the Jewish population of Europe),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''United_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum''2024-371][^cite_note-373] and between 200,000 and 1,500,000 [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porajmos).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHancock2004383–396-374][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''United_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum''2024-371] The victims were killed in concentration and extermination camps and in [ghettos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghettos_in_Nazi-occupied_Europe), and through mass shootings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960946-375][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans200815-376] Many victims of the Holocaust were murdered in [gas chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber) or shot, while others died of starvation or disease or [while working as slave labourers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extermination_through_labour).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960946-375][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans200815-376] In addition to eliminating Jews, the Nazis planned to reduce the population of the conquered territories by 30 million people through starvation in an action called the [Hunger Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunger_Plan). Food supplies would be diverted to the German army and German civilians. Cities would be razed, and the land allowed to return to forest or resettled by German colonists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnyder2010162–163,_416-377] Together, the Hunger Plan and *Generalplan Ost* would have led to the starvation of 80 million people in the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorland20096-378] These partially fulfilled plans resulted in additional deaths, bringing the total number of civilians and prisoners of war who died in the [democide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democide) to an estimated 19.3 million people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERummel1994112\[httpwwwhawaiiedupowerkillsNAZISTAB11GIF_table\]-370]

Hitler's policies resulted in the killing of nearly two million non-Jewish [Polish civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''United_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum''n.d.-379] over three million [Soviet prisoners of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_mistreatment_of_Soviet_prisoners_of_war),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnyder2010184-380] communists and other political opponents, [homosexuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_homosexual_men_in_Nazi_Germany), the physically and mentally disabled,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENiewykNicosia200045-381][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoldhagen1996290-382] [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Jehovah's_Witnesses_in_Nazi_Germany), [Adventists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventists), and trade unionists. Hitler never spoke publicly about the killings and seems to have never visited the concentration camps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDowning200533-383] The Nazis embraced the concept of [racial hygiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_hygiene). On 15 September 1935, Hitler presented two laws—known as the [Nuremberg Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws)—to the Reichstag. The laws banned sexual relations and marriages between Aryans and Jews and were later extended to include "Gypsies, Negroes or their bastard offspring".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGellately2001216-384] The laws stripped all non-Aryans of their German citizenship and forbade the employment of non-Jewish women under the age of 45 in Jewish households.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw1999567–568-385] Hitler's early [eugenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_eugenics) policies targeted children with physical and developmental disabilities in a programme dubbed [Action Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_euthanasia_in_Nazi_Germany), and he later authorised a [euthanasia](9587.md) programme for adults with serious mental and physical disabilities, now referred to as *[Aktion T4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktion_T4)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery2005252-386]

## Leadership style

Hitler ruled the Nazi Party [autocratically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocratic) by asserting the *[Führerprinzip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrerprinzip)* (leader principle). The principle relied on absolute obedience of all subordinates to their superiors; thus, he viewed the government structure as a pyramid, with himself—the [infallible leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaderism)—at the apex. Rank in the party was not determined by elections—positions were filled through appointment by those of higher rank, who demanded unquestioning obedience to the will of the leader.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008170,_172,_181-387] Hitler's leadership style was to give contradictory orders to his subordinates and to place them into positions where their duties and responsibilities overlapped with those of others, to have "the stronger one \[do] the job".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971281-388] In this way, Hitler fostered distrust, competition, and infighting among his subordinates to consolidate and maximise his own power. [His cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_Cabinet) never met after 1938, and he discouraged his ministers from meeting independently.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEManvellFraenkel200729-389][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008323-390] Hitler typically did not give written orders; instead, he communicated verbally, or had them conveyed through his close associate [Martin Bormann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bormann).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008377-391] He entrusted Bormann with his paperwork, appointments, and personal finances; Bormann used his position to control the flow of information and access to Hitler.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971333-392]

Hitler dominated his country's war effort during World War II to a greater extent than any other national leader. He strengthened his control of the armed forces in 1938, and subsequently made all major decisions regarding Germany's military strategy. His decision to mount a risky series of offensives against Norway, France, and the Low Countries in 1940, against the advice of the military, proved successful, though the diplomatic and military strategies he employed in attempts to force the United Kingdom out of the war ended in failure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery2005a421–425-393] Hitler deepened his involvement in the war effort by appointing himself commander-in-chief of the Army in December 1941; from this point forward, he personally directed the war against the Soviet Union, while his military commanders facing the Western Allies retained a degree of autonomy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2012169–170-394] Hitler's leadership became increasingly disconnected from reality as the war turned against Germany, with the military's defensive strategies often hindered by his slow decision-making and frequent directives to hold untenable positions. Nevertheless, he continued to believe that only his leadership could deliver victory.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOvery2005a421–425-393] In the final months of the war, Hitler refused to consider peace negotiations, regarding the destruction of Germany as preferable to surrender.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2012396–397-395] The military did not challenge Hitler's dominance of the war effort, and senior officers generally supported and enacted his decisions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008171–395-396]

## Personal life

### Family

Main article: [Hitler family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_family)

See also: [Sexuality of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexuality_of_Adolf_Hitler)



Hitler and Braun in 1942

Hitler created a public image as a celibate man without a domestic life, dedicated entirely to his political mission and the nation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960130-157][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999563-397] He met his lover, Eva Braun, in 1929,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008378-398] and married her on 29 April 1945, one day before they both committed suicide.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008947–948-399] In September 1931, his half-niece, [Geli Raubal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geli_Raubal), committed suicide with Hitler's gun in his Munich apartment. It was rumoured among contemporaries that Geli was in a romantic relationship with him, and her death was a source of deep, lasting pain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962393–394-400] [Paula Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Hitler), the younger sister of Hitler and the last living member of his immediate family, died in June 1960.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw20084-21]

### Views on religion

Main article: [Religious views of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_views_of_Adolf_Hitler)

Hitler was born to a practising [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) mother and an [anti-clerical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clerical) father; after leaving home, Hitler never again attended [Mass](5684685.md) or received the [sacraments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacraments_of_the_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw20085-401][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERißmann200194–96-402][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToland19929–10-403] Albert Speer stated that Hitler railed against the church to his political associates, and though he never officially left the church, he had no attachment to it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971141–142-404] Speer added that Hitler felt that in the absence of organised religion, people would turn to mysticism, which he considered regressive.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971141–142-404] According to Speer, Hitler believed that [Japanese religious beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Japan) or [Islam](6037917.md) would have been a more suitable religion for Germans than Christianity, with its "meekness and flabbiness".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971143-405] The historian [John S. Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_S._Conway_\(historian\)) states that Hitler was fundamentally opposed to the Christian churches.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConway19683-406] According to Bullock, Hitler did not believe in God, was anticlerical, and held Christian ethics in contempt because they contravened his preferred view of "[survival of the fittest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_of_the_fittest)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999385,_389-407] He favoured aspects of [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) that suited his own views, and adopted some elements of the Catholic Church's hierarchical organisation, [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy), and phraseology.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERißmann200196-408] In a 1932 speech, Hitler stated that he was not a Catholic, and declared himself a [German Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Christians_\(movement\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeirGreenberg2022694-409] According to Albert Speer, even after 1942 Hitler maintained the hope that "Through me the [Evangelical Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) could become the established church, as in England."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971142-410]

Hitler viewed the church as an important politically conservative influence on society,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971141-411] and he adopted a strategic relationship with it that "suited his immediate political purposes".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConway19683-406] In public, Hitler often praised Christian heritage and German Christian culture, though professing a belief in an "Aryan Jesus" who fought against the Jews.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteigmann-Gall200327,_108-412] However, in *[Hitler’s Table Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler%E2%80%99s_Table_Talk)* he was recorded as saying Christianity was an "absurdity"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler200059-413] and nonsense founded on lies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitler2000342-414]

Shirer commented that "the Nazi regime intended to destroy Christianity in Germany, if it could, and substitute the old paganism of the early tribal Germanic gods with the new paganism of the Nazi extremists".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer1960240-415] According to Bullock, Hitler wanted to wait until after the war before executing this plan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962219,_389-416] [Richard Steigmann-Gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Steigmann-Gall) has concluded there was no firm evidence of such a plan to replace Christianity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteigmann-Gall2003259-417] Swedish historian Mikael Nilsson notes that many of Hitler's religious statements are sourced from *Table Talk*, which he considers highly unreliable: much of the content was written days after the event and was heavily modified to suit the agendas of [Martin Bormann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bormann) and later [François Genoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Genoud).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENilsson202023–24,_51,_113,_191–192-418] Speer wrote that Hitler had a negative view of Himmler's and [Alfred Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Rosenberg)'s mystical notions and Himmler's attempt to mythologise the SS. Hitler was more pragmatic, and his ambitions centred on more practical concerns.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971141,_171,_174-419][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999729-420]

### Health

Main article: [Health of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_of_Adolf_Hitler)

See also: [Psychopathography of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathography_of_Adolf_Hitler)

Researchers have variously suggested that Hitler suffered from [irritable bowel syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irritable_bowel_syndrome), [skin lesions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_lesion), [irregular heartbeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irregular_heartbeat), [coronary sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_sclerosis),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans2008508-421] [Parkinson's disease](22228064.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''BBC_News''1999-307][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962717-422] [syphilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syphilis),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1962717-422] [giant-cell arteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant-cell_arteritis),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERedlich1993-423] [tinnitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinnitus),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERedlich2000129–190-424] and [monorchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monorchism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''The_Guardian''2015-425]

In a report prepared for the OSS in 1943, [Walter Charles Langer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Charles_Langer) of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) described Hitler as a "neurotic [psychopath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopath)" and speculated that he exhibited masochistic tendencies and deep feelings of self-loathing, which he projected onto others, particularly Jews.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanger1972126-426][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFuchs200058-427]

In his 1977 book *[The Psychopathic God: Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Psychopathic_God:_Adolf_Hitler)*, the historian [Robert G. L. Waite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_G._L._Waite) proposes that Hitler suffered from [borderline personality disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borderline_personality_disorder).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWaite1993356-428] The historians Henrik Eberle and Hans-Joachim Neumann consider that while he suffered from several illnesses, including Parkinson's disease, Hitler did not experience pathological delusions and was always fully aware of, and therefore responsible for, his decisions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGunkel2010-429][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJones1989-327]

Sometime in the 1930s, [Hitler adopted a mainly vegetarian diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler_and_vegetarianism),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullock1999388-430][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToland1992256-431] avoiding all meat and fish from 1942 onwards. At social events, he sometimes gave graphic accounts of the slaughter of animals in an effort to make his guests shun meat.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilson1998-432] Bormann had a greenhouse constructed near the [Berghof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berghof_\(residence\)) (near [Berchtesgaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berchtesgaden)) to ensure a steady supply of fresh fruit and vegetables for Hitler.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGovern196832–33-433] Hitler stopped drinking alcohol around the time he became vegetarian and thereafter only very occasionally drank beer or wine on social occasions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELinge200938-434][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitlerTrevor-Roper1988176,_22_January_1942-435] He was a non-smoker for most of his adult life, but smoked heavily in his youth (25 to 40 cigarettes a day); he eventually quit, calling the habit "a waste of money".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEProctor1999219-436] He encouraged his close associates to quit by offering a gold watch to anyone able to break the habit.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToland1992741-437] Hitler began using [amphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphetamine) occasionally after 1937 and became addicted to it in late 1942.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHestonHeston1980125–142-438] Speer linked this use of amphetamine to Hitler's increasingly erratic behaviour and inflexible decision-making (for example, rarely allowing military retreats).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHestonHeston198011–20-439]

Prescribed 90 medications during the war years by his personal physician, [Theodor Morell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Morell), Hitler took many pills each day for chronic stomach problems and other ailments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008782-440] He regularly consumed [amphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphetamine), [barbiturates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbiturate), [opiates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opiate), and [cocaine](7701.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhaemi2011190–191-441] as well as [potassium bromide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_bromide) and [atropa belladonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atropa_belladonna) (the latter in the form of [Doktor Koster's Antigaspills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doktor_Koster's_Antigaspills)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDoyle20058-442] He suffered [ruptured eardrums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruptured_eardrum) as a result of the [20 July plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_July_plot) bomb blast in 1944, and 200 wood splinters had to be removed from his legs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELinge2009156-443] Newsreel footage of Hitler shows tremors in his left hand and a shuffling walk, which began before the war and worsened towards the end of his life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2008782-440] [Ernst-Günther Schenck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst-G%C3%BCnther_Schenck) and several other doctors who met Hitler in the last weeks of his life also formed a diagnosis of Parkinson's disease.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEO'Donnell200137-444]

## Legacy

Further information: [Historiography of Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography_of_Adolf_Hitler), [Consequences of Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consequences_of_Nazism), and [Neo-Nazism](54361.md)



Outside of a building in [Braunau am Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunau_am_Inn), Austria, where Hitler was born, is a [memorial stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_birthplace_memorial_stone) placed as a reminder of World War II. The inscription translates as:

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZialcita2019-445]

For peace, freedom\
&#x20;and democracy\
&#x20;never again fascism\
&#x20;millions of dead warn \[us]

According to the historian [Joachim Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Fest), Hitler's suicide was likened by numerous contemporaries to a "spell" being broken.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFest1974753-446] Similarly, Speer commented in *[Inside the Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_the_Third_Reich)* on his emotions the day after Hitler's suicide: "Only now was the spell broken, the magic extinguished."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpeer1971617-447] Public support for Hitler had collapsed by the time of his death, which few Germans mourned; Kershaw argues that most civilians and military personnel were too busy adjusting to the collapse of the country or fleeing from the fighting to take any interest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2012348–350-448] According to the historian [John Toland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Toland_\(historian\)), Nazism "burst like a bubble" without its leader.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToland1992892-449]

Kershaw describes Hitler as "the embodiment of modern political evil".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000bxvii-6] "Never in history has such ruination—physical and moral—been associated with the name of one man", he adds.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKershaw2000b841-450] Hitler's political programme brought about a world war, leaving behind a devastated and impoverished Eastern and Central Europe. Germany suffered wholesale destruction, characterised as *[Stunde Null](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunde_Null)* (Zero Hour).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFischer1995569-451] Hitler's policies inflicted human suffering on an unprecedented scale;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDel_TestaLemoineStrickland200383-452] according to [R. J. Rummel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._J._Rummel), the Nazi regime was responsible for the [democidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democidal) killing of an estimated 19.3 million civilians and prisoners of war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERummel1994112\[httpwwwhawaiiedupowerkillsNAZISTAB11GIF_table\]-370] In addition, 28.7 million soldiers and civilians died as a result of military action in the [European theatre of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_theatre_of_World_War_II).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERummel1994112\[httpwwwhawaiiedupowerkillsNAZISTAB11GIF_table\]-370] The number of civilians killed during the Second World War was unprecedented in the history of warfare.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurrayMillett2001554-453] Historians, philosophers, and politicians often use the word "evil" to describe the Nazi regime.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWelch20012-454] Many European countries have [criminalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_against_Holocaust_denial) both the promotion of Nazism and [Holocaust denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBazyler20061-455]

The historian [Friedrich Meinecke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Meinecke) described Hitler as "one of the great examples of the singular and incalculable power of personality in historical life".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShirer19606-19] The English historian [Hugh Trevor-Roper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Trevor-Roper) saw him as "among the 'terrible simplifiers' of history, the most systematic, the most historical, the most philosophical, and yet the coarsest, cruelest, least magnanimous conqueror the world has ever known".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitlerTrevor-Roper1988xxxv-456] For the historian [John M. Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Roberts_\(historian\)), Hitler's defeat marked the end of a phase of European history dominated by Germany.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoberts1996501-457] In its place emerged the [Cold War](325329.md), a global confrontation between the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc), dominated by the United States and other [NATO](21133.md) nations, and the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc), dominated by the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELichtheim1974366-458] The historian [Sebastian Haffner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Haffner) asserted that without Hitler and the displacement of the Jews, the modern nation-state of Israel would not exist. He contends that without Hitler, the [de-colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-colonisation) of former European spheres of influence would have been postponed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaffner1979100–101-459] Further, Haffner claimed that other than [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), Hitler had a more significant impact than any other comparable historical figure, in that he too caused a wide range of worldwide changes in a relatively short time span.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaffner1979100-460]
