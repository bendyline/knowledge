Brazilian visa

Visitors to **[Brazil](3383.md)** must obtain a [visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_\(document\)) from one of the [Brazilian diplomatic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Brazil) unless they are nationals of one of the visa-exempt countries or have the option to obtain an electronic visa. Visa exemptions to enter Brazil have generally been [reciprocal](1046231.md), on the principle that the other country should similarly offer visa-free entry to Brazilian nationals.[^cite_note-folha-1][^cite_note-interpreter-2]

For stays longer than 90 days or for employment in Brazil, all foreign nationals must have a visa or residency authorization.&#x20;

Visitors entering Brazil under a visa exemption are subject to the same restrictions as those entering Brazil with a visitor visa (for tourism, business, transit, artistic and sport activities, without payment from Brazilian sources).[^cite_note-decree-122] They are required to hold a ticket for entering and leaving Brazil, proof of accommodation or a notarized invitation letter, and proof of funds of at least [R\$](34201323.md)248 per day.[^cite_note-123]

Visas are not required for airport transit, from any nationality, as long as the traveler does not leave the international transit area.[^cite_note-decree-122]

Nationals of Brazil who also have another nationality are allowed to enter and leave Brazil with the passport of the other country, and may apply for a Brazilian visa on such passport if needed, including an electronic visa if applicable for such nationality.[^cite_note-124] When entering Brazil, if they also present a [Brazilian identity card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_identity_card), they are admitted as Brazilians, otherwise they are admitted as foreigners, subject to the regular limitations as such.[^cite_note-125]

The Mexican government unilaterally suspended its visa exemption agreement with Brazil, requiring an electronic authorization for Brazilians to travel to [Mexico](3966054.md) from 11 December 2021,[^cite_note-mx2021-129] and a visa from 18 August 2022.[^cite_note-mx2022-130] However, the Brazilian government maintained the visa exemption for Mexicans to travel to Brazil at the time. In July 2023, both governments announced their intention to adopt electronic visas for each other's nationals.[^cite_note-mx2023-131] In September 2024, the Brazilian government contracted [VFS Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VFS_Global) for this purpose.[^cite_note-132] In September 2025, Brazil announced that it would implement electronic visas for Mexicans after Mexico did so for Brazilians.[^cite_note-133] Mexico implemented electronic visas for Brazilians on 5 February 2026.[^cite_note-134]

In March 2025, the [Brazilian Senate](6132308.md) approved a proposal to restore the unilateral visa exemption for nationals of [Australia](4689264.md), [Canada](5042916.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) (as well as [Japan](15573.md), which had a reciprocal exemption).[^cite_note-135] To take effect, the proposal would also need approval by the [Chamber of Deputies](1589485.md) but not by the [president](273638.md).[^cite_note-136]

### Reciprocity

Brazil has generally required [reciprocity](1046231.md) for visa exemptions, meaning that it would exempt from its visa requirement only nationals of countries that did not require a visa from Brazilians.[^cite_note-folha-1][^cite_note-interpreter-2] However, sometimes Brazil adopted unilateral visa exemptions for nationals of certain countries, or continued exemptions after other countries suspended reciprocity.

From 1956 to 1978, Brazil unilaterally exempted from its visa requirement the nationals of all countries in the [Americas](29833.md) that had diplomatic relations with it.[^cite_note-decree1955-23][^cite_note-decree1978-25]

Brazil unilaterally exempted nationals of Australia, Canada, Japan and the United States from its visa requirement from 1 June to 18 September 2016, to facilitate tourism during the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics) held there.[^cite_note-aucajpus2016-98] Under the presidency of [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md), Brazil again unilaterally exempted nationals of these four countries from its visa requirement on 17 June 2019.[^cite_note-aucajpus2019-109] After three extensions, the subsequent [Lula](99328.md) administration revoked the unilateral exemption on 10 April 2025.[^cite_note-aucaus2023-110]

In response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), [Ireland](14560.md) suspended its visa exemption for nationals of Brazil and other [South American](26769.md) countries from 28 January to 16 June 2021,[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] but Brazil maintained its visa exemption for nationals of Ireland during this period.

Mexico suspended its visa exemption agreement with Brazil and started requiring visas from Brazilians on 18 August 2022,[^cite_note-mx2021-129][^cite_note-mx2022-130] but Brazil maintained its visa exemption for Mexicans. Both countries intended to restore reciprocity with electronic visas.[^cite_note-mx2023-131]

## Visa types and requirements

### Visitor visa

The visitor visa (VIVIS) allows stays of up to 90 days, for the following purposes:[^cite_note-decree-122][^cite_note-145]

- Tourism, including cultural and recreational activities, family visits, attending conferences, volunteer work, research, study and teaching;

- Business, including meetings, events, reporting, filming, surveying, signing contracts, audits, consulting, airplane and ship crew;

- Transit;

- Artistic and sport activities.

Holders of visitor visas are not allowed to receive payment from Brazilian sources for the activities during their stay, except for daily allowances for living expenses, payments for entertainment performances, compensation for management of their own business, reimbursement of travel expenses, and competition prizes.[^cite_note-decree-122]

The visitor visa is usually valid for multiple entries during the visa validity period, which is generally one year but may be longer for some nationalities. Each stay is initially limited to 90 days, but an extension may be requested from the [Federal Police](2361410.md) after arrival. The combined stays must not exceed 180 days per any one-year period.[^cite_note-decree-122]

### Temporary visa

Many types of temporary visas (VITEM) are available, for stays longer than 90 days. Certain types of visas allow employment in Brazil. For some visas based on work or investment, the applicant must obtain authorization from the General Coordination of Immigration (CGIG) before requesting the visa.[^cite_note-decree-122]

All holders of temporary visas intending to stay for more than 90 days are required to register with the Federal Police within 90 days after arrival. After registration, they receive a national migration registration card (CRNM) and are granted residency for a certain period. In some cases this period may be "indeterminate" ([permanent residency](1284324.md)). Temporary residents may later apply to renew their residency period or convert it to permanent residency in some cases. Only the time spent as a permanent resident qualifies for [naturalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_nationality_law).[^cite_note-decree-122]

In addition to registration, a [taxpayer number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadastro_de_Pessoas_F%C3%ADsicas) (CPF) is required for various transactions, and a [work card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_card) (CTPS) is required for those who will be employed in Brazil.[^cite_note-146] These documents may be obtained in digital format online, free of charge.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

#### Humanitarian visa

VITEM III is a [humanitarian visa](50128538.md) for nationals or [stateless](789786.md) residents of countries experiencing serious instability, armed conflict, disaster or violations of human rights. Residency is granted initially for two years, after which the applicant may request permanent residency.[^cite_note-humanitarian-160]

Brazil has designated [Afghanistan](737.md) for this purpose.[^cite_note-161] Previous designations of [Haiti](13373.md),[^cite_note-162] [Syria](7515849.md)[^cite_note-163] and [Ukraine](31750.md)[^cite_note-164] ended in 2026 for new visas, but nationals of these countries who previously entered Brazil may continue to request residency under this category.[^cite_note-humanitarian-160]

#### Working holiday

VITEM VI is a [working holiday visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_holiday_visa), whose primary purpose must be tourism but paid work is also allowed. This visa is available only by international agreement with the country of nationality.[^cite_note-decree-122] Such agreements are in effect with [Australia](4689264.md),[^cite_note-165] [France](5843419.md),[^cite_note-166] [Germany](11867.md),[^cite_note-167] [New Zealand](4913064.md),[^cite_note-168] and [South Korea](27019.md),[^cite_note-169] and an agreement with [Japan](15573.md) is set to take effect on 1 December 2026.[^cite_note-170] These agreements require that the applicant be between ages 18 and 30 (up to age 34 for South Koreans), and allow a stay of up to one year.

#### Investment

VITEM IX is available for three types of investment. One type is for managers and executives whose companies invest at least [R\$](34201323.md)600,000 in a Brazilian company, or at least R\$150,000 and also generate at least 10 new jobs within two years.[^cite_note-171] Another type is for applicants who personally invest at least R\$500,000 in a Brazilian company, or at least R\$150,000 in research activities.[^cite_note-172] In both cases, the applicant is granted permanent residency from the start.

The other type of investment requires the personal purchase of urban real estate, for at least R\$700,000 if located in the [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil) or [Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Region,_Brazil) region, or at least R\$1 million if located in [another region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Brazil). In this case, residency is granted initially for four years, after which the applicant may request permanent residency.[^cite_note-173]

#### Family reunification

VITEM XI is available for spouses, domestic partners, children, grandchildren, parents, grandparents, dependent siblings and dependent stepchildren of a Brazilian national, or of a person holding or applying for Brazilian residency not also based on family reunification, and for [legal guardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_guardian) of a Brazilian national. For this visa, residency is granted initially for the same period as the family member. Applicants may request permanent residency when the family member acquires it or after four years of residency.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-ordinance3-2018-175]

#### International agreements

VITEM XIII is available for nationals of countries with residency agreements. Agreements providing permanent residency from the start are in effect with [Argentina](18951905.md)[^cite_note-176] and [Uruguay](31843.md).[^cite_note-177] A [Mercosur](25869317.md) agreement is also in effect with [Bolivia](3462.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Colombia](5222.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), [Paraguay](23420.md) and [Peru](170691.md), providing residency initially for two years, after which the applicant may request permanent residency.[^cite_note-178]

#### Immigration policy

VITEM XIV is available in various categories under the Brazilian immigration policy.

One category is for retirees and beneficiaries of survivor pensions who have a monthly income of at least [US\$](18717338.md)2,000 and can transfer it to Brazil. Residency is granted initially for two years, after which the applicant may request permanent residency.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

Another category is for [digital nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_nomad), who [work remotely](158555.md) for a foreign employer using [telecommunications](33094374.md) technology, with a monthly income of at least US\$1,500 from a foreign payer or bank funds of at least US\$18,000. Residency is granted for one year and may be renewed.[^cite_note-181]

Based on an agreement of the [Community of Portuguese Language Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Portuguese_Language_Countries), VITEM XIV is also available for nationals of [Angola](701.md), [Cape Verde](18962637.md), [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md), [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md), [Mozambique](19301.md), [Portugal](23033.md), [São Tomé and Príncipe](20026306.md) and [Timor-Leste](19159399.md) who are professors, researchers, technicians, teachers, businesspeople, artists, athletes, managers of cultural and sport events, and exchange students. Nationals of these countries who are already in Brazil, regardless of occupation or immigration status, may request residency, which is granted initially for two years, after which they may request permanent residency.[^cite_note-182]

#### Medical training

VITEM XV (VICAM) is available for medical doctors to participate in the [Mais Médicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mais_M%C3%A9dicos) medical training program. This visa allows a stay of up to four years, renewable for four more years.[^cite_note-183]

### Diplomatic, official and courtesy visas

Brazil issues diplomatic visas (VIDIP) to representatives of foreign governments or international organizations, as well as official visas (VISOF) to their staff. It also issues courtesy visas (VICOR) to notable people for unofficial trips, to family members and domestic workers of holders of diplomatic or official visas, and to artists and athletes for free cultural events.[^cite_note-decree-122]

### Accepted travel documents

For issuing visitor and temporary visas, Brazil accepts passports of all entities that have diplomatic relations with it (all [member states](31969.md) and [observer states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_observers) of the [United Nations](31769.md), and the [Order of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Malta)), as well as [Kosovo](17391.md) and [Taiwan](25734.md). If the applicant does not hold any of these passports, the visa is issued on a [laissez-passer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-passer).[^cite_note-visa-3]

For issuing diplomatic and official visas, Brazil only accepts passports of entities that have diplomatic relations with it.[^cite_note-visa-3]
