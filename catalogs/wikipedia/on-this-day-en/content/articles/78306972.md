Multiple [protests](49760486.md) have been held across the [United States](3434750.md) and other countries against U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) since his [2024 re-election](59568546.md).

Demonstrations have been held to protest and show disapproval for promises he made during his [2024 re-election campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign), proposals he has made to various domestic and international matters and decisions and policies he has signed off on.

While many protests and actions have been led by pre-existing organisations, there has also been a large amount of organising and attendance by the wider public alongside action taken during non-political events such as the [Super Bowl](27718.md).

### 2024

On November 6, the day after Trump's re-election, protesters gathered in [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California) and [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), California,[^cite_note-MercuryNews2024-1] [downtown Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Cleveland),[^cite_note-2] [Chicago](6886.md),[^cite_note-November2024-3] [New York City](645042.md),[^cite_note-November2024-3] and [Philadelphia](50585.md)[^cite_note-November2024-3] to protest [border militarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border#Border_enforcement) and the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-MercuryNews2024-1][^cite_note-November2024-3] Protesters outside the [Trump International Hotel and Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_International_Hotel_and_Tower_\(Chicago\)) in Chicago were filmed chanting "Donald Trump, you [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) clown."[^cite_note-November2024-3]

The [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) issued a statement on the same day indicating that they would continue to fight against Trump's rhetoric and unlawful agenda outlined in [Project 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_2025).[^cite_note-4]

### 2025

#### January–March



A [50501](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50501) protest against Trump in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio), on February 17, 2025

See also: [People's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_March), [Day Without Immigrants (2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_Without_Immigrants_\(2025\)), [50501 movement](79143789.md), [Response to the Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Response_to_the_Department_of_Government_Efficiency), [Economic Blackout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Blackout), and [Stand Up for Science 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_for_Science_2025)

Two days before [Trump's inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump), the [People's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_March) was held in all 50 states, as well as [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)[^cite_note-Brookings-5] The rally involved hundreds of events and thousands of participants.[^cite_note-Brookings-5][^cite_note-People's_March-6] Protesters focused on [abortion rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_rights), [immigration rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_reform_in_the_United_States), and the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-People's_March-6]

The [Day Without Immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_Without_Immigrants_\(2025\)) was held in cities across the nation in February 2025. Thousands of people protested [mass deportation of illegal immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_in_the_second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump) in [Dallas](53838.md) and Southern California, including in [Los Angeles](18110.md), where protesters blocked all lanes of [U.S. Route 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_101) and caused gridlock.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

On February 3, protesters gathered outside the [United States Office of Personnel Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Personnel_Management) to denounce Elon Musk's appointment to government as being a coup.[^cite_note-9] Multiple notable figures, including politicians like [Jared Golden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Golden)[^cite_note-10] and [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer)[^cite_note-11] have denounced Musk as an unelected official. [Yassamin Ansari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yassamin_Ansari) said "Never in my wildest dreams, could I have imagined that in the United States of America in the year 2025 we would be witnessing an unelected billionaire, Elon Musk, and [oligarchs](22315.md) and so many unelected individuals taking complete control of our [Federal Government](195149.md) \[...]"[^cite_note-12]

A series of protests against Trump, [Elon Musk](909036.md), and [Project 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_2025) were held on February 5, 2025. Referred to as [50501](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50501), short for "50 protests, 50 states, one day," the protests were largely held in front of state capitol buildings, though some were held in other cities.[^cite_note-13]

On February 9, more than 1000 protesters marched through downtown [San Diego](28504.md). The protest ended with a vigil at [St. Joseph Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph_Cathedral_\(San_Diego,_California\)), which fits 900 people. Every pew was filled. Roman Catholic Cardinal [Robert McElroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Walter_McElroy), who was recently elected [Archbishop of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Washington) (DC), led a vigil focusing on protecting immigrants in the afternoon. The predominantly Latino march was joined by U.S. Representative [Juan Vargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Vargas).[^cite_note-14]

#### January

Following [the US bombing of Venezuela and capture of its president on January 3](82021123.md), weekend protests were planned in over one hundred US cities.[^cite_note-30] Activist group The ANSWER Coalition set up a website listing protest locations and times.[^cite_note-31] Hundreds gathered in New York City's [Times Square](31389.md) at 2 p.m. Eastern Time. A poster read "No war on Venezuela! Stop the bombings!"[^cite_note-32] Approximately one hundred people protested in Cincinnati, carrying signs with slogans such as "No Blood for Oil" and "U.S. War Machine: Real Threat to Peace."[^cite_note-33]

Following the [killing of Renée Good](82055905.md) on January 7, 2026, by an ICE agent, [Americans protested her killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good_protests) and the Trump administration's immigration policy.[^cite_note-34] On January 23, 50,000 protestors marched and hundreds of businesses closed as a part of a [general strike](82198050.md) demanding ICE operations cease within the city. At least a hundred clergy members were arrested at a separate protest at [Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul_International_Airport).[^cite_note-35] The [killing of Alex Pretti](82203651.md) on January 24 also caused significant protests against the Trump administration.

#### May

On May 1, 2026, the [May Day](83082967.md) protests were held on [International Workers' Day](39353050.md) calling for better treatment of workers, and [against the Iran war](82544763.md) and the [fuel crisis](82827398.md) caused by the war.[^cite_note-38]

ICE agents advance on protesters in a [human chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock-on_\(protest_tactic\)), striking them with [batons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_\(law_enforcement\)) and pushing them back, May 27

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [2026 Delaney Hall hunger strike and protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delaney_Hall_hunger_strike_and_protests).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2026_Delaney_Hall_hunger_strike_and_protests\&action=edit)]

On May 22, 2026, in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), immigrants detained in [Delaney Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaney_Hall), a [private prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_prison) run by [GEO Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEO_Group) used for holding immigrants detained by [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE), held a month-long [hunger](222292.md) and [labor strike](155134.md) protesting conditions inside the facility. Detainees have been given expired food with worms or mold, sleep on the floor in overcrowded rooms, have been denied medical care for serious conditions, and have reportedly been abused by guards. Protests supporting the strikers escalated outside the facility with over 90 arrests. Conditions inside the facility worsened, with reports of [batons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_\(law_enforcement\)), [pepper spray](157302.md), and [tear gas](1202646.md) being used against strikers and multiple ambulances arriving at the facility throughout the strike. The strikes ended due to actions taken by guards to intimidate, segregate, and discipline strikers, such as by putting strike leaders in [solitary confinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitary_confinement), threatening to [force-feed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force-feeding) strikers, and transporting strikers to unknown facilities around the country.

Some protesters blocked ICE vehicles from entering or exiting the facility. ICE agents attacked protesters and journalists with batons, pepper spray, and [tasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taser). Local officials including Governor [Mikie Sherrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikie_Sherrill) were denied access to the facility, and Senator [Andy Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Kim) was pepper sprayed by ICE while trying to de-escalate.

The October 18 No Kings Protests was participated in internationally, in locations such as London, Madrid and Barcelona.[^cite_note-52]

In Paris, the American flag was also burned by protesters.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]
