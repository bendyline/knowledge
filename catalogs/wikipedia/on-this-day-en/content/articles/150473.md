**Shreveport** ([/ˈʃriːvpɔːrt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*SHREEV-port*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a city in the U.S. state of [Louisiana](18130.md). It is the [third-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Louisiana) in Louisiana after [New Orleans](53842.md) and [Baton Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_Rouge,_Louisiana). The bulk of Shreveport is in [Caddo Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo_Parish,_Louisiana), of which it is the parish seat.[^cite_note-GR6-5] It extends along the west bank of the [Red River](194242.md) into neighboring [Bossier Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish,_Louisiana). The [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census) tabulation for the city's population was 187,593,[^cite_note-2020_Census_(City)-6] while the [Shreveport–Bossier City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport%E2%80%93Bossier_City_metropolitan_area) had a population of 393,406.[^cite_note-7]

Shreveport was founded in 1836 by the Shreve Town Company, a corporation established to develop a town at the juncture of the newly navigable Red River and the [Texas Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Trail), an overland route into the newly independent [Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas).[^cite_note-8] It grew throughout the 20th century and, after the discovery of oil in Louisiana, became a national center for the oil industry. [Standard Oil of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil_of_Louisiana) and [United Gas Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Gas_Corporation) were headquartered in the city until the 1960s and 1980s, respectively. After the loss of jobs in the oil industry, the closure of [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors)' [Shreveport Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Operations), and other economic problems, it struggled with a declining population, poverty, drugs, and violent crime.[^cite_note-TIM-9] The city continues in its efforts to revitalize its infrastructure,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:2-11][^cite_note-12] revive the economy through diversification,[^cite_note-\:3-13][^cite_note-\:8-14][^cite_note-\:9-15] and lower crime.[^cite_note-\:12-16][^cite_note-17]

Shreveport is the educational, commercial and cultural center of the [Ark-La-Tex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark-La-Tex) region, where [Arkansas](1930.md), Louisiana, and [Texas](29810.md) meet. It is the location of [Centenary College of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenary_College_of_Louisiana), [Louisiana State University Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University_Shreveport), [Louisiana Tech University Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Tech_University), [Southern University at Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University_at_Shreveport), and [Louisiana Baptist University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Baptist_University). It forms part of the [I-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_20_in_Louisiana) Cyber Corridor linking Shreveport to [Dallas](53838.md) and [Atlanta](3138.md).[^cite_note-\:4-18][^cite_note-\:5-19][^cite_note-\:6-20][^cite_note-\:7-21] Companies with significant operations or headquarters in Shreveport are [Amazon](90451.md), [Regions Financial Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_Financial_Corporation), [JPMorgan Chase](231001.md), [Sam's Town Hotel and Gambling Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam's_Town_Hotel_and_Gambling_Hall,_Shreveport), [AT\&T Mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Mobility), [United Parcel Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Parcel_Service), [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart), [SWEPCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Electric_Power#Southwestern_Electric_Power_Company), [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric), [UOP LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UOP_LLC),[^cite_note-22] [Calumet Specialty Products Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calumet_Specialty_Products_Partners), and APS Payroll.

## History

Main articles: [History of Shreveport, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Shreveport,_Louisiana) and [Timeline of Shreveport, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Shreveport,_Louisiana)

### Early settlers

Shreveport was established to create a town at the meeting point of the Red River and the Texas Trail. The Red River was made navigable by Captain [Henry Miller Shreve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Miller_Shreve), who led the [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) efforts to clear it. A 180-mile-long (290 km) stretch of intermittent natural log jam, the [Great Raft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Raft), had previously obstructed passage to shipping. Shreve used a specially modified [riverboat](835031.md), the *Heliopolis*, to remove the log jam. The company and the village of Shreve Town were named in Shreve's honor.[^cite_note-23]

Shreve Town was originally contained within the boundaries of a piece of land sold to the company in 1835 by the indigenous [Caddo Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo_Indians). In 1838 [Caddo Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo_Parish) was created from the large [Natchitoches Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natchitoches_Parish), and Shreve Town became its parish seat. On March 20, 1839, the town was incorporated as Shreveport. Originally, the town consisted of 64 city blocks, created by eight streets running west from the Red River and eight streets running south from Cross Bayou, one of its tributaries.

Shreveport soon became a center of [steamboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat) commerce, carrying mostly cotton and agricultural crops from the plantations of Caddo Parish. Shreveport also had a [slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) market, though slave trading was not as widespread as in other parts of the state. By 1860, Shreveport had a population of 2,200 free people and 1,300 enslaved people within the city limits.

### Civil War and Reconstruction

During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), Shreveport was the capital of the [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) state of [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_in_the_American_Civil_War) from 1863 to 1865, having succeeded [Baton Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_Rouge,_Louisiana) and [Opelousas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opelousas,_Louisiana) after each fell under [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) control. The city was a Southern stronghold throughout the war and was the site of the headquarters of the [Trans-Mississippi Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Mississippi_Department) of the Confederate Army. Fort Albert Sidney Johnston was built on a ridge northwest of the city. Because of limited development in that area, the site is relatively undisturbed in the 21st century.

Isolated from events in the east, the Civil War continued in the Trans-Mississippi theater for several weeks after [Robert E. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Lee)'s surrender in April 1865, and the Trans-Mississippi was the last Confederate command to surrender, on May 26, 1865. "The period May 13–21, 1865, was filled with great uncertainly after soldiers learned of the surrenders of Lee and Johnston, the Good Friday assassination of President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) and the rapid departure of their own generals."[^cite_note-prime-24] In the confusion there was a breakdown of military discipline and rioting by soldiers. They destroyed buildings containing service records, a loss that later made it difficult for many to gain Confederate pensions from state governments.[^cite_note-prime-24]

The Red River, opened by Shreve in the 1830s, remained navigable throughout the Civil War. But seasonal water levels got so low at one point that Union [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [David Dixon Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Dixon_Porter) was trapped with his gunboats north of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Louisiana). His engineers quickly constructed a temporary dam to raise the water level and free his fleet.

In 1873, Shreveport lost 759 citizens in an 80-day period to a [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever) epidemic, with over 400 additional victims eventually succumbing. The total death toll from August through November was approximately 1,200.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] In aggregate it is estimated that about one quarter of the population of Shreveport was lost, making it one of the deadliest local epidemics in American history. About 800 were interred in a mass grave at Oakland Cemetery.[^cite_note-ST-27] Five Roman Catholic priests in the city and two religious sisters died while caring for yellow fever victims in the city.[^cite_note-28]

Providence Academy was established for African American students in the city.[^cite_note-29]

### 20th century to present

[Greenwood Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood_Cemetery_\(Shreveport,_Louisiana\)) was established in 1893. A number of local African American musicians became nationally famous. By the 1910s, [Huddie William Ledbetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_Belly)—also known as "Lead Belly", a [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues) singer and guitarist—was performing for Shreveport audiences in St. Paul's Bottoms, the notable [red-light district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-light_district) of Shreveport that operated legally from 1903 to 1917. Ledbetter began to develop his own style of music after exposure to a variety of musical influences on Fannin Street, a row of saloons, [brothels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothel), and dance halls in the Bottoms. Bluesmen [Jesse Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Thomas_\(musician\)), [Dave Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Alexander_\(blues_musician\)), and [Kenny Wayne Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Wayne_Shepherd), and the early [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) and [ragtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragtime) composers Bill Wray and Willian Christopher O'Hare were all from Shreveport. Lead Belly achieved international fame.

By 1914, neglect and lack of use, due to diversion of freight traffic to railroad lines, resulted in the Red River becoming unnavigable. In projects accomplished over decades, in 1994, the [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) restored navigability by completion of a series of federally funded lock-and-dam structures and a navigation channel.

As early as 1924, the citizens of Shreveport became interested in hosting a military flying field. In 1926, Shreveport citizens learned that the 3rd Attack Wing stationed at [Fort Crockett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Crockett), Texas, would be enlarged by 500 percent and would require at least 20,000 acres (81 km<sup>2</sup>) to support aerial gunnery and a bombing range. The efforts to procure the government's commitment to build the facility in the Greater Shreveport metropolitan area resulted in today's [Barksdale Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barksdale_Air_Force_Base).

In September, 1941, the capture of the city of Shreveport was the objective of a U.S. Army war game, or [military exercise](1225714.md), known as the [Louisiana Maneuvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Maneuvers). The field exercise's mission was accomplished largely due to General [George S. Patton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_S._Patton), who commanded the mock "Blue" army's 2nd Armored Division.[^cite_note-The_Louisiana_Maneuvers,_The_National_WWII_Museum_New_Orleans-30]



[Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley) at the Louisiana Hayride

Shreveport was home to the *[Louisiana Hayride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Hayride)* radio program, broadcast weekly from the [Shreveport Municipal Memorial Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Municipal_Memorial_Auditorium). During its heyday from 1948 to 1960, this [Country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music) program stimulated the careers of some of the greatest figures in American music. Among them were [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams) and [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley), who made his broadcasting debut there in 1954. In the mid-1950s, [KWKH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KWKH) was the first major radio station to feature the music of Presley on its long-running *Louisiana Hayride* program at the Shreveport Municipal Auditorium. Horace Logan, long-term KWKH program manager and originator of the *Hayride*, and Frank Page introduced Presley on the *Hayride*.

African American veterans of World War II were among activists in Shreveport through the 1960s who worked in the [civil rights movement](49001.md) to correct injustices under [Jim Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow) and the [disenfranchisement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disfranchisement_after_Reconstruction_era) of blacks. While activism gradually increased, 1963 was a particularly violent year in Shreveport because of white resistance. The Shreveport home of Dr. C. O. Simpkins was bombed in retaliation for his work with Dr. [Martin Luther King Jr.](20076.md)[^cite_note-holmes-31][^cite_note-galilee-32]

In September 1963 [George W. D'Artois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._D'Artois), Public Service Commissioner, refused a permit for a march to the Little Union Baptist Church in Shreveport, where mourners gathered to honor and commemorate four black girls killed in the [16th Street Baptist Church Bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_Street_Baptist_Church_Bombing) on September 15 in [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama). D'Artois and other officers entered the church on horseback and took out the pastor, Dr. Harry Blake, beating him severely.[^cite_note-holmes-31][^cite_note-ktbstv-33]

Also in 1963, headlines across the country reported that African American musician [Sam Cooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Cooke) was arrested in Shreveport after his band tried to register at a "whites-only" [Holiday Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_Inn), where they planned to stay before performing in the city. Public facilities in Louisiana were still segregated.[^cite_note-34] In the months following, Cooke recorded the civil rights era song, "[A Change Is Gonna Come](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Change_Is_Gonna_Come)". In 1964 Congress passed the [Civil Rights Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964) to end segregation of public facilities.

In the mid-1990s, the coming of [riverboat gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverboat_gambling) to Shreveport attracted numerous new patrons to the downtown and spurred a revitalization of the adjacent riverfront areas. Many downtown streets were given a facelift through the "Streetscape" project. Traditional brick sidewalks and crosswalks were built, and statues, sculptures, and [mosaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic) were added to create a better pedestrian environment. The O.K. Allen Bridge, commonly known as the [Texas Street bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long%E2%80%93Allen_Bridge_\(Shreveport\)), was lit with [neon lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_light). Residents predictably had a variety of reactions to these changes.[^cite_note-35] Shreveport was named an [All-American City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-America_City_Award) in 1953, 1979, and 1999.[^cite_note-36]

In the 1990s, Shreveport became known for its rap music scene, and acquired its famous aka name, *Ratchet City*.[^cite_note-Durden-37] The term was first used by the group Lava House in its 1999 single "Ratchet".[^cite_note-Durden-37]

During the [September 11, 2001 attacks](5058690.md), President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) was taken to the nearby [Barksdale Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barksdale_Air_Force_Base). He also made a visit to speak in the city on March 11, 2005.[^cite_note-38]

Since the downturn in the oil industry and other economic problems, the city has struggled with a declining population, unemployment, poverty, drugs and violent crime.[^cite_note-TIM-9] City data from 2017 showed a dramatic increase in certain violent crimes from the previous year, including a 138 percent increase in homicides, a 21 percent increase in forcible rapes and more than 130 percent increases in both business armed robberies and business burglaries.[^cite_note-TIM-9] In 2018 the local government and police authorities reported a [crime drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_drop) in most categories; it was part of an overall reduction in crime since the late 20th century.[^cite_note-\:12-16] As Shreveport continued its economic resurgence,[^cite_note-\:2-11][^cite_note-\:8-14] the [Adrian Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Perkins) administration saw the coming of Advanced Aero Services,[^cite_note-\:9-15] Tomakk Glass Partners,[^cite_note-39] and the revitalization plan of the Shreveport Economic Recovery Task Force after the Cross Bayou redevelopment plan was rejected.[^cite_note-\:13-40][^cite_note-41]

In June 2020, rapper [Hurricane Chris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Chris_\(rapper\)) was arrested in Shreveport for [second-degree murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-degree_murder).[^cite_note-42] Following the [George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd) killing in Minnesota, multiple protests were held in the city.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The city experienced the largest number of homicides in its recorded history in 2021, eclipsing the previous record set in 1993.[^cite_note-45]

On Sunday, April 19, 2026, [a man opened fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Shreveport,_Louisiana,_shooting) at three homes in Shreveport, leaving eight children dead and two women injured.[^cite_note-46] It is the deadliest mass shooting in the United States since the [2024 Joliet shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Joliet_shootings).

## Geography



Red River between Shreveport and Bossier City with Barksdale Air Force Base in background, 2008

Shreveport is located in [Northwest Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Louisiana). It is the center of the [Ark-La-Tex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark-La-Tex) region where [Arkansas](1930.md), Louisiana, and Texas meet. It is also part of the I-20 Cyber Corridor linking the tech-centered [Dallas–Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas%E2%80%93Fort_Worth_metroplex), [Shreveport–Bossier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport%E2%80%93Bossier_City_metropolitan_area), [Greater Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe,_Louisiana_metropolitan_area), and [Greater Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_metropolitan_area) metropolitan areas together.[^cite_note-\:4-18][^cite_note-\:5-19][^cite_note-\:7-21][^cite_note-\:6-20][^cite_note-47] The city of Shreveport is 188 miles (303 km) from [Dallas](53838.md),[^cite_note-48] 98 miles (158 km) from [Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler,_Texas),[^cite_note-49] and 41 miles (66 km) from [Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall,_Texas), Texas;[^cite_note-50] 215 miles (346 km) from [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) and 73 miles (117 km) from [Texarkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texarkana,_Arkansas), Arkansas;[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] and 250 miles (400 km) from the state capital of [Baton Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_Rouge,_Louisiana),[^cite_note-53] 99 miles (159 km) from [Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe,_Louisiana),[^cite_note-54] 69 miles (111 km) from [Ruston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruston,_Louisiana),[^cite_note-55] and 30 miles (48 km) from [Minden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minden,_Louisiana), Louisiana.[^cite_note-56] The city's proximity to the nearby cities makes it North Louisiana and the Ark-La-Tex's transportation hub.

Shreveport is the parish seat of [Caddo Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo_Parish,_Louisiana). Portions of the city extend into neighboring [Bossier Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish,_Louisiana), bordering [Bossier City](115504.md). Shreveport sits on a low elevation overlooking the Red River.[^cite_note-57] Western and northern portions of Shreveport have an elevation over 253 feet (77 m) above sea level.[^cite_note-58] [Pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine) forests, [cotton fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland), and [waterways](174851.md) mark the outskirts of the city. According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 123.396 square miles (319.59 km<sup>2</sup>), of which, 107.798 square miles (279.20 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 15.548 square miles (40.27 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.[^cite_note-CenPopGazetteer2023-59]

### Cityscape

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Shreveport)



The [Long-Allen Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long%E2%80%93Allen_Bridge_\(Shreveport\)) (also known as the Texas Street Bridge) connects Shreveport and Bossier City.

Shreveport—since the mid-1990s—has been a major gambling center with a modest downtown skyline. The "Streetscape" project, inspired by the coming of riverboat gaming, gave Shreveport's downtown traditional brick sidewalks, statues, sculptures, and mosaics. The O.K. Allen Bridge (Texas Street bridge) was lit with neon lights. Since then, Downtown Shreveport has seen minor changes until the 2010s; the whole of Shreveport has been improving roads since the mid-2010s, with continued road projects in 2018.[^cite_note-60] In 2018, buildings in Shreveport's downtown and nearby districts were revitalized due to re-investment in the area.[^cite_note-\:2-11][^cite_note-\:3-13] In 2020, plans were unveiled for the I-49 Connector and further redevelopment of the city.[^cite_note-\:13-40]

### Climate

Shreveport has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*). Rainfall is abundant, with the normal annual precipitation averaging over 51 inches (1,300 mm), with monthly averages ranging from less than 3 inches (76 mm) in August to more than 5 inches (130 mm) in June. Severe thunderstorms with heavy rain, [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail), damaging winds and [tornadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornadoes) occur in the area during the spring and summer months. The winter months are normally mild, with an average of 35 days of freezing or below-freezing temperatures per year, with ice and [sleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_pellets) storms possible.

Summer months are hot and humid, with maximum temperatures exceeding 90 °F (32 °C) on an average of 91 days per year, with high to very high relative average humidity. The extreme temperatures range from −5 °F (−21 °C) on [February 12, 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Blizzard_of_1899),[^cite_note-nws-61] to 110 °F (43 °C) on August 18, 1909, and August 25 and 26, 2023.[^cite_note-nws-61] Shreveport is home to a branch of the [National Weather Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Weather_Service_Shreveport,_Louisiana) which provides forecasts and warnings for the greater [Ark-La-Tex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark-La-Tex) region.

Shreveport's population was initially 1,728 at the [1850 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1850_United_States_census), and has experienced growth to a historic high of 206,989 at the [1980 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_census). As of 2024, estimates by the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) show the population has fallen to 176,578, a decrease of 30,411 (-14.7%) since the 1980 high.

According to the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), there were 187,593 people, 73,114 households, and 42,775 families residing in the city; the 2020 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey) determined an estimated 189,890 people resided in the city, purporting a slight population rebound.[^cite_note-\:14-67] The 2020 census estimates showed Shreveport had 75,680 households with an average of 2.4 people per household. Of the households, 39% were married-couple households, though 44% of its male population and 40% of its female population have never married.

Throughout the city, there were 89,523 housing units, with an 85% occupancy rate; among them, 54% were owner-occupied. Among its units, 73% were single-unit detached homes and 31% of its population moved into those homes from 2015 to 2016. The median value of its owner-occupied housing units were \$151,700, and 30% of its units were estimated to be under \$100,000; 38% of its units were estimated to cost from \$100,000 to \$200,000.[^cite_note-\:14-67] From 2014 to 2018, the median value of an owner-occupied housing unit was \$144,800. The median monthly cost with a mortgage was \$1,178 and the median monthly cost without a mortgage was \$364; the city of Shreveport had a median gross rent of \$810.

The median income from 2014 to 2018 was \$36,338, and the mean income was \$55,582.[^cite_note-68] The per capita income was \$25,022. By the 2020 American Community Survey, its median household income increased to \$40,809.[^cite_note-69] The median income for families grew to \$54,023 with a mean income of \$82,854; married-couple families \$84,282 with a mean of \$112,363; and non-family households \$26,628 with a mean of \$41,090.[^cite_note-70] According to census estimates, 25% of its population earned from \$50,000 to \$100,000 annually; 13% \$100,000 to \$200,000; and 5% over \$200,000. Approximately 24.9% of Shreveport lived at or below the poverty line, down from 2014 to 2018's census estimates of 25.4%.[^cite_note-quickfacts-71]

### Religion

[Christianity](5211.md) is the city and metropolitan area's dominant religion, being part of the [Bible Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Belt). Its residents were predominantly [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) through the nineteenth century, and today, [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists_in_the_United_States) form the majority of Christians in Shreveport, followed by [Methodists](20119.md) and [Roman Catholics](606848.md).[^cite_note-\:1-79] Many Baptist and Methodist churches are affiliated with [evangelical Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) denominations, though several are also affiliated with [mainline Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant); among Baptists, the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention),[^cite_note-80] [National Baptist Convention (USA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baptist_Convention,_USA,_Inc.), [National Baptist Convention of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baptist_Convention_of_America_International,_Inc.), and [Full Gospel Baptist Church Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Gospel_Baptist_Church_Fellowship) are the largest Baptist denominations in the city. The [Progressive National Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_National_Baptist_Convention) is the largest [Progressive Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Baptists) group in the area.[^cite_note-81] Methodists are mainly affiliates of the [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church) or [Christian Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Methodist_Episcopal_Church), though some also claimed affiliation to the mainline [United Methodist Church](32304.md).[^cite_note-82] The Roman Catholic community is primarily served by the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Shreveport).[^cite_note-83]



[Antioch Baptist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_Baptist_Church_\(Shreveport,_Louisiana\))

Of note among its predominantly Baptist population, the First Baptist Church of Shreveport was once pastored by Monroe E. Dodd, founder of the former [Dodd College for Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodd_College).[^cite_note-84] Former [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Louisiana) [Jimmie Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Davis), also a Shreveport city commissioner, taught history for a year under Dodd. Other historic large Baptist churches include [Antioch Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_Baptist_Church_\(Shreveport,_Louisiana\)), Galilee Missionary Baptist, Calvary Baptist, Broadmoor Baptist, Summer Grove Baptist, and Mount Canaan Missionary Baptist Church. Summer Grove Baptist Church was previously pastored by Wayne L. DuBose, a Baptist denominational officer.[^cite_note-85] Mount Canaan was previously pastored by civil rights era icon Dr. Harry Blake,[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] and Galilee was likewise pastored by Dr. E. Edward Jones, another civil rights icon.[^cite_note-88]

Among its Methodist churches has been the large [First Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Methodist_Church_\(Shreveport,_Louisiana\)), established in 1884. The current edifice dates to 1913. Among its former pastors were D. L. Dykes Jr. and John E. Fellers. During a severe thunderstorm in 2009, the fiberglass steeple of the church toppled and fell onto a passing car.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] It has since been replaced. A second prominent Methodist congregation is named for J. S. Noel Jr.[^cite_note-91] The church was begun as a mission in 1906. In 2023, First United Methodist voted to leave the United Methodist Church.[^cite_note-92]



[St. Mark's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_Cathedral_\(Shreveport,_Louisiana\)), an Episcopal church

The large [Holy Trinity Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_\(Shreveport,_Louisiana\)), located downtown, was founded in 1858; it served [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) immigrants as well as native-born residents. Five priests died of [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever) in the 1873 epidemic. The current sanctuary in [Romanesque revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_Revival_architecture) style architecture dates to 1896.[^cite_note-93] Particularly striking in size and architecture is [St. Mark's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_Cathedral_\(Shreveport,_Louisiana\)), an [Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)) congregation dating its establishment to the first religious service held in Shreveport in 1839.[^cite_note-94] It became the see of the [Episcopal Diocese of Western Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Diocese_of_Western_Louisiana) in 1990.[^cite_note-95]

In Shreveport's interdenominational and Pentecostal population, Shreveport Community Church (an [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) church affiliated with [Assemblies of God USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God_USA))[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] owns and operates [Evangel Christian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangel_Christian_Academy), a pre‑K through 12th grade private school. The church has produced a biblical musical, *Songs of the Season*, during the Christmas holidays for over 20 years.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

The [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md) has maintained a presence in Shreveport since the early 1900s.[^cite_note-100] The oldest Orthodox church in the city is St. George Greek Orthodox Church of the [Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Archdiocese_of_America), followed by St. Nicholas Orthodox Church ([Antiochian Orthodox Christian Archdiocese of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochian_Orthodox_Christian_Archdiocese_of_North_America)),[^cite_note-101] and the Holy Nativity of the Lord Church of the [Orthodox Church in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America).



Original B'nai Zion Temple, designated a [National Historic Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places)

The [Jewish](15624.md) community of Shreveport dates to the organization of Congregation Har El in 1859, made up primarily of [German Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Jewish) immigrants in its early years. It developed as [B'nai Zion Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Nai_Zion_Temple), today the city's [Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Judaism) congregation, which built the city's largest synagogue. Agudath Achim, founded in 1905 as an [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) congregation of immigrants from Eastern Europe, is today a traditional Jewish synagogue. Shreveport, historically, has had a large and civic-minded Jewish community and has elected three Jewish mayors.[^cite_note-102]

The [Islamic](6037917.md) community in Shreveport-Bossier constituted approximately 14% of Louisiana's total Muslim population in 2018.[^cite_note-\:02-103] The majority of Shreveporter Muslims are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), followed by the [Nation of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_of_Islam) and [non-denominational Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslim).

## Economy



[Regions Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_Tower), the tallest building in downtown Shreveport



Health care is a major industry in Shreveport; Christus Schumpert Medical Center is the secondary leading cancer-treatment facility in the South, behind Willis Knighton.



The Shreveport Convention Center

Shreveport was formerly a major player in United States oil business, and once hosted the [Standard Oil of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil_of_Louisiana) offices, a branch of [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil). The Louisiana branch was later absorbed by [Standard Oil of New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil_of_New_Jersey). Beginning in 1930, [United Gas Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Gas_Corporation), the nation's busiest pipeline operator and massive integrated oil company, was headquartered in Shreveport. [Pennzoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennzoil) performed a [hostile takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostile_takeover) in 1968, and forced a merger. In the 1980s, the oil and gas industry suffered a large economic downturn. This severely affected the regional economy, and many companies cut back jobs or went out of business, including a large retail shopping mall (South Park Mall) which closed in the late 1990s. Shreveport's economy entered a major economic recession, and many residents left the area.

At one time, Shreveport was home to numerous manufacturers, including [Shreveport Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Operations), a [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) plant that permanently closed in August 2012. The plant produced the [Chevrolet Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Colorado), [GMC Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMC_Canyon), Hummer H3 series, and the [Isuzu i-Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isuzu_i-Series).[^cite_note-104] In January 2013, the plant was leased from Caddo Parish by [Elio Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_Motors).[^cite_note-Klayman-105] However, Elio Motors never went into production at the Shreveport plant.

In addition to GM, other notable large companies that have had or still have Shreveport manufacturing/assembly or production facilities or operations include: [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric) (electric transformer production), [Western Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Electric) (payphone manufacturing, approximately 7,500 employees at its peak, changed ownership through the years but closed in 2001)[^cite_note-106] [Honeywell UOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UOP_LLC), [Libbey-Owens-Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libbey-Owens-Ford), [Beaird-Poulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poulan) (the originator of and, for decades, the only manufacturer of the single-operator [chainsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chainsaw) in the world),[^cite_note-107] [Calumet Specialty Products Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calumet_Specialty_Products_Partners) (originally [United Gas Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Gas_Corporation)'s Atlas Processing Unit and then [Pennzoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennzoil)), and Frymaster, LLC (a subsidiary of [The Manitowoc Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Manitowoc_Company)). Today, only Calumet Packaging and Honeywell UOP survive as a large manufacturing employer in Shreveport.

By 2017, manufacturing and other goods-producing (e.g. petrochemical refining) jobs accounted for just 5% of Shreveport occupations, compared to 8% for the nationwide percentage of the workforce involved in manufacturing.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

Shreveport has since largely transitioned to a service economy. In particular, there has been rapid growth in the gaming industry. The city hosts various riverboat gambling [casinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino), and, before [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) in 2005, was second only to [New Orleans](53842.md) in Louisiana tourism. Nearby [Bossier City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_City) is home to one of the three horse racetracks in the state, [Louisiana Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Downs). Casinos in Shreveport-Bossier include [Sam's Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam's_Town_Hotel_and_Gambling_Hall,_Shreveport), [Bally's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bally's_Shreveport), [Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_Bossier_City), [Boomtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomtown_Bossier_City), and [Margaritaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaritaville_Resort_Casino). Diamond Jacks Casino (formerly Isle of Capri) closed in 2020.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] The Shreveport-Bossier Convention and Tourist Bureau is the official tourism information agency for the region. The bureau maintains a comprehensive database of restaurants, accommodations, attractions, and events.

In May 2005, the [Louisiana Boardwalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Boardwalk), a 550,000-square-foot (51,000 m<sup>2</sup>) shopping and entertainment complex, opened in Bossier City across from Shreveport's downtown. It features outlet shopping, several restaurants, a 14‑screen movie theater, a bowling complex, and [Bass Pro Shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_Pro_Shops).

A 350,000-square-foot (33,000 m<sup>2</sup>) convention center was completed in the Shreveport Downtown Riverfront. Managed by [SMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMG_\(property_management\)), it includes an 800-space parking garage. An adjoining Hilton Hotel opened in June 2007. It was constructed by and owned by the city, which has been a controversial issue, and the subject of discussions about use of public funds.

In November 2008, development of the [Haynesville Shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haynesville_Shale) area, with new jobs in the natural gas industry were expected to be created over the next few years. Residents in the region have been given large bonuses for signing mineral rights leases up to \$25,000 per acre. However, economic downturn had resulted in a lower market price for natural gas and slower-than-expected drilling activity. The city expected to generate revenue by leasing the mineral rights on public lands in the near future as neighboring municipalities had already done. However, with advances in fracking methods in the Haynesville shale and starting in 2022 with the increase of natural gas prices after Covid to as high as \$8/Mcf (thousand cubic feet), rig activity has greatly increased in the Haynesville to be the 2nd largest basin in the United States by number of active drilling rigs (2nd only to the Permian Basin - largest basin in the United States). It has since slowed to a degree since natural gas prices have decreased but is still the 3rd highest ranked basin in the US in terms of number of active drilling rigs.

Gambling and hotel industries in Shreveport, [JPMorgan Chase](231001.md), [Capital One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One), and [Regions Financial Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_Financial_Corporation) have regional offices in Shreveport's downtown and surrounding districts and neighborhoods. AT\&T's regional headquarters is located in Downtown Shreveport. [Amazon](90451.md) and [Governor Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bel_Edwards) announced plans to open a fulfillment center in 2021.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

Amazon began construction on the \$200 million fulfillment center in 2021 with completion expected by the end of 2022. The fulfillment center was expected to create 1,000 direct jobs.[^cite_note-114] Additionally, other business investments alongside Amazon during the early 2020s contributed more than \$750 million to revitalizing and expanding the municipal and metropolitan economy.[^cite_note-115] By October 2023, Amazon planned to open a second facility in Shreveport.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

In December 2023, the city council approved a lease of Millennium Studios to rapper [50 Cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50_Cent)'s new production company.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

In 2014, the city government pumped \$16.5 million into [Mall St. Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_St._Vincent) in an attempt to attract new customers to the mall.[^cite_note-122] However, by 2021, Auntie Anne's, Gymboree, [Grimaldi's Pizzeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimaldi's_Pizzeria), and Sears had closed their Mall St. Vincent operations, leaving Dilliards as the only anchor store.[^cite_note-123]

*[Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_\(magazine\))* magazine ranked Shreveport the "#1 place to start a business" in 2015.[^cite_note-124] In 2020, Advanced Aero Services planned to open a facility at Shreveport Regional Airport,[^cite_note-\:9-15][^cite_note-\:8-14] while employer Libbey Glass closed its doors for good after 47 years of operation, resulting in the loss of 450 jobs.[^cite_note-125] On July 31, 2020, the Shreveport Economic Recovery Task Force released a revitalization plan with a primary focus on the downtown area.[^cite_note-\:13-40]

#### Mardi Gras

[Mardi Gras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardi_Gras) celebrations in Shreveport date to the mid‑19th century when [krewes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krewe) and parades were organized along the lines of those of New Orleans. Mardi Gras in Shreveport did not survive the cancellations caused by [World War I](4764461.md). Attempts to revive it in the 1920s were unsuccessful, and the last Carnival celebrations in Shreveport for decades were held in 1927. Mardi Gras in Shreveport was revived beginning in 1984 with the organization of the Krewe of Apollo. The Krewes of Gemini, Centaur, Aesclepius, Highland, Sobek, Harambee, and others, followed during the next decade and a half. The first krewe to revive parading was Gemini in 1989. Today, Mardi Gras is again an important part of the cultural life of the Shreveport-Bossier metropolitan area.[^cite_note-137]

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Shreveport-Bossier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Shreveport-Bossier)



Independence Stadium, 2015

Dating back to 1911, the state fairgrounds (and later [Independence Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Stadium_\(Shreveport\)), formerly State Fair Stadium) has traditionally hosted a college football game or two during the State Fair of Louisiana, an event currently dubbed the [Red River State Fair Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_State_Fair_Classic). Since 1976, Independence Stadium has served as host of college football's annual [Independence Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Bowl).[^cite_note-138] Also, the [Louisiana Tech Bulldogs football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Tech_Bulldogs_football) team occasionally hosts games at Independence Stadium.

Shreveport has been home to several professional football teams. The Houston franchise of the professional [World Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Football_League) relocated to Shreveport rebranded as the [Shreveport Steamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Steamer) midway through the 1974 season, but the franchise along with the WFL folded midway through the 1975 season. Another franchise named the [Shreveport Steamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Steamers) played as a member of the [American Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_Association_\(1978%E2%80%931983\)) from 1979 until folding in 1981. Shreveport's Independence Stadium was also home to the [Shreveport Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Pirates), an unsuccessful professional [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) franchise that opened play in 1994 but folded after the 1995 season.

Baseball in Shreveport has an extensive history. The city had affiliated [Minor League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball) teams from 1968 to 2002. The most memorable team was the [Shreveport Captains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Captains) of the [Texas League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_League). Baseball teams in Shreveport have gone through eight different name changes and seven different leagues all since 1895. Shreveport's most recent independent minor league baseball team, the [Shreveport-Bossier Captains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport-Bossier_Captains), ceased operations in 2011 and moved to [Laredo, Texas](136773.md).

The city also has an extensive history in basketball and soccer. The [Shreveport Crawdads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Crawdads) and Shreveport Storm operated in 1994 and 1995 as members of the [Continental Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Basketball_Association). The [Shreveport-Bossier Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport-Bossier_Mavericks) of the [American Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association_\(2000%E2%80%93present\)) played from 2013 to 2015 before relocating to [Owensboro, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owensboro,_Kentucky); since 2021, the Mavericks returned as the [Shreveport Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Mavericks) within [The Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Basketball_League).[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] The Shreveport/Bossier Lions played in 1998 as affiliates of the [United Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_League). [NPSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Premier_Soccer_League)-affiliate [Shreveport Rafters FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Rafters_FC) operated from 2016 to 2018; their expansion team for the [Women's Premier Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Premier_Soccer_League) operated for one season in 2017. Shreveport almost had a USL expansion team in 2020 before its rejection by the city council,[^cite_note-141] though [USL League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_Two) announced the establishment of [Blue Goose SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Goose_SC) in 2022.[^cite_note-142] The city was also considered as location for an [NBA G League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_G_League)-affiliate of the [New Orleans Pelicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans_Pelicans). The city council unanimously rejected the proposal.[^cite_note-143]

The [Shreveport Mudbugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Mudbugs) are a [Tier II junior ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_ice_hockey#Tier_II) team that has competed in the [North American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Hockey_League) since 2016.

On the collegiate front, the [Centenary Gentlemen and Ladies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenary_Gentlemen_and_Ladies) compete in [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III) as a member of the [Southern Collegiate Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Collegiate_Athletic_Conference). The [LSU–Shreveport Pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSU%E2%80%93Shreveport_Pilots) compete in the [NAIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Intercollegiate_Athletics) as a member of the [Red River Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Athletic_Conference).

## Government

Main article: [Politics of Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Shreveport)



Caddo Parish Courthouse

Founded in 1836 and incorporated in 1839, Shreveport is the parish seat of [Caddo Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo_Parish,_Louisiana). It is part of the First Judicial District, housing the parish courthouse. It also houses the Louisiana Second Circuit Court of Appeal, which consists of nine elected judges representing twenty parishes in Northwest Louisiana. A portion of east Shreveport extends into [Bossier Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish,_Louisiana) due to the changing course of the [Red River](194242.md).

The city of Shreveport has a [mayor-council government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-council_government). The elected municipal officials include the [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Shreveport,_Louisiana), [Tom Arceneaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Arceneaux?action=edit\&redlink=1), and seven members of the [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council). Cedric Glover, a member of the [Louisiana House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_Legislature), was the first African American to hold the mayoral position.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] Under the mayor-council government, the mayor serves as the executive officer of the city. As the city's chief administrator and official representative, the mayor is responsible for the general management of the city and for seeing that all laws and [ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ordinance) are enforced.

## Crime

According to the most recent FBI statistics, the total crime rate in Shreveport (as of 2022) is 5,722.4 per 100,000 people, or 143.92% higher than the national rate of 2,346.0 per 100,000 people and 62.39% higher than the Louisiana total crime rate of 3,523.8 per 100,000 people.[^cite_note-HS-146] The violent crime rate in Shreveport is 923.0 per 100,000 people, or 138.01% higher than the national rate of 387.8 per 100,000 people, and 44.35% higher than the Louisiana violent crime rate of 639.4 per 100,000 people.[^cite_note-HS-146]

Shreveport has one of the highest crime rates in [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America) compared to all communities of all sizes. One's chance of becoming a victim of either violent or property crime is 1 in 15. Within Louisiana, more than 93% of the communities had a lower crime rate than Shreveport. [NeighborhoodScout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeighborhoodScout) found Shreveport to be one of the top 100 most dangerous cities in the United States.[^cite_note-147] Shreveport was the first city in [Louisiana](18130.md) to have [Crips](3434023.md) and [Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloods) gangs.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] In 1993, Shreveport hit a peak in murders, with 86 killings. Most of the killings were drug- or gang-related homicides.[^cite_note-150] In 2017, Shreveport was placed 18th on *24/7 Wall St.*'s list of "America's 25 Murder Capitals."[^cite_note-151] Shreveport's crime rate was 71% higher than the Louisiana average. The crime rate was also 149% higher than the national average.[^cite_note-152]

The city had a so-called ["saggy pants" law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagging_\(fashion\)) since 2007.[^cite_note-153] The city ordinance was repealed by the city council in June 2019.[^cite_note-154]

## Education



[C. E. Byrd High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._E._Byrd_High_School)

Main article: [Caddo Public Schools (Louisiana)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo_Public_Schools_\(Louisiana\))

Caddo Public Schools is a [school district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) based in Shreveport.[^cite_note-155] The district serves all of Caddo Parish, including the parish's sections of Shreveport.[^cite_note-156] Its founding superintendent was [Clifton Ellis Byrd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.E._Byrd?action=edit\&redlink=1), a [Virginia](32432.md) native, who assumed the chief administrative position in 1907 and continued until his death in 1926. [C. E. Byrd High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._E._Byrd_High_School), which was established in 1925 on Line Avenue at the intersection with Kings Highway, bears his name. There are a number of private schools in the city as well, including [Loyola College Prep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_College_Prep), a coeducational high school founded in 1902 as the all-male St. John's High School.

Portions of Shreveport in Bossier Parish are instead within the [Bossier Parish School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish_School_District).[^cite_note-157]

### Colleges and universities



Louisiana State University Shreveport

Shreveport has several colleges, including the [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist)-affiliated [Centenary College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenary_College_of_Louisiana) (founded at [Jackson, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson,_Louisiana), in 1825; relocated to Shreveport in 1908) and [Louisiana State University in Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University_in_Shreveport), which opened as a two-year institution in 1967; it became a four-year institution in 1976. [Louisiana State University Health Sciences Center Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University_Health_Sciences_Center_Shreveport), the only [medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_school) in Northern Louisiana, opened in 1969. Shreveport also has one of the largest nursing schools in northern Louisiana, the [Northwestern State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_State_University) College of Nursing.

[Louisiana Tech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Tech_University) at Shreveport-Bossier City was launched in 2012 offering their [Executive MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_MBA) and main campus undergraduate and graduate degree programs at the university's Shreveport Center.[^cite_note-158] [Southern University at Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University_at_Shreveport) (SUSLA) offers a two-year [associate's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate's_degree) program. Founded in 1973, [Louisiana Baptist University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Baptist_University) and Theological Seminary is also located in Shreveport, at 6301 Westport Avenue.

Both Caddo and Bossier parishes are in the areas of [Bossier Parish Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish_Community_College) and [Northwest Louisiana Technical Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Louisiana_Technical_Community_College?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-159]

Ayers Career College is a Shreveport-based college that offers career training in the medical and [HVAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HVAC) fields.[^cite_note-160] Since July 2007, Shreveport is home to a local [Remington College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remington_College) campus. This location offers both diploma and degree programs, and is active in the Shreveport community.[^cite_note-161] [Virginia College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_College) opened in 2012. Located in Shreveport-Bossier City, it offers career training in areas such as business and office, health and medical, and medical billing.[^cite_note-162]

## Media

Further information: [Media in Shreveport, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Shreveport,_Louisiana)



[KSLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSLA), a [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Broadcasting_Company) affiliate, is the oldest television station in Shreveport. Established in the former Washington Youree Hotel in 1954, it was moved to Fairfield Avenue in the early 1970s.

Shreveport and its surrounding area are served by a variety of local newspapers, magazines, television stations and radio stations. The major daily newspaper serving the area is *[The Shreveport Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_\(Shreveport\))*, owned by *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)* parent [Gannett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gannett). The newspaper's former rival, the afternoon *[Shreveport Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Journal)*, ceased publication in 1991. Other major newspapers include *[The Shreveport Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shreveport_Sun)*, *Caddo Citizen*, and *SB Magazine*. *The Shreveport Sun* is the area's primary [African American newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_newspapers).[^cite_note-163]

Across the Red River, Shreveport's sister city of Bossier City is served by the daily *[Bossier Press-Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Press-Tribune)*. *The Barksdale Warrior* is the weekly newspaper of record for the [Barksdale Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barksdale_Air_Force_Base). Alternative publications include *The Forum Newsweekly*, *City Lights*, *The Inquisitor* and *The Shreveport Catalyst*. Twice annually, *[North Louisiana History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Louisiana_History)*, the journal of the North Louisiana Historical Association, is published in Shreveport.

Shreveport and Bossier City are primarily served by two major cable television and internet companies: Shreveport is served by [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) and Bossier City is served by [Suddenlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suddenlink).

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Shreveport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Shreveport)

#### Highways and roads



Texas Street



Texas Avenue

Shreveport's past reflects the need for mass transit and public roads. As far back as the 1870s, residents used [mule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mule)-[drawn street cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsecar) that were converted to electric motorized streetcars by 1890. [Commuter rail](6286.md) systems in Shreveport flourished for many decades, and rail car lines extended out to rural areas. By the 1930s, trolleys and streetcars began to be replaced by buses, although motor buses did not finally replace all trolley service until the 1960s. The local public transportation provider, [SporTran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SporTran), provides moderately extensive bus service throughout Shreveport and Bossier City. Sportran operates seven days a week on seventeen bus routes (five night routes) from 6\:00 a.m. to 1\:00 am, with no night service on Sunday.&#x20;

In the 1960s, the [Interstate Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) came to the area with the construction of [Interstate 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_20_in_Louisiana). The highway system has a cross-hair and loop freeway structure similar to that of [Texas](29810.md) cities like [Houston](13774.md) and [Dallas](53838.md). The loop consists of the Outer Loop Freeway [Interstate 220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_220_\(Louisiana\)) on the north and the Inner Loop Freeway, [Louisiana Highway 3132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Highway_3132), on the south, forming approximately an 8-mile-diameter (13 km) semi-loop around downtown. Another loop is formed by the Bert Kouns Industrial Loop (Louisiana Highway 526) and circles further south, crossing [Interstate 49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_49_in_Louisiana). I-49 now extends north to [Interstate 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_30) in [Arkansas](1930.md), though there is a gap in I-49 within Shreveport.

Shreveport lies along the route of the proposed [Interstate 69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_69_in_Louisiana) [North American Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Free_Trade_Agreement) (NAFTA) superhighway that will link Canada, the U.S. industrial [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest), Texas, and [Mexico](3966054.md).

#### Airports

Shreveport is served by two airports. The larger is [Shreveport Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Regional_Airport) (SHV), established in 1952, and is served by [Allegiant Air](1372018.md), [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines), [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines), and [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) (as United Express). The smaller airport, [Shreveport Downtown Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport_Downtown_Airport) (DTN), was built in 1931 and is located north of the Downtown Business District along the Red River. It is currently a general aviation and reliever airport, but was originally Shreveport's commercial airport.

### Military installations



Entrance to Barksdale AFB

[Barksdale Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barksdale_Air_Force_Base) is located in [Bossier Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Parish) across the river from Shreveport, which annexed and donated the land for its construction in the 1920s. Named for pioneer army aviator Lt. [Eugene Hoy Barksdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Hoy_Barksdale) and originally called Barksdale Army Air Field, it opened in 1933 and became Barksdale Air Force Base in 1947. Headquartered here are the [Air Force Global Strike Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Global_Strike_Command), 8th Air Force, 2d Bomb Wing, and 307th Wing. The primary aircraft housed here is the Boeing [B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md). In earlier years, the base was the home to other famous aircraft, including the [B-47 Stratojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-47_Stratojet).

Shreveport is home to the two [108th Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/108th_Cavalry_Regiment_\(United_States\)) Squadrons, the reconnaissance element of the [256th Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/256th_Infantry_Brigade). Three of the squadron's four cavalry troops are located at 400 East Stoner Avenue in a historic armory known as "Fort Humbug". It got the name due to the Confederate Army burning logs to look like cannons and placing them along the Red River. This caused Union ironclad ships sailing north on the Red River to be tricked into turning back south.[^cite_note-167]
