[Nigeria](21383.md) is [the most populous country in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_population)[^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] and the [sixth most populous in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Nigeria is also one of the most densely populated countries in [Africa](5334607.md), with approximately 237 million people as of 2025[^cite_note-8] in an area of 923,768 km<sup>2</sup> (356,669 sq mi).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

54.3% of Nigerians are urban dwellers, with the annual rate of urbanisation being estimated at 3.92%.[^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-11] Nigeria is home to 250 ethnic groups speaking [over 500 languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Nigeria)[^cite_note-\:2-2] and the variety of customs and traditions among them gives the country great cultural diversity. The three largest ethnic groups, namely the [Hausas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_people), [Yorubas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people), and [Igbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people) constitute more than 60% of the population.[^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The [Ijaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijaw_people), [Efik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efik_people), [Ibibio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibibio_people), [Annang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annang), [Ogoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogoni_people), [Tiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiv_people), [Urhobo-Isoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urhobo-Isoko), [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_people) and [Itsekiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itsekiri_people) are some of the other sizeable ethnic groups.[^cite_note-15] Over 1.2 million people living in Nigeria (0.5% of its total population, or 1 in every 200 people living in Nigeria) are from a continent other than Africa. In particular, 100,000 people are from the United States,[^cite_note-16] 75,000 are from [Lebanon](17771.md),[^cite_note-17] 60,000 are from China[^cite_note-18] and 16,000 are from the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-19]

Nigeria has a young population overall, with 42.54% of inhabitants between the ages of 0–14.[^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-20] There is also a very high dependency ratio at 88.2 dependents per 100 non-dependents.[^cite_note-\:2-2] The three main religious groups are Muslims (estimated to be 53.5% of the total population), Christians (estimated at 45.9%), and adherents of indigenous religions (estimated at 0.6%).[^cite_note-ciafactbook-21] The predominantly Christian [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people) are found in the south-east.[^cite_note-22] [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) is the largest Christian [denomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominations_of_Christianity) in [Igboland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igboland),[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] but [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) is also strong, as are [Pentecostalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism) and other [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) denominations.

Persons of different ethnic backgrounds most commonly communicate in English, although knowledge of two or more Nigerian languages is widespread. [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_language), [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_language) and [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language) are the most widely used Nigerian languages. [Nigerian Pidgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Pidgin) is used widely as an unofficial medium of communication, especially in the Nigerian cities of [Warri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warri), [Sapele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapele,_Delta), [Ughelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ughelli), [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_City), and [Port Harcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Harcourt).[^cite_note-26]

Nigeria's population has been increasing rapidly for at least the last 5 decades due to very high birth rates, quadrupling its population during this time. Growth was fastest in the 1980s, after child mortality dropped rapidly. It has slowed slightly since then as both the birth rate and total fertility rate have declined marginally since a 1978 peak. According to the 2017 revision of the [World Population Prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Population_Prospects) the total population was 191 million in 2016, compared to only 38 million in 1950. Fertility rates turned out to be lower than expected in Nigeria, with double-digit decreases.[^cite_note-27]

The proportion of children under the age of 15 in 2010 was 44.0%, 53.2% were between 15 and 65 years of age, while 2.7% were 65 years or older. There is a large degree of [population momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_momentum), with 3.2 per cent growth leading to the projected population of 546 million by 2100.[^cite_note-auto-28]

The federal government has not elected to implement the type of controversial [family planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning) programs that have reduced population growth of other developing nations, a result of low political support for these programs and a cultural preference for large families as well as high levels of social instability. Rising educational levels and health care improvements may enable future parents to plan for smaller families.[^cite_note-ciafactbook-21]

The former Nigeria's chairman of [National Population Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Population_Commission), Eze Duruiheoma, delivering Nigeria's statement in [New York City](645042.md) on [sustainable cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_cities), human mobility and [international migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_migration) in the 51st session of Commission on Population and Development, said that "Nigeria remains the most populous in Africa, the seventh globally with an estimated population of over 198 million. The World Population Prospects predicts that by 2050, Nigeria will become the third most populated country in the world. Over the last 50 years, Nigeria's urban population has grown at an average annual growth rate of more than 6.5% without commensurate increases in social amenities and infrastructure." He also stated that the population "grew substantially from 17.3% in 1967 to 49.4% in 2017."[^cite_note-29]

### Population projections

The total population in sub-Saharan Africa is projected to increase to almost one billion people, making it the most populated region outside of South-Central Asia.[^cite_note-\:1-34] According to the United Nations, the population of Nigeria will reach 375 million by 2050. Nigeria might then be the 3rd most populous country in the world. In 2100, the population of Nigeria may reach 541 million.[^cite_note-auto-28] While the overall population is expected to increase, the growth rate is estimated to decrease from 1.2 per cent per year in 2010 to 0.4 per cent per year in 2050.[^cite_note-\:1-34] The [birth rate](515898.md) is also projected to decrease from 20.7 to 13.7, while the [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) is projected to increase from 8.5 in 2010 to 9.8 in 2050.[^cite_note-\:1-34] By 2050, 69.6% of the population is estimated to be living in urban areas compared to 50.6% in 2010.[^cite_note-\:1-34]

## Emigration

Further information: [Emigration from Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration_from_Africa)

Further information: [Nigerian British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_British), [Nigerian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_American), [Nigerian Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Canadians), [Nigerian Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Australian), [Nigerians in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerians_in_the_Netherlands), [Nigerians in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerians_in_Switzerland), [Nigerians in Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerians_in_Ireland), and [Nigerians in Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerians_in_Vietnam)

Today millions of [ethnic Nigerians](13008384.md) live abroad, the largest communities can be found in the United Kingdom (500,000–3,000,000)[^cite_note-51] and the United States (600,000–1,000,000 Nigerians), other countries that followed closely are South Africa, [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia), and Canada respectively. There are also large groups in Ireland, [Portugal](23033.md) and many other countries.[^cite_note-52] Inspiration for emigration is based heavily on socio-economical issues such as warfare, insecurity, economical instability and civil unrest.

Between 1400 and 1900, of 1.4 million of 2 million emigrants were slaves sent to the Americas with the other 600,000 being sent to other destinations via the trans-Saharan, Red Sea and Indian Ocean routes. This is due to the fact that the land now known as Nigeria was a central point for 4 slave trades during the 19th century. Though bondage represented a great deal, an estimated 30,000 Nigerian inhabitants would relocate to Kano City and Gambia to take advantage of financial opportunities afforded by fertile land and available natural resources. What's more, the presence of gold mines and rail lines along the Gold Coast, present-day Ghana, attracted an estimated 6,500 Nigerian citizens to attain financial gain and opportunity. The population of Nigerians in Ghana rose to roughly 149,000 before the 1969 alien expulsion order would displace nearly the entire population to surrounding countries.[^cite_note-53]

## Religion

Main article: [Religion in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Nigeria)

According to Pew Research Center, the population in 2015 was 50% Muslim, 48.1% Christian and approximately 2% other.[^cite_note-54]

Nigeria is nearly equally divided between Islam and Christianity. The majority of Nigerian Muslims are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and mostly live in the northern, central and south-western states of the country, while Christians dominate in some central states (especially Plateau and Benue states), and the south-east and south-south regions. Other religions practised in Nigeria include [African Traditional Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Nigeria?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), Judaism, The [Grail Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grail_Movement), and the Reformed Ògbóni Fraternity, one of the traditional socio-religious institutions of the [Yorùbá people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yor%C3%B9b%C3%A1_people) and their Òrìṣà religion known as Ẹ̀sìn Òrìṣà Ìbílẹ̀ in the [Yorùbá language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yor%C3%B9b%C3%A1_language).[^cite_note-55]

According to a 2009 Pew survey, 50.4% of Nigeria's population were [Muslims](19541.md).[^cite_note-56] A later Pew study in 2011 calculated that Christians now formed 50.8% of the population.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] Adherents of other religions made up 1% of the population.[^cite_note-59]

The shift of population balance between Muslims and Christians is a result of northern and southern Nigeria being in different stages of demographic transition. The Muslim-dominated north is in an earlier stage of the demographic transition with much higher fertility rates than the south, whose split Christian/Muslim population is further along in the transition, and whose fertility rates are declining. Decreasing fertility can be linked to more access to education, use of contraceptives, and differing beliefs regarding family planning.[^cite_note-\:0-60]

The 1999 introduction of [Sharia law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia_law) in twelve northern Nigerian states led to massive violence and unrest and caused an ethnic and religious rift between Sharia and Non-Sharia states, a divide that has deepened with time.[^cite_note-\:0-60]
