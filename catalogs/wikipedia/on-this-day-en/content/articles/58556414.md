Parliamentary elections were held in [Bulgaria](3415.md) on 4 April 2021 at the end of the term of the [National Assembly](2122384.md) [elected in 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Bulgarian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-1] Parties in the governing coalition led by [Boyko Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyko_Borisov) lost seats and no party leader was able to form a coalition government within the time limit. This triggered the [July 2021 Bulgarian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2021_Bulgarian_parliamentary_election).

## Background

Main article: [2020–2021 Bulgarian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_Bulgarian_protests)

## Electoral system

The 240 members of the National Assembly are elected by [open list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) [proportional representation](23298.md) from 31 multi-member constituencies ranging in size from 4 to 16 seats. The electoral threshold is 4% for parties, with seats allocated using the [largest remainder method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_remainder_method).[^cite_note-2]

## Parties and coalitions

The [incumbent government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Borisov_Government) was a coalition between the conservative [GERB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GERB) party of Prime Minister [Boyko Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyko_Borisov) and the nationalist [United Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Patriots) alliance (formed from [IMRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMRO_%E2%80%93_Bulgarian_National_Movement), [Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_\(political_party\)) and the [NFSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_for_the_Salvation_of_Bulgaria)), with the support of the populist [Volya Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volya_Movement). Together they held 132 out of 240 seats in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Bulgaria).

During [The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greens_\(Bulgaria\))' 2020 national meeting, the party representatives voted in favor of a coalition at "the next parliamentary election with the other two members of [Democratic Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Bulgaria)". The party representatives voted against a "coalition with any of the political parties in the current National Assembly" - namely, [GERB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GERB), [BSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Socialist_Party), [DPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Rights_and_Freedoms), [Volya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volya_Movement) and [OP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Patriots).[^cite_note-3]

The deputy chairman of [ITN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Are_Such_People), Toshko Yordanov, said in an interview for the [Bulgarian National Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Radio), that the party "will not enter a coalition with GERB, DPS or BSP".[^cite_note-4]

The cochairman of Democratic Bulgaria, [Hristo Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hristo_Ivanov_\(politician\)), stated in an interview for [bTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTV_\(Bulgaria\)), that "there will be no coalition with GERB, whether with or without [Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyko_Borisov)".[^cite_note-5]

The chairman of [Bulgaria for Citizens Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_for_Citizens_Movement), Dimitar Delchev, announced that his party was joining [Stand Up.BG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up.BG) during a public presentation of the citizens' platform at [Slaveykov Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaveykov_Square), in August 2020.[^cite_note-6] The same was done by the chairman of [Volt Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt_Bulgaria) - [Nastimir Ananiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastimir_Ananiev),[^cite_note-auto-7] as well as the chairman of the party [Movement 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_21) - [Tatyana Doncheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatyana_Doncheva).[^cite_note-8] The citizens' organization [The System Kills Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_System_Kills_Us) announced their support for Nikola Vaptsarov as their representative within [Stand Up.BG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up.BG).[^cite_note-auto-7]

## Analysis

Both GERB and BSP had very poor results and there was a large turnover with a third of the seats taken by parties not represented in the previous parliament.[^cite_note-bin-22] A central theme in the election was purported corruption in the GERB-led government, which saw GERB lose seats and various anti-corruption parties gain, most notably Slavi Trifonov's ITN, but also DB and ISMV. The Bulgarian Socialist Party suffered from division between its leader Korneliya Ninova and other factions.[^cite_note-washpo-23] The BSP recorded their worst-ever result in a democratic election.[^cite_note-bin-22] The far-right parties also suffered from splits, losing their representation in parliament;[^cite_note-washpo-23] the [Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_\(political_party\)) party and the two remaining parties from the [United Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Patriots) alliance (the [National Front for the Salvation of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_for_the_Salvation_of_Bulgaria) and [Volya Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volya_Movement)) contested the elections separately, with none winning a seat. The three combined results of the three parties suggested they could have crossed the electoral threshold if they had run together.

The election happened during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), which necessitated a greater focus on online campaigning.[^cite_note-washpo-23] Contrary to expectations, voter turnout was broadly unchanged.[^cite_note-bin-22]

## Government formation

After his offer of a technocrat government was rejected by the opposition, Borisov said that as leader of the largest party, he would try to form a coalition government, and that he would also be open to supporting an ITN-led government. However, Borisov himself stated he would likely be unsuccessful in forming a coalition, with the likeliest outcomes being either a caretaker government followed by new elections or a coalition of parties new to Parliament.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-washpo-23]

After former foreign minister [Daniel Mitov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Mitov), whom [Boyko Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyko_Borisov) had nominated as [GERB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GERB)'s candidate for prime minister,[^cite_note-25] failed to form a government,[^cite_note-26] the mandate was then offered to [Slavi Trifonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavi_Trifonov)'s [ITN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Is_Such_a_People). Chess grandmaster [Antoaneta Stefanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoaneta_Stefanova), whom Trifonov appointed to take the mandate from president [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) immediately returned it in accordance with Trifonov's wish.[^cite_note-27] [Korneliya Ninova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korneliya_Ninova) of [BSPzB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSP_for_Bulgaria) formally received the final mandate from president [Radev](51575780.md) on 5 May 2021, but refused to form a government due to a lack of support from other parliamentary opposition parties.[^cite_note-auto1-28] This triggered an [early election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2021_Bulgarian_parliamentary_election) (to be held on 11 July), and the president appointed a [caretaker government](240683.md) led by Stefan Yanev[^cite_note-29] to run the country until the elections are over and a permanent administration is formed.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-auto1-28]
