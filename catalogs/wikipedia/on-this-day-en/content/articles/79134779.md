**Gyirong Port** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 吉隆口岸; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Jílóng kǒu'àn*; [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_script): སྐྱིད་གྲོང་འགག་སྒོ་།)[^cite_note-1] was a Chinese [land port and border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports_of_entry_of_China) on the [China–Nepal border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Nepal_border). The port complex stood at the confluence of the [Trishuli River](27394714.md) with the Lhende Khola, near Risur ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 热索; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Rèsuǒ*; [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_script): རི་ཟུར་།[^cite_note-2]), in [Gyirong County](11219645.md) in the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region), opposite [Rasuwagadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasuwa_Fort) (रसुवागढी, *Rasuvāgaḍhī*) in Nepal. The two sides were connected by the Nepal–China Friendship Bridge, also known as the Miteri Bridge (from मितेरि, the [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) word for "friendship") or Risur Bridge (from its location in Risur, Gyirong County).[^cite_note-bridge2019-3]

The Gyirong valley had long served as a trade and travel route between Nepal and Tibet. After modernization, the port became important for cross-border trade and tourism.[^cite_note-b905-4][^cite_note-q808-5] Its road connection joined [China National Highway 216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_216) with Nepal's [National Highway 42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishuli_Corridor). The complex was damaged in the [2025 Nepal floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nepal_floods) and was ultimately destroyed during the [2026 Nepal floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nepal_floods).

## Location and transport

Road traffic crossed the border over the 110-metre (360 ft) Nepal–China Friendship Bridge, which opened in June 2019 after its predecessor was damaged in the [2015 Nepal earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2015_Nepal_earthquake).[^cite_note-bridge2019-3] The 2019 bridge was swept away by floods in July 2025. A temporary [Bailey bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_bridge) completed in December allowed the border to reopen before being destroyed by the 2026 floods.[^cite_note-6]

On the Chinese side, [China National Highway 216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_216) (G216) terminated at Gyirong Port and connected it northward with [Gyirong Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyirong_Town) and the Tibetan road network. In 2017, the Tibet Autonomous Region government described the 94.38-kilometre (58.65 mi) G216 approach between [Dzongka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzongka), the county seat, and Risur Bridge as having 23 geological-hazard sites, an average of approximately one every four kilometres (2.5 mi).[^cite_note-g216-7]

On the Nepali side, the road continued south from Rasuwagadhi as the [Trishuli Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishuli_Corridor) (NH42), through [Syaphrubesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syaphru), Mailung, Trishuli and [Galchhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galchhi_Rural_Municipality). NH42 is designated as a 197-kilometre (122 mi) national highway between Rasuwagadhi and [Thori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thori_Rural_Municipality) on the Nepal–India border.[^cite_note-dor-roads-8] Kathmandu-bound traffic can leave NH42 at Galchhi for [NH17](8203073.md), while the [Pasang Lhamu Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasang_Lhamu_Highway) (NH18) provides the older route from Syaphrubesi through Dhunche and Trishuli to Kathmandu. The opening of the Mailung–Syaphrubesi section in 2018 shortened the Galchhi–Rasuwagadhi journey by approximately 25 kilometres (16 mi) compared with the older route through Dhunche.[^cite_note-9]

## History

### Background

The Gyirong valley has been one of the most important transportation routes between Nepal and Tibet for over a millennium. In the 7th century, [Bhrikuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhrikuti) of Nepal entered Tibet via Gyirong to become a consort of [Songtsen Gampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songtsen_Gampo), the first [emperor of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Tibet). The [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) diplomat [Wang Xuance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xuance) travelled via Gyirong on his way to India, leaving one of the oldest stone inscriptions in Tibet near [Dzongka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzongka). [Padmasambhava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmasambhava), who according to tradition established [Vajrayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana) Buddhism in Tibet in the 8th century, crossed the Himalayas through the same route. The [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) army under [Fukang'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukang'an) counterattacked through the Gyirong valley in 1792 during the [Sino-Nepalese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Nepalese_War). A battle was fought over the river crossing at the present-day location of Gyirong Port.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

A customs office was established in Gyirong in 1961. From 1978, residents of the border area were permitted to cross the border for local trade. Because of limited transport and energy infrastructure, cross-border traffic remained largely confined to small-scale local trade for the following two decades.[^cite_note-13]

### Development



Under construction in 2013

Development of Gyirong Port began in the early 1990s, with infrastructure intended to facilitate trade between China and Nepal.[^cite_note-14] Around 2009, the think-tank Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation under the [Ministry of Commerce](638506.md) proposed to designate a 44.5 km<sup>2</sup> (17.2 mi<sup>2</sup>) cross-border economic cooperation zone in the area. In 2011, an environmental study was finished, in which it was found that 11.8 km<sup>2</sup> (4.6 mi<sup>2</sup>) of the planning area had suitable geological conditions.[^cite_note-15]

Its expansion into a modern logistics hub accelerated after the [2015 Nepal earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Nepal_earthquake) severely damaged the neighbouring [Zhangmu–Kodari crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Nepal_Friendship_Bridge), which had been the principal facility for bilateral overland trade.[^cite_note-u378-16][^cite_note-n323-17] The Chinese government subsequently prioritized Gyirong as an alternative trade corridor and invested in its infrastructure.[^cite_note-d214-18]

By 2018, the road between Gyirong Town and the border had been upgraded and a border-trade market had been built. In that year, Gyirong Port handled 75,332 tonnes of goods, with a total trade value of [CN¥](66958.md)1.39 billion.[^cite_note-n092-19] In 2020, more than 60 per cent of trade between China and Nepal passed through Gyirong.[^cite_note-20] In 2024, the trade volume reached CN¥4.25 billion, accounting for almost 30 per cent of China–Nepal trade.[^cite_note-zaobao-21]

Gyirong Port was part of China's [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative) as a node in the China–Nepal Economic Corridor.[^cite_note-b905-4][^cite_note-v599-22] The Chinese government implemented policies intended to attract investment and increase trade, including tax incentives for businesses operating through the port and streamlined approval processes for cross-border projects.[^cite_note-o083-23]

### 2025 flood damage

Gyirong Port closed after the [2025 Rasuwagadhi flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nepal_floods) on 8 July destroyed the "Friendship Bridge" across the border and left 17 people missing in the surrounding area.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-bjnews2026-25] Customs clearance resumed over a temporary [Bailey bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_bridge) in January 2026.[^cite_note-26]

### 2026 flood destruction

Floodwater and debris engulfing the port complex during the [2026 Nepal floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nepal_floods). The view is downstream along the Trishuli River, with the flood coming down from the Lhende Khola from the left.

On 26 August 2026, floodwater and debris engulfed the port complex during the [2026 Nepal floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nepal_floods).[^cite_note-27] Roads and communication links serving the port were disrupted.[^cite_note-28]

Radar imagery acquired by the PowerChina-1 satellite on 27 August showed that the flood had widened the river channel at Gyirong Port.[^cite_note-29] By 28 August, rescuers who reached the area reported that the port complex and several nearby bridges had been destroyed.[^cite_note-\:0-30] A [Chinese flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_China) was planted on the former site of the checkpoint building by 3 September, signifying that a rescue corridor has been opened to the site.[^cite_note-31]

As of September 6, 2026, Chinese authorities reported 43 people dead and 519 missing on the Chinese side of the border,[^cite_note-32] including 261 foreign nationals.[^cite_note-33]
