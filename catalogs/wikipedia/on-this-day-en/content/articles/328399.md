**Aqābaʾ** ([/ˈækəbə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*AK-ə-bə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key),[^cite_note-Dictionary-2] [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) also [/ˈɑːk-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*AHK-*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-3] [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الْعَقَبَة, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-ʿAqaba*, pronounced [\[ælˈʕæqɑba, ælˈʕæɡæba\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)) is the only coastal city in [Jordan](7515964.md) and the largest and most populous city on the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba).[^cite_note-Ad_Dustour-4] Situated in southernmost Jordan, Aqaba is the administrative center of the [Aqaba Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Governorate).[^cite_note-aqabazone-5] The city had a population of 148,398 in 2015 and a land area of 375 square kilometres (144.8 sq mi).[^cite_note-Demographics-6]

Aqaba's location at the northeastern tip of the [Red Sea](26191.md) between the continents of Asia and Africa has made its port important for millennia.[^cite_note-worldfolio-7] The ancient city was called *[Elath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elath)*, whose proximity to copper mines made it a regional hub for copper production and trade in the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) period. Under [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) rule, Aela became a bishopric, and the [Aqaba Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Church), the world's oldest known purpose-built Christian church, was constructed there.[^cite_note-First_purpose-built_church-8] Following the [Islamic conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) around AD 650, the city became known as *Ayla* and later became a Latin Catholic [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see). The name *Aqaba* did not come into use until the late medieval period.[^cite_note-refD1-9] During the [Great Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Arab_Revolt), the prominent [Battle of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aqaba) saw joint Arab and British victory over [Ottoman](22278.md) troops.

Aqaba's location next to [Wadi Rum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Rum) and [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra) has made it one of the major tourist attractions in Jordan.[^cite_note-UEFA-10] The city is administered by the [Aqaba Special Economic Zone Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Special_Economic_Zone_Authority), which has turned Aqaba into a low-tax, duty-free city, attracting several tourist resort area like [Ayla Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayla_Oasis), [Saraya Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraya_Aqaba), [Marsa Zayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsa_Zayed), and Tala Bay.[^cite_note-king_checks-11] They are expected to turn the city into a major tourism hub in the region.[^cite_note-yourmiddleast-12] However, industrial and commercial activities remain important, due to the strategic location of the city as the country's only [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport).[^cite_note-Jean-13]

The city is served by the [King Hussein International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein_International_Airport), while the [Port of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Aqaba) serves as Jordan's principal seaport and a gateway for other countries in the region.[^cite_note-worldfolio-7] The city sits right across the border from [Eilat](66162.md), likewise Israel's only port on the Red Sea. After the 1994 [Israel–Jordan peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Jordan_peace_treaty), there were plans and hopes of establishing a trans-border tourism and economic area, but few of those plans have become real.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Name

### Elath and derivates

The [ ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East) [endonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym) (name given by the local population) of the city was **_[Elath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elath)_** or **_Ailath_**. The name may be derived from the [Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages) name of a tree in the genus *[Pistacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistacia)*,[^cite_note-17] or from the [West Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Semitic) word \**'ayl* 'ram' with the feminine suffix *-at*. A [ Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_language) variant of the name אֵלוֹת is mentioned once in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) in [1 Kings 9\:26](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=1%20Kings%209\:26\&version=nrsv), reflecting the [Phoenician vowel shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_language#Vowels).[^cite_note-18] Modern [Eilat](66162.md) (established 1947), situated about 5 km (3.1 mi) north-west of Aqaba, also takes its name from the ancient settlement. In the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), it was renamed ***Berenice*** ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Βερενίκη *Bereníkē*), but the original name survived, and under [Roman rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Petraea) was re-introduced in the forms **_Aila_**,[^cite_note-19] *Aela* or *Haila*, adopted in Byzantine Greek as Αἴλα *Aíla* and in Arabic as *Ayla* (آيلا).[^cite_note-20] The [crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) called the city ***Elyn***.[^cite_note-auto-21]

### Aqaba

The present name *al-ʿAqaba* (العقبة) is a shortened form of *ʿaqabat ʿAylah* (عقبة آيلة, usually translated as 'the mountain-pass of Ayla'), a name first mentioned in the 12th century by [Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi), at a time when the settlement had been mostly reduced to a military stronghold (not positively identified by archaeologists; see [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Fortress#Crusader/Ayyubid_period)),[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] possibly referring to the pass just to the north-east of the settlement ([29°33′32″N 35°05′42″E  /  29.559°N 35.095°E  / 29.559; 35.095](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Aqaba\&params=29.559_N_35.095_E_), now traversed by the Jordanian [Aqaba Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Highway)).

The root of *ʿaqaba\[t]* means slope, the word itself meaning either 'mountain road', 'pass', 'ascent', or in a wider sense 'obstacle'. Authors like Harold W. Glidden (1952) connect the name to the pass to the north through Wadi el-Yutm (see the [King's Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Highway_\(ancient\))), with [Alois Musil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Musil) seeing behind the *aqabat* the pass to the south toward [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) (see [History of the Hajj: Egyptian route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Hajj#Egyptian_route)), while Donald Whitcomb suggests a connection to a hillock inside the settlement, interpreting the name as "slope/obstacle of Ayla".[^cite_note-DW97-24]

## History



Main historical sites in the northern part of the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba)

### Nearby Chalcolithic sites



Tall Hujayrat Al-Ghuzlan excavations

Excavations at two [tells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_\(archaeology\)) (archaeological mounds) [Tall Hujayrat Al-Ghuzlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tall_Hujayrat_Al-Ghuzlan) and [Tall Al-Magass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tall_Al-Magass), both a few kilometres north of modern-day Aqaba city, revealed inhabited settlements from c. 4000 BC during the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) period, with thriving [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) production on a large scale.[^cite_note-historic_places-25] This period is largely unknown due to the absence of written historical sources.[^cite_note-academia-26] [University of Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jordan) archaeologists have discovered the sites, where they found a small building whose walls were inscribed with human and animal drawings, suggesting that the building was used as a religious site. The people who inhabited the site had developed an extensive water system in irrigating their crops which were mostly made up of grapes, olives and wheat. Several different-sized clay pots were also found suggesting that copper production was a major industry in the region, the pots being used in melting the copper and reshaping it. Scientific studies performed on-site revealed that it had undergone two earthquakes, with the latter one leaving the site completely destroyed.[^cite_note-27]

### Early history

#### Elath

Further information: [Elath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elath)

The [Edomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edom), who ruled over [Edom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edom) just south of the [Dead Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea), are believed to have built the first port in Aqaba called [Elath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elath) around 1500 BC, turning it into a major hub for the trade of copper as the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) helped them develop their maritime economy. They profited from its strategic location at the junction of trading routes between Asia and Africa.

#### Tell el-Kheleifeh

Archaeologists have investigated an [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) settlement at [Tell el-Kheleifeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_el-Kheleifeh), immediately west of Aqaba, inhabited between the 8th and 4th centuries BCE.[^cite_note-\:0-28]

#### Undefined

Around 735 BC, the city was conquered by the [Assyrian empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire). Because of the wars the Assyrians were fighting in the east, their trading routes were diverted to the city and the port witnessed relative prosperity. The [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire) conquered it in 600 BC. During this time, Elath witnessed great economic growth, which is attributed to the business background of its rulers who realized how important the city's location was. The Persian [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) took the city in 539 BC.[^cite_note-AQABA-29]

### Classical antiquity

#### Hellenistic period

Further information: [History of the Romans in Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Romans_in_Arabia), [Arabia Petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Petraea), and [Via Traiana Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Traiana_Nova)

The city continued to grow and prosper which made it a major trading hub by the time of the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_Greece) rule by 300 BC, after the [Wars of Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_Alexander_the_Great), it was described by a Greek historian to be "one of the most important trading cities in the Arab World".[^cite_note-AQABA-29] The [Ptolemaic Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Greek) called it *Berenice*.[^cite_note-ahp1-30]

The [Nabatean kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabatean_kingdom) had its capital north of the city, at [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra).

#### Roman period



[Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) milestone that marked the starting point of the [Via Nova Traiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Nova_Traiana)

In 64 BC, following the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) conquest, they annexed the city and called it *Aela* (also *Haila*, *Aelana*, in Greek rendered Αἴλα *Aila*).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-\:0-28]

Both Petra and Aela were under strong [Nabatean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabatean) influence despite the Roman rule. Aela reached its peak during Roman times, the great long-distance road the [Via Traiana Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Traiana_Nova) led south from [Bostra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bostra) through [Amman](45007.md), terminating in Aela, where it connected with a west road leading to the [Paralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralia_\(Seleucid_eparchy\)) and [Roman Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Egypt).

Around AD 106 Aela was one of the main ports for the Romans.

#### Late Roman and Byzantine periods

Further information: [Aqaba Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Church) and [Palaestina Tertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Tertia)

The [Aqaba Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Church) was constructed under Roman rule between 293 and 303 and is considered to be the oldest known purpose-built Christian [church](6325.md) in the world.[^cite_note-First_purpose-built_church-8] By the time of [Eusebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius), Aela became the garrison of the [Legio X Fretensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legio_X_Fretensis), which was moved to Aela from Jerusalem.[^cite_note-32]



Aqaba Church from around AD 300

One of the oldest known texts written in the [Arabic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet) is a late 4th-century inscription found in [Jabal Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Ram) 50 kilometres (31 miles) east of Aqaba.[^cite_note-33]

The city became a [Christian](5211.md) [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric) at an early stage. Its bishop Peter was present at the [First Council of Nicaea](11118.md),[^cite_note-34] the first [ecumenical council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_council), in 325. Beryllus was at the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon) in 451, and Paul at [the synod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Jerusalem_\(536\)) called by [Patriarch Peter of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_Theophilus_I_of_Jerusalem) in 536 against [Patriarch Anthimus I of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Church_of_Alexandria), a council attended by bishops of the Late [Roman provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Palaestina Prima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Prima), [Palaestina Secunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Secunda) and [Palaestina Tertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Tertia), to the last-named of which Aela belonged.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

A citadel was also built in the area that became the focal point of the Roman southern defense system.

In the 6th century, [Procopius of Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procopius_of_Caesarea) mentioned a Jewish population in Eilat and its surroundings which enjoyed autonomy until the time of [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) (r. 527–565).

According to [Ibn Ishaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Ishaq), [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) himself reached Aila during the [expedition of Tabuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_Tabuk) of 630, and extracted tribute from the city.[^cite_note-37]

During the Late Byzantine or even Early Muslim period, Aila was the origin of what came to be known as the [Ayla-Axum amphoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayla-Axum_amphoras?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-38]

### Early Muslim Ayla

Aila fell to the [Islamic armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_conquest_of_the_Levant) by 629, and the ancient settlement was left to decay, while a new Arab city was established outside its walls under [Uthman ibn Affan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman_ibn_Affan),[^cite_note-39] known as *Ayla* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): آيلا).

The Early Muslim city was excavated in 1986 by a team from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). Artefacts are now on exhibit at [Aqaba Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Archaeological_Museum) and [Jordan Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Archaeological_Museum) in [Amman](45007.md). The fortified city was inscribed in a rectangle of 170 by 145 metres (558 ft × 476 ft), with walls 2.6 metres (8 ft 6 in) thick and 4.5 metres (14 ft 9 in) high, surrounding a fortified structure, occupying an area of 35 by 55 metres (115 ft × 180 ft). 24 towers defended the city. The city had four gates on all four sides, defining two main lines intersecting at the centre. The intersection of these two thoroughfares was indicated by a [tetrapylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapylon) (a four-way arch), which was later transformed into a luxury residential building decorated with frescoes dated to the tenth century. This type of urban structure, known as a *[misr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsar)* (pl. *amsar*), is typical of early Islamic fortified settlements.

The city prospered from 661 to 750 under the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) and beyond under the [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasids) (750–970) and the [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids) (970–1116). Ayla took advantage of its key position as an important step on the road to [India](14533.md) and Arab spices (frankincense, myrrh), between the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md). The city is also mentioned in several stories of the [Arabian Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Nights).

The geographer [Shams Eddin Muqaddasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Muqaddasi) describes Ayla as nearby the ruined ancient city.[^cite_note-40]

The city was mentioned in Medieval Arabic sources as having a mixed population of Jews and Christians. It subsequently became an important station for pilgrim caravans on the way to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca).[^cite_note-41]

The city was "completely destroyed" by the [1068 Near East earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1068_Near_East_earthquake).[^cite_note-Klinger-42]

### Crusader/Ayyubid and Mamluk periods

See also: [Lordship of Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordship_of_Transjordan)



The [Muslim fortress of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Fortress)

[Baldwin I of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_I_of_Jerusalem) took over the city in 1115 without encountering much resistance. The centre of the city then moved to 500 meters along the coast to the south, and the crusader fortress of *Elyn* was built, which allowed the [Kingdom of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) to dominate all roads between [Damascus](8914.md), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate), and [Arabia](47858.md), protecting the [Crusader states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states) from the east and allowing for profitable raids on trade caravans passing through the area. In order to secure this strategic position, Baldwin also built and garrisoned a fortress on [Pharaoh's Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh's_Island) (called *Île de Graye* by the Franks), the modern *Jazīrat Fir'aun* in [Egyptian](8087628.md) territorial waters about 7 kilometres (4.3 miles) west of Aqaba.[^cite_note-auto-21]

The garrison of Elyn (now serving primarily as a military outpost) was further strengthened in 1142 by [Pagan the Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_the_Butler), [Lord of Oultrejourdain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oultrejourdain), who pursued an ambitious program of castle building throughout his domain. However, there was no large-scale settlement of Europeans in the area, and the region between the [Dead Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea) and the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba) remained mainly inhabited by Bedouins, who were obliged to pay tribute to the Lordship of Oultrejourdain.[^cite_note-43] Despite all efforts to fortify the region, the city was captured in 1170 by a squadron sent by [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin) as he was besieging [Gaza](55387.md); while it was successfully raided by [Raynald of Châtillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raynald_of_Ch%C3%A2tillon) in 1182,[^cite_note-44] it was never retaken by the Crusaders.[^cite_note-45]

What is known today as [Aqaba Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Fortress) was built by [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)) sultan [Al-Ashraf Qansuh Al-Ghuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ashraf_Qansuh_Al-Ghuri) in the early 16th century, possibly on top of the Crusader fort. For the next four centuries, the site was a simple fishing village of little importance.

### Modern history

During World War I, the Ottoman forces were forced to withdraw from Aqaba in 1917 after the [Battle of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aqaba), led by [T. E. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._E._Lawrence) and the Arab forces of [Auda Abu Tayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auda_Abu_Tayi) and [Sherif Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_ibn_Ali_ibn_Hussein?action=edit\&redlink=1). The capture of Aqaba allowed the British to supply the Arab forces.[^cite_note-clio-46] In 1918, the regions of Aqaba and Ma'an were officially incorporated into the [Kingdom of the Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Hejaz). In 1925, [Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud) the ruler of [Nejd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nejd) with the help of his Wahhabi [Ikhwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikhwan) troops successfully annexed the Hejaz, but gave up the Ma'an and Aqaba to the British [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) of [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Transjordan).[^cite_note-47]

The Jordanian census of 1961 found 8,908 inhabitants in 'Aqaba.[^cite_note-48]

In 1965, [King Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein), through an exchange deal with [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), gave 6,000 square kilometres (2,317 square miles) of desert land in Jordanian territories in exchange for other territories, including 12 kilometres (7 miles) of an extension of prime coastline south of Aqaba, which included the Yamanieh [coral reef](87410.md).[^cite_note-49] Aqaba was a major site for imports of [Iraqi](7515928.md) goods in the 1980s until the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War).[^cite_note-50]

In 1997, the [Aqaba Marine Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Marine_Reserve) was established within the southern boundaries of the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba).

On 27 June 2022, a port in Aqaba became the scene of a [toxic gas leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Aqaba_toxic_gas_leak) when a chemical storage container carrying 25 tons of chlorine fell from a crane onto a ship and ruptured. The leak resulted in the deaths of 13 people.

## Geography

The city lies at Jordan's southernmost point, on the Gulf of Aqaba lying at the tip of the Red Sea. Its strategic location is shown in the fact that it is located at the crossroads of the continents of Asia and Africa, while bordering Israel, Egypt and Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-51]

### Climate

Aqaba has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWh*) with mild, sometimes warm winters and very hot dry summers. Subzero temperatures can be observed every few years. The record high temperature of 49.6 °C (121.3 °F) was registered on 14 August 2025 [^cite_note-52]. The record low temperature of −3.9 °C (25.0 °F) was on January 16, 2008, as in [Eilat](66162.md).

## Local government

In August 2000, the Aqaba Special Economic Zone Authority ([ASEZA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEZA)) was established which acted as the statutory institution empowered with administrative, fiscal, regulatory and economic responsibilities.[^cite_note-55]

### Administrative divisions

Jordan is divided into 12 administrative divisions, each called a [Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate). [Aqaba Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Governorate) divides into 3 [Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahias_of_Jordan), some of which are divided into Subdistricts and further divided into villages.

## Economy

With status as Jordan's special economic zone, Aqaba's economy is based on the tourism and port industry sectors.[^cite_note-Ad_Dustour-4][^cite_note-worldfolio-7] Aqaba's location next to [Wadi Rum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Rum) and [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra) has strengthened the city's location on the world map and made it one of the major tourist attractions in Jordan.[^cite_note-UEFA-10] The city is administered by the [Aqaba Special Economic Zone Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Special_Economic_Zone_Authority), which has turned Aqaba into a low-tax, duty-free city, attracting several mega projects like [Ayla Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayla_Oasis), [Saraya Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraya_Aqaba), [Marsa Zayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsa_Zayed) and expansion of the Port of Aqaba.[^cite_note-king_checks-11] They are expected to turn the city into a major tourism hub in the region.[^cite_note-yourmiddleast-12] Industrial and commercial activities remain important, due to the strategic location of the city as the country's only [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport).[^cite_note-Jean-13]

Aqaba is the only seaport of Jordan so virtually all of Jordan's exports depart from here. Heavy machinery industry is also flourishing in the city with regional assembly plants being located in Aqaba such as the [Land Rover Aqaba Assembly Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover_Aqaba_Assembly_Plant). By 2008 the ASEZ had attracted \$18bn in committed investments, beating its \$6bn target by 2020 by a third and more in less than a decade. The goal was adjusted to bring in another \$12bn by 2020, but in 2009 alone, deals worth \$14bn were inked.[^cite_note-oxfordbusinessgroup.com-56] Some projects currently under construction are:

- [Marsa Zayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsa_Zayed) a \$10 billion is the largest mega mixed-use development project ever envisioned in both Jordan and the region. Marsa Zayed will host facilities including residential neighborhoods, commercial outlets and amenities, entertainment venues, financial and business facilities, and a number of hotels. Additionally, the property will feature marinas and a cruise ship terminal. Marsa Zayed will encompass 6.4 million square meters of built-up property.

- [Saraya Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraya_Aqaba), a \$1.5 billion resort with a man made lagoon, luxury hotels, villas, and townhouses that will be completed by 2017.

- [Ayla Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayla_Oasis), Ayla Oasis is a mixed-use waterfront development in [Aqaba](328399.md), southern [Jordan](7515964.md), developed by Ayla Oasis Development Company (AODC), a subsidiary of the [Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astra_Industrial_Group?action=edit\&redlink=1) group. It is located in the Al Nakhil area near Al Farouq Street, approximately 330 kilometers from the capital, Amman, roughly five minutes from Aqaba's city center and ten minutes from [King Hussein International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein_International_Airport).&#x20;

  [^cite_note-57]

  &#x20;The development spans approximately 4.3 million square meters with a 235-meter waterfront along the [Red Sea](26191.md). Ayla Oasis adds approximately an additional 17 km of waterfront to the city’s shoreline through its lagoon system, which was developed by constructing a series of artificial lakes and lagoons with a total area of around 75 hectares, and represents a total investment of approximately US\$2.1 billion. It includes lagoon-front, golf-front, and beachfront residential communities, as well as hotels, commercial spaces, and recreational facilities. The development also borders the archaeological site of [Tell el-Kheleifeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_el-Kheleifeh). The project originated in 2002 when the Jordanian government allocated the 4.3 million square meter site in Aqaba for investment. [Sabih Al-Masri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabih_al-Masri) established AODC through his Astra group in June 2003 to develop the project.&#x20;

The port relocation 20 kilometres (12 miles) to the south will cost an estimated \$600m and will improve infrastructure, while freeing up space for development in the city. Plans for upgrading the King Hussein International Airport (KHIA) and the development of a logistics centre will also help position Aqaba as a regional hub for trade and transport.

### Tourism



View of Tala Bay resort south of Aqaba



Aqaba's coral reefs have made it one of the best diving spots in the world

Aqaba has a number of luxury hotels, including in the Tala Bay resort 20 kilometres (12 miles) further to the south, which service those who come for fun on the beaches as well as [Scuba diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving). Aqaba offers more than thirty primary diving locations, with the majority of them accommodating divers of all skill levels. These diving sites comprise fringing reefs that extend for over 25 kilometres (16 miles), reaching all the way to the border with Saudi Arabia.

It also offers activities which take advantage of its desert location. Its many coffee shops offer [mansaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansaf) and [knafeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knafeh), and [baqlawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqlawa) desserts. Another very popular venue is the [hamam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammam) built in 306 AD, in which locals and visitors alike come to relax after a hot day.



A beach in Aqaba.

In 2006, the Tourism Division of the Aqaba Special Economic Zone Authority ([ASEZA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEZA)) reported that the number of tourists visiting the Zone in 2006 rose to about 432,000, an increase of 5% over previous year. Approximately 65%, or 293,000 were Jordanians. Of foreign tourists, Europeans visited the Zone in the largest numbers, with about 98,000 visiting during the year. The division has financed tourism advertising and media campaigns with the assistance of the European Union.[^cite_note-62]

Aqaba has been chosen for the site of a new waterfront building project that would rebuild Aqaba with new man-made water structures, new high-rise residential and office buildings, and more tourist services to place Aqaba on the investment map and challenge other centers of waterfront development throughout the region.

Aqaba was chosen as the Arab Tourism City of 2011.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-Jean-13]

During the 5-day holiday at both the end of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) and [Eid Al-Adha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_Al-Adha), Jordanian and western expats flock into the city with numbers reaching up to 50,000 visitors. During this time the occupancy rate of most hotels there reaches as high as 90%, and are often fully booked.[^cite_note-66]

The several development projects (i.e. Ayla, Saraya etc.) now taking place in Aqaba provide "opportunities of empowerment" for local populations that want to expand their agency within the city. According to Fulbright scholar Kimberly Cavanagh development projects will help exhibit the ways global- local partnerships and the resultant cultural exchanges,  can result in mutually beneficial outcomes.[^cite_note-67]

Skyline of Aqaba

## Transport

### Rail

The [Aqaba railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba_Railway_Corporation) which transported phosphate to the old port ceased operations in 2018. A successor line to transport phosphate from [Al Shidiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Shidiya?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Ghor es-Safi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghor_es-Safi) to the new terminal in [Port of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Aqaba) is planned through an agreement between Jordan's Ministry for Transport and [Etihad Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad_Rail).[^cite_note-72]

There has been propositions to connect [Eilat](66162.md) to Aqaba by rail.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

### Airports

[King Hussein International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein_International_Airport) is the only civilian airport outside of Amman in the country, located to the north of Aqaba. It is a 20-minutes drive away from the city center. Regular flights are scheduled from Amman to Aqaba with an average flying time of 45 minutes which is serviced by [Royal Jordanian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Jordanian_Airlines) and [Jordan Aviation Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Aviation). Several international airlines connect the city to Istanbul, Dubai, Alexandria, Sharm el-Sheikh, and other destinations in Asia and Europe.[^cite_note-Getting_to_Aqaba-75] Since the 1994 Peace Treaty between Israel and Jordan, there were plans to jointly develop airport infrastructure in the region.[^cite_note-76] However, when Israel built [Ramon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Airport) only 10 km distant from King Hussein International Airport, this happened without consulting the Jordanian side, which caused a slight deterioration of bilateral relations between the two countries and concerns over the safety of having two airports so close together.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

### Roads



Taxis in Aqaba

Aqaba is connected by an 8,000 kilometres (5,000 mi) modern highway system to surrounding countries. The city is connected to the rest of Jordan by the [Desert Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_15_\(Jordan\)) and the [King's Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_65_\(Jordan\)) that provides access to the resorts and settlements on the [Dead Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea).[^cite_note-Getting_to_Aqaba-75] Aqaba is connected to [Eilat](66162.md) in [Israel](9282173.md) by taxi and bus services passing through the [Wadi Araba crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Araba_crossing), as well as to [Haql](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haql) in Saudi Arabia by the [Durra Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durra_Border_Crossing). There are many bus services between Aqaba and Amman and the other major cities in [Jordan](7515964.md), JETT and Trust International are the most common lines. These tourist buses are spacious and installed with air conditioning and bathrooms.[^cite_note-p266-79]

### Port



The [Port of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Aqaba) is the only port in Jordan.

The [Port of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Aqaba) is the only port in Jordan. Regular ferry routes to Taba are available on a daily basis and are operated by several companies such as Sindbad for Marine Transportation and [Arab Bridge Maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Bridge_Maritime). The routes serve mainly the Egyptian coastal cities on the gulf like Taba and Sharm Al Sheikh.[^cite_note-Getting_to_Aqaba-75] In 2006, the port was ranked as being the "Best Container Terminal" in the Middle East by *[Lloyd's List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd's_List)*. The port was chosen for its recent improvements and its ability to handle local traffic as well as international traffic to four neighboring countries.[^cite_note-80]

## Wildlife

The [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba) is rich with marine life. The gulf is home to approximately 500 fish species, with many being permanent residents, like [lion fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_fish) and [octopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopus), while others are migratory, typically appearing during the summer, such as [sailfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailfish), considered the fastest fish in the ocean, as well as the world's largest fish, the [whale shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_shark). Marine mammals and reptiles also inhabit the gulf during summer, [hawksbill sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawksbill_sea_turtle), and [bottle nosed dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottlenose_dolphin) call Aqaba's gulf home as well. A large number of predatory [shark](43617.md) species used to inhabit Aqaba's gulf but, due to overfishing and pollution, this population is in decline. While mostly deep water sharks such as the [tiger sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_shark) and [thresher sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thresher_shark) are present, there are also a small number of [reef sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_shark) as well. The world's fastest shark, the [short-fin mako shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortfin_mako_shark) is the most commonly caught species by fishermen in Aqaba. [Whale sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_sharks), locally known as Battan, are the most commonly sighted. Conservationists are working hard to protect Aqaba's shark population.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

The gulf of Aqaba hosts more than 390 bird species including migratory birds such as the [greater flamingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_flamingo), [great white pelican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_white_pelican) and the [pink-backed pelican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink-backed_pelican).[^cite_note-83]
