The **Egypt national football team**,[^cite_note-2] nicknamed **the Pharaohs**, represents [Egypt](8087628.md) in men's [international](11049.md) [football](10568.md), and is governed by the [Egyptian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Football_Association) (EFA), the governing body of [football in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Egypt). It has been a member of [FIFA](11049.md) since 1923 and was a founding member of the [CAF](715265.md) in 1957.[^cite_note-3]

Egypt is the oldest and most successful team in the history of African football, having won the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) for a [record 7 times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Cup_of_Nations_records_and_statistics) and reached the final 10 times.[^cite_note-4] They won the cup 3 times consecutively in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) which is a feat unmatched by any other team.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Internationally, Egypt is the first country in the world outside Europe and the Americas to participate in the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) and the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md). The Egyptians qualified to 13 Olympic Games (an African record), as well as 4 World Cups. Egypt’s first Olympic football participation came in [1920 Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics). Their first participation in a World Cup, slated to be in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) but the team couldn't travel due to a storm,[^cite_note-7] was in the [1934 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup). Their best result came in [2026](36581929.md), when they reached the Round of 16 after advancing past the group stage for the first time in their history.

Egypt achieved 4th place at 3 Olympics: in [1928 Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics), [1964 Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics), and [2024 Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament). The Egyptians have also reached the Quarter-finals of Olympic football at 5 other editions. Moreover, [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_national_under-20_football_team) earned the honor of becoming a [bronze medalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship#Final_ranking) at the [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) [FIFA U-20 World Cup](1168189.md) in [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship).[^cite_note-8] In 2010, Egypt reached 9th place in the [FIFA world rankings](719095.md),[^cite_note-9] marking a massively successful era in Egyptian history that saw the Pharaohs win the [2010 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and defeated [World Champions Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in the [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) the year before.[^cite_note-9th-10][^cite_note-11]

## History

Main pages: [Category\:Egypt at the men's Olympic football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Egypt_at_the_men's_Olympic_football_tournament), [Egypt at the FIFA World Cup](38717106.md), and [Egypt at the Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_at_the_Africa_Cup_of_Nations)

### 1920s: International successes before continental domination

The inaugural [Olympic football tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) of 1900 and 1904 have seen the participation of European and North American club teams respectively. However, for a long history between 1908 and 1948, the Olympic Games were the world’s most prestigious [Association football](10568.md) competition. Hence, Full "National A" teams used to participate in Olympic football tournaments.



Egypt national team at the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Summer_Olympics)



Egypt national team finished in the fourth place at the [1928 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics)

In light of this, the first Egyptian national football team was constituted in 1920. The Pharaohs became the first [African football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Africa) to compete in the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics) in [Belgium](3343.md). Egypt became the first country in the world outside Europe and the Americas to participate in the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics). The opening match of their campaign was a close 2–1 loss against the [Italians](362466.md).

Egypt then made its second Olympic appearance at the [1924 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics) in [Paris](22989.md). In the second round, the Egyptians have astonishingly defeated the global elites [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) 3–0. But they were ultimately eliminated in the quarter-finals against [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team).[^cite_note-12]

The Egyptian team made history at the [1928 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics) in [Amsterdam](844.md) by finishing in 4th place, becoming the first African and non-European team to reach the semi-finals of a major global football tournament.[^cite_note-13]

### 1934–1952: First World Cup appearance

After the creation of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), the [FIFA](11049.md) has restricted the participation of elite players in the Olympics to prevent the prestigious [Olympic football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) tournament from eclipsing its newly born international cup.

Between 1952 and 1980, national amateur teams were the type of team allowed by FIFA to participate in the Olympics. However, countries from the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact), mainly Eastern Europe, competed with essentially professional players nominally employed by organs of the state such as armed forces.

While in 1984 and 1988, national teams (with [UEFA](32332.md)/[CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) restrictions) were allowed to participate. Then in 1992, the FIFA decided national U23 teams can participate. But since 1996, national U23 team with 3 overage players are allowed to compete at the Olympics.



Egypt national football team at the [1934 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) in Italy

Since the inauguration of the FIFA World Cup, [Egypt has qualified for the FIFA World Cup 4 times](38717106.md): in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), in [1990](157232.md), in [2018](13327177.md), and in [2026](36581929.md). In the [1934 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), Egypt became the first African country to qualify for the World Cup, beating [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine_national_football_team) in the first leg by a score of 7–1 in [Cairo](6293.md) and the second leg by a score of 4–1 in [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-14]

In the 1920s and 1930s, the Pharaohs featured iconic pioneers of Egyptian football, including [Mohamed Latif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Latif), [Mostafa Taha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Taha), [Mahmoud Mokhtar El-Tetsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Mokhtar_El-Tetsh), [Mustafa Mansour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Mansour), and [Hassan El-Far](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_El-Far). They lost to [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) 4–2 in their first and only match in 1934, with the two Egyptian goals scored by [Abdulrahman Fawzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulrahman_Fawzi).



[Abdulrahman Fawzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulrahman_Fawzi) scored two goals in the [1934 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) in Italy.

In the [qualification process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the [1938 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup). The Egypt national football team competed at the [1936 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) in [Berlin](3354.md), officially finishing in equal 9th place. They played only one match, a Round of 16 fixture on 5 August 1936, where they were defeated 3–1 by [Austria](695226.md), with [Abdel Karim Sakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel-Karim_Sakr) scoring Egypt's lone goal. Egypt finished in joint-ninth place overall at the [1948 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1948_Summer_Olympics) in [London](17867.md). They began their campaign with a 7–1 victory over [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) but were subsequently eliminated in the next round, suffering a 2–1 loss to [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) in extra time. The Pharaohs featured several notable stars of the era: forwards: [Yehia Emam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehia_Emam), [Hanafy Bastan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafy_Bastan), [El-Sayed El-Dhizui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Sayed_El-Dhizui), [Abdel-Karim Sakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel-Karim_Sakr), [Ahmed Mekkawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mekkawi), and [Abdel Aziz El-Hammami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Aziz_El-Hammami). Egypt withdrew. They did not enter [1950 qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification).

### 1952–1970: First African Cup titles and another Olympic achievement

The Egypt national football team competed at the [1952 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1952_Summer_Olympics) in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki), finishing in joint 9th place. The Egyptian squad defeated Chile 5–4 in a thrilling match in Lahti, where [El-Sayed El-Dhizui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Sayed_El-Dhizui) scored a [hat-trick](411541.md), and [Ahmed Mekkawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mekkawi) and [Sharif El-Far](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_El-Far) added a goal each. Egypt then was eliminated after losing 3–1 to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_national_football_team_results) on 20 July 1952. The Pharaohs won the [gold medal](526488.md) at the inaugural [1953 Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1953_Arab_Games) hosted in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria). Managed by [Abdelrahman Fawzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelrahman_Fawzy), the Egyptian squad dominated the tournament, securing victories against [Palestine](1302455.md) by a score of 8–1, [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) 10–2, and defeating [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team) 4–0 in the final. The squad featured key players from the era's top clubs, including [Hanafy Bastan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafy_Bastan), [El-Sayed El-Dhizui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Sayed_El-Dhizui), [Sharif El-Far](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_El-Far), and [Ad-Diba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad-Diba).[^cite_note-15]



Egypt's captain [Hanafy Bastan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafy_Bastan) (right) with the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) trophy in 1957

In [1954 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), they faced [Italy](362466.md) but failed to qualify. The Egyptian team won the [gold medal](526488.md) at the [1955 Mediterranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1955_Mediterranean_Games) held in [Barcelona](4443.md). This marked Egypt's first-ever tournament championship in [Mediterranean Games football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Mediterranean_Games).[^cite_note-16] The squad was managed and represented by a golden generation of Egyptian football, featuring key players such as defender [Hanafy Bastan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafy_Bastan), [Essam Baheeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essam_Baheeg), and [Raafat Attia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raafat_Attia). In 1957, The Pharaohs first participated in the first [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) tournament in 1957. In their first game, a semi-final, they faced [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_national_football_team), winning 2–1; Egypt won 4–0 in the final.[^cite_note-17]



Egyptian squad in 1959

Egypt withdrew for the [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC/CAF%E2%80%93UEFA_play-off\)) World Cup due to the fact that they had to play Israel in qualification[^cite_note-18], who was a political enemy (see [Egypt–Israel relations](21337265.md) article). [One year later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_African_Cup_of_Nations), Egypt won the AFCON tournament for the second time in a row. There were only three teams in that tournament, Ethiopia, Sudan, and Egypt, who were undefeated.[^cite_note-19] Between 1958 and 1961, the country had a political union with Syria and went under the name of [United Arab Republic (UAR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic), though the Egyptian team's records are attributed to Egypt only by [FIFA](11049.md) as it was represented by Egyptian footballers.&#x20;

The Egyptian squad competed at the 1960 Summer Olympics in Rome under the name UAR. They finished third in Group C, recording one win, one draw, and one loss to be eliminated in the preliminary round. Additionally, they were tied in 13th place with Chinese Taipei, Tunisia, and India. (see [Rome 1960 Olympics Football Results](https://www.olympics.com/en/olympic-games/rome-1960/results/football/football-men)) In the [1962 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Africa_Cup_of_Nations),[^cite_note-20] Egypt lost 4-2 to Ethiopia, marking the first time to get eliminated from the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md). The Pharaohs withdrew from the [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) and [1970 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) tournaments afterwards.

Egypt's fourth AFCON appearance came in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_African_Cup_of_Nations) in [Ghana](12067.md). Egypt was placed in Group B with Sudan and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team), defeating [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) 6–3, but drawing 2–2 against Sudan. Despite being undefeated in the group stage, they were ranked second behind Sudan by goal difference (see [African Nations Cup 1963](https://www.rsssf.org/tables/63a.html)). Egypt, as runners-up in Group B, participated in the third-place match, playing against Ethiopia, winning 3–0.[^cite_note-21]

The Pharaohs had again achieved their best-ever Olympic result at the [1964 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics) in [Tokyo](30057.md) by finishing in 4th place (see [Tokyo 1964 Olympics Football Results](https://www.olympics.com/en/olympic-games/tokyo-1964/results/football/football-men)). This remains tied with their 1928 performance as the highest placement by an African nation at the time. Egypt was placed in Group C alongside [Brazil](149286.md), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team), and [South Korea](1018627.md). They advanced to the quarterfinals by securing 2nd place in their group. Notably, Egypt held Brazil to a 1–1 draw and defeated South Korea 10–0. In the knockout Stage, Egypt defeated [Ghana](1029620.md) 5–1 in the quarterfinals. They were defeated against [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) in the semifinals and subsequently lost the bronze medal match to the [United Team of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Team_of_Germany_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics).

In 1965, Egypt won the football gold medal at the [1965 Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1965_Arab_Games) in Cairo. The Egyptian squad featured notable players, including Abdel Karim El-Gohary, [Hassan El-Shazly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_El-Shazly), [Moustafa Reyadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moustafa_Reyadh), [Mahmoud Abou-Regaila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Abou-Regaila), and [Taha Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha_Ismail). In the group stage, Egypt was drawn with [Palestine](241405.md), [Iraq](7515928.md) [Lebanon](17771.md), and [South Yemen](718749.md) (also called Aden after their capital city). Their first match against Iraq was initially postponed due to overcrowding concerns, but in the end, Egypt went to win that match 1–0. They also thrashed Aden 14–0, beat Lebanon 3–0, and won against Palestine 2–1 to top the group with 8 points across their four matches.

In the semifinals, Egypt earned another heavy win, beating Libya 8–1. In the final, they ended their campaign with a 2–0 win against Sudan, winning the tournament.[^cite_note-22]

For the [1965 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Egypt qualified for the tournament, but withdrew because of their diplomatic relationship with hosts Tunisia.[^cite_note-23] They also withdrew in 1968.[^cite_note-24]

### 1970s

In the [1970 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), hosted again in Sudan, Egypt were in Group B along with [Ghana](1029620.md), [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team), and the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_national_football_team), known as Congo-Kinshasa at the time. In their opening match, Egypt defeated Guinea 4–1 and drew Ghana 1–1, before defeating Congo-Kinshasa 1–0. Egypt topped the group, thus advancing to the next round, where they faced Sudan. Egypt eventually lost their first game in the AFCON by a score of 2–1, then in the third place match, won against [Ivory Coast](1039856.md) 3–1.[^cite_note-25]

Egyptian national team in 1974

Egyptian team at the [1974 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_African_Cup_of_Nations)

The Pharaohs won the inaugural [1972 Arab Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Palestine_Cup_of_Nations) hosted in [Baghdad](4492.md). They defeated the host country, Iraq, 3–1 in the final, with goals scored by [Hassan El-Shazly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_El-Shazly) and two goals from Abdel Aziz Abdel Shafi.[^cite_note-26] In the same year, Egypt failed to qualify for the AFCON for the first time in 1972 after being eliminated by [Morocco](1024865.md) by an aggregate score of 5–3.[^cite_note-27]

The Pharaohs won the Bronze medal at the [1973 All-Africa Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_All-Africa_Games). The next year, Egypt entered [1974 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC_and_OFC\)), but did not qualify. However, Egypt returned for [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_African_Cup_of_Nations), hosting the event, eventually finishing in third place.[^cite_note-28] Egypt won the [1975 Arab Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Palestine_Cup_of_Nations), which was the third and final edition of the tournament. Hosted in [Tunisia](30188.md), the Egyptian team claimed their second title by defeating Iraq 1–0 after extra time in the final. Under the captaincy of [Taha Basry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha_Basry) and with a squad featuring [Hassan Shehata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Shehata), who was the tournament's top goal scorer. Egypt dominated the tournament by defeating South Yemen 5–0 and Saudi Arabia 2–1. They advanced to the final by defeating Sudan 3–1, and securing the title on with a 1–0 victory over Iraq, with the winning goal scored by Mahmoud Abdel Dayem. [One year later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_African_Cup_of_Nations), in Ethiopia, they were in Group A with the hosts, Guinea, and Uganda. Egypt defeated Uganda 2–1, but drew against Uganda and Ethiopia. Egypt advanced to the final round, and lost all matches.[^cite_note-29] This is the first Africa Cup of Nations tournament that Egypt lost three consecutive games. They then failed to qualify for [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_African_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-30]

### 1980–2006: African titles and World Cup appearance

Egypt reappeared for the [1980 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_African_Cup_of_Nations), defeating Ivory Coast and Tanzania, but losing to hosts Nigeria 1–0. They progressed to the semi-finals to face [Algeria](1029528.md), who defeated the Egyptians on penalties.[^cite_note-31] [Four years later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Egypt defeated Cameroon and Ivory Coast, and drew Togo, eventually finishing fourth behind Algeria.[^cite_note-32]



Egypt's captain [Hassan Shehata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Shehata) at the [1982 World Cup qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) against [Morocco](1024865.md) in [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), April 1981

Egypt's [1982 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) campaign ended in the third round after initially advancing past the first and the second rounds, where they lost to [Morocco](1024865.md) 1–0 on aggregate over two legs. In 1983, Egypt won the Bronze Medal at the [1983 Medditeranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1983_Mediterranean_Games). In 1984, the Egyptian national football team reached the quarter-finals of the [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics) in [Los Angeles](18110.md), and finished in fourth place at the [1984 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) hosted by [Ivory Coast](57744.md).[^cite_note-33]

In [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Egypt hosted; they lost to [Senegal](1017436.md) 1–0. However, Egypt went on to win their two remaining games in the group stage, 2–0 against the Ivory Coast and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_national_football_team). Egypt advanced to the final for the first time since 1962, eventually winning.[^cite_note-34]

The Pharaohs won their first Gold medal at the [1987 All-Africa Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_All-Africa_Games). In 1989, Egypt qualified for the [1990 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), losing all group stage matches, thus making Egypt fail to obtain at least one point for the first time in the Africa Cup of Nations,[^cite_note-rsssf.com-35] while appearing in the [World Cup that same year](157232.md). After beating Algeria 1–0 in the playoffs, they were drawn in Group F, with [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team), [England](9904.md) and the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team). They scored only one goal in the tournament; a 1–1 draw with [UEFA Euro 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988) champions Netherlands gave the Egyptians their first point in the World Cup. This was followed by a draw against Ireland and a 0–1 loss to England.

The team lost both of their matches in the [1992 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-rsssf.com-35] In the [1994 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in [Tunisia](30188.md), Egypt defeated Gabon 4–0 and drew Nigeria 0–0. They lost to [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team) 1–0 in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-36] They secured their second Gold Medal at the [1995 All-Africa Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1995_All-Africa_Games). At the [1996 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) hosted in [South Africa](17416221.md), they reached the quarter-finals. The Pharaohs then secured their fourth title at the [1998 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), defeating South Africa in the final.[^cite_note-37]

The Egyptian team entered the [2000 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) as defending champions, reaching the quarter-finals, before being eliminated by a 1-0 defeat to Tunisia. The tournament took place in Ghana and Nigeria, where Egypt was eliminated from the knockout stage by a 22nd-minute penalty kick in the quarter-final. The Egyptian team reached the quarter-finals of the [2002 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) hosted in [Mali](19127.md). Managed by the legendary Mahmoud El-Gohary, the squad featured a mix of domestic stars and emerging talents, including the likes of [Essam El Hadary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essam_El_Hadary), [Hany Ramzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hany_Ramzy), [Hazem Emam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazem_Emam), and [Mido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mido_\(footballer\)). Egypt qualified for the [2004 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in Tunisia, and were placed in Group C with Algeria, [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team), and Cameroon. With a 2–1 win against Zimbabwe, the team then lost 2–1 to Algeria, and drew 0–0 against Cameroon, failing to qualify for the quarter-finals due to Algeria scoring more goals than Egypt in the group stage, after being equal on points and goal difference.[^cite_note-38]

### 2006–2010: Modern golden generation

The Egyptian national football team experienced its most dominant historical era between 2006 and 2010, winning an unprecedented 3 consecutive Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) titles.

Managed by [Hassan Shehata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Shehata), this "Golden Generation" relied on a brilliant core of domestic stars, and won the [2006 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) was hosted in Egypt; the hosts were in Group A with Libya, Morocco, and the Ivory Coast. After accumulating seven points in the group stage, the Pharaohs would eventually win the tournament on penalties. Followed by the [2008 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), in which Egypt secured its sixth title by defeating Cameroon 1-0 in the final. In the [2010 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), the Pharaohs became the first and only African nation to win three consecutive tournaments, sealing their seventh overall AFCON title by beating Ghana 1-0 in the final.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### 2010–2018: Post-golden generation

In light of political turmoil, they would then fail to qualify for the next 3 AFCONs (before this Egypt was the sole African country to never miss an African Cup of Nations). Egypt qualified for the 2017 tournament, drawing against [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team) in their first match in Group D.[^cite_note-41] Successive 1–0 wins against [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team) and [Ghana](1029620.md) saw the Pharaohs qualify to the quarter-finals as group winners.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Egypt faced Morocco at the quarter-final stage, and defeated them for the first time in 31 years to set up a semi-final clash with [Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso_national_football_team).[^cite_note-44] [Mohamed Salah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Salah)'s goal against the Stallions was canceled out by an [Aristide Bancé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Banc%C3%A9) strike; however, veteran goalkeeper [Essam El Hadary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essam_El_Hadary) saved two spot-kicks in the penalty shootout to earn a spot in the final for Egypt.[^cite_note-45] Egypt then faced Cameroon, and lost the final for the second time.



Egypt national team at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia

On 8 October 2017, Egypt qualified for the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md) after topping [Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_third_round) over [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team), [Ghana](1029620.md) and [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team).[^cite_note-46] In the World Cup, Egypt was drawn with [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), [Uruguay](679782.md) and the hosts, [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) in Group A. They started their first game against Uruguay, without Salah, who was injured in the [UEFA Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_UEFA_Champions_League_Final). Egypt lost 1–0, and goalkeeper [Mohamed El Shenawy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_El_Shenawy) was voted man of the match. He refused the reward due to sponsorship by [Budweiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budweiser).[^cite_note-47] Salah returned to the starting lineup when Egypt faced hosts Russia. After being down 3–0, Salah scored Egypt's first World Cup goal in 28 years. Egypt's third and final match was a Red Sea derby against Saudi Arabia, also out of contention after two losses. [Essam El Hadary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essam_El_Hadary) became the oldest player in the World Cup at 45 years and 161 days. Despite Salah scoring a goal, the Egyptians lost 2–1.[^cite_note-48]

[Héctor Cúper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_C%C3%BAper), who was criticized due to his defensive strategies against Saudi Arabia, was sacked.[^cite_note-49] The [Egyptian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Football_Association) was also criticised due to having its base in Chechnya, far from where Egypt's matches were played.[^cite_note-50] The Egyptian media and the public heavily criticized EFA's management of the team.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### 2018–present: World Cup reappearance

In the [2019 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), hosts Egypt were knocked out by [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) in the round of 16, despite three wins in the group stage.[^cite_note-54] In the [2021 Africa Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Egypt advanced to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_Final) after dire performances in the group stage and beating Ivory Coast on penalties after a 0–0 draw. Egypt then beat Cameroon 3–1 on penalties after a scoreless draw, to reach their 10th final, beating Ghana's record of final appearances. Egypt suffered their first of two losses to Senegal in 2022, both on penalties (in the AFCON final and in [World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\))), with both winning penalties scored by [Sadio Mané](34758489.md).[^cite_note-55]



Egyptian team in 2022

The Pharaohs would then qualify for the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md) for their 4th appearance.[^cite_note-56] During the 2026 World Cup group stage, Egypt's match against [Iran](14653.md) in [Seattle](11388236.md) was designated by the local host-city organisers as the tournament's official "Pride Match".[^cite_note-57] After drawing against [Belgium](3343.md) in their first match due to an own goal by [Mohamed Hany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Hany), Egypt won their first-ever World Cup match against [New Zealand](1023035.md) in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md). Egypt advancing to the knockout stage, in the round of 32, defeated Australia in penalties. In round of 16 against Argentina, despite scoring 2 goals, conceded 3 goals in the last 10 minutes of the match and were eliminated from the World Cup.

### Kits and crest

Egypt's home kit is traditionally a red jersey, white shorts, and black socks, whilst their current away kit is predominantly white, having been green traditionally. ### Home stadium



[Misr Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misr_Stadium)

For the first 40 years of their existence, Egypt played their home matches all around the country, until the opening of the [Cairo Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_International_Stadium) in [Cairo](6293.md) in 1960. The stadium became Egypt's permanent home stadium during the 1960s. Several matches were also played on the grounds of the 86,000 seated [Borg El Arab Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borg_El_Arab_Stadium) in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria). In 2024, the [Misr Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misr_Stadium) in Egypt's proposed [new capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Capital) was opened, and started to host several matches for the national team, besides the Cairo Stadium.[^cite_note-58]
