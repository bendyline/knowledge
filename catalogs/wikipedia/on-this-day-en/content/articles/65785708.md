The **2026 United States Senate elections** are scheduled to be held on November 3, 2026, with 33 of the 100 seats in the [Senate](24909346.md) being contested in regular elections. In these elections, voters will elect candidates to six-year terms that begin on January 3, 2027, and expire on January 3, 2033.

Senators are divided into three [classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classes_of_United_States_senators) whose terms are staggered, so that a different class is elected every two years. Class 2 senators were last elected in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Senate_elections) and are up for election in 2026. With the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Senate_Republican_Conference_leadership_election) of [John Thune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Thune) as leader of the [Republican Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Republican_Conference), this will be the first election year since [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_United_States_Senate_elections) in which Senate Republicans are not led by [Mitch McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_McConnell). The [Senate Democratic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Democratic_Caucus) has been led by [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer) since 2017.

[Special elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_elections) have been scheduled in [Ohio](22199.md) and [Florida](18933066.md), following the resignation of [JD Vance](53396477.md) to become [vice president](32759.md) and [Marco Rubio](5502549.md)'s resignation to become [United States secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_secretary_of_state).

Two incumbent senators—[Bill Cassidy](9386382.md) of [Louisiana](18130.md) and [John Cornyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cornyn) of [Texas](29810.md)—lost renomination in their respective primaries. The last elected incumbent senator to lose renomination was [Richard Lugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lugar) in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_election_in_Indiana),[^cite_note-PrimaryLoss-5] and the last time multiple elected incumbent senators lost renomination in primaries was in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Senate_elections).[^cite_note-6] The last incumbent senator to place no better than third in a primary before Cassidy was [Hattie Caraway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattie_Caraway) in [1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_United_States_Senate_election_in_Arkansas).[^cite_note-SittingSenatorsPlaceThird-7][^cite_note-8]

Two nominees—[Graham Platner](80792208.md) and [Lindsey Graham](363279.md)—were replaced, respectively by a convention and a special primary, as their party's nominee in [Maine](65822713.md) and [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina). The last nominee to be replaced by a convention or special primary was [John Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walsh_\(Montana_politician\)) in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_United_States_Senate_election_in_Montana).[^cite_note-9] The last time multiple nominees were replaced was in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_United_States_Senate_elections), with [Robert Torricelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Torricelli) of [New Jersey](21648.md) and [Paul Wellstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wellstone) of [Minnesota](19590.md).

## Partisan composition

All 33 [Class 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classes_of_United_States_senators) Senate seats and two [Class 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classes_of_United_States_senators) seats are up for election in 2026. Class 2 currently consists of 20 Republicans and 13 Democrats.[^cite_note-10] Democrats are defending two seats in states won by [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in [2024](59568546.md), in both cases by less than 3 percentage points: [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Michigan), where incumbent [Gary Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Peters) is not running for reelection; and Georgia, where the incumbent is [Jon Ossoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Ossoff). Michigan elected Democrat [Elissa Slotkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elissa_Slotkin) to the Senate in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Michigan) by 0.3 percentage points. Georgia did not have a Senate election in 2024, although in the most recent Senate election, in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_Senate_election_in_Georgia), Democrat [Raphael Warnock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Warnock) won by three points in a runoff.[^cite_note-margin-11][^cite_note-12]

Five incumbent Democratic senators represent states won by [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) by single-digit margins in 2024: New Hampshire's [Jeanne Shaheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeanne_Shaheen), Minnesota's [Tina Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Smith), Virginia's [Mark Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Warner), New Jersey's [Cory Booker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Booker), and New Mexico's [Ben Ray Luján](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Ray_Luj%C3%A1n).[^cite_note-margin-11] Shaheen and Smith are not running for reelection.[^cite_note-13] [Susan Collins](375195.md)'s seat in Maine is the only seat being defended by a Republican in a state that Kamala Harris won in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Maine).[^cite_note-14] Republican [Thom Tillis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_Tillis) of North Carolina is retiring[^cite_note-politico_carney-15] from the Senate in a state that Trump won by a single-digit margin in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-margin-11]

The 2026 Senate map is considered favorable to Republicans, even though Democrats are defending 13 seats and Republicans 22. Most rating groups identify two Republican-held seats as highly competitive and three or four others as somewhat competitive. By contrast, Democrats must flip at least four seats to win a majority while defending two highly vulnerable seats and two others viewed as somewhat vulnerable.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Pollsters have found Democrats' chances to flip the Senate to be improving due to nominees in states such as [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_North_Carolina), [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_special_election_in_Ohio), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas), and [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Alaska) running highly competitive campaigns, and because of [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s unpopularity amid the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

## Predictions

Multiple sites and political pundits have published predictions of competitive seats. These predictions have considered factors such as the strength of the [incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incumbent) (if the incumbent was running for reelection) and the other candidates, and the state's partisan lean, reflected in part by the state's [Cook Partisan Voting Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Partisan_Voting_Index) rating. The predictions typically assign ratings to each seat, indicating the predicted advantage that a party had in winning that seat. Most election predictors use:

- "tossup": no advantage

- "tilt" (used by some predictors): advantage that is not quite as strong as "lean"

- "lean": slight advantage

- "likely": significant, but surmountable, advantage

- "safe" or "solid": near-certain chance of victory

## Potentially competitive seats

These are seats that polling and predictions currently have listed as at least somewhat close.

### Republican incumbents with Democratic challengers

One [Republican](32070.md)-held senate seat, [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_North_Carolina), is considered competitive but favoring the Democrats.[^cite_note-\:0-66] Former [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_North_Carolina) [Roy Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Cooper) overwhelmingly won the Democratic nomination for the open seat.[^cite_note-nc-67] Incumbent Republican senator [Thom Tillis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_Tillis) is retiring,[^cite_note-Tillis-68] with former [Republican National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_National_Committee) Chairman [Michael Whatley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Whatley) becoming the Republican nominee, endorsed by [Donald Trump](4848272.md).[^cite_note-69]

Five Republican-held Senate races are expected to be highly competitive: [Maine](65822713.md), [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_special_election_in_Ohio), [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Alaska), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas), and [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Iowa). Incumbent [Susan Collins](375195.md) is seeking reelection in Maine;[^cite_note-el-70] it is regarded as competitive due to the [state's blue lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Partisan_Voting_Index), but Collins kept her seat during cycles favorable to Democrats nationally ([2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine) and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine)). Military veteran [Graham Platner](80792208.md) was the Democratic nominee in Maine until he withdrew from the race due to allegations of sexual assault. He was replaced by former [Maine Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Senate) president [Troy Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Jackson).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Incumbents in Ohio ([Jon Husted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Husted)) and Alaska ([Dan Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Sullivan_\(U.S._senator\))) are expected to have competitive races against former U.S. senator [Sherrod Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherrod_Brown) and former U.S. representative [Mary Peltola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Peltola), respectively.[^cite_note-el-70] Both [Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Ohio) and [Peltola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_in_Alaska) lost reelection in 2024. In Texas, incumbent Senator [John Cornyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cornyn) lost the Texas Senate Republican primary election to [Texas Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Attorney_General) [Ken Paxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Paxton), who is expected to have a competitive race against [State Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_House_of_Representatives) [James Talarico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Talarico). In Iowa, the incumbent [Joni Ernst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Ernst) is not seeking reelection;[^cite_note-ernst-29] the race to succeed Ernst between Republican U.S. representative [Ashley Hinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Hinson) and Democratic state representative [Josh Turek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Turek) is expected to be competitive.&#x20;

Five other Republican-held Senate races are expected to favor Republicans over Democrats but are viewed as somewhat competitive by some predictors: [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_special_election_in_Florida), [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Mississippi), [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina), [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Kansas) and [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Louisiana). In Louisiana, incumbent Senator [Bill Cassidy](9386382.md) was challenged by [Louisiana State Treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_Treasurer) [John Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fleming_\(Louisiana_politician\)) and U.S. representative [Julia Letlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Letlow), with Letlow winning the primary election, marking the first time an elected incumbent senator had lost their primary in any state since [2012 in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_election_in_Indiana).[^cite_note-PrimaryLoss-5] In Kansas, where Democrats have not won a Senate election since [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_Senate_election_in_Kansas), incumbent Republican Senator [Roger Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Marshall) faces Democratic nominee [Adam Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Hamilton_\(pastor\)), a pastor.&#x20;

### Republican incumbents with independent challengers

Four Senate races with Republican incumbents have [independent](2172378.md) candidates as the main challengers to the Republicans: [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Nebraska), [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Idaho), [Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Montana), and [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Dakota). In Nebraska, Idaho, and South Dakota, the Democratic candidates dropped out of the race.[^cite_note-73] In Nebraska, incumbent Senator [Pete Ricketts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Ricketts) is challenged by independent [Dan Osborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Osborn), who ran against Republican [Deb Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deb_Fischer) in the [Class I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Nebraska) race in 2024. Osborn was the most successful challenger of a Republican-held seat during the [2024 election cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_elections), losing by only 6.7 points, compared to [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris)'s loss by 20.5 points in Nebraska in the [concurrent presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Nebraska). In August 2026, the Nebraska Democratic Party endorsed Osborn.[^cite_note-74]

### Democratic incumbents

The [Democratic](5043544.md)-held Senate race expected to be the most competitive is in [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Michigan), where senator [Gary Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Peters) has announced his retirement. The state has an even [PVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Partisan_Voting_Index) score.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-el-70] Former US Representative for the [8th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan's_8th_congressional_district) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Michigan) U.S. Senate nominee [Mike Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Rogers_\(Michigan_politician\))[^cite_note-MRogers-76] is the Republican nominee. Former [Wayne County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_County,_Michigan) Health, Human, and Veterans Services Department Director [Abdul El-Sayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_El-Sayed)[^cite_note-El_Sayed-77] is the Democratic nominee. In [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_New_Hampshire), where incumbent Democratic senator [Jeanne Shaheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeanne_Shaheen) is retiring, the race is also expected to be competitive. U.S. Representative [Chris Pappas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pappas_\(American_politician\)) is the Democratic nominee and former U.S. Senator [John E. Sununu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_E._Sununu) is the Republican nominee. Sununu lost to Shaheen in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_Senate_election_in_New_Hampshire).[^cite_note-JohnSununu-78] Some polls have shown the race as a dead heat,[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-EmersonNHMarch2026-80] with two August polls showing Sununu ahead.

In [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Georgia), incumbent Democratic senator [Jon Ossoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Ossoff) is running against Republican [Mike Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Collins_\(politician\)), the [U.S. representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._representative) from [Georgia's 10th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia's_10th_congressional_district).[^cite_note-81] The race was initially expected to be very competitive,[^cite_note-el-70] but election predictors have become increasingly confident of Ossoff's prospects.[^cite_note-82]

One-term Republican [Cynthia Lummis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Lummis) was [elected in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Senate_election_in_Wyoming) with 72.8% of the vote and is not seeking reelection.[^cite_note-Lummis-38]

[U.S. Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Representative) [Harriet Hageman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Hageman) defeated [Wyoming Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_Army_National_Guard) veteran Jimmy Skovgard for the Republican nomination on August 18, 2026.[^cite_note-\:8-326]

Former [state Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_House_of_Representatives) [James W. Byrd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_W._Byrd) defeated landscaper Billy Benavidez for the Democratic nomination on August 18, 2026.[^cite_note-\:8-326]
