**Cyanide poisoning** is [poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisoning) that results from exposure to any of a number of forms of [cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanide).[^cite_note-Dor2011-4] Early symptoms include [headache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headache), [dizziness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizziness), [fast heart rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachycardia), [shortness of breath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyspnea), and vomiting.[^cite_note-EU2013-2] This phase may then be followed by [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizures), [slow heart rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradycardia), [low blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension), loss of consciousness, and [cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest).[^cite_note-EU2013-2] Onset of symptoms usually occurs within a few minutes.[^cite_note-EU2013-2][^cite_note-Ham2011-3] Some survivors have long-term neurological problems.[^cite_note-EU2013-2]

Toxic cyanide-containing compounds include [hydrogen cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_cyanide) gas and cyanide [salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salts), such as [potassium cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_cyanide).[^cite_note-EU2013-2] Poisoning is relatively common following breathing in smoke from a [house fire](3326072.md).[^cite_note-EU2013-2] Other potential routes of exposure include workplaces involved in [metal polishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_polishing), certain [insecticides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecticides), the medication [sodium nitroprusside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_nitroprusside), and certain seeds such as those of apples and apricots.[^cite_note-Ham2011-3][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-AHFS2016-8] Liquid forms of cyanide can be absorbed through the skin.[^cite_note-NIH2015-9] Cyanide [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ions) interfere with [cellular respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_respiration), resulting in the body's tissues being unable to use [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen).[^cite_note-EU2013-2]

Diagnosis is often difficult.[^cite_note-EU2013-2] It may be suspected in a person following a house fire who has a decreased [level of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_of_consciousness), low blood pressure, or high [lactic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid).[^cite_note-EU2013-2] Blood levels of cyanide can be measured but take time.[^cite_note-EU2013-2] Levels of 0.5–1 mg/L are mild, 1–2 mg/L are moderate, 2–3 mg/L are severe, and greater than 3 mg/L generally result in death.[^cite_note-EU2013-2]

If exposure is suspected, the person should be removed from the source of the exposure and [decontaminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_decontamination).[^cite_note-Ham2011-3] Treatment involves [supportive care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supportive_care) and giving the person 100% oxygen.[^cite_note-EU2013-2][^cite_note-Ham2011-3] [Hydroxocobalamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxocobalamin) (vitamin B12<sub>a</sub>) appears to be useful as an [antidote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidote) and is generally first-line.[^cite_note-EU2013-2][^cite_note-Thom2012-6] [Sodium thiosulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_thiosulfate) may also be given.[^cite_note-EU2013-2] Historically, cyanide has been used for [mass suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_suicide) and it was used for [genocide](12441.md) by [Nazi Germany](21212.md).[^cite_note-Ham2011-3][^cite_note-10]

## Signs and symptoms

### Acute exposure

If [hydrogen cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_cyanide) is inhaled, it can cause a [coma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma) with [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizures), [apnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apnea), and [cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest), with death following in a matter of seconds. At lower doses, loss of consciousness may be preceded by general weakness, dizziness, [headaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headache), [vertigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertigo_\(medical\)), [confusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_confusion), and perceived difficulty in breathing. At the first stages of unconsciousness, breathing is often sufficient or even rapid, although the state of the person progresses towards a deep coma, sometimes accompanied by [pulmonary edema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_edema), and finally cardiac arrest. A cherry red skin color that darkens may be present as the result of increased venous [hemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoglobin) [oxygen saturation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen_saturation_\(medicine\)).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Despite the similar name, cyanide does not directly cause [cyanosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanosis).[^cite_note-13] A fatal dose for humans is on average 1.52 mg/kg of body weight, with the lowest recorded lethal dose being 0.56 mg/kg.[^cite_note-14] Other sources claim a lethal dose is 1–3 mg per kg body weight for vertebrates.[^cite_note-15]

### Chronic exposure

Exposure to lower levels of cyanide over a long period, e.g. after use of improperly processed [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) roots, results in increased blood cyanide levels, which can result in weakness and a variety of symptoms, including permanent [paralysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralysis), nervous [lesions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesion),[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-Soto-Blanco_B,_Stegelmeier_BL,_Pfister_JA,_et_al._2008_356–63-17][^cite_note-18] [hypothyroidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothyroidism),[^cite_note-Soto-Blanco_B,_Stegelmeier_BL,_Pfister_JA,_et_al._2008_356–63-17] and [miscarriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Other effects include mild liver and kidney damage.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## Causes



Removal of cyanide poison from [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) in Nigeria

Cyanide poisoning can result from the ingestion of cyanide salts, imbibing [prussic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussic_acid), skin absorption of prussic acid, [intravenous infusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous_therapy) of [nitroprusside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitroprusside) for [hypertensive crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_crisis),[^cite_note-AHFS2016-8] or the inhalation of hydrogen cyanide gas. The last typically occurs through one of three mechanisms:

- The gas is directly released from canisters (e.g., as part of a [pesticide](48340.md), [insecticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecticide), or [Zyklon B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zyklon_B)).

- It is generated on site by reacting [potassium cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_cyanide) or [sodium cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_cyanide) with [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid) (e.g., in a modern American [gas chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber)).

- Fumes arise during a [building fire](3326072.md) or any similar scenario involving the burning of [polyurethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethane),

  &#x20;[vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_polymer)

  [^cite_note-24]

  &#x20;or other polymer products that required [nitriles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrile) in their production.

As potential contributing factors, cyanide is present in:

- [Tobacco](30942.md) smoke.

- Certain seeds or kernels such as those of [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond#Sweet_and_bitter_almonds), [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricot_kernel), [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple#Toxicity_of_seeds), [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)), and [flaxseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaxseed).

  [^cite_note-25]

- Foods including [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) (also known as tapioca, yuca or manioc) and [bamboo shoots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_shoot).

As a potential harm-reduction factor, [vitamin B12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B12), in the form of [hydroxocobalamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxocobalamin) (also spelled hydroxycobalamin), might reduce the negative effects of chronic exposure; whereas, a deficiency might worsen negative health effects following exposure to cyanide.[^cite_note-National_Library_of_Medicine-26]

## Mechanism

Cyanide is a potent [cytochrome *c* oxidase (COX, a.k.a. Complex IV) inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytochrome_c_oxidase#Inhibition), causing [asphyxiation](98534.md) of cells. As such, cyanide poisoning is a form of [histotoxic hypoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histotoxic_hypoxia), because it interferes with the ability of cells to take or use oxygen in [aerobic respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_respiration#Aerobic_respiration).[^cite_note-Berg_2002-27]<sup>: 1475 </sup>

Specifically, cyanide binds to the [heme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heme) a3-CuB binuclear center of COX[^cite_note-Leavesley_2008-28] (and thus is a [non-competitive inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-competitive_inhibitor) of it). This prevents electrons passing through COX from being transferred to O<sub>2</sub>, which not only blocks the mitochondrial [electron transport chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_transport_chain), it also interferes with the pumping of a proton out of the [mitochondrial matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_matrix) which would otherwise occur at this stage. Therefore, cyanide interferes not only with aerobic respiration but also with the [ATP synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_synthesis) pathway it facilitates, owing to the [close relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemiosmosis) between those two processes.[^cite_note-Nelson_2004-29]<sup>: 705 </sup>

One [antidote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidote) for cyanide poisoning, [nitrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrite) (i.e., via [amyl nitrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyl_nitrite)), works by converting [ferrohemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoglobin) to [ferrihemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methemoglobin), which can then compete with COX for free cyanide (as the cyanide will bind to the iron in its heme groups instead). Ferrihemoglobin cannot carry [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen), but the amount of ferrihemoglobin that can be formed without impairing oxygen transport is much greater than the amount of COX in the body.[^cite_note-Berg_2002-27]<sup>: 1475 </sup>

Cyanide is a broad-spectrum poison because the reaction it inhibits is essential to aerobic metabolism; COX is found in multiple forms of life.[^cite_note-EMBOJ-30] However, susceptibility to cyanide is far from uniform across affected species; for instance, [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plants) have an alternative electron transfer pathway available that passes electrons directly from [ubiquinone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubiquinone) to O<sub>2</sub>, which confers cyanide resistance by bypassing COX.[^cite_note-Nelson_2004-29]<sup>: 704 </sup>

## Diagnosis

[Lactate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid) is produced by [anaerobic glycolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaerobic_glycolysis) when [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) concentration becomes too low for the normal [aerobic respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobic_respiration) pathway. Cyanide poisoning inhibits aerobic respiration and therefore increases anaerobic glycolysis which causes a rise of lactate in the plasma. A lactate concentration above 10 mmol per liter is an indicator of cyanide poisoning, as defined by the presence of a blood cyanide concentration above 40 μmol per liter. Lactate levels greater than 6 mmol/L after reported or strongly suspected pure cyanide poisoning, such as cyanide-containing smoke exposure, suggests significant cyanide exposure.[^cite_note-31] However, lactate alone is not diagnostic of cyanide poisoning because lactosis is also triggered by other things, including mitochondrial dysfunction.[^cite_note-Parikh_Goldstein_Koenig_Scaglia_2015_pp._689–701-32]

Methods of detection include colorimetric assays such as the [Prussian blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_blue_\(medical_use\)) test, the [pyridine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyridine)-[barbiturate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbiturate) assay, also known as the "Conway diffusion method"[^cite_note-33] and the [taurine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurine) [fluorescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence)-[HPLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_liquid_chromatography) but like all colorimetric assays these are prone to [false positives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_positives_and_false_negatives). [Lipid peroxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_peroxidation) resulting in "[TBARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBARS)", an artifact of [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction) produces [dialdehydes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldehyde) that cross-react with the pyridine-barbiturate assay. Meanwhile, the taurine-fluorescence-HPLC assay used for cyanide detection is identical to the assay used to detect [glutathione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutathione) in [spinal fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebrospinal_fluid).

Cyanide and thiocyanate assays have been run with [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry) ([LC/MS/MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_chromatography%E2%80%93mass_spectrometry)), which are considered specific tests. Since cyanide has a short [half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life), the main [metabolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolite), [thiocyanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiocyanate) is typically measured to determine exposure.

## Treatment

### Decontamination

Decontamination of people exposed only to the gas form of hydrogen cyanide requires removal of the outer clothing and the washing of their hair.[^cite_note-NIH2015-9] Those exposed to liquids or powders generally require [full decontamination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_decontamination).[^cite_note-NIH2015-9]

### Antidote

The [International Programme on Chemical Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Programme_on_Chemical_Safety) issued a survey (IPCS/CEC Evaluation of Antidotes Series) that lists the following antidotal agents and their effects: oxygen, sodium thiosulfate, [amyl nitrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyl_nitrite), sodium nitrite, 4-dimethylaminophenol, hydroxocobalamin, and dicobalt edetate ('Kelocyanor'), as well as several others.[^cite_note-34] Another historical antidote is 'solutions A and B' (a solution of [ferrous sulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrous_sulfate) in aqueous [citric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citric_acid), and aqueous [sodium carbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_carbonate), respectively). However, such use is no longer recommended.

The United States standard cyanide [antidote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidote) kit first uses a small inhaled dose of [amyl nitrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyl_nitrite), followed by intravenous [sodium nitrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_nitrite), followed by intravenous [sodium thiosulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_thiosulfate).[^cite_note-35] [Hydroxocobalamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxocobalamin) was approved for use in the US in late 2006[^cite_note-36] and is available in [Cyanokit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanokit) antidote kits.[^cite_note-EmedTreat-37] [Sulfanegen TEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfanegen_TEA), which could be delivered to the body through an [intra-muscular (IM) injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuscular_injection), detoxifies cyanide and converts the cyanide into [thiocyanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiocyanate), a less toxic substance.[^cite_note-38] Alternative methods of treating cyanide intoxication are used in other countries.

The Irish [Health Service Executive](4017657.md) (HSE) has recommended against the use of solutions A and B because of their limited [shelf life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelf_life), potential to cause [iron poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_poisoning), and limited applicability (effective only in cases of cyanide ingestion, whereas the main modes of poisoning are inhalation and skin contact). The HSE has also questioned the usefulness of amyl nitrite due to storage/availability problems, risk of abuse, and lack of evidence of significant benefits. It also states that the availability of [kelocyanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelocyanor) at the workplace may mislead doctors into treating a patient for cyanide poisoning when this is an erroneous diagnosis. The HSE no longer recommends a particular cyanide antidote.[^cite_note-39]

## History

### Fires

The [República Cromañón nightclub fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rep%C3%BAblica_Croma%C3%B1%C3%B3n_nightclub_fire) broke out in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), Argentina on 30 December 2004, killing 194 people and leaving at least 1,492 injured. Most of the victims died from inhaling poisonous gases, including [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide). After the fire, the technical institution INTI found that the level of toxicity due to the materials and volume of the building was 225 ppm of cyanide in the air. A lethal dose for rats is between 150 ppm and 220 ppm, meaning the air in the building was highly toxic.

On 27 January 2013, [a fire at the Kiss nightclub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_nightclub_fire) in the city of [Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_Rio_Grande_do_Sul), in the south of Brazil, caused the poisoning of hundreds of young people by cyanide released by the combustion of soundproofing foam made with [polyurethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethane). By March 2013, 242 fatalities were confirmed.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] It is the deadliest fire incident involving cyanide poisoning.

### Gas chambers



Empty [Zyklon B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zyklon_B) canisters, found by the [Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) in January 1945 at [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz)

Research of hydrogen cyanide by chemists [Carl Wilhelm Scheele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Wilhelm_Scheele) and [Claude Bernard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bernard) would become central to understanding the lethality of future gas chambers.[^cite_note-qh19z-53] In early 1942, [Zyklon B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zyklon_B), which contains hydrogen cyanide, emerged as the preferred killing tool of [Nazi Germany](21212.md) for use in [extermination camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extermination_camp) during [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELongerich2010281–282-54] The chemical was used to murder roughly one million people in [gas chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber) installed in extermination camps at [Auschwitz-Birkenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz-Birkenau), [Majdanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek), and elsewhere.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes20042,_272-55] Most of the people who were murdered were [Jews](25955086.md), and by far the majority of these murders took place at Auschwitz.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiper1994161-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes2004272-57][^cite_note-59] The constituents of Zyklon B were manufactured by several companies under licenses for [Degesch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degesch), a corporation co-owned by [IG Farben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben), [Degussa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evonik_Industries) and [Th. Goldschmidt AG](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evonik_Goldschmidt). It was sold to the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(1935%E2%80%931945\)) and the [Schutzstaffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel) (SS) by the distributors Heli and [Testa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesch_&_Stabenow), with Heli supplying it to concentration camps at [Mauthausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauthausen-Gusen_concentration_camp), [Dachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp), and [Buchenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald) and Testa to Auschwitz and Majdanek.[^cite_note-60] Camps also occasionally bought Zyklon B directly from the manufacturers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes2004288–289-61] Of the 729 [tonnes](31185.md) of Zyklon B sold in Germany in 1942–44, 56 tonnes (about eight percent of domestic sales) were sold to concentration camps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes2004296-62] Auschwitz received 23.8 tonnes, of which six tonnes were used for fumigation. The remainder was used in the gas chambers or lost to spoilage (the product had a stated shelf life of only three months).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes2004294–297chpt._"\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid5Amej-jkAMwCqZyklonexpiration_Degesch_and_Zyklon_B.\]"-63] Testa conducted fumigations for the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) and supplied them with Zyklon B. They also offered courses to the SS in the safe handling and use of the material for fumigation purposes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes2004283-64] In April 1941, the German agriculture and interior ministries designated the SS as an authorized applier of the chemical, and thus they were able to use it without any further training or governmental oversight.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes2004284-65]

Hydrogen cyanide gas has been used for [judicial execution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_execution) in some states of the United States, where cyanide was generated by reaction between [potassium cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_cyanide) (or [sodium cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_cyanide)[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]) dropped into a compartment containing [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid), directly below the chair in the [gas chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber#United_States).[^cite_note-68]

### Suicide

For information on methods of suicide intervention, see [Suicide prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_prevention).

For the pill commonly used for cyanide suicide, see [Suicide pill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_pill).

Cyanide salts are sometimes used as fast-acting suicide devices. Cyanide reacts at a higher level with high stomach [acidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acidity).

- [Viktor Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Meyer), 19th-century German chemist, died by suicide in 1897 after taking cyanide.

  [^cite_note-69]

- On 26 January 1904, company promoter and swindler [Whitaker Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitaker_Wright) died by ingesting cyanide in a court anteroom immediately after being convicted of fraud.

- [Gustav Wied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Wied), Danish novelist, poet, and playwright, committed suicide in 1914.

- [Badal Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badal_Gupta), a revolutionary from Bengal, who launched an attack on the Writers' Building in Kolkata, consumed cyanide in 1930 immediately after the attack.

- [Pritilata Waddedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pritilata_Waddedar), an Indian revolutionary nationalist, took cyanide in 1932 to avoid capture by [Indian Imperial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Imperial_Police), British India.

- In February 1937, the Uruguayan short story writer [Horacio Quiroga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Quiroga) died by drinking cyanide at a hospital in [Buenos Aires](255919.md).

- In 1937, polymer chemist [Wallace Carothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Carothers) died by drinking cyanide.

- [Erwin Rommel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Rommel) (1944), [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md)'s wife [Eva Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Braun) (1945),

  [^cite_note-70]

  &#x20;and Nazi leaders [Heinrich Himmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Himmler) (1945), possibly [Martin Bormann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bormann) (1945), and [Hermann Göring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_G%C3%B6ring) (1946) all died by ingesting cyanide. Pure liquid [prussic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussic_acid) (a historical name for hydrogen cyanide) was the favored [suicide agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_agent) of [Nazi Germany](21212.md).

- It is speculated that, in 1954, [Alan Turing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing) used an apple that had been injected with a solution of cyanide to die after being convicted of having a [homosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual) relationship, which was [illegal at the time in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_indecency), and forced to undergo hormonal castration to avoid prison. An inquest determined that Turing's death from cyanide poisoning was a suicide, although this has been disputed.

- In 1958, [Douglas Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Kelley), a former [United States Army](32087.md) [Military Intelligence Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_Corps_\(United_States_Army\)), ingested a capsule of [potassium cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_cyanide). He was an officer who served as chief psychiatrist at [Nuremberg Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Prison) during the first months of the [Nuremberg trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_trials). Kelley examined 22 high ranking officials of the Nazi party, including Adolf Hitler's second-in-command Hermann Göring and Hitler's deputy [Rudolf Hess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Hess).

- Members of the [Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Tigers_of_Tamil_Eelam), whose [insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_civil_war) lasted from 1983 to 2009, used to wear cyanide vials around their necks with the intention of taking them if captured by the government forces.

- On 22 June 1977, Moscow, [Aleksandr Dmitrievich Ogorodnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Dmitrievich_Ogorodnik), a Soviet diplomat accused of spying on behalf of the Colombian Intelligence Agency and the US [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md), was arrested by Soviet authorities. During the interrogations, Ogorodnik offered to write a full confession and asked for his pen. Inside the pen cap was a hidden cyanide pill, provided by his CIA handlers to be used in the event of his capture. Ogorodnik bit the pen cap, releasing the cyanide and causing Ogorodnik to die before he hit the floor, according to the Soviets.

  [^cite_note-71]

- On 18 November 1978, 909 people died in [Jonestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonestown), some of them from apparent cyanide poisoning, in an event termed "revolutionary suicide" by Jones and some members on an audio tape of the event and in prior discussions. The poisonings in Jonestown followed the murder of five others by [Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples_Temple) members at [Port Kaituma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Kaituma), including United States Congressman [Leo Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Ryan), an act that Jones ordered. Four other Temple members died by murder-suicide in [Georgetown](56702.md) at Jones' command.

- On 6 June 1985, serial killer [Leonard Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Lake) died in custody after having ingested cyanide pills he had sewn into his clothes.

- [Jason Altom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Altom), a promising graduate student in the lab of Nobel Prize–winning chemist [EJ Corey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elias_James_Corey) at Harvard, died after drinking potassium cyanide in 1998.

- MP Prasad, a [goldsmith](323246.md) in [Palakkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palakkad), India, died by suicide in 2006. Prasad is reportedly the only person to have documented the taste of potassium cyanide.
