**South America** is a [continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent)[^cite_note-15] entirely in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md)[^cite_note-16] and most in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere), with a considerably smaller portion in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere). It can also be described as the southern [subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subregion#Americas) of the [Americas](29833.md).&#x20;

South America is bordered on the west by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), on the north and east by the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), and on the south by the [Drake Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Passage); [North America](21139.md), the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) lying to the northwest, and the [Antarctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Circle), [Antarctica](18959138.md), and the [Antarctic Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Peninsula) to the south.

The continent includes twelve [sovereign countries](23604120.md): [Argentina](18951905.md), [Bolivia](3462.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Colombia](5222.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), [Guyana](17238691.md), [Paraguay](23420.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Suriname](26828.md), [Uruguay](31843.md), and [Venezuela](32374.md); two [dependent territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory): the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md) and [South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Georgia_and_the_South_Sandwich_Islands);[^cite_note-17] and one [internal territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division): [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana).[^cite_note-18]

The [Caribbean South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_South_America) [ABC islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_islands_\(Leeward_Antilles\)) ([Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba), [Bonaire](108737.md), and [Curaçao](65205.md)) and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) are geologically located on the South-American continental shelf,[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] and thus may be considered part of South America as well. [Panama](22997.md), [Ascension Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Island) (a part of [Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Helena,_Ascension_and_Tristan_da_Cunha)) and [Bouvet Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouvet_Island) (a [dependency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependencies_of_Norway) of [Norway](21241.md)) may also be considered parts of South America.

South America has an area of 17,840,000 square kilometers (6,890,000 sq mi). Its population as of 2021 has been estimated at more than 434 million.[^cite_note-UN_WPP-1][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-2] South America ranks fourth in area (after Asia, Africa, and North America) and [fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_continents_and_continental_subregions_by_population) in population (after Asia, Africa, Europe, and North America). Brazil is by far the most populous South American country, with almost half of the continent's population, followed by Colombia, Argentina, Venezuela, and Peru. In recent decades, Brazil has also generated half of the continent's GDP and has become the continent's first regional power.[^cite_note-academia.edu-21]

Most of the population lives near the continent's western or eastern coasts while the interior and the [far south](65433.md) are sparsely populated. The geography of western South America is dominated by the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) mountains; in contrast, the eastern part contains both highland regions and vast lowlands where rivers such as the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River), [Orinoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco) and [Paraná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River) flow. Most of the continent lies in the tropics, except for a large part of the [Southern Cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cone) located in the [middle latitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_latitudes).

The continent's cultural and ethnic outlook has its origin with the interaction of [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America) with European [conquerors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) and immigrants and, more locally, with [African slaves](49005.md). Given a long [history of colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas), the overwhelming majority of South Americans speak [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) or [Spanish](26825.md), and societies and states are rich in [Western traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture). Relative to Africa, Asia, and Europe, post-1900 South America has been [a peaceful continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_long_peace) with few wars,[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Franch-23][^cite_note-25] although [high rates of violent crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_and_violence_in_Latin_America) remain a concern in some countries.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## History

Main article: [History of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_America)

### Prehistory

Further information: [History of South America § Pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_America#Pre-Columbian_era)



The prehistoric [Cueva de las Manos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cueva_de_las_Manos), or "Cave of the Hands", in Argentina

South America is thought to have been first inhabited by humans when people were crossing the [Bering Land Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Land_Bridge) (now the [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait)) at least 15,000 years ago from the territory that is present-day Russia. They migrated south through North America, and eventually reached South America through the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama).

Amongst the oldest evidence for human presence in South America is the [Monte Verde II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Verde) site in Chile, suggested to date to around 14,500 years ago.[^cite_note-28] From around 13,000 years ago, the [Fishtail projectile point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishtail_projectile_point) style became widespread across South America, with its disappearance around 11,000 years ago coincident with the disappearance of South America's [megafauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megafauna).[^cite_note-\:12-29]

Ancient DNA evidence indicates that the [peopling of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peopling_of_the_Americas) was more complex than a single southward [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-modern_human_migration) followed by uninterrupted population continuity. A genomic study identified at least three major dispersals into the subcontinent, followed by regional differentiation and long-term genetic continuity in some regions, with evidence of population replacement in others.[^cite_note-30]

Maize was present in northern South America by around 6,000 years ago.[^cite_note-31] South American cultures began domesticating [llamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama) and [alpacas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpaca) in the highlands of the Andes circa 3500 BC. Besides their use as sources of meat and wool, these animals were used for transportation of goods.[^cite_note-OBrienP-Oxford_Atlas-32]

By 2000 BC, many [agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarian_society) communities had been settled throughout the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) and the surrounding regions. Fishing became a widespread practice along the coast, helping establish fish as a primary source of food. Irrigation systems were also developed at this time, which aided in the rise of an [agrarian society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarian_society).[^cite_note-OBrienP-Oxford_Atlas-32]

### Pre-Columbian civilizations

Main article: [Pre-Columbian era § South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era#South_America)



The Inca estate of [Machu Picchu](80019.md), [Peru](170691.md), is one of the [New Seven Wonders of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Seven_Wonders_of_the_World#Winners).

The rise of plant growing and the subsequent appearance of permanent human settlements allowed for the multiple and overlapping beginnings of civilizations in South America.

One of the earliest known South American civilizations was [Caral–Supe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caral%E2%80%93Supe), on the central Peruvian coast. Though a pre-ceramic culture, the monumental architecture of Caral–Supe created one of the first cities of the world, generally dated to around 3500 BC, at [Huaricanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaricanga) in the [Fortaleza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza_River_\(Peru\)) area, contemporaneous with the pyramids of [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), one of the [oldest-known civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization) in the Americas and one of the six sites where civilization separately originated in the ancient world.[^cite_note-AncientAndes-33] Caral–Supe governing class established a trade network and developed agriculture then followed by [Chavín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn) by 900 BC, according to some estimates and archaeological finds. Artifacts were found at a site called [Chavín de Huantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn_de_Huantar) in modern Peru at an elevation of 3,177 meters (10,423 ft). Chavín civilization spanned 900 BC to 300 BC.

In the central coast of Peru, around the beginning of the 1st millennium AD, [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) (100 BC – 700 AD, at the northern coast of Peru), [Paracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracas_culture) and [Nazca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_culture) (400 BC – 800 AD, Peru) cultures flourished with centralized states with permanent militia improving agriculture through [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) and new styles of ceramic art. At the [Altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano), Tiahuanaco or [Tiwanaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwanaku) (100 BC – 1200 AD, Bolivia) managed a large commercial network based on religion.

Around the 7th century, both Tiahuanaco and Wari or [Huari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari_culture) Empire (600–1200, Central and northern Peru) expanded its influence to all the Andean region, imposing the Huari urbanism and Tiahuanaco religious iconography.

The [Muisca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca) were the main indigenous civilization in what is now Colombia. They established the [Muisca Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca_Confederation) of many clans, or *[cacicazgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacique)*, that had a free trade network among themselves. Many were goldsmiths and farmers.

Other important Pre-Columbian cultures include: the [Cañaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1ari) (in south central Ecuador), [Chimú Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chim%C3%BA_Empire) (1300–1470, Peruvian northern coast), [Chachapoyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachapoyas_culture), and the Aymaran kingdoms (1000–1450, Western Bolivia and southern Peru). Holding their capital at the great city of [Cusco](72735.md), the [Inca civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_civilization) dominated the Andes region from 1438 to 1533. Known as *Tawantin suyu*, and "the land of the four regions", in [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_languages), the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire) was highly distinct and developed. Inca rule extended to nearly a hundred linguistic or ethnic communities, some nine to fourteen million people connected by a 25,000 kilometer [road system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_road_system). Cities were built with precise, unmatched stonework, constructed over many levels of mountain terrain. [Terrace farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_farming) was a useful form of agriculture.

The [Mapuche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche) in Central and Southern Chile resisted the European and Chilean settlers, waging the [Arauco War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arauco_War) for more than 300 years.

### European colonization

Main articles: [Spanish colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) and [Portuguese colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_colonization_of_the_Americas)



The Portuguese explorer [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral) landing in Brazil in 1500

In 1494, [Portugal](23033.md) and [Spain](26667.md), the two great maritime European powers of that time, on the expectation of new lands being discovered in the west, signed the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas), by which they agreed, with the support of the Pope, that all the land outside Europe should be an exclusive [duopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duopoly) between the two countries.[^cite_note-34]



The [Inca–Spanish confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Inca_Empire) in the [Battle of Cajamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cajamarca) left thousands of natives dead.

The treaty established an imaginary line along a north–south [meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_\(geography\)) 370 [leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_\(unit\)) (approximately 1,110 mi (1,790 km)) west of the [Cape Verde Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde_Islands), roughly 46° 37' W. In terms of the treaty, all land to the west of the line (known to comprise most of the South American soil) would belong to Spain, and all land to the east, to Portugal. As accurate measurements of [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) were impossible at that time, the line was not strictly enforced, resulting in a [Portuguese expansion of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_colonization_of_the_Americas) across the meridian.

Beginning in the 1530s, the people and natural resources of South America were repeatedly exploited by foreign [conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistadors), first from Spain and later from Portugal. These competing colonial nations claimed the land and resources as their own and divided it into colonies.

European infectious diseases ([smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox), [influenza](19572217.md), [measles](58911.md), and [typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus)) – to which the [native populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) had no immune resistance – caused large-scale depopulation of the native population under Spanish control. Systems of forced labor, such as the [haciendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacienda) and mining industry's [mit'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mit'a) also contributed to the depopulation. After this, [enslaved Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enslaved_Africans), who had developed immunities to these diseases, were quickly brought in to replace them.



An interpretation of the extent of the Spanish and Portuguese colonies in the Americas in 1790

The Spaniards were committed to converting their native subjects to Christianity and were quick to purge any native cultural practices that hindered this end; however, many initial attempts at this were only partially successful, as native groups simply blended [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) with their established beliefs and practices. Furthermore, the Spaniards brought their language to the degree they did with their religion, although the Roman [Catholic Church](606848.md)'s evangelization in [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_language), [Aymara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_language), and [Guaraní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaran%C3%AD_language) actually contributed to the continuous use of these native languages albeit only in the oral form.

Eventually, the [natives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) and the Spaniards interbred, forming a [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) class. At the beginning, many mestizos of the Andean region were offspring of Amerindian mothers and Spanish fathers. After independence, most mestizos had native fathers and European or mestizo mothers.

Many native artworks were considered pagan idols and destroyed by Spanish explorers; this included many gold and silver sculptures and other artifacts found in South America, which were melted down before their transport to Spain or Portugal. Spaniards and Portuguese brought the western European architectural style to the continent, and helped to improve infrastructures like bridges, roads, and the sewer system of the cities they discovered or conquered. They also significantly increased economic and trade relations, not just between the old and new world but between the different South American regions and peoples. Finally, with the expansion of the Portuguese and Spanish languages, many cultures that were previously separated became united through that of Latin American.

[Guyana](17238691.md) was initially [colonized by the Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonisation_of_the_Guianas) before coming under [British control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Guiana), though there was a brief period during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) when it was occupied by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire). The region was initially partitioned between the Dutch, French and British before fully coming under the control of Britain.

Suriname was first explored by the Spanish in the 16th century and then [settled by the English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_\(English_colony\)) in the mid-17th century. It became a [Dutch colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_\(Dutch_colony\)) in 1667.[^cite_note-35]

The indigenous peoples of the Americas in various European colonies were forced to work in European plantations and mines; along with enslaved Africans who were also introduced in the proceeding centuries via the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md). European colonists were heavily dependent on indigenous labor during the initial phases of settlement to maintain the subsistence economy, and natives were often captured by expeditions. The importation of African slaves began midway through the 16th century, but the enslavement of indigenous peoples continued well into the 17th and 18th centuries. The slave trade brought enslaved Africans primarily to South American colonies, beginning with the Portuguese since 1502.[^cite_note-36] The main destinations of this phase were the [Caribbean](18956035.md) colonies and Brazil, as European nations built up economically slave-dependent colonies in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World). Nearly 40% of all African slaves trafficked to the Americas went to Brazil. An estimated 4.9 million slaves from Africa came to Brazil during the period from 1501 to 1866.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

In contrast to other European colonies in the Americas which mainly used the labor of African slaves, Spanish colonists mainly enslaved indigenous Americans. In 1750, the [Portuguese Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Crown) abolished the enslavement of indigenous peoples in [colonial Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Brazil), under the belief that they were unfit for labor and less effective than enslaved Africans. Enslaved Africans were brought to the Americas on [slave ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_ship), under inhuman conditions and ill-treatment, and those who survived were sold in [slave markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_market).[^cite_note-Yeager1995-39] After independence, all South American countries maintained slavery for some time. The first South American country to abolish slavery was Chile in 1823, Uruguay in 1830, Bolivia in 1831, Guyana in 1833, Colombia and Ecuador in 1851, Argentina in 1853, Peru and Venezuela in 1854, Suriname in 1863, Paraguay in 1869, and in 1888 Brazil was the last South American nation and the last country in the Western world to abolish slavery.[^cite_note-c-40]

### Independence from Spain and Portugal

Main articles: [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) and [Independence of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil)

The European [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War) (1807–14), a theater of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), changed the political situation of the Spanish and Portuguese colonies. First, Napoleon invaded Portugal, but the [House of Braganza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Braganza) avoided capture by [escaping to Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_the_Portuguese_court_to_Brazil). Napoleon captured King [Ferdinand VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_VII) of Spain, and appointed his own brother instead. This appointment provoked popular resistance, which created [Juntas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junta_\(Peninsular_War\)) to rule in the name of the captured king.



Coronation of [Pedro I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_I_of_Brazil) as 1st [Emperor of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Brazil)

Many cities in the Spanish colonies, however, considered themselves equally authorized to appoint local Juntas like those of Spain. This began the [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) between the patriots, who promoted such autonomy, and the [royalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalist_\(Spanish_American_Revolution\)), who supported Spanish authority over the Americas. The Juntas, in both Spain and the Americas, promoted the ideas of the [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment). Five years after the beginning of the war, Ferdinand VII returned to the throne and began the [Absolutist Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutist_Restoration), as the royalists got the upper hand in the conflict.

The independence of South America was secured by [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) (Venezuela) and [José de San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn) (Argentina), the two most important *[Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertadores)*. Bolívar led a great uprising in the north, then led his army south towards [Lima](85423.md), the capital of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru). Meanwhile, San Martín led an army across the Andes Mountains, along with Chilean expatriates, and liberated Chile. He organized a fleet to reach Peru by sea, and sought the military support of various rebels from the Viceroyalty of Peru. In 1822 the two men met at the [Guayaquil Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guayaquil_Conference) in Ecuador, where they failed to agree on governance strategies for the liberated nations.[^cite_note-guay-41] Two years later Bolívar's forces beat the Spanish at the [Battle of Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ayacucho), securing the independence of Peru and the rest of South America.[^cite_note-guay-41]

In the [Portuguese Kingdom of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Portugal,_Brazil_and_the_Algarves), [Dom Pedro I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Pedro_I) (also Pedro IV of Portugal), son of the Portuguese King [Dom João VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Jo%C3%A3o_VI), proclaimed the independent [Kingdom of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Brazil) in 1822, which later became the [Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil). Despite the Portuguese loyalties of garrisons in [Bahia](199070.md), [Cisplatina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatina) and [Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par%C3%A1), independence was diplomatically accepted by Portugal in 1825, on condition of a high compensation paid by Brazil mediated by the United Kingdom.

### Nation-building and fragmentation



The [Thirty-Three Orientals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-Three_Orientals) proclaimed the independence of [Cisplatine Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatine_Province).



Battle scene in Southern Brazil during the [Ragamuffin War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragamuffin_War)

The newly independent nations began a process of fragmentation, with several civil and international wars. However, it was not as strong as in Central America. Some countries created from provinces of larger countries stayed as such up to modern times (such as Paraguay or Uruguay), while others were reconquered and reincorporated into their former countries (such as the [Republic of Entre Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Entre_R%C3%ADos) and the [Riograndense Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riograndense_Republic)).

The first separatist attempt was in 1820 by the Argentine province of [Entre Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entre_R%C3%ADos_Province), led by a [caudillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudillo).[^cite_note-42] In spite of the "Republic" in its title, [General Ramírez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Ram%C3%ADrez_\(governor\)), its caudillo, never really intended to declare an independent Entre Rios. Rather, he was making a political statement in opposition to the monarchist and centralist ideas that back then permeated [Buenos Aires](255919.md) politics. The "country" was reincorporated at the United Provinces in 1821.

In 1825, the [Cisplatine Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatine_Province) declared its independence from the [Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil), which led to the [Cisplatine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatine_War) between the imperials and the Argentine from the [United Provinces of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) to control the region. Three years later, the United Kingdom intervened in the question by proclaiming a tie and creating in the former Cisplatina a new independent country: The [Oriental Republic of Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Republic_of_Uruguay).

Later in 1836, while Brazil was experiencing the chaos of the regency, [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md) [proclaimed its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragamuffin_War) motivated by a tax crisis. With the anticipation of the coronation of [Pedro II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_II) to the throne of Brazil, the country could stabilize and fight the separatists, which the province of [Santa Catarina](256305.md) had joined in 1839. The Conflict came to an end by a process of compromise by which both [Riograndense Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riograndense_Republic) and [Juliana Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliana_Republic) were reincorporated as provinces in 1845.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]



The [Chilean Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Army) in the battlefield of the [Battle of Chorrillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chorrillos), 1883

The [Peru–Bolivian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%E2%80%93Bolivian_Confederation), a short-lived union of Peru and Bolivia, was blocked by Chile in the [War of the Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Confederation) (1836–1839) and again during the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) (1879–1883). Paraguay was virtually destroyed by Argentina, Brazil and Uruguay in the [Paraguayan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_War).

### Wars and conflicts

Despite the [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) and the [Brazilian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_War_of_Independence), the new nations quickly began to suffer with internal conflicts and wars among themselves. Most of the countries' borders who had initially accepted the 1810 borders on the *uti possidetis iuris* principle had by 1848 either been altered by war or were contested.[^cite_note-45]

In 1825, the proclamation of independence of Cisplatina led to the [Cisplatine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatine_War) between historical rivals the [Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil) and the [United Provinces of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata), Argentina's predecessor. The result was a stalemate, ending with the [British government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_government) arranging for the independence of Uruguay. Soon after, another Brazilian province proclaimed its independence leading to the [Ragamuffin War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragamuffin_War) which Brazil won.

Between 1836 and 1839, the [War of the Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Confederation) broke out between the short-lived [Peru-Bolivian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru-Bolivian_Confederation) and Chile, with the support of the [Argentine Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Confederation). The war was fought mostly in the actual territory of [Peru](170691.md) and ended with a Confederate defeat and the dissolution of the Confederacy and annexation of many territories by Argentina.

Meanwhile, the [Argentine Civil Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Civil_Wars) plagued Argentina since its independence. The conflict was mainly between those who defended the centralization of power in Buenos Aires and those who defended a confederation. During this period it can be said that "there were two Argentines": the [Argentine Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Confederation) and the [Argentine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Republic). At the same time, the political instability in Uruguay led to the [Uruguayan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguayan_Civil_War) among the main political factions of the country. All this instability in the [platine region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platine_region) interfered with the goals of other countries such as Brazil, which was soon forced to take sides. In 1851, the [Brazilian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Empire), supporting the centralizing unitarians, and the [Uruguayan government](31843.md) invaded Argentina and deposed the caudillo, [Juan Manuel Rosas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Rosas), who ruled the confederation with an iron hand. Although the [Platine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platine_War) did not put an end to the political chaos and civil war in Argentina, it brought temporary peace to Uruguay where the [Colorados faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Party_\(Uruguay\)) won, supported by [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Empire), [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Britain_and_Ireland), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire) and the [Unitarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarian_Party) of Argentina.[^cite_note-Latin_America's_Wars-46]

Peace lasted only a short time: in 1864, the Uruguayan factions faced each other again in the [Uruguayan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguayan_War). The [Blancos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(Uruguay\)) supported by Paraguay started to attack Brazilian and Argentine farmers near the borders. The Empire made an initial attempt to settle the dispute between Blancos and Colorados without success. In 1864, after a Brazilian ultimatum was refused, the imperial government declared that Brazil's military would begin reprisals. Brazil declined to acknowledge a formal state of war, and, for most of its duration, the Uruguayan–Brazilian armed conflict was an undeclared war which led to the deposition of the *Blancos* and the rise of the pro-Brazilian *Colorados* to power again. This angered the Paraguayan government, which even before the end of the war invaded Brazil, beginning the longest and bloodiest inter-state war in Latin American history: the Paraguayan War.[^cite_note-47]



[Imperial Brazilian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Brazilian_Navy) [siege of Paysandú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paysand%C3%BA), 1865

The Paraguayan War began when the Paraguayan dictator [Francisco Solano López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Solano_L%C3%B3pez) ordered the invasion of the Brazilian provinces of [Mato Grosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mato_Grosso) and [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md). His attempt to cross Argentinian territory without Argentinian approval led the pro-Brazilian Argentine government into the war. The pro-Brazilian Uruguayan government showed its support by sending troops. In 1865 the three countries signed the [Treaty of the Triple Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Triple_Alliance) against Paraguay. At the beginning of the war, the Paraguayans took the lead with several victories, until the Triple Alliance organized to repel the invaders and fight effectively. This was the second [total war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_war) experience in the world after the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). It was deemed the greatest war effort in the history of all participating countries, taking almost 6 years and ending with the complete devastation of Paraguay. The country lost 40% of its territory to Brazil and Argentina and lost 60% of its population, including 90% of the men. The dictator Lopez was killed in battle and a new government was instituted in alliance with Brazil, which maintained occupation forces in the country until 1876.[^cite_note-48]

The last South American war in the 19th century was the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) with Bolivia and Peru on one side and Chile on the other. In 1879 the war began with Chilean troops occupying Bolivian ports, followed by Bolivia declaring war on Chile which activated an alliance treaty with Peru. The Bolivians were completely defeated in 1880 and Lima was occupied in 1881. Peace was signed with Peru in 1883 while a truce was signed with Bolivia in 1884. Chile annexed territories of both countries leaving Bolivia [landlocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked).[^cite_note-49]



The Brazilian [*Minas Geraes* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minas_Geraes-class_battleship) kindled an [Argentine–Brazilian–Chilean naval arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_dreadnought_race).

In the new century, as wars became less violent and less frequent, Brazil entered into a small conflict with Bolivia for the possession of the Acre, which was acquired by Brazil in 1902. In 1917 Brazil declared war on the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers), joined the allied side in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) and sent a small fleet to the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and some troops to be integrated with the British and French forces in the region. Brazil was the only South American country that participated in the First World War.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Later in 1932 Colombia and Peru entered a [short armed conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leticia_Incident) for territory in the Amazon. In the same year Paraguay declared [war on Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaco_War) for possession of the Chaco, in a conflict that ended three years later with Paraguay's victory. Between 1941 and 1942 Peru and Ecuador [fought for territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian%E2%80%93Peruvian_War) claimed by both that were annexed by Peru, usurping Ecuador's frontier with Brazil.[^cite_note-52]

Also in this period, the first major naval battle of [World War II](32927.md) took place in the South Atlantic close to the continental mainland: the [Battle of the River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_River_Plate), between a British [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser) squadron and a German [pocket battleship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_battleship).[^cite_note-53] The Germans still made numerous attacks on Brazilian ships on the coast, causing Brazil to declare war on the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) in 1942, being the only South American country to fight in this war (and in both World Wars). Brazil sent naval and air forces to [combat German and Italian submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic) off the continent and throughout the South Atlantic, in addition to sending an [expeditionary force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Expeditionary_Force) to fight in the [Italian Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Campaign_\(World_War_II\)).[^cite_note-Ibidem_Maximiano_2011-54][^cite_note-55]

A brief war was fought between Argentina and the UK in 1982, following an Argentine invasion of the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md), which ended with an Argentine defeat. The last international war to be fought on South American soil was the 1995 [Cenepa War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenepa_War) between Ecuador and Peru along their mutual border.

### Rise and fall of military dictatorships

Wars became less frequent in the 20th century, with Bolivia-Paraguay and Peru-Ecuador fighting the last inter-state wars. Early in the 20th century, the [three wealthiest South American countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_countries) engaged in [a vastly expensive naval arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_dreadnought_race) which began after the introduction of a new warship type, the "[dreadnought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreadnought)". At one point, the Argentine government was spending a fifth of its entire yearly budget for just two dreadnoughts, a price that did not include later in-service costs, which for the Brazilian dreadnoughts was sixty percent of the initial purchase.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]



Argentine soldiers during the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War)

The continent became a battlefield of the [Cold War](325329.md) in the late 20th century. Some democratically elected governments of Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Uruguay and Paraguay were overthrown or displaced by military dictatorships in the 1960s and 1970s. To curtail opposition, their governments detained tens of thousands of [political prisoners](37307.md), many of whom were tortured or killed on [inter-state collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Condor). Economically, they began a transition to [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal) economic policies. They placed their own actions within the US Cold War doctrine of "National Security" against internal subversion. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, Peru suffered from [an internal conflict](9624647.md).

In 1982, Argentina invaded the [Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War), a British [dependent territory](207114.md). The [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War) began and 74 days later [Argentine forces surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_surrender_in_the_Falklands_War).[^cite_note-58]

Colombia has had an ongoing, though diminished internal conflict, which started in 1964 with the creation of [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) [guerrillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrillas) (FARC-EP) and then involved several illegal armed groups of leftist-leaning ideology as well as the private armies of powerful drug lords. Many of these are now defunct, and only a small portion of the ELN remains, along with the stronger, though also greatly reduced, FARC.

Revolutionary movements and right-wing military dictatorships became common after World War II, but since the 1980s, a wave of democratization passed through the continent, and democratic rule is widespread now.[^cite_note-59] Nonetheless, allegations of corruption are still very common, and several countries have developed crises which have forced the resignation of their governments, although, on most occasions, regular civilian succession has continued.

[International indebtedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_of_developing_countries) became a significant problem in the late 1980s, and some countries, despite having strong democracies, have not developed political institutions capable of handling such crises without resorting to unorthodox economic policies. This was illustrated by Argentina's [default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_economic_crisis_\(1999-2002\)) in the early 21st century.[^cite_note-60] There has been an increased push towards [regional integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integration_of_Latin_America), with the creation of uniquely South American institutions such as the [Andean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Community), [Mercosur](25869317.md) and [Unasur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unasur). Starting with the election of [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez) in Venezuela in 1998, the region experienced a [pink tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_tide) – the election of several leftist and center-left administrations in most countries, except the Guianas and Colombia.

### Contemporary issues

South America's political geography since the 1990s has been characterized by a desire to reduce foreign influence.[^cite_note-natgeo-61] The nationalization of industries, by which the state controls entire economic sectors (as opposed of private companies doing it), has become a prominent political issue in the region.[^cite_note-natgeo-61] Some South American nations have nationalized their electricity industries.[^cite_note-natgeo-61]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_South_America)



South America's landforms and land cover



A composite relief image of South America

South America occupies the southern portion of the Americas. The continent is generally delimited on the northwest by the [Darién watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap) along the [Colombia–Panama border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia%E2%80%93Panama_border), although some may consider the border instead to be the [Panama Canal](24849.md). [Geopolitically](163225.md)[^cite_note-62] and geographically, all of [Panama](22997.md) – including the segment east of the Panama Canal in the isthmus – is typically included in North America alone[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-North_America_Atlas-65] and among the countries of [Central America](6121.md).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Almost all of mainland South America sits on the [South American Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Plate).

South America is home to several superlatives, including the [world's highest uninterrupted waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_waterfalls_by_height), [Angel Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Falls) in Venezuela; the highest single-drop waterfall [Kaieteur Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaieteur_Falls) in Guyana; the largest river by volume, the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River); the longest mountain range, the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) (whose highest mountain is [Aconcagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aconcagua) at 6,962 m or 22,841 ft); the driest non-polar place on earth, the [Atacama Desert](18952975.md);[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] the wettest place on earth, [López de Micay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B3pez_de_Micay) in Colombia; the largest rainforest, the [Amazon rainforest](48139.md); the highest capital city, [La Paz](42821.md), [Bolivia](3462.md); the highest commercially navigable lake in the world, [Lake Titicaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Titicaca); and, excluding research stations in [Antarctica](18959138.md), the world's southernmost permanently inhabited community, [Puerto Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Toro), Chile. South America had the highest annual deforestation rate in 2015–2025 of any region (4.22 million ha), however this was a reduction of almost 50% from the rate in the region in 1990–2000 (8.24 million ha).[^cite_note-71]

South America's major [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) resources are gold, silver, copper, iron ore, [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin), and petroleum. These resources have brought high income to its countries, especially in times of war or of rapid economic growth by industrialized countries elsewhere. However, the concentration in producing one, or few, major export commercial [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(business\)) has often hindered the development and diversification of its economies. The fluctuation in the price of commodities in international markets has led historically to major highs and lows, booms and busts, in the economies of South American states, often causing political instability.[^cite_note-auto13-72] This has led for calls to diversify production and increase trade within South America itself.[^cite_note-auto13-72]

Brazil is the largest country in South America, covering a little less than half of the continent's land area and encompassing around half of the continent's population.[^cite_note-73] The remaining countries and territories are divided among four [subregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subregion#South_America): the [Andean states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Community), [Caribbean South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_South_America), [The Guianas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guianas), and the [Southern Cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cone).[^cite_note-74]

### Outlying islands



The Gran Roque village, the largest settlement of the [Los Roques Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Roques_Archipelago), Venezuela

Physiographically, South America also includes some of the nearby islands. The Dutch [ABC islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_islands_\(Leeward_Antilles\)) ([Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba), [Bonaire](108737.md), and [Curaçao](65205.md)), the islands of [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) ([Trinidad Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_Island) and [Tobago Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobago_Island) etc.), the [State of Nueva Esparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Esparta), and the [Federal Dependencies of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Dependencies_of_Venezuela) sit on the northern portion of the South American [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf) and are sometimes considered parts of the continent. Geopolitically, all the island countries and territories in the [Caribbean](18956035.md) have generally been grouped as a [subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subregion#North_America) of North America instead. By contrast, [Aves Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aves_Island) (administered by [Venezuela](32374.md)) and the [Archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago_of_San_Andr%C3%A9s,_Providencia_and_Santa_Catalina) ([San Andrés Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andr%C3%A9s_Island), [Providencia Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providencia_Island), and [Santa Catalina Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Catalina_Island_\(Colombia\)) etc., which are administered by [Colombia](5222.md)) are politically parts of South American countries but physiographically parts of North America.[^cite_note-North_America_Atlas-65][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

Other islands often associated with geopolitical South America are the [Chiloé Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilo%C3%A9_Archipelago) and [Robinson Crusoe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinson_Crusoe_Island) (both administered by Chile), [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island) (culturally a part of Oceania, also administered by Chile),[^cite_note-protectedareas-77] the [Galápagos Islands](171118.md) (administered by [Ecuador](9334.md), sometimes considered part of Oceania),[^cite_note-protectedareas-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] and [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego) (split between Argentina and Chile). In the Atlantic Ocean, Brazil administers [Fernando de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha), [Trindade and Martim Vaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trindade_and_Martim_Vaz), and the [Saint Peter and Saint Paul Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter_and_Saint_Paul_Archipelago), while the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md) (Spanish: *Islas Malvinas*) and [South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Georgia_and_the_South_Sandwich_Islands) ([biogeographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_realm) and [hydrologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Convergence) associated with Antarctica)[^cite_note-80] have been administered as two [British Overseas Territories](207114.md) under [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown), whose sovereignty over the islands is [disputed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_dispute) by [Argentina](18951905.md).

#### Special cases

An isolated [volcanic island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_island) on the [South American Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Plate), [Ascension Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Island) is geologically a part of South America.[^cite_note-81] Administered as a dependency of [Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Helena,_Ascension_and_Tristan_da_Cunha), the island is geopolitically [a part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme_for_Africa#Western_Africa) of Africa.

### Climate

See also: [Arid Diagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid_Diagonal)



[Köppen-Geiger climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) map for South America

[^cite_note-82]

Most of the world's major climate zones are present in South America.[^cite_note-Danuta-83]

The distribution of the average temperatures in the region presents a constant regularity from the [30° of latitude south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_parallel_south), when the isotherms tend, more and more, to be confused with the degrees of latitude.[^cite_note-Climate-84]

In temperate latitudes, winters and summers are milder than in North America. This is because the most extensive part of the continent is in the equatorial zone (the region has more areas of equatorial plains than any other region),[^cite_note-Climate-84] therefore giving the [Southern Cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cone) more oceanic influence, which moderates year round temperatures.

The average annual temperatures in the Amazon basin oscillate around 27 °C (81 °F), with low thermal amplitudes and high rainfall indices. Between the [Maracaibo Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracaibo_Lake) and the mouth of the Orinoco, that also includes parts of the Brazilian territory, an [equatorial climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_climate) typical of the Congolese regions in Central Africa predominates.[^cite_note-Climate-84]

The east-central Brazilian plateau has a humid and warm tropical climate. The northern and eastern parts of the Argentine pampas have a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics) with dry winters and humid summers commonly classified as a "[Chinese type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_China)" climate, while the western and eastern ranges have a subtropical climate similar to the [Dinaric Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinaric_Alps) in Europe. At the highest points of the Andean region, climates are colder than the ones occurring at the highest point of the Norwegian fjords. In the Andean plateaus, the warm climate prevails, although it is tempered by the altitude, while in the coastal strip, there is an equatorial climate commonly classified as a "Guinean type" climate. North of the Andean plateaus up to the north of the Chilean coast a [Mediterranean oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) dominates with temperate summers and cold winters akin to Cape Breton. In [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego) a cold climate persists that is commonly referred to as a "[Siberian](29639.md) type" climate.[^cite_note-Climate-84]



Map of all tropical cyclone tracks from 1945 to 2006

The distribution of rainfall is related to the regime of winds and air masses. In most of the [tropical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_region) east of the Andes, winds blowing from the northeast, east and southeast carry moisture from the Atlantic, causing abundant rainfall. However, due to a consistently strong [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) and a weak [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone), [South Atlantic tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_tropical_cyclone) are rare.[^cite_note-85] In the [Orinoco Llanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco_Llanos) and in the [Guianas Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guianas), the precipitation levels go from moderate to high. The Pacific coast of Colombia and northern Ecuador are rainy regions, with [Chocó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Choc%C3%B3) in Colombia being the rainiest place in the world along with the northern slopes of Indian Himalayas.[^cite_note-86] The Atacama Desert, along this stretch of coast, is one of the driest regions in the world. The central and southern parts of Chile are subject to [extratropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone), and most of the Argentine [Patagonia](65433.md) is [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert). In the [Pampas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampas) of Argentina, Uruguay and South of Brazil the rainfall is moderate, with rains well distributed during the year. The moderately dry conditions of the Chaco oppose the intense rainfall of the eastern region of Paraguay. In the [semiarid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiarid) coast of the Brazilian Northeast the rains are linked to a monsoon regime.[^cite_note-Climate-84]

Important factors in the determination of climates are sea currents such as the [Humboldt Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Current) and [Falkland Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Current). The [Equatorial Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Equatorial_Current) of the South Atlantic strikes the coast of the Northeast where is divided into two others: the current of [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_Current) and a coastal current that flows to the northwest towards the [Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles) from where it turns northeast forming the most important and famous ocean current in the world, the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream).[^cite_note-Climate-84][^cite_note-Know\.net-87]

### Fauna

Main article: [Fauna of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_South_America)

South America is one of the most biodiverse continents on Earth. It is home to many unique species of animals including the [llama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama), [anaconda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaconda), [piranha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piranha), [jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar), [eel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eel), [vicuña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicu%C3%B1a), and [tapir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapir), and to one of the largest known insects in the world, the [Titan beetle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_beetle). The Amazon rainforests possess high [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity), with Brazil estimated to contain 10% of Earth's [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species).[^cite_note-88] 83% of South America's large mammals ([megafauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megafauna)) became extinct at the end of the Pleistocene around 11,000 years ago as part of the [Quaternary extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_extinction_event), among the highest of any continent, with the casualties including [saber-toothed cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smilodon), [ground sloths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_sloth), [glyptodonts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyptodont), [gomphotheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomphothere), the equines *[Hippidion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippidion)* and *[Equus neogeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equus_neogeus)*, and all remaining [South American native ungulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_native_ungulates).[^cite_note-89]

## Politics

See also: [Democracy in Latin America and the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Latin_America_and_the_Caribbean)



Headquarters of the [UNASUR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNASUR) in Quito, [Ecuador](9334.md)

Historically, the Hispanic countries were founded as Republican dictatorships led by [caudillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudillo). Brazil was a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) for its first 67 years of independence, until a coup d'état proclaimed a republic. In the late 19th century, the most democratic countries were [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil),[^cite_note-90] [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chile_during_the_Parliamentary_Era_\(1891%E2%80%931925\)), Argentina and Uruguay.[^cite_note-91]

Most South American countries are [presidential republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_republic). [Guyana](17238691.md) and [Suriname](26828.md) are [parliamentary republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic). [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) is a [French overseas department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_overseas_department), while the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md) and [South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Georgia_and_the_South_Sandwich_Islands) are British overseas territories. It is currently the only inhabited continent in the world without monarchies; the [Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil) existed during the 19th century and there was an unsuccessful attempt to establish a [Kingdom of Araucanía and Patagonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Araucan%C3%ADa_and_Patagonia) in southern Argentina and Chile. Also in the twentieth century, [Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_\(Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands\)) was established as a constituent kingdom of the [Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands) and [Guyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyana_\(1966%E2%80%931970\)) retained the [British monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_monarch) as [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) for 4 years after its independence.

Recently, an intergovernmental entity has been formed which aims to merge the two existing customs unions: [Mercosur](25869317.md) and the [Andean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Community), thus forming the third-largest trade bloc in the world.[^cite_note-92] This new political organization, known as [Union of South American Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_American_Nations), seeks to establish free movement of people, economic development, a common defense policy and the elimination of [tariffs](55551.md).

Since 1930, the continent has experienced growth and diversification in most economic sectors. Most agricultural and livestock products are destined for the domestic market and local consumption. However, the export of [agricultural products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) is essential for the [balance of trade](4810.md) in most countries.[^cite_note-Economy-106]

The main agrarian crops are export crops, such as [soy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy) and wheat. The production of staple foods such as vegetables, corn or beans is large, but focused on domestic consumption. Livestock raising for meat exports is significant in Argentina, Paraguay, Uruguay and Colombia. In tropical regions the most important crops are coffee, [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean) and [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bananas), mainly in Brazil, Colombia and Ecuador. Traditionally, the countries producing sugar for export are Peru, Guyana and Suriname, and in Brazil, [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane) is also used to make [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol). On the coast of Peru, northeast and south of Brazil, cotton is grown. 51% of South America's land surface is covered by forest.[^cite_note-107] Transnational companies have settled in the [Amazon rainforest](48139.md) to exploit timber destined for export.

The Pacific coastal waters are the most important for commercial fishing. The anchovy catch reaches thousands of tonnes, and tuna is abundant, Peru is a major exporter. The capture of [crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustaceans) is large, particularly in northeastern Brazil and Chile.[^cite_note-Economy-106]

Brazil and Argentina are part of the [G20](2206608.md) industrial countries, while only Brazil is part of the [G8+5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G8+5) (the most powerful and influential nations in the world). In the tourism sector, negotiations began in 2005 to promote tourism and increase air connections within the region. [Punta del Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_del_Este), [Florianópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florian%C3%B3polis) and [Mar del Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_del_Plata) are among the largest resorts in South America.[^cite_note-Economy-106]

The most industrialized countries in South America are Brazil, Argentina, Colombia and Chile respectively. These countries alone account for more than 80% of the region's economy. Industries in South America significantly developed from the 1930s, when the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) in the United States and elsewhere, boosted industrial production on the continent. The region partially moved away from agriculture and began to achieve high rates of economic growth that remained until the early 1990s, when they slowed due to political instabilities and economic crises.[^cite_note-Economy-106]

Since the end of the economic crisis in Brazil and Argentina between 1998 and 2002, which led to [economic recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_recession), rising unemployment and falling income, the industrial and service sectors have recovered. South America has shown good signs of economic stability, with controlled inflation and exchange rates, continuous growth, a decrease in [social inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality) and unemployment – factors that favor industry.[^cite_note-Economy-106]

The main industries are: electronics, textiles, food, automotive, metallurgy, aviation, naval, clothing, beverage, steel, tobacco, timber, chemical, among others.[^cite_note-Economy-106]

The [economic gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_gap) between rich and poor in most South American nations is larger than most other continents. The richest 10% receive over 40% of the nation's income in Bolivia, Brazil and Colombia,[^cite_note-share-108] while the poorest 20% receive 4% or less.[^cite_note-109] This wide gap can be seen in large South American cities where makeshift shacks and slums lie in the vicinity of skyscrapers and luxury apartments; many South Americans live on less than \$2 per day.[^cite_note-wb2-103]

### Energy

Due to the diversity of [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) and pluviometric precipitation conditions, the region's water resources vary enormously in different areas. In the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes), navigation possibilities are limited, except for the Magdalena River, [Lake Titicaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Titicaca) and the lakes of the southern regions of Chile and Argentina. Irrigation is an important factor for agriculture from northwestern Peru to Patagonia. Less than 10% of the known electrical potential of the Andes had been used until the mid-1960s.

The [Brazilian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Highlands) have a much higher hydroelectric potential than the [Andean region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) and its possibilities of exploitation are greater due to the existence of several large rivers with high margins and the occurrence of great differences forming huge cataracts, such as those of Paulo Afonso, Iguaçu and others. The [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) system has about 13,000 km (8,100 mi) of waterways, but its possibilities for [hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power) use are still unknown.

&#x20;Most of the continent's energy is generated through [hydroelectric power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power_plants), but there is also an important share of [thermoelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoelectric) and [wind energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_energy). Brazil and Argentina are the only South American countries that generate [nuclear power](22153.md), each with two [nuclear power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plants). In 1991 these countries signed a peaceful nuclear cooperation agreement.

The Brazilian government has undertaken an ambitious program to reduce dependence on imported petroleum. Imports previously accounted for more than 70% of the country's oil needs but Brazil became self-sufficient in oil in 2006–2007. Brazil was the 10th largest oil producer in the world in 2019, with 2.8 million barrels/day. Production manages to supply the country's demand.[^cite_note-auto3-180] In the beginning of 2020, in the production of [oil](23195.md) and natural gas, the country exceeded 4 million barrels of oil equivalent per day, for the first time. In January this year, 3.168 million barrels of oil per day and 138.753 million cubic meters of natural gas were extracted.[^cite_note-auto2-182]

Brazil is one of the main world producers of [hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power). In 2019, Brazil had 217 hydroelectric plants in operation, with an installed capacity of 98,581 MW, that constitutes 60.16% of the country's energy generation.[^cite_note-190] In 2019 Brazil reached a total of 170,000 megawatts of installed power generation capacity, of which more than 75% are from renewable sources (the majority, hydroelectric).[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192]

In 2013, the [Southeast Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Region,_Brazil) used about 50% of the load of the National Integrated System (SIN), being the main energy consuming region in the country. The region's installed electricity generation capacity totaled almost 42,500 MW, which represented about a third of Brazil's generation capacity. Hydroelectric generation represented 58% of the region's installed capacity, with the remaining 42% coming mostly from thermoelectric generation. São Paulo accounted for 40% of this capacity; Minas Gerais for about 25%; Rio de Janeiro for 13.3%; and Espírito Santo accounted for the rest. The [South Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Region,_Brazil) owns the [Itaipu Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Dam), which was the largest hydroelectric plant in the world for several years, until the inauguration of [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) in China. It remains the second largest operating hydroelectric power generation capacity in the world. Brazil is the co-owner of the Itaipu Plant with [Paraguay](23420.md): the dam is located on the [Paraná River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River), located on the border between countries. It has an installed generation capacity of 14 GW for 20 generating units of 700 MW each. [North Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil) has large hydroelectric plants, such as [Belo Monte Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belo_Monte_Dam) and [Tucuruí Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucuru%C3%AD_Dam), which produce much of the national energy. Brazil's hydroelectric potential has not yet been fully exploited, so the country still has the capacity to build several renewable energy plants in its territory.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194]

As of July 2022, according to ONS, total installed capacity of [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) was 22 GW, with average [capacity factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacity_factor) of 58%.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] While the world average wind production capacity factors is 24.7%, there are areas in Northern Brazil, specially in Bahia State, where some wind farms record average capacity factors of over 60%;[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-\:0-198] the average capacity factor in the [Northeast Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Region,_Brazil) is 45% in the coast and 49% in the interior.[^cite_note-199] In 2019, wind energy represented 9% of the energy generated in the country.[^cite_note-auto-200] In 2019, it was estimated that the country had an estimated wind power generation potential of around 522 GW (this, only onshore), enough energy to meet three times the country's current demand.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] In 2021 Brazil ranked 7th in the world in terms of installed wind power capacity (21 GW),[^cite_note-RENEWABLE_CAPACITY_STATISTICS_2021-203][^cite_note-204] and the 4th largest producer of wind energy in the world (72 TWh), behind only China, United States and Germany.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-auto5-206]

Nuclear energy accounts for about 4% of Brazil's electricity.[^cite_note-uic-207] The nuclear power generation monopoly is owned by [Eletronuclear (Eletrobrás Eletronuclear S/A)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eletronuclear), a wholly owned subsidiary of [Eletrobrás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eletrobr%C3%A1s). Nuclear energy is produced by [two reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_nuclear_reactors#Brazil) at [Angra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angra_Nuclear_Power_Plant). It is located at the Central Nuclear Almirante Álvaro Alberto (CNAAA) on the Praia de Itaorna in [Angra dos Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angra_dos_Reis), Rio de Janeiro. It consists of two [pressurized water reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressurized_water_reactor), Angra I, with capacity of 657 MW, connected to the power grid in 1982, and Angra II, with capacity of 1,350 MW, connected in 2000. A third reactor, Angra III, with a projected output of 1,350 MW, is planned to be finished.[^cite_note-merco-208]

As of October 2022, according to ONS, total installed capacity of [photovoltaic solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_solar) was 21 GW, with average [capacity factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacity_factor) of 23%. Some of the most [irradiated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_irradiance) Brazilian States are MG ("Minas Gerais"), BA ("Bahia") and GO (Goiás), which have indeed world [irradiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_irradiance) level records.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-\:0-198][^cite_note-210] In 2019, solar power represented 1.27% of the energy generated in the country.[^cite_note-auto-200] In 2021, Brazil was the 14th country in the world in terms of installed solar power (13 GW),[^cite_note-211] and the 11th largest producer of solar energy in the world (16.8 TWh).[^cite_note-212]

In 2020, Brazil was the 2nd largest country in the world in the production of energy through [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) (energy production from solid biofuels and renewable waste), with 15,2 GW installed.[^cite_note-213]

After Brazil, [Colombia](5222.md) is the country in South America that most stands out in energy production. In 2020, the country was the 20th largest petroleum producer in the world, and in 2015 it was the 19th largest exporter. In natural gas, the country was, in 2015, the 40th largest producer in the world. Colombia's biggest highlight is in coal, where the country was, in 2018, the world's 12th largest producer and the 5th largest exporter. In renewable energies, in 2020, the country ranked 45th in the world in terms of installed wind energy (0.5 GW), 76th in the world in terms of installed solar energy (0.1 GW) and 20th in the world in terms of installed hydroelectric power (12.6 GW).

[Venezuela](32374.md), which was one of the world's largest oil producers (about 2.5 million barrels/day in 2015) and one of the largest exporters, due to its political problems, has had its production drastically reduced in recent years: in 2016, it dropped to 2.2 million, in 2017 to 2 million, in 2018 to 1.4 million and in 2019 to 877 thousand, reaching only 300,000 barrels/day at a given point. The country also stands out in hydroelectricity, where it was the 14th country in the world in terms of installed capacity in 2020 (16,5 GW).

[Argentina](18951905.md) was in 2017 the 18th largest producer of natural gas in the world, and the largest producer of natural gas in Latin America, in addition to being the 28th largest oil producer; although the country has the Vaca Muerta field, which holds close to 16 billion barrels of technically recoverable shale oil, which is the second largest natural shale gas deposit in the world, the country lacks the capacity to exploit the deposit: it lacks the necessary capital, technology and knowledge that can only come from offshore energy companies, who view Argentina and its erratic economic policies with considerable suspicion, not wanting to invest in the country. In renewable energies, in 2020, the country ranked 27th in the world in terms of installed wind energy (2.6 GW), 42nd in the world in terms of installed solar energy (0.7 GW) and 21st in the world in terms of installed hydroelectric power (11.3 GW). The country has great potential for the production of wind energy in the Patagonia region.

Chile, although currently not a major energy producer, has great future potential for solar energy production in the Atacama Desert region. [Paraguay](23420.md) stands out today in hydroelectric production thanks to the Itaipu Power Plant. [Bolivia](3462.md) stand out in the production of natural gas, where it was the 31st largest in the world in 2015. [Ecuador](9334.md), because it consumes little energy, is part of OPEC and was the 27th largest oil producer in the world in 2020, being the 22nd largest exporter in 2014.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216][^cite_note-auto5-206]

### Transport

Transport infrastructure in South America includes roads, railways, [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) and airports. The railway and [fluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluvial) sector, although having more robust contemporary potential, are still somewhat treated in a secondary way.

#### Roads



Ruta 9 / 14, in [Zarate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%A1rate,_Buenos_Aires), Argentina

Due to the [Andes Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes_Mountains), [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) and [Amazon Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Forest), there have always been difficulties in implementing larger scale transcontinental or bi-oceanic highways. Practically the only route that existed was the one that connected Brazil to Buenos Aires, (in Argentina) and later to Santiago, (in Chile). However, in recent years, with the combined effort of countries, new routes have started to emerge, such as Brazil-Peru ([Interoceanic Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interoceanic_Highway)), and a new highway between Brazil, Paraguay, northern Argentina and northern Chile ([Bioceanic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bi-Oceanic_railway)).

Some of the most modern roads extend through northern and south-east Argentina; and south of Brazil, a vast road complex aims to link [Brasília](4752.md), the federal capital, to the South, Southeast, Northeast and Northern regions of Brazil.

Brazil has more than 1.7 million kilometres (1.1 million miles) of roads, of which 215,000 km (134,000 mi) are paved, and about 14,000 km (8,700 mi) are [divided highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divided_highways). The two most important highways in the country are [BR-101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-101) and [BR-116](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-116).[^cite_note-auto1-217] Argentina has more than 600,000 km (370,000 mi) of roads, of which about 70,000 km (43,000 mi) are paved, and about 2,500 km (1,600 mi) are divided highways. The three most important highways in the country are [Route 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_9_\(Argentina\)), [Route 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_7_\(Argentina\)) and [Route 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_14_\(Argentina\)).[^cite_note-auto1-217] Colombia has about 210,000 km (130,000 mi) of roads, and about 2,300 km (1,400 mi) are divided highways.[^cite_note-218] Chile has about 82,000 km (51,000 mi) of roads, 20,000 km (12,000 mi) of which are paved, and about 2,000 km (1,200 mi) are divided highways. The most important highway in the country is the [Route 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_Route_5) ([Pan-American Highway](301108.md)).[^cite_note-219] These 4 countries are the ones with the best road infrastructure and with the largest number of double-lane highways.

In addition, there is a reputed [Pan-American Highway](301108.md), which crosses Argentina and the Andean countries from north to south, although various stretches are unfinished.[^cite_note-220]

#### Rail

Two primary railroads are continental: the Transandina, which connects [Buenos Aires](255919.md), in Argentina to [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso), in Chile, and the Brazil–Bolivia Railroad, which makes it the connection between the port of [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos,_S%C3%A3o_Paulo) in Brazil and the city of [Santa Cruz de la Sierra](673987.md), in Bolivia.

Two areas of greater density occur in the railway sector: the platinum network, which develops around the [Platine region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platine_region), largely belonging to Argentina, with more than 45,000 km (28,000 mi) in length; And the [Southeast Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Brazil) network, which mainly serves the [state of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo), [state of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Rio_de_Janeiro) and [Minas Gerais](222651.md).

The Argentine rail network, with 47,000 km (29,000 mi) of tracks, at one stage was one of the largest in the world and continues to be the most extensive in Latin America. It came to have about 100,000 km (62,000 mi) of rails, but the lifting of tracks and the emphasis placed on motor transport gradually reduced it. It has four different trails and international connections with Paraguay, Bolivia, Chile, Brazil and Uruguay. The Brazilian railway network has an extension of about 30,000 kilometres (19,000 miles). It is basically used for transporting ores.[^cite_note-223] Chile has almost 7,000 km (4,300 mi) of railways, with connections to Argentina, Bolivia and Peru. Colombia has about 3,500 km (2,200 mi) of railways.[^cite_note-224]

#### Waterways



The mouth of the Amazon River

South America has one of the largest bays of navigable inland waterways in the world, represented mainly by the [Amazon basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_basin), the [Platine basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platine_basin), the São Francisco and the Orinoco basins, with Brazil having about 54,000 km (34,000 mi) navigable, while Argentina has 6,500 km (4,000 mi) and Venezuela has 1,200 km (750 mi).

Among the main Brazilian [waterways](174851.md), two stand out: the one is [Hidrovia Tietê-Paraná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidrovia_Tiet%C3%AA-Paran%C3%A1?action=edit\&redlink=1)—which has a length of 2,400 kilometres (1,500 miles), of which 1,600 kilometres (990 miles) are on the Paraná River and 800 kilometres (500 miles) are on the Tietê River—draining agricultural production from the states of Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Goiás and part of Rondônia, Tocantins and Minas Gerais; the second one is [Hidrovia do Solimões-Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidrovia_do_Solim%C3%B5es-Amazonas?action=edit\&redlink=1) with two sections: Solimões which extends from Tabatinga to Manaus with a length of approximately 1,600 kilometres (990 miles), and Amazonas which extends from Manaus to Belém with a length of approximately 1,650 kilometres (1,030 miles). Almost all passenger transport in the Amazon plain is done via this waterway, in addition to practically all cargo transportation that is directed to the major regional centers of Belém and Manaus.

In Brazil, water transport is still underutilised: the most important waterway stretches, from an economic point of view, are found in the Southeast and South of the country. Its full use still requires the construction of locks, major dredging works and the development of ports that allow intermodal integration.&#x20;

In [Argentina](18951905.md), the waterway network is made up of the La Plata, Paraná, Paraguay and Uruguay rivers. The main river ports are [Zárate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%A1rate,_Buenos_Aires) and [Campana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campana_Partido). The port of Buenos Aires is historically the first in individual importance, but the area known as Up-River, which stretches along 67 kilometres (42 miles) of the Santa Fé portion of the Paraná River, brings together 17 ports that concentrates around 50% of the total exports of the country.

#### Public transport

The primary public transport in major cities is the bus. Many cities have a diverse transport system of metro and subway trains, the first of which was the [Buenos Aires subte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Underground), opened 1913.[^cite_note-225] The [Santiago subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_subway)[^cite_note-Home-226] is the longest subway network in South America spanning 103 kilometres (64 miles), while the [São Paulo subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_subway) is the most heavily utilised with more than 4.6 million passengers per day.[^cite_note-2014ridership-227]

[Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) installed the continent's first railroad in 1854. Today the city has a diversified system of metropolitan trains, integrated with buses and subway. It has a Light Rail System called [VLT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro_Light_Rail), with small electrical trams at low speed, while [São Paulo](390875.md) has inaugurated a [monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_15_\(S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Metro\)). In Brazil, an express bus system called Bus Rapid Transit (BRT), which operates in several cities, has been developed. [Mi Teleférico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi_Telef%C3%A9rico), also known as Teleférico [La Paz](42821.md)–[El Alto](3475756.md) (La Paz–El Alto Cable Car), is an aerial cable car urban transit system serving the La Paz–El Alto metropolitan area in Bolivia.

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_South_America)

See also: [List of South American countries by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_American_countries_by_population) and [List of South American countries by life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_American_countries_by_life_expectancy)



Satellite view of South America at night from [NASA](18426568.md), showing the contrast between heavily populated coastal areas and the more remote regions of the Amazonian interior and Patagonia

South America has a population of over 428 million people. They are distributed as to form a "hollow continent" with most of the population concentrated around the margins of the continent.[^cite_note-natgeo-61] On one hand, there are several sparsely populated areas such as [tropical forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_forest), the [Atacama Desert](18952975.md) and the icy portions of [Patagonia](65433.md). On the other hand, the continent presents regions of high population density, such as the great urban centers. The population is formed by descendants of Europeans (mainly [Spaniards](963152.md), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) and Italians), [Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora) and [Amerindians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindians). There is a high percentage of [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizos) that vary greatly in composition by place. There is a population of about 5 million Asians, mostly [East Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asians), especially in Brazil, [Peru](170691.md), and [Argentina](18951905.md). The two main languages are by far Spanish and Portuguese, followed by English, French and Dutch in smaller numbers.

### Language

Main article: [Languages of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_South_America)



Official languages in South America

Spanish and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) are the most spoken languages in South America, with approximately 200 million speakers each. Spanish is the official language of most countries, along with other native languages in some countries. Portuguese is the official language of Brazil.[^cite_note-229] [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) is the official language of [Suriname](26828.md); English is the official language of [Guyana](17238691.md), although there are at least twelve other languages spoken in the country, including [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), Chinese, [Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Hindustani) and several native languages.[^cite_note-230] English is also spoken in the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md). French is the official language of [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) and the second language in [Amapá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amap%C3%A1), Brazil.

[Indigenous languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas) of South America include [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_languages) in Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Chile, Argentina and Colombia; [Wayuunaiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayuu_language) in northern Colombia ([La Guajira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guajira_Department)) and northwestern Venezuela ([Zulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulia)); [Guaraní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarani_language) in Paraguay and, to a much lesser extent, Bolivia; [Aymara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_language) in Bolivia, Peru, and less often in Chile; and [Mapudungun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapudungun) is spoken in certain pockets of southern Chile. At least three South American indigenous languages (Quechua, Aymara, and Guarani) are recognized along with Spanish as national languages.

Other languages found in South America include [Sranan Tongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sranan_Tongo), [Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Hindustani) and [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) in Suriname; Italian in Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay and Venezuela; and German in certain pockets of Argentina, Chile and Brazil. German is also spoken in many regions of the southern states of Brazil, [Riograndenser Hunsrückisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riograndenser_Hunsr%C3%BCckisch) being the most widely spoken German dialect in the country; among other Germanic dialects, a Brazilian form of [East Pomeranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pomeranian) is also well represented and is experiencing a revival. [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) remains spoken and written in the historic towns of [Trelew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trelew) and [Rawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawson,_Chubut) in the Argentine [Patagonia](65433.md), [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language) is spoken in southern Chile, Arabic speakers, often of [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people), [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian), or [Palestinian](23267.md) descent, can be found in Arab communities in Argentina, Colombia, Brazil, Venezuela and in Paraguay.[^cite_note-231]

### Religion



[Las Lajas Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Lajas_Sanctuary), [Ipiales](4513789.md), [Colombia](5222.md)

An estimated 90% of South Americans are Christians[^cite_note-232] (82% [Roman Catholic](606848.md), 8% other Christian denominations mainly traditional [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants) and [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicals) but also [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodoxy)), accounting for 19% of Christians worldwide.

African descendant and Indigenous religions are common throughout South America; some examples are [Santo Daime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Daime), [Candomblé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candombl%C3%A9), and [Umbanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbanda). [Crypto-Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto-Jews) or [Marranos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrano), [conversos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversos), and [Anusim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anusim) were an important part of colonial life in Latin America. Buenos Aires and São Paulo figure among the largest [Jewish populations by urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_population_by_urban_areas).

[East Asian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_religions) such as [Japanese Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Buddhism), [Shintoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shintoism), and [Shinto-derived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) [Japanese New Religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_New_Religions) are common in Brazil and Peru. [Korean Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Confucianism) is especially found in Brazil, while [Chinese Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Buddhism) and [Chinese Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) have spread throughout the continent. [Kardecist Spiritism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardecist_Spiritism) can be found in several countries.

[Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) form 25% of the Guyanese population and 22% of Suriname's.[^cite_note-\:1-233][^cite_note-\:2-234]

Muslims account for 7% of the Guyanese population and 14% of the Surinamese population.[^cite_note-\:1-233][^cite_note-\:2-234]

### Ethnic demographics

Main article: [Ethnic groups in South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_South_America)



A Japanese-Brazilian [Miko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miko) during a festival in [Curitiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curitiba)



President of Brazil Lula and members of the [Italian Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Brazilian) community during the Grape Festival at [Caxias do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caxias_do_Sul)



[Peruvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian) woman and her son

[Genetic admixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_admixture) occurs at high levels in South America. In Argentina, European influence accounts for 65–80% of the genetic background, [Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindian) (indigenous people) 17–31% and sub-Saharan African 2–4%. In Colombia, the sub-Saharan African genetic background varied 1% to 89%, while the European genetic background varied from 20 to 79%, depending on the region. In Peru, Depending on the region, Peruvian regions can range from 10-20% (central) to 40-50% (north) of European ancestry.[^cite_note-236] The Genographic Project determined the average Peruvian from Lima had about 25% European ancestry, 68% Native American, 3% Southwest Asian ancestry and 2% sub-Saharan African.[^cite_note-237] According to a [genetic research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_research) in 2020, Peruvian genetic admixture indicates 63,7% Amerindian, 29,6% European, 4,8% African ancestry and 2,9% Asian.[^cite_note-238]

Descendants of [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America), such as the [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechuas) and [Aymara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_people), or the [Urarina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urarina)[^cite_note-239] of [Amazonia](48139.md), make up the majority of the population in Bolivia (56%) and Peru (44%).[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241] In Ecuador, Amerindians comprise two-fifths of the population. The indigenous population is also a significant element in most other countries in South America.

People who identify as of primarily or totally [European descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_skin), or identify their [phenotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotype) as corresponding to such group, are a majority in Uruguay,[^cite_note-blanca-242] Argentina[^cite_note-243] and are 43.5% of the population in Brazil.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246] In Venezuela, according to the census, 42% of the population is of primarily Spanish, Italian or Portuguese descendence.[^cite_note-247] In Colombia, people who identify as from European descendants are about 20%.[^cite_note-The_Society_and_Its_Environment-248][^cite_note-schwartzman-249] In Peru, European descendants are the third group in number (15%).[^cite_note-250]

[Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) (mixed European and Amerindian) are the largest ethnic group in Peru, Paraguay, Venezuela, Colombia[^cite_note-The_Society_and_Its_Environment-248] and Ecuador and the second group in Bolivia, Chile and Argentina.

South America is home to one of the largest populations of [Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africans). This group is significantly present in Brazil, Colombia, [Guyana](17238691.md), [Suriname](26828.md), French Guiana, Venezuela and Ecuador. Brazil, followed by Peru, has the largest Japanese, Korean and Chinese communities in South America. Lima has the largest ethnic Chinese community in Latin America.[^cite_note-251] Guyana and Suriname have the largest ethnic [East Indian](14533.md) community.

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_South_America)



Teatro Solis, Uruguay



[National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Nacional_do_Rio_de_Janeiro), Brazil



[Arya Diwaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arya_Diwaker) Hindu temple, [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo), Suriname

South Americans are culturally influenced by their indigenous peoples, the historic connection with the Iberian Peninsula and Africa, and waves of immigrants from around the globe.

South American nations have a rich variety of [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_music). Some of the most famous genres include [vallenato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallenato) and [cumbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbia) from Colombia, [pasillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasillo) from Colombia and Ecuador, [samba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba), [bossa nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossa_nova) and [música sertaneja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BAsica_sertaneja) from Brazil, [joropo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joropo) from Venezuela and [tango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_music) from Argentina and Uruguay. Also well known is the non-commercial folk genre [Nueva Canción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Canci%C3%B3n) movement which was founded in Argentina and Chile and quickly spread to the rest of the Latin America.



[Tango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango) show in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), typical Argentine dance



[Carmen Miranda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Miranda), a Portuguese Brazilian singer, helped popularize [samba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba) internationally.

People on the Peruvian coast created the fine guitar and [cajon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajon) duos or trios in the most [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) (mixed) of South American rhythms such as the Marinera (from Lima), the [Tondero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondero) (from Piura), the 19th-century popular Creole Valse or Peruvian Valse, the soulful Arequipan Yaravi, and the early-20th-century Paraguayan [Guarania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarania_\(music\)). In the late 20th century, [Spanish rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_en_Espa%C3%B1ol) emerged by young hipsters influenced by British pop and American rock. [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Brazil) has a Portuguese-language pop rock industry as well a great variety of other music genres. In the central and western regions of Bolivia, Andean and folklore music like [Diablada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diablada), [Caporales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caporales) and [Morenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morenada) are the most representative of the country, which were originated by European, Aymara and Quechua influences.

The [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_literature) of South America has attracted considerable critical and popular acclaim, especially with the [Latin American Boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Boom) of the 1960s and 1970s, and the rise of authors such as [Mario Vargas Llosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Vargas_Llosa), [Gabriel García Márquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Garc%C3%ADa_M%C3%A1rquez) in novels and [Jorge Luis Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Borges) and [Pablo Neruda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Neruda) in other genres. The Brazilians [Machado de Assis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machado_de_Assis) and [João Guimarães Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Guimar%C3%A3es_Rosa) are widely regarded as the greatest Brazilian writers.

### Food and drink

Main article: [Cuisine of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_South_America)



[Caipirinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caipirinha), a Brazilian [cocktail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocktail), of [São Paulo](229422.md) origin

Because of South America's broad ethnic mix, [South American cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_cuisine) has African, Mestizo, South Asian, East Asian, and European influences. [Bahia](199070.md), Brazil, is especially well known for its West African–influenced cuisine, while São Paulo's cuisine is a mix of [caipira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caipiras) and European's cuisine, especially the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine). Argentines, Chileans, Uruguayans, Brazilians, Bolivians, and Venezuelans regularly consume wine. People in Argentina, Paraguay, Uruguay, southern Chile, Bolivia and Southern Brazil drink [mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mate_\(beverage\)), an herb which is brewed. The Paraguayan version, [terere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terere), differs from other forms of mate in that it is served cold. [Pisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisco) is a liquor distilled from grapes in Peru and Chile. Peruvian cuisine mixes elements from Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, Italian, African, Arab, Andean, and Amazonic food.

### Plastic arts

Main article: [Art of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_South_America)



*Bird* ([UOB Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UOB_Plaza), Singapore), sculpture of Colombian artist [Fernando Botero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Botero)

The artist [Oswaldo Guayasamín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswaldo_Guayasam%C3%ADn) (1919–1999) from [Ecuador](9334.md), represented with his painting style the feeling of the peoples of Latin America[^cite_note-253] highlighting social injustices in various parts of the world. The Colombian [Fernando Botero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Botero) (1932–2023) was one of the greatest exponents of painting and sculpture that was able to develop a recognizable style of his own.[^cite_note-254] For his part, the Venezuelan [Carlos Cruz-Diez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Cruz-Diez) has contributed significantly to contemporary art,[^cite_note-255] with the presence of works around the world.

Currently several emerging South American artists are recognized by international art critics: [Guillermo Lorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Lorca?action=edit\&redlink=1), a Chilean painter,[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257] [Teddy Cobeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Cobe%C3%B1a), an Ecuadorian sculptor and recipient of international sculpture award in France,[^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260] and Argentine artist [Adrián Villar Rojas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adri%C3%A1n_Villar_Rojas),[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] winner of the Zurich Museum Art Award, among many others.

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_South_America)

A wide range of sports are played in the continent of South America, with [football](10568.md) being the most popular overall, while baseball is the most popular in Venezuela.

&#x20;Other sports include basketball, [cycling](857311.md), [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo), volleyball, [futsal](188197.md), [motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsports), [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football) (mostly in Argentina and Uruguay), [handball](13730.md), tennis, golf, [field hockey](10886.md), [boxing](4243.md), and [cricket](25675557.md).



[Maracanã Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium) in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

South America hosted its first [Olympic Games](22576.md) in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in 2016, and has hosted the [Youth Olympic Games](11315968.md) in Buenos Aires, Argentina, in 2018. A multi-sport event, the [South American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Games), are held every four years. The first edition was held in [La Paz](42821.md) in 1978 and the most recent took place in [Santiago](51572.md) in 2014.

South America shares, with Europe, supremacy over football: all winners in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) and [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) have come from these two continents. Brazil has won the FIFA World Cup a record five times; Argentina three times, Uruguay twice. Five South American nations have hosted the tournament including the first edition in Uruguay (1930). Two were in Brazil (1950, 2014), Chile (1962), and Argentina (1978). South America is home to the longest-running international football tournament, the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), which has been contested since 1916. Argentina has [won the Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_finals) 16 times, the most among all countries.

[South American Cricket Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Cricket_Championship) is an international [one-day cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-day_cricket) tournament played since 1995 featuring national teams from [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_South_America) and certain other invited sides including teams from North America, currently played annually but until 2013 was usually played every two seasons.
