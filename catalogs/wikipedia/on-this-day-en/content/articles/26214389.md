**Doha**[^cite_note-2] is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_city) city, and main [financial hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_hub) of [Qatar](8238258.md). Located on the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) coast in the east of the country, north of [Al Wakrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Wakrah) and south of [Al Khor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Khor_\(city\)) and [Lusail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusail), it is home to most of the country's population.[^cite_note-3] It is also Qatar's fastest growing city and over 80% of the nation's population live in Doha or its surrounding [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb), which are known collectively as the [Doha Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Metropolitan_Area).[^cite_note-OBGReport2016-4]

Doha was founded in the 1820s as an offshoot of [Al Bidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bidda). It was officially declared as the country's capital in 1971, when Qatar gained independence from being a [British protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Qatar#British_protectorate_.281916.E2.80.931971.29).[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-5] As the commercial capital of Qatar and one of the emergent financial centers in the [Middle East](19323.md), Doha is ranked "Beta+" on the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) scale of "integration into the world city network".[^cite_note-6] Metropolitan Doha includes parts of [Al Rayyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Rayyan) such as [Education City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_City), an area devoted to research and education, and [Hamad Medical City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_Medical_City), an administrative area of medical care. It also includes Doha Sports City, or [Aspire Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspire_Zone), an international sports destination that includes [Khalifa International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_International_Stadium), [Hamad Aquatic Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_Aquatic_Centre) and the [Aspire Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspire_Dome).

The city was host to the first ministerial-level meeting of the [Doha Development Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Development_Round) of [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) negotiations. It was also selected as host city of several sporting events, including the [2006 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Asian_Games), the [2011 Pan Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Pan_Arab_Games), the [2019 World Beach Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Beach_Games), the [2024 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Aquatics_Championships), the [FIVB Volleyball Club World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIVB_Volleyball_Men's_Club_World_Championship), the [WTA Finals](1356185.md) and most of the games at the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup). In December 2011, the [World Petroleum Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Petroleum_Council) held the 20th World Petroleum Conference in Doha.[^cite_note-7] Additionally, the city hosted the [2012 UNFCCC Climate Negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference), the 140th [Inter-Parliamentary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Parliamentary_Union) Assembly in 2019 and the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md).[^cite_note-8] The city will host the [2027 FIBA Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup).

## Etymology

According to the [Ministry of Municipality and Environment of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Municipality_\(Qatar\)), the name "Doha" originated from the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) term *dohat*, meaning "roundness"—a reference to the rounded bays surrounding the area's coastline.[^cite_note-gis-9]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Doha) and [Al Bidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bidda)



A satellite view of Doha on the East coast of Qatar. As with most world cities, Doha developed on the waterfront. Established in today's [Souq Waqif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souq_Waqif) area, it spread out gradually in a radial pattern with ring roads.

### Establishment of Al Bidda

The city of Doha was formed seceding from another local settlement known as [Al Bidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bidda). The earliest documented mention of Al Bidda was made in 1681 by the [Carmelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmelites) Convent, in an account that chronicles several settlements in Qatar. In the record, the ruler and a fort in the confines of Al Bidda are alluded to.[^cite_note-origins1-10][^cite_note-11] [Carsten Niebuhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsten_Niebuhr), a German explorer who visited the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), created one of the first maps to depict the settlement in 1765, in which he labelled it as 'Guttur'.[^cite_note-origins1-10][^cite_note-12]

David Seaton, a British [political resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_Resident) in [Muscat](57053.md), wrote the first English record of Al Bidda in 1801. He refers to the town as 'Bedih' and describes the geography and defensive structures in the area.[^cite_note-13] He stated that the town had recently been settled by the [Sudan tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_\(tribe\)) (singular Al-Suwaidi), whom he considered to be [pirates](50715.md). Seaton attempted to bombard the town with his [warship](263960.md), but returned to Muscat upon finding that the waters were too shallow to position his warship within striking distance.[^cite_note-origins2-14][^cite_note-15]

In 1820, British surveyor R. H. Colebrook, who visited Al Bidda, remarked on the recent depopulation of the town. He wrote:[^cite_note-origins2-14][^cite_note-16]

> Guttur – Or Ul Budee \[Al‐Bidda], once a considerable town, is protected by two square Ghurries \[forts] near the seashore; but containing no freshwater they are incapable of defense except against sudden incursions of Bedouins, another Ghurry is situated two miles inland and has fresh water with it. This could contain two hundred men. There are remaining at Ul Budee about 250 men, but the original inhabitants, who may be expected to return from Bahrein, will augment them to 900 or 1,000 men, and if the Doasir tribe, who frequent the place as divers, again settle in it, from 600 to 800 men.

The same year, an agreement known as the [General Maritime Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Maritime_Treaty_of_1820) was signed between the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) and the sheikhs of several Persian Gulf settlements (some of which were later known as the [Trucial Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucial_Coast)). It acknowledged [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) authority in the Persian Gulf and sought to end piracy and the slave trade. Bahrain became a party to the treaty, and it was assumed that Qatar, perceived as a dependency of Bahrain by the British, was also a party to it.[^cite_note-cs-17] Qatar, however, was not asked to fly the prescribed Trucial flag.[^cite_note-18] As punishment for alleged piracy committed by the inhabitants of Al Bidda and breach of the treaty, an East India Company vessel bombarded the town in 1821. They razed the town, forcing between 300 and 400 natives to flee and temporarily take shelter on the islands between Qatar and the Trucial Coast.[^cite_note-19]

### Formation of Doha

Doha was founded in the vicinity of Al Bidda sometime during the 1820s.[^cite_note-origins3-20] In January 1823, the British [political resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_Residency) John MacLeod visited Al Bidda to meet with the ruler and initial founder of Doha, Buhur bin Jubrun, who was also the chief of the [Al-Buainain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Buainain) tribe.[^cite_note-origins3-20][^cite_note-21] MacLeod noted that Al Bidda was the only substantial trading port in the peninsula during this time. Following the founding of Doha, written records often conflated Al Bidda and Doha due to their extremely close proximity.[^cite_note-origins3-20] Later that year, Lieutenant Guy and Lieutenant Brucks mapped and wrote a description of the two settlements. Despite being mapped as two separate entities, they were referred to under the collective name of Al Bidda in the written description.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

Map of Al Bidda

Al Bidda: view from the bay, 1823

In 1828, Mohammed bin Khamis, a prominent member of the Al-Buainain tribe and successor of Buhur bin Jubrun as chief of Al Bidda, was embroiled in controversy. He had murdered a native of [Bahrain](18933277.md), prompting the [Al Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Khalifa) sheikh to imprison him. In response, the Al-Buainain tribe revolted, provoking the Al Khalifa to destroy the tribe's fort and evict them to [Fuwayrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuwayrit) and [Ar Ru'ays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ar_Ru'ays). This incident allowed the Al Khalifa additional jurisdiction over the town.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] With essentially no effective ruler, Al Bidda and Doha became a sanctuary for pirates and outlaws.[^cite_note-origins6-26]



'Trigonometrical plan of the harbor of El Biddah on the Arabian side of the Persian Gulf', 1823

In November 1839, an outlaw from [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) named Ghuleta took refuge in Al Bidda, evoking a harsh response from the British. A. H. Nott, a [British naval](26061.md) commander, demanded that Salemin bin Nasir Al-Suwaidi, chief of the Sudan tribe (Suwaidi) in Al Bidda, take Ghuleta into custody and warned him of consequences in the case of non-compliance. Al-Suwaidi obliged the British request in February 1840 and also arrested the pirate [Jasim bin Jabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasim_bin_Jabir) and his associates. Despite the compliance, the British demanded a fine of 300 German [krones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_krone) in compensation for the damages incurred by pirates off the coast of Al Bidda; namely for the piracy committed by bin Jabir. In February 1841, British naval squadrons arrived in Al Bidda and ordered Al-Suwaidi to meet the British demand, threatening consequences if he declined. Al-Suwaidi ultimately declined on the basis that he was uninvolved in bin Jabir's actions. On 26 February, the British fired on Al Bidda, striking a fort and several houses. Al-Suwaidi then paid the fine in full following threats of further action by the British.[^cite_note-origins6-26][^cite_note-27]

[Isa bin Tarif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isa_bin_Tarif), a powerful tribal chief from the [Al Bin Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bin_Ali) tribe, moved to Doha in May 1843. He subsequently evicted the ruling Sudan tribe and installed the [Al-Maadeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Maadeed) and Al-Kuwari tribes in positions of power.[^cite_note-origins4-28] Bin Tarif had been loyal to the Al Khalifa, however, shortly after the swearing-in of a new ruler in Bahrain, bin Tarif grew increasingly suspicious of the ruling Al Khalifa and switched his allegiance to the deposed ruler of Bahrain, [Abdullah bin Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Khalifa#List_of_monarchs_of_Bahrain_of_the_Al_Khalifa_family), whom he had previously assisted in deposing of. Bin Tarif died in the [Battle of Fuwayrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuwayrit#Battle_of_Fuwayrit) against the ruling family of Bahrain in 1847.[^cite_note-origins4-28]

### Arrival of the House of Al Thani

The [Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Thani) family migrated to Doha from [Fuwayrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuwayrit) shortly after Bin Tarif's death in 1847 under the leadership of [Mohammed bin Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Thani).[^cite_note-origins5-29][^cite_note-30] In the proceeding years, the Al Thani family assumed control of the town. At various times, they swapped allegiances between the two prevailing powers in the area: the [Al Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Khalifa) of Bahrain and the [Saudis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Saud).[^cite_note-origins5-29]



Plan of Al Bidda Harbour, drawn in 1860, indicating the principal settlements and landmarks

In 1867, many ships and troops were sent from Bahrain to assault the towns [Al Wakrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Wakrah) and Doha over a series of disputes. [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) joined on Bahrain's behalf due to the perception that Al Wakrah served as a refuge for fugitives from [Oman](22316.md). Later that year, the combined forces sacked the two Qatari towns with around 2,700 men in what came to be known as the [Qatari–Bahraini War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari%E2%80%93Bahraini_War).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] A British record later stated that "the towns of Doha and Wakrah were, at the end of 1867 temporarily blotted out of existence, the houses being dismantled and the inhabitants deported".[^cite_note-33]

The joint Bahraini-Abu Dhabi incursion and subsequent Qatari counterattack prompted the British political agent, Colonel [Lewis Pelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Pelly), to impose a settlement in 1868. Pelly's mission to Bahrain and Qatar and the peace treaty that resulted were milestones in Qatar's history. It implicitly recognized Qatar as a distinct entity independent from Bahrain and explicitly acknowledged the position of Mohammed bin Thani as the main representative of the peninsula's tribes.[^cite_note-34]



A part of Doha as seen in January 1904. Most of the development was low-rise and the use of locally available natural materials like rammed earth and palm fronds was common practice.

In December 1871, the [Ottomans](22278.md) established a presence in the country with 100 of their troops occupying the Musallam fort in Doha. This was accepted by Mohammad bin Thani's son, [Jassim Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jassim_bin_Mohammed_Al_Thani), who wished to protect Doha from Saudi incursions.[^cite_note-35] The Ottoman commander, Major Ömer Bey, compiled a report on Al Bidda in January 1872, stating that it was an "administrative centre" with around 1,000 houses and 4,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-zekeriya-36]

Disagreement over tribute and interference in internal affairs arose, eventually leading to the [Battle of Al Wajbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Al_Wajbah) in March 1893. Al Bidda Fort served as the final point of retreat for Ottoman troops. While they were garrisoned in the fort, their [corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) fired indiscriminately at the townspeople, killing many civilians.[^cite_note-37] The Ottomans eventually surrendered after Jassim Al Thani's troops cut off the town's water supply.[^cite_note-38] An Ottoman report compiled the same year reported that Al Bidda and Doha had a combined population of 6,000 inhabitants, jointly referring to both towns by the name of 'Katar'. Doha was classified as the eastern section of Katar.[^cite_note-zekeriya-36][^cite_note-39] The Ottomans held a passive role in Qatar's politics from the 1890s onward until fully relinquishing control during the beginning of the [First World War](4764461.md).[^cite_note-cs-17]

### 20th century



The city's coastline in 1904 largely highlights the local community which was based on fishing and pearl diving.

[Pearling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_hunting) had come to play a pivotal commercial role in Doha by the 20th century. The population increased to around 12,000 inhabitants in the first half of the 20th century due to the flourishing pearl trade.[^cite_note-urbanevolution-40] A British political resident noted that should the supply of pearls drop, Qatar would 'practically cease to exist'.[^cite_note-leader-41] In 1907, the city accommodated 350 pearling boats with a combined crew size of 6,300 men. By this time, the average prices of pearls had more than doubled since 1877.[^cite_note-42] The pearl market collapsed that year, forcing Jassim Al Thani to sell the country's pearl harvest at half its value. The aftermath of the collapse resulted in the establishment of the country's first [custom house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custom_house) in Doha.[^cite_note-leader-41]

#### Lorimer report (1908)

British administrator and historian [J. G. Lorimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gordon_Lorimer_\(civil_servant\)) authored an extensive handbook for British agents in the Persian Gulf entitled *[Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazetteer_of_the_Persian_Gulf,_Oman_and_Central_Arabia)* in 1908. In it, he gives a comprehensive account of Doha at the time:



"Dohah looking northwest", photographed by the Royal Air Force during a reconnaissance of the Qatar Peninsula on 9 May 1934

> Generally so styled at the present day, but Bedouins sometimes call it Dohat-al-Qatar, and it seems to have been formerly better known as Bida' (*Anglice* "Bidder"): it is the chief town of Qatar and is situated on the eastern side of that peninsula, about 63 miles south of its extremity at [Ras Rakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Rakan) and 45 miles north of [Khor-al Odaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khawr_al_Udayd) Harbour. Dohah stands on the south side of a deep bay at the south-western corner of a natural harbor which is about 3 miles in extent and is protected on the north-east and south-east sides by natural reefs. The entrance, less than a mile wide, is from the east between the points of the reefs; it is shallow and somewhat difficult, and vessels of more than 15 feet draught cannot pass. The soundings within the basin vary from 3 to 5 fathoms and are regular: the bottom is white mud or clay.
>
> Townsite and quarters, — The south-eastern point of the bay are quite low but the land on the western side is stony desert 40 or 50 feet above the level of the sea. The town is built up the slope of some rising ground between these two extremes and consists of 9 Fanqs or quarters, which are given below in their order from the east to the west and north: the total frontage of the place upon the sea is nearly 2 miles.[^cite_note-43]



An old district in Doha, planned with narrow streets and rough plastered walls, give a glimpse of the city's past.

Lorimer goes on to list and describe the districts of Doha, which at the time included the still-existing districts of [Al Mirqab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mirqab_\(Doha\)), [As Salatah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Salatah), [Al Bidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bidda) and [Rumeilah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumeilah_\(Doha\)).[^cite_note-44] Remarking on Doha's appearance, he states:

> The general appearance of Dohah is unattractive; the lanes are narrow and irregular the houses dingy and small. There are no date palms or other trees, and the only garden is a small one near the fort, kept up by the Turkish garrison.[^cite_note-gaz540-45]

As for Doha's population, Lorimer asserts that "the inhabitants of Dohah are estimated to amount, inclusive of the Turkish military garrison of 350 men, to about 12,000 souls". He qualified this statement with a tabulated overview of the various tribes and ethnic groups living in the town.[^cite_note-gaz540-45]

#### British protectorate (1916–1971)

In April 1913, the Ottomans agreed to a British request to withdraw all their troops from Qatar. Ottoman presence in the peninsula ceased, when in August 1915, the Ottoman fort in Al Bidda was evacuated shortly after the start of World War I.[^cite_note-46] One year later, Qatar agreed to be a British [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) with Doha as its official capital.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]



Camels next to [Al Koot Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Koot_Fort), built in 1927, by [Abdullah bin Jassim Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_bin_Jassim_Al_Thani)

Buildings at the time were simple dwellings of one or two rooms, built from mud, stone, and coral. Oil concessions in the 1920s and 1930s, and subsequent oil drilling in 1939, heralded the beginning of slow economic and social progress in the country. However, revenues were somewhat diminished due to the devaluation of pearl trade in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) brought on by the introduction of the [cultured pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultured_pearl) and the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression).[^cite_note-49] The collapse of the pearl trade caused a significant population drop throughout the country.[^cite_note-urbanevolution-40] It was not until the 1950s and 1960s that the country saw significant monetary returns from oil drilling.[^cite_note-cs-17]



A view of Doha in the 1980s showing the [Sheraton Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheraton_Grand_Doha_Resort_&_Convention_Hotel) (pyramid-like building in the background) in [West Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bay_\(Doha\)) without any of the high-rises around it

Qatar was not long in exploiting the new-found wealth from oil concessions, and slum areas were quickly razed to be replaced by more modern buildings. In 1950, British adviser to the Emir, Phillip L. Plant, initiated several municipal projects. Starting with remodeling the old complex of the [Old Amiri Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Amiri_Palace), Plant then initiated the construction of a seaside road about a half-mile in length which opened up and made accessible the half dozen [jetties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetty) along Doha's most built-up section.[^cite_note-50] The first formal boys' school was established in Doha in 1952, followed three years later by the establishment of a girls' school.[^cite_note-51] Historically, Doha had been a commercial port of local significance. However, the shallow water of the bay prevented bigger ships from entering the port until the 1970s, when its deep-water port was completed. Further changes followed with extensive land reclamation, which led to the development of the crescent-shaped bay.[^cite_note-52] From the 1950s to 1970s, the population of Doha grew from around 14,000 inhabitants to over 83,000, with foreign immigrants constituting about two-thirds of the overall population.[^cite_note-53]

### Post-independence



[The Pearl-Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pearl-Qatar) at night



[Banana Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_Island_\(Qatar\)) is an artificial island measuring 13 hectares.

In April 1970, the first Qatari constitution was ratified under an Interim Basic Statute. This demanded that there be a formation of the first council of ministers, as well as a cabinet. The following year, Sheikh Khalifa bin Hamad Al Thani, the heir apparent, and prime minister dissolved the 1916 treaty, officially declaring Qatar as an independent state.[^cite_note-54] In 1973, the [University of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Qatar) was opened by emiri decree,[^cite_note-55] and in 1975, the [Qatar National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Museum) opened in what was originally the ruler's palace.[^cite_note-56] During the 1970s, all old neighborhoods in Doha were razed and the inhabitants moved to new suburban developments, such as [Al Rayyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Rayyan), [Madinat Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madinat_Khalifa_North) and [Al Gharafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gharrafa). The [Doha Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Metropolitan_Area)'s population grew from 89,000 in the 1970s to over 434,000 in 1997. Additionally, land policies resulted in the total land area increasing to over 7,100 hectares (about 17,000 acres) by 1995, an increase from 130 hectares in the middle of the 20th century.[^cite_note-urbanevolution2-57]



[Amiri Diwan of the State of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiri_Diwan_of_the_State_of_Qatar) in Doha

In 1983, a hotel and conference center was developed at the north end of the [Corniche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Corniche). The 15-story [Sheraton hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheraton_Grand_Doha_Resort_&_Convention_Hotel) structure in this center was the tallest structure in Doha until the 1990s.[^cite_note-urbanevolution2-57] In 1993, the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Qatar_Open) became the first major sports event to be hosted in the city.[^cite_note-urbanevolution3-58] Two years later, Qatar stepped in to host the [FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship), with all the matches being played in Doha-based stadiums.[^cite_note-1995fifa-59]



Developments in Doha's West Bay district have seen an increase in the population density of the area with the construction of several high-rises.



A view of a water feature in Sheraton Park with the West Bay skyline in the background

The [Al Jazeera](30204052.md) Arabic news channel began broadcasting from Doha in 1996.[^cite_note-60] In the late 1990s, the government planned the construction of [Education City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_City), a 2,500 hectare Doha-based complex mainly for educational institutes.[^cite_note-61] Since the start of the 21st century, Doha attained significant media attention due to the hosting of several global events and the inauguration of several architectural mega-projects.[^cite_note-demystifying-62] One of the largest projects launched by the government was [The Pearl-Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pearl-Qatar), an artificial island off the coast of [West Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bay_\(Doha\)), which launched its first district in 2004.[^cite_note-63] In 2006, Doha was selected to host the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Asian_Games), leading to the development of a 250-hectare sporting complex known as [Aspire Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspire_Zone).[^cite_note-urbanevolution3-58] During this time, new cultural attractions were constructed in the city, with older ones being restored. In 2006, the government launched a restoration program to preserve [Souq Waqif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souq_Waqif)'s architectural and historical identity. Parts constructed after the 1950s were demolished whereas older structures were refurbished. The restoration was completed in 2008.[^cite_note-64] [Katara Cultural Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katara_Cultural_Village) was opened in the city in 2010 and has hosted the [Doha Tribeca Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Tribeca_Film_Festival) since then.[^cite_note-65]

The main outcome of the [World Trade Organization Ministerial Conference of 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization_Ministerial_Conference_of_2013) was the Trade Facilitation Agreement. The agreement aims to make it easier and cheaper to import and export by improving customs procedures and making rules more transparent. Reducing global trade costs by 1% would increase worldwide income by more than US\$40 billion, 65% of which would go to developing countries. The gains from the Trade Facilitation Agreement are expected to be distributed among all countries and regions, with developing landlocked countries benefiting the most.[^cite_note-WTO-66]

The Trade Facilitation Agreement will enter into force upon its ratification by 2/3 of WTO Members. The EU ratified the agreement in October 2015.[^cite_note-WTO-66]

In Bali, WTO members also agreed on a series of Doha agriculture and development issues.[^cite_note-WTO-66] Now modernizing the city while preserving traditions is part of the country's long-term plan, [Qatar National Vision 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Vision_2030).&#x20;

On 9 September 2025, Israel [carried out an airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_strike_on_Hamas_headquarters_in_Qatar) in Doha, targeting a residential complex in the [West Bay Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bay_Lagoon) district which housed senior Hamas negotiators in Doha, who were working on a ceasefire agreement with Israel to end the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] It was the first time that Israel launched a direct attack on one of the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) members.[^cite_note-69]

## Geography

See also: [Geography of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Qatar)



A view of Doha from the [International Space Station](15043.md) in 2010 highlights the rapid development the city underwent since the discovery of oil in the 1960s.

### Topography and geology

Doha is located on the central-east portion of Qatar, bordered by the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) on its coast. Its elevation is 10 m (33 ft).[^cite_note-70] Doha is highly urbanized. [Land reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation) off the coast has added 400 hectares of land and 30 km (19 mi) of coastline.[^cite_note-71] Half of the 22 km<sup>2</sup> (8.5 sq mi) of surface area which [Hamad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_International_Airport) was constructed on was reclaimed land.[^cite_note-72] The geology of Doha is primarily composed of weathered unconformity on the top of the [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) period Dammam Formation, forming [dolomitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(mineral\)) [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone).[^cite_note-73]

### Wildlife

[Street cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral_cat), having first been introduced in the 1960s to deal with the country's rodent infestations, are now widespread in Doha, with government estimates putting the total population at between 2 million and 3 million.[^cite_note-74] The [Gulf sand gecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_short-fingered_gecko), having been first observed in Qatar on Doha's islands in 2013, is now also found on the promontory in coastal and saline places.[^cite_note-75]

Common vegetation found in Doha include creeping sorrel (*[Oxalis corniculata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxalis_corniculata)*) as a turf [weed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weed),[^cite_note-76] winter cherry (*[Physalis angulata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physalis_angulata)*) as a weed,[^cite_note-77] rye grass (*[Lolium rigidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolium_rigidum)*) in cultivated areas,[^cite_note-78] *qurdi* (*[Ochradenus baccatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochradenus_baccatus)*) near roadsides,[^cite_note-79] indigofera (*[Indigofera oblongifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigofera_oblongifolia?action=edit\&redlink=1)*) near the central market area,[^cite_note-80] curved hard grass (*[Parapholis incurva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapholis_incurva)*) near the coast,[^cite_note-81] cat's tail (*[Typha domingensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typha_domingensis)*) near moist areas,[^cite_note-82] oat brome (*[Bromus danthoniae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromus_danthoniae)*) near the [Al Sadd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sadd_\(Qatar\)) area,[^cite_note-83] and *ndeewa* (*[Cressa cretica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cressa_cretica)*).[^cite_note-84]

Flowers of the sacred datura (*[Datura innoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datura_innoxia)*),[^cite_note-85] crown daisy (*[Glebionis coronaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glebionis_coronaria)*),[^cite_note-86] wild chamomile (*[Matricaria chamomilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matricaria_chamomilla)*),[^cite_note-87] and glandular speedwell (*[Veronica cymbalaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_cymbalaria)*)[^cite_note-88] have also been observed in rare instances, the latter as a weed. Herbs such as *rabl* (*[Plantago psyllium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantago_psyllium)*), which is found primarily near roadsides, and *shih* (*[Artemisia inculta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemisia_inculta)*)[^cite_note-89] also occur infrequently.

*[Prosopis juliflora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosopis_juliflora)*, a type of [mesquite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesquite) tree locally known as *ghoweif* and was once the only feature on Palm Tree Island. It was previously a popular tree cultivated around Qatar's urban areas, but this practice has stopped since 2005 and since then attempts have been made to eliminate traces of the tree, to varying degrees of success.[^cite_note-90]

### Islands and coast



The [Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pearl-Qatar) is a purpose-built artificial island off the coast of Doha, connected to the mainland by a bridge.

[The Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pearl-Qatar) is an [artificial island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_island) in Doha with a surface area of nearly 400 ha (1,000 acres)[^cite_note-91] The total project has been estimated to cost \$15 billion upon completion.[^cite_note-92] Other islands off Doha's coast include Palm Tree Island, Shrao's Island, [Al Safliya Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Safliya_Island), and [Al Aaliya Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Aaliya_Island).[^cite_note-93]

In a 2010 survey of Doha's coastal waters conducted by the [Qatar Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Statistics_Authority), it was found that its maximum depth was 7.5 meters (25 ft) and minimum depth was 2 meters (6 ft 7 in). The waters had an average pH of 7.83, a [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity) of 49.0 psu, an average temperature of 22.7 °C (72.9 °F) and 5.5 mg/L of dissolved oxygen.[^cite_note-94]

### Climate

Doha has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWh*) with long, extremely hot summers and short, mild to warm winters. The average high temperatures between May and September surpass 38 °C (100 °F) and often approach 45 °C (113 °F). Humidity is usually the lowest in May and June. [Dewpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewpoint) can surpass 30 °C (86 °F) in the summer. Throughout the summer, the city averages almost no precipitation, and less than 20 mm (0.79 in) during other months.[^cite_note-95] Rainfall is scarce, at a total of 75 mm (2.95 in) per year, falling on isolated days mostly between October and March. The winter's days are relativity warm while the sun is up and cool during the night. The temperature rarely drops below 7 °C (45 °F). The highest temperature recorded was 50.4 °C (122.7 °F) on 14 July 2010, which is the highest temperature ever recorded in Qatar.[^cite_note-96]

### Ethnicity and languages

The population of Doha is overwhelmingly composed of [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriates), with [Qatari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari) nationals forming a minority. The largest portion of expatriates in Qatar are from South-East and [South Asian](21566765.md) countries, mainly [India](14533.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Sri Lanka](26750.md), [Nepal](171166.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), and [Bangladesh](3454.md) with large numbers of expatriates also coming from the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) countries, Djibouti, [Somalia](27358.md), North Africa, and [East Asia](19605700.md). Doha is also home to many expatriates from [Europe](9239.md), [North America](21139.md), [South Africa](17416221.md) and [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-qcri-112]



A typical [bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilingual) traffic sign in Doha denotes the zone numbers, street names and street numbers of two perpendicular streets.

[Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) is the official language of Qatar. [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) is commonly used as a second language,[^cite_note-113] and a rising [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca), especially in commerce.[^cite_note-114] As there is a large expatriate population in Doha, languages such as [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language), [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), [Spanish](26825.md), [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) and [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) are widely spoken.[^cite_note-qcri-112]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Qatar)

The majority of residents in Doha are [Muslim](6037917.md).[^cite_note-119] A majority of mosques are either [Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement) or [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni)-oriented.[^cite_note-120]

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Qatar)



View of the city, with the [Qatar petroleum district](11292570.md) on the right

Doha is the economic center of Qatar. The city is the headquarters of numerous domestic and international organizations, including the country's largest oil and gas companies, [QatarEnergy](11292570.md) and [QatarEnergy LNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QatarEnergy_LNG). Doha's economy is built primarily on the revenue the country has made from its [oil](4207510.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) industries.[^cite_note-dumper-127] Doha was included in Fortune's 15 best new cities for business in 2011.[^cite_note-128] Beginning in the late 20th century, the government launched numerous initiatives to diversify the country's economy to decrease its dependence on oil and gas resources. [Doha International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_International_Airport) was constructed in a bid to solidify the city's diversification into the tourism industry.[^cite_note-dumper-127] This was replaced by [Hamad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_International_Airport) in 2014. The new airport is almost twice the size of the former and features two of the longest runways in the world.[^cite_note-129] Thirty-nine new hotels were under construction in the city in 2011.[^cite_note-bullivant-130]



[Qatar Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Airways) aircraft on the [apron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_apron) of [Hamad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_International_Airport)

As a result of Doha's rapid population boom and increased housing demands, real estate prices rose significantly through 2014.[^cite_note-housingdohanews-131] Real estate prices experienced a further spike after Qatar won the rights to host the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md).[^cite_note-132] Al Asmakh, a Qatari real estate firm, released a report in 2014 which revealed substantial increases in real estate prices following a peak in 2008. Prices increased 5 to 10% in the first quarter of 2014 from the end of 2013.[^cite_note-housingdohanews-131][^cite_note-133] A 2015 study conducted by [Numbeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbeo), a crowd-sourced database, named Doha as the 10th most expensive city to live in globally.[^cite_note-134] This rate of growth led to the development of planned communities in and around the city.[^cite_note-135] Although the [fall in oil prices since 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010s_oil_glut) and a [diplomatic crisis with Qatar's neighbors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_diplomatic_crisis) slowed growth in the city's population, government spending was increased to maintain the growth in real estate in [metropolitan Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Metropolitan_Area).[^cite_note-136]

Expatriate workers remitted \$60bn between 2006 and 2012, with 54 percent of the workers' [remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) of \$60bn routed to Asian countries, followed by Arab nations that accounted for nearly half that volume (28 percent). India was the top destination of the remittances, followed by the Philippines, while the US, Egypt and the neighbouring UAE followed.[^cite_note-137] Remittances in 2014 totaled \$11.2 billion, amounting to 5.3% of Qatar's GDP.[^cite_note-138]<sup>: 45 </sup>

## Architecture

See also: [Architecture of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Qatar) and [List of tallest buildings in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Qatar)

### Traditional architecture



Historic [arcaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_\(architecture\)) house in the [Old Al Ghanim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Al_Ghanim) district



Traditional buildings at Katara Cultural Village

In Doha's traditional architecture, the primary building material was locally quarried [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), with walls typically measuring 0.4 metres (1 ft 4 in) to 0.6 metres (2 ft 0 in) thick, constructed using two rows of large facing stones with a core of mud, gravel, and smaller stones. These walls were then covered with clay-rich mud or [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum)-based render. [Plaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaster), known as *juss*, was made from crushed [beachrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachrock), shell, baked lime, or gypsum, and applied in three layers for both functional and decorative purposes. Roofing was characteristically flat, utilizing [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) beams (*danshal*) imported from [East Africa](260987.md), overlaid with split [bamboo](39029.md), woven [reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_\(plant\)), and [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae) mats, then sealed with tamped earth.[^cite_note-149] Distinctive architectural elements included colonnaded [verandas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veranda) ([liwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwan)) with lintels made of mangrove poles bound with [jute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jute) rope, and wooden [waterspouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterspout) for drainage. Valuable materials such as wooden beams, windows, and doors were often recycled from older structures.[^cite_note-150]

Many of the older structures (1960–1970s) in the Old Doha districts have been demolished to make space for new buildings.[^cite_note-bouassa-126] A number of schemes have been taken to preserve the city's cultural and architectural heritage, such as the [Qatar Museums Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Museums_Authority)'s 'Al Turath al Hai' ('living heritage') initiative.[^cite_note-151] Katara Cultural Village is a model village in Doha launched by [Sheikha Moza bint Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moza_bint_Nasser) under [Qatar Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Foundation) to preserve the cultural identity of the country.[^cite_note-152]

### Modern architecture



Doha's [Al Dafna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Dafna) area with the high-rises seen on the waterfront and the villa compounds and other residential areas seen in the background

During the mid-20th century, Qatar saw a transition from traditional building techniques to more modern ones. In the 1950s, a notable change was the adoption of [concrete blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_block) with shell inclusions. These blocks were primarily produced locally and incorporated a distinctive shelly sand rich in small [gastropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastropod) remains, a material abundant along Qatar's coastline.[^cite_note-eddisford13-153]



Doha Skyline and [Qatar Petroleum District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Petroleum_District)

As Qatar progressed into the latter half of the 20th century, construction methods evolved, with the use of shell-infused concrete blocks gradually giving way to more standardized building materials. This newer phase of construction is characterized by the use of plain concrete blocks without shell content and [reinforced concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforced_concrete) structures. New materials such as manufactured wood products including [plywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plywood) and metal materials such as [corrugated iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrugated_iron) were also introduced. This period also saw the widespread adoption of air conditioning systems.[^cite_note-eddisford13-153] In 2011, more than 50 towers were under construction in Doha,[^cite_note-bullivant-130] the largest of which was the [Doha Convention Center Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Convention_Center_Tower).[^cite_note-154] Constructions were suspended in 2012 following concerns that the tower would impede flight traffic[^cite_note-155] and the site is being redeveloped into a park.

In 2014, [Abdullah Al Attiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_bin_Hamad_Al_Attiyah), a senior government official, announced that Qatar would spend \$65bn on new infrastructure projects in upcoming years in preparation for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) as well as progressing towards its objectives set out in the [Qatar National Vision 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Vision_2030).[^cite_note-156]

[Msheireb Downtown Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msheireb_Downtown_Doha), a 31-hectare development costing an estimated \$5bn, was dubbed the largest inner city redevelopment of its kind when launched. Comprising several quarters opened over different phases, Msheireb aims to preserve the [historic downtown area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushayrib).

### Atmosphere

In the pre-modern era, Doha's architectural landscape was characterized by densely clustered dwellings interconnected by narrow passageways, a design that offered mutual shade, privacy, and safety to residents. This urban configuration persisted until the latter part of the 20th century. Traditional building techniques in Doha emphasized climate adaptation. Thick stone walls, often reaching 0.6 metres (2 ft 0 in) in width, served as effective thermal insulators, mitigating heat transfer to the interior. Facades typically featured minimal [fenestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenestration_\(architecture\)) to maintain privacy and regulate internal temperatures. When present, windows were often internal and shuttered, frequently shielded by expansive [verandas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veranda) to minimize solar heat gain.[^cite_note-eddisford7-175]

The *badgheer* system was used to facilitate air circulation for cooling. This encompassed both [windcatchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windcatcher) and wall vents, with the latter being more prevalent in Doha. Large open [courtyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard) were integral to promoting air movement within structures. Design elements such as horizontal apertures at wall bases and within roof screens generated cooling breezes and air circulation. Ornate, perforated [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum) panels served dual purposes, acting as decorative elements while enhancing airflow within domiciles.[^cite_note-eddisford7-175]

Due to excessive heat from the sun during the summer, some Doha-based building companies implemented various forms of [cooling technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning) to alleviate the extremely torrid climatic conditions in the late 20th century. This included creating optical phenomena such as [shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow), as well as more expensive techniques like ventilation, coolants, refrigerants, cryogenics, and dehumidifiers.[^cite_note-176] Discussions regarding temperature control have also been features of various scheduled events involving large crowds.[^cite_note-177] Other initiatives attempt to counter the heat by altering working hours, weather alteration methods such as [cloud seeding](449660.md),[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] and using [whiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White) and brighter construction materials to increase the [albedo effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo_effects).[^cite_note-180] Nonetheless, despite these measures, Doha and other areas of Qatar could become [uninhabitable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_habitability) for humans due to climate change by the end of the 21st century.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Doha)

To support the expanding city and increasing numbers of residents and commuters, Qatar has heavily invested in upgrading the infrastructure of Doha and Qatar. Since 2004, Doha has been undergoing a huge expansion to its transportation network, including the addition of new [highways](48519.md), a [new airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_International_Airport) in 2014, a new [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_Port) in 2016, and an 85 kilometres (53 mi) [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system which went operational in 2019.[^cite_note-cwo1-185]

### Roads



Dukhan Highway connects the city of [Dukhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukhan) on the West coast of the country with the country's capital, Doha.

The main highways in Doha include Dukhan Highway connecting to [Dukhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukhan);[^cite_note-186] the 95.2 kilometres (59.2 mi) Al Shamal Road connecting the Doha Metropolitan Area to the northern region of [Al Shamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Shamal);[^cite_note-187] the 33 kilometres (21 mi) Al Khor Coastal Road connecting to [Al Khor](19309079.md),[^cite_note-188] the 95 kilometres (59 mi) Salwa Road connecting to the [Saudi Arabian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_border);[^cite_note-189] the 195 kilometres (121 mi) [Al Majd Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Majd_Road) which is the longest highway in Qatar;[^cite_note-190] the 5.5 kilometres (3.4 mi) Lusail Expressway connecting to [Lusail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusail);[^cite_note-191] the 8.7 kilometres (5.4 mi) F-Ring Road linking Rawdat Al Khail Street to Al Matar Street;[^cite_note-192] the 22 kilometres (14 mi) G-Ring Road connecting Hamad International Airport to the south of the [Industrial Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Area_\(Doha\));[^cite_note-193] and the Industrial Area Road, a 12.5 kilometres (7.8 mi)-dual carriageway positioned south of the Doha Industrial Area.[^cite_note-194]

One of the latest projects, expected to be delivered in 2024 is the Sharq Crossing in [Doha Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Bay). At a cost of approximately \$12 billion, the crossing is expected to connect Hamad International Airport with both [Katara Cultural Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katara_Cultural_Village) and [West Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bay_\(Doha\)).[^cite_note-195]

### Rail



Doha Metro interior

Three of the [Doha Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Metro)'s four lines are currently fully operational. The system consists of the Red Line, the Gold Line, the Green Line, and the incomplete Blue Line. The Blue Line is expected to be completed in the second phase in 2025.[^cite_note-196] [Msheireb Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msheireb_station) is the transfer station for all of the metro lines.[^cite_note-cwo1-185]

### Air

Doha is served by [Hamad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_International_Airport) which is Qatar's principal international gateway. The airport opened in 2014, replacing [Doha International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_International_Airport). The airport is named after the previous Emir of Qatar, [Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani](295929.md). Hamad International Airport became the first [Middle Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern) airport to be awarded the [Skytrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skytrax)'s World Best Airport for 2021 in the 2021 World Airport Awards, ending the 7-year dominance of [Singapore](27318.md)'s [Changi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changi_Airport).[^cite_note-198] Qatar's Hamad International Airport has been named the World's Best Airport for the second year running. The announcement took place at the Skytrax 2022 World Airport Awards, held at Passenger Terminal EXPO in Paris, France.[^cite_note-199]

[Education City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_City), a 14 km<sup>2</sup> (5.4 sq mi) education complex launched by non-profit organization [Qatar Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Foundation), began construction in 2000.[^cite_note-fteducation-206] It houses eight universities, the country's top high school, and offices for [Al Jazeera's children television channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JeemTV).[^cite_note-fteducation-206] It is geographically located in [Al Rayyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Rayyan) municipality's [Al Luqta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Luqta), [Al Gharrafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gharrafa), [Gharrafat Al Rayyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharrafat_Al_Rayyan) and [Al Shagub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Shagub) districts, but falls under the umbrella of [Metropolitan Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-gis-9]

In 2009, the government launched the [World Innovation Summit for Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Innovation_Summit_for_Education) (WISE), a global forum that brings together education stakeholders, opinion leaders and decision-makers from all over the world to discuss educational issues.[^cite_note-207] The first edition was held in Doha in November 2009.[^cite_note-208]

## Sports

Main article: [Sport in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Qatar)

### Football



[Al Sadd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sadd_SC) is the most successful team in the [Qatar Stars League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Stars_League).

See also: [List of football stadiums in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_stadiums_in_Qatar)

[Football](10568.md) is the most popular sport in Doha. There are six Doha-based sports clubs with football teams competing in the [Qatar Stars League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Stars_League), the country's top football league. They are [Al Ahli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ahli_SC_\(Doha\)), [Al Arabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Arabi_SC_\(Doha\)), [Al Sadd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sadd_SC), [Al-Duhail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Duhail_SC) and [Qatar SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_SC).[^cite_note-211] Al Sadd, Al Arabi and Qatar SC are the three most successful teams in the league's history.[^cite_note-212]

Numerous football tournaments have been hosted in Doha. The most prestigious tournaments include the [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_AFC_Asian_Cup) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup) editions of the [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup)[^cite_note-213] and the [1995 FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship).[^cite_note-1995fifa-59]

In December 2010, Qatar won the right to host the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md).[^cite_note-214] Three of the nine newly announced stadiums were constructed in Doha, including [Sports City Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_City_Stadium_\(Doha\)), [Doha Port Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Port_Stadium), and [Qatar University Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_University_Stadium). Additionally, the [Khalifa International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_International_Stadium) underwent expansion.[^cite_note-215]

Considering the country's rapid development for the 2022 World Cup, FIFA awarded the hosting rights of [2019 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) and [2020 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) also to Qatar.[^cite_note-216]

### Basketball

Doha was the host of the official [2005 FIBA Asia Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIBA_Asia_Championship), where [Qatar's national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar's_national_basketball_team) finished 3rd, its best performance to date, and subsequently qualified for the [Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_World_Cup).[^cite_note-217]

The city will host matches for the [2027 FIBA Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup).

The majority of the teams that make up the official [Qatari Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari_Basketball_League) are based in Doha.

### Volleyball

Doha four times was the host of the official [FIVB Volleyball Men's Club World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIVB_Volleyball_Men's_Club_World_Championship) and three times host [FIVB Volleyball Women's Club World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIVB_Volleyball_Women's_Club_World_Championship). Doha also hosted the [Asian Volleyball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_Men's_Volleyball_Championship).[^cite_note-218]

### Other sports



Orry the Oryx, mascot of the [15th Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Asian_Games), on the [Doha Corniche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Corniche) in 2014

In 2001, Qatar became the first country in the Middle East to hold a women's tennis tournament with the inauguration of its [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Ladies_Open) tournament.[^cite_note-219] Doha also hosts [International Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tennis_Federation) (ITF) ladies tournaments. Since 2008, the [Sony Ericsson Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Ericsson_Championships) (equivalent to the ATP's season-ending Championships) has taken place in Doha, in the Khalifa International Tennis Complex, and features record prize money of \$4.45 million, including a check of \$1,485,000 for the winner, which represents the largest single guaranteed payout in women's tennis.[^cite_note-220]

Doha hosted the [15th Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Asian_Games), held in December 2006, spending a total of \$2.8 billion for its preparation.[^cite_note-221] The city also hosted the [3rd West Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asian_Games) in December 2005.[^cite_note-222] Doha was expected to host the [2011 Asian Indoor Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Asian_Indoor_Games); but the [Qatar Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Olympic_Committee) cancelled the event.[^cite_note-223]



Powerboat races in [Doha Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Bay)

The city submitted a [bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_bid_for_the_2016_Summer_Olympics) for the [2016 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Olympics).[^cite_note-224] On 4 June 2008, the city was eliminated from the shortlist for the 2016 Olympic Games. On 26 August 2011, it was confirmed that Doha would [bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_bid_for_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) for the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-225] Doha however failed to become a Candidate City for the 2020 Games.[^cite_note-226]

The [MotoGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MotoGP) motorcycling grand prix of Doha is held annually at [Losail International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losail_International_Circuit), located just outside the city boundaries.[^cite_note-227] The city is also the location of the Grand Prix of Qatar for the [F1 Powerboat World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1_Powerboat_World_Championship), annually hosting a round in [Doha Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Bay).[^cite_note-228] Beginning in November 2009, Doha has been host of [The Oryx Cup World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oryx_Cup), a hydroplane boat race in the [H1 Unlimited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H1_Unlimited) season. The races take place in Doha Bay.[^cite_note-229]

In April 2012 Doha was awarded both the [2014 FINA World Swimming Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FINA_World_Swimming_Championships_\(25_m\))[^cite_note-230] and the 2012 World Squash Championships.[^cite_note-231] The fourth World Mindsports Championships took place in Doha from 19 – 27 August 2017, with the participation of more than 1,000 competitors.[^cite_note-232]

In 2014, Qatar was selected as the host of the [2019 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Athletics_Championships), which is the seventeenth edition of the [IAAF World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_World_Athletics_Championships).[^cite_note-233] Doha won the bid to host the event over [Barcelona](4443.md) and [Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon).[^cite_note-234]

In 2020, Doha hosted the [Qatar ExxonMobil Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Qatar_ExxonMobil_Open), which received the Tournament of the Year award in the 250 category from the 2019 [ATP Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Awards). The tournament won the award for the third time in five years.[^cite_note-235]

Doha will host the [2030 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_Asian_Games).[^cite_note-236]

Doha was chosen as the [Arab Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Capital_of_Culture) in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Arab_Capital_of_Culture).[^cite_note-239] Cultural weeks organized by the Ministry of Culture, which featured both Arab and non-Arab cultures, were held in Doha from April to June to celebrate the city's selection.[^cite_note-240]

### Arts

Main article: [Qatari art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari_art)

Further information: [Public art in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_art_in_Qatar) and [Collecting practices of the Al-Thani Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collecting_practices_of_the_Al-Thani_Family)

The [Museum of Islamic Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Islamic_Art,_Doha) or MIA in Doha, opened in 2008, is regarded as one of the best museums in the region.[^cite_note-241] This, and several other Qatari museums located in the city, like the [Arab Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathaf:_Arab_Museum_of_Modern_Art), falls under the [Qatar Museums Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Museums_Authority) (QMA) which is led by Sheikha [Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al-Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mayassa_bint_Hamad_bin_Khalifa_Al-Thani), the sister of the emir of Qatar.[^cite_note-242]

### Cinema

Main article: [Cinema of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Qatar)

The [Doha Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Film_Institute) (DFI) is an organisation established in 2010 to oversee film initiatives and create a sustainable film industry in Qatar. DFI was founded by [H.E. Sheikha Al Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al-Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mayassa_bint_Hamad_bin_Khalifa_Al-Thani).[^cite_note-243]

The [Doha Tribeca Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Tribeca_Film_Festival) (DTFF), partnered with the American-based [Tribeca Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca_Film_Festival), was held annually in Doha from 2009 to 2012.[^cite_note-244] It emanated from a collaboration between the [Doha Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Film_Institute) and [Tribeca Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca_Enterprises).[^cite_note-245] Celebrities such as [Robert De Niro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Niro),[^cite_note-246] [Salma Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salma_Hayek),[^cite_note-247] [Adel Emam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adel_Emam),[^cite_note-248] [Mira Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mira_Nair),[^cite_note-249] and [Kevin Spacey](16768.md)[^cite_note-250] attended the festivals.[^cite_note-251] The festivals and premieres took place in the [Katara Cultural Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katara_Cultural_Village) annually,[^cite_note-252] as well as the [Museum of Islamic Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Islamic_Art,_Doha) in 2012.[^cite_note-253] The DTFF premiered up to 87 films from all around the world.[^cite_note-254] The major cinema operators in Doha are Vox, NOVO and Flik.[^cite_note-255]

### Theatre

Main article: [Theatre in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_in_Qatar)

The first official theatre troupe in the country was created in 1972 as the "Qatari Theatrical Troupe". It went on to produce its first play the same year.[^cite_note-mofa-264] The next year, a second troupe was founded as the Al Sadd Theatrical Troupe.[^cite_note-tamra-265] By 1986, the first company had been founded with the intent of aiding troupes and actors in producing plays.[^cite_note-mofa-264] Two further troupes were also created during this period: the Lights Theatrical Troupe and Folk Theatrical Troupe. In 1994, the four troupes were amalgamated into two troupes which were named the Qatari Theatrical Troupe and the Doha Theatrical Troupe.[^cite_note-tamra-265] Theatre was introduced to Qatar in the mid-20th century. Theatrical performances are held at [Qatar National Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Theater) and at the [Qatar National Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Convention_Centre) in Doha.
