**Stourbridge** ([/ˈstaʊərbrɪdʒ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) in the [Metropolitan Borough of Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Dudley) in the [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\)), England. Situated on the [River Stour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Stour,_Worcestershire), the town lies around 11 miles (18 kilometres) west of [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), at the southwestern edge of the [West Midlands Conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Conurbation). [Historically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) in [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire), it was the centre of British [glass making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_making) during the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution). The [2021 UK census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_United_Kingdom_censuses) recorded the town's population as 56,950.[^cite_note-1]

## Geography

Stourbridge is about 10 miles (16 kilometres) west of [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham). It is part of the Metropolitan Borough of Dudley at the southwestern edge of the [Black Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Country) and the [West Midlands conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation), Stourbridge includes the villages and suburbs of [Amblecote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amblecote), [Lye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lye,_West_Midlands), [Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norton,_Dudley), [Oldswinford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldswinford), [Pedmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedmore), [Stambermill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stambermill?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Stourton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourton,_Staffordshire), [Wollaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollaston,_West_Midlands), [Wollescote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollescote) and [Wordsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordsley).

Much of Stourbridge consists of residential streets interspersed with green spaces. [Mary Stevens Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Stevens_Park), opened in 1931, has a lake, a bandstand, a cafe, and open spaces. There are two other large parks also gifted to Stourbridge people by the Stevens family, one in Lye/Wollescote and the other in Quarry Bank.

Bordered by [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt) land, Stourbridge is close to countryside with the [Clent Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clent_Hills) to the south and southwest [Staffordshire](58733.md) and [Kinver Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinver_Edge) to the west.

## History

See also: [History of Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Worcestershire) and [Evolution of Worcestershire county boundaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_Worcestershire_county_boundaries)



St. Thomas' Church

Stourbridge was listed in the 1255 [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire) assize roll as Sturbrug or Sturesbridge.[^cite_note-2] The medieval township was named for a bridge which crossed the [River Stour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Stour,_Worcestershire). The settlement was originally known as Bedcote and was likely [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) in origin.[^cite_note-3] It lay within the manor of Swynford or Suineford (now [Oldswinford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldswinford)), which appears in [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror)'s [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book) of 1086.[^cite_note-4]

*Pigot and Co.'s National Commercial Directory for 1828-9* describes Stourbridge as a "populous, wealthy, and flourishing market town" and gives its population in 1821 as 5,090.[^cite_note-Pigot-5]

In 1966, the Stourbridge border between Worcestershire and [Staffordshire](58733.md), which for centuries had been marked by the [River Stour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Stour,_Worcestershire), was moved a couple of miles north when [Amblecote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amblecote) was incorporated into the Borough of Stourbridge. Following the [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), Stourbridge was amalgamated into the [Metropolitan Borough of Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Dudley) and became part of the wider West Midlands county in 1974.

### Glass Making in Stourbridge



[Red House Cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_House_Cone) and Visitor Centre

The town gives its name to local glass production, which has been manufactured since the early 1600s. The local clay proved particularly suitable for the industry, taken up predominantly after the immigration of French coal miners in the [Huguenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) diaspora.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] However, most of the glass industry was actually located in surrounding areas including Wordsley, Amblecote and Oldswinford. The rich natural resources of coal and fireclay for lining furnaces made it the perfect location for the industry. Glass making peaked in the 19th century, encouraged by the famous glass-making family, the Jeavons.[^cite_note-takao-8]

The 1861 census identified that 1,032 residents of Stourbridge were involved in the glass trade in some way. Of these, 541 were glass workers - an increase from 409 in 1851, believed to be partly caused by the collapse of the glass industry in nearby [Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley) in the 1850s.[^cite_note-9] The vast majority of those involved in the glass trade came from [Staffordshire](58733.md), [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire), [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire) and [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire). 9% came from other parts of England and 0.2% had come from abroad. Of particular note are glass cutters, as 8.1% had come from Ireland, believed to be as a result of the decline of the Irish glasscutting industry in the first half of the 1800s. The houses inhabited by glassworkers were of a much better quality in comparison to the slums in which the nailmakers of [Lye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lye,_West_Midlands) and [Wollescote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollescote) lived. However, only a few glassworkers owned their own houses.[^cite_note-takao-8]

The [Red House Cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_House_Cone), thought to be the only complete remaining glass cone of its kind, stands on the Stourbridge Canal at Wordsley. It is the site of the Red House Glass Museum and there are regular demonstrations of traditional glass blowing.

### Modern history

The town centre has seen major regeneration in recent years. In 2014, Lion Health medical centre opened in the renovated former foundry of [Foster, Rastrick and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster,_Rastrick_and_Company) – where the [Stourbridge Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Lion) locomotive was manufactured. The next phase of regeneration on the foundry site will create parkland next to [Stourbridge Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Canal) with a "heritage and community hub" named Riverside House.

Crown Centre Shopping Mall at the bottom of Stourbridge High Street opened in 2013 at the site of the old Crown Centre and Bell Street [multi-storey car park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-storey_car_park), which were demolished between 2012 and 2013. Costing £50m, the new mall is home to a 60,000 sq ft (5,600 m<sup>2</sup>) [Tesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesco) anchor store, a two-level underground car park, six retail stores and a central food court.[^cite_note-10] Stourbridge Bus Station underwent substantial redevelopment and re-opened as [Stourbridge Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Interchange) in April 2012.

In 2010, Stourbridge was awarded [Fairtrade Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairtrade_Town) status. Stourbridge Farmers' and Craft Market takes place on the first and third Saturday of every month in the Clock Square. Throughout the summer, [Mary Stevens Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Stevens_Park) hosts outdoor live music.

In August 2026, Stourbridge was heavily affected by [wildfires](83685479.md#August) with the Stourbridge golf course and local homes being destroyed.[^cite_note-11] [West Midlands Fire Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Fire_Service) said the fires caused "extensive devastation".[^cite_note-12]

## Governance

[Margot James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_James), [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) held the [Stourbridge parliamentary constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) 2010–2019.[^cite_note-14] She was succeeded in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_United_Kingdom_general_election) by [Suzanne Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Webb) of the same party. After the 2024 General Election, [Cat Eccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_Eccles), [Labour](19279158.md), became MP.

## Transport

Three main roads meet in Stourbridge, these being the [A451](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_4_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), the [A458](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A458) and the [A491](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A491), the last forming the one way Stourbridge Ring Road.

Stourbridge has two railway stations, the main one being [Stourbridge Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Junction_railway_station). From here, it is around 30 minutes to Birmingham, 30 minutes to Worcester and between two and 2.5 hours to London. The other station, [Stourbridge Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Town_railway_station), is served only by a shuttle to and from Stourbridge Junction. At just over 1⁄2 mi (800 m), the [Stourbridge Town Branch Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Town_Branch_Line) is believed to be the shortest railway [branch line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_line) in Europe.[^cite_note-15] The former main line to [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton) via [Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley), and branches to [Wombourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wombourne) and [Walsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall) closed in the 1960s. However the line towards Dudley remains open for freight as far as the Round Oak Steel Terminal north of [Brierley Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brierley_Hill). In January 2021, proposals were made to reopen the line to Brierley Hill to passengers using a light rail vehicle similar to that used on the Stourbridge Branch Line.

[Stourbridge Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Interchange) is the main bus station, located in the town centre next to Stourbridge Town railway station. The Interchange opened in 2012 at a cost of £7 million.[^cite_note-16] Most services are operated by [National Express West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Express_West_Midlands) and [Diamond Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_West_Midlands) which offer links to local areas such as [Wollaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollaston,_West_Midlands) and Pedmore, and further destinations like [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton) and [Kidderminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidderminster). [Diamond Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_West_Midlands) operate the tendered service 242 to [Kinver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinver) which was previously operated by The Green Bus Company but more recently Select Bus Services and added additional journeys.

By bike, National Route 54 of the [National Cycle Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cycle_Network) links Stourbridge with Dudley via the canal towpaths.[^cite_note-17]

The [Stourbridge Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Canal) links the town to the [Staffordshire and Worcestershire Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_and_Worcestershire_Canal) and the Dudley No. 1 Canal. This places Stourbridge on the [Stourport Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourport_Ring), navigable by [narrowboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrowboat) and popular with holidaymakers.

## Education



[King Edward VI College, Stourbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_College,_Stourbridge) on Lower High Street

There is one college in Stourbridge. [King Edward VI College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_College,_Stourbridge) was founded in 1552, becoming a sixth form college in 1976.[^cite_note-18] [Stourbridge College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_College), south of the town centre, was formed in 1958 and specialised in art and design, but was closed in 2019.[^cite_note-19]

There is also a [sixth form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form) at [Old Swinford Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swinford_Hospital) school, which was founded in 1667 by the Stourbridge-born politician [Thomas Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Foley_\(1616%E2%80%931677\)).[^cite_note-20] In 2018, the boarding school was named the best secondary school in Dudley, closely followed by [Redhill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redhill_School,_Stourbridge), an academy also in Stourbridge.[^cite_note-21]

[Elmfield Rudolf Steiner School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmfield_Rudolf_Steiner_School) is an independent school which follows the international Steiner Waldorf Education curriculum.[^cite_note-ofsted1-22]

## Culture

### Festival of Glass

The International Festival of Glass is held at Ruskin Mill in Stourbridge every two years. The British Glass Biennale is the festival's flagship exhibition, featuring contemporary work by glass makers in the UK.[^cite_note-23]

### Music

In the late-1980s and early 1990s, three Stourbridge indie bands – [The Wonder Stuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wonder_Stuff), [Pop Will Eat Itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_Will_Eat_Itself) and [Ned's Atomic Dustbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned's_Atomic_Dustbin) – all had chart success, selling millions of albums between them and gracing the covers of *[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME)* and *[Melody Maker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melody_Maker)*.[^cite_note-24] Pop Will Eat Itself's former frontman [Clint Mansell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Mansell) has since composed musical scores for films including *[Black Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Swan_\(film\))* and *[Requiem for a Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_for_a_Dream)*.[^cite_note-Quietus-25]

The 80s metal bands [Diamond Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Head_\(English_band\)), [Witchfinder General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witchfinder_General_\(band\)) and 80s pop band [Kayran Dache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayran_Dache?action=edit\&redlink=1) also came from Stourbridge and [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin)'s [Robert Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Plant) once attended [King Edward VI College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_College,_Stourbridge) (then King Edward VI Grammar School for Boys).

### Media

Stourbridge is covered by these newspapers: the *[Stourbridge News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_News)* (weekly), and the *Stourbridge Chronicle* (weekly).

From the 1860s until the early 1980s, Stourbridge was covered by the *County Express* newspaper. The archives are now on microfilm in Stourbridge Library.

Historical records about Stourbridge are held in multiple archives across the Black Country and Worcestershire, due to Stourbridge historically being a part of Worcestershire.[^cite_note-26]

### Sport

[Stourbridge Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_F.C.), founded in 1876 and nicknamed "The Glassboys", shares the [War Memorial Athletic Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Memorial_Athletic_Ground) in Amblecote with [Stourbridge Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_Cricket_Club). [Stourbridge Rugby Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge_R.F.C.) play at Stourton Park in nearby [Stourton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourton,_Staffordshire). [Dudley and Stourbridge Harriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_and_Stourbridge_Harriers) have trained at the Dell Stadium since 1964. Other teams include Redhill Volleyball Club, which plays at [Redhill School.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redhill_School,_Stourbridge) [Stourbridge Running Club](http://www.srclub.co.uk/index.php/en) also train at the [War Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Memorial_Athletic_Ground) in Amblecote.
