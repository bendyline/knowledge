**Kagoshima Prefecture** (鹿児島県, *Kagoshima-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[ka.ɡo.ɕi.ma, -maꜜ.keɴ, ka.ŋo-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located on the island of [Kyushu](16914.md) and the [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md).[^cite_note-3] Kagoshima Prefecture has a population of 1,527,019 (1 February 2025) and has a geographic area of 9,187 [km<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (3,547 [sq mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile)). Kagoshima Prefecture borders [Kumamoto Prefecture](220713.md) to the north and [Miyazaki Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki_Prefecture) to the northeast.

[Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima) is the capital and largest city of Kagoshima Prefecture, with other major cities including [Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima,_Kagoshima), [Kanoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanoya,_Kagoshima), and [Satsumasendai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsumasendai,_Kagoshima).[^cite_note-4] Kagoshima Prefecture is located at the southernmost point of Kyūshū and includes the [Satsunan Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsunan_Islands) group of the Ryukyu Islands. Kagoshima Prefecture's mainland territory extends from the [Ariake Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariake_Sea) to Shibushi Bay on the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) coast, and is characterized by two large [peninsulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) created by [Kagoshima Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima_Bay). Kagoshima Prefecture formed the core of the [Satsuma Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Domain), ruled from [Kagoshima Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima_Castle), one of the most important [Japanese domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_system) of the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) and the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration).

## History

Main article: [History of Kagoshima Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kagoshima_Prefecture)

See also: [Historic Sites of Kagoshima Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Kagoshima_Prefecture)

Kagoshima Prefecture corresponds to the ancient Japanese provinces [Ōsumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csumi_Province) and [Satsuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Province), including the northern part of the [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md) (*[Satsunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsunan_Islands)*).[^cite_note-5] This region played a key role in the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration) ([Saigō Takamori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saig%C5%8D_Takamori)), and the city of [Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima,_Kagoshima) was an important naval base during Japan's 20th century wars and the home of admiral [Tōgō Heihachirō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dg%C5%8D_Heihachir%C5%8D). More recent incidents are the sinking of a North Korean spy ship (100 ton class) in 2001 by the [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard), which was later salvaged and exhibited in Tokyo, and the [abduction of an office clerk and her boyfriend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean_abductions_of_Japanese_citizens) from a Kagoshima beach in 1978 by agents from the same country. This became known only recently under the [Koizumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junichiro_Koizumi) administration.

## Demographics

The two main ethnic groups of Kagoshima Prefecture are the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people) and the [Ryukyuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuans) (Amami Islands).

## Geography

Kagoshima Prefecture is located at the southwest tip of Kyushu on the [Satsuma Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Peninsula) and [Ōsumi Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csumi_Peninsula). This prefecture also includes a chain of islands stretching further to the southwest of Kyushu for a few hundred kilometers. The most important group is the [Amami Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Islands). Surrounded by the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea) to the west, [Okinawa Prefecture](22477.md) in the south, [Kumamoto Prefecture](220713.md) to the north, and [Miyazaki Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki_Prefecture) to the east, it has 2,632 km (1,635 mi) of coastline (including the 28 islands). It has a bay called [Kagoshima Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima_Bay) (Kinkowan), which is sandwiched by two peninsulas, [Satsuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Peninsula) and [Ōsumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csumi_Peninsula). Its position made it a 'gateway' to Japan at various times in history. While Kyushu has about 13 million people, there are less than 2 million in this prefecture.

The prefecture boasts a chain of active and dormant [volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano), including the great [Sakurajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakurajima), which towers out of the Kagoshima bay opposite [Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima) city. A steady trickle of smoke and ash emerges from the [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera), punctuated by louder mini-eruptions on an almost daily basis. On active days in Kagoshima city an umbrella is advisable to ward off the ash. Sakurajima is one of Japan's most active volcanoes. Major eruptions occurred in 1914, when the island mountain spilled enough material to become permanently connected to the mainland, and a lesser eruption in 1960. Volcanic materials in the soil make Sakurajima a source for record [daikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daikon) radishes, roughly the size of a [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_\(ball\)). Many beaches around the Kagoshima Bay are littered with well-worn pumice stones. A crater lake in the southwestern tip of the prefecture, near the spa town of [Ibusuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibusuki), is home to a rare species of giant eel.

As of 31 March 2019, 13% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely the [Amami Guntō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Gunt%C5%8D_National_Park), [Kirishima-Kinkowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima-Kinkowan_National_Park), [Unzen-Amakusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unzen-Amakusa_National_Park), and [Yakushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakushima_National_Park) National Parks; [Koshikishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshikishima_Quasi-National_Park) and [Nichinan Kaigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichinan_Kaigan_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks; and [Akune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akune_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Bōnoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C5%8Dnoma_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Fukiagehama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukiagehama_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Imutaike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imutaike_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Ōsumi Nanbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csumi_Nanbu_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Sendaigawa Ryūiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendaigawa_Ry%C5%ABiki_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Takakumayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takakumayama_Prefectural_Natural_Park), and [Tokara Rettō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokara_Rett%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park) Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Economy

Most of the economic sector is focused in Kagoshima City and the surrounding area, corresponding to the extent of the former [Satsuma Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Province). The eastern part of the prefecture, the former [Ōsumi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csumi_Province), is mostly rural and shows a general population decline.

The prefecture has strong agricultural roots, which are reflected in its most well-known exports: [green tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_tea), [sweet potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato), [radish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radish), Pongee [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [Satsuma ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_ware), [Berkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_\(pig\)) pork ("kurobuta") and local Black [Wagyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagyu) beef. Kagoshima prefecture's production of [bonito flakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonito_flakes) is second only to that of [Shizuoka](180999.md). In addition, it produces Japan's largest volume of unagi eels. Kagoshima is also the largest [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef)-producing and [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork)-producing prefecture in Japan.

The [Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Aerospace_Exploration_Agency) (JAXA) has several facilities within the prefecture, including the country's [main launch facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanegashima_Space_Center) on [Tanegashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanegashima) and the [Uchinoura Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uchinoura_Space_Center).

The prefecture's [gross domestic product](12594.md) is approximately 5,548.7 trillion yen, with a work force of about 791,000 people (2018).[^cite_note-8]

## Culture

### Food

Kagoshima Prefecture has a distinct and rich food culture. The warm weather and diverse environments allow for the agriculture and aquaculture of Kagoshima to thrive. Numerous restaurants around Kagoshima feature [Satsuma Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Province) local cuisine. Popular cuisine incorporating local agriculture include [sweet potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato), *kibinago sashimi* (silver-striped herring), *buri* amberjack, *kampachi* yellowtail, "Black Label Products" such as *kuro-ushi* Wagyu beef, *kuro-buta* Berkshire pork dishes, and *kuro-Satsuma jidori* chicken (sometimes served as raw, chicken *sashimi*); smoked eel, keihan, and *miki* (fermented rice milk consumed among residents of the [Amami Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Islands)).

#### *Satsuma-age*

*[Satsuma-age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma-age)*, or deep-fried fish cake, comes in great variety in Kagoshima. Though the deep-fried fishcake can be found throughout the country, the Satsuma Domain (modern-day Kagoshima Prefecture) is commonly believed to be the birthplace of the snack. It is said, though, the concept was introduced from the Ryūkyū Kingdom (modern-day Okinawa Prefecture) by Satsuma Lord, Nariakira Shimazu.

#### Sweets

There are many types of sweets produced in Kagoshima Prefecture. Kagoshima-based Seika Foods Co., Ltd. ([Seika shokuhin kabushiki-kaisha](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%BB%E3%82%A4%E3%82%AB%E9%A3%9F%E5%93%81%E6%A0%AA%E5%BC%8F%E4%BC%9A%E7%A4%BE)) has produced some of Japan's most popular and timeless sweets such as [hyōroku mochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Droku_mochi) *Bontan Ame* (ボンタンアメ), *Satsuma Imo* Caramel (さつまいもキャラメル), and green tea-flavored Hyōroku mochi, Minami "[shirokuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirokuma)" shaved ice desserts, etc. Traditional treats outside of Seika Food Co., Ltd. products include *[karukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karukan)* (sweet cakes made from steamed yams and rice flour), [*jambo-mochi*](http://kic-update.com/en/travel/jambo-mochi/), *kokutō* brown sugar from the Amami Islands, *getanha* brown sugar cake, etc.

#### Beverages

In 1559, at Kōriyama Hachiman Shrine (郡山八幡神社) in Isa City (伊佐市) a carpenter wrote atop a wooden board "the Shintō Priest of this shrine is too stingy to offer me *[Shōchū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dch%C5%AB)* (焼酎) showing an early love for the spirits. Kagoshima Prefecture is officially recognized (by the World Trade Organization) as the home to one of the most traditional beverages of Japan, *shōchū*. In Kagoshima there are 113+ distilleries, producing about 1,500 highly acclaimed brands, placing Kagoshima in the top for production quantity and shipment. While visiting Kagoshima, one may notice labels reading *Honkaku-shōchū* (本格焼酎). *Honkaku-shōchū* is a distilled beverage produced with traditional skills using ingredients such as natural spring water, sweet potatoes, locally grown sugar cane, and grains. Varieties of *honkaku-shōchū* include *Imo-jōchū* (芋焼酎), *shōchū* distilled from sweet potatoes, *Mugi-jōchū* (麦焼酎), distilled from barley, and *Kome-jōchū* (米焼酎) distilled from rice. Another type of *shōchū* is *Kokutō-shōchū* (黒糖焼酎), *shōchū* distilled with brown sugar). *Shōchū* has long gained international favor and has come to be comparable to Bordeaux for wine, Scotch for whiskey, and Cognac for brandy. Also, the Amami Islands of Kagoshima Prefecture are the only areas sanctioned to bear the label of *kokutō-shōchū*.

*Kurozu* (黒酢) or black vinegar is another item of the "Black Label Products" of Kagoshima, the other of which is *Kokutō-shōchū*. There are a number of *kurozu* farms around Kagoshima, most of which are located around the [Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima,_Kagoshima) area. Most *kurozu* farms produce *kurozu* bottled vinegar (fermented for a single year, 2+ years) along with other items such as salad dressing, powders, capsules, spices, candy, etc. *Kurozu* also comes in different flavors such as grape, orange, *ume plum*, etc. though the most popular flavor by far is apple. The farms are open to visitations and often offer tours.

### Dialect

See also: [Amami Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Japanese)

Today, Kagoshima is home to a distinctive dialect of Japanese known as Satsugū dialect (薩隅方言, *Satsugū Hōgen*) or [Kagoshima dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima_dialect) (鹿児島弁, *Kagoshima-ben*; or *Kagomma-ben*), differing from the usual [Kyushu](16914.md) dialects with its pronunciations of the [yotsugana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yotsugana).

For the most part, Satsugū dialect is mutually unintelligible with [Standard Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Japanese),[^cite_note-9] though most Satsugū speakers know both as a result of language standardization in Japan.
