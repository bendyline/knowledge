**Sergey Viktorovich Lavrov**[^cite_note-1] ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Сергей Викторович Лавров, IPA: [\[sʲɪrˈɡʲej ˈvʲiktərəvʲɪtɕ lɐˈvrof\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian); born 21 March 1950) is a Russian diplomat who has served as [Minister of Foreign Affairs](1316703.md) since 2004. He is the country's longest-serving foreign minister since the Tsarist era.[^cite_note-Official-bio-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

After graduating from the [Moscow State Institute of International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Institute_of_International_Relations) (MGIMO) in 1972, Lavrov began his diplomatic career in [Sri Lanka](26750.md) and speaks fluent [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language), [Dhivehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_language), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), in addition to his native [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language). From 1981 to 1988 he held several posts in the [Soviet Permanent Mission to the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Mission_of_Russia_to_the_United_Nations) in New York City. Starting in the late 1980s he was deputy director and then director of the [Foreign Ministry](4432213.md)'s Department of International Organizations before becoming a Deputy Minister of Foreign Affairs in 1992. He served as Russia's Permanent Representative to the [United Nations](31769.md) from 1994 to 2004, where he gained a reputation for assertively defending Russian interests during crises, including the [Kosovo War](16760.md) and the [Iraq invasion](201936.md).[^cite_note-Official-bio-2][^cite_note-5] Throughout his tenure, Lavrov has remained a central figure in shaping [Russia's foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Russia) under President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md).

## Early life and education

Lavrov was born on 21 March 1950[^cite_note-dub2012jun-6] in [Tbilisi](42298.md) ([Georgian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)), or in Moscow.[^cite_note-13] His father, Viktor Gabrielovich Kalantarov, who descended from a branch of the [medieval Armenian princely family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melik) of the Kalantaryans that became [Russified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) as Kalantarovs on [settling in Tbilisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Tbilisi) at the end of the 19th century, worked in [foreign trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_trade_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-KM-10][^cite_note-RP-12][^cite_note-14] His mother, Kaleriya Borisovna,[^cite_note-15] came from a [station master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_master)'s family in [Noginsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noginsk), [Moscow Oblast](372315.md), [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Socialist_Federative_Soviet_Republic), and similarly held a position at the Soviet [Ministry of Foreign Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Trade_\(Soviet_Union\)).[^cite_note-MD-9][^cite_note-AM-8] With his parents frequently away on [secondments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondment), or due to family reasons, Lavrov was raised from an early age in Noginsk by his maternal grandparents.[^cite_note-KM-10][^cite_note-MD-9][^cite_note-RP-12] He followed a curriculum with extended English first at the [V. Korolenko School No. 2 in Noginsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._Korolenko_School_No._2_in_Noginsk?action=edit\&redlink=1) and then at the [Moscow High School No. 607](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_High_School_No._607?action=edit\&redlink=1), from which he graduated with a [silver medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_medal_\(Russia\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-KM-10][^cite_note-MD-9][^cite_note-RP-12] Since his favourite class was physics, he considered applying to the [National Research Nuclear University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Engineering_Physics_Institute_\(National_Research_Nuclear_University\)) or the [Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology), but eventually graduated from the [Moscow State Institute of International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Institute_of_International_Relations) (MGIMO) in 1972.[^cite_note-dub2012jun-6]

During his education at the MGIMO, Lavrov studied [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations). Soon he learned [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language), then the only official language of [Sri Lanka](26750.md), as well as [Dhivehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhivehi_language), the official language of the [Maldives](19117.md). He also learned [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language). After he was admitted to the university, Lavrov, along with other students, was sent for a month to a [student construction brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_construction_brigade) building the [Ostankino Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostankino_Tower).[^cite_note-16]

During his summer vacations, Lavrov also worked in his university's student construction brigades in [Khakassia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khakassia), [Tuva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuva) and the [Russian Far East](173135.md). Each semester, Lavrov with his fellow students conducted drama performances, which were later presented on the main stage of the university. During the third year of his studies, Lavrov was married.[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

## Career

### Soviet diplomat in Sri Lanka (1972–1975)

Lavrov graduated in 1972. According to the rules of that time, a graduate of the [Moscow State Institute of International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Institute_of_International_Relations) had to work for the Foreign Ministry for a certain amount of time. Lavrov was employed in the Soviet embassy in [Sri Lanka](26750.md) as an advisor, as he was already a specialist on the country. At the time, the Soviet Union and Sri Lanka had close market and economic cooperation and the Soviet Union launched the production of [natural rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_rubber) in the country.[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

The Soviet embassy in Sri Lanka also maintained relations with the Maldives. The embassy in Sri Lanka employed only 24 diplomats. Lavrov was given the task of continuously analysing the situation in the country, but he also worked as a translator, personal secretary and assistant to [Rafiq Nishonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafiq_Nishonov), who would later become the [12th First Secretary of the Communist Party of Uzbek SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Uzbekistan). In addition, he gained the diplomatic rank of an [attaché](1653163.md).[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

### USSR Section for International Economic Relations and the UN

In 1976, Lavrov returned to Moscow. He worked as a third and second secretary in the Section for International Economic Relations of the USSR. There, he was involved in analytics and his office also worked with various international organizations, including the [United Nations](31769.md).

In 1981, he was sent as a senior adviser to the Soviet mission to the United Nations in [New York City](645042.md).

In 1988, Lavrov returned to Moscow and was named Deputy Chief of the Section for International Economic Relations of the USSR. Between 1990 and 1992 he worked as Director of the International Organization of the Soviet Foreign Ministry.[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

### Soviet-to-CIS transition (1992–1994)

In October 1991, [Andrey Kozyrev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kozyrev), who was in charge of monitoring international organizations at the time, was named Foreign Minister of the [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) (Russian SFSR). In that year, the powers of the Soviet Foreign Ministry and the Foreign Ministry of the Russian SFSR were distributed. Until then the Russian SFSR had only a ceremonial role. In October 1992, the foreign ministers of all Soviet republics, except [Georgia](48768.md) and the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), held a meeting where they dealt with the Union of Foreign Ministries.[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

In November 1991, the State Council decided to change its name from the Union of Foreign Ministries to the Foreign Ministry of the Soviet Union. In April 1992, he was named deputy foreign minister.[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

In December 1991, the Foreign Ministry of Soviet Russia became the Foreign Ministry of the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Russia_\(1991%E2%80%93present\)).

In 1992, Lavrov was named director of the Department for International Organizations and Global Issues in the Foreign Ministry of the Russian Federation.[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

Lavrov was asked to oversee the activities of the Human Rights and International Cultural Cooperation and the two departments – for the [CIS](36870.md) countries, international organizations and international economic cooperation.[^cite_note-Lenta-17]

Lavrov was promoted to the diplomatic rank of the Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary — the highest diplomatic rank in the Russian Federation — by the Decree of the then [President of Russia](239646.md), [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin), on 5 June 1992 No. 568.[^cite_note-18]

### Russian Permanent Representative to the UN (1994–2004)

Lavrov worked for the Ministry of Foreign Affairs until 1994 when he returned to work in the [United Nations](31769.md), this time as the Permanent Representative of Russia. While in the latter position, he was the President of the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) in December 1995, June 1997, July 1998, October 1999,[^cite_note-19] December 2000, April 2002, and June 2003.[^cite_note-20]

On 9 March 2004, President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) appointed Lavrov to the post of minister of foreign affairs.[^cite_note-dub2012jun-6] He succeeded [Igor Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Ivanov) in the post.

Lavrov held on to his position through [Vladimir Putin's Second Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin's_Second_Cabinet) while [Dmitri Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Medvedev) occupied the presidency from 2008 to 2012.

On 21 May 2012, Lavrov was reappointed foreign minister to [the cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev's_First_Cabinet) led by prime minister [Dimitri Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitri_Medvedev).[^cite_note-dub2012jun-6]

Lavrov is regarded as continuing in the style of his predecessor: a brilliant diplomat but a civil servant rather than a politician. A [Russian foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Russia) expert at London's [Chatham House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_House) has described him as "a tough, reliable, extremely sophisticated negotiator" but adds that "he's not part of [Putin's inner sanctum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putin's_inner_sanctum)" and that the toughening of Russian foreign policy has got very little to do with him.[^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-21]

US politicians have been much more critical in their appraisal of Lavrov, seeing him as emblematic of President Putin's resurgent violent foreign policies. Then US [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_the_United_States) [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) found that Lavrov treated her poorly during negotiations, like a "jerk."[^cite_note-22] Several [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_administration) officials interviewed for a profile on Lavrov described him as an “anti-diplomat” with “nothing redeemable” about his “uncharismatic, offensive, uncompromising, cruel, unlikeable, brusque, caddish” character.[^cite_note-23]

On 15 January 2020, he resigned as part of the cabinet, after President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) delivered the [Presidential Address to the Federal Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Address_to_the_Federal_Assembly), in which he proposed several amendments to the constitution.[^cite_note-24] On 21 January 2020, he maintained his position in [Mikhail Mishustin's Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Mishustin's_First_Cabinet).[^cite_note-25]

#### Civil war in Syria

In 2012, in the early stages of the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md), a Russian delegation travelled to Syria to affirm Russia's backing of the Syrian government of the President [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md). Lavrov and [Mikhail Fradkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Fradkov), who were part of the delegation, were given a favourable welcome by thousands of pro-Assad supporters. The supporters waved Russian flags in thanks to Russia's veto of a UN resolution calling for tough sanctions on the Syrian government.[^cite_note-26]

In September 2013, then Secretary of State [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) and Lavrov reached a breakthrough agreement that would destroy almost all [chemical weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_weapons) stored in Assad's Syria. The deal was reached after three challenging rounds of talks in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Switzerland](26748.md). Soon after, Syria fully accepted this plan, and by June 2014 all chemical weapons submitted by the Syrian government were safely incinerated in the Eastern Mediterranean. The director general of the [Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons](36832.md) at the [United Nations](31769.md) declared that this treaty was a major benchmark.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In October 2019, Lavrov condemned [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s decision to send American troops to guard [Syria's oil fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Syria) and possibly exploit them, saying that any "exploitation of natural resources of a sovereign state without its consent is illegal".[^cite_note-29]

Amid the [2024 Syrian opposition offensives](78460726.md) and subsequent [fall of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Damascus_\(2024\)), Lavrov was reported to have led efforts to facilitate the [departure](78548066.md) of president Assad by negotiating with Turkey and Qatar to persuade [Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham](53015755.md) to let Assad leave Syria.[^cite_note-30]

Following the [annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), on 30 March 2014 Lavrov proposed that Ukraine should be independent of any bloc, that the Russian language be recognized officially, and that the constitution be organized along federal lines.[^cite_note-31] In an interview with the [Russia-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia-24) TV channel, Lavrov said that the [zero-sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-sum) "either-or" bloc-politics of Ukraine were first suggested in 2004 by [Karel De Gucht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_De_Gucht), then Foreign Minister of Belgium.[^cite_note-32]

When [G8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Eight) leaders voted to officially suspend Russia's membership on 24 March, Lavrov stated that the G8 was an informal organization and membership was optional for Russia.[^cite_note-CNN.com-33]

In a 30 March interview, he spoke of the [21 February agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_settlement_of_political_crisis_in_Ukraine) which was signed by [Viktor Yanukovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovich), [Vitaly Klitchko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Klitchko), [Arseniy Yatsenyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arseniy_Yatsenyuk), and [Oleg Tyagnibok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Tyagnibok) as well as the Foreign Ministers of Poland, France and Germany to promote peaceful changes in Ukrainian power. Lavrov stressed federalism as a solution to the constitutional impasse in Ukraine, and deplored the [de-officialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of the Russian language. He noticed the work of the secretariat of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) at the [Venice Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Commission) to prevent a legitimation of the Crimean referendum, and to expel Russia. Lavrov was "taken aback" when US President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) called Russia a "regional power". He deplored the misuse of the [Schengen Agreement](179592.md) to force Crimeans to visit [Kyiv](585629.md) in order to gain a Schengen [visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_\(document\)), and noticed that the EU proposes a visa-free regime for Ukrainian citizens. Lavrov stated that the [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) in Kyiv and the results of the Crimean referendum should both be accepted equally by the West. He reiterated the three-part Russian proposal for the progress of Ukraine:

1. Constitutional federalism

2. Recognition of linguistic minorities

3. That Ukraine be a non-aligned state

The Kyiv government on 30 March denounced Lavrov's proposals as amounting to "the complete capitulation of Ukraine, its dismemberment, and the destruction of Ukrainian statehood.[^cite_note-34]

While Lavrov acknowledged that Russia is in contact with the [Ukrainian separatist rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas) he denied US and EU allegations that Moscow sponsored the rebellion and accused the United States of aggravating the conflict. "Our American colleagues still prefer to push the Ukrainian leadership toward a confrontational path." He added that chances for settling the Russo-Ukrainian war would have been higher if it only depended on Russia and Europe.[^cite_note-35] Lavrov said the separatists want to "defend their culture, their traditions, celebrate their holidays rather than anniversaries of [Roman Shukhevych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Shukhevych) and [Stepan Bandera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Bandera)."[^cite_note-36]

In June 2016, Lavrov stated that Russia will never attack any [NATO](21133.md) country, saying: "I am convinced that all serious and honest politicians know perfectly well than Russia will never attack a member state of NATO. We have no such plans."[^cite_note-newsweek-37] He also said: "In our security doctrine it is clearly stated that one of the main threats to our safety is the further expansion of NATO to the east."[^cite_note-newsweek-37]

#### 2017 North Korea crisis

Lavrov likened the war of words between US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and North Korean leader [Kim Jong-un](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong-un) to a kindergarten fight between two children, saying "Together with China we'll continue to strive for a reasonable approach and not an emotional one like when children in a kindergarten start fighting and no-one can stop them."[^cite_note-38]

Lavrov also said that the United States would not carry out a strike on North Korea because "they know for sure – rather than suspect – that it has atomic bombs." He said the [US invaded Iraq](201936.md) "solely because they had 100 percent information that there were no weapons of mass destruction left there."[^cite_note-39]

#### US sanctions

Lavrov criticized [US sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_sanctions) against countries like [Iran](14653.md), [Turkey](11125639.md) and Russia. In August 2018, Lavrov said, "unilateral enforcement measures are illegitimate in international affairs" \[...]. "One way to counter these illegitimate barriers and restrictions is we can use national currencies on our bilateral trade". "I strongly believe that abuse of the role the U.S. dollar plays as an international currency will eventually result in its role being undermined".[^cite_note-40]

Businesses involved in [Nord Stream 2](50335419.md) natural gas pipeline from Russia to [Germany](11867.md) have been sanctioned by the United States with the passing of the [National Defense Authorization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act) for Fiscal Year 2020 on 20 December 2019.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Lavrov said that [US Congress](31756.md) "is literally overwhelmed with the desire to do everything to destroy" the [Russia–United States relations](4482509.md).[^cite_note-43]

#### Ukraine's education law

Lavrov condemned [Ukraine](31750.md)'s 2017 [education law](5231751.md), which makes [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) the [only language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_policy_in_Ukraine) of education in state schools. According to Lavrov, the "reaction of Brussels to the Ukrainian Law on Education is utterly vague although it crudely violates Kyiv's commitments on linguistic and educational rights."[^cite_note-osce-44] Russia's Foreign Ministry stated that the law is designed to "forcefully establish a mono-ethnic language regime in a multinational state."[^cite_note-45]

#### Non-citizens in Latvia and Estonia

As early as 2011 Lavrov criticized the status of "[non-citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-citizens_\(Latvia\))" in [Latvia](17514.md) and [Estonia](28222445.md), calling the problem of Russian speaking stateless persons "shameful for the EU."[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-osce-44][^cite_note-47]

#### NATO's Defender-Europe 2021

In 2021, Lavrov was critical of a massive [NATO](21133.md)-led military exercise called Defender-Europe 21,[^cite_note-Defender-Europe_21-48] one of the largest [NATO-led military exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NATO_exercises) in Europe in decades, which began in March 2021. It included "nearly simultaneous operations across more than 30 training areas" in [Estonia](28222445.md), [Bulgaria](3415.md), [Romania](25445.md) and other countries.[^cite_note-Army_Times-49][^cite_note-50] He said that Russia's response was inevitable.[^cite_note-Defender-Europe_21-48]

##### 2022

In January 2022, the United States accused Russia of sending [saboteurs](29462.md) into Ukraine to stage "a false-flag operation" that would create a pretext for Russia to invade Ukraine. Lavrov dismissed the US claim as "total disinformation".[^cite_note-51] On 4 February 2022, Lavrov dismissed as "nonsense" and "craziness" allegations by the United States that Russia was preparing a fake video of the Ukrainian forces attacking the separatist-held [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) as a pretext for starting a war in Ukraine.[^cite_note-52] On 10 February 2022, Lavrov met with British Foreign Secretary [Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Truss). In the context of tensions between Russia and the West over a build up of Russian troops near the [Russia–Ukraine border](36430484.md) talks between the two foreign ministers were described as "difficult". Lavrov denied that Russia has any plans to invade Ukraine.[^cite_note-53] He described Western "demands to remove Russian troops from Russian territory" as "regrettable".[^cite_note-54]

In August 2022, 'Foreign Policy' reported Western diplomats stating that Lavrov has no real influence on Russia's foreign policy and has become a mere [propagandist](45060755.md) and mouthpiece for Putin.[^cite_note-55] Regarding Russia's invasion of Ukraine, foreign policy expert [Angela Stent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Stent) said: "As far as we know, Lavrov himself only knew \[the invasion] was happening as it was taking place."[^cite_note-56] According to sources close to the Kremlin, Lavrov was informed of Putin's plan to attack Ukraine at short notice before the invasion.[^cite_note-57]

On 25 February 2022, the day after Russia began an [invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Lavrov claimed that Putin ordered the invasion to "free Ukrainians from oppression".[^cite_note-58] The same day, the US, UK, EU and Canada announced sanctions against Lavrov as well as Putin.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] The US added Lavrov to the [Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Designated_Nationals_and_Blocked_Persons_List).[^cite_note-61] On 26 February, Australia announced similar sanctions on Lavrov.[^cite_note-62] On 1 March most diplomats at the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) (UNHRC) in Geneva staged a walkout in protest at the Russian invasion of Ukraine as Lavrov began to speak to the assembly via video from Moscow.[^cite_note-ytwo-63] Lavrov criticized the West on some policies relating to Ukraine, denouncing his prevention on flying to Geneva due to the ban on Russian aircraft on EU airspace as "Trying to avoid a candid face-to-face dialogue or direct contacts designed to help identify political solutions to pressing international issues." He was also quoted "The West clearly has lost self-control in venting anger against Russia and has destroyed its own rules and institutions."[^cite_note-64] Lavrov, who read from a prepared text,[^cite_note-ytwo-63] repeated [Putin's 23 February goal statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_conducting_a_special_military_operation): "The goal of our actions is to save people by fulfilling our allied obligations, as well as to [demilitarize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demilitarize) and [denazify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denazify) Ukraine so that such things never happen again."[^cite_note-denata-65]

On 25 February 2022, Lavrov claimed that "available statistics confirm" that "no strikes are being made on civilian infrastructure."[^cite_note-66]

On 2 March 2022, Lavrov explained in an interview with *[Al Jazeera](30204052.md)*, Moscow, how the invasion of Ukraine came about in the context of an international crisis that already existed well before 2014. According to him, Russia had to annexe the Crimea in 2014 due to the unacceptable risk that NATO naval bases would replace the Russian military port there. He primarily contests the legitimacy of the putsch against [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych), who according to the Russian leadership already initiated "peace" in Ukraine, with respect to all Russian speaking minorities. He accuses the West of not supporting the special status of those minorities, before Yanukovych was deposed by the [Orange Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Revolution) in 2004–2005. He continued that Zelenskyy did not improve the situation any further, and that Putin had to order the invasion of Ukraine, because the US did not comply or even address the security concerns of Russia's western flank. Lavrov claimed the US exerted similar pressures on Iraq in 2003, which the US invaded later for no reason other than "a vial of unidentified chemicals". At the same time, Lavrov tries to portray the current Ukrainian government as "nationalistic" and "right wing" because it does not incorporate historical and linguistic ties to Russia into national policies, and only excels to separate itself from a shared history and culture.[^cite_note-67]

Putin, Lavrov and Kazakh President [Kassym-Jomart Tokayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassym-Jomart_Tokayev) in Astana, Kazakhstan, 9 November 2023

He urged the involved parties to resume negotiations and avoid military actions resulting in [civilian casualties or damage to infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_war_crimes#2026_Iran_war).[^cite_note-145] Critics pointed out the irony of the remarks, which mirrored early failed Russian expectations regarding the invasion of Ukraine, as Lavrov maintained that military actions against Tehran would meet a firm response.[^cite_note-146]

## Personal life

Lavrov speaks [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [Dhivehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhivehi_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language).[^cite_note-147]

Lavrov is a keen sportsman.[^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-21] He likes to watch [football](10568.md) games on television[^cite_note-148] and is an ardent fan of the Moscow club [Spartak Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Spartak_Moscow).[^cite_note-149] He has been married since 1971 to Maria Lavrova and they have one daughter and two grandchildren.[^cite_note-150] Their daughter Ekaterina Sergeyevna Lavrova, who lived in the US and London while her father was working for the United Nations, is a graduate of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). Having stayed in [New York City](645042.md) until 2014, and spent a long time outside Russia, she is not fluent in Russian.[^cite_note-151] She is married to Russian businessman [Alexander Vinokurov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Vinokurov_\(businessman\)).[^cite_note-Russconnect-152]

Lavrov has allegedly had a relationship with his [mistress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistress_\(lover\)), Svetlana Polyakova, since the early 2000s.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-\:1-154] In 2016, her daughter Polina Kovaleva purchased an apartment in London's elite district of [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington) for £4.4 million in cash when she was 21 years old.[^cite_note-\:1-154][^cite_note-\:0-155][^cite_note-156] She has a master's degree from [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London).[^cite_note-\:0-155] On 25 March 2022, the British government sanctioned her over allegations of "dirty money" as a part of a broader sanctions regime against corrupt Russian interests following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) in late February.[^cite_note-ft20220325-157][^cite_note-msn20220325-158]

## Sanctions

In February 2022, the [European Union](9317.md), [Switzerland](26748.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), the [United States](3434750.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Japan](15573.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md) and [Australia](4689264.md) placed Lavrov under personal sanctions for his role in the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] In March 2022, the UK sanctioned Lavrov's stepdaughter, Polina Kovaleva.[^cite_note-161] In April 2022, Canada imposed sanctions against Lavrov's wife and daughter, Maria Lavrova and Ekaterina Vinokurova.[^cite_note-162] The latter was soon included in Australia's sanction list as well.[^cite_note-163]

### Rescinded honorary doctorate

Lavrov was given an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) by the [University of Tromsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Troms%C3%B8) in [Norway](21241.md) in 2011 for his role in peacefully negotiating a maritime delimitation line between Norwegian and Russian sector of the Barents sea.[^cite_note-164] In 2022 due to his involvement in the invasion of Ukraine and related violations of international law, the degree was revoked. Lavrov is the only person to have had an honorary doctoral degree rescinded in Norway.[^cite_note-khrono-165]
