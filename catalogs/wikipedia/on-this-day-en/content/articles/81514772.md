**UPS Airlines Flight 2976** was a scheduled domestic [cargo flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_cargo) in the United States from [Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport](264229.md) in [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), to [Honolulu](13887.md), Hawaii. On November 4, 2025, the [McDonnell Douglas MD-11](382371.md) operating the flight separated from its left engine during its takeoff roll and crashed into an industrial area seconds after liftoff from the runway, at about 5\:13 p.m. local time (22\:13 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time)). The crash killed all three crew members on board the aircraft, and twelve people on the ground, one of whom survived until December 25.

The accident is being investigated by the [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board). Following the accident, [UPS](9704675.md) retired its remaining MD-11 fleet in January 2026.

## Background

### Airline

[UPS Airlines](9704675.md) is the United States' second-largest cargo airline (after [FedEx Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx_Express)).[^cite_note-2] Founded in 1988, the company had suffered only two fatal accidents before Flight 2976: [UPS Airlines Flight 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPS_Airlines_Flight_6) in 2010 and [UPS Airlines Flight 1354](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPS_Airlines_Flight_1354) in 2013, each of which killed two people (two crew members on board in each case).[^cite_note-Courier_Journal-3][^cite_note-LEX_18_News-4]

Flight 2976 was a scheduled domestic cargo flight, operated by UPS Airlines from its hub in [Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport](264229.md) in [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), to [Daniel K. Inouye International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_K._Inouye_International_Airport) in [Honolulu](13887.md), Hawaii, an 8.5-hour flight.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

### Aircraft

The aircraft, N259UP, was a 34-year-old [McDonnell Douglas MD-11F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_MD-11F), first delivered to [Thai Airways International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Airways_International) in 1991 with the registration HS-TME,[^cite_note-White-7] after which it was converted to a cargo aircraft and delivered to UPS Airlines in 2006.[^cite_note-8] It had flown 21,043 cycles and for about 92,992 hours,[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9] and was equipped with three [General Electric CF6-80C2D1F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_CF6) turbofan engines.[^cite_note-Flightradar24_Blog-10] The last visual inspections of the left [pylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_pylon) aft mount were performed in October 2021. More rigorous "Special Detailed Inspections" for the mount lugs and wing clevis were not yet due, as the aircraft's 21,043 accumulated cycles were well below the 28,000 and 29,200 cycle thresholds required for those checks. NTSB revealed in May 2026 that Boeing asked the FAA to increase the inspection interval for the lugs from 19,900 to 29,200 flight cycles even though it had received five reports of the spherical bearing, which is housed within the lugs, fracturing long before the aircraft hit its designated inspection interval.[^cite_note-SeattleTimes20May2026-11] Two months before the crash, it had been grounded for six weeks to repair a cracked fuel tank, and corrosion was later found along two structural beams in the fuselage. The aircraft re-entered service a few weeks before the crash.[^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-12]

### Crew

The crew consisted of [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_in_command) Richard Wartenberg (57), a retired [U.S. Air Force](32090.md) [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel) who served with the [445th Airlift Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/445th_Airlift_Wing),[^cite_note-13] and was the [pilot monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_monitoring). He had accumulated about 8,613 total flight hours, including 4,918 in the MD-11. [First officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_officer_\(aviation\)) Lee Truitt (45) was the [pilot flying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_flying) and had accumulated about 9,200 total hours, including 994 in the MD-11. [Relief officer](70939494.md) Dana Diamond (62) had accumulated about 15,250 total flight hours, including 8,775 in the MD-11.[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9][^cite_note-ups.com_Statement-14][^cite_note-Graziosi-15]

## Accident

The aircraft took off from runway 17R, heading south, at about 5\:13 p.m. EST (UTC−5). Weather at the time was clear,[^cite_note-CNN-Mascarenhas-16] with wind at 6 knots (11 km/h; 7 mph) from the southeast with visibility of 10 miles (16 km).[^cite_note-White-7] According to initial flight tracking data, the aircraft reached an altitude of 175 feet (53 m)[^cite_note-CBS-17] and a maximum ground speed of 186 knots (344 km/h; 214 mph).[^cite_note-Flightradar24_Blog-10]



Still images showing the left engine separating from the wing of the aircraft, from surveillance footage obtained by the NTSB

During [takeoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeoff), shortly after [rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_\(aeronautics\)), the left engine, with most of its [pylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint) still attached, separated from the wing and flipped backwards over the wing's leading edge.[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9][^cite_note-18] As it detached, a fire ignited on the engine, and it tumbled up and over the aircraft, coming to rest on the grass on the right side of runway 17R. A fire also broke out near the left pylon attachment point and continued to burn until impact.[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9] The aircraft subsequently entered a descent.[^cite_note-Horton-19][^cite_note-CNN-Live-Engine-20]

The jet's left main landing gear struck the roof of a UPS Supply Chain warehouse,[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9] leaving a 300-foot (90 m) gash.[^cite_note-CNN-Mascarenhas-16] Its left wing then hit a stand of fuel tanks at the Kentucky Petroleum Recycling company's depot,[^cite_note-CNN-Mascarenhas-16] causing a fire to erupt and the aircraft to [roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_principal_axes) more than 90 degrees to the left. Finally, the airplane crashed into a [semi-truck](29381.md) parking area and an auto scrap yard, Grade A Auto Parts.[^cite_note-21] The debris field stretched approximately 3,000 feet (900 m) south-southeast from the UPS warehouse.[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9] The flight was not carrying hazardous cargo.[^cite_note-YangClayton2025-22]

Video analysis indicates that the tail-mounted engine also experienced a malfunction, with bursts of flame seen from the exhaust. Aviation experts have suggested that debris from the detached engine may have been ingested into the tail engine, inducing a [compressor stall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressor_stall) and thus causing the engine to surge,[^cite_note-Horton-19] but this is yet to be confirmed by the NTSB.[^cite_note-23]

Multiple buildings were set on fire or destroyed, with reports of people trapped inside.[^cite_note-CNN-Live-Trapped-24][^cite_note-25]

## Victims

The crash was the deadliest in UPS Airlines history, killing fifteen people: all three crew members on board the aircraft and another twelve on the ground.[^cite_note-Courier_Journal-3][^cite_note-LEX_18_News-4][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Swinney-27] The dead included a three-year-old child and her grandfather.[^cite_note-Graziosi-15] As of November 20, 2025, twenty-three people were known to have suffered injuries, two of which were described as "serious" and twenty-one as "minor".[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9] On December 25, 2025, Mayor Greenberg announced that a man who sustained severe injuries had died, raising the death toll to fifteen.[^cite_note-Swinney-27]

### Timeline of identification

Two employees of the auto yard were initially reported as unaccounted for, and it was unknown how many customers were on the premises at the time.[^cite_note-28] Fifteen families initially reported missing relatives. On November 6, Louisville Mayor [Craig Greenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Greenberg) reported that six of the missing had been accounted for, leaving nine still missing.[^cite_note-29] Two days later, Greenberg said that the missing people were believed to have been found.[^cite_note-30]

The day after the crash, a spokesperson reported that fifteen people had been treated at [UofL Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UofL_Health) facilities, all but two of whom had been discharged.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-CNN-Live-UofL-32]

## Aftermath



Smoke plume viewed from the ground

All flights to and from the airport were immediately canceled.[^cite_note-YangClayton2025-22] Louisville-Jefferson County Emergency Management issued a [shelter-in-place order](20852570.md) centered on the airport with a radius of 5 miles (8 km). This was later reduced to 1 mi (1,600 m). [Wireless Emergency Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_Emergency_Alerts) messages were issued to those in the shelter-in-place area. At the last briefing of the day, Mayor Greenberg said that more than 100 firefighters were at the scene. Local police officials said families should not seek loved ones at hospitals, to avoid overwhelming them, but to visit a reunification center at the police training academy.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-Louisville_Public_Media-34] UPS temporarily suspended operations at its [Worldport air hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldPort_\(UPS_air_hub\)).[^cite_note-35] The fire was nearly contained by 10\:30 p.m., freeing up first responders to search for victims.[^cite_note-36] All public schools in the [Jefferson County School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_County_Public_Schools_\(Kentucky\)) were closed the day after the crash.[^cite_note-CBS-17]

The radius of the shelter-in-place order was reduced a second time, effective at 7\:30 a.m. on November 5, centered near 7501 Grade Lane, to 1⁄4 mi (400 m).[^cite_note-Louisville_Public_Media-34] The [Louisville Metro Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville_Metro_Government) set up a website for people to report and document debris, which they were urged not to touch, and numerous departures were delayed or canceled.[^cite_note-Louisville_Public_Media-34] At 7\:40 a.m., Louisville International Airport announced that Runway 11/29 was open.[^cite_note-37] UPS resumed operations from Louisville later that day.[^cite_note-38] The incident runway, 17R/35L, was reopened around 4\:45 p.m. [EST](496028.md) on November 6, returning the airfield to full operational status.[^cite_note-39]

On November 8, UPS, [FedEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx_Express), and [Western Global Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Global_Airlines) temporarily grounded their respective MD-11 fleets "out of an abundance of caution" following instructions from their manufacturer, [Boeing](18933266.md) (inherited from [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas)).[^cite_note-40] At the time of the incident, UPS operated 26 MD-11s, a small fraction of its fleet of over 500 aircraft, while FedEx had 28 MD-11s in a fleet of more than 700.[^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-12][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] The [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) issued an [emergency airworthiness directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_airworthiness_directive) (EAD) for MD-11 aircraft requiring inspections and any applicable corrective actions before flight.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] On November 14, the FAA issued an EAD grounding the [DC-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-10), the MD-11's predecessor aircraft, for the same reason.[^cite_note-45]

The temporary grounding of the MD-11 fleet disrupted overnight cargo operations at several major U.S. airports, including Memphis, Dallas–Fort Worth, and Ontario, California, causing short-term delays in supply chains reliant on express freight. The incident occurred during the [2025 federal government shutdown](81225965.md), which strained staffing at aviation agencies and led regulators to impose traffic reductions and operational limits. Observers stated that these conditions complicated logistics and could slow non-urgent investigatory and regulatory work. Industry and government sources urged expedited inspections to minimize economic losses.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

On January 27, UPS announced the complete retirement of the MD-11 from its fleet and recorded a charge of \$137 million.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] FedEx retired five MD-11 freighters in its fiscal year ending May 31, 2026.[^cite_note-51]

On May 11, the FAA lifted its grounding order, clearing the MD-11 to return to service.[^cite_note-reuters20260511-52]

### Lawsuits

[Morgan & Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_&_Morgan) announced the first lawsuit filed by two plaintiffs against UPS, Boeing, and the [General Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Aerospace) for [negligence](21436.md) in federal court on November 7.[^cite_note-53] The first plaintiff is claimed to have suffered smoke inhalation; the second plaintiff is claimed to have suffered from property damage to two of his businesses.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

On December 3, 2025, Clifford Law Offices and Sam Aguiar Injury Lawyers announced lawsuits against UPS, Boeing, General Electric, and VT San Antonio Aerospace, alleging negligence by UPS.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] On December 12, [ST Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ST_Engineering) was added to the lawsuit.[^cite_note-58] On December 17, another lawsuit was filed against UPS on behalf of another victim.[^cite_note-59] On August 17, 2026, Trinidad Chavez's family received compensation from Clear Path Insurance, which represents the defendants in the Clifford and Aguiar lawsuit.[^cite_note-60]

On January 23, 2026, the Clifford Law Firm and attorney Sam Aguiar also announced another lawsuit filed against the same defendants on behalf of the family of Alain Rodriguez Colina, who was injured from the crash and later died on Christmas Day. The lawsuit seeks damages for criminal negligence.[^cite_note-61] Also, Texas law firms filed in the county court a lawsuit on behalf of Carlos Arias Fernandez, against ST Engineering.[^cite_note-62]

The wife of Captain Dana Diamond, the relief officer who died in the UPS air crash, filed a lawsuit against The Boeing Company, General Electric, and ST San Antonio Aerospace through the Lanier Law Firm in Jefferson County on February 25, 2026.[^cite_note-63]

Peterson Law and Whiteford Taylor & Preston represent the vast majority of crash victims, filing fifteen comprehensive lawsuits on May 7, 2026, in Jefferson County Circuit Court on behalf of more than 100 individuals and multiple businesses affected by the crash. The lawsuits name UPS, Boeing Company, General Electric, VT San Antonio Aerospace, multiple insurance companies, and the estate of Captain Richard R. Wartenberg as defendants, alleging negligence and seeking damages for wrongful death, catastrophic injuries, and business destruction.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

On July 13, 2026, the Alex R. White Lawyers PLLC law firm sued UPS and others for criminal negligence on behalf of the family of the deceased estate of Matthew Hagan Sweets, which seeks damages for trauma, wrongful death, mental anguish, and loss of consortium.[^cite_note-66]

## Investigation



The charred cockpit voice recorder (left) and flight data recorder (right) pictured the day after the crash



Pieces recovered from the accident: on the left is the fitting where the engine is attached to the wing, on the right are the fractured lugs of the engine pylon

The [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board) (NTSB) investigated the crash along with the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA).[^cite_note-67] The NTSB launched a [go-team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board#Accident_and_incident_investigations) of 28 personnel.[^cite_note-CNN-Live-NTSB-68] At a media briefing held on November 5, with board member Todd Inman serving as the on-scene spokesperson,[^cite_note-69] they announced the recovery of the [cockpit voice recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockpit_voice_recorder) (CVR) and [flight data recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_data_recorder).[^cite_note-Louisville_Public_Media-34] UPS Airlines announced it was in close contact with and cooperating with the NTSB and the FAA.[^cite_note-ups.com_Statement-14]

On November 6, NTSB officials announced that the last [ADS-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADS-B) message, received at 5\:13\:32 p.m. EST (UTC−5), showed the aircraft had reached a maximum speed of 183 knots (339 km/h; 211 mph) and an altitude of 475 feet (145 m) above sea level, only ten feet (3 m) above ground level. They also said they had downloaded the flight's data from the cockpit voice recorder and flight data recorder.[^cite_note-70] An NTSB spokesman said the CVR audio contained a persistent bell sound.[^cite_note-71]

On November 20, the NTSB released their preliminary report on the accident.[^cite_note-72] The report stated that the cracks had been found in the left pylon resulting from [metal fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_fatigue)—specifically, inspection of the pylon showed fatigue cracks on both fracture surfaces of the aft mount's aft lug and along the bore of the aft mount forward lug's inboard fracture surface.[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9] However, no deformation or pre-existing fractures were found in the forward top flange of the aft mount assembly. The report also noted the crash of [American Airlines Flight 191](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_191) in 1979 (involving a DC-10 with very similar engine mounts), which had crashed under similar circumstances.[^cite_note-DCA26MA024-prelim-9]

On January 14, 2026, the NTSB released an update stating that all engine data recorded by the FDR was normal until 17\:13\:11 and fatigue cracks were found in engine no. 1's spherical bearing assembly.[^cite_note-73]

In May 2026, NTSB revealed that Boeing asked the FAA to increase the inspection interval for the lugs from 19,900 to 29,200 flight cycles even though it had received five reports of the spherical bearing, which is housed within the lugs, fracturing long before the aircraft hit its designated inspection interval.[^cite_note-SeattleTimes20May2026-11] In two cases, the spherical bearing fractured before reaching 15,000 flight cycles; in the other three, the bearing fractured under 10,000 flight cycles.[^cite_note-SeattleTimes20May2026-11]
