The **Government of Colombia** is a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) with [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) into an [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), [judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), and [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_branch). The executive is led by the [president](251302.md), who acts as both the head of state and government, the judiciary includes four high courts which manage different fields of the law, and the national legislature is a bicameral [congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Colombia) composed of the [senate](645242.md) and [chamber of representatives](645347.md).

The country is principally divided into 32 [departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia) and one [capital district](211271.md).

The [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) rated Colombia a "[flawed democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index#Definitions)" in 2024.[^cite_note-democracy-index-1]

### President

Main article: [President of Colombia](251302.md)

The president of Colombia is elected by a direct popular vote, with elections held every four years. They are the head of state and government, as well as the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) and supreme administrative authority.[^cite_note-\:4-2] In 2015, Congress limited the presidency to a single four-year term, preventing the president from seeking re-election.[^cite_note-3]

For 150 years, excluding [military dictatorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Military_Junta) in the 1950s, the president came from either the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Liberal_Party) or the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Conservative_Party), with the country operating under a [two-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system). But, every president following the [2002 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Colombian_presidential_election) has come from a third party.[^cite_note-4]

### Vice President

Main article: [Vice President of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Colombia)

The Vice President is the second-highest executive office in Colombia. They are elected directly by appearing on a presidential candidate's ticket. The 1991 constitution reestablished the office after being abolished in 1905.[^cite_note-5]

### Council of Ministers

Main article: [Council of Ministers of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Colombia)

The [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Colombia) is the national [government cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)) of Colombia, composed of the most senior appointed politicians of the executive branch. In addition to the president and vice president, members are the heads of [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)) and administrative departments. Its composition has changed throughout history, but the council currently includes 19 ministers[^cite_note-6] and 6 directors of administrative departments, all appointed by the president.[^cite_note-7]

## Legislature

Main articles: [Legislative Branch of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Branch_of_Colombia), [Congress of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Colombia), and [Elections in Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Colombia)

The legislative branch of Colombia's national government is the bicameral Congress, composed of the Senate and Chamber of Representatives. Its primary duties are lawmaking and legislative regulation; these include drafting, enacting, interpreting, amending, and repealing laws.[^cite_note-\:4-2][^cite_note-\:2-8]

### Senate

Main article: [Senate of Colombia](645242.md)

The Senate of Colombia is composed of 108 seats, with members elected to 4-year terms in direct elections through a party-list proportional representation system.[^cite_note-\:2-8] The Senate has 7 permanent commissions and a board of directors.[^cite_note-\:7-9]

### Chamber of Representatives

Main article: [Chamber of Representatives of Colombia](645347.md)

The Chamber of Representatives of Colombia is composed of 188 members elected to 4 year terms in direct elections through a party-list proportional representation system.[^cite_note-\:2-8] One seat is dedicated to representing the [Raizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raizal) ethnic group, two seats are dedicated to the [Indigenous peoples in Colombia](4053513.md), and one is dedicated to [Colombians abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_diaspora).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:7-9]

## Judiciary

Main article: [Judiciary of Colombia](13224622.md)

The judiciary of Colombia interprets and applies the country's laws to ensure equal justice under the law and to provide a mechanism for dispute resolution. The judicial branch of Colombia's national government is composed of four high courts: the [Supreme Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Justice_of_Colombia) which takes on matters of criminal law, the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(Colombia\)) which handles administrative law, the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Colombia) dealing with constitutional law, and the [Superior Council of Judicature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Council_of_Judicature) which manages jurisdictional conflicts and judicial administration.[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-12] Colombia’s legal system follows [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)).[^cite_note-\:4-2]

### Supreme Court of Justice of Colombia

Main article: [Supreme Court of Justice of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Justice_of_Colombia)

The Supreme Court of Justice of Colombia handles criminal law,[^cite_note-\:0-11] composed of 23 judges appointed to non-renewable 8-year terms by Congress from a list of candidates.[^cite_note-\:2-8] Justices must be lawyers who have served a minimum of 10 years in the judiciary, the public ministry, or at an established educational institution and be natural-born Colombian citizens.[^cite_note-\:6-13] The court was created in 1886[^cite_note-14] and is led by the [President of the Supreme Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_Justice). Below the Supreme Court are Judicial District Superior Tribunals and, below them, district courts.[^cite_note-\:4-2] Before the 1991 constitution, it was the highest legal, civil, criminal, and constitutional court in Colombia.[^cite_note-\:6-13]

### Council of State

Main article: [Council of State (Colombia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(Colombia\))

The Council of State handles administrative law.[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-\:6-13] It was established in 1817 by the first president of Colombia [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar).[^cite_note-15]

### Constitutional Court

Main article: [Constitutional Court of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Colombia)

The Constitutional Court handles constitutional law and international treaties.[^cite_note-\:0-11] It was established by the constitution of 1991,[^cite_note-16] which reorganized the country's high courts.[^cite_note-\:6-13]

### Superior Council of Judicature

Main article: [Superior Council of Judicature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Council_of_Judicature)

The Superior Council of Judicature handles jurisdictional conflicts and judicial administration; it is headed by a president.[^cite_note-\:0-11] The council is composed of two chambers: the administrative chamber and the disciplinary jurisdictional chamber, made up of 6 and 7 judges, respectively.[^cite_note-17] It is also the job of the superior council to submit lists of potential justices for the Supreme Court of Justice.[^cite_note-\:6-13]

### Special jurisdictions

Military tribunals handle infractions by police and active military personnel according to the Military Penal Code.[^cite_note-\:4-2] The National Electoral Council is a legal body that has final say on electoral issues. Due to the ongoing [Colombian conflict](1857628.md), special legal jurisdictions (the Special Jurisdiction for Peace and Judicial Chambers for Justice and Peace) have been created to try individuals for crimes committed during the conflict.[^cite_note-\:4-2]

## Subdivisions

Colombia is principally divided into 32 [departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia) and one [capital district](211271.md).[^cite_note-\:4-2] There are four other cities ([Cartagena](63927.md), [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla), [Santa Marta](547214.md), and [Buenaventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenaventura,_Valle_del_Cauca)) which also have district status due to their national importance but remain within a department.[^cite_note-\:1-18]

### Departments

Main article: [Departments of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia)

Each department has a governor and department assembly and is granted limited autonomy.[^cite_note-\:5-19] Departments are primarily composed of municipalities, of which there are 1123 in Colombia,[^cite_note-\:1-18] each with a mayor and municipal council. Each department has its own department capital.[^cite_note-\:5-19]

The most recent changes to departments were made in the [1991 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991), which created the modern departments of [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_Department,_Colombia), [Arauca](549644.md), [Casanare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casanare_Department), [Guainía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guain%C3%ADa_Department), [Guaviare](549674.md), [Putumayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putumayo_Department), [San Andrés y Providencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andr%C3%A9s_and_Providencia), [Vapués](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaup%C3%A9s_Department), and [Vichada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichada_Department) that were previously a part of the "[National Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Territory_\(Colombia\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)".[^cite_note-20]

### Capital District

The capital city of [Bogotá](211271.md) has the same administrative status as a department. It is governed by the [Superior Mayor of Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Mayor_of_Bogot%C3%A1) and [Bogotá City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_City_Council). The council is the supreme authority of the district, autonomous in administrative, budgetary, and financial matters.[^cite_note-21] While Bogotá is outside of the Cundinamarca Department, it is the department's official capital.[^cite_note-\:5-19]

### Indigenous territories

Main article: [Indigenous territory (Colombia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_territory_\(Colombia\))

Indigenous territories or indigenous reserves in Colombia are the "constitutional form through which the state recognizes and formalizes the collective ownership of \[indigenous] peoples' ancestral territories"; there are 846 territories recognized by law.[^cite_note-\:3-22] The territories total 35,608,579.2 hectares or 31% of the nation as of 2023 according to the National Land Agency.[^cite_note-\:3-22]

According to the 2018 census, 64% of Colombia's 1.9 million indigenous people lived within these territories.[^cite_note-\:3-22]

The [National Indigenous Territories Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Indigenous_Territories_Commission?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CNTI) was established in 1996 by Decree 1397 to ensure full territorial rights for indigenous peoples. It is composed of 10 delegates representing indigenous organizations within the territories who consult with and relay issues to the national government.[^cite_note-23]

### Trust

In 2022, 21% of Colombians reported high or moderately high trust in the national government, 22% in their local government, and 20% in the civil service.[^cite_note-24]

### Climate change

Colombia's government has been recognized as taking a determined approach to reducing its dependence on [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) and contributing to a reversal in global [climate change](5042951.md) trends.[^cite_note-25]
