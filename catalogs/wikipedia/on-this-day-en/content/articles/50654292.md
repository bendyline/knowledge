**Logan Alexander Paul** (born April 1, 1995) is an American [influencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influencer), [professional wrestler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestler), entrepreneur, [boxer](4243.md), singer and actor. He has over 23 million subscribers on his [YouTube](3524766.md) channel *Logan Paul Vlogs* and has ranked on the *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* list for the highest-paid YouTube creators in 2017, 2018, and 2021.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] He is the co-founder of beverage company [Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_\(drink\)) and [snack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snack) brand [Lunchly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunchly). Paul has also run the *Impaulsive* [podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast) since November 2018, which has over four million YouTube subscribers.[^cite_note-9] In December 2025, Paul was appointed General Partner of the venture capital firm Anti Fund, which was co-founded by his brother [Jake Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Paul).[^cite_note-10] As a wrestler, he has been signed to [WWE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE) since June 2022, where he performs on the [Raw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_\(WWE_brand\)) brand. He is a member of [The Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vision_\(professional_wrestling\)) [stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_\(wrestling\)). He is also a former [one-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WWE_United_States_Champions) [WWE United States Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE_United_States_Championship) and [one-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Tag_Team_Champions) [World Tag Team Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tag_Team_Championship_\(WWE\)).

In 2013, Paul gained a following by posting sketches on the now-defunct video-sharing application [Vine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_\(service\)).[^cite_note-11] He registered his first YouTube channel, *TheOfficialLoganPaul*, on October 18, 2013, where he started posting regularly following the closure of the Vine app.[^cite_note-12] He later created the *Logan Paul Vlogs* channel on August 29, 2015, which has since become his most-subscribed YouTube channel.[^cite_note-Social_Blade_Summary-13] As of August 2023, the channel has received 23.6 million subscribers and almost 6 billion views.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Social_Blade_Summary-13]

As an actor, Paul's television and film work includes guest appearances on *[Law & Order: Special Victims Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_&_Order:_Special_Victims_Unit)* and *[Bizaardvark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bizaardvark)*, and roles in films *[The Thinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thinning)* (2016) and *[The Thinning: New World Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thinning:_New_World_Order)* (2018).[^cite_note-SR-15][^cite_note-Verge-16] He has also explored other avenues; he released his debut single "2016" in 2016, and fought English media personality [KSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSI) in a [white-collar boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-collar_boxing) match in 2018. [The bout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSI_vs_Logan_Paul) ended in a [majority draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_draw). In the 2019 [rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSI_vs._Logan_Paul_II), which was a [professional bout](4283645.md), KSI won by [split decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_decision). In 2023, Paul won his first professional boxing match when he defeated [Dillon Danis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dillon_Danis) via disqualification. Paul also transitioned to [exhibition boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_fight) in 2021, fighting [Floyd Mayweather Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_Mayweather_Jr.) in a non-scored bout. After a couple of brief appearances in WWE in 2021, he made his professional wrestling debut as [The Miz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miz)'s tag team partner in a [tag team match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tag_team_match) at [WrestleMania 38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_38) in April 2022, which they won and Paul received praise for his performance. He then signed a contract with WWE in June that year and later won the WWE United States Championship in November 2023 and the World Tag Team Championship in March 2026.

Paul has been involved in several controversies, most notably in relation to a trip to Japan in December 2017, during which he visited the [Aokigahara forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aokigahara) (a notorious [suicide site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_suicide_locations)), filmed himself finding the corpse of a suicide victim, and uploaded the footage to his YouTube channel.[^cite_note-\:0-17] Others include his promotion of scams and failed ventures, including [*CryptoZoo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CryptoZoo_\(video_game\))—in which he failed to compensate more than \$1.5 million to investors despite promising to do so.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Early life

Logan Alexander Paul[^cite_note-20] was born on April 1, 1995, in [Westlake, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westlake,_Ohio), a suburb of [Cleveland](5951.md).[^cite_note-Yosemite-21] His mother is Pamela Ann Stepnick (*née* Meredith) and his father Gregory Allan Paul is a realtor.[^cite_note-22] He claims Irish, Welsh, Jewish, French and German ancestry.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] He was raised in Westlake with his younger brother [Jake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Paul) (born 1997), who is also a media personality.[^cite_note-BusinessInsider-25] Paul began creating videos for a YouTube channel called Zoosh when he was 10 years old.[^cite_note-AdWeek-26][^cite_note-27] He attended [Westlake High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westlake_High_School_\(Ohio\)), being named *[The Plain Dealer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Plain_Dealer)'s* All-Star football linebacker in 2012,[^cite_note-28] and qualifying for the state-level [Ohio High School Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_High_School_Athletic_Association) 2013 Division I Wrestling Individual Championships.[^cite_note-Cleveland_Highschool-29][^cite_note-30]

## YouTube career

### 2015–2017: YouTube beginnings



Paul in 2016

By the time Paul began attending college, his YouTube channel had attained a modest following via the platform [Vine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_\(service\)).[^cite_note-Cleveland_Highschool-29] He took classes in [industrial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_engineering) at [Ohio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_University) before dropping out in 2014 to pursue a career as a full-time social media entertainer in Los Angeles,[^cite_note-BusinessInsider-25][^cite_note-31] moving into an apartment complex in the city with other Vine stars.[^cite_note-AdWeek-26][^cite_note-BusinessInsider-25]

Paul rose to fame as a member on the Internet video sharing service Vine.[^cite_note-CBSNews-32] In February 2014, he had over 3.1 million followers on various social media platforms.[^cite_note-33] By April 2014, he had attained 105,000 Twitter followers, 361,000 [Instagram](31591547.md) followers, 31,000 likes on his Facebook page and about 150,000 subscribers to his YouTube channel. A YouTube compilation video of his Vine work garnered more than four million views the first week it was posted.[^cite_note-Cleveland_Highschool-29] In 2015 he was ranked as the 10th most influential figure on Vine, with his six-second videos earning him hundreds of thousands of dollars in advertising revenue.[^cite_note-BusinessInsider-25] By that October, his Facebook videos alone had more than 300 million views.[^cite_note-AdWeek-26] Logan refers to his following as the "Logang", a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) of "Logan" and "[gang](80408.md)".[^cite_note-34]

His YouTube channel was known for his vlogs about his daily life which included pranks, comedy, controversy and drama. In 2017, Paul was estimated by Forbes magazine to be the fourth-highest paid YouTuber, earning at least \$12.5m (£9.3m). He also earned a significant amount from his Maverick clothing line.[^cite_note-EsquireMiddleEastLoganPaulDubai-35][^cite_note-BBCLoganPaulJapanVideo-36][^cite_note-ArabianBusinessLoganPaulDigitalInfluence-37][^cite_note-BuzzFeedNewsLoganPaulVlogs-38] In 2018, Paul estimated that at one point he was documenting 90 per cent of his life on the internet.[^cite_note-BillboardLoganPaulOneMoreChance-39]

### 2017–2018: Suicide forest controversy

Further information: [List of YouTube videos § We found a dead body in the Japanese Suicide Forest... (2017)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_YouTube_videos#We_found_a_dead_body_in_the_Japanese_Suicide_Forest..._\(2017\))

On December 31, 2017, Paul uploaded a [vlog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlog) to his YouTube channel depicting the corpse of a recently-deceased man who had died by [hanging himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_by_hanging) in [Aokigahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aokigahara) at the base of [Mount Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fuji) in Japan, known as the "suicide forest" due to its infamy as a [suicide site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_suicide_locations). Initially intended to be part three of his "Tokyo Adventures" series, Paul and his group had planned to camp in the woods, but in response to finding the corpse, decided to notify the authorities and cancel their plans. The video gained 6.3 million views within 24 hours of being uploaded.[^cite_note-\:0-17] Paul's video depicting the corpse, whose face was [censored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fogging_\(censorship\)), and his group's reactions to it, were criticized by celebrities and politicians.[^cite_note-Mash-40][^cite_note-McCurry-41] In addition, he was accused by other members of the [YouTube community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_YouTube_celebrities) of being insensitive to suicide victims.[^cite_note-42] He was also criticized for other misbehavior he was captured taking part in during the trip, including climbing onto a moving forklift truck at the [Tsukiji fish market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukiji_fish_market), removing his clothing on a crowded street and then proceeding to fight with one of his traveling companions, and throwing a giant [Poké Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Ball) plush toy at passing citizens, including an officer of the [Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department](30871408.md).[^cite_note-43] Several petitions were posted to [Change.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change.org) urging YouTube to delete Paul's channel in light of the controversy, the largest of which received more than 720,000 signatures as of February 9, 2018.[^cite_note-44] As a result of the backlash, Paul removed the video from his YouTube channel, following up with a written apology on Twitter on January 1, 2018. The following day, on January 2, a subsequent video apology was released to YouTube in which Paul admitted to making "a severe and continuous lapse in \[his] judgement" and described his behavior as a "[coping mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coping)", asking his fans to stop defending his actions in the process.[^cite_note-Metro-SV-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] On January 9, YouTube issued a statement via Twitter condemning Paul's video: "It's taken us a long time to respond, but we've been listening to everything you've been saying. We know that the actions of one creator can affect the entire community, so we'll have more to share soon on steps we're taking to ensure a video like this is never circulated again."[^cite_note-48] On January 10, YouTube announced it was removing Paul's channels from [Google Preferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Preferred), its preferred ad program, and [*New World Order*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thinning:_New_World_Order), the sequel to his YouTube film *The Thinning*, was placed on hold,[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] with the airing of *Logan Paul VS.* being halted as well. He was also cut from season 4 of the YouTube Red series *[Foursome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foursome_\(2016_TV_series\))* and the role of Alec Fixler was terminated.[^cite_note-51]

[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer) postponed the release of the film *[Valley Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Girl_\(2020_film\))*, featuring Paul, following the controversies.[^cite_note-52] On January 15, Paul was seen at [LAX](18131.md) by reporters from [TMZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMZ). He said that he has learned a lot from his mistakes and believes he has been treated "fairly". When asked whether he deserves a second chance, Paul replied, "Everyone deserves second chances, bro."[^cite_note-53] In response, he donated \$1 million to [suicide prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_prevention) agencies, a quarter of which is going to the [National Suicide Prevention Lifeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/988_Suicide_&_Crisis_Lifeline).[^cite_note-54] In response to the controversy, Paul hired Mike Majlak, a former marketing manager at furniture company [LoveSac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LoveSac), to "make sure that no bad things happen" and work with Logan to help improve his reputation.[^cite_note-55] On February 4, Paul officially returned to his daily vlogs on YouTube after taking a 3-week-long hiatus.[^cite_note-56] YouTube CEO [Susan Wojcicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Wojcicki) said on February 12 that Paul did not violate YouTube's three-strike policy and did not meet the criteria for being banned from the platform.[^cite_note-57] In the wake of the controversy regarding the suicide video, Maverick Apparel, a brand for juniors and children, threatened Paul with legal action for giving his clothing line a similar name ("Maverick by Logan Paul"), believing shoppers are confusing their line with Paul's, resulting in a deep decline in sales.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

## Music career

On May 18, 2017, Paul released “Help Me Help You,” a single featuring the boy band [Why Don't We](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_Don't_We). The song became popular on [YouTube](3524766.md) and has since accumulated more than 79 million streams on [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify).[^cite_note-HotNewHipHopLoganPaul-81]

On July, 2017, Paul uploaded a diss track response against his brother [Jake Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Paul) titled the fall of Jake Paul which received over 195 million views. The brothers would later reconcile and release a song I love you bro and then the rise of the Pauls which received over 66 million views.[^cite_note-PolygonYouTubeDissTracksMakeMillions-82][^cite_note-RollingStoneLoganJakePaulYouTubeMegastars-83]

On November 23, 2017, Paul released his new single, "No Handlebars", a track that draws heavily on an interpolated sample of the song "[Handlebars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handlebars_\(Flobots_song\))" by the American alternative hip hop group [Flobots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flobots). The song was heavily criticized for its perceived sexual objectification of women, including a scene in its music video where Paul rides several women like a bicycle. Flobots frontman [Jamie Laurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Laurie) lambasted Paul for both the "sexist" lyrical content of the song and for unauthorized use of the sample, calling him the face of "douchebag entitlement". Laurie would later go on to release a track with lyrics deriding Paul, titled "Handle Your Bars". Paul did not respond to Laurie's comments nor the backlash towards "No Handlebars".[^cite_note-84] After Flobots sued Paul for copyright infringement in 2019, he deleted the song from YouTube.[^cite_note-85]

The match against Reigns received universal acclaim, with critics highly praising Paul's wrestling skills.[^cite_note-CJ2022-119] It was initially reported that Paul had suffered a torn meniscus, MCL, and ACL during the match, however, it was later revealed that Paul had only sprained his meniscus and MCL.[^cite_note-120]

However, the referee caught Owens with the brass knuckles in his hand, giving Paul the victory by disqualification to retain the title.[^cite_note-133] On the February 16 episode of *SmackDown,* Paul defeated The Miz in his first televised match in WWE to qualify for the men's [Elimination Chamber match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_Chamber_match) at [Elimination Chamber: Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_Chamber:_Perth). At the event, he was eliminated by [Randy Orton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Orton) but later returned to hit him with brass knuckles, costing him the match.[^cite_note-134] On Night 2 of [WrestleMania XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_XL), Paul successfully defended the United States Championship against Kevin Owens and Randy Orton in a [triple threat match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_threat_match)[^cite_note-135] after an assist by [IShowSpeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IShowSpeed).[^cite_note-136]

On the May 10 episode of *SmackDown,* SmackDown general manager [Nick Aldis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Aldis) announced that Paul would be the next opponent against [Undisputed WWE Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undisputed_WWE_Championship) [Cody Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Rhodes) and a [Champion vs. Champion match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champion_vs._Champion_match) was scheduled for [King and Queen of the Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_and_Queen_of_the_Ring_\(2024\)).[^cite_note-SmackDown05102024-137] On the following episode, during the contract signing for the match, the contract stated that the match would be for both titles. However, when Paul was about to sign the contract, he tore it up, before bringing out his own contract, which stated that only Rhodes' title would be on the line. After Paul mocked Rhodes, he signed the contract, confirming that the match would only be for Rhodes' title.[^cite_note-SmackDown05172024-138] At the event on May 25, Paul failed to win the title from Rhodes after interference from special guest ring announcer Ibrahim Al Hajjaj.[^cite_note-139] At [SummerSlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SummerSlam_\(2024\)) on August 3, Paul lost the title to [LA Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Knight), ending his reign at 273 days.[^cite_note-SSlam2024-140]

#### The Vision (2025–present)

Main article: [The Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vision_\(professional_wrestling\))

On the November 3, 2025 episode of *Raw*, Paul confronted new World Heavyweight Champion CM Punk before being caught in a brawl involving [The Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vision_\(professional_wrestling\)) ([Bron Breakker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bron_Breakker) and [Bronson Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronson_Reed)). Later that night, Punk and Jey Uso faced Breakker and Reed in a match that ended in a double count-out. After the bout, The Vision attacked Uso. Paul then returned to the ring appearing to assist Punk, but instead struck Punk with his brass knuckles and handed them to Paul Heyman, revealing his alignment with the group.[^cite_note-150] On November 29, Paul's team was victorious in the men's [WarGames match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarGames_match) at [Survivor Series: WarGames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_Series:_WarGames_\(2025\)).[^cite_note-SSeries2025-151] On the following episode of *Raw*, Paul officially joined The Vision.[^cite_note-Raw12012025-152]

At the [Royal Rumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Rumble_\(2026\)) on January 31, 2026, Paul entered the match at No. 20 being eliminated by eventual winner [Roman Reigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Reigns). On the February 27 episode of SmackDown, Paul replaced Jey Uso by defeating [Jacob Fatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Fatu) after interference from Drew McIntyre to qualify for the Elimination Chamber match the following night. At [Elimination Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_Chamber_\(2026\)) On February 28, Paul eliminated [Je'Von Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je'Von_Evans), [LA Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Knight) and [Trick Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trick_Williams) before being eliminated by [Cody Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Rhodes) after interference from a returning [Seth Rollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rollins) who delivered a Stomp allowing Paul to be eliminated by Rhodes. On the March 30 episode of *Raw*, The Vision (Paul and [Austin Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Theory)) defeated [The Usos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Usos) ([Jey Uso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jey_Uso) and [Jimmy Uso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Uso)) in a [Street Fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_fight_\(professional_wrestling\)) to win the [World Tag Team Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tag_Team_Championship_\(WWE\)) after interference from [IShowSpeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IShowSpeed).[^cite_note-VisionWorldTag-153] On Night 1 of [WrestleMania 42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_42) on April 18, Paul and Theory teamed with IShowSpeed in a losing effort against LA Knight and The Usos ending their feud. Following the match, Paul attacked IShowSpeed, after which Speed performed a [top-rope splash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling_aerial_techniques#Diving_splash) onto Paul through a broadcast table with assistance from Knight and The Usos.[^cite_note-WMania42-154] At [Saturday Night's Main Event XLIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night's_Main_Event_XLIV) on May 23, Paul and Theory succuessfully defended their titles against [The Street Profits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Street_Profits) ([Angelo Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Dawkins) and [Montez Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montez_Ford)). During the match, however, Paul legitimately suffered a torn tricep which required surgery, putting him out of action for up to six months.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] Breakker defended the title in his behalf during Paul's absence.[^cite_note-VisionFreebird-157]

## Personal life

In October 2015, Paul lived in the same apartment complex on [Hollywood and Vine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine) in Hollywood, California as other social media celebrities including [Amanda Cerny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Cerny), [Juanpa Zurita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juanpa_Zurita), and [Andrew Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Bach), with his roommates Mark Dohner and Evan Eckenrode. This proximity facilitated various collaborations on their respective videos.[^cite_note-CBSNews-32] In October 2017, Paul and Eckenrode relocated to an estate in [Encino, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encino,_California).[^cite_note-158] In February 2021, Paul announced that he would be moving to [Dorado, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorado,_Puerto_Rico) from Los Angeles. He stated that the high taxes in California were the main motivator for the move.[^cite_note-159] As of June 2021, he was renting a \$13 million mansion there for \$55,000 a month.[^cite_note-160]

In February 2026, after his brother Jake posted on X a critical post of [Bad Bunny](55943877.md) and his Super Bowl Halftime show, Paul quote tweeted the post, disagreeing with his brother's criticism of Puerto Rico and Bad Bunny.[^cite_note-161]

### Relationships

From 2016 to early 2017, Paul dated influencer [Amanda Cerny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Cerny). He then had an on and off relationship with actress [Chloe Bennet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloe_Bennet), with whom he was photographed making out in Hawaii on a break from filming their movie, *[Valley Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Girl_\(2020_film\))*, in July 2017. The two officially broke up in October 2018. Paul and Josie Canseco started dating in January 2020 and broke up in November 2020. Besides, Logan was romantically linked with *[America's Next Top Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Next_Top_Model)* contestant [Jessica Serfaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Serfaty) in 2015, influencer [Alissa Violet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alissa_Violet) in 2017, actress and model [Olga Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Safari) from late 2017 to early 2018, and actress [Corinna Kopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinna_Kopf) in 2019.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-Emily-164]

Paul began a relationship with Danish model [Nina Agdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Agdal) in 2022. They announced their engagement on July 9, 2023.[^cite_note-165] On April 15, 2024, Paul revealed that he and Agdal were expecting their first child, a daughter born in September 2024.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-Emily-164] They were married on August 15, 2025, in [Lake Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Como), Italy, the location of their engagement.[^cite_note-167]

### Health

Paul said in an episode of *Impaulsive* that he tore the cartilage in his knee playing football, requiring him to stay out of school for 3 months.[^cite_note-168] In February 2019, Paul said that he has long-term brain damage, which he sustained from playing high school football. He stated that [Daniel Amen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Amen), the doctor who diagnosed him, says it affects his ability to have [empathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empathy) and a human connection with others.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] When filming a video for his Vine channel in 2014, Paul attempted a stunt during which he landed on a chair and damaged his right testicle.[^cite_note-171]
