The **Monza Circuit**, officially called the **Autodromo Nazionale di Monza** ([Italian](14708.md) for 'Monza National Autodrome'), is a 5.793 km (3.600 mi) [race track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_track) near the city of [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monza), north of [Milan](36511.md), in Italy. Built in 1922, it was the world's fourth purpose-built motor racing circuit after [Aspendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspendale_Racecourse), [Brooklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklands) and [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Motor_Speedway), and the oldest in [mainland Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Europe).[^cite_note-MonzaNetHistory-6] The circuit's biggest event is the [Italian Grand Prix](511451.md). With the exception of the [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Italian_Grand_Prix) running when the track was closed while undergoing refurbishment, the race has been hosted there since [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Italian_Grand_Prix), being one of only three other tracks (namely [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverstone_Circuit), [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Monaco), and [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Spa-Francorchamps)), to consistently feature on the Formula One calendar since its [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Formula_One_season).[^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7] The circuit is also known as "**the Temple of Speed**" due to its long straights and high-speed corners.

Built in the [Royal Villa of Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Villa_of_Monza) park in a woodland setting,[^cite_note-MonzaNet1922-8] the site has three tracks – the 5.793 km (3.600 mi) [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motor_racing) track,[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] the 2.405 km (1.494 mi) Junior track,[^cite_note-MonzaNetStats-5] and a 4.250 km (2.641 mi) high speed oval track with steep bankings, which was left unused for decades and had been decaying until it was restored in the 2010s.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7] The major features of the main Grand Prix track include the *Curva Grande*, the *Curva di Lesmo*, the *Variante Ascari,* and the *Curva Alboreto* (formerly *Curva Parabolica*[^cite_note-10]). The high-speed curve, Curva Grande, is located after the *Variante del Rettifilo,* which is located at the end of the front straight or *Rettifilo Tribune*, and is usually taken flat out by Formula One cars.

In addition to [Formula One](10854.md), the circuit previously hosted the [1,000 km Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_Hours_of_Monza), an endurance sports car race held as part of the [World Sportscar Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Sportscar_Championship) and the [Le Mans Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans_Series). Monza also featured the unique [Race of Two Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_of_Two_Worlds) events, which attempted to run Formula One and [USAC National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAC_National_Championship) cars against each other. The racetrack also previously held rounds of the [Grand Prix motorcycle racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing) ([Italian motorcycle Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_motorcycle_Grand_Prix)), [WTCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Touring_Car_Championship), [TCR International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_International_Series), [Superbike World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superbike_World_Championship), [Formula Renault 3.5 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Renault_3.5_Series), and [Auto GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_GP). Monza currently hosts rounds of the [Blancpain GT Series Endurance Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blancpain_GT_Series_Endurance_Cup), [International GT Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_GT_Open), and [Euroformula Open Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroformula_Open_Championship), as well as various local championships such as the [TCR Italian Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_Italian_Series), [Italian GT Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_GT_Championship), [Porsche Carrera Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_Cup) Italia, and [Italian F4 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_F4_Championship), as well as the [Monza Rally Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monza_Rally_Show). In 2020, Monza hosted the [2020 World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_World_Rally_Championship) final round, [ACI Rally Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Rally_Monza), with the circuit hosting 10 of the 16 rally stages.

Monza also hosts cycling and running events, most notably the Monza 12h Cycling Marathon[^cite_note-auto-11] and Monza 21 Half Marathon.[^cite_note-auto-11] The venue was also selected by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc) scientists for the [Breaking2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking2) event, where three runners attempted to break the 2-hour barrier for the [marathon](49132.md). [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge) ran 2\:00\:25.[^cite_note-12]

A very fast circuit, Monza has been [the site of many fatal accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Autodromo_Nazionale_Monza_fatal_accidents), especially in the early years of the Formula One world championship,[^cite_note-Clarin-13] and has claimed the lives of 52 drivers and 35 spectators. Track modifications have continuously occurred to improve spectator safety and reduce curve speeds,[^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7] but it is still criticised by the current drivers for its lack of [run-off areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run-off_area), most notoriously at the [chicane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicane) that cuts the *Variante della Roggia*.[^cite_note-Clarin-13]

## History

### Early history



The starting grid of the "[1st Cup Fiera di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppa_Fiera_di_Milano)" race held in 1925



A race in 1925 with cars racing across the bridge



The Pista di Alta Velocità banking, 2003

The first track was built from May to July 1922 by 3,500 workers, financed by the Milan Automobile Club[^cite_note-MonzaNet1922-8] – which created the *Società Incremento Automobilismo e Sport* (SIAS) (English: *Society for the Promotion of Motor Racing and Sport*) to run the track.[^cite_note-F1db-14] The initial form was a 3.4 km<sup>2</sup> (1.31 sq mi) site containing a paved 4.490 km (2.790 mi) oval and a 5.500 km (3.418 mi) road course which could be run as a combined 10.000 km (6.214 mi) course via their shared front straight.

The track was officially opened on 3 September 1922, with the maiden race the second Italian Grand Prix held on 10 September 1922. Monza's close proximity to Milan, the center of Italy's economy, the largest metropolitan area in Italy and one of Europe's leading major cities made Monza a particularly convenient location for racing and other events.[^cite_note-F1db-14]

In 1928, the most serious Italian racing accident to date[^cite_note-MonzaNet1922-8][^cite_note-Clarin-13] ended in the death of driver [Emilio Materassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Materassi) and 27 spectators at [that year's Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Italian_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-MonzaNet1922-8][^cite_note-Clarin-13] The accident led to further Grand Prix races' confinement to the high-speed loop until 1932.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1929-15] For these reasons the Italian Grand Prix was not held again until 1931; in the meantime the 1930 [Monza Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monza_Grand_Prix) was held on the high speed ring only, while in 1930 [Vincenzo Florio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Florio) introduced the Florio Circuit. The [1933 Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Italian_Grand_Prix) was held on the original complete layout but it was marred by the deaths of three drivers ([Giuseppe Campari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Campari), [Baconin Borzacchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baconin_Borzacchini) and [Stanisław Czaykowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Czaykowski)) in the supporting Monza Grand Prix held on the same day—which became known as the "Black Day of Monza"—over the shorter oval circuit[^cite_note-F1db-14][^cite_note-Monza33-16][^cite_note-17] and the Grand Prix layout was changed: in 1934 a short circuit with two lanes of the straight line joined by a hairpin, *Curva Sud* of the banking (with a double chicane) driven in the opposite direction than usual, the "Florio link" and the *Curva Sud* (with a small chicane). This configuration was considered too slow and in 1935 Florio Circuit was used again, this time with four temporary chicanes and another one permanent (along the *Curva Sud* of the banking). In 1938 only the last one was used.[^cite_note-18]

There was major rebuilding in 1938–39, constructing new stands and entrances, resurfacing the track, removing the high speed ring and adding two new bends on the southern part of the circuit.[^cite_note-F1db-14][^cite_note-MonzaNet1929-15] The resulting layout gave a Grand Prix lap of 6.300 km (3.915 mi), in use until 1954.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1940-19] The outbreak of World War II meant racing at the track was suspended until 1948[^cite_note-MonzaNet1940-19] and parts of the circuit degraded due to the lack of maintenance and military use.[^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7] Monza was renovated over a period of two months at the beginning of 1948[^cite_note-F1db-14] and a Grand Prix was held on 17 October 1948.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1940-19]

### High speed oval

In 1954, work began to entirely revamp the circuit, resulting in a 5.750 km (3.573 mi) course, and a new 4.250 km (2.641 mi) high-speed oval with banked *sopraelevata* curves (the southern one was moved slightly north).[^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7][^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20] The two circuits could be combined to re-create the former 10.000 km (6.214 mi)[^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7] long circuit, with cars running parallel on the main straight. The first Lesmo curve was modified to be made faster, and the track infrastructure and facilities were also updated and improved to better accommodate the teams and spectators.[^cite_note-F1db-14][^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20]

The [Automobile Club of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Club_of_Italy) held 500 mi (800 km) [Race of Two Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_of_Two_Worlds) exhibition competitions, intended to pit [United States Auto Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Auto_Club) [IndyCars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Championship_Car_Racing) against European Formula One and sports cars.[^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7][^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20] The races were held on the oval at the end of June in 1957 and 1958,[^cite_note-ChampCarStats-21] with three 63 lap[^cite_note-ChampCarStats58H1-22] 267.67 km (166.32 mi) heat races each year, races which colloquially became known as the *Monzanapolis* series.[^cite_note-ChampCarStats-21][^cite_note-About_Milan-23] Concerns were raised among the European drivers that flat-out racing on the banking would be too dangerous,[^cite_note-About_Milan-23] so ultimately only [Ecurie Ecosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecurie_Ecosse) and [Maserati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati) represented European racing at the first running.[^cite_note-ChampCarStats57H1-24] The American teams had brought special [Firestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firestone_Tire_and_Rubber_Company) tyres with them, reinforced to withstand high-speed running on the bumpy Monza surface, but the Maseratis' steering was badly affected by the larger-than-usual tyre size, leading to the [Modena](62418.md)-based team withdrawal.[^cite_note-ChampCarStats57H1-24]

Ecurie Ecosse's three [Jaguar D-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_D-type) sports cars used their [Le Mans](1401596.md)-specification tyres with no ill-effects, but since they raced at less than their practice speeds to conserve their tyres, they were completely outpaced. Two heats in 1957 were won by [Jimmy Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Bryan) in his [Kuzma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuzma_\(constructor\))-[Offenhauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenhauser) *Dean Van Lines Special*,[^cite_note-ChampCarStats57H1-24][^cite_note-ChampCarStats57H2-25] and the last by [Troy Ruttman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Ruttman) in the [Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Watson)-Offenhauser *John Zink Special*.[^cite_note-ChampCarStats57H3-26] In 1958 [Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Cars), [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari) and Maserati teams appeared alongside the Indy roadsters,[^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20][^cite_note-ChampCarStats58H2-27] but once again the American cars dominated the event and [Jim Rathmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Rathmann_\(race_car_driver\)) won the three races in a Watson-Offenhauser car.[^cite_note-ChampCarStats-21]

Formula One used the 10.000 km (6.214 mi) high speed track in the [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Italian_Grand_Prix), [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Italian_Grand_Prix), [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Italian_Grand_Prix) and [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Italian_Grand_Prix) Grands Prix.[^cite_note-F1.comHistory-7][^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20] [Stirling Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling_Moss) and [Phil Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Hill) both won twice in this period, with Hill's win at Monza making him the first American to win a Formula One race. The combined circuit was not used for 3 years because during the 1956 event the Ferraris and Maseratis were suffering mechanically on the banking, and the combined circuit was used again in 1960 because Ferrari's main strength that year was straight-line speed and the organizers wanted to maximise this advantage.[^cite_note-History_F1-28] The 1961 race saw the death of [Wolfgang von Trips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_von_Trips) and fifteen spectators when a collision with [Jim Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Clark)'s [Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Lotus) sent von Trips' car airborne and into the barriers at the approach to the Parabolica.[^cite_note-F1db-14][^cite_note-History_F1-28]

Although the accident did not occur on the oval section of the track, the high speeds were deemed unsafe and F1 use of the oval was ended. Another attempt was made to use the combined circuit for the 1963 race, but the extremely bumpy nature and poor overall quality of the concrete banking saw some cars suffer mechanically. The teams protested and threatened to leave unless only the road circuit was used, which is ultimately what happened.[^cite_note-Banking_F1-29] Future Grands Prix were held on the shorter road circuit,[^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20] with the banking appearing one last time in the film *[Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_\(1966_film\))*.[^cite_note-Banking_F1-29] New safety walls, rails and fences were added before the next race and the refuelling area was moved further from the track. Chicanes were added before both bankings in 1966, and another fatality in the 1968 1000 km Monza race led to run-off areas added to the curves, with the track layout changing the next year to incorporate permanent chicanes before the banked curves – extending the track length by 100 m (110 yd).[^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20]

The combined circuit held its last major race in 1969 with the 1000 km of Monza, the event moving solely to the road circuit the next year; the banking has never been used again for any major races.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20] The banking still exists, albeit in a decayed state in the years since the last race, escaping demolition in the 1990s. It is used once a year for the Monza Rally, which served part of the [2021 World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_World_Rally_Championship), which was the first FIA championship event since 1969.[^cite_note-Banking_F1-29] The banked oval was used several times for record breaking until the late 1960s, although the severe bumping was a major suspension and tyre test for the production cars attempting endurance records, such as the [Ford Corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Corsair) GT which in 1964 captured 13 records.[^cite_note-30]

### Circuit changes and modernisation



The race accident at the 1973 Nations motorcycle Grand Prix, where [Renzo Pasolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Pasolini) and [Jarno Saarinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarno_Saarinen) died

Both car and [Grand Prix motorcycle racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing) were regular attractions at Monza.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1955-20] These races involved drivers constantly slipstreaming competing cars, which produced several close finishes, such as in [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Italian_Grand_Prix), [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Italian_Grand_Prix), and [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Italian_Grand_Prix).

As the speed of the machines increased, two [chicanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicane) were added in 1972 to reduce racing speeds – the Variante del Rettifilo at the middle of the start/finish straight,[^cite_note-MonzaNet1972-31] and the Variante *Ascari*.[^cite_note-F1db-14] This resulted in a new circuit length of 5.755 km (3.576 mi).[^cite_note-MonzaNet1972-31] Grand Prix motorcycles continued to use the un-slowed road track until two serious accidents resulted in five deaths, including [Renzo Pasolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Pasolini) and [Jarno Saarinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarno_Saarinen),[^cite_note-MonzaNet1972-31] in 1973, and motorcycle racing did not return to Monza until 1981.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1972-31] The 1972 chicanes were soon seen to be ineffective at slowing cars; the *Vialone* was remade in 1974,[^cite_note-MonzaNet1972-31] the other, *Curva Grande* in 1976,[^cite_note-F1db-14] and a third also added in 1976 before the *Lesmo*, with extended run-off areas.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1972-31] The Grand Prix lap after these alterations was increased to 5.800 km (3.604 mi) long.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1972-31]

With technology still increasing vehicle speeds the track was modified again in 1979 with added safety measures such as new kerbs, extended run-off areas and tyre-barriers to improve safety for drivers off the track.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1979-32] The infrastructure was also improved, with pits able to accommodate 46 cars, and an upgraded paddock and scrutineering facilities.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1979-32] These changes encouraged world championship motorcycling to return in 1981, but further safety work was undertaken through the 1980s.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1979-32] Also in the 1980s the podium, paddock and pits complex, stands,[^cite_note-MonzaNet1979-32][^cite_note-MonzaNet1989-33] and camp site were either rebuilt or improved.[^cite_note-F1db-14]

As motorsport became more safety conscious following the deaths of [Ayrton Senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrton_Senna) and [Roland Ratzenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Ratzenberger) in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Formula_One_World_Championship) at the [Imola circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodromo_Enzo_e_Dino_Ferrari), the three main long curves were "squeezed" in order to install larger gravel traps, shortening the lap to 5.770 km (3.585 mi).[^cite_note-MonzaNet1989-33] In [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Formula_One_World_Championship) the stands were reworked to expand capacity to 51,000.[^cite_note-MonzaNet1989-33] In [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Formula_One_World_Championship), the chicane on the main straight was altered, changing from a double left-right chicane to a single right-left chicane in an attempt to reduce the frequent accidents at the starts due to the conformation of the braking area, although it is still deemed unsafe in terms of motorcycle racing. The second chicane was also re-profiled. In the [Formula 1 Grand Prix of the same year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Italian_Grand_Prix), the first to use these new chicanes, a fire marshal, Paolo Gislimberti, was killed by flying debris after a big pileup at the second chicane.[^cite_note-Clarin-13]

In 2007, the run-off area at the second chicane was changed from gravel to asphalt. The length of the track in its current configuration is 5.793 km (3.600 mi).[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] At the [2010 Monza Superbike World Championship round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Monza_Superbike_World_Championship_round), Italian rider [Max Biaggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Biaggi) set the fastest ever motorcycle lap of Monza when he rode his [Aprilia RSV4 1000 F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aprilia_RSV_4) to pole position in a time of 1\:42.121. In the Superpole qualification for the 2011 race, he improved on this lap time, for a new lap record of 1\:41.745 and his speed was captured at more than 330 km/h (210 mph).

In late 2016, work was planned on a new first bend, which would have bypassed the first chicane and the Curva Grande. Drivers were to go through a fast right hand kink and into a new, faster chicane. Work was planned for to be completed by 2017 in hopes of a renewed contract for Formula 1. Gravel would have also returned to the run-off area at the Parabolica bend.[^cite_note-Monza2017-34] However, plans for the track's change were suspended due to the track being in the historic [Monza Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monza_Park). Ahead of the [2024 Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Grand_Prix), the Monza Circuit underwent significant changes. This forced [WEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) to abandon the [6 Hours of Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_Hours_of_Monza), moving it to the [Imola Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imola_Circuit).[^cite_note-35] In addition to facility improvements, changes included a resurfacing of the entire track.[^cite_note-36]

## A lap of the circuit in a Formula One car



Satellite photograph of the circuit from 2018



The 1.12 km (0.70 mi) start/finish straight.

[^cite_note-37]

Monza has been known for its high-speed, simplistic nature (compared to "harder" circuits such as [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Bay_Street_Circuit) or [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Monaco), which are tight, unforgiving street circuits with lots of corners) thanks to its largely unaltered 1920s design. It is currently the fastest track on the Formula One calendar and has been so since 1991. Monza consists of very long straights and tight chicanes, putting a premium on good braking stability and traction. The 5.793 km (3.600 mi)[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] circuit is very hard on engines; Formula 1 engines are at full throttle for nearly 80% of the lap, with engine failures common, notably [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) in the [2006 Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Italian_Grand_Prix) or [Nico Rosberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Rosberg) in the [2015 Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Italian_Grand_Prix).

Drivers reach high speeds: 372 km/h (231 mph) during the mid-2000s [V10 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V10_engine) formula, although in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Italian_Grand_Prix) with the 2.4L [V8 engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_engine), top speeds in Formula One rarely reached over 340 km/h (210 mph); the 1.6L turbocharged [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle) [V6 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V6_engine), reduced-[downforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downforce) formula of 2014 displayed top speeds of up to 360 km/h (220 mph). The circuit is generally flat, but has a gradual gradient from the second Lesmos to the Variante Ascari. Due to the low [aerodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamics) profile needed, with its resulting low downforce,[^cite_note-Clarin-13] the grip is very low; [understeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Understeer) is a more serious issue than at other circuits; however, the opposite effect, [oversteer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oversteer), is also present in the second sector, requiring the use of a very distinctive [opposite lock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_lock) technique. Since both maximum [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(physics\)) and minimal drag are keys for speed on the straights, only competitors with enough power or aerodynamic efficiency at their disposal are able to challenge for the top places.[^cite_note-Clarin-13]

Formula One cars are set up with one of the smallest wing angles on the F1 calendar to ensure the lowest level of drag on the straights. There are only 6 corner complexes at Monza: the first two chicanes, the two *Lesmos*, the *Ascari* complex and the *Parabolica*. Thus cars are set up for maximum performance on the straights.

Cars approach the first corner at 340 km/h (210 mph) in eighth gear,[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] and brake at about 120 m (130 yd) before the first chicane—the *Variante del Rettifilo*—entering at 86 km/h (53 mph) in second gear, and exiting at 72 km/h (45 mph) in second gear.[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] This is the scene of many first-lap accidents. Higher kerbs were installed at the first two chicanes in 2009 to prevent cutting.[^cite_note-The_Official_Formula_1_Website-38]

Good traction out of the first corner is imperative for a quick lap. Conservation of speed through the first chicane is made possible by driving the straightest line, as a small mistake here can result in a lot of time being lost through the *Curva Grande* down to the *Variante della Roggia* chicane in eighth gear, at 330 km/h (210 mph).[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] The braking point is just under the bridge. The kerbs are brutal and it is very easy for a car to become unbalanced and a driver to lose control, as [Kimi Räikkönen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimi_R%C3%A4ikk%C3%B6nen) did in 2005. This chicane is probably the best overtaking chance on the lap, as it is the only one with the "slow corner, long straight, slow corner"; one of the characteristics of modern circuits.



Aerial photo of the Autodromo of Monza, with the village of Villasanta in the upper part. The city of Monza is southwards off to the right of the photo.

The *Curve di Lesmo* are two corners that are not as fast as they used to be, but are still challenging corners. The first is blind, entered at 273 km/h (170 mph) in sixth gear, apexing at 217 km/h (135 mph),[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] and has a slight banking. The second is a seventh gear entry at 278 km/h (173 mph), apexing in fifth gear at 203.5 km/h (126.4 mph),[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] and it is very important that all the kerb is used. A mistake at one of these corners will result in a spin into the gravel, while good exits can set a driver up for an overtaking move into *Variante Ascari*.

The downhill approach to Variante Ascari, particularly beneath the bridge, is known for its uneven surface. The Variante Ascari chicane is regarded as one of the circuit's more technically demanding sections and is considered important for achieving competitive laptime.

The final challenge is the *Curva Parabolica Alboreto*: approaching at 335 km/h (208 mph) in eighth gear,[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] cars quickly dance around the corner, apexing in sixth gear at 229 km/h (142 mph)[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] and exiting in sixth gear at 285 km/h (177 mph),[^cite_note-F1.comLap-4] accelerating onto the main start/finish straight. A good exit and slipstream off a fellow driver along the main straight can produce an overtaking opportunity under heavy braking into *Variante del Rettifilo*; however, it is difficult to follow a leading car closely through the *Parabolica* as the tow will reduce downforce and cornering speed.

The maximum speed recorded in a [2025](72768531.md) Formula One car was 364.1 km/h (226.2 mph), established at the end of the start/finish straight.[^cite_note-39] They experience a maximum [g-force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-force) of 4.50 during deceleration, and the track has many dramatic high to low speed transitions.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-F1.comLap-4]
