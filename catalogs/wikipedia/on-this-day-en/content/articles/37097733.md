**Elisa Longo Borghini** (born 10 December 1991) is an Italian professional [road cyclist](1755460.md), who rides for [UCI Women's WorldTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_Women's_Team) [UAE Team L'Imad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE_Team_L'Imad).[^cite_note-3]

Longo Borghini is a two-time winner of the [Giro d'Italia Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giro_d'Italia_Women) ([2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Giro_d'Italia_Women) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Giro_d'Italia_Women)), as well as two bronze medals in the road race at the [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md), taking third-place finishes in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_individual_road_race) and [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_individual_road_race).[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-5] She has also won two bronze medals in the equivalent event at the [UCI Road World Championships](7171411.md), doing so in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race) and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] She has won several races often considered [ classics of women's cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_cycle_races#Women's_events), including: [Trofeo Alfredo Binda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofeo_Alfredo_Binda-Comune_di_Cittiglio) in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Trofeo_Alfredo_Binda-Comune_di_Cittiglio) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Trofeo_Alfredo_Binda-Comune_di_Cittiglio), [Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Tour_of_Flanders_for_Women) in 2015 and 2024, [Strade Bianche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strade_Bianche_Donne) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Strade_Bianche_Women), and [Paris–Roubaix Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Paris%E2%80%93Roubaix_Femmes) in 2022.[^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-\:4-9]

## Career



Longo Borghini after winning bronze in the [women's road race at the 2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_individual_road_race)

&#x20;Longo Borghini turned professional in 2011, joining the [Top Girls Fassa Bortolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Girls_Fassa_Bortolo) team. In 2012, she rode in the [road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race) at the [2012 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in [Valkenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkenburg_aan_de_Geul), where she won the bronze medal.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-gazzetta-11]

In 2013, she won her first major title at the [Trofeo Alfredo Binda-Comune di Cittiglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Trofeo_Alfredo_Binda-Comune_di_Cittiglio).[^cite_note-12] In 2014, she won her first national championship by winning the time trial.&#x20;

### Wiggle–Honda

On 30 September 2014 it was announced she would ride for [Wiggle–Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiggle_High5_Pro_Cycling) in the 2015 season.[^cite_note-13]

In 2015, she won her first [cycling monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_monument), winning the [Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Tour_of_Flanders_for_Women).[^cite_note-\:2-8] In 2016, she won a bronze medal at the [individual road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_individual_road_race) at the Olympic Games in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_individual_road_race).[^cite_note-\:0-4] She won the national time trial championship for the second time.

In 2017, she won [Strade Bianche Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Strade_Bianche_Women) and finished 2nd at the [Giro Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Giro_Rosa), as well as winning the Italian rider classification for the third time.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-CN10-15] She also became national time trial champion for the third time, winning the road race as well.

### Trek–Segafredo

In August 2018 Longo Borghini confirmed that she would be joining the new [Trek–Segafredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidl%E2%80%93Trek_\(women's_team\)) team for 2019, after the Wiggle team had announced their demise.[^cite_note-16]

In 2020, she became the Italian national champion after winning both the road race and time trial at the national championships. She finished 3rd at the [Giro Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Giro_Rosa), winning the Italian rider classification for the sixth time.[^cite_note-17] She also rode in the [road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race) at the [2020 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in [Imola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imola), where she won the bronze medal.[^cite_note-CN-18]

In 2021, she won [Trofeo Alfredo Binda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Trofeo_Alfredo_Binda-Comune_di_Cittiglio) for the second time,[^cite_note-19] with two 3rd places at [Paris–Roubaix Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Paris%E2%80%93Roubaix_Femmes) and [Liège–Bastogne–Liège Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:1-21] In July 2021, she competed in the 137-kilometre (85-mile)-long [women's Olympic road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_individual_road_race) in [Tokyo](30057.md), and won the bronze medal.[^cite_note-Benson-22] She followed an attack by [Annemiek van Vleuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annemiek_van_Vleuten) with 2.1 kilometres (1.3 miles) to go, with Van Vleuten securing second place and Longo Borghini securing third place.[^cite_note-Benson-22] She also won the road race and time trial at the national championships for the second year in a row. She also rode in the [mixed team relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Mixed_team_relay) at the [2021 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in [Flanders](10878.md), where she won the bronze medal.

In 2022, she won her second cycling monument, winning [Paris–Roubaix Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Paris%E2%80%93Roubaix_Femmes).[^cite_note-\:4-9] In July 2022, she was named as one of the pre-race favourites for the [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Tour_de_France_Femmes) of the [Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France_Femmes).[^cite_note-23] She ultimately finished the race in sixth place overall.[^cite_note-24] She then won [The Women's Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_The_Women's_Tour) by just one second,[^cite_note-\:12-25] as well as winning the time trial at the national championships for the third year in a row. She rode in the [mixed team relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Mixed_team_relay) at the [2022 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in [Wollongong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollongong), where she won the silver medal.

In 2023, Longo Borghini won the [UAE Tour Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UAE_Tour_Women) at the start of the year.[^cite_note-26] In the [Giro Donne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Giro_Donne), she was considered a favourite prior to the race.[^cite_note-\:3-27] However, she crashed out of the race on stage 5 while 2nd overall.[^cite_note-28] The Trek–Segafredo team rebranded as [Lidl–Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidl%E2%80%93Trek_\(women's_team\)) prior to the [Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tour_de_France_Femmes), where once again she was considered a favourite. However she withdrew from the race before stage 7 due to illness while 4th overall.[^cite_note-29]

In July 2024, Longo Borghini become the first home winner of the [Women's Giro d'Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Giro_d'Italia_Women) since 2008. [^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] In August, she skipped [Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Tour_de_France_Femmes) due to the lingering effects of a training crash.[^cite_note-32]

### Team UAE ADQ

In February 2025, Longo Borghini won the [UAE Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UAE_Tour_Women), her second victory in the event, and she also won stage 3.[^cite_note-33]

## Personal life

Longo Borghini is the daughter of cross country skier [Guidina Dal Sasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidina_Dal_Sasso). Her older brother [Paolo Longo Borghini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Longo_Borghini) also competed professionally as a cyclist,[^cite_note-gazzetta-11] between 2004 and 2014. In 2023 she married [^cite_note-34] fellow cyclist [Jacopo Mosca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Mosca), who rides for the men's [Lidl–Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidl%E2%80%93Trek_\(men's_team\)) team.[^cite_note-35]
