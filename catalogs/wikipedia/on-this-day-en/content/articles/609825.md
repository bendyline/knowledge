The **Spain national football team**[^cite_note-3], nicknamed ***La Roja***, represents [Spain](26667.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) competitions since 1920, and is governed by the [Royal Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation). They are the reigning World and [European champions](32332.md#Current_title_holders), having won the most recent [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [2026](36581929.md) and the latest [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024). Spain also won the World Cup in [2010](168079.md), and won the [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League) in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals). They have competed in 12 of the 17 UEFA European Championships and have won a record four titles. They are one of two European national teams, the other being [France](250197.md), to have won all three available major titles: the World Cup, the European Championship, and the Nations League.

The achievements of Spain's team from 2008 to 2012 have led many experts and commentators to consider them one of the best squads in football history.[^cite_note-9] During this period, Spain became the first national team to win [three consecutive major titles](88184.md#International_double), including the World Cup in 2010 and back-to-back European Championships in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012).[^cite_note-10] Spain has won the [FIFA Team of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Men's_World_Rankings#Team_of_the_Year) award seven times, including every year from 2008 to 2013.[^cite_note-11]

Spain holds the world record of the longest undefeated streak in men's international football, having played 41 consecutive matches since March 2024 without a loss.[^cite_note-12] This streak gave them the highest ever [Elo rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Football_Elo_Ratings), surpassing the 1954 [Golden Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Team) from [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team).[^cite_note-13] Following their 2026 World Cup victory, Spain became the first national team to hold the men’s and women’s World Cup titles simultaneously[^cite_note-14] and the first national team to win multiple World Cup titles in the 21st century.[^cite_note-15]

## History

See also: [History of the Spain national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Spain_national_football_team)



The Spain national football team in the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Summer_Olympics) in [Antwerp](32149462.md)



Spaniards celebrating the 2010 FIFA World Cup in [Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salamanca)

Spain has been a member of [FIFA](11049.md) since its founding in 1904, although the Spanish Football Federation was not established until 1909. The first Spain national football team was created in 1920 to compete the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics) in [Belgium](3343.md). Spain made their debut at the tournament on 28 August 1920 against [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team), silver medalists at the last two Olympic tournaments. Spain won the match 1–0 and eventually finished with the silver medal.[^cite_note-16]

Spain qualified for their first [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup). They defeated [Brazil](149286.md) in their first game before losing in the quarter-finals to eventual champions [Italy](362466.md).[^cite_note-17] The [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) and [World War II](32927.md) prevented Spain from playing any competitive matches between the 1934 World Cup and the [1950 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification). At the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) in Brazil, Spain topped their group and finished fourth in the tournament.[^cite_note-18] Until 2010, this was Spain's highest finish at the World Cup.[^cite_note-19]

Spain won its first major international title when it hosted the [1964 European Nations' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_European_Nations'_Cup), defeating the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) 2–1 in the final at the [Santiago Bernabéu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Bernab%C3%A9u_Stadium).[^cite_note-Sanghera-20] The victory would stand as Spain's lone major title for 44 years. Spain was selected as host of the [1982 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), reaching the second round and [four years later](157231.md) they reached the quarter-finals before a [penalty shootout](511302.md) defeat to [Belgium](679716.md).[^cite_note-21] At [UEFA Euro 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984), they lost the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984_final) against [France](250197.md).[^cite_note-22] Spain reached the quarter-finals of the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup). The match became controversial when Italian defender [Mauro Tassotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauro_Tassotti) struck [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique) with his elbow inside Italy's penalty area, causing Luis Enrique to bleed profusely from his nose and mouth, but it was not noticed nor sanctioned by referee [Sándor Puhl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1ndor_Puhl). Had the official awarded a foul, Spain would have received a penalty kick.[^cite_note-23] In the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), Spain won its three group play matches, then defeated the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team) on penalties in the second round. They faced co-hosts [South Korea](1018627.md) in the quarter-finals, losing in a shootout after having two goals controversially called back for alleged infractions during regular and extra time.[^cite_note-24]



Spain celebrates winning the [2010 World Cup](168079.md) with a parade as they pass in front of the [Air Force](3853398.md) headquarters in [Madrid](41188263.md)

At [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), Spain won all their games in [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_Group_D). Italy were the opponents in the quarter-finals, where Spain won 4–2 on penalties. They then met [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) again in the semi-finals, beating them 3–0.[^cite_note-25] In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_final), Spain defeated [Germany](250204.md) 1–0, with [Fernando Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Torres) scoring the only goal of the game,[^cite_note-26] as Spain won their first major title since the 1964 European Championship. [Xavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavi_\(footballer,_born_1980\)) was awarded the player of the tournament.[^cite_note-27] The following year the side finished third at the [2009 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) breaking their 35-match unbeaten streak that began in November 2006 after a loss to the [United States](582488.md).[^cite_note-NYT-Spain-28] In the [2010 World Cup](168079.md), Spain advanced to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_final) for the first time ever by defeating Germany 1–0. In the decisive match against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), [Andrés Iniesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta) scored the match's only goal, coming in extra time. Spain became the third team to win a World Cup outside their own continent, and the first European team to do so. They then qualified for [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012), finishing on top of [Group I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualifying_Group_I) with a perfect 100% record.[^cite_note-BBC_2012-4] They became the first team to retain the European Championship, winning the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_final) 4–0 against Italy, while Fernando Torres won the Golden Boot for top scorer of the tournament.[^cite_note-Euro_2012:_Spain_striker_Fernando_Torres_wins_Golden_Boot_award-29]

Spain advanced to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_final) of the [2013 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), losing to hosts Brazil,[^cite_note-30] and the following year they were eliminated from the group stage of the [2014 World Cup](656933.md).[^cite_note-31] At [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) and the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md), the side were eliminated in the round of 16 in both tournaments, losing to Italy 2–0 and Russia 4–3 on penalties after a 1–1 draw.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] In the [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), held in 2021 after the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) caused delays, Spain made a breakthrough, reaching the last four of a major tournament for the first time since 2012, before losing to eventual champions Italy 4–2 on penalties after a 1–1 draw. The team finished the tournament with two wins and four draws (including two penalty shootouts).[^cite_note-34] The same year they managed to reach the [Nations League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Nations_League_final), losing against France.[^cite_note-35] In the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md), Spain finished second in their group before losing to [Morocco](1024865.md) 3–0 on penalties after a 0–0 draw, their third consecutive elimination from a major tournament in penalty shootouts.[^cite_note-36]

Spain finished first in their group in [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) without conceding a goal, and went on to defeat [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team) in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_knockout_stage#Spain_vs_Georgia) by a score of 4–1.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] They eventually eliminated hosts Germany in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_knockout_stage#Spain_vs_Germany) with a 2–1 win and defeated France in the [semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_knockout_stage#Spain_vs_France) with the same result, qualifying for their [fifth European Championship final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_final),[^cite_note-39] where they won their record-breaking fourth European title by defeating [England](9904.md) 2–1.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-bbc_2024final-41] Spain became the first team to win all seven matches in a single European Championship tournament,[^cite_note-apnews-42] while setting a new record of 15 goals scored in a single European Championship.[^cite_note-43]

Spain continued its success during the [2024–25 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League), where they defeated Netherlands in the quarter-finals and then France in the semi-finals, thus reaching their second consecutive UEFA Nations League final and their third overall appearance, with some commentators drawing similarities to Spain's previous "golden age".[^cite_note-44] With their victory over France, Spain extended their undefeated record to 23 matches.[^cite_note-45]



Spain playing against Belgium during the quarter-finals of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) at [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles)

Spain qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) after [placing first in their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_E).[^cite_note-46] During their 1–0 victory over Portugal in the [round of 16](36581929.md#Round_of_16), Spain's goalkeeper [Unai Simón](58209770.md) achieved a record sixth consecutive match without conceding a goal, surpassing the previous record of five matches set by [Walter Zenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Zenga) in 1990.[^cite_note-47] The victory matched the country's previous record of 36th consecutive undefeated streak, having last lost against Colombia in a [friendly match in March 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_in_Spanish_football). After defeating [Belgium](679716.md) 2–1 in the quarter-finals, Spain advanced to the semi-finals for the second time in their history, and the first time since achieving their World Cup title in [2010](168079.md).[^cite_note-48] Spain achieved a 2–0 victory over France in the semi-finals, advancing to the [World Cup final](75845879.md) for the second time in their history. They defeated Argentina 1–0 in the final with a goal from [Ferran Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferran_Torres) in the 106th minute, claiming their second World Cup title.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

## Team image

### Nicknames

Spain's team was known in the past by some fans as "*La Furia Española*", 'the Spanish Fury'; this nickname was originally given by a Dutch newspaper, recalling the "[Sack of Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Antwerp)" – an episode in the military history of Spain.[^cite_note-furiaespanola-52] More modernly, the team is called "*La Roja*" ('the Red One').[^cite_note-roja-53] It's called "*La Furia Roja*" ('the Red Fury'),[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-roja2-55] only by foreign journalists.

### Style of play

Main article: [Tiki-taka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiki-taka)



Spain, [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) winners



Spain's players celebrate winning the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md)



Spain, [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012) winners

Between 2008 and 2012, the team played a style of football dubbed 'tiki-taka', a systems approach to football founded upon the ideal of team unity and a comprehensive understanding in the geometry of space on a football field.[^cite_note-davies-eplindex-56]

Tiki-taka has been variously described as "a style of play based on making your way to the back of the net through short passing and movement",[^cite_note-marcotti-57] a "short passing style in which the ball is worked carefully through various channels",[^cite_note-hynter-58] and a "nonsensical phrase that has come to mean short passing, patience and possession above all else".[^cite_note-lowe_2008-06-59] The style involves roaming movement and positional interchange amongst [midfielders](548981.md), moving the ball in intricate patterns,[^cite_note-pearce-60] and sharp, one or two-touch passing.[^cite_note-tiki-taka-61] Tiki-taka is "both defensive and offensive in equal measure" – the team is always in possession, so doesn't need to switch between defending and attacking.[^cite_note-honigstein-62] Commentators have contrasted tiki-taka with "[Route One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#R) physicality"[^cite_note-marcotti-57] and with the higher-tempo passing of [Barcelona](68187.md) and [Arsène Wenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars%C3%A8ne_Wenger)'s 2007–08 [Arsenal](2174.md) side, which employed Cesc Fàbregas as the only channel between defence and attack.[^cite_note-hynter-58] Tiki-taka is associated with flair, creativity, and touch,[^cite_note-clegg-63] but can also be taken to a "slow, directionless extreme" that sacrifices effectiveness for aesthetics.[^cite_note-lowe_2008-06-59]

Tiki-taka was successfully employed by Spain to win [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), the [2010 World Cup](168079.md) and [Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012). The 2008–12 teams are regarded as being among the greatest of international teams in football history.[^cite_note-Independent-6][^cite_note-BBC_2012-4][^cite_note-BBC_Sport-5]

> They have the Barcelona "carousel" of [Xavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavi_\(footballer,_born_1980\)) and [Andrés Iniesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta) augmented by Real Madrid's [Xabi Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xabi_Alonso) in midfield.

— Phil McNulty of the [BBC](19344654.md) on the midfield players at the heart of Spain's tiki-taka passing style of play.[^cite_note-BBC_2012-4]

[Sid Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sid_Lowe) identifies [Luis Aragonés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Aragon%C3%A9s)' tempering of tiki-taka with pragmatism as a key factor in Spain's success in Euro 2008. Aragonés used tiki-taka to "protect a defense that appeared suspect \[...], maintain possession and dominate games" without taking the style to "evangelical extremes". None of Spain's first six goals in the tournament came from tiki-taka: five came from direct breaks and one from a set play.[^cite_note-lowe_2008-06-59] For Lowe, Spain's success in the 2010 World Cup was evidence of the meeting of two traditions in Spanish football: the "powerful, aggressive, direct" style that earned the silver medal-winning [1920 Antwerp Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics) team the nickname *La furia española* ('The Spanish Fury') and the tiki-taka style of the contemporary Spain's team, which focused on a collective, short-passing, technical and possession-based game.[^cite_note-lowe_2010-06-64]

Analyzing Spain's [semi-final victory over Germany at the 2010 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Germany_vs_Spain), [Raphael Honigstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Honigstein) described Spain's tiki-taka style as "the most difficult version of football possible: an uncompromising passing game, coupled with intense, high pressing". For Honigstein, tiki-taka is "a significant upgrade" of the Netherlands' [Total Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Football) because it relies on ball movement rather than players switching position. Tiki-taka allowed Spain to "control both the ball and the opponent".[^cite_note-honigstein-62]



Spain, [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) winners

Spain held possession of ball more than their opponents in all matches from 2008 to 2024, a record of 136 games.[^cite_note-tactification-65]

### Kits and crest

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Spain national football team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Spain%20national%20football%20team%20kits).

Spain's home kit is traditionally a red jersey with yellow trim, dark blue shorts, and black socks, whilst their current away kit is predominantly yellow, having been white traditionally. The color of the home socks started to alter throughout the 1990s from black to the same blue color as the shorts, then matching either the blue of the shorts or the red of the shirt until the mid-2010s when they returned to their traditional black before continuing the rotation in the 2020s. Spain's kits have been produced by manufacturers including [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) (from 1981 until 1983), [Le Coq Sportif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Coq_Sportif) (from 1984 until 1990) and Adidas once again (since 1991). Rather than displaying the logo of the Spanish Football Federation, Spain's jersey traditionally features the [country's coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Spain) over the left side. After winning the 2010 World Cup, the World Cup winners badge was added to the right side of the jersey and a golden star at the top of Spain's coat of arms. A second gold star was added after their victory at the 2026 FIFA World Cup.&#x20;

### Home stadium

Main article: [List of football stadiums in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_stadiums_in_Spain)

Spain does not have a designated [national stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_stadiums). The capital city of [Madrid](41188263.md) ([Bernabéu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Bernab%C3%A9u_Stadium) and [Metropolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitano_Stadium)), [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville) ([Pizjuán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_S%C3%A1nchez_Pizju%C3%A1n_Stadium), [Villamarín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Benito_Villamar%C3%ADn) and [La Cartuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_de_La_Cartuja)), [Valencia](63861.md) ([Mestalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestalla_Stadium) and [Orriols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadi_Ciutat_de_Val%C3%A8ncia)) and [Barcelona](4443.md) ([Camp Nou](222488.md) and [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadi_Ol%C3%ADmpic_Llu%C3%ADs_Companys)), are the four Spanish cities that have hosted more than 15 [national team matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_football_team_results#Venues_in_Spain), while also being home to the largest stadiums in the country.[^cite_note-68]

Other friendly matches, as well as qualifying fixtures against smaller opponents, are played in provincial stadia. The [2018 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) campaign included matches at the [Reino de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Reino_de_Le%C3%B3n) in [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain),[^cite_note-69] [Los Cármenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Estadio_de_Los_C%C3%A1rmenes) in [Granada](38264.md),[^cite_note-70] [El Molinón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Molin%C3%B3n) in [Gijón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gij%C3%B3n),[^cite_note-71] and the [Rico Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Jos%C3%A9_Rico_P%C3%A9rez) in [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante).[^cite_note-72]

### Media coverage

Spain's [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League), [UEFA European Qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship_qualifying) and all friendly matches, are televised nationwide by [La 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_1_\(Spanish_TV_channel\)), flagship television channel of the public broadcaster [TVE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televisi%C3%B3n_Espa%C3%B1ola).[^cite_note-73]

## Rivalries

Spain has rivalries with seven countries, four of them being Europe's "[Big Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_\(association_football\))" nations and the others with its [Iberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) neighbour Portugal, the Netherlands,[^cite_note-74] and its southern neighbour Morocco.[^cite_note-bbc.com-75]

- Their [rivalry with Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy%E2%80%93Spain_football_rivalry), sometimes referred to as the "[Mediterranean](19006.md) derby", has been contested since 1920.

  [^cite_note-76]

  [^cite_note-77]

  &#x20;Their first meeting was at the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics), in a 2–0 triumph for Spain on 2 September 1920 in [Antwerp](32149462.md).

  [^cite_note-78]

  &#x20;Although the two nations are not immediate geographical neighbours, their rivalry at international level is enhanced by the strong performances of the representative clubs in UEFA competitions, in which they are among the leading associations and have each enjoyed spells of dominance.

  [^cite_note-79]

  [^cite_note-80]

  &#x20;Since the quarter-final match between the two countries at [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), the rivalry has renewed, with its most notable match between the two sides being in the [UEFA Euro 2012 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_final), which Spain won 4–0.

  [^cite_note-81]

  [^cite_note-82]

  [^cite_note-83]

  &#x20;Spain has the advantage in the head-to-head, with 14 wins, 16 draws, and 11 losses, after 41 games, but both teams evenly split with 6 wins each when only competitive matches are counted.

- Their [rivalry with Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal%E2%80%93Spain_football_rivalry), also known as the "[Iberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) derby", has been contested since 1921.

  [^cite_note-84]

  &#x20;Their first meeting was a 3–1 triumph for Spain on 18 December 1921 in Madrid, the first ever international game for the Portuguese. Portugal had first draw in 1926, and their first win (4–1) in 1947. In this friendly rivalry, Spain dominates the head-to-head record with 18 victories, 18 draws, and 6 losses, after 42 games. They also lead Portugal in competitive matches, with 6 wins to Portugal's 1 with 6 draws.

  [^cite_note-85]

  &#x20;Their last competitive match resulted in a 1–0 win for Spain during the [2026 FIFA World Cup round of 16](36581929.md#Round_of_16).

- Their [rivalry with France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France%E2%80%93Spain_football_rivalry), sometimes referred to as the "[Pyrenean](24707.md) derby", has been contested since 1922.

  [^cite_note-86]

  &#x20;Their first meeting was a 4–0 triumph for Spain on 30 April 1922 in [Bordeaux](4097.md),

  [^cite_note-87]

  &#x20;though their first competitive meeting came in the [UEFA Euro 1984 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984_final), which France won to receive their first international trophy.

  [^cite_note-88]

  [^cite_note-89]

  [^cite_note-90]

  &#x20;Spain has the advantage in the head-to-head, with 19 wins, 7 draws, and 13 losses, after 37 games. France has a slight advantage in competitive matches, with 6 wins compared to 5 of Spain. However, Spain leads in the 21st century, with 10 victories out of 14 matches between both teams,

  [^cite_note-91]

  &#x20;including defeats for France in the semi-finals of the last three major tournaments – [2024 UEFA Euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), [2025 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals) and [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) – earning the Spanish side the nickname of France's "[Bête Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%AAte_Noire)".

  [^cite_note-92]

  [^cite_note-93]

  [^cite_note-94]

- Their rivalry with [England](9904.md) has been contested since 1929. Their first meeting was a 4–3 victory for Spain on 15 May 1929 in Madrid, the first ever English defeat outside the British Isles.

  [^cite_note-esp-eng-95]

  &#x20;The first of their nine competitive fixtures came at the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Maracanã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium), where Spain won 1–0 with a [Telmo Zarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telmo_Zarra) legendary goal.

  [^cite_note-96]

  &#x20;In another World Cup meeting in [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), Spain received England as hosts at the [Bernabéu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Bernab%C3%A9u_Stadium), in a second round stage match ended 0–0.

  [^cite_note-97]

  &#x20;One year before, in 1981, Spain achieved its first victory at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)).

  [^cite_note-98]

  &#x20;At the European Championship, they have met three times. In [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980), England won 2–1, sixteen years before a [quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996) at Wembley ended 0–0 after extra time, with England advancing on penalties.

  [^cite_note-99]

  &#x20;At the [UEFA Euro 2024 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_final), as England were aiming to win their first European title, the Spaniards won 2–1 in Berlin, clinching a record fourth Euros.

  [^cite_note-100]

  [^cite_note-101]

  [^cite_note-bbc_2024final-41]

  &#x20;The head-to-head result for Spain is 12 wins, 4 draws and 13 losses, after 29 games.

  [^cite_note-102]

  [^cite_note-esp-eng-95]

- Their rivalry with [Germany](250204.md) has been contested since 1935. Their first meeting was a 2–1 victory for Spain on 12 May 1935 in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne).

  [^cite_note-103]

  &#x20;Spain met a German side for the first time in a competitive fixture in 1966, as part of the [1966 World Cup](61629.md), where West Germany (competing separately from East Germany at the time) came back to win 2–1.

  [^cite_note-104]

  &#x20;Since the [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Germany) in 1990, Spain has remained undefeated in competitive fixtures against Germany, winning 1–0 the [UEFA Euro 2008 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_final),

  [^cite_note-105]

  [^cite_note-106]

  &#x20;0–1 the [2010 World Cup](168079.md) semi-final, 6–0 in the [2020–21 Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Nations_League_A),

  [^cite_note-107]

  &#x20;and most recently in the [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) quarter-finals, 2–1 against the hosts after extra-time.

  [^cite_note-108]

  &#x20;The head-to-head result is a tie with 9 wins each and 9 draws, after 27 games.

- Their rivalry with the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), is one that has developed in recent years. Their first significant meeting came in the “final” of the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Summer_Olympics), where Spain defeated the Dutch 3–1 to win their first silverware in history. The team would meet again in another final 90 years later, this at the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md). In a very heated contest, marked by many fouls by the Dutch side, Spain would win their first ever World Cup. Spain and the Netherlands would not see each other again until four years later in the group stage of the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md); in a rematch of the 2010 final, the Dutch handed Spain their biggest defeat in 60 years as they won 5–1. Their next competitive game came in the [2025 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League) of the Nations League, where Spain would go on to win the penalty shootout and eliminating the Dutch from the tournament.

- Their rivalry with [Morocco](1024865.md) has also developed in recent years, although not seen as intense or important as their rivalry with France, Italy and Portugal. Many people from Spain and the media do not consider Morocco rivals and this is mostly seen as a one sided rivalry. But the existence are for reasons related to both sporting and non-sporting matters.

  [^cite_note-bbc.com-75]

  &#x20;Before the 21st century, both teams had only met in two competitive matches in the [1962 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification#CAF_v_UEFA), with Spain winning both matches. It would not be until the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) that both teams would meet again in an official match after being placed together in the same group, with their match ending in a 2–2 draw which helped Spain sealed the top of the group and eliminating Morocco. Four years later, in the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), both teams met again in the round of 16, resulting in Morocco eliminating Spain in the penalty shootout. Both countries, along with Portugal, will serve as hosts for the [2030 FIFA World Cup](39367087.md), with conflict arising over who should host the final match.

  [^cite_note-109]

## Individual records

### Player records

Main articles: [List of Spain international footballers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spain_international_footballers) and [Spain national football team records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_football_team_records_and_statistics)

[Sergio Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ramos) holds the record for most appearances for the Spain's team with 180.[^cite_note-RSSSFApps-113] In second place is [Iker Casillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iker_Casillas) with 167, followed by [Sergio Busquets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Busquets) with 143.[^cite_note-RSSSFApps-113]

[David Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Villa) holds the title of Spain's highest goalscorer, scoring 59 goals from 2005 to 2017, during which time he played for Spain on 98 occasions.[^cite_note-RSSSFApps-113] [Raúl González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_\(footballer\)) is the second highest goalscorer, scoring 44 goals in 102 appearances between 1996 and 2006.[^cite_note-RSSSFApps-113]

### FIFA World Cup

Main article: [Spain at the FIFA World Cup](28100938.md)

Although often entering tournaments as one of the favorites, Spain have sometimes been perceived as underachieving at the World Cup.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] Spain's first World Cup was in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup); at that tournament, their campaign started by defeating Brazil 3–1 to advance to the quarter-finals, where they lost to hosts Italy in a replay.[^cite_note-125] Before Spain's success in 2010, their best result came in 1950, where they reached the last four. Spain were paired with the hosts Brazil, as well as Uruguay and Sweden.[^cite_note-1950WC-126] Spain managed a draw against Uruguay but defeats from Brazil and Sweden meant that Spain would end up in fourth place.[^cite_note-1950WC-126] At the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) held in South Africa, Spain became world champions for the first time after defeating the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) 1–0 in the final, becoming the eighth country to win the World Cup.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

Sixteen years after their victory in South Africa, Spain would reach their second final in history at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), where they went on to win 1–0 against [Argentina](454699.md).

### UEFA European Championship

Main article: [Spain at the UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_at_the_UEFA_European_Championship)

Spain have won the most UEFA European Championships, with four titles.[^cite_note-130] *La Roja* are also the only nation to date to have won consecutive championships. They have hosted the tournament once, in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1964) (one city was used to host games at [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020)) and have appeared in a total of twelve tournaments.

The team won their first international trophy on home soil in 1964, defeating the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) 2–1.[^cite_note-Sanghera-20][^cite_note-131] Spain would reach the final twenty years later in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984_final), where they would lose the final to [France](250197.md). Spain would not reach the final again until [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_final), where they would defeat Germany 1–0. Four years later, Spain earned back-to-back titles, comprehensively defeating [Italy](362466.md) 4–0 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_final) in Kyiv.

It would take *La Roja* twelve years to reach another European final, doing so in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_final) against [England](9904.md), a match they won 2–1 to stand alone as the most successful national team in the competition's history with four titles, while at the same time becoming the first team ever to win all seven matches in a single tournament.[^cite_note-apnews-42]

### UEFA Nations League

Since the inaugural [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League), *La Roja* have remained in League A and have reached the [UEFA Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League_Finals) on three occasions, only failing to do so in the inaugural season. At the [2021 finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals), Spain won their semi-final after defeating Italy 2–1 but lost to France in the final.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133] In the following campaign, *La Roja* would again reach the final thanks to another win against Italy.[^cite_note-UNL22-134] Spain would then beat [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) on penalties after a 0–0 draw.[^cite_note-UNL22-134] Spain reached a record third consecutive final at the [2025 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals) by defeating the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals, and [France](250197.md) in the semi-finals 5–4. In the first ever [Iberian final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal%E2%80%93Spain_football_rivalry), Spain faced [Portugal](576328.md) in Munich. Despite dominating the game, Spain were unable to break through the Portuguese defense. The score ended 2–2 after 90 minutes and extra time, and Spain lost on penalties.

### CONMEBOL–UEFA Cup of Champions

After winning a record fourth European Championship in 2024, Spain qualified to their first ever CONMEBOL–UEFA Cup of Champions, where they were scheduled to face [Argentina](454699.md), the champions of the [2024 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). However, the match was later cancelled after both sides could not come to an agreement as to which stadium would host the game.

### FIFA Confederations Cup

Main article: [Spain at the FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_at_the_FIFA_Confederations_Cup)

Spain made two appearances at the FIFA Confederations Cup. Their first appearance came in 2009 as European champions when they won a third place medal,[^cite_note-135] after losing 2–0 to the United States in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-136] At the next edition, Spain qualified as both World and European champions.[^cite_note-137] *La Roja* reached the final in Brazil, but lost 3–0 to the hosts.[^cite_note-138]
