**Antoine Serlom Semenyo** (born 7 January 2000) is a professional [footballer](10568.md) who plays as a [winger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winger_\(association_football\)) or [wide midfielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_midfielder) for [Premier League](11250.md) club [Manchester City](165813.md). Born in England, he plays for the [Ghana national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_men's_national_association_football_team).

He made his professional debut for [Bristol City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_City_F.C.) in the [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) in 2018, and became a regular player from 2020 after loans to other clubs. In January 2023, he transferred to [Bournemouth](451121.md) of the Premier League for a £10 million fee. Three years later, he signed for Manchester City for a reported fee of £64 million and won the [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) and [FA Cup](11237.md) in his first half-season, scoring the only goal of the [latter final](80461614.md).

Semenyo made his international debut for Ghana in 2022. He was selected for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [2022](17742072.md) and [2026](36581929.md), and the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).

## Club career

Semenyo had unsuccessful trials for London clubs [Arsenal](2174.md), [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) and [Millwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.).[^cite_note-5] At the age of 15, he was rejected by [Crystal Palace](385313.md) after an eight-week trial. He initially decided to give up on becoming a professional footballer before [Dave Hockaday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Hockaday) convinced him to join his youth academy program at [South Gloucestershire and Stroud College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Gloucestershire_and_Stroud_College).[^cite_note-sgscol-6] Semenyo's development saw [Birmingham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_F.C.), Crystal Palace and [Bristol City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_City_F.C.) show interest in signing him.[^cite_note-sundayleague-7] Prior to signing for Bristol City's academy in September 2017,[^cite_note-sgscol-6][^cite_note-8] Semenyo had a brief spell at [Hellenic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Football_League) side [Highworth Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highworth_Town_F.C.), playing the final 13 minutes of an [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_FA_Cup_qualifying_rounds) preliminary round tie against [Marlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlow_F.C.) on 20 August 2017.[^cite_note-9]

### Bristol City

#### Early appearances and loans

Semenyo signed his first professional contract in January 2018 and joined [Bath City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_City_F.C.) on a short-term loan.[^cite_note-10] He scored six goals from 16 appearances for the [National League South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_South) club before returning to Bristol City.[^cite_note-11] He made his Bristol City debut on the final day of the [2017–18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Bristol_City_F.C._season) season coming on as a second-half substitute for [Lloyd Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Kelly) in the 3–2 loss to [Sheffield United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.) at [Ashton Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashton_Gate_Stadium).[^cite_note-BBC_Football-12]

On 18 July 2018, Semenyo joined [EFL League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_Two) club [Newport County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_County_A.F.C.) on a season-long loan.[^cite_note-13] He made his Newport debut in a 3–0 defeat against [Mansfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_Town_F.C.) on 4 August 2018 as a second-half substitute[^cite_note-14] and scored his first goal for the club in a 4–1 [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_EFL_Cup) first round win over [Cambridge United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_United_F.C.).[^cite_note-15] At Newport, he began to play on the wing rather than his original position of striker.[^cite_note-sundayleague-7] On 6 January 2019, he played the full 90 minutes as Newport knocked [Premier League](11250.md) club [Leicester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_City_F.C.) out of the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_FA_Cup) in the third round with a 2–1 win.[^cite_note-16] He also played the entirety of the 1–1 draw away at [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) side [Middlesbrough](298603.md) on 26 January.[^cite_note-17] The following day, he was recalled by Bristol city after making 32 appearances in all competitions for the Welsh club.[^cite_note-18]

Upon his return to Bristol City he was allocated the shirt number 18 and named as a substitute for the [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_EFL_Championship) match away to [Blackburn Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.) on 9 February 2019. On 2 March, he appeared as a 53rd-minute substitute for [Callum O'Dowda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callum_O'Dowda) in 1–1 draw with [Preston North End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston_North_End_F.C.) and, seven days later, he made his first start for the club in a 1–0 home loss to [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_A.F.C.).[^cite_note-19] On 27 April 2019, Semenyo controversially received his first career red card against [Derby County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.) for a challenge on [Tom Huddlestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Huddlestone).[^cite_note-20] In June 2019, he signed a new four-year contract with the club.[^cite_note-21]

After making eleven appearances for Bristol City, in the first half of the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Bristol_City_F.C._season), Semenyo was linked with a loan move to [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland), [Doncaster Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster_Rovers_F.C.) and a return to Newport County.[^cite_note-22] On 31 January 2020, Semenyo joined [EFL League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_One) club Sunderland on loan for the remainder of the season.[^cite_note-23] He made seven appearances for Sunderland without scoring, making his only start in his final appearance at [Bristol Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Rovers_F.C.) on 10 March 2020. After the season was curtailed by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), he returned to Bristol City.[^cite_note-24]

#### First team breakthrough

Semenyo began the [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Bristol_City_F.C._season) by scoring his first goal for Bristol City in an [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) first round tie against [Exeter City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_City) on 5 September 2020.[^cite_note-25] Eleven days later, he assisted goals for [Chris Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Martin_\(footballer,_born_1988\)) and [Kasey Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasey_Palmer), before scoring himself in a 4–0 win over [Northampton Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_Town_F.C.) in the second round of the same competition.[^cite_note-26]

During November 2020, he recorded assists in three consecutive [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) matches against [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_A.F.C.), [Cardiff City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_City_F.C.) and [Derby County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.). He ended the season having played fifty competitive games, scoring five and assisting seven goals.[^cite_note-27] He was named Bristol City's Young Player of the Year on 10 May 2021.[^cite_note-28]

During the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Bristol_City_F.C._season), Semenyo registered eight goals and twelve assists in 32 games across all competitions.[^cite_note-29] He won the [EFL Championship Player of the Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship_Player_of_the_Month) award for January 2022 after scoring three goals and getting three assists.[^cite_note-30]

Semenyo began the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Bristol_City_F.C._season) by scoring in a 3–1 [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_EFL_Cup) first round win over [Wycombe Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wycombe_Wanderers_F.C.) on 24 August.[^cite_note-31] He followed this up by scoring in his first two [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) appearances of the season, against [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool_F.C.) on 27 August and Huddersfield Town on 31 August.[^cite_note-32]

During January 2023, he scored in three consecutive league matches against [Coventry City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.),[^cite_note-33] [Birmingham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_F.C.)[^cite_note-34] and [Blackburn Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.),[^cite_note-35] as well as an [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_FA_Cup) third round tie against [Swansea City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea_City_A.F.C.).[^cite_note-36] This form saw reported interest from [Premier League](11250.md) clubs [Southampton](81576.md) and Crystal Palace, as well as two rejected offers from [Bournemouth](451121.md).[^cite_note-37]



Semenyo playing for Bournemouth in 2025

### Bournemouth

On 27 January 2023, Semenyo completed a transfer to Bournemouth worth £10 million on a four-and-a-half-year contract.[^cite_note-38] He made his debut for the club in 1–0 [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Premier_League) loss at [Brighton & Hove Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_&_Hove_Albion_F.C.) on 4 February[^cite_note-39] and scored his first goal on 30 April 2023 in a 4–1 home win over [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.).[^cite_note-40]

On 19 August 2023, Bournemouth's first away match of the [2023–24 Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Premier_League) season, Semenyo gave the team a 1–0 lead over [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) at [Anfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfield) to register his second goal for the club. On 28 October, he scored in Bournemouth's first league win of the season and first under [Andoni Iraola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andoni_Iraola) as they beat [Burnley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_F.C.) 2–1 in matchday 10 of the Premier League. He ended his first full Premier League season with eight goals and two assists from 33 appearances.[^cite_note-41]

On 8 July 2024, he signed a new contract with the club until 2029.[^cite_note-42] Semenyo began the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_AFC_Bournemouth_season) by scoring an 86th-minute equaliser in a 1–1 draw with [Nottingham Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.) on 17 August.[^cite_note-43] Two weeks later he scored Bournemouth's first goal of a 3–2 comeback win over [Everton](91155.md) at [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park).[^cite_note-44] On 2 November 2024, he scored in Bournemouth's 2–1 win over Premier League champions [Manchester City](165813.md) at [Dean Court](1821645.md), ending City's eleven-month unbeaten run in the competition.[^cite_note-45] His fifth goal of the season came in a 3–0 win over [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) at [Old Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trafford) on 22 December.[^cite_note-46]

On 25 January 2025, he scored the fifth goal of Bournemouth's 5–0 win over Nottingham Forest, extending the club's unbeaten run in the Premier League to eleven matches.[^cite_note-47] On 14 April, Semenyo scored his tenth goal of the season to give Bournemouth a 1–0 win over [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) and end their run of six matches without a Premier League win. His goal came after 53 seconds of the match, making it Bournemouth's third-fastest Premier League goal.[^cite_note-48] On 25 May, Semenyo scored both goals in a 2–0 win over [Leicester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_City_F.C.) to end the season with a personal best eleven [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Premier_League) goals.[^cite_note-49]

On 1 July 2025, Semenyo signed a contract extension at Bournemouth until 2030.[^cite_note-50]

On 15 August, Semenyo scored two goals away against [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) in the opening match of the [2025–26 Premier League](77711894.md) season. His goals came in the 64th and 76th minutes to tie the match at 2–2 after Bournemouth had trailed 2–0 to the league champions, however, the match ended in a 4–2 defeat with Liverpool scoring two late goals.[^cite_note-51] He continued his strong start to the season with goals against Brighton and Hove Albion[^cite_note-52] and Leeds United[^cite_note-53] in September, before scoring two goals and assisting [Justin Kluivert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Kluivert) in a 3–1 win over Fulham on 3 October, taking him to six goals from the opening seven Premier League matches.[^cite_note-54]

After going seven matches without scoring, Semenyo scored in a 4–4 draw with Manchester United on 15 December.[^cite_note-55] He followed this up with goals against Burnley[^cite_note-56] and [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C.) in Bournemouth's next two matches.[^cite_note-57]

On 7 January 2026, his 26th birthday, having been strongly linked with a move to Manchester City in the January [transfer window](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_window), Semenyo scored in his final game for Bournemouth; a last-minute winner in a 3–2 win against [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) at [Vitality Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitality_Stadium), ending the Cherries' eleven-match run without a win.[^cite_note-58]

### Manchester City

On 9 January 2026, Manchester City announced the signing of Semenyo on a five-and-a-half-year contract for a reported fee of £64 million.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] He made an immediate impact on his debut the following day, scoring once and providing an assist in a 10–1 FA Cup victory over Exeter City.[^cite_note-61] Three days later, he opened the scoring in a 2–0 away win against [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) in the first leg of the EFL Cup semi-final, helping City progress 5–1 on aggregate to reach the [2026 EFL Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_EFL_Cup_final).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

On 24 January, Semenyo scored his first Premier League goal for City in a 2–0 victory over [Wolverhampton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers_F.C.).[^cite_note-64] His impressive run of form throughout February earned him the Premier League Player of the Month award.[^cite_note-65] On 22 March, he featured in Manchester City's 2–0 victory over Arsenal in the EFL Cup final, securing his first trophy with the club.[^cite_note-66]

On 16 May, Semenyo scored the decisive goal in the [2026 FA Cup final](80461614.md) against [Chelsea](7473.md), producing a backheel finish to seal a 1–0 victory and deliver the trophy to Manchester City.[^cite_note-67]

## International career

Semenyo has said that his family were passionate supporters of the Ghana national team. He remembers watching Ghana’s 2010 World Cup quarter-final defeat, which he says left a lasting impression.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

Semenyo was called up to the [Ghana national team](1029620.md) on 26 May 2022[^cite_note-70] and made his debut for the *Black Stars* in a 3–0 [2023 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) win over [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_national_football_team) on 1 June.[^cite_note-71]

On 14 November 2022, Semenyo was included in Ghana's 26-man squad for the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar.[^cite_note-72] He scored his first international goal in the team's final warm-up match for the tournament, a 2–0 win over [Switzerland](681862.md) on 17 November.[^cite_note-73] At the tournament finals, he made two substitute appearances in the group matches against [Portugal](576328.md)[^cite_note-74] and [Uruguay](679782.md).[^cite_note-75]

Semenyo's first competitive goal for Ghana came in the sixth minute of stoppage time in an [Africa Cup of Nations qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification_Group_E) against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team) on 23 March 2023, giving the *Black Stars* a 1–0 win.[^cite_note-76]

In January 2024, he was named in Ghana's squad for the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-77] He started all three group matches at the tournament as Ghana were eliminated with two draws and a loss.[^cite_note-78]

On 2 June 2026, Semenyo was named by Ghana's coach [Carlos Queiroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Queiroz) in his list of 26 players in order to compete in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-79]

## Personal life

Semenyo was born in England and is of Ghanaian descent. Semenyo's father, Larry, is a former footballer, having played as a midfielder for [Okwawu United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okwawu_United_S.C.) in the [Ghana Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Premier_League).[^cite_note-80] Antoine also holds French citizenship from his mother.[^cite_note-81] Semenyo's younger brother [Jai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Semenyo) is also a professional footballer, currently at [Championnat National 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championnat_National_1) side [FC Lorient B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient_B).[^cite_note-82]

On 18 November 2025, Semenyo proposed to his long-time girlfriend, Jordeen Buckley.[^cite_note-83]

Semenyo is a [Christian](5211.md). He has said, “God is my inspiration. \[He] is the only person I fear.”[^cite_note-84]
