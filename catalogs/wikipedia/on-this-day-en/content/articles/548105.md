**Muhammad Yunus** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): মুহাম্মদ ইউনূস; born 28 June 1940) is a Bangladeshi economist and statesman. Yunus pioneered the modern concept of [microcredit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcredit) and [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance), for which he was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md) in 2006. He is the founder of [Grameen Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Bank) and the first [Bangladeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi) to win the Nobel Peace Prize. Following the [July Uprising](78109234.md), he was appointed as the 5th [chief adviser of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_adviser_of_Bangladesh), the head of the interim government, serving from 2024 to 2026.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Born in [Hathazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathazari_Upazila), [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_District), Yunus passed his matriculation and intermediate examinations from [Chittagong Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Collegiate_School) and [Chittagong College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_College), respectively. He completed his BA from [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka) and joined as a lecturer in Chittagong College. He obtained his PhD in economics from [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) in the United States.

After the [famine of 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_famine_of_1974), Yunus started to work on poverty alleviation in Bangladesh. He began experimenting with microfinance in the late 1970s. In 1983, the Grameen Bank was established. The success of the Grameen microfinance model inspired similar efforts in about 100 [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world) and even in developed countries including the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-thehindu_2006-3]

Yunus was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 2006 for founding the Grameen Bank and pioneering the concepts of microcredit and microfinance.[^cite_note-4] Yunus has received several other national and international honors, including the United States [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom) in 2009 and the [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal) in 2010.[^cite_note-5]

In 2012, Yunus became [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_\(education\)) of [Glasgow Caledonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Caledonian_University) in Scotland, a position he held until 2018.[^cite_note-BBC-Glasgow-6][^cite_note-7] Previously, he was a professor of economics at [Chittagong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_University) in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-8] He published several books related to his finance work. He is a founding board member of Grameen America and [Grameen Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Foundation), which supports microcredit.[^cite_note-9] Yunus also served in the board of directors of the [United Nations Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Foundation), a public charity to support UN causes, from 1998 to 2021.[^cite_note-test-10]

Following the [resignation of Sheikh Hasina](77539154.md), President [Mohammed Shahabuddin](73010562.md) gave Yunus a mandate to form an [interim government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunus_Ministry), acceding to calls from [Students Against Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_Against_Discrimination) for his appointment.[^cite_note-Wapo20240806-11] His government appointed a [Constitutional Reform Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Reform_Commission_\(Bangladesh\)) and [National Consensus Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Consensus_Commission) to draft revisions to the [Constitution of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh), and also hold the [2026 general election](72614737.md) and [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bangladeshi_constitutional_referendum) on the [July Charter](80557195.md) alongside the election.[^cite_note-12]

His name was listed in *[The 500 Most Influential Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_500_Most_Influential_Muslims)* in 2024.[^cite_note-13] In 2025, he was named one of Time Magazine's [100 Most Influential People in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100).[^cite_note-14]

## Early life and education



Yunus as a [Boy Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scout), in 1953

The third of nine children,[^cite_note-15] Muhammad Yunus was born on 28 June 1940 to a [Bengali Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslim) family of [Saudagars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudagar_\(surname\)) in the village of Bathua, by the [Kaptai road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptai_road) at [Hathazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathazari_Upazila) in the [Chittagong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_District) of [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency) (now in Bangladesh).[^cite_note-\$27-16][^cite_note-Hero-17] His father was Haji Muhammad Dula Mia Saudagar, a [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) jeweller, and his mother was Sufia Khatun. His early childhood was spent in the village. In 1944, his family moved to the city of [Chittagong](380451.md), and he moved from his village school to Lamabazar Primary School.[^cite_note-\$27-16][^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] By 1949, his mother was afflicted with psychological illness.[^cite_note-Hero-17] Later, he passed the matriculation examination from [Chittagong Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Collegiate_School) ranking 16th out of 39,000 students in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan).[^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] During his school years, he was an active [Boy Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Boy_Scouts_Association), and travelled to [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) and India in 1952, and to [Canada in 1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_World_Scout_Jamboree) to attend [Jamborees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamboree_\(Scouting\)).[^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] Later, while Yunus was studying at [Chittagong College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_College), he became active in cultural activities and won awards for drama.[^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] In 1957, he enrolled in the Department of Economics at [Dhaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_University) and completed his BA in 1960 and [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1961.[^cite_note-edd-19][^cite_note-edd2-20]

## Career

Main article: [Grameen Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Bank)

### Early career



Yunus visiting [Chittagong Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Collegiate_School), in 2003

After his graduation, Yunus joined the Bureau of Economics at [Dhaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka) as a research assistant to economists [Nurul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurul_Islam_\(economist\)) and [Rehman Sobhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehman_Sobhan).[^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] Later, he was appointed lecturer in economics in Chittagong College in 1961.[^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] During that time, he also set up a profitable [packaging factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packaging_and_labeling) on the side.[^cite_note-Hero-17] In 1965, he received a [Fulbright scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_scholarship) to study in the United States. He obtained his PhD in economics from [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) through their Graduate Program in Economic Development in 1969.[^cite_note-edd-19][^cite_note-edd2-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-vanb-22] From 1969 to 1972, Yunus was an assistant professor of economics at [Middle Tennessee State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Tennessee_State_University) in [Murfreesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murfreesboro,_Tennessee).[^cite_note-edd-19][^cite_note-edd2-20]

During the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) in 1971, Yunus founded a citizen's committee and ran the Bangladesh Information Center, with other Bangladeshis in the United States, to raise support for liberation.[^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] He also published the *Bangladesh Newsletter* from his home in [Nashville](22018.md). After the War, he returned to Bangladesh and was appointed to the government's Planning Commission headed by Nurul Islam. However, he found the job boring and resigned to join [Chittagong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_University) as head of the Economics department.[^cite_note-B2P-23] After observing the [famine of 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_famine_of_1974), he became involved in [poverty reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_reduction) and established a rural economic programme as a research project. In 1975, he developed a *Nabajug Tebhaga Khamar* (lit. 'New Era Three-share Farm') which the government adopted as the Packaged Input Programme.[^cite_note-Palo-interview-18] To make the project more effective, Yunus and his associates proposed the *Gram Sarkar* (lit. 'Village government') programme.[^cite_note-Ramon-24] Introduced by President [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman) in the late 1970s, the government formed 40,392 village governments as a fourth layer of government in 2003. On 2 August 2005, in response to a petition by [Bangladesh Legal Aid and Services Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Legal_Aid_and_Services_Trust) (BLAST), the High Court declared village governments illegal and unconstitutional.[^cite_note-25]

His concept of [microcredit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcredit) for supporting innovators in multiple developing countries also inspired programmes such as the Info lady Social Entrepreneurship Programme.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### Grameen and microfinance

Further information: [Grameen family of organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_family_of_organizations) and [Yunus Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunus_Centre)



Grameen Bank Head Office at Mirpur-2, [Dhaka](56656.md)



Muhammad Yunus in conversation with the participants of a management workshop at the [University of St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St._Gallen), Switzerland (1995)

In 1976, during visits to the poorest households in the village of Jobra near Chittagong University, Yunus discovered that very small loans could make a disproportionate difference to a poor person. Village women who made [bamboo](39029.md) furniture had to take [usurious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usury) loans to buy bamboo, and repay their profits to the lenders. Traditional banks did not want to make tiny loans at reasonable interest to the poor due to high risk of default.[^cite_note-bbc2Mar2011-29] But Yunus believed that, given the chance, the poor will not need to pay high interest on the money, can keep any profits from their own labour, and hence microcredit was a viable [business model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_model).[^cite_note-30] Yunus lent US\$27 of his money to 42 women in the village, who made a profit of [BDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_taka) 0.50 (US\$0.02) each on the loan.[^cite_note-31] Thus, Yunus is credited with the idea of microcredit.[^cite_note-yun-32]

In December 1976, Yunus secured a loan from the government owned [Janata Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janata_Bank) to lend to the poor in Jobra. The institution continued to operate, securing loans from other banks for its projects. By 1982, it had 28,000 members. On 1 October 1983, the pilot project began operation as a full-fledged bank for poor Bangladeshis and was renamed Grameen Bank ("Village Bank"). By July 2007, Grameen had issued US\$6.38 billion to 7.4 million borrowers.[^cite_note-33] To ensure repayment, the bank uses a system of "solidarity groups". These small informal groups apply together for loans and its members act as co-guarantors of repayment and support one another's efforts at economic self-advancement.[^cite_note-Ramon-24]

In the late 1980s, Grameen started to diversify by attending to underutilized fishing ponds and irrigation pumps like deep tube wells.[^cite_note-IntFam-34] In 1989, these diversified interests started growing into separate organisations. The fisheries project became Grameen Motsho ("Grameen Fisheries Foundation") and the irrigation project became Grameen Krishi ("Grameen Agriculture Foundation").[^cite_note-IntFam-34] In time, the Grameen initiative grew into a multi-faceted group of profitable and non-profit ventures, including major projects like Grameen Trust and [Grameen Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Fund), which runs equity projects like Grameen Software Limited, Grameen CyberNet Limited, and Grameen Knitwear Limited,[^cite_note-35] as well as [Grameen Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Telecom), which has a stake in [Grameenphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameenphone) (GP), the biggest private phone company in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-36] From its start in March 1997 to 2007, GP's [Village Phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Phone) (*Polli Phone*) project had brought cell-phone ownership to 260,000 rural poor in over 50,000 villages.[^cite_note-37]

> In 1974 we ended up with a famine in the country. People were dying of hunger and not having enough to eat. And that's a terrible situation to see around you. And I was feeling terrible that here I teach elegant theories of economics, and those theories are of no use at the moment with the people who are going hungry. So I wanted to see if as a person, as a human being, I could be of some use to some people.

— Muhammad Yunus while talking about reason behind creating Grameen Bank

[^cite_note-38]

The success of the Grameen microfinance model inspired similar efforts in about 100 [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world) and even in developed countries including the United States.[^cite_note-thehindu_2006-3] Many microcredit projects retain Grameen's emphasis of lending to women. More than 94% of Grameen loans have gone to women, who suffer disproportionately from poverty and who are more likely than men to devote their earnings to their families.[^cite_note-39]

For his work with Grameen, Yunus was named an [Ashoka: Innovators for the Public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka:_Innovators_for_the_Public) Global Academy Member in 2001.[^cite_note-40] According to Rashidul Bari, the Grameen's social business model has gone from being theory to an inspiring practice adopted globally by leading universities, entrepreneurs, social business and corporations.[^cite_note-41]

The [Yunus Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunus_Centre), located in [Dhaka](56656.md), Bangladesh, is a think tank focused on social business, poverty alleviation, and sustainability. Founded in 2008 and chaired by Yunus, it promotes his philosophy of social business and serves as a resource center for related initiatives. The centre's activities include poverty eradication campaigns, research and publications, support for [social business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_business) [start-ups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company), organizing the Global Social Business Summit, and developing academic programs on social business with international universities.[^cite_note-42]

### International career

In July 2007, in [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa, [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela), [Graça Machel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gra%C3%A7a_Machel) and [Desmond Tutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Tutu) convened a group of world leaders "to contribute their wisdom, independent leadership and integrity to tackle some of the world's toughest problems."[^cite_note-43] Nelson Mandela announced the formation of this new group, [The Elders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elders_\(organization\)), in a speech he delivered on the occasion of his 89th birthday.[^cite_note-44] Yunus attended the launch of the group and was one of its founding members. He stepped down as an Elder in September 2009, stating that he was unable to do justice to his membership due to the demands of his work.[^cite_note-45]

Yunus is a member of the [Africa Progress Panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Progress_Panel) (APP), a group of ten distinguished individuals who advocate at the highest levels for equitable and sustainable development in Africa. Every year, the Panel releases a report, the Africa Progress Report, that outlines an issue of immediate importance to the continent and suggests a set of associated policies.[^cite_note-46] In July 2009, Yunus became a member of the [SNV Netherlands Development Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNV_Netherlands_Development_Organisation) International Advisory Board to support the organisation's poverty reduction work.[^cite_note-47] Since 2010, Yunus has served as a Commissioner for the [Broadband Commission for Digital Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband_Commission_for_Digital_Development), a UN initiative which seeks to use broadband internet services to accelerate social and economic development.[^cite_note-48] In March 2016, he was appointed by [United Nations Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) to the High-Level Commission on Health Employment and Economic Growth, which was co-chaired by presidents [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande) of France and [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma) of South Africa.[^cite_note-49] Following the [Rohingya genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide) in 2016–2017, Yunus urged [Myanmar](19457.md) to end violence against [Rohingya Muslims](1918632.md).[^cite_note-50]

## Early political career

For many years, Yunus remained a follower of Hasina's father, [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman), Former President of Bangladesh.[^cite_note-webs-51] While teaching at [Middle Tennessee State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Tennessee_State_University),[^cite_note-mtsu-52] Yunus founded the Bangladesh Citizens' Committee (BCC) as a response to West Pakistan's aggression against Bangladesh.[^cite_note-Stuth_transforming_cannons-53]<sup>: 74 </sup> After the outbreak of the [war of liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War), the BCC selected Yunus to become editor of its *Bangladesh News Letter*.[^cite_note-bdnews24-54] Inspired by the birth of Bangladesh in 1971, Yunus returned home in 1972. The relationship continued after Mujib's death.

### Adviser to the caretaker government

In 1996, Muhammad Yunus served as an advisor to the [caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habibur_Rahman_ministry) led by former [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Bangladesh) [Muhammad Habibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Habibur_Rahman). He was responsible for overseeing the [Ministry of Primary and Mass Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Primary_and_Mass_Education), the [Ministry of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science_and_Technology_\(Bangladesh\)), and the [Ministry of Environment and Forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Environment,_Forest_and_Climate_Change_\(Bangladesh\)).[^cite_note-cabinet_list-55][^cite_note-56]

### Nagorik Shakti

Further information: [Nagorik Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorik_Shakti)

In early 2006, Yunus, along with other members of the civil society including [Rehman Sobhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehman_Sobhan), [Muhammad Habibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Habibur_Rahman), [Kamal Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamal_Hossain), [Matiur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matiur_Rahman_\(journalist\)), [Mahfuz Anam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahfuz_Anam) and [Debapriya Bhattacharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debapriya_Bhattacharya), participated in a campaign for honest and clean candidates in national elections.[^cite_note-57] He considered entering politics in the later part of that year.[^cite_note-58] On 11 February 2007, Yunus wrote an open letter, published in the Bangladeshi newspaper *[The Daily Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Star_\(Bangladesh\))*, where he asked citizens for views on his plan to float a political party to establish political goodwill, proper leadership and good [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance). In the letter, he called on everyone to briefly outline how he should go about the task and how they can contribute to it.[^cite_note-59] Yunus finally announced that he is willing to launch a political party tentatively called [Nagorik Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorik_Shakti) (lit. 'Citizens' Power') on 18 February 2007.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] There was speculation that the army supported a move by Yunus into politics.[^cite_note-62] On 3 May, however, Yunus declared that he had decided to abandon his political plans following a meeting with the head of the [caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caretaker_government_of_Bangladesh), [Fakhruddin Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhruddin_Ahmed).[^cite_note-aljazeera-63]

## Chief Adviser of Bangladesh (2024–2026)

Further information: [Interim government of Muhammad Yunus](77721275.md), [Yunus ministry](77545215.md), [List of international trips made by Muhammad Yunus as Chief Adviser of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_trips_made_by_Muhammad_Yunus_as_Chief_Adviser_of_Bangladesh), and [List of speeches by Muhammad Yunus as Chief Adviser of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_speeches_by_Muhammad_Yunus_as_Chief_Adviser_of_Bangladesh)

### Reforms under Muhammad Yunus's administration

#### July Charter

Main article: [July Charter](80557195.md)

The administration created the [July Charter](80557195.md), which is a political declaration based on a consensus reached by 30 political parties for constitutional, administrative and electoral reforms.[^cite_note-90]

#### Creation of a bicameral parliament (senate)

Main article: [Senate of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Bangladesh)

The consensus was reached in discussions of the July Charter for the creation of two separate houses.[^cite_note-\:0-91] The [Jatiya Sangsad](577333.md) with 400 members and a newly formed [upper house (senate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Bangladesh) with 100 members elected through [Proportional representation](23298.md) and 5 members appointed by the president.[^cite_note-\:0-91]

The senate cannot introduce legislation but can review and suggest amendments and delay bills passed by the national assembly.[^cite_note-\:0-91]

#### Unified public transport system

The administration announced that all buses in the capital city of Dhaka will be brought under a unified system, following regulations and routes set by the government to create an efficient and functioning public transport system and prevent chaos.[^cite_note-92]

#### Traffic signals and road safety laws implementation

The administration implemented semi-automatic traffic signals at key intersections of the capital city and announced plans to implement traffic signals on a wide scale in [Dhaka](56656.md).[^cite_note-93]

A real time traffic monitoring centre was also launched in Dhaka, and it is equipped with advanced ITS technologies, such as real-time traffic monitoring, vehicle speed tracking, data analysis, and incident management.[^cite_note-94]

#### NHRC's emergence as a powerful organization

The administration gave the [National Human Rights Commission of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Human_Rights_Commission_of_Bangladesh) new powers in order to enable it to become a more powerful organization.[^cite_note-\:1-95]

Since 2009 under the government of [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md) banned the NHRC from investigating members of law enforcement, which was removed through the ordinance issued by Yunus's government.[^cite_note-\:1-95]

The ordinance grants the organization new duties and powers such as investigation, prosecution, legal representation of complainants.[^cite_note-\:1-95] The new ordinance allows the organization to arrest and investigate suspects regardless of rank or political position independently.[^cite_note-\:1-95] The ordinance also gives the organization new powers such as visiting and searching facilities to identify secret detention centres. It can also issue warrants to inspect prisons or other premises where enforced disappearances could have taken place.[^cite_note-\:1-95]

The rights commission can also authorise law enforcers to make arrests with the ordinance stating investigating officers cannot be part of the organization that is being investigated.[^cite_note-\:1-95]

#### Increased police accountability

Due to the [Bangladesh Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Police)'s history of committing human rights abuses, especially during the [July Uprising](78109234.md), the Yunus administration vowed to make the police an accountable and just force.[^cite_note-\:2-96][^cite_note-\:3-97]

A police reform commission was formed which suggested introducing clear-walled interrogation rooms in every police station to eliminate the risk of custodial torture. To prevent enforced disappearances, the introduction of GPS trackers and bodycams was recommended along with the introduction of an independent police commission for accountability.[^cite_note-\:3-97][^cite_note-\:2-96]

The government announced the procurement of 40,000 bodycams for the police to promote accountability for the upcoming [2026 Bangladeshi general election](72614737.md).[^cite_note-98]

#### Legal system reforms (introduction of pre-litigation mediation, digital legal aid launch)

The government launched mandatory pre-litigation mediation (formal or informal process where a neutral third party, a mediator, helps disputing parties reach a resolution before a formal lawsuit is filed in court).[^cite_note-99]

The government also launched e-family courts (a new digital system for conducting family court proceedings entirely online).[^cite_note-100]

The Yunus government also introduced a digital legal aid program which offers free legal assistance to underprivileged communities. Legal aid aims to make justice faster, fairer, and more accessible.[^cite_note-\:4-101]

Law adviser [Asif Nazrul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asif_Nazrul) stated, "If implemented successfully, the amended legal aid system has the potential to transform lives. By expanding mediation and bringing justice closer to people, especially those who cannot afford lengthy litigation, we can ensure that no citizen is left without protection of the law."[^cite_note-\:4-101]

### Breakdown in relations between Hasina and Yunus (2007)

Yunus maintained a professional relationship with Hasina. Yunus appointed Hasina—along with U.S. First Lady [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton)—as co-chair of a microcredit summit held 2–4 February 1997. In her statement she praised, "the outstanding work done by Professor Yunus and the Grameen Bank he founded. ... The success of the Grameen Bank has created optimism about the viability of banks engaged in extending micro-credit to the poor".[^cite_note-himalmag-102] The inaugural ceremony of Grameen Phone, Bangladesh's largest telephone service, took place at Hasina's office on 26 March 1997. Using Grameen Phone, Hasina made the first call to [Thorbjorn Jagland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorbjorn_Jagland), the then-Norwegian prime minister. When her conversation ended, she received another call, from Laily Begum, a Grameen Phone employee.

On 11 January 2007, Army General [Moeen U Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moeen_U_Ahmed) staged a military intervention,[^cite_note-103] and [Fakhruddin Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhruddin_Ahmed) took office on 11 January 2007 as Chief Advisor[^cite_note-oneindia-104] saying he intended to arrange free and fair elections but also to clean up corruption. While [Khaleda Zia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaleda_Zia) and Hasina criticised Fakruddin and claimed that it was not his job to clean up corruption, Yunus expressed his satisfaction. In an interview with the AFP news agency, Yunus remarked "There is no ideology here."[^cite_note-bbc18Jan2011-105] In reaction to Yunus's comments Sheikh Hasina called him a "usurer who has not only failed to eradicate poverty but has also nurtured poverty."[^cite_note-bdnews24b-106] This was Hasina's first public statement against Yunus.

The [Awami League](404861.md) government of [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md) campaigned against Grameen and Yunus. *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported, "Her actions appear to be retaliation for Mr. Yunus's announcement in 2007 that he would seek public office, even though he never went through with his plans".[^cite_note-nytimes7-107] According to *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)*, one other factor contributed to her decision against Yunus: the Nobel Peace Prize.[^cite_note-amadershomoybd-108]

It was rumored that Hasina thought that she would win the Nobel Peace Prize for signing the 1997 [Chittagong Hill Tracts peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts_Peace_Accord). On 9 March, [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Bangladesh) [Mahbubey Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahbubey_Alam) expressed the government's attitude when he said, "Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina should have been awarded the Nobel Peace Prize". He went on to challenge the wisdom of the Nobel committee.[^cite_note-google-109]

### Dismissal from and cases against Grameen Bank (2011–2013)

The second Awami League government announced a review of Grameen Bank activities on 11 January 2011.[^cite_note-Ahmed-110] In February 2011, several international leaders, such as [Mary Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Robinson), stepped up their defence of Yunus through a number of efforts, including the founding of a formal network of supporters known as "Friends of Grameen".[^cite_note-111]

On 15 February 2011, the [Finance Minister of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Minister_of_Bangladesh), [Abul Maal Abdul Muhith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Maal_Abdul_Muhith), declared that Yunus should "stay away" from Grameen Bank while it is being investigated.[^cite_note-112] On 2 March 2011, Muzammel Huq, a former Bank employee, whom the government had appointed chairman in January, announced that Yunus had been fired as managing director of the Bank.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-113][^cite_note-114] However, Bank General Manager Jannat-E Quanine issued a statement that Yunus was "continuing in his office" pending review of the legal issues surrounding the controversy.[^cite_note-115]

In March 2011, Yunus petitioned the [Bangladesh High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_High_Court) challenging the legality of the decision by the [Bangladeshi Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Bank) to remove him as managing director of Grameen Bank.[^cite_note-116] The same day, nine elected directors of Grameen Bank filed a second petition.[^cite_note-117] U.S. Senator [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) expressed his support to Yunus in a statement on 5 March 2011 and declared that he was "deeply concerned" by this affair.[^cite_note-118] The same day in Bangladesh, thousands of people protested and formed human chains to support Yunus.[^cite_note-119] The High Court hearing on the petitions, was planned for 6 March 2011 but postponed. On 8 March 2011, the Court confirmed Yunus's dismissal.[^cite_note-120]

On 2 August 2012, Sheikh Hasina approved a draft of "Grameen Bank Ordinance 2012"[^cite_note-bdnews24c-121] to increase government control over the bank.[^cite_note-bdnews24c-121] Hasina also ordered a fresh investigation into Yunus's activities and financial transactions[^cite_note-theguardian2-122] in his later years as managing director of Grameen. The prime minister also alleged that Yunus had received his earnings without the necessary permission from the government, including his Nobel Peace Prize earnings and book royalties.[^cite_note-bbc3-123]

On 4 October 2013, Bangladesh's cabinet approved the draft of a new law that would give the country's central bank greater control over Grameen Bank,[^cite_note-wsj-124] raising the stakes in the long-running dispute. The Grameen Bank Act 2013 was approved at a cabinet meeting chaired by Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina[^cite_note-125] and was passed by parliament on 7 November 2013.[^cite_note-126] It replaced the Grameen Bank Ordinance, the law that underpinned the creation of Grameen Bank as a specialised microcredit institution in 1983.[^cite_note-127] '**[The New York Times](30680.md)'** reported in August 2013:

> Since then, the government has started an investigation into the bank and is now planning to take over Grameen—a majority of whose shares are owned by its borrowers—and break it up into 19 regional lenders.[^cite_note-nytimes7-107]

Vikas Bajaj wrote on 7 November 2013:

> The government of Bangladesh has played its trump card in its long-running campaign against Grameen Bank and its founder Muhammad Yunus. Last week, legislators passed a law that effectively nationalizes the bank, which pioneered the idea of making small loans to poor women, by wresting control of it from the 8.4 million rural women that own a majority of its shares.[^cite_note-nytimes6-128]

### Legal cases and trials (2010–2024)

Yunus faced 174 lawsuits in Bangladesh, 172 of which were civil cases. Allegations included labour law violations, corruption, and money laundering, which Yunus alleged were politically motivated.[^cite_note-New_Age-129]

Hasina launched a series of trials against Yunus.[^cite_note-ti19Sep2013-130] The former put the latter on trial in 2010 and ultimately removed him from [Grameen Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Bank),[^cite_note-dawn-131] citing his age.[^cite_note-theguardian-132] The government launched the first trial against Yunus in December 2010, alleging that in 1996 he had transferred approximately \$100 million to a sister company of Grameen Bank. Yunus denied[^cite_note-133] the allegations and he was found innocent by the Norwegian government.[^cite_note-thedailystar-134] In 2013, he was tried a second time, because he had supposedly received earnings without the necessary government permission, including his Nobel Peace Prize earnings and royalties from his book sales.[^cite_note-huffingtonpost-135] The series of trials against Yunus[^cite_note-nytimes-136] puzzled figures worldwide, from the 8.3 million underprivileged women served by Grameen Bank to U.S. President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama).[^cite_note-nytimes4-137][^cite_note-cnn-138]

On 27 January 2011, Yunus appeared in court in a [food-adulteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_adulteration) case filed by the [Dhaka City Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_City_Corporation) (DCC) Food Safety Court, accusing him of producing an "adulterated" yogurt[^cite_note-139] whose fat content was below the legal minimum. This yogurt is produced by [Grameen Danone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Danone), a [social business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_business) joint venture between Grameen Bank and [Danone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danone) that aims to provide opportunities for street vendors who sell the yogurt and to improve child nutrition with the nutrient-fortified yogurt. According to Yunus's lawyer, the allegations are "false and baseless".[^cite_note-140]

On 1 January 2024, a court in Bangladesh sentenced Yunus to a six-month prison term, along with three employees from [Grameen Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Telecom) for labor law violations. However, the court granted bail pending appeals.[^cite_note-141] [Amnesty International](18947898.md) declared Yunus's conviction a "blatant abuse" of the justice system.[^cite_note-142] The conviction was overturned on 7 August 2024 following an appeal.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] He was acquitted in a graft case filed by the [Anti-Corruption Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Corruption_Commission_\(Bangladesh\)) (ACC) just four days after getting acquittal for the labour violations case.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

### Allegations of political targeting



Yunus and [Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem](7424329.md) at the 2025 World Economic Forum

In December 2010, Grameen Bank was quickly cleared by the Norwegian government of all allegations surrounding misused or misappropriated funds.[^cite_note-Ahmed-110] Yet, in March 2011, the Bangladeshi government launched a three-month investigation of all Grameen Bank's activities.[^cite_note-Ahmed-110] This inquiry prevented Muhammad Yunus from participating in the [World Economic Forum](172185.md).[^cite_note-147]

In January 2011, Yunus appeared in court in a defamation case filed by a local politician from a minor left-leaning party in 2007, complaining about a statement that Yunus made to the [AFP news agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFP_news_agency), "Politicians in Bangladesh only work for power. There is no ideology here".[^cite_note-148] At the hearing, Yunus was granted bail and exempted from personal appearance at subsequent hearings.[^cite_note-bbc18Jan2011-105]

These investigations fueled suspicion that many attacks might be [politically motivated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politically_motivated_trials),[^cite_note-theguardian-132] due to difficult relations between Sheikh Hasina and Yunus since early 2007, when Yunus created his own political party, an effort he dropped in May 2007.[^cite_note-aljazeera-63] In 2013, he faced a state-backed smear campaign that accused him of being un-Islamic and promoting homosexuality, after he signed a joint statement criticising the [prosecution of gay people in Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Uganda) in 2012 with three other nobel laureates.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

## Personal life



Yunus with his family members including [Monica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Yunus) at the Grand Hotel in [Oslo](22309.md), Norway in 2006

Yunus identifies as a [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim).[^cite_note-B2P-23] In 1967, while Yunus attended [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University), he met Vera Forostenko, a student of [Russian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_literature) at Vanderbilt University and daughter of Russian immigrants to [Trenton, New Jersey](57843.md), United States. They were married in 1970.[^cite_note-Hero-17][^cite_note-B2P-23] Yunus's marriage with Vera ended within months of the birth of their baby girl, [Monica Yunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Yunus), in 1977 in Chittagong,[^cite_note-151] as Vera returned to [New Jersey](21648.md) claiming that Bangladesh was not a good place to raise a baby.[^cite_note-Hero-17][^cite_note-B2P-23] Monica Yunus became an [operatic soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operatic_soprano) based in New York City.[^cite_note-myunus-152] Yunus later married Afrozi Yunus in 1980, who was then a researcher in physics at [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University).[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-B2P-23] She was later appointed as a professor of physics at [Jahangirnagar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangirnagar_University). Their daughter Dina Afroz Yunus was born in 1986.[^cite_note-B2P-23]

Yunus's brother [Muhammad Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ibrahim_\(academic\)) is a former professor of physics at the [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka) and the founder of The Center for Mass Education in Science (CMES), which brings science education to adolescent girls in villages.[^cite_note-154] His other brother Muhammad Jahangir (d. 2019) was a television presenter and a social activist in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-155]

## Awards and recognitions

Main article: [List of awards and honours received by Muhammad Yunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_honours_received_by_Muhammad_Yunus)

Yunus was awarded the 2006 [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md), along with Grameen Bank, for their efforts to create economic and social development:

> Muhammad Yunus has shown himself to be a leader who has managed to translate visions into practical action for the benefit of millions of people, not only in Bangladesh, but also in many other countries. Loans to poor people without any financial security had appeared to be an impossible idea. From modest beginnings three decades ago, Yunus has, first and foremost through Grameen Bank, developed micro-credit into an ever more important instrument in the struggle against poverty.

— [Norwegian Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee)[^cite_note-Nobel-156]



[Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) speaks to Stephen Hawking and on the left Yunus

Yunus was the first Bangladeshi to ever get a Nobel Prize. He established Grameen Bank in 1983, which plays a significant role in poverty alleviation in various countries of the world including [Bangladesh](3454.md). In 2006, he and the Grameen Bank he founded jointly won the Nobel Peace Prize.[^cite_note-157] After receiving the news of the important award, Yunus announced that he would use part of his share of the \$1.4 million (equivalent to \$2.24 million in 2025) award money to create a [company to make low-cost, high-nutrition food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Danone) for the poor; while the rest would go towards establishing the Yunus Science and Technology University in his home district as well as setting up an eye hospital for the poor in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-158]

Former U.S. president [Bill Clinton](3356.md) was a vocal advocate for the awarding of the Nobel Prize to Yunus. He expressed this in *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* magazine[^cite_note-159] as well as in his autobiography *[My Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_\(Bill_Clinton_autobiography\)).*[^cite_note-160] In a speech given at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 2002, President Clinton described Yunus as "a man who long ago should have won the Nobel Prize \[in Economics and] I'll keep saying that until they finally give it to him."[^cite_note-161] Conversely, *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* stated explicitly that while Yunus was doing excellent work to fight poverty, it was not appropriate to award him the Peace Prize, stating: "... the Nobel committee could have made a braver, more difficult, choice by declaring that there would be no recipient at all."[^cite_note-162]



Yunus at the Annual Meeting 2009 of the [World Economic Forum](172185.md) in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos), Switzerland

He is one of only seven persons to have won the Nobel Peace Prize, [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom),[^cite_note-163] and the [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal).[^cite_note-gpo.gov-164] Other notable awards include the [Ramon Magsaysay Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Magsaysay_Award) in 1984,[^cite_note-Ramon-24] the [World Food Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Food_Prize),[^cite_note-165] the [International Simon Bolivar Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Simon_Bolivar_Prize) (1996),[^cite_note-166] the [Prince of Asturias Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Asturias_Award) for Concord[^cite_note-167] and the [Sydney Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Peace_Prize) in 1998,[^cite_note-168] and the [Seoul Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Peace_Prize) in 2006. Additionally, Yunus has been awarded 72 honorary doctorate degrees from universities across 27 countries, and 113 international awards from 26 countries including state honours from 10 countries.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Bangladesh government brought out a [commemorative stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_stamp) to honour his Nobel Award.[^cite_note-sydney-172] Yunus received honorary doctorate degrees from universities from Albania, Argentina, Australia, Bangladesh, Belgium, Bolivia, Brazil, Canada, Costa Rica, China, Greece, India, Italy, Ireland, Japan, Korea, Lebanon, Malaysia, Mexico, Peru, Russia, South Africa, Spain, Thailand, Turkey, the United Kingdom, and the United States.[^cite_note-173] United Nations Secretary-General [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) invited Yunus to serve as an MDG Advocate. Yunus sits on the Board of [United Nations Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Foundation), [Schwab Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwab_Foundation), [Prince Albert II of Monaco Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Albert_II_of_Monaco_Foundation), Grameen Credit Agricole Microcredit Foundation. He has been a member of [Fondation Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac_Foundation_for_Sustainable_Development_and_Cultural_Dialogue)'s honour committee,[^cite_note-174] ever since the foundation was launched in 2008 by former French president [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac) in order to promote world peace.[^cite_note-175]

Yunus was named by *[Fortune Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Magazine)* in March 2012 as one of 12 greatest entrepreneurs of the current era.[^cite_note-176] In its citation, Fortune Magazine said "Yunus' idea inspired countless numbers of young people to devote themselves to social causes all over the world."[^cite_note-177]

In January 2008, [Houston](13774.md), Texas declared 14 January as "Muhammad Yunus Day".[^cite_note-178]

Yunus was named among the most desired thinkers the world should listen to by the FP 100 (world's most influential elite) in the December 2009 issue of *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy)* magazine.[^cite_note-179] In 2010, the British magazine *[New Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Statesman)* listed Yunus at 40th in the list of "The World's 50 Most Influential Figures 2010".[^cite_note-htanna-180]



Yunus with [Brazilian President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_President) [Lula Da Silva](99328.md) (right) in 2008 after winning Nobel Peace Prize

Yunus has delivered numerous lectures around the world,[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] and has appeared on popular television shows, including *[The Daily Show with Jon Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Show_with_Jon_Stewart)* and *[The Oprah Winfrey Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oprah_Winfrey_Show)* in 2006, *[The Colbert Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Colbert_Report)* in 2008, *[Real Time with Bill Maher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Time_with_Bill_Maher)* in 2009 and *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons)* in 2010.[^cite_note-186] On [Google+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google+), Yunus was one of the most followed people worldwide, with over two million followers until Google closed its operations in 2019.[^cite_note-187]

He has played a key advisory role in the [Paris 2024 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics), promoting social business principles and encouraging sustainable, socially responsible projects. His influence led to initiatives like prioritizing social businesses in public tenders and integrating social housing into the athletes' village redevelopment.[^cite_note-188]
