**Ismael Saibari Ben El Basra[^cite_note-1]** (born 28 January 2001) is a professional [footballer](10568.md) who plays as an [attacking midfielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacking_midfielder) or [forward](428681.md) for [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) club [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern). Born in Spain, he represents the [Morocco national team](1024865.md).

## Early life

Ismael Saibari was born on 28 January 2001 in [Terrassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrassa), Spain, to a Moroccan family from [Ksar el-Kebir](644641.md).[^cite_note-\:0-2] Saibari initially struggled walking because of a [clubfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clubfoot) malformation, he wore [orthopedic braces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthopedic_brace) for over a year which delayed his first independent steps until he was two years old.[^cite_note-3]

When Saibari was six, his family moved to [Willebroek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willebroek) in Belgium where he was raised. In 2020, Saibari moved to the Netherlands alone when he joined PSV Eindhoven.[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-\:0-2]

Saibari holds triple Moroccan, Spanish and Belgian citizenship.[^cite_note-5] Saibari can speak Dutch, French, English, Arabic and Spanish.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

## Club career

### Youth career

Saibari began his youth career in Spain and Belgium, spending time at [Beerschot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Beerschot_VA), [Anderlecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSC_Anderlecht), [KV Mechelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV_Mechelen) and [Genk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KRC_Genk), before moving to the Netherlands to join [PSV Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSV_Eindhoven) on 29 July 2020, signing a three-year contract.[^cite_note-6] He initially featured for [Jong PSV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jong_PSV) in the [Eerste Divisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eerste_Divisie).

### PSV

Saibari made his senior debut for PSV in the [Eredivisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eredivisie) on 1 November that year, coming on as a substitute for [Mario Götze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_G%C3%B6tze) in a 4–0 victory over [ADO Den Haag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADO_Den_Haag).[^cite_note-7]

On 19 August 2023, Saibari netted his first goal for PSV in a 3–1 away win over [Vitesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SBV_Vitesse).[^cite_note-8] Later that month, on 30 August, he scored twice in a 5–1 win over [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangers_F.C.) in the [Champions League](44220.md) play-off round, securing a spot in the competition's group stage and earning Man of the Match honors.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Having qualified for the Champions League, he made his first group stage appearance on 20 September, starting against [Arsenal](2174.md) at the [Emirates Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Stadium).[^cite_note-11]

Saibari extended his contract with the club until 2028.[^cite_note-12] Later that year, on 29 November, he scored his first goal in the Champions League in a 3–2 away victory over [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC).[^cite_note-13]

In the [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_PSV_Eindhoven_season) season, Saibari began to have more playing time and helped the team win the [2023–24 Eredivisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Eredivisie), playing in 31 matches and scoring 8 goals. In the following season, he became a regular starter and was champion of the [2024–25 Eredivisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Eredivisie), playing in 44 matches and having excellent numbers, with 15 goals and 13 assists in all competitions.[^cite_note-14] On 26 October 2025, he scored his first career hat-trick in a 3–2 away win over [Feyenoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feyenoord).[^cite_note-15] PSV eventually retained the league title in the [2025–26 Eredivisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Eredivisie) season, with Saibari being named Player of the Year with 15 goals and 8 assists.[^cite_note-16]

### Bayern Munich

Ahead of the [2026–27 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_Bundesliga), Saibari signed a five-year contract with [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) club [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) on 1 July 2026, for a reported €50 million transfer fee.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] On 28 August, he made his Bundesliga debut, providing two assists in a 5–1 victory over [VfB Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfB_Stuttgart).[^cite_note-19]

## International career



Saibari with Morocco in 2026

Saibari was eligible for [Morocco](1024865.md), [Belgium](679716.md) and [Spain](609825.md).[^cite_note-\:1-4] Saibari chose to represent Morocco, joining the youth ranks and representing them at under-15, under-16, and under-20 levels.[^cite_note-20]

Before the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), Morocco's coach, [Walid Regragui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walid_Regragui), indicated that he had contacted Saibari and other players, telling them they needed to fight for regular starting positions at their clubs to secure their place in the national team.[^cite_note-21] In 2022, Belgium's coach, [Roberto Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Martinez), contacted Saibari to persuade him to represent the Belgian national team. Saibari commented on this, saying, "I thought it was fantastic. But I told him I had chosen Morocco."[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

In June 2023, Saibari was included in the final squad of the [under-23 national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_national_under-23_football_team) for the [2023 U-23 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_U-23_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), hosted by Morocco itself,[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] where the *Atlas Lions* won their first title,[^cite_note-\:3-26][^cite_note-\:4-27] and qualified for the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-\:3-26][^cite_note-28]

On 11 December 2025, Saibari was called up to the Morocco squad for the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md).[^cite_note-cafonline.com22-29] Saibari made international news on 18 January 2026 when he was penalized by the [CAF](715265.md) with a three-match suspension and \$100,000 fine for unsporting conduct during the [AFCON final](81129500.md).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Saibari and teammate [Achraf Hakimi](52004386.md) had blocked attempts to give Senegalese goalkeeper [Édouard Mendy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Mendy) a towel for his gloves, while also helping ball boys steal towels from behind the goal during the rainy match against [Senegal](1017436.md).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] The suspension was later reduced from three matches to two matches.[^cite_note-36]

On 26 May 2026, Saibari was selected in the 26-man squad for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-37] He scored his first World Cup goal in Morocco's opening 1–1 draw against [Brazil](149286.md).[^cite_note-38] He scored the only goal in the second match, a 1–0 win over [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team), becoming the second African player after [Mohamed Salah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Salah) to score in his first two World Cup matches, and also the fastest Moroccan scorer at a World Cup, as his goal came within the opening two minutes.[^cite_note-39] On 24 June, he scored his third World Cup goal in a 4–2 victory over [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti_national_football_team), becoming Morocco's joint-highest goalscorer in the competition alongside [Youssef En-Nesyri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youssef_En-Nesyri), and the first African player to score in each of his team's first three matches at a World Cup.[^cite_note-40] In addition, he became the second African player to score three goals in a single World Cup group stage, following [Papa Bouba Diop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_Bouba_Diop) in 2002.[^cite_note-41] On 29 June, Saibari scored the winning penalty during the shoot-out against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in the [round of 32](81667041.md), helping Morocco secure a place in the round of 16.[^cite_note-42] He sustained a hamstring injury during a 3–0 victory over [Canada](723688.md) in the first knockout stage which forced him to miss the quarter-final defeat by [France](250197.md).[^cite_note-43]

## Style of play

Primarily an attacking midfielder during his time at PSV, Saibari can also play as an out-and-out striker, a central midfielder, and as a winger on the right flank. As both a goalscorer and a creator, Saibari is renowned for his positional sense and off-the-ball movement, an example being making late runs into the penalty box to create or take scoring chances. He is also known to be good at carrying the ball through midfield and being able to break lines.[^cite_note-44] [^cite_note-45] He is capable of both fulfilling his pressing duties and scoring goals with composure, while his physicality and stature makes him a capable ball-winner.[^cite_note-46]
