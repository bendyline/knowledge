***The Odyssey*** is a 2026 epic action fantasy film written and directed by [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan), who produced it with his wife [Emma Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Thomas). An adaptation of [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer)'s ancient Greek epic poem the *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey)*, it stars an ensemble cast including [Matt Damon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Damon), [Tom Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Holland), [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway), [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson), [Lupita Nyong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Nyong'o), [Samantha Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Morton), [Zendaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zendaya), and [Charlize Theron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlize_Theron). In the film, [Odysseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus) (Damon), the Greek king of [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer's_Ithaca), undergoes a long and perilous journey home after the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War) and encounters mythical beings as he attempts to reunite with his wife, [Penelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope) (Hathaway).

Nolan began writing the script in March 2024 and secured the project with [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures) by October. *The Odyssey* was announced in December. Casting took place from late 2024 through early 2025, and Damon was confirmed for the lead role in February 2025. Nolan studied several [translations of the *Odyssey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translations_of_the_Odyssey) before deciding upon a realistic interpretation of [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), drawing inspiration from the epic historical films *[Andrei Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Rublev_\(film\))* (1966) and *[Ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ran_\(film\))* (1985), as well as the films of special effects artist [Ray Harryhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Harryhausen). Filming took place on location from February to August 2025 in Morocco, Greece, Italy, Scotland, Iceland, Western Sahara, and Malta, as well as at the [Universal Studios Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios_Lot) in Los Angeles. With an estimated budget of [US\$](18717338.md)250 million, it is among the most expensive films of [Nolan's career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan_filmography), and the first feature film to be shot entirely on [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX)'s [70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film#IMAX) cameras.

*The Odyssey* premiered at the [Odeon Luxe Leicester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_Luxe_Leicester_Square) in London on July 6, 2026, ahead of a theatrical release by Universal Pictures on July 17 in the United States and the United Kingdom. It was made available in IMAX and multiple premium large formats. The film received acclaim for its direction, performances, and set pieces, though some criticized Nolan's [deviations from the source material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan's_interpretation_of_the_Odyssey). It has grossed US\$1.76 billion worldwide, becoming Nolan's highest-grossing film, the [second-highest-grossing film of 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_in_film#Highest-grossing_films), the [highest-grossing R-rated film ever](60598574.md), the [highest-grossing film distributed by Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures#Highest-grossing_films), and the [eleventh-highest grossing film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_films).

## Plot

For twenty years, [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer's_Ithaca) has been kingless since [Odysseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus) did not return from the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War). Several [suitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suitors_of_Penelope), including nobleman [Antinous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinous_of_Ithaca), aggressively court Odysseus's wife [Penelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope) to claim the throne from Odysseus and Penelope's son [Telemachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemachus). Only Penelope, Telemachus, and Odysseus' half-blind servant [Eumaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eumaeus) still believe Odysseus will return. Penelope tells Telemachus she fears attacks by murderous "[Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples)" who violate [Zeus's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenia_\(Greek\)), the custom of respect between guests and hosts. Telemachus travels with Odysseus's friend [Mentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentor_\(Odyssey\)) to [Menelaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus), King of [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta), to ascertain his father's whereabouts. Antinous learns about this and conspires to kill Telemachus upon his return and plunder Ithaca. Menelaus and his wife, [Helen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy), tell Telemachus about the war, but express uncertainty about Odysseus's fate.

Meanwhile, the goddess [Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena) appears to Odysseus on the island of [Ogygia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogygia), where the nymph [Calypso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso_\(mythology\)) is infatuated with Odysseus. Athena commands Odysseus to return home; Calypso dislikes this, but encourages Odysseus to remember his past. Years earlier, the Greek army, led by Menelaus' brother [Agamemnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agamemnon), conquered [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy) using Odysseus' stratagem, the [Trojan Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_Horse), hiding Greeks inside, with the soldier [Sinon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinon) sacrificed as a decoy. While Menelaus and Agamemnon returned home, Odysseus and his crew, including second-in-command [Eurylochus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurylochus), searched an island for supplies. However, the cyclops [Polyphemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphemus) traps them in a cave and devours several soldiers. They blind Polyphemus and escape, but hear him cry out to his father, the sea god [Poseidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poseidon). Consequently, the crew suffers harsh sea conditions. They land on [Telepylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepylus) where [Laestrygonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laestrygonians) (man-eating giants), kill most of them and sink two of their three ships. The survivors rebel, leaving Odysseus with their ship as they seek refuge on [Aeaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeaea) with the witch [Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe), who turns them into pigs. After Odysseus persuades her to change his men back, Circe advises that to reach Ithaca, Odysseus must cross the river [Oceanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanus#Geography) into [Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_underworld).

There, Odysseus meets his fallen soldiers, including Sinon's [shade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shade_\(mythology\)), who reproaches him for deceiving him, and reveals that Antinous manipulated him into taking his place in the Greek army. Next, Agamemnon's shade reveals that his wife [Clytemnestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clytemnestra) murdered him upon his return as revenge for sacrificing their daughter [Iphigenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iphigenia) to [Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis) for fair seas. Finally, Odysseus speaks with the shade of the prophet [Tiresias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiresias), who predicts that, by divine will, only Odysseus will return home. Chased from the underworld by Odysseus' fallen army, Odysseus follows Tiresias' guidance to survive encounters with the [Sirens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siren_\(mythology\)) and sea monsters [Charybdis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charybdis) and [Scylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scylla). On [Thrinacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrinacia), Odysseus warns his men not to kill the [cattle of Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_of_Helios), as Tiresias foretold Odysseus' men would die if they did, but the starving men disobey. [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus) sends a storm that sinks their ship, killing all but Odysseus. He washes ashore unconscious on Ogygia, where Calypso's lotus flowers render him [amnesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrograde_amnesia).&#x20;

After seven years, Odysseus regains his memories, builds a raft, and surrenders his fate to the storm. He washes ashore in Ithaca, where he disguises himself and bears the alias "Sinon". Eumaeus tells him that the suitors sent bandits to assassinate Telemachus and Mentor. The bandits kill Mentor, but Odysseus rescues Telemachus and kills the bandits. Odysseus's elderly dog [Argos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argos_\(dog\)) dies as he recognizes Odysseus, and Telemachus deduces Odysseus's identity. During a feast, Odysseus confronts Antinous about betraying Sinon. Antinous thrashes Odysseus before Telemachus intervenes.&#x20;

After the meal, Odysseus, still disguised, confesses to Penelope his guilt for violating Zeus's law during the [sacking of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War#Sack_of_Troy), desecrating [Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena)'s statue after killing her priestess (whose form Athena assumed in visions), and initiating the [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse). He reveals that the "sea people" were the Greeks themselves, and begs Penelope's forgiveness. Penelope announces her readiness to remarry and instructs the suitors to return at dawn.

Penelope declares she will marry whomever can string Odysseus' bow and shoot an arrow through a row of axe heads. All the suitors fail, but Odysseus succeeds, thereby revealing his identity. The hall erupts in battle. Telemachus aids Odysseus in killing many of the challenging suitors, including Antinous. After the remaining suitors surrender, Odysseus cedes his throne to Telemachus and leaves with Penelope to seek peace in exile. Sailing west to honor Odysseus' fallen soldiers, the couple expect civilization's renewal and muse that time will forget Odysseus' mistakes.

## Production

### Development and writing

Picture of director Christopher Nolan smiling at the premiere of The Odyssey in New York

Director, writer, and producer [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan) at the film's premiere in New York City

By November 2023, director [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan) had yet to decide what his next film would be after *[Oppenheimer](66850554.md)* (2023). While he was receptive to various ideas, he felt he needed to "own it completely".[^cite_note-NolanThomasTease-29] After winning several [Academy Awards](324.md) for *Oppenheimer* in March 2024, including [Best Picture](61702.md) and [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director),[^cite_note-VarietyMar2024-30][^cite_note-BelloniMar2024-31] Nolan and [Emma Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Thomas), his wife and producing partner, felt the success provided them the opportunity to explore what Nolan's next film would be.[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12][^cite_note-NolanThomasTease-29] Nolan began writing the script shortly after.[^cite_note-VarietyMar2024-30][^cite_note-BelloniMar2024-31][^cite_note-NolanThomasTease-29] Nolan was revealed in October to be developing his next film at [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures), after working with that studio on *Oppenheimer*. He was confirmed to be writing the script and producing once again with Thomas through their production company, [Syncopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncopy_Inc.), and filming was scheduled to begin in early 2025. Unlike *Oppenheimer*, which Universal acquired at auction, *The Odyssey* was set up at the studio and given a release date of July 17, 2026.[^cite_note-DevelopmentRevealTHR-32][^cite_note-DevelopmentRevealDeadline-33]

The [logline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logline) and additional details of its premise were kept largely secret compared to Nolan's previous films, which prompted much speculation about its subject matter.[^cite_note-Pattinson-34][^cite_note-Theron-35] This included rumors that it would either be a vampire [period piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_piece), a [reboot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reboot_\(fiction\)) of the 1983 helicopter action-thriller film *[Blue Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Thunder)*,[^cite_note-NolanEarlyRumors-36][^cite_note-Pattinson-34][^cite_note-Theron-35] or an adaptation of the British spy television series *[The Prisoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prisoner)* (1967–1968), which Nolan was previously attached to in 2009,[^cite_note-VarietyMar2024-30][^cite_note-DevelopmentRevealTHR-32] but all of these were debunked.[^cite_note-Pattinson-34][^cite_note-Theron-35][^cite_note-DevelopmentRevealTHR-32] Nolan later reflected the "disadvantage of never telling people what we're doing" was the invention and circulation of rumors, though he nonetheless enjoyed them.[^cite_note-NolanEarlyRumors-36] In December 2024, Universal announced Nolan's new film as *The Odyssey*, an adaptation of [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer)'s ancient Greek [epic poem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poem), the *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey)*. The studio described it as a "mythic action epic" that would be filmed worldwide.[^cite_note-TheOdysseyAnnounceDeadline-37][^cite_note-TheOdysseyAnnounceTHR-38] Nolan was previously attached to direct *[Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_\(film\))* (2004)—based on Homer's *Odyssey* predecessor the *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad)*—that [Wolfgang Petersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Petersen) had developed for [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures), before Petersen chose to direct *Troy* and Nolan subsequently made *[Batman Begins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Begins)* (2005).[^cite_note-NolanTroyConfirm-39][^cite_note-NolanTroy-40][^cite_note-NolanTroyGoyer-41] Nolan believed he was "in a little over \[his] head" for *Troy*, but felt he had become more qualified to adapt the "sweeping story" of the *Odyssey* after working on several large-scale productions during the following 20 years.[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12][^cite_note-Empire2MillionFilm-42] *The Odyssey* was expected to be the most expensive film of [Nolan's career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan_filmography) with a net budget of \$250 million.[^cite_note-IMAX70mmPresalesTHR-43][^cite_note-TeaserLeakTHR-3]

Nolan described *The Odyssey* as the largest and most challenging production of his career due to its scale.[^cite_note-Nolan60Minutes-44] He explained that he chose to adapt the *Odyssey* because it had not been adapted into a large-scale production from a major Hollywood studio, calling it "an odd gap in movie history".[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12] Nolan and Thomas described *The Odyssey* as "foundational" because it incorporates aspects of the horror, [mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_film), romance, and thriller genres of stories.[^cite_note-EmpireCoverStoryJan2026-9]<sup>: 59 </sup>[^cite_note-Empire2MillionFilm-42] Nolan also said that *The Odyssey* had influenced his previous films.[^cite_note-EmpireCoverStoryJan2026-9]<sup>: 50 </sup> He read and studied *The Odyssey* and [several of its translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translations_of_the_Odyssey), including those by classicists [Emily Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Wilson_\(classicist\)), [E. V. Rieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._V._Rieu), and [Robert Fagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fagles).[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12][^cite_note-NolanNYTJun2026-13] He wrote the script in a [non-linear fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_narrative).[^cite_note-NolanColbertTrailer-45] While the script was said to be "very faithful" to Homer's text, Nolan made some alterations, such as more prominently featuring Odysseus's faithful dog [Argos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argos_\(dog\)); more interactions between Odysseus and Telemachus; humanizing the goddess and witch [Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe) to be unsettling yet sympathetic; and complicating the reunion between [Helen of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy) and Spartan king [Menelaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus).[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12] He was interested in portraying Odysseus's cleverness and inventiveness, particularly citing his characterization in Wilson's [2017 translation of the *Odyssey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey_\(Emily_Wilson_translation\)).[^cite_note-CharacterFirstLooks-7]

To combine the *Odyssey*'s fantastical elements with Nolan's use of "tactile realism", the director chose to take a realistic approach in depicting the evidence and actions of the gods through natural phenomena once considered "supernatural" during the story's time period. Nolan called this a major creative breakthrough.[^cite_note-EmpireCoverStoryJan2026-9]<sup>: 59 </sup>[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12] Nolan cited [Andrei Tarkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Tarkovsky)'s *[Andrei Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Rublev_\(film\))* (1966) and [Akira Kurosawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Kurosawa)'s *[Ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ran_\(film\))* (1985) as major inspirations, particularly regarding its visual style and atmospheric tone.[^cite_note-NolanNYTJun2026-13] Nolan also drew inspiration from various [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology)–inspired films he watched in his youth, particularly the works of animator and special effects artist [Ray Harryhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Harryhausen).[^cite_note-Empire2MillionFilm-42] Nolan had considered casting actors to portray [Greek gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_gods) throwing thunderbolts from [Mount Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olympus), but found depicting the characters' fear of the gods' actions provided a more immersive experience than outright showing the gods.[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12]

### Design and special effects

Production designer [Ruth De Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_De_Jong) and costume designer [Ellen Mirojnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Mirojnick) returned to work with Nolan from *Oppenheimer*.[^cite_note-FavignanaFilming-68][^cite_note-DeJong-69][^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12] Mirojnick discussed her emphasis "on tactility—giving every piece of linen, leather, armour and sandal the texture and dimensions of lived history." Her costume design extended to the [Mycenaean Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece) costumes for both kings and soldiers, and the draping costumes of both [Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena) and [Calypso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso_\(mythology\)).[^cite_note-MaterialWorldDesigns-70] De Jong designed the sets and ornaments, including the 36 foot (11 metres) tall and 8,800-pound model of the [Trojan Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_Horse) that was transported in 27 crates to the seashore.[^cite_note-MaterialWorldDesigns-70][^cite_note-TrojanHorsePremiere-71]

To depict the armor, weapons, and ships, Nolan said that the production thoroughly researched the "very fragmentary archeological records" of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) during Homer's life. On the production choices, Nolan explained that the "oldest depictions of Homeric characters tend to be depicted in the manner of people living in Homer's time. So there's a pretty strong case there for portraying things that way because that's the way the first audience received the story." Nolan added that Mirojnick sought to convey through the armor worn by [Agamemnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agamemnon) that he had an elevated status by using materials more expensive to obtain, such as [blackened bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatizon), silver, and [sulfur](27127.md).[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12]

The Cyclops was achieved via a combination of [animatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animatronics), [puppetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetry), and an on-set performance by Bill Irwin, with its design taking influence from the [Francisco Goya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Goya) painting *[Saturn Devouring His Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Devouring_His_Son)*.[^cite_note-LATimesOdysseyInterview-16] The soldiers eaten by the Cyclops were small semi-edible models with an inner skeleton and burstable bags of blood.[^cite_note-72] Likewise, the sequence where [Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe) transforms Odysseus' crew into pigs was achieved through a combination of animatronics, puppetry, [makeup effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosthetic_makeup) and [stage magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_magic) techniques.[^cite_note-MortonCirceVF-73][^cite_note-OdysseyHairMakeupAllure-74] [Wētā Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C4%93t%C4%81_Workshop) provided special effects for the production.[^cite_note-WētāWorkshop-75]

### Filming



*The Odyssey* was the first feature film shot entirely on [IMAX 70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film#IMAX) cameras; a soundproof blimp (pictured) was specially constructed to mute the camera's sound during dialogue scenes.

[^cite_note-NolanNYTJun2026-13]

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography) lasted for 91 days between February 25 and August 5, 2025,[^cite_note-GQCoverStory-11][^cite_note-MoroccoFilmingStart-76] using the [working title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title) *Charlie's Tale*,[^cite_note-IcelandProductionReimbursed-77] and [wrapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrap_\(filmmaking\)) nine days ahead of schedule.[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12] [Location shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_shooting) primarily took place in six countries: Morocco, Western Sahara, Greece, Italy, Iceland, Scotland, and the United States.[^cite_note-GQCoverStory-11][^cite_note-TrailerST-78] Nolan sought to embrace the "physicality of the real world" by filming on location to help inform the storytelling and capture "how hard those journeys would have been for people. And the leap of faith that was being made in an unmapped, uncharted world." The director felt that the shifting weather conditions amplified this practical approach due to the challenges they posed.[^cite_note-Empire2MillionFilm-42] [Hoyte van Hoytema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoyte_van_Hoytema) reunited with Nolan as cinematographer.[^cite_note-DamonOdysseusFirstLook-6]

After using [IMAX 70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film#IMAX) extensively since *[The Dark Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight)* (2008), *The Odyssey* became the first feature film shot entirely with [IMAX film cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX_film_cameras), primarily using a newly manufactured lighter and quieter version Nolan had specifically developed for the production alongside older versions.[^cite_note-TheOdysseyAnnounceTHR-38][^cite_note-IMAXFilmCamerasTHR-79][^cite_note-IMAXFilmCamerasFC-80] For close-up scenes requiring audible dialogue, the IMAX cameras were often concealed in large [sound blimps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_blimp) on set, while a system of mirrors was set up to help the actors maintain eye contact when the cameras obscured their view.[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12][^cite_note-IMAXFilmCamerasFC-80] Over 2 million feet (610 kilometres) of IMAX 70 mm film was used to shoot *The Odyssey*.[^cite_note-Empire2MillionFilm-42] As a single IMAX camera can only hold roughly three minutes of film, actors had to pause during longer scenes while the camera was reloaded before resuming their performances.[^cite_note-VarietyIMAX-81][^cite_note-GuardianIMAX-82]

### Editing and visual effects

Over 2 million feet of 15-perf 65mm [negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_\(photography\)) film stock shot for *The Odyssey* was developed and processed at [FotoKem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FotoKem) in [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California), the last film laboratory producing 70mm prints.[^cite_note-113] The cost of the film stock alone totaled roughly \$3 million.[^cite_note-114] The film stock was scanned and converted into high-resolution digital files for editor [Jennifer Lame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lame) to make initial cuts. The film negative would then be cut and spliced by hand.[^cite_note-Croxton_2026-115] [Color grading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_grading) was done through the analog method of color filters on a [light table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_table) with numerical values assigned to each shot for the final print.[^cite_note-Croxton_2026-115]

[DNEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNEG) provided the [visual effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_effects) for *The Odyssey*,[^cite_note-ArtOfVFX-116] with [Andrew Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson_\(visual_effects\)) serving as visual effects supervisor and editor [Jennifer Lame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lame) returning from previous Nolan productions.[^cite_note-Lame-117][^cite_note-ArtOfVFX-116] Unlike the Cyclops and Laestrygonians, the six headed [Scylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scylla) was a predominantly digital effect, with Nolan stating the creature was inspired by the works of [Guillermo del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_del_Toro), saying, "What I learned from him is that a monster is not a monster. You have to approach them the way you approach any other character."[^cite_note-LATimesOdysseyInterview-16] A ratchet system was devised to lift stunt performers into the air for the Scylla's attack, while the nearby [whirlpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlpool) was created by having [jet skis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_ski) ride in circles as reference material to be replaced later by digital effects.[^cite_note-YahooOdysseyBTS-118]

## Music

Main article: [*The Odyssey* (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odyssey_\(soundtrack\))

By April 2025, [Ludwig Göransson](35249266.md) was composing the musical score for *The Odyssey* after previously working with Nolan on *Tenet* and *Oppenheimer*.[^cite_note-Göransson-119] Nolan wanted to avoid the typical orchestral music commonly heard in other [sword-and-sandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword-and-sandal) films. To "evoke the materiality" and setting of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), Göransson included in the score: various bronze [gongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong) layered with synthesizers; bronze instruments; at Nolan's suggestion, the sound of the ancient [lyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyre) for when Odysseus plucks his bow,[^cite_note-TimeCoverStory-12] and the ancient [aulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulos); [Albanian iso-polyphony folk chorals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_iso-polyphony) and flutes recorded by Albanian shepherds; and cacophonies of sheet metal alarm drums.[^cite_note-GöranssonInstruumentsRS-120][^cite_note-BlakeScore-121][^cite_note-NolanVultureOverwhelm-17][^cite_note-122]

Musicians Callum Armstrong and Rosa Fragorapti played replicas of an ancient [aulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulos) and [lyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyre). Due to the obsolescence of the instruments, they had to research ancient source material to use them properly.[^cite_note-GöranssonInstruumentsRS-120] English electronic musician [James Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blake_\(musician\)) and American rapper [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott) (who appears in the film) also contributed vocals, and the two collaborated with Göransson on an original song, "When I'm Home", which plays over the closing credits.[^cite_note-SoundtrackDetails-123][^cite_note-BlakeScore-121][^cite_note-THRCastGuide-21] Nolan is credited as a co-writer of the song.[^cite_note-THRCastGuide-21]

The soundtrack album was released digitally by Universal's [Back Lot Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Lot_Music) label on July 17, 2026, coinciding with the release date. Record manufacturer Mutant released a triple [vinyl LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_LP) version of the soundtrack on July 31, 2026, followed by a [double album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_album) [CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD) release on August 14, 2026.[^cite_note-SoundtrackRelease-124]

### Theatrical

*The Odyssey* held its [world premiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_premiere) on July 6, 2026, at the [Odeon Luxe Leicester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_Luxe_Leicester_Square) in London.[^cite_note-Premiere-152] As part of the global promotional tour, a two-day promotional showcase and official premiere event was held from July 10 to 11, 2026, in Mumbai, marking the first time a film directed by Christopher Nolan held an official premiere event in India.[^cite_note-IndiaTodaySchedule-153][^cite_note-TheHinduMumbaiPremiere-154] Another premiere was held on July 14, 2026, at the [AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres) venue on [Lincoln Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Square,_Manhattan) in New York City.[^cite_note-PremiereNYC-155]

*The Odyssey* was released theatrically by [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures) on July 17, 2026, in the United States and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-TheOdysseyAnnounceTHR-38] It has been presented in a variety of [aspect ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio_\(image\)) depending on the exhibition format: 1.43\:1 on [IMAX 70 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film#IMAX) film prints, 1.90\:1 on [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX) digital screens, 2.20\:1 on [70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film) prints, 2.39\:1 on [35 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_mm_movie_film) prints, and either 2.39\:1 or 1.85\:1 for other [premium large format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_large_format) (PLF) screens, such as [D-Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Box) and RX, depending on the specifications of individual auditoriums.[^cite_note-ReleaseFormats-156] Only 41 screens worldwide can present the film in IMAX 70mm, 25 in the United States, 9 in Canada, 3 in the UK, and 1 each in Australia, Belgium, France and the Czech Republic, with the [IMAX Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX_Melbourne) screen in Australia being the only venue in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere) capable of doing so.[^cite_note-GuardianIMAX-82][^cite_note-41IMAXScreensWiredME-157] This is an increase from the 30 IMAX 70mm prints for Nolan's previous film *Oppenheimer*, due to an effort from the [IMAX Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX_Corporation) to locate, refurbish and install disused IMAX 70mm projectors, as well as train 60 new projectionists.[^cite_note-VarietyIMAX70mmGelfond-158] *The Odyssey* was released theatrically in China on nearly 800 IMAX screens on August 14, 2026, following early previews beginning on August 1 and 2 in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou and Shenzhen.[^cite_note-159]

### Home media

*The Odyssey* will be available on digital platforms and [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray) from November 17, 2026, 120 days after its theatrical release.[^cite_note-160] Presented in a 4K [HDR transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-dynamic-range_television) in a 1.78\:1 aspect ratio with a [DTS-HD MA 5.1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DTS-HD_Master_Audio) soundtrack, the decision to simultaneously release on UHD Blu-ray and digital has been interpreted as part of Nolan's longstanding proponency for [physical media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_media) over [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming).[^cite_note-Choose_TV-161]

## Reception

### Box office



IMAX 70 mm film reels of *The Odyssey* at a [Celebration Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebration_Cinema) in [Grand Rapids, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Rapids,_Michigan)

As of October 2, 2026, *The Odyssey* has grossed \$619 million in the United States and Canada, and \$1.14 billion in other territories, for a worldwide total of \$1.76 billion.[^cite_note-BOM-4][^cite_note-NUM-5][^cite_note-162] Produced on a \$250 million budget, *The Odyssey* is among the most expensive of [Nolan's career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan_filmography) and is [one of the most expensive films ever made](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_films).[^cite_note-IMAX70mmPresalesTHR-43] The film became Nolan's first to surpass \$1 billion since *The Dark Knight Rises.*[^cite_note-163]

#### Pre-sale ticket records

Tickets for select opening weekend IMAX 70 mm screenings were made available on July 17, 2025, one year before its release, which was considered an unprecedented move by a major film distributor.[^cite_note-IMAX70mmPresalesTHR-43] Some of these showings sold out within the first 12 hours of their availability, including half of the 22 theaters available in the United States, earning approximately \$1.5 million.[^cite_note-IMAX70mmPresalesTHR-43][^cite_note-IMAX70mmPresalesDeadline-164]

Tickets for the remainder of IMAX, IMAX 70 mm, and premium format screenings went on sale on June 4, 2026, ahead of general admission tickets being made available later that month.[^cite_note-DeadlinePresales-165] Ticketing websites [Fandango Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandango_Media) and [AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres) experienced delayed queues, with initial wait times lasting up to an hour due to the high influx of users attempting to purchase tickets, comparable to that of the 2023 concert film *[Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift:_The_Eras_Tour)*.[^cite_note-VarietyPresales-166][^cite_note-IndieWirePresales-167][^cite_note-DeadlinePresales-165][^cite_note-TheWrapPresales-148] Emily St. Martin at the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* described attempts to purchase tickets as "an epic journey of its own".[^cite_note-LATPresales-168] \$3.4 million was accumulated from approximately 150,000 pre-sale tickets in the first 24 hours, making it the highest pre-sale gross in four years at that time.[^cite_note-BFIPresales-169] The first-day PLF pre-sales were also the highest pre-sales for a major studio release for AMC since 2022, surpassing the first-day pre-sales for any film from 2025 and early 2026.[^cite_note-DeadlinePresales-165] *The Odyssey* sold 28,000 presale tickets at the [BFI IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI_IMAX) in 24 hours, earning £750,000 (\$1 million). This more than doubled the £366,000 (\$490,000) gross of the venue's previous record holder, *[Dune: Part Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune:_Part_Two)* (2024), and surpassed the £254,000 (\$340,000) gross of *Oppenheimer* during the same timeframe.[^cite_note-BFIPresales-169] [Richard Gelfond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gelfond) confirmed that by July 16, 2026, many IMAX venues had already sold out into the film's fifth weekend.[^cite_note-VarietyIMAX70mmGelfond-158]

#### Performance

In France, *The Odyssey* opened on July 15, 2026, in 730 theaters and drew 230,000 viewers on its opening day, surpassing the opening of *Oppenheimer* (160,000 viewers).[^cite_note-FranceBOOpening-170] In the United States and Canada, it earned \$51.2 million from its opening day, including \$17.6 million from Thursday night previews, which was the highest for any film during that time frame for the year, surpassing *[Toy Story 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy_Story_5)* (\$17.5 million). It went on to gross \$41.66 million and \$31.7 million during its opening weekend, for a total of \$123.5 million from 3,919 theaters, ranking first ahead of *[Moana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moana_\(2026_film\))*.[^cite_note-OpeningBODeadline-171] This was the best opening for Canada and the United States within a year; the third-best for a Nolan film, behind *The Dark Knight Rises* (\$190.9 million in 2012) and *[The Dark Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight)* (\$158.4 million in 2008); as well as the highest debut for Damon, surpassing *[The Bourne Ultimatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bourne_Ultimatum_\(film\))* (\$69.2 million in 2007); an R-rated Universal film, surpassing *[Fifty Shades of Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_of_Grey_\(film\))* (\$85.1 million in 2015) and *Oppenheimer* (\$180 million); and the fourth highest opening for an R-rated film overall after *[Deadpool & Wolverine](52234178.md)* (\$211.4 million in 2024), *[Deadpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_\(film\))* (\$132.4 million in 2016), and *[Deadpool 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_2)* (\$125.5 million in 2018).[^cite_note-OpeningBODeadline-171] It achieved the highest live-action opening weekend for a 2026 film, beating *[Michael](70014183.md)* (\$97.2 million), as well as the third-highest opening weekend of that year, only behind *Toy Story 5* (\$159.6 million) and *[The Super Mario Galaxy Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Super_Mario_Galaxy_Movie)* (\$131.2 million).[^cite_note-OpeningBODeadline-171] The film also generated the second-highest IMAX opening weekend for the United States and Canada with \$29.6 million, after *[Star Wars: The Force Awakens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Force_Awakens)* (\$30.1 million in 2015).[^cite_note-indiewire_1235206287-172] Within other countries, it debuted to \$130.6 million from 12,700 theaters in 73 markets, marking the largest foreign debut for Nolan, with the highest markets including France, Italy, India, Spain, Brazil, the Netherlands, and Poland, as well as the biggest opening for Universal in 11 markets.[^cite_note-OpeningBODeadline-171] The film delivered the biggest opening weekend at the BFI IMAX with £219,867 (\$296,147), surpassing the previous record gross of £216,454 (\$290,979) held by *[Star Wars: The Last Jedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Last_Jedi)* (2017).[^cite_note-173] The combined opening grosses resulted in a worldwide opening total of \$264.1 million, the highest ever for Nolan, surpassing *The Dark Knight Rises* (\$249 million).[^cite_note-OpeningBODeadline-171]

In the United States and Canada, *The Odyssey* earned \$90 million from its second weekend, marking the 14th all-time highest second-weekend gross for a film in the region and the second-best second-weekend gross for an R-rated film, behind *Deadpool & Wolverine*. After the second weekend, *The Odyssey* has grossed \$639.9 million globally, including \$286.3 million in the United States and Canada, where it became the highest grossing R-rated film of the year and the second highest grossing R-rated Universal film, behind *Oppenheimer*.[^cite_note-Weekend2Deadline-174][^cite_note-Weekend2HR-175] It managed to hold well in its third weekend with \$51 million despite being dethroned by *[Spider-Man: Brand New Day](65419000.md)*.[^cite_note-176] Originally finishing August 17, IMAX 70mm screenings were extended until September 16, due to high demand selling out screens for seven weeks in a row,[^cite_note-Pequeno2026-177] sending IMAX stock prices to an all time high.[^cite_note-RoushForbes-178] By August 10, *The Odyssey* had surpassed *[ Avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_\(2009_film\))* (2009) to become the highest-grossing IMAX film, earning \$289 million globally, \$37.5 million of which came from the 41 IMAX 70mm screens.[^cite_note-AnklerSonnyBunch-179]

Following the commercial success, Greek Prime Minister [Kyriakos Mitsotakis](2780816.md) congratulated Nolan in a video call, thanking him for promoting ancient Greek culture internationally and for choosing Greece as the principal shooting location. ### Critical response

Picture of Matt Damon, Christopher Nolan, Emma Thomas, and Anne Hathaway at the premiere of The Odyssey in New York

The film received widespread critical acclaim, with praise for the performances, sets, scale, and Nolan's direction.[^cite_note-ReviewRoundup-181] On [review aggregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregation) website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), 94% of 514 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 8.7/10. The website's consensus reads: "Reinvigorating an ancient adventure with majestic sweep and sterling work by its colossal ensemble, Christopher Nolan's *The Odyssey* imbues myth with primal human feeling."[^cite_note-182] It is Nolan's highest-rated film on Rotten Tomatoes.[^cite_note-OpeningBODeadline-171] [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), which uses a weighted average, assigned it a score of 88 out of 100, based on 63 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".[^cite_note-183] Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore) gave it an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale, while 81% of those surveyed at [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak) said they would definitely recommend it.[^cite_note-OpeningBODeadline-171]

Randy Myers of *[The Mercury News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mercury_News)* called *The Odyssey* "another feather in Nolan's masterpiece cap, a grandiose achievement that fills audiences with the same child-like glee experienced when watching *[Jason and the Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_and_the_Argonauts_\(film\))* and *[Clash of the Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clash_of_the_Titans_\(1981_film\))* for the first time".[^cite_note-MercuryNewsReview-184] Jake Wilson of *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)* wrote that Nolan "has a way with a time paradox" and that "he can take one of the oldest stories in Western culture and make it feel new".[^cite_note-SydneyMorningHeraldReview-185] In *[The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md)*, [Robbie Collin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Collin) wrote: "Nolan and his collaborators have constructed a strange, fearsome and trailblazing machine of a movie – by some distance, the best of the year so far."[^cite_note-TelegraphReview-186] Alistair Harkness of *[The Scotsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scotsman)* called it "a thrilling, thoughtful, bloody epic", writing that it "feels like Nolan's angriest film" and one of his best.[^cite_note-ScotsmanReview-187] Clarisse Loughrey of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* described it as "a blockbuster of literally unprecedented scale", and Nolan's best film.[^cite_note-IndependentReview-188] In *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, [Manohla Dargis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manohla_Dargis) wrote: "It is an anomalous big-studio entertainment that Nolan has filled with stars and polished to a high gleam, turning a 3,000-year-old poem into a smart, thoughtful film with Old Hollywood allure."[^cite_note-NYTimesReview-189] [Justin Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Chang) of *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* praised Nolan's realistic approach and wrote that his rationalism provides "just the note of real-world groundedness that a great fantasy needs to achieve liftoff".[^cite_note-NewYorkerReview-190]

However, there was some criticism for the [sound mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_mixing), dialogue, and deviations from its source material.[^cite_note-ReviewRoundup-181] [Richard Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Brody) of *The New Yorker* felt that Nolan "turns an epic poem prosaic".[^cite_note-NewYorkerReview2-191] In *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))*, [Stephanie Zacharek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Zacharek) called it an "eye-glazing dud".[^cite_note-TimeReview-192] Odie Henderson of *[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe)* wrote: "Pattinson has fun channeling [Roddy McDowall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roddy_McDowall)'s snarky performance in *[Cleopatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleopatra_\(1963_film\))*. But like that epic flop, *The Odyssey* takes itself too seriously. The film ultimately sinks under its bleakness like a lead balloon."[^cite_note-BostonGlobeReview-193] Mohamed Sleiman Labat in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* criticized "the decision to shoot in [Moroccan-occupied Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara), where the Indigenous \[[Sahrawis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawis)] people can't tell their stories without fear of imprisonment", and accused Nolan of participating in a "state-sponsored PR campaign designed to legitimise an illegal occupation".[^cite_note-WesternSaharaResponseGuardian-194] [Angelica Jade Bastién](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelica_Jade_Basti%C3%A9n) of [Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_\(website\)) felt *The Odyssey* was "limited by cinematic sexism" and uninterested in its female characters.[^cite_note-195]

### Views of Nolan's interpretation of Homer's poem

Main article: [Christopher Nolan's interpretation of the Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan's_interpretation_of_the_Odyssey)

Critics have noted multiple differences between Nolan's and Homer's versions of *The Odyssey*. Some have questioned whether Nolan accurately depicts [Mycenaean Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece); others have argued that Homer's epic was simply a myth, and others again have stated that while *The Odyssey* is a story, it was rooted in history.[^cite_note-History_Extra_2026-196] The film has been described as both the "most anticipated" and the "most contested" of 2026.[^cite_note-Barber_BBC_2026-197]

The process of adapting an ancient book-length poem to a single 172-minute film makes changes and omissions inevitable.[^cite_note-Tryhorn_2026-198] Nolan cut some plot elements and characters, including several gods, while attempting to preserve an "appropriate tone". He decided to portray Odysseus as a veteran tired of war and haunted by slaughter. That offers a modern explanation for the length of Odysseus's homecoming—that he was avoiding going home.[^cite_note-Elle_2026-199] The historian Andrew E. Larsen admired the visuals, but considered the Greek costumes unhistorically drab, and found Agamemnon's armour silly.[^cite_note-Larsen_2026-200] The classicist [Armand D'Angour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_D'Angour) liked the use of an ancient musical instrument, the *[aulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulos)*.[^cite_note-D'Angour_2026-201] Others critiqued the armour and ship designs.[^cite_note-TrailerST-78][^cite_note-InaccuraciesGR-202][^cite_note-InaccuraciesTheTimes-203] Some critics argued that the adaptation reshaped the poem's ancient moral framework to align with modern attitudes.[^cite_note-NewYorkerReview2-191][^cite_note-GuardianHauser-204] The casting of the Kenyan-Mexican actress [Lupita Nyong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Nyong'o) as [Helen of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy) was criticised by [conservative American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) commentators as [blackwashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwashing).[^cite_note-208] Several commentators called this criticism racist.[^cite_note-211] Larsen commented that Greek literature accepted the black princess [Andromeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andromeda_\(mythology\)) as extremely beautiful.[^cite_note-Larsen_2026-200]

The English classicist [Mary Beard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Beard) criticised *The Odyssey* as lacking the sex and humour of the poem. She said it failed to capture the poem's nuance and called the character dynamics "feebly handled".[^cite_note-BeardGuardian-212][^cite_note-BeardTimes-213] [Emily Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Wilson_\(classicist\)), whose 2017 translation inspired Nolan, called the film a simple action film[^cite_note-WilsonRinger-214][^cite_note-WilsonAP-215] lacking "psychological, emotional, political and ethical depth", abysmally written, and with "nothing convincing to say" about the *Odyssey's* major themes.[^cite_note-lrb-216]

Others argued that *The Odyssey* succeeds on its own terms, creating a popular film matching the expectations of a modern audience.[^cite_note-218] The British critic [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) praised its ambition and scope, offering a vision of grim resolution in the face of loss.[^cite_note-TheGuardianReview-219] Guy Lodge of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* described it as a "vast, thrilling, slightly aloof epic", offering the audience so much that the chilliness works.[^cite_note-VarietyReview-220]

### Casting choices

The casting of Kenyan-Mexican actress [Lupita Nyong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Nyong'o) as [Helen of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy)—often described as the most beautiful woman in the world—was criticized by [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) commentators, such as [Matt Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Walsh_\(political_commentator\)) and [Elon Musk](909036.md), as [blackwashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwashing).[^cite_note-221] Several commentators called this criticism racist,[^cite_note-222] comparing it to similar casting criticisms of the docudrama *[Queen Cleopatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Cleopatra_\(Netflix\))* (2023) and the [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney) film *[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid_\(2023_film\))* (2023).[^cite_note-CastingBacklashSalon-206][^cite_note-CastingBacklashDailyNation-210] Referencing Greek professor [Dimitris Liantinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitris_Liantinis), Philip Chrysopoulos of the *Greek Reporter* acknowledged that Homer described Helen as pale or "white-armed" but noted that he did not describe her in detail, allowing everyone to imagine her for themselves.[^cite_note-223] *[Salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon.com)*'s CK Smith wrote that the backlash was "selective" and more about social issues over minority representation than fidelity to the source material.[^cite_note-CastingBacklashSalon-206] Nolan defended Nyong'o's casting, saying he believed she conveyed the "strength and the poise" essential for the role. Nyong'o opted to emphasize Helen's character beyond her appearance, saying, "You can't perform beauty." She added that the cast was meant to be "representative of the world" while "occupying the epic narrative of our time".[^cite_note-Nyong'oBacklashResponse-205] Writing for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, Greek film critic Chris Cotonou called this an "honourable intention", but argued that it made the absence of Greek actors "even more glaring", as they are the people "most authentically connected to the source".[^cite_note-CastingBacklashGuardian-209]

The casting of [Elliot Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Page) was criticized. Page's role was not initially announced but was rumored to be [Achilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles).[^cite_note-CastingBacklashDailyNation-210] *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* described some of the online backlash as "[transphobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transphobic) memes about Page playing a role that may not even be his".[^cite_note-224]

### Linguistic register

The use of [American accents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_accent) and 21st-century [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) dialogue from the cast, including British actors Holland and Pattinson, was also criticized.[^cite_note-AccentsTHR-147][^cite_note-AccentsSMH-225] [James Hibberd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hibberd_\(writer\)) of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* called this decision a "striking departure from the unwritten Hollywood rule of [characters in historical epics employing British accents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Latin)".[^cite_note-AccentsTHR-147] Hibberd and Nell Geraets at *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)* contended that using [ancient Greek dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_dialects) would not be accessible for contemporary audiences,[^cite_note-AccentsTHR-147][^cite_note-AccentsSMH-225] unlike an American accent.[^cite_note-AccentsMensJournal-226] This change was perceived as [anachronistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anachronistic) for an adaptation of ancient [Greek literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_literature),[^cite_note-AccentsTHR-147] with Geraets noting neither British nor American accents were linguistically authentic to the ancient Greek setting,[^cite_note-AccentsSMH-225] while Hibberd said using American accents "risks sounding a bit silly at times", unlike the "'just foreign enough'" modern British accent.[^cite_note-AccentsTHR-147] Nolan insisted on the cast using American accents rather than British or Greek,[^cite_note-PatelJun2026-54] which *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN)*'s Scott Collura defended, noting that Wilson's translation of the poem similarly translated the text into [plain English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_English), quoting her rationale: "Translation always, necessarily, involves interpretation."[^cite_note-AccentsIGN-227] The accents and everyday-language dialogue were mocked by some online commenters.[^cite_note-AccentsTHR-147][^cite_note-AccentsSMH-225][^cite_note-AccentsIGN-227]

## Themes

### Memory, time, and autonomy

In discussing the themes with [MS NOW](159846.md), Nolan stated that "There are so many different themes, so many different elements of Homer's original poem that appear in movies and have been the sort of bedrock of Hollywood for so long... It hasn't really been told as a modern film for modern audiences. And so, for a director, that's a pretty thrilling opportunity to try and challenge yourself and try to bring something new to the table." For Daniel Chin, the central themes are its emphasis on the nonlinear experience of time and the exploration of memory in the principal characters, especially of Odysseus and his alternating experience of remembering and forgetting his homeland in Ithaca.[^cite_note-231] In his review for *The Gospel Coalition*, Brett McCracken saw the central theme as that of human autonomy struggling with divine intervention, writing: "*The Odyssey*'s marketing campaign tagline, 'Defy the gods,' is an extension of this theme (human autonomy). It's not that Nolan dislikes Greek mythology. He seems to appreciate it as a beautiful story that helped build Western civilization. But Damon's version of Odysseus doesn't particularly need the gods. He often feels skeptical of them ('Why can't God \[*sic*] speak in ways we understand?') and puts faith more in himself than in deities."[^cite_note-232]

### Breakdown of societal norms and decorum

Critics interpreted *The Odyssey* as a reframing of the poem into a meditation on the moral consequences of war and the erosion of the [social norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_norm) and [decorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decorum) that underpin civilization.[^cite_note-TheGuardianReview-219][^cite_note-GuardianHauser-204][^cite_note-EarpGuardian-233] It explores the Greek concept of [xenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenia_\(Greek\))—the mutual respect between hosts and guests—using it to reframe the Trojan Horse and Odysseus' journey as explorations of [post-traumatic stress disorder](82974.md), war guilt, [moral injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_injury), and the breakdown of trust in [civic norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civics), with some interpreting it as a 21st-century political allegory.[^cite_note-TheGuardianReview-219][^cite_note-234][^cite_note-EarpGuardian-233]
