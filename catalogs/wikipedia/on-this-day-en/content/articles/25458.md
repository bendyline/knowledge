**Rome**[^cite_note-6] is the [capital](181337.md) and largest city of [Italy](14532.md). It is also the [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) of the [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio) [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) and of the [Metropolitan City of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Rome). A special municipality named *Roma Capitale* with a population of 2.7 million in an area of 1,287.36 km<sup>2</sup> (497.1 mi<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-population-4] Rome is the [third most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_European_Union_by_population_within_city_limits) in the [European Union](9317.md) by population within city limits. The [Metropolitan City of Rome Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Rome_Capital), with a population of 4.2 million, is the most populous [metropolitan city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_cities_of_Italy) in Italy. [Its metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_metropolitan_area) is the third-most populous within Italy.[^cite_note-citypop-7] Rome is located in the central-western portion of the [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula), within Lazio ([Latium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latium)), along the shores of the [Tiber Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber_Valley). [Vatican City](32408.md)—the [smallest country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area) in the world and headquarters of the worldwide [Catholic Church](606848.md) under the governance of the [Holy See](13393.md)—is an independent country within Rome's municipal boundaries, the only existing example of a country within a city. Rome is often referred to as the "[City of Seven Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_hills_of_Rome)" due to its geography, and also as the "Eternal City". Rome is generally considered to be one of the cradles of [Western civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_civilization) and [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christianity) [Christian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_culture), and the centre of the Catholic Church.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

[Rome's history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rome) spans 28 centuries. While [Roman mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_mythology) dates the [founding of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_of_Rome) at around 753 BC, the site has been inhabited for much longer, making it a major human settlement for over three millennia and one of the [oldest continuously occupied cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities) in Europe.[^cite_note-Heiken,_G._2005-11] The city's early population originated from a mix of [Latins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latins_\(Italic_tribe\)), [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization), and [Sabines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabines). Eventually, the city successively became the capital of the [Roman Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Kingdom), the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) and the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), and is regarded by many as the first-ever Imperial city and [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis).[^cite_note-12] It was first called *The Eternal City* ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Urbs Aeterna*; [Italian](14708.md): *La Città Eterna*) by the Roman poet [Tibullus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibullus) in the 1st century BC, and the expression was also taken up by [Ovid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovid), [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil), and [Livy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livy).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Rome is also called [*Caput Mundi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caput_Mundi#Rome) (Capital of the World).

After the [fall of the Empire in the west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire), which marked the beginning of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Rome slowly fell under the political control of the [Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papacy), and in the 8th century, it became the capital of the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States), which lasted until 1870. Beginning with the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), almost all popes since [Nicholas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V) (1447–1455) pursued a coherent architectural and urban programme over four hundred years, aimed at making the city the artistic and cultural centre of the world.[^cite_note-15] In this way, Rome first became one of the major centres of the Renaissance[^cite_note-16] and subsequently the birthplace of both the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) style and [Neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism). Famous artists, painters, sculptors, and architects made Rome the centre of their activity, creating masterpieces throughout the city. In 1871, Rome became the capital of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), which, in 1946, became the Italian Republic. It hosted the [Summer Olympics](27566.md) in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics).

In 2019, Rome was the 14th most visited city in the world with 8.6 million tourists, the third most visited city in the European Union, and the most popular tourist destination in Italy.[^cite_note-17] Its historic centre is listed by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-whc.unesco.org-18] Rome is also the seat of several specialised agencies of the [United Nations](31769.md), such as the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization), the [World Food Programme](33582.md), and the [International Fund for Agricultural Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fund_for_Agricultural_Development). The city also hosts the Secretariat of the Parliamentary Assembly of the [Union for the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Mediterranean),[^cite_note-19] as well as the headquarters of several Italian multinational companies such as [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni), [Enel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enel), [TIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppo_TIM), [Leonardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.), and banks such as [BNL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banca_Nazionale_del_Lavoro). Numerous companies are based within Rome's [EUR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUR,_Rome) business district, such as the luxury fashion house [Fendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fendi) located in the [Palazzo della Civiltà Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Civilt%C3%A0_Italiana). The presence of renowned international brands in the city has made Rome an important centre of fashion and design, and the [Cinecittà Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinecitt%C3%A0) have been the set of many [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award)–winning movies.[^cite_note-20]

## Name and symbol

### Etymology

According to the Ancient Romans' [founding myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_of_Rome),[^cite_note-livy1797-21] the name *Roma* came from the city's founder and first [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rex_\(king\)), [Romulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus).[^cite_note-britannica-2]

However, it is possible that the name Romulus was actually derived from Rome itself.[^cite_note-22] As early as the 4th century, there have been alternative theories proposed on the origin of the name *Roma*. Several hypotheses have been advanced focusing on its linguistic roots which however remain uncertain:[^cite_note-23]

- From *Rumon* or *Rumen*, archaic name of the [Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber), which in turn is supposedly related to the Greek verb ῥέω (*rhéō*) 'to flow, stream' and the Latin verb *ruō* 'to hurry, rush';

  [^cite_note-24]

- From the [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_language) word 𐌓𐌖𐌌𐌀 (*ruma*), whose root is *\*rum-* "teat", with possible reference either to the [totem wolf that adopted and suckled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She-wolf_\(Roman_mythology\)) the cognately named twins [Romulus and Remus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_and_Remus), or to the shape of the [Palatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_Hill) and [Aventine Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aventine_Hill);

- From the Greek word ῥώμη (*rhṓmē*), which means *strength*.

  [^cite_note-25]

### Other names and symbols

Rome has also been called in ancient times simply "Urbs" (central city),[^cite_note-Vergari_Marco_Luberti_Pica_Del_Monte_2020_pp._6–17-26] from *urbs roma*, or identified with its ancient Roman [initialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym) of [SPQR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPQR), the symbol of Rome's [constituted republican government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Roman_Republic). In 192, [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor) [Commodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodus) renamed the city *Colonia Lucia Annia Commodiana* after himself, but the name was reverted to Rome after [his assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodus#Assassination_\(192\)) at the end of the year. Furthermore, Rome has been called Urbs Aeterna (The Eternal City), Caput Mundi (The [Capital of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_the_world)), Throne of [St. Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter) and Roma Capitale.

[Dionysius of Halicarnassus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysius_of_Halicarnassus) states that the [lagobolon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagobolon) was a symbol of the settlement of the city of Rome, and recounts that following the destruction by fire of a sacred hut of Mars on the Palatine Hill, a lagobolon, which Romulus used to delineate the regions for taking auspices when founding the city, was found miraculously preserved among the ashes.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Rome).

### Earliest history

Main article: [Founding of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_of_Rome)



Model of archaic Rome. The image faces northeast, with the Capitoline hill on left and the Palatine on right. The city would not have looked like this prior to the seventh century BC.

Archaeological evidence of human occupation of the Rome area has been found from approximately 14,000 years ago, although the dense layer of much younger debris obscures [Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeolithic) and [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) sites.[^cite_note-Heiken,_G._2005-11] Evidence of stone tools, pottery, and stone weapons attest to about 10,000 years of human presence in the Rome area. Several excavations support the view that Rome grew from [pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism) settlements on the [Palatine Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_Hill) built above the area of the future [Roman Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum). Between the end of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and the beginning of the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age), each hill between the sea and the Capitoline Hill was topped by a village (on the Capitoline, a village is attested since the end of the 14th century BC).[^cite_note-coa9-29] However, none of them yet had an urban quality.[^cite_note-coa9-29]

Nowadays, there is a wide consensus that the city developed gradually through the aggregation ("[synoecism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synoecism)") of several villages around the largest one, placed above the Palatine.[^cite_note-coa9-29] This aggregation was facilitated by the increase of agricultural productivity above the [subsistence level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_economy), which also allowed the establishment of [secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector) and [tertiary activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector). These, in turn, boosted the development of trade with the Greek colonies of southern Italy (mainly [Ischia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischia) and [Cumae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumae)).[^cite_note-coa9-29] These developments, which according to archaeological evidence took place during the mid-eighth century BC, can be considered as the "birth" of the city.[^cite_note-coa9-29] Despite recent excavations at the Palatine Hill, the view that Rome was founded deliberately in the middle of the eighth century BC, as the legend of Romulus suggests, remains a fringe hypothesis.[^cite_note-foundation-30]

#### Legend of the founding of Rome

Main articles: [Romulus and Remus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_and_Remus) and [Romulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus)



*[Capitoline Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitoline_Wolf)*, a sculpture of the [mythical she-wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She-wolf_\(Roman_mythology\)) suckling the infant twins [Romulus and Remus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_and_Remus)

Traditional stories handed down by the [ancient Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Romans) themselves explain the earliest [history of their city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rome) in terms of [legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend) and [myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myth). The most familiar of these myths, and perhaps the most famous of all [Roman myths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_mythology), is the story of [Romulus and Remus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_and_Remus), the twins who were suckled by a [she-wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She-wolf_\(Roman_mythology\)).[^cite_note-livy1797-21] They decided to build a city, but after an argument, [Romulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus) killed his brother and the city took his name. According to the Roman [annalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annalist), this happened on 21 April 753 BC.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196473-31] This legend had to be reconciled with a dual tradition, set earlier in time, that had the [Trojan refugee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War) [Aeneas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeneas) escape to Italy and found the line of Romans through his son [Iulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascanius), the namesake of the [Julio-Claudian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio-Claudian_dynasty).[^cite_note-livy2005-32] This was accomplished by the Roman poet [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil) in the first century BC. In addition, [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) mentions an older story, that the city was an [Arcadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadia_\(ancient_region\)) colony founded by [Evander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evander_of_Pallene). Strabo also writes that [Lucius Coelius Antipater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Coelius_Antipater) believed that Rome was founded by Greeks.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Monarchy and republic

Main articles: [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Roman Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Kingdom), and [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic)

&#x20;built in 120–80 BC



The [Roman Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum) contains the ruins of the buildings that represented the political, legal, religious and economic centre of ancient Rome, constituting the "nerve centre" of all Roman civilisation.

[^cite_note-36]

After the foundation by Romulus (according to a legend),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196473-31] Rome was ruled for a period of 244 years by a [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy). The first rulers were of [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latins_\(Italic_tribe\)) and [Sabine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabines) origin, and later ones were [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscans). The tradition handed down seven kings: [Romulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus), [Numa Pompilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numa_Pompilius), [Tullus Hostilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullus_Hostilius), [Ancus Marcius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancus_Marcius), [Tarquinius Priscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarquinius_Priscus), [Servius Tullius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servius_Tullius) and [Lucius Tarquinius Superbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Tarquinius_Superbus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196473-31]

In 509 BC, the Romans expelled the last king from their city and established an [oligarchic](22315.md) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) led by two annually elected [consuls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_consul). Rome then began a period characterised by internal struggles between [patricians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrician_\(ancient_Rome\)) (aristocrats) and [plebeians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebs) (small landowners), and by constant warfare against the populations of central Italy: Etruscans, Latins, [Volsci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volsci), [Aequi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aequi), and [Marsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196477-37] After becoming master of [Latium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latium), Rome led several wars (against the [Gauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls), [Osci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osci)-[Samnites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samnites) and the Greek colony of [Taranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taranto), allied with [Pyrrhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhus_of_Epirus), king of [Epirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epirus)) whose result was the conquest of the [Italian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_peninsula), from the central area down to [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196479-38]

The 3rd and 4th century BC saw the establishment of Roman hegemony over the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) and the [Balkans](4829.md) through the three [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars) (264–146 BC) fought against [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage) and the three [Macedonian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Wars) (212–168 BC) against [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(ancient_kingdom\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196481–83-39] The first [Roman provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) were established at this time: [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilia_\(Roman_province\)), [Sardinia and Corsica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinia_and_Corsica), [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania), [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(Roman_province\)), [Achaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaea_\(Roman_province\)) and [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_\(Roman_province\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196481–85-40]

From the beginning of the 2nd century BC, power was contested between two groups of aristocrats: the [optimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimates), representing the conservative part of the [Roman Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate), and the [populares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populares), who relied on the help of the [plebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebs) (urban lower class) to gain power. In the same period, the bankruptcy of the small farmers and the establishment of large slave estates caused large-scale migration to the city. The continuous warfare led to the establishment of a professional army, which turned out to be more loyal to its generals than to the republic. Because of this, in the late 2nd and early 1st century BC there were several conflicts both abroad and internally: after the failed attempt of social reform of the populares [Tiberius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius_Gracchus) and [Gaius Gracchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Gracchus),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196489-41] and the war against [Jugurtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugurtha),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196489-41] there was [a civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulla's_civil_war) from which the general [Sulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulla) emerged victorious.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196489-41] A [major slave revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Servile_War) under [Spartacus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartacus) followed,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196491-42] and then the establishment of the [first Triumvirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Triumvirate) with [Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar), [Pompey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey) and [Crassus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Licinius_Crassus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196491-42]

Caesar's conquest of [Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul) made him immensely powerful and popular, which led to a [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar's_Civil_War) against the Senate and Pompey. After his victory, Caesar established himself as [dictator for life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator_perpetuo).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196491-42] His assassination in 44 BC led to a [second Triumvirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Triumvirate) among [Octavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian) (Caesar's grandnephew and heir), [Mark Antony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Antony) and [Lepidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidus), and to a [final civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Actium) between Octavian and Antony.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196493-43]

### Empire

Main article: [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire)



The Ancient-Imperial-Roman palaces of the Palatine, a series of palaces located in the [Palatine Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_Hill), express power and wealth of emperors from Augustus until the 4th century.



The [Imperial fora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fora) belong to a series of *monumental fora* (public squares) constructed in Rome by the emperors. Also seen in the image is [Trajan's Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan's_Market).



Model of Imperial Rome at the [Museum of Roman Civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Roman_Civilization) in Rome. The [Temple of Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Claudius) is situated to the south (left) of the [Colosseum](49603.md).

In 27 BC, Octavian was named *[Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_\(title\))* and *[princeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeps)*, founding the [principate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principate), a [diarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diarchy) between the *princeps* and the senate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196493-43] Over time, the new monarch came to be known as the *[imperator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperator)* (whence [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor)), meaning 'commander'.[^cite_note-SimonHornblower-44] During the reign of [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero), two-thirds of the city was ruined after the [Great Fire of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_Rome), and the [persecution of Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire) commenced.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] Rome's empire reached its greatest expansion in the second century under the Emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan). Rome was known as the *[Caput Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caput_Mundi)*, i.e. the capital of the known world, an expression which had already been used in the Republican period. During its first two centuries, the empire was ruled by emperors of the [Julio-Claudian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio-Claudian_dynasty),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196497-48] [Flavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavian_dynasty)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196497-48] and [Antonine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonine_dynasty) dynasties.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196499-49] This time was also characterised by the spread of the Christian religion, preached by [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) in [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea) in the first half of the first century (under [Tiberius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius)) and popularised by his [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament) through the empire and beyond.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964107-50] The Antonine age is considered the zenith of the Empire, whose territory ranged from the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) and from [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) to [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann196499-49]



[Colosseum](49603.md) at dusk

After the end of the [Severan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severan_dynasty) in AD 235, the Empire entered into a 50-year period known as the [Crisis of the Third Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_of_the_Third_Century), during which numerous generals fought for power and the central authority in Rome weakened dramatically. Around the same time, the [Plague of Cyprian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_of_Cyprian) (c. 250–270) afflicted the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-51] Instability caused economic deterioration, and there was a rapid rise in inflation as the government debased the currency in order to meet expenses. The [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_tribes) along the Rhine and north of the Balkans made serious uncoordinated incursions that were more like giant raiding parties rather than attempts to settle. The [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) invaded from the east several times during the 230s to 260s but were eventually defeated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964101-52] The civil wars ended in 285 with the final victory of [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian), who undertook the restoration of the State. He ended the [Principate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principate) and introduced a new authoritarian model known as the [Dominate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominate), derived from his title of *dominus* ('lord'). His most marked feature was the unprecedented intervention of the State down to the city level: whereas the State had submitted a tax demand to a city and allowed it to allocate the charges, from his reign the State did this down to the village level. In a vain attempt to control inflation, he imposed [price controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_controls) which did not last.

Diocletian divided the empire in 286, ruling over the eastern half from [Nicomedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomedia), while his co-emperor [Maximian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximian) ruled the western half from [Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum) (when not on the move).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964101-52] The empire was further divided in 293, when Diocletian named two [caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar_\(title\)), one for each [augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_\(title\)) (emperor). Diocletian tried to turn into a system of non-dynastic succession, similar to the Antonine dynasty. Upon abdication in 305, both caesars succeeded and they, in turn, appointed two colleagues for themselves.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964101-52] However, a [series of civil wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_wars_of_the_Tetrarchy) between rival claimants to power resulted in the unification of the empire under [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great) in 324. Hereditary succession was restored, but the east–west division was maintained. Constantine undertook a major reform of the bureaucracy, not by changing the structure but by rationalising the competencies of the several ministries. The so-called [Edict of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Milan) of 313, actually a fragment of a letter from his co-emperor [Licinius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licinius) to the governors of the eastern provinces, granted freedom of worship to everyone, including Christians, and ordered the restoration of confiscated church properties upon petition to the newly created vicars of dioceses. He funded the building of several churches and allowed clergy to act as arbitrators in civil suits (a measure that did not outlast him but which was restored in part much later). In 330, he transformed [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium) into [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), which became his new capital. However, it was not officially anything more than an imperial residence like [Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum), [Augusta Treverorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Treverorum), or [Nicomedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomedia) until given a city prefect in 359 by [Constantius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantius_II).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964103-53]

Constantine, following Diocletian's reforms, regionalised the administration, which fundamentally changed the way it was governed by creating regional dioceses. The existence of regional fiscal units from 286 served as the model for this unprecedented innovation.[^cite_note-54] The emperor quickened the process of removing military command from governors. Henceforth, civilian administration and military command would be separate. He gave governors more fiscal duties and placed them in charge of the army logistical support system as an attempt to control it by removing the support system from its control.

Christianity in the form of the Nicene Creed became the official religion of the empire in 380, via the [Edict of Thessalonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Thessalonica) issued in the name of three emperors – [Gratian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gratian), [Valentinian II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentinian_II), and [Theodosius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I) – with Theodosius clearly the driving force behind it. He was the last emperor of a unified empire: after his death in 395, his young children, [Honorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorius_\(emperor\)) and [Arcadius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadius), inherited the [western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) and [eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire) empires respectively. The seat of government in the Western Roman Empire was transferred to [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna) in 408, but from 450 the emperors mostly resided in Rome.[^cite_note-55]



The [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) [sacking Rome in 410](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(410\)), by [Joseph-Noël Sylvestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph-No%C3%ABl_Sylvestre) (1890), the first time in c. 800 years that Rome had fallen to a foreign enemy

Rome, which had lost its central role in the administration of the empire, [was sacked in 410](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(410\)) by the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) led by [Alaric I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaric_I),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964115-56] but very little physical damage was done, most of which was repaired. What could not be so easily replaced were portable items such as artwork in precious metals and items for domestic use (loot). The popes embellished the city with large basilicas, such as [Santa Maria Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Maggiore) (with the collaboration of the emperors). The population of the city had fallen from 800,000 to 450–500,000 by the time the city was sacked in 455 by [Genseric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genseric), king of the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964117-57] The weak emperors of the fifth century could not stop the decay, leading to the deposition of [Romulus Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_Augustus), who resided on Ravenna, on 4 September 476. This marked the end of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) and, for many historians, the beginning of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinderHilgemann1964103-53]

The decline of the city's population was caused by the loss of grain shipments from North Africa, from 440 onward, and the unwillingness of the senatorial class to maintain donations to support a population that was too large for the resources available. Even so, strenuous efforts were made to maintain the monumental centre, the Palatine, and the imperial baths, which continued to function until the Gothic siege of 537. The [Baths of Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baths_of_Constantine_\(Rome\)) on the [Quirinal Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirinal_Hill) were even repaired in 443, and the extent of the damage exaggerated and dramatised.[^cite_note-58]

However, the city gave an appearance overall of shabbiness and decay because of the large abandoned areas due to population decline. The population declined to 500,000 by 452 and 100,000 by AD 500 (perhaps larger, though no certain figure can be known). After the Gothic siege of 537, the population dropped to 30,000 but had risen to 90,000 by the papacy of [Gregory the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I).[^cite_note-59] The population decline coincided with the general collapse of urban life in the West in the fifth and sixth centuries, with few exceptions. Subsidized state grain distributions to the poorer members of society continued right through the sixth century and probably prevented the population from falling further.[^cite_note-60] The figure of 450,000–500,000 is based on the amount of pork, 3,629,000 lbs. distributed to poorer Romans during five winter months at the rate of five Roman lbs per person per month, enough for 145,000 persons or 1/4 or 1/3 of the total population.[^cite_note-61] Grain distribution to 80,000 ticket holders at the same time suggests 400,000 (Augustus set the number at 200,000 or one-fifth of the population).

### Middle Ages

Further information: [Fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire)



The [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) [sacking Rome in 455](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(455\)), by [Karl Briullov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Briullov) (1830s)

After the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_of_the_Roman_Empire) in AD 476, Rome was first under the control of [Odoacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoacer) and then became part of the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom) before returning to [East Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) control after the [Gothic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_War_\(535%E2%80%93554\)), which devastated the city [in 546](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(546\)) and [550](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Rome_\(549%E2%80%93550\)). Its population declined from more than a million in AD 210 to 500,000 in AD 273[^cite_note-62] to 35,000 after the Gothic War (535–554),[^cite_note-63] reducing the sprawling city to groups of inhabited buildings interspersed among large areas of ruins, vegetation, vineyards and market gardens.[^cite_note-64] It is generally thought the population of the city until AD 300 was 1 million (estimates range from 2 million to 750,000) declining to 750–800,000 in AD 400, then 450–500,000 in AD 450 and down to 80–100,000 in AD 500 (though it may have been twice this).[^cite_note-65]

The Bishop of Rome, called the [Pope](23056.md), was important since the early days of Christianity because of the martyrdom of both the apostles [Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Peter) and [Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus) there. The Bishops of Rome were also seen (and still are seen by Catholics) as the successors of Peter, who is considered the first Bishop of Rome. The city thus became of increasing importance as the centre of the [Catholic Church](606848.md).

After the [Lombard invasion of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_invasion_of_Italy#Invasion_and_conquest_of_the_Italian_peninsula) (569–572), the city remained nominally Byzantine, but in reality, the popes pursued a policy of equilibrium between the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks), and the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192519-66] In 729, the Lombard king [Liutprand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liutprand,_King_of_the_Lombards) donated the north Latium town of [Sutri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutri) to the Church, starting its [temporal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporalities) power.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192519-66] In 756, [Pepin the Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepin_the_Short), after having defeated the Lombards, gave the Pope temporal jurisdiction over the [Duchy of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Rome) and the [Exarchate of Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exarchate_of_Ravenna), thus creating the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192519-66] Since this period, three powers tried to rule the city: the pope, the nobility (together with the chiefs of militias, the judges, the Senate and the populace), and the Frankish king, as king of the Lombards, patricius, and Emperor.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192519-66] These three parties (theocratic, republican, and imperial) were a characteristic of Roman life during the entire Middle Ages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192519-66] On Christmas night of 800, [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) was crowned in Rome as [Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor) by [Pope Leo III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_III): on that occasion, the city hosted for the first time the two powers whose struggle for control was to be a constant of the Middle Ages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192519-66] This event marks the beginning of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire), the first phase of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire).



Detail from an illustration by [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaello_Sanzio_da_Urbino), portraying the crowning of [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) in [Old Saint Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Saint_Peter's_Basilica), on 25 December 800

In 846, Muslim Arabs [unsuccessfully stormed the city's walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_raid_against_Rome), but managed to loot [St. Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_St._Peter's_Basilica)'s and St. Paul's basilica, both outside the city wall.[^cite_note-67] After the decay of [Carolingian power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire), Rome fell prey to feudal chaos: several noble families fought against the pope, the emperor, and each other. This period saw the rise of figures like [Theodora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodora_\(senatrix\)) and her daughter [Marozia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marozia), concubines and mothers of several popes, and of [Crescentius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescentius_the_Younger), a powerful feudal lord, who fought against the Emperors [Otto II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_II) and [Otto III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_III).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192520-68] The scandals of this period forced the papacy to reform itself: the election of the pope was reserved to the cardinals, and reform of the clergy was attempted. The driving force behind this renewal was the monk [Ildebrando da Soana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ildebrando_da_Soana), who once elected pope under the name of [Gregory VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII) became involved into the [Investiture Controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture_Controversy) against Emperor [Henry IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192520-68] Subsequently, Rome [was sacked and burned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(1084\)) by the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) under [Robert Guiscard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Guiscard) who had entered the city in support of the Pope, then besieged in [Castel Sant'Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Sant'Angelo).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192520-68]

During this period, the city was autonomously ruled by a *senatore* or *patrizio*. In the 12th century, this administration, like other European cities, evolved into the [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_commune), a new form of social organisation controlled by the new wealthy classes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192520-68] Pope [Lucius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_II) fought against the Roman commune, and the struggle was continued by his successor [Pope Eugenius III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Eugenius_III): by this stage, the commune, allied with the aristocracy, was supported by [Arnaldo da Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaldo_da_Brescia), a monk who was a religious and social reformer.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69] After the pope's death, Arnaldo was taken prisoner by Pope [Adrian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Adrian_IV), which marked the end of the commune's autonomy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69] Under [Pope Innocent III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III), whose reign marked the apogee of the papacy, the commune liquidated the senate, and replaced it with a *Senatore*, who was subject to the pope.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69]

In this period, the papacy played a role of secular importance in [Western Europe](33800.md), often acting as arbitrators between Christian [monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch) and exercising additional political powers.[^cite_note-Faus-70][^cite_note-Papal_Arbitration-71][^cite_note-72]

In 1266, [Charles of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_of_Anjou), who was heading south to fight the [Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen) on behalf of the pope, was appointed Senator. Charles founded the [Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), the university of Rome.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69] In that period the pope died, and the cardinals, summoned in [Viterbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viterbo), could not agree on his successor. This angered the people of the city, who then unroofed the building where they met and imprisoned them until they had nominated the new pope; this marked the birth of the [conclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conclave).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69] In this period the city was also shattered by continuous fights between the aristocratic families: [Annibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annibaldi_family), [Caetani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caetani), [Colonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonna_family), [Orsini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsini_family), [Conti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conti_di_Segni), nested in their fortresses built above ancient Roman edifices, fought each other to control the papacy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69]



Pope [Gregory XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_XI) returned to Rome in 1376 and ended the [Avignon Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon_Papacy).

[Pope Boniface VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Boniface_VIII) of the Caetani family was the last pope to fight for the church's [universal domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_domain?action=edit\&redlink=1); he proclaimed a crusade against the [Colonna family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonna_family) and, in 1300, called for the first [Jubilee of Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubilee_\(Christianity\)), which brought millions of [pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage) to Rome.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69] However, his hopes were crushed by the French king [Philip the Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_IV_of_France), who took him prisoner and held him hostage for three days at [Anagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagni).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192521-69] The Pope was able to return to Rome, but died a month later, it was said of shock and grief. Afterwards, a new pope faithful to the French was elected, and the papacy was [briefly relocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon_papacy) to [Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon) (1309–1377).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] During this period Rome was neglected, until a plebeian man, [Cola di Rienzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cola_di_Rienzo), came to power.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] An idealist and a lover of ancient Rome, Cola dreamed about a rebirth of the Roman Empire: after assuming power with the title of *[Tribuno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribune)*, his reforms were rejected by the populace.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] Forced to flee, Cola returned as part of the entourage of Cardinal [Albornoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_%C3%81lvarez_Carrillo_de_Albornoz), who was charged with restoring the Church's power in Italy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] Back in power for a short time, Cola was soon lynched by the populace, and Albornoz took possession of the city. In 1377, Rome became the seat of the papacy again under [Gregory XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_XI).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] The return of the pope to Rome in that year unleashed the [Western Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Schism) (1377–1418), and for the next forty years, the city was affected by the divisions which rocked the Church.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73]

### Early modern history

Main article: [Roman Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Renaissance)



Almost 500 years old, this map of Rome by [Mario Cartaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Cartaro) (from 1575) shows the city's primary monuments.



[Castel Sant'Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Sant'Angelo), or Hadrian's Mausoleum, is a Roman monument built in 134 AD, radically altered in the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, and crowned with 16th and 17th-century statues.



[Fontana della Barcaccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontana_della_Barcaccia), created by [Gian Lorenzo Bernini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Lorenzo_Bernini) in 1629

In 1418, the [Council of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Constance) settled the [Western Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Schism), and a Roman pope, [Martin V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_V), was elected.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] This brought to Rome a century of internal peace, which marked the beginning of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] The ruling popes until the first half of the 16th century, from [Nicholas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_V), founder of the [Vatican Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Library), to [Pius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_II), humanist and literate, from [Sixtus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixtus_IV), a warrior pope, to [Alexander VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_VI), immoral and [nepotist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepotism), from [Julius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_II), soldier and patron, to [Leo X Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_X), who gave his name to this period ("the century of Leo X"), all devoted their energy to the greatness and the beauty of the Eternal City and to the patronage of the arts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73]

During those years, the centre of the [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance) moved to Rome from Florence. Majestic works, as the new [Saint Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Basilica), the [Sistine Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistine_Chapel) and *[Ponte Sisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Sisto)* (the first bridge to be built across the [Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber) since antiquity, although on Roman foundations) were created. To accomplish that, the Popes engaged the best artists of the time, including [Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo), [Perugino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Perugino), [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael), [Ghirlandaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Ghirlandaio), [Luca Signorelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Signorelli), [Botticelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Botticelli), and [Cosimo Rosselli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosimo_Rosselli).

The period was also infamous for papal corruption, with many Popes fathering children, and engaging in [nepotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepotism) and [simony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simony). The corruption of the Popes and the huge expenses for their building projects led, in part, to the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation) and, in turn, the [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation). Under extravagant and rich popes, Rome was transformed into a centre of art, poetry, music, literature, education and culture. Rome became able to compete with other major European cities of the time in terms of wealth, grandeur, the arts, learning and architecture.

The Renaissance period changed the face of Rome dramatically, with works like the [Pietà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet%C3%A0_\(Michelangelo\)) by Michelangelo and the frescoes of the [Borgia Apartments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgia_Apartments). Rome reached the highest point of splendour under [Pope Julius II della Rovere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Julius_II) (1503–1513) and his successors [Leo X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_X) and [Clement VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII), both members of the [Medici family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Medici).



[Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival) in Rome, c. 1650, by [Johannes Lingelbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Lingelbach)



*A View of the Piazza Navona, Rome*, by [Hendrik Frans van Lint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Frans_van_Lint), c. 1730

In this twenty-year period, Rome became one of the greatest centres of art in the world. The old St. Peter's Basilica built by Emperor [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great)[^cite_note-74] (which by then was in a dilapidated state) was demolished and a new one begun. The city hosted artists like [Ghirlandaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridolfo_Ghirlandaio), [Perugino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Perugino), [Botticelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Botticelli) and [Bramante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donato_Bramante), who built the temple of [San Pietro in Montorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pietro_in_Montorio) and planned a great project to renovate the [Vatican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Palace). Raphael, who in Rome became one of the most famous painters of Italy, created frescoes in the [Villa Farnesina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Farnesina), the [Raphael's Rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Rooms), plus many other famous paintings. Michelangelo started the decoration of the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel and executed the famous statue of the [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses) for the tomb of Julius II.

Its economy was rich, with the presence of several Tuscan bankers, including [Agostino Chigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agostino_Chigi), who was a friend of Raphael and a patron of arts. Before his early death, Raphael also promoted for the first time the preservation of the ancient ruins. The [War of the League of Cognac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_League_of_Cognac) caused the first plunder of the city in more than five hundred years since [the previous sack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(1084\)); in 1527, the [Landsknechts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsknecht) of Emperor [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) [sacked the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(1527\)), bringing an abrupt end to the golden age of the Renaissance in Rome.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73]

Beginning with the [Council of Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Trent) in 1545, the Church began the Counter-Reformation in response to the Reformation, a large-scale questioning of the Church's authority on spiritual matters and governmental affairs. This loss of confidence led to major shifts of power away from the Church.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] Under the popes from [Pius IV Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_IV) to [Sixtus V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixtus_V), Rome became the centre of a reformed Catholicism and saw the building of new monuments which celebrated the papacy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192523-75] The popes and cardinals of the 17th and early 18th centuries continued the movement by having the city's landscape enriched with baroque buildings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192523-75]

This was another nepotistic age; the new aristocratic families ([Barberini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barberini_family), [Pamphili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamphili_family), [Chigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chigi_family), [Rospigliosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rospigliosi_family), [Altieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altieri_family), [Odescalchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odescalchi_family)) were protected by their respective popes, who built huge baroque *[palazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace#Italy)* for their relatives.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192523-75] During the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment), new ideas reached the Eternal City, where the papacy supported archaeological studies and improved the people's welfare.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73] But not everything went well for the Church during the Counter-Reformation. There were setbacks in the attempts to assert the Church's power, a notable example being in 1773 when Pope [Clement XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIV) was forced by secular powers to have the [Jesuit order suppressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppression_of_the_Society_of_Jesus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBertarelli192522-73]

### Late modern and contemporary



[Bersaglieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bersaglieri) troops breaching the [Aurelian Walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelian_Walls) at [Porta Pia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Pia) during the *[Capture of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Rome)* (1870), the final event of the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification). Painting by [Carlo Ademollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Ademollo).

The rule of the Popes was interrupted by the short-lived [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic_\(18th_century\)) (1798–1800), which was established under the influence of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution). The [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States) were restored in June 1800, but during [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon)'s reign Rome was [annexed as a *Département*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_\(department\)) of the [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire): first as *Département du Tibre* (1809–1810) and then as *Département Rome* (1810–1814). After the fall of Napoleon, the Papal States were reconstituted by a decision of the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) of 1814.

In 1849, [a second Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic_\(19th_century\)) was proclaimed during a year of [revolutions in 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_in_1848). Two of the most influential figures of the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification), [Giuseppe Mazzini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Mazzini) and [Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi), fought for the short-lived republic.

Rome then became the focus of hopes of Italian reunification after the rest of Italy was united as the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) in 1861 with the temporary capital in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence). That year Rome was declared the capital of Italy even though it was still under the Pope's control. During the 1860s, the last vestiges of the Papal States were under French protection thanks to the foreign policy of [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III). French troops were stationed in the region under Papal control. In 1870 the French troops were withdrawn due to the outbreak of the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War). Italian troops were able to [capture Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Rome) entering the city through a breach near [Porta Pia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Pia). [Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX) declared himself a [prisoner in the Vatican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_in_the_Vatican). In 1871 the capital of Italy was moved from Florence to Rome.[^cite_note-76] In 1870 the population of the city was 212,000, all of whom lived with the area circumscribed by the ancient city, and in 1920, the population was 660,000. A significant portion lived outside the walls in the north and across the Tiber in the Vatican area.



Bombardment of Rome by [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) planes, 1943

Soon after World War I in late 1922 Rome witnessed the rise of [Italian Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Fascism) led by [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), who led a [march on the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_on_Rome). He did away with democracy by 1926, eventually declaring a new [Italian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Italy_\(fascist\)) and allying Italy with [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in 1938. Mussolini demolished fairly large parts of the city centre in order to build wide avenues and squares which were supposed to celebrate the fascist regime and the resurgence and glorification of classical Rome.[^cite_note-77] The interwar period saw a rapid growth in the city's population which surpassed one million inhabitants soon after 1930. During World War II, due to the art treasuries and the presence of the Vatican, Rome largely escaped the tragic destiny of other European cities. However, on 19 July 1943, the [San Lorenzo district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartiere_San_Lorenzo) was [subject to Allied bombing raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Rome_in_World_War_II), resulting in about 3,000 fatalities and 11,000 injuries, of whom another 1,500 died.[^cite_note-78] Mussolini [was arrested on 25 July 1943](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Fascist_regime_in_Italy). On the date of the [Italian Armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Cassibile) 8 September 1943 the city was occupied by the Germans. Allied bombing raids continued throughout 1943 and extended into 1944. Rome was liberated on 4 June 1944.

Rome developed greatly after the war as part of the "[Italian economic miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_economic_miracle)" of post-war reconstruction and modernisation in the 1950s and early 1960s. During this period, the years of *la dolce vita* ("the sweet life"), Rome became a fashionable city, with popular classic films such as *[Ben Hur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Hur_\(1959_film\))*, *[Quo Vadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quo_Vadis_\(1951_film\))*, *[Roman Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Holiday)* and *[La Dolce Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Dolce_Vita)* filmed in the city's iconic [Cinecittà Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinecitt%C3%A0). The rising trend in population growth continued until the mid-1980s when the *comune* had more than 2.8 million residents. After this, the population declined slowly as people began to move to nearby suburbs.

## Geography



Satellite image of Rome

Rome is in the [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio) region of [central Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Italy) on the [Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber) ([Italian](14708.md): *Tevere*) river. The original settlement developed on hills that faced onto a ford beside the [Tiber Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber_Island), the only natural ford of the river in this area. The Rome of the Kings was built on seven hills: the [Aventine Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aventine_Hill), the [Caelian Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caelian_Hill), the [Capitoline Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitoline_Hill), the [Esquiline Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquiline_Hill), the [Palatine Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_Hill), the [Quirinal Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirinal_Hill), and the [Viminal Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viminal_Hill). Modern Rome is also crossed by another river, the [Aniene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aniene), which flows into the Tiber north of the historic centre.

Although the city centre is about 24 km (15 mi) inland from the [Tyrrhenian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea), the city territory extends to the shore, where the south-western district of [Ostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostia_\(Rome\)) is located. The altitude of the central part of Rome ranges from 13 m (43 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) (at the base of the [Pantheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome)) to 139 m (456 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) (the peak of [Monte Mario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Mario)).[^cite_note-79] The *Comune* of Rome covers an overall area of about 1,285 km<sup>2</sup> (496 sq mi), including many green areas.

### Parks and gardens

Main article: [List of parks and gardens in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_and_gardens_in_Rome)



The [Temple of Aesculapius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Aesculapius_\(Villa_Borghese\)), in the [Villa Borghese gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Borghese_gardens)

Public parks and nature reserves cover a large area in Rome, and the city has one of the largest areas of green space among European capitals.[^cite_note-80] The most notable part of this green space is represented by the large number of villas and landscaped gardens created by the Italian aristocracy. While most of the parks surrounding the villas were destroyed during the building boom of the late 19th century, some of them remain. The most notable of these are the [Villa Borghese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Borghese_gardens), [Villa Ada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Ada), and [Villa Doria Pamphili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Doria_Pamphili). Villa Doria Pamphili is west of the [Janiculum Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janiculum), comprising some 1.8 km<sup>2</sup> (0.7 [sq mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile)). The [Villa Sciarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Sciarra_\(Rome\)) is on the hill, with playgrounds for children and shaded walking areas. In the nearby area of [Trastevere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trastevere), the [Orto Botanico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orto_Botanico_dell'Universit%C3%A0_di_Roma_%22La_Sapienza%22) (Botanical Garden) is a cool and shady green space. The old Roman hippodrome ([Circus Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Maximus)) is another large green space: it has few trees but is overlooked by the Palatine and the Rose Garden ('roseto comunale'). Nearby is the lush [Villa Celimontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Celimontana), close to the gardens surrounding the [Baths of Caracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baths_of_Caracalla). The Villa Borghese garden is the best known large green space in Rome, with famous art galleries among its shaded walks. Overlooking [Piazza del Popolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_del_Popolo) and the Spanish Steps are the gardens of the [Pincio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pincio) and [Villa Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Medici). There is also a notable pine wood at [Castelfusano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelfusano), near Ostia. Rome also has a number of regional parks of much more recent origin, including the [Pineto Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineto_Regional_Park) and the [Appian Way Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appian_Way_Regional_Park). There are also nature reserves at Marcigliana and at Tenuta di Castelporziano.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Rome)



[Stone pines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_pine) in the [Villa Doria Pamphili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Doria_Pamphili)

Rome has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*),[^cite_note-81] with hot, dry summers and mild, humid winters.

Its average annual temperature is above 21 °C (70 °F) during the day and 9 °C (48 °F) at night. In the coldest month, January, the average temperature is 12.6 °C (54.7 °F) during the day and 2.1 °C (35.8 °F) at night. In the warmest month, August, the average temperature is 31.7 °C (89.1 °F) during the day and 17.3 °C (63.1 °F) at night.

December, January and February are the coldest months, with a daily mean temperature of approximately 8 °C (46 °F). Temperatures during these months generally vary between 10 and 15 °C (50 and 59 °F) during the day and between 3 and 5 °C (37 and 41 °F) at night, with colder or warmer spells occurring frequently. Snowfall is rare but not unheard of, with light snow or flurries occurring on some winters, generally without accumulation, and major snowfalls on a very rare occurrence (the most recent ones were in 2018, 2012 and 1986).[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

The average [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) is 75%, varying from 72% in July to 77% in November. Sea temperatures vary from a low of 13.9 °C (57.0 °F) in February to a high of 25.0 °C (77.0 °F) in August.[^cite_note-seatemperature.org2-85]

The highest temperature ever recorded in Rome was 42.9 °C (109.2 °F) on 18 July 2023.[^cite_note-record-86]

By 550 BC, Rome was the second largest city in Italy after only Taras (modern [Taranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taranto)) on the [Salento Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salento_Peninsula).[^cite_note-92] It had an area of about 285 ha (700 acres) and an estimated population of 35,000. Other sources suggest the population was just under 100,000 from 600 to 500 BC.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] When the Republic was founded in 509 BC the census recorded a population of 130,000.[^cite_note-95] The republic included the city itself and the immediate surroundings. Other sources suggest a population of 150,000 in 500 BC. It surpassed 300,000 by 150 BC.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

The size of the city at the time of the Emperor [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) is a matter of speculation, with estimates based on grain distribution, grain imports, aqueduct capacity, city limits, population density, census reports, and assumptions about the number of unreported women, children and slaves providing a very wide range. Glenn Storey estimates 450,000 people, [Whitney Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Oates) estimates 1.2 million, Neville Morely provides a rough estimate of 800,000 and excludes earlier suggestions of 2 million.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] Estimates of the city's population towards and after the end of the Roman empire also vary. A.H.M. Jones estimated the population at 650,000 in the mid-fifth century. The damage caused by the sackings may have been overestimated. The population had already started to decline from the late fourth century onward, although around the middle of the fifth century it seems that Rome continued to be the most populous city of the two parts of the Empire.[^cite_note-105] According to Krautheimer it was still close to 800,000 in 400 AD; had declined to 500,000 by 452, and dwindled to perhaps 100,000 in 500 AD. After the Gothic Wars, 535–552, the population may have dwindled temporarily to 30,000. During the pontificate of [Pope Gregory I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I) (590–604), it may have reached 90,000, augmented by refugees.[^cite_note-106] Lancon estimates 500,000 based on the number of 'incisi' enrolled as eligible to receive bread, oil and wine rations; the number fell to 120,000 in the [reform of 419](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_of_419?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-107] Neil Christie, citing free rations for the poorest, estimated 500,000 in the mid-fifth century and still a quarter of a million at the end of the century.[^cite_note-108] Novel 36 of Emperor [Valentinian III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentinian_III) records 3.629 million pounds of pork to be distributed to the needy at 5 lbs. per month for the five winter months, sufficient for 145,000 recipients. This has been used to suggest a population of just under 500,000. Supplies of grain remained steady until the seizure of the remaining provinces of North Africa in 439 by the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals), and may have continued to some degree afterwards for a while. The city's population declined to less than 50,000 people in the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages) from 700 onward. It continued to stagnate or shrink until the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).[^cite_note-109]

When the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) annexed Rome in 1870, the city had a population of about 225,000. Less than half the city within the walls was built up in 1881 when the population recorded was 275,000. This increased to 600,000 by the eve of World War I. The [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) regime of Mussolini tried to block an excessive demographic rise of the city but failed to prevent it from reaching one million people by the early 1930s. Population growth continued after the Second World War, helped by a post-war economic boom. A construction boom also created many suburbs during the 1950s and 1960s.

As of 2026, the population is 2,745,062, of which 47.6% are males, and 52.4% are females. Minors make up 14.4% of the population, and seniors make up 24.4%.[^cite_note-population-4]

The urban area of Rome extends beyond the administrative city limits with a population of around 3.9 million.[^cite_note-World_Urban_Areas-110] Between 3.2 and 4.2 million people live in the [Rome metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

### Language

Main articles: [Roman dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_dialect) and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin)



Advertisement in [Romanesco dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesco_dialect) at a subway station in Rome

Rome's historic contribution to language in a worldwide sense is extensive. Through the process of [Romanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_\(cultural\)), the peoples of Italy, [Gallia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallia), the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) and [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia) developed languages which derive directly from Latin and were adopted in large areas of the world, all through cultural influence, colonisation and migration. Moreover, also modern English, because of the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest), borrowed a large percentage of its vocabulary from the Latin language. The [Roman or Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet) is the most widely used writing system in the world used by the greatest number of languages.[^cite_note-120]

The medieval [Roman dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_dialect) belonged to the southern family of Italian dialects, and was thus much closer to the [Neapolitan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_language) than to the Florentine.[^cite_note-sie-121][^cite_note-122] A typical example of Romanesco of that period is *[Vita di Cola di Rienzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronica_dell'Anonimo_Romano?action=edit\&redlink=1)* ("Life of [Cola di Rienzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cola_di_Rienzo)"), written by an anonymous Roman during the 14th century.[^cite_note-sie-121] Starting with the 16th century, the Roman dialect underwent a stronger and stronger influence from the [Tuscan dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_dialect) (from which modern Italian derives) starting with the reigns of the two [Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Medici) popes ([Leo X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_X) and [Clement VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_VII)) and with the [Sack of Rome in 1527](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_\(1527\)), two events which provoked a large immigration from [Tuscany](21967242.md).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-dia-124]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Rome)

See also: [Vatican City](32408.md) and [History of the Jews in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Rome)



[Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbasilica_of_Saint_John_Lateran), Rome's Cathedral, built in 324, and partly rebuilt between 1660 and 1734



One of the [Seven Pilgrim Churches of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Pilgrim_Churches_of_Rome), [Santa Maria Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Maggiore) is the city's largest [Catholic Marian church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Marian_church_buildings).



Celebration of the 2777th [Natale di Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natale_di_Roma) at the [Circus Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Maximus)

Much like the rest of Italy, Rome is predominantly [Christian](5211.md), and the city has been an important centre of religion and [pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage) for centuries, the base of the ancient Roman religion with the [pontifex maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifex_Maximus) and later the seat of the [Vatican](13393.md) and the pope. Before the arrival of the Christians in Rome, the [Religio Romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_ancient_Rome) (literally, the "Roman Religion") was the major religion of the city in classical antiquity. The first gods held sacred by the Romans were [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_\(mythology\)), the Most High, and [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_\(mythology\)), the god of war, and father of Rome's twin founders, [Romulus and Remus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_and_Remus), according to tradition. Other deities such as [Vesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesta_\(mythology\)) and [Minerva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minerva) were honoured. Rome was also the base of several mystery cults, such as [Mithraism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithraic_Mysteries). Later, after [St Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter) and [St Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle) were martyred in the city, and the first Christians began to arrive, Rome became Christian, and the [Old St. Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_St._Peter's_Basilica) was constructed in 313 AD. Despite some interruptions (such as the [Avignon papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon_Papacy)), Rome has for centuries been the home of the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) and the [Bishop of Rome](23056.md), otherwise known as the Pope.

Despite the fact that Rome is home to the [Vatican City](32408.md) and St. Peter's Basilica, Rome's cathedral is the [Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbasilica_of_Saint_John_Lateran), in the south-east of the city centre. There are around 900 churches in Rome in total. Aside from the cathedral itself, some others of note include the [Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_Maggiore), the [Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls), the [Basilica di San Clemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Clemente), [San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlo_alle_Quattro_Fontane) and the [Church of the Gesù](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Gesu). There are also the ancient [Catacombs of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs_of_Rome) underneath the city. Numerous highly important religious educational institutions are also in Rome, such as the [Pontifical Lateran University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Lateran_University), [Pontifical Biblical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Biblical_Institute), [Pontifical Gregorian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Gregorian_University), and [Pontifical Oriental Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Oriental_Institute).

The territory of [Vatican City](32408.md) is part of the *Mons Vaticanus* ([Vatican Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Hill)), and of the adjacent former Vatican Fields, where [St. Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Basilica), the [Apostolic Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Palace), the [Sistine Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistine_Chapel), and museums were built, along with various other buildings. The area was part of the Roman [rione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rione) of [Borgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgo_\(rione_of_Rome\)) until 1929. Being separated from the city on the west bank of the [Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber), the area was a suburb that was protected by being included within the walls of [Leo IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_IV), later expanded by the current fortification walls of [Paul III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_III), [Pius IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IV), and [Urban VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII). When the [Lateran Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateran_Treaty) of 1929 that created the Vatican state was being prepared, the boundaries of the proposed territory were influenced by the fact that much of it was all but enclosed by this loop.

Rome has been a major [Christian pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage) site since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). People from all over the [Christian world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_world) visit Vatican City, within the city of Rome, the seat of the papacy. The city became a major [pilgrimage](23763.md) site during the Middle Ages. Apart from brief periods as an independent city during the Middle Ages, Rome kept its status as Papal capital and holy city for centuries, even when the Papacy [briefly relocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon_papacy) to [Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon) (1309–1377). Catholics believe that the Vatican is the last resting place of St. Peter. Pilgrimages to Rome can involve visits to many sites, both within Vatican City and in Italian territory. A popular stopping point is the [Pilate's stairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scala_sancta): these are, according to the Christian tradition, the steps that led up to the [praetorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetorium) of [Pontius Pilate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontius_Pilate) in [Jerusalem](16043.md), which [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) stood on during his [Passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_\(Christianity\)) on his way to trial.[^cite_note-125]

In addition, since the end of the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic), Rome has hosted a [Jewish](25955086.md) community,[^cite_note-126] which was once based in [Trastevere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trastevere), and later confined to living in a [ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Ghetto) that was eventually abolished in 1870. In the area of the former ghetto lies the synagogue, the *[Tempio Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Synagogue_of_Rome)*.

Rome also hosts multiple [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) temples,[^cite_note-127] and a variety of [Roman modern pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstructionist_Roman_religion) temples [^cite_note-128] held by the [Associazione Tradizionale Pietas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietas_Comunit%C3%A0_Gentile) which every year takes part in the religious festivities of the [Natale di Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natale_di_Roma), historically known as *Dies Romana* and also referred to as Romaia, the festival linked to the [foundation of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_of_Rome), celebrated on 21 April.[^cite_note-Plutarch12-129] According to [legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend), [Romulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus) is said to have founded the city of Rome on 21 April, [753 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/753_BC). From this date, the Roman chronology derived its system, known by the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) phrase *[Ab Urbe condita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab_Urbe_condita)*, meaning *"from the founding of the City"*, which counted the years from this presumed foundation.

## Government

See also: [Mayor of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Rome), [City Council of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Council_of_Rome), [Elections in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Rome), and [Administrative subdivisions of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_subdivisions_of_Rome)



[Palazzo Senatorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_del_Campidoglio), seat of the municipality of Rome. It has been a [town hall](383746.md) since 1144, making it the oldest town hall in the world.

[^cite_note-Falconi-130]



[Palazzo del Quirinale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_del_Quirinale), current seat of the [President of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy)

Rome constitutes a *[comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune) speciale*, named *"Roma Capitale"*,[^cite_note-131] and is the largest both in terms of land area and population among the 8,101 *comuni* of Italy. It is governed by a mayor and a city council. The seat of the *comune* is the *Palazzo Senatorio* on the [Capitoline Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitoline_Hill), the historic seat of the city government. The local administration in Rome is commonly referred to as *"Campidoglio"*, the Italian name of the hill. *Palazzo Senatorio*, seat of the municipality of Rome, has been a [town hall](383746.md) since AD 1144, making it the oldest town hall in the world.[^cite_note-Falconi-130]

Since 1972, the city has been divided into administrative areas, called *municipi* (sing. *[municipio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipio)*) (until 2001 named *circoscrizioni*).[^cite_note-132] They were created for administrative reasons to increase decentralisation in the city. Each *municipio* is governed by a president and a council of twenty-five members who are elected by its residents every five years. The *municipi* frequently cross the boundaries of the traditional, non-administrative divisions of the city. The municipi were originally 20, then 19,[^cite_note-133] and in 2013, their number was reduced to 15.[^cite_note-134]

Rome is also divided into differing types of non-administrative units. The historic centre is divided into 22 *[rioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rioni_of_Rome)*, all of which are located within the [Aurelian Walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelian_Walls) except [Prati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prati) and [Borgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgo_\(rione_of_Rome\)). These originate from the [14 regions of Augustan Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_regions_of_Augustan_Rome), which evolved in the Middle Ages into the [medieval rioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_regions_of_Medieval_Rome).[^cite_note-135] In the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), under Pope [Sixtus V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixtus_V), they again reached fourteen, and their boundaries were finally defined under [Pope Benedict XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV) in 1743.

Rome is the principal town of the [Metropolitan City of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Rome), operative since 1 January 2015. The Metropolitan City replaced the old [provincia di Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Rome), which included the city's metropolitan area and extends further north until [Civitavecchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitavecchia). The Metropolitan City of Rome is the largest by area in Italy. At 5,352 km<sup>2</sup> (2,066 sq mi), its dimensions are comparable to the region of [Liguria](71864.md). Moreover, the city is also the capital of the [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio) region.[^cite_note-136]

Rome is the national capital of Italy and is the seat of the [Italian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Italy). The official residences of the [President of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy) and the [Italian Prime Minister](273741.md), the seats of both houses of the [Italian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Italy) and that of the [Italian Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Italy) are located in the historic centre. The state ministries are spread out around the city; these include the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, which is located in [Palazzo della Farnesina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Farnesina) near the Olympic stadium.

### International relations



[Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) headquarters in Rome, Circo Massimo

Among the [global cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_cities), Rome is unique in having two sovereign entities located entirely within its city limits, the Holy See, represented by the Vatican City State, and the territorially smaller [Sovereign Military Order of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_Military_Order_of_Malta). The Vatican is an enclave of the Italian [capital city](181337.md) and a sovereign possession of the [Holy See](13393.md), which is the Diocese of Rome and the supreme government of the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church). For this reason, Rome has sometimes been described as the capital of two states.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] Rome is the seat of the so-called "Polo Romano" made up by three main international agencies of the [United Nations](31769.md): the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization), the [World Food Programme](33582.md) and the [International Fund for Agricultural Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fund_for_Agricultural_Development).[^cite_note-139]

Rome has traditionally been involved in the process of European political integration. The [Treaties of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_the_EU) are located in [Palazzo della Farnesina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Farnesina). In 1957 the city hosted the signing of the [Treaty of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_Rome), which established the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community) (predecessor to the [European Union](9317.md)), and also played host to the official signing of the proposed [European Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_a_Constitution_for_Europe) in July 2004. Rome is the seat of the [European Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Olympic_Committee) and of the [NATO Defense College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Defense_College). The city is the place where the [Statute of the International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court) and the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) were formulated. The city hosts also other important international entities such as the [International Development Law Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Development_Law_Organization), the [International Centre for the Study of the Preservation and Restoration of Cultural Property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_the_Study_of_the_Preservation_and_Restoration_of_Cultural_Property) and the [UNIDROIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIDROIT) (International Institute for the Unification of Private Law).

### Architecture

This section is an excerpt from [Architecture of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Rome).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Architecture_of_Rome\&action=edit)]

The [architecture of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Rome) over the centuries has greatly developed from [Ancient Roman architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_architecture) to [Italian modern and contemporary architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_modern_and_contemporary_architecture). Rome was once the world's main epicentres of [Classical architecture](52687.md), developing new forms such as the [arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch), the [dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome) and the [vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_\(architecture\)). The [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture) style in the 11th, 12th and 13th centuries was also widely used in Roman [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture), and later the city became one of the main centres of [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) and [Baroque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture).[^cite_note-Architecture_of_Rome_r1-161] Rome's cityscape is also widely [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) and [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_architecture) in style.

#### Fountains and aqueducts

Main articles: [List of fountains in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fountains_in_Rome) and [List of aqueducts in the city of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aqueducts_in_the_city_of_Rome)



Construction of the [Trevi Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevi_Fountain) began during the time of Ancient Rome and was completed in 1762 by a design of [Nicola Salvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Salvi).

Rome is a city known for its numerous fountains, built-in all different styles, from Classical and Medieval, to Baroque and Neoclassical. The city has had [fountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain) for more than two thousand years, and they have provided drinking water and decorated the [piazzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza) of Rome. During the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), in 98 AD, according to [Sextus Julius Frontinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextus_Julius_Frontinus), the Roman consul who was named *[curator aquarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curator_Aquarum)* or guardian of the water of the city, Rome had nine [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_\(watercourse\)) which fed 39 monumental fountains and 591 public basins, not counting the water supplied to the Imperial household, baths, and owners of private villas. Each of the major fountains was connected to two different aqueducts, in case one was shut down for service.[^cite_note-162]

During the 17th and 18th century, the Roman popes reconstructed other degraded Roman aqueducts and built new display fountains to mark their termini, launching the golden age of the Roman fountain. The fountains of Rome, like the paintings of [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens), were expressions of the new style of Baroque art. In these fountains, sculpture became the principal element, and the water was used simply to animate and decorate the sculptures. They, like baroque gardens, were "a visual representation of confidence and power".[^cite_note-163]

#### Statues

See also: [Talking statues of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_statues_of_Rome)



Fontana dei Fiumi by [Gian Lorenzo Bernini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Lorenzo_Bernini), 1648

Rome is well known for its statues but, in particular, the [talking statues of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_statues_of_Rome). These are usually ancient statues which have become popular soapboxes for political and social discussion, and places for people to (often satirically) voice their opinions. There are two main talking statues: the [Pasquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasquino) and the [Marforio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marforio), yet there are four other noted ones: *[il Babuino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Babuino)*, [Madama Lucrezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madama_Lucrezia), *[il Facchino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Facchino)* and [Abbot Luigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbot_Luigi). Most of these statues are ancient Roman or classical, and most of them also depict mythical gods, ancient people or legendary figures; il Pasquino represents [Menelaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus), Abbot Luigi is an unknown Roman magistrate, *il Babuino* is supposed to be [Silenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silenus), Marforio represents [Oceanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanus), Madama Lucrezia is a bust of [Isis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isis), and *il Facchino* is the only non-Roman statue, created in 1580, and not representing anyone in particular. They are often, due to their status, covered with placards or [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti) expressing political ideas and points of view. Other statues in the city, which are not related to the talking statues, include those of the Ponte Sant'Angelo, or several monuments scattered across the city, such as that to [Giordano Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giordano_Bruno) in the Campo de'Fiori.

#### Obelisks and columns

Main article: [List of obelisks in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_obelisks_in_Rome)



[Flaminio Obelisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaminio_Obelisk), [Piazza del Popolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_del_Popolo)

The city hosts eight [ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) and five [ancient Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) [obelisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obelisk), together with a number of more modern obelisks; there was also formerly (until 2005) an [ancient Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksumite_Empire) obelisk in Rome.[^cite_note-164] The city contains some of obelisks in [piazzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza), such as in [Piazza Navona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Navona), [St Peter's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter's_Square), [Piazza Montecitorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_di_Monte_Citorio), and [Piazza del Popolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_del_Popolo), and others in [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa), [thermae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermae) parks and gardens, such as in [Villa Celimontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mattei), the [Baths of Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baths_of_Diocletian), and the [Pincian Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pincian_Hill). Moreover, the centre of Rome hosts also [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan's_Column)'s and [Antonine Column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_of_Marcus_Aurelius), two ancient Roman columns with spiral relief. The Column of Marcus Aurelius is located in [Piazza Colonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Colonna) and it was built around AD 180 by [Commodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodus) in memory of his parents. The Column of Marcus Aurelius was inspired by Trajan's Column at [Trajan's Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan's_Forum), which is part of the [Imperial Fora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Fora).[^cite_note-165]

#### Bridges

Main article: [List of bridges in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bridges_in_Rome)



[Ponte Vittorio Emanuele II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Vittorio_Emanuele_II) at sunset

The city of Rome contains numerous famous bridges which cross the [Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber). The only bridge to remain unaltered until today from the classical age is [Ponte dei Quattro Capi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pons_Fabricius), which connects the [Isola Tiberina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isola_Tiberina) with the left bank. The other surviving – albeit modified – ancient Roman bridges crossing the Tiber are [Ponte Cestio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pons_Cestius), [Ponte Sant'Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Sant'Angelo) and [Ponte Milvio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Milvio). Considering [Ponte Nomentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Nomentano), also built during ancient Rome, which crosses the [Aniene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aniene), currently there are five ancient Roman bridges still remaining in the city.[^cite_note-166] Other noteworthy bridges are [Ponte Sisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Sisto), the first bridge built in the Renaissance above Roman foundations; [Ponte Rotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Rotto), actually the only remaining arch of the ancient *Pons Aemilius*, collapsed during the flood of 1598 and demolished at the end of the 19th century; and [Ponte Vittorio Emanuele II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Vittorio_Emanuele_II), a modern bridge connecting Corso Vittorio Emanuele and Borgo. Most of the city's public bridges were built in Classical or Renaissance style, but also in Baroque, Neoclassical and Modern styles. According to the *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica)*, the finest ancient bridge remaining in Rome is the Ponte Sant'Angelo, which was completed in 135 AD, and was decorated with ten statues of the angels, designed by [Bernini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Lorenzo_Bernini) in 1688.[^cite_note-167]

#### Catacombs

Main article: [Catacombs of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs_of_Rome)



The [Vatican Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Necropolis) are the location of many papal burials.

Rome has an extensive amount of ancient catacombs, or underground burial places under or near the city, of which there are at least forty, some discovered only in recent decades. Though most famous for Christian burials, they include [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_ancient_Rome) and Jewish burials, either in separate catacombs or mixed together. The first large-scale catacombs were excavated from the 2nd century onwards. Originally they were carved through [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff), a soft [volcanic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_rock), outside the boundaries of the city, because [Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law) forbade burial places within city limits. Currently, maintenance of the catacombs is in the hands of the [Papacy](23056.md) which has invested in the [Salesians of Don Bosco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco) the supervision of the Catacombs of St. Callixtus on the outskirts of Rome.

### Entertainment and performing arts

Main articles: [Music in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_in_Rome) and [Events in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Events_in_Rome)



The [Teatro dell'Opera di Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_dell'Opera_di_Roma) at the Piazza Beniamino Gigli

Rome is an important centre for music, and it has an intense musical scene, including several prestigious music conservatories and theatres. It hosts the [Accademia Nazionale di Santa Cecilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_di_Santa_Cecilia) (founded in 1585), for which new concert halls have been built in the new [Parco della Musica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_della_Musica), one of the largest musical venues in the world. Rome also has an opera house, the [Teatro dell'Opera di Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_dell'Opera_di_Roma), as well as several minor musical institutions. The city also played host to the [Eurovision Song Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1991) in 1991 and the [MTV Europe Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Europe_Music_Awards_2004) in 2004.

Rome has also had a major impact on music history. The [Roman School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_School_\(music\)) was a group of composers of predominantly church music, which were active in the city during the 16th and 17th centuries, therefore spanning the late [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_music) and early [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music) eras. The term also refers to the music they produced. Many of the composers had a direct connection to the [Vatican](13393.md) and the [papal chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistine_Chapel), though they worked at several churches; stylistically they are often contrasted with the [Venetian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_School_\(music\)) of composers, a concurrent movement which was much more progressive. By far the most famous composer of the Roman School is [Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Pierluigi_da_Palestrina), whose name has been associated for four hundred years with smooth, clear, [polyphonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphony) perfection. However, there were other composers working in Rome, and in a variety of styles and forms.

Between 1960 and 1970 Rome was considered to be as a "new Hollywood" because of the many actors and directors who worked there; Via Vittorio Veneto had transformed into a glamour place where you could meet famous people.[^cite_note-168]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Roman cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_cuisine)



*Spaghetti [alla carbonara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonara)*, a typical Roman dish



*Spaghetti [cacio e pepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacio_e_pepe)*, a typical Roman dish

Rome's cuisine has evolved through centuries and periods of social, cultural, and political changes. Rome became a major gastronomical centre during the [ancient age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome). [Ancient Roman cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_cuisine) was highly influenced by Ancient Greek culture, and after, the empire's enormous expansion exposed Romans to many new, provincial culinary habits and cooking techniques.[^cite_note-171]

Later, during the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), Rome became well known as a centre of high-cuisine, since some of the best chefs of the time worked for the popes. An example of this was [Bartolomeo Scappi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeo_Scappi), who was a chef working for [Pius IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IV); he acquired fame in 1570 when his cookbook *Opera dell'arte del cucinare* was published. In the book he lists approximately 1,000 recipes of the Renaissance [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine) and describes cooking techniques and tools, giving the first known picture of a [fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork).[^cite_note-172]

The [Testaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testaccio), Rome's trade and slaughterhouse area, was often known as the "belly" or "slaughterhouse" of Rome, and was inhabited by butchers, or *vaccinari*.[^cite_note-Eyewitness_Travel_2006_pg._312_-_313-173] The most common or ancient Roman cuisine included the "fifth quarter".[^cite_note-Eyewitness_Travel_2006_pg._312_-_313-173] The old-fashioned *[coda alla vaccinara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coda_alla_vaccinara)* (oxtail cooked in the way of butchers)[^cite_note-Eyewitness_Travel_2006_pg._312_-_313-173] is still one of the city's most popular meals and is part of most of Rome's restaurants' menus. Lamb is also a very popular part of Roman cuisine, and is often roasted with spices and herbs.[^cite_note-Eyewitness_Travel_2006_pg._312_-_313-173]

In the modern age, the city developed its own peculiar cuisine, based on products of the nearby [Campagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Campagna), [globe artichokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artichoke) are common.[^cite_note-174] In parallel, Roman Jews – present in the city since the 1st century BC – developed their own cuisine, the *cucina giudaico-romanesca*. The Roman cuisine widely use offal, resulting in dishes such as the entrail-based [rigatoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigatoni) with *[pajata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigatoni_con_la_Pajata)* sauce. *[Abbacchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbacchio)* is a meat dish based on lamb from the Roman cuisine.

Examples of Roman dishes include *[saltimbocca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltimbocca) alla romana* – a veal cutlet, Roman-style, topped with raw ham and sage and simmered with white wine and butter; *[carciofi alla romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carciofi_alla_romana)* – artichokes Roman-style, outer leaves removed, stuffed with mint, garlic, breadcrumbs and braised; *[carciofi alla giudia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carciofi_alla_giudia)* – artichokes fried in olive oil, typical of Roman Jewish cooking, outer leaves removed, stuffed with mint, garlic, breadcrumbs and braised; *[spaghetti alla carbonara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonara)* – [spaghetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaghetti) with [bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon), [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_\(food\)) and *[pecorino romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_romano)*; *[spaghetti cacio e pepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacio_e_pepe)* – spaghetti with *[pecorino romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_romano)* and [black pepper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_pepper); and *[gnocchi di semolino alla romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnocchi_alla_romana)* – [semolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semolina) dumpling, Roman-style.[^cite_note-175]

### Cinema

Main articles: [List of films set in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_set_in_Rome) and [List of films set in ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_set_in_ancient_Rome)



Entrance to the [Cinecittà studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinecitt%C3%A0), the largest film studio in [Europe](9239.md)

[^cite_note-cinecittà-176]

Rome hosts the [Cinecittà Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinecitt%C3%A0),[^cite_note-romefile1-177] the largest film and television production facility in continental Europe and the centre of the [Italian cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Italy), where many of today's biggest box office hits are filmed. The 99-acre (40 ha) studio complex is 9.0 km (5.6 mi) from the centre of Rome and is part of one of the biggest production communities in the world, second only to [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles,_California), with well over 5,000 professionals – from period costume makers to visual effects specialists. More than 3,000 productions have been made on its lot.[^cite_note-178]

Founded in 1937 by [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), the studios were bombed by the [Western Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) during the Second World War. In the 1950s, Cinecittà was the filming location for several large American film productions, and subsequently became the studio most closely associated with [Federico Fellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Fellini). Today, Cinecittà is the only studio in the world with pre-production, production, and full post-production facilities on one lot, allowing directors and producers to walk in with their script and "walkout" with a completed film.

[^cite_note-179]

[Association football](10568.md) is the most popular sport in Rome, as in the rest of the country. [Stadio dei Marmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_dei_Marmi)

Rome hosted the [1960 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics), with great success, using many ancient sites such as the [Villa Borghese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Borghese_gardens) and the [Thermae of Caracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baths_of_Caracalla) as venues. For the Olympic Games many new facilities were built, notably the new large Olympic Stadium (which was then enlarged and renewed to host several matches and the final of the [1990 FIFA World Cup](157232.md)), the [Stadio Flaminio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Flaminio), the Villaggio Olimpico (Olympic Village, created to host the athletes and redeveloped after the games as a residential district), ecc. Rome made a [bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_bid_for_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) to host the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics) but it was withdrawn.[^cite_note-olympic.org-181][^cite_note-Bladesplace.id.au-182]

Further, Rome hosted the [EuroBasket 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1991) and is home to the internationally recognised basketball team [Virtus Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtus_Roma). [Rugby union](25405.md) is gaining wider acceptance. Until 2011 the Stadio Flaminio was the home stadium for the [Italy national rugby union team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team), which has been playing in the [Six Nations Championship](26547.md) since 2000. The team now plays home games at the Stadio Olimpico because the Stadio Flaminio needs works of renovation in order to improve both its capacity and safety. Rome is home to local rugby union teams such as [Rugby Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Roma_Olimpic) (winner of five Italian championships), [Unione Rugby Capitolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unione_Rugby_Capitolina) and [S.S. Lazio Rugby 1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.S._Lazio_Rugby_1927) (rugby union branch of the multisport club S.S. Lazio).

Every May, Rome hosts the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Open_\(tennis\)), an [ATP Masters 1000](30872553.md) tennis tournament, on the clay courts of the [Foro Italico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Italico). Cycling was popular in the post-World War II period, although its popularity has faded. Rome has hosted the final portion of the [Giro d'Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giro_d'Italia) three times, in 1911, 1950, and 2009. Other local sports teams include volleyball ([M. Roma Volley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Roma_Volley)), [handball](13730.md) or [waterpolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo).

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Rome)



Panoramic view of the [EUR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUR,_Rome) [business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_district)



[Palazzo Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Eni), the headquarters of [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni), one of the world's oil and gas "[supermajors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajors)".

[^cite_note-183]



[Enel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enel)'s headquarters in Rome, the second largest [power company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_company) in the world by revenue after the [State Grid Corporation of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Grid_Corporation_of_China).

[^cite_note-184]



[Orizzonte Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orizzonte_Europa), [BNL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banca_Nazionale_del_Lavoro) headquarters in Rome

As the capital of Italy, Rome hosts the country's principal political institutions, including the [Presidency of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy), the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Italy) and its ministries, the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Italy), and the country's highest judicial institutions. The city is also home to the diplomatic missions accredited to Italy and to the [Holy See](13393.md). Rome hosts numerous international institutions and cultural and scientific organisations, including the [American Academy in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_in_Rome), the [British School at Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_School_at_Rome), the [French Academy at Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_at_Rome), the [German Archaeological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Archaeological_Institute), and several Scandinavian research institutes. There are also several specialised agencies and organisations of the [United Nations](31769.md) based in Rome, notably the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) (FAO), the [International Fund for Agricultural Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fund_for_Agricultural_Development) (IFAD), and the [World Food Programme](33582.md) (WFP). Rome is also home to the [NATO Defense College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Defense_College) and the [International Centre for the Study of the Preservation and Restoration of Cultural Property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_the_Study_of_the_Preservation_and_Restoration_of_Cultural_Property) (ICCROM).

According to the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city#GaWC_study) (GaWC), Rome was classified as a "Beta+" world city in 2024.[^cite_note-GaWC2024-185] In the 2025 *Kearney Global Cities Index*, Rome ranked 27th worldwide, rising four places from 31st in 2024 and ranking ahead of [Milan](36511.md), which placed 30th.[^cite_note-Kearney2025-186]

Rome's economy is predominantly service-based, with major sectors including public administration, tourism, commerce, finance and insurance, information technology, telecommunications, aerospace and defence, research, construction, media, and the creative industries. The city is also an important centre for energy, transport, professional services, and higher education. Rome hosts the headquarters of several of Italy's largest companies, including [Enel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enel), [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni), [Leonardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.), [Poste Italiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poste_Italiane), and [Ferrovie dello Stato Italiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_dello_Stato_Italiane).

[Leonardo da Vinci–Fiumicino Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Fiumicino_Airport), the principal airport serving Rome, is the busiest airport in Italy. In 2025, it handled approximately 50.9 million arriving and departing passengers, exceeding the 50-million-passenger threshold for the first time; including direct transit passengers, total traffic amounted to approximately 51.3 million.[^cite_note-ENAC2025-187] It is also one of Europe's major international aviation hubs. And for the eighth time since 2018, and for the fifth consecutive year, [ACI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Council_International) has ranked the airport as the best airport in Europe.[^cite_note-188]

Rome is an important centre for higher education, research, broadcasting, and film production. The city is home to [Cinecittà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinecitt%C3%A0), one of the principal centres of the Italian film industry, which has operated since its establishment in the 1930s.

The city's principal concentrations of modern office, commercial, and business activity include [EUR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUR,_Rome), Torrino, [Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magliana), Parco de' Medici, and the Tiburtina Valley, particularly along the [Via Tiburtina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Tiburtina). Other important concentrations of economic and professional activity are found in the city centre and in areas such as [Ostiense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostiense) and [Flaminio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaminio_\(Rome\)).

### Tourism

Main articles: [Tourism in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Rome) and [List of tourist attractions in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tourist_attractions_in_Rome)



[Fontana della Barcaccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontana_della_Barcaccia) in [Piazza di Spagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_di_Spagna), the [Spanish Steps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Steps) and [Trinità dei Monti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinit%C3%A0_dei_Monti)



[Piazza Navona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Navona)



As of 2022, the [Vatican Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Museums) are the [second most visited art museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_visited_art_museums) in the world.

[^cite_note-189]

Rome today is one of the most important tourist destinations of the world, due to the incalculable immensity of its archaeological and artistic treasures, as well as for the charm of its unique traditions, the beauty of its panoramic views, and the majesty of its magnificent "villas" (parks). Among the most significant resources are the many museums – [Capitoline Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitoline_Museums), [the Vatican Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Museums) and the [Galleria Borghese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Borghese) and others dedicated to modern and contemporary art – [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_\(watercourse\)), [fountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain), churches, [palaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace), historical buildings, the [monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument) and ruins of the [Roman Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum), and the [Catacombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs). Rome is the third most visited city in the EU, after London and Paris, and receives an average of 7–10 million tourists a year, which sometimes doubles on holy years. The [Colosseum](49603.md) (4 million tourists) and the Vatican Museums (4.2 million tourists) are the 39th and 37th (respectively) most visited places in the world, according to a recent study.[^cite_note-itvnews.tv-190]

Rome is a major archaeological hub, and one of the world's main centres of [archaeological research](18951655.md). There are numerous cultural and research institutes located in the city, such as the [American Academy in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_in_Rome),[^cite_note-191] and The Swedish Institute at Rome.[^cite_note-192] Rome contains numerous [ancient sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ancient_monuments_in_Rome), including the [Forum Romanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum), [Trajan's Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan's_Market), [Trajan's Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan's_Forum),[^cite_note-193] the [Colosseum](49603.md), and the [Pantheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome), to name but a few. The Colosseum, arguably one of Rome's most iconic archaeological sites, is regarded as a [wonder of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonders_of_the_World).[^cite_note-brewers-194][^cite_note-miller-195]

Rome contains a vast collection of art, sculpture, [fountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain), [mosaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic), [frescos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresco), and paintings, from all different periods. Rome first became a major artistic centre during ancient Rome, with forms of important [Roman art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_art) such as [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_ancient_Rome), painting, sculpture and mosaic work. [Metal-work](266443.md), [coin die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_die) and gem engraving, [ivory carvings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_carving), figurine glass, [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_pottery), and book illustrations are considered to be 'minor' forms of Roman artwork.[^cite_note-196] Rome later became a major centre of [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) art, since the popes spent vast sums of money for the constructions of grandiose [basilicas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica), [palaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace), [piazzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza) and public buildings in general. Rome became one of Europe's major centres of Renaissance artwork along with [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), and able to compare to other major cities and cultural centres, such as Paris and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice). Some of the greatest artists of this time found work in Rome: [Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo), [Raffaello Sanzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaello_Sanzio), [Donato Bramante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donato_Bramante), [Sandro Botticelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Botticelli), [Ghirlandaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghirlandaio), [Piero della Francesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_della_Francesca), [Pietro Perugino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Perugino), [Cosimo Rosselli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosimo_Rosselli), [Gentile da Fabriano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentile_da_Fabriano), [Beato Angelico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beato_Angelico), [Leon Battista Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Battista_Alberti), [Giovanni Battista Penni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Penni?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Giulio Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Romano), [Rosso Fiorentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosso_Fiorentino), [Parmigianino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmigianino), [Bernardo Rossellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Rossellino), [Benozzo Gozzoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benozzo_Gozzoli), [Pisanello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisanello) and others. The city was affected greatly by the [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Baroque), and Rome became the home of numerous artists and architects, such as [Bernini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Lorenzo_Bernini), [Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio), [Carracci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annibale_Carracci), [Borromini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Borromini), [Stefano Maderno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Maderno), [Francesco Mochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Mochi), [Domenichino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenichino), [Pietro Paolo Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Paolo_Rubens?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Guido Reni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Reni), [Diego Velázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez), [Artemisia Gentileschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemisia_Gentileschi), [Orazio Gentileschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orazio_Gentileschi), [Andrea Pozzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Pozzo), [Baciccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baciccia) and [Cortona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_da_Cortona).[^cite_note-197] In the late 18th century and early 19th century, the city was one of the centres of the [Grand Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour),[^cite_note-198] when wealthy, young English and other European aristocrats visited the city to learn about [ancient Roman culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_ancient_Rome), art, philosophy, and architecture. Rome hosted a great number of neoclassical and rococo artists, such as [Pannini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Paolo_Pannini) and [Bernardo Bellotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Bellotto). Today, the city is a major artistic centre, with numerous art institutes[^cite_note-199] and museums.

Rome has a growing stock of contemporary and modern art and architecture. The National Gallery of Modern Art has works by Balla, Morandi, Pirandello, Carrà, De Chirico, De Pisis, Guttuso, Fontana, Burri, Mastroianni, Turcato, Kandisky, and Cézanne on permanent exhibition. 2010 saw the opening of Rome's newest arts foundation, a contemporary art and architecture gallery designed by acclaimed Iraqi architect Zaha Hadid. Known as [MAXXI – National Museum of the 21st Century Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAXXI_%E2%80%93_National_Museum_of_the_21st_Century_Arts) it restores a dilapidated area with striking modern architecture. Maxxi[^cite_note-200] features a campus dedicated to culture, experimental research laboratories, international exchange and study and research. It is one of Rome's most ambitious modern architecture projects alongside [Renzo Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Piano)'s Auditorium Parco della Musica[^cite_note-201] and [Massimiliano Fuksas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimiliano_Fuksas)' Rome Convention Center, Centro Congressi Italia EUR, in the EUR district, due to open in 2016.[^cite_note-202] The convention centre features a huge translucent container inside which is suspended a steel and teflon structure resembling a cloud and which contains meeting rooms and an auditorium with two piazzas open to the neighbourhood on either side.

## Education



[Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome). It was founded in 1303 and is as such one of the world's oldest universities,

[^cite_note-203]

&#x20;and with 122,000 students, it is the [largest university in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_universities_by_enrollment).

[^cite_note-204]

Rome is a nationwide and major international centre for higher education, containing numerous academies, colleges and universities. It boasts a large variety of academies and colleges, and has always been a major worldwide intellectual and educational centre, especially during [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) and the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), along with Florence.[^cite_note-205] According to the City Brands Index, Rome is considered the world's second most historically, educationally and culturally interesting and beautiful city.[^cite_note-206]

Rome has many universities and colleges. Its first university, [La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) (founded in 1303), is one of the largest in the world, with more than 140,000 students attending; in 2005 it ranked as Europe's 33rd best university[^cite_note-207] and in 2013 the Sapienza University of Rome ranked as the 62nd in the world and the top in Italy in its *World University Rankings*,[^cite_note-208] and has been ranked among Europe's 50 and the world's 150 best colleges.[^cite_note-209] In order to decrease the overcrowding of La Sapienza, two new public universities were founded during the last decades: [Tor Vergata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_Tor_Vergata) in 1982, and [Roma Tre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Tre_University) in 1992. Rome hosts also the [LUISS School of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUISS_School_of_Government),[^cite_note-210] Italy's most important graduate university in the areas of international affairs and European studies as well as [LUISS Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUISS_Business_School), Italy's most important business school. Rome [ISIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_superiore_per_le_industrie_artistiche_\(ISIA\)) was founded in 1973 by [Giulio Carlo Argan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Carlo_Argan) and is Italy's oldest institution in the field of [industrial design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_design).



[Biblioteca Casanatense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Casanatense)

Rome contains many [pontifical universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_university) and other institutes, including the [British School at Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_School_at_Rome), the [French Academy in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_in_Rome), the [Pontifical Gregorian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Gregorian_University) (the oldest [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) university in the world, founded in 1551), [Istituto Europeo di Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Europeo_di_Design), the [Scuola Lorenzo de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_de'_Medici_School), the Link [Campus of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Campus), and the [Università Campus Bio-Medico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A0_Campus_Bio-Medico). Rome is also the location of two American Universities; [The American University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_University_of_Rome)[^cite_note-211] and [John Cabot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cabot_University) as well as [St. John's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_University_\(Italy\)) branch campus, [John Felice Rome Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Felice_Rome_Center), a campus of [Loyola University Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_University_Chicago) and Temple University Rome, a campus of [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University).[^cite_note-212] The [Roman Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Colleges) are several [seminaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) for students from foreign countries studying for the [priesthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_priesthood) at the Pontifical Universities.[^cite_note-NAC-213] Examples include the [Venerable English College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venerable_English_College), the [Pontifical North American College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_North_American_College), the [Scots College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_College_\(Rome\)), and the [Pontifical Croatian College of St. Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Croatian_College_of_St._Jerome). Rome's major libraries include: the [Biblioteca Angelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Angelica), opened in 1604, making it Italy's first public library; the [Biblioteca Vallicelliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Vallicelliana), established in 1565; the [Biblioteca Casanatense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Casanatense), opened in 1701; the [National Central Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Central_Library_\(Rome\)), one of the two national libraries in Italy, which contains 4,126,002 volumes; The Biblioteca del Ministero degli Affari Esteri, specialised in diplomacy, foreign affairs and modern history; the Biblioteca dell'Istituto dell'Enciclopedia Italiana; the Biblioteca Don Bosco, one of the largest and most modern of all Salesian libraries; the Biblioteca e Museo teatrale del Burcardo, a museum-library specialised in history of drama and theatre; the Biblioteca della [Società Geografica Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societ%C3%A0_Geografica_Italiana), which is based in the [Villa Celimontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mattei) and is the most important geographical library in Italy, and one of Europe's most important;[^cite_note-214] and the [Vatican Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Library), one of the oldest and most important libraries in the world, which was formally established in 1475, though in fact much older and has 75,000 [codices](5691.md), as well as 1.1 million printed books, which include some 8,500 [incunabula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incunabulum). There are also many specialist libraries attached to various foreign cultural institutes in Rome, among them that of the [American Academy in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_in_Rome), the French Academy in Rome and the [Bibliotheca Hertziana – Max Planck Institute of Art History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliotheca_Hertziana_%E2%80%93_Max_Planck_Institute_of_Art_History), a German library, often noted for excellence in the arts and sciences.[^cite_note-215]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Rome)



[Leonardo da Vinci–Fiumicino Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci%E2%80%93Fiumicino_Airport).



[Port of Civitavecchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Civitavecchia)

Rome is at the centre of the radial network of roads that roughly follow the lines of the ancient Roman roads which began at the [Capitoline Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitoline_Hill) and connected Rome with its empire. Today central Rome is circled, at a distance of about 10 km (6 mi) from the Capitol, by the ring-road (the [Grande Raccordo Anulare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Raccordo_Anulare) or GRA).

Due to its location in the centre of the Italian peninsula, Rome is the principal [railway node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_node) for central Italy. Rome's main railway station, [Termini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Termini_railway_station), is one of the largest railway stations in Europe and the most heavily used in Italy, with around 400 thousand travellers passing through every day. The second-largest station in the city, [Roma Tiburtina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Tiburtina_railway_station), has been redeveloped as a [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Italy) terminus.[^cite_note-216] As well as frequent high-speed day trains to all major Italian cities, Rome is linked nightly by 'boat train' sleeper services to Sicily, and internationally by overnight sleeper services to Munich and Vienna.

Rome is served by three airports. The intercontinental [Leonardo da Vinci International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci%E2%80%93Fiumicino_Airport), Italy's chief airport is located in the nearby [Fiumicino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiumicino), south-west of Rome. The older [Rome Ciampino Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciampino%E2%80%93G._B._Pastine_International_Airport) is a joint civilian and military airport. It is commonly referred to as "Ciampino Airport", as it is located beside [Ciampino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciampino), south-east of Rome. A third airport, the [Rome Urbe Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Urbe_Airport), is a small, low-traffic airport located about 6 km (4 mi) north of the city centre, which handles most helicopter and private flights. The main city airport of [Fiumicino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiumicino), with over 49 million passengers transported in 2024, is the largest and busiest airport in Italy.

Although the city has its own quarter on the Mediterranean Sea ([Lido di Ostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostia_\(Rome\))), this has only a marina and a small channel-harbour for fishing boats. The main harbour which serves Rome is [Port of Civitavecchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Civitavecchia), located about 62 km (39 mi) northwest of the city.[^cite_note-217]

The city suffers from traffic problems largely due to this radial street pattern, making it difficult for Romans to move easily from the vicinity of one of the radial roads to another without going into the historic centre or using the ring-road. These problems are not helped by the limited size of Rome's metro system when compared to other cities of similar size. Rome has only 21 taxis for every 10,000 inhabitants, far below other major European cities.[^cite_note-218] Chronic congestion caused by cars during the 1970s and 1980s led to restrictions being placed on vehicle access to the inner city-centre during daylight hours. Areas, where these restrictions apply, are known as Limited Traffic Zones (*Zona a Traffico Limitato* (ZTL)). More recently, heavy night-time traffic in [Trastevere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trastevere), [Testaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testaccio) and [San Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Lorenzo_\(Rome\)) has led to the creation of night-time ZTLs in those districts.



Rome Metro map updated to 2026



[Conca d'Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conca_d'Oro_\(Rome_Metro\)) metro station

A three-line metro system called the *[Metropolitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Metro)* operates in Rome. Construction on the first branch started in the 1930s.[^cite_note-219] The line had been planned to quickly connect the [main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Termini_railway_station) with the newly planned E42 area in the southern suburbs, where the 1942 [World Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_world_expositions) was supposed to be held. The event never took place because of war, but the area was later partly redesigned and renamed [Esposizione Universale Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUR,_Rome) in the 1950s to serve as a modern business district. The line was finally opened in 1955, and it is now the south part of the B Line.

The A line opened in 1980 from Ottaviano to Anagnina stations, later extended in stages (1999–2000) to Battistini. In the 1990s, an extension of the B line was opened from Termini to Rebibbia. The A and B lines intersect at Roma Termini station. A new branch of the B line (B1) opened on 13 June 2012 after an estimated building cost of €500 million. B1 connects to line B at Piazza Bologna and has four stations over a distance of 3.9 km (2 mi).

A third line, the C line, is under construction with an estimated cost of €3 billion and will have 30 stations over a distance of 25.5 km (16 mi). It will partly replace the existing [Termini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Termini_railway_station)-Pantano rail line. It will feature full automated, driverless trains.[^cite_note-220] The first section with 15 stations connecting Pantano with the quarter of Centocelle in the eastern part of the city, opened on 9 November 2014.[^cite_note-221] The end of the work was scheduled in 2015, but archaeological findings often delay underground construction work.

A fourth line, D line, is also planned. It will have 22 stations over a distance of 20 km (12 mi). The first section was projected to open in 2015 and the final sections before 2035, but due to the city's financial crisis, the project has been put on hold.

Above-ground public transport in Rome is made up of a bus, tram and urban train network (FR lines). The bus network has in excess of 350 bus lines and over eight thousand bus stops, whereas the more-limited tram system has 39 km (24 mi) of track and 192 stops.[^cite_note-222] There are also [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Rome).[^cite_note-juts2009-223]
