**Dausa district** is a district in the [Indian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_state) of [Rajasthan](26291.md). It is part of the [Jaipur division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur_division)–one of the ten [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Rajasthan#Divisions) of Rajasthan. The city of [Dausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dausa) is the district headquarters. Other important cities are [Bandikui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandikui), [Lalsot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalsot), and [Mahuwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahwa,_Rajasthan). It is surrounded by [Alwar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alwar_district) in the north, [Bharatpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatpur_district) in the east, [Gangapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangapur_district) in the south-east, [Sawai Madhopur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawai_Madhopur_district) in the south, and [Jaipur Rural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur_Rural_district) in the west. It has an area of 3,432 km<sup>2</sup> (1,325 sq mi) and a population of 1,634,409 (2011 census).

The district is named after the city of Dausa, derived from a [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) word *Dhau-Sa* which means "Beautiful like Heaven".[^cite_note-4]

## History

Dausa is situated in a region known as [Dhundhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhundhar). It was ruled by the [Chauhans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauhan_Dynasty) and [Badgurjars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargujar) during the 10th century CE. It became the first capital of Dhundhar. In the 11th century CE, [Dulha Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulha_Rai) won it from the Badgurjars and continued to rule until 1036 CE.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-history-6] Later, Dulha Rai changed his capital from Dausa to [Khoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoh).[^cite_note-Hooja-7]

When [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) went to [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer) as a pilgrim to [Khwaja Moinuddin Chisti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwaja_Moinuddin_Chisti) in 1562 CE, he stayed in Dausa and met with Rupsi Bairagi, the [Hakim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakim_\(title\)) of Dausa at the time and brother of the then ruler of [Amber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Amber), Raja [Bharmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharmal).[^cite_note-8]

Dausa district was constituted on 10 April 1991 by separating four [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil), namely Dausa, Baswa, Sikrai, & [Lalsot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalsot) from [Jaipur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur_district). Mahwa Tehsil of [Sawai Madhopur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawai_Madhopur_district) was included in this district on 15 August 1992.[^cite_note-history-6]

## Geography

The district is situated between 22°33' and 27°33' north latitudes and 76°50' and 76°90' east longitudes. Dausa district is located in the eastern part of Rajasthan within [Jaipur division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur_division). The area of the district is 3,432 km<sup>2</sup> (1,325 sq mi) and ranks at 32nd among [districts of Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Rajasthan).[^cite_note-Geo1-9] It is bordered by the district of [Alwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alwar_district) to the north, [Bharatpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatpur_district) to the east, [Gangapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangapur_district) to the south-east, [Sawai Madhopur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawai_Madhopur_district) to the south, and [Jaipur Rural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur_Rural_district) to the west.[^cite_note-10]

The [Banganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banganga_River_\(Rajasthan\)) and [Morel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morel_River) rivers run through the district. However, there are no perennial rivers in the district. The district falls within the three corresponding river basins namely "[Banganga River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banganga_River_\(Rajasthan\))" in northern part, "[Banas River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banas_River)" in southern part, and "[Gambhir River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambhir_River_\(Rajasthan\))" in lower eastern part.[^cite_note-Geo1-9][^cite_note-Geo2-11]

The general [topographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) [elevation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevation) in the district is between 250 m to 300 m above sea level. Elevation ranges from a minimum of 203.2 m above sea level in [Mahwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahwa,_Rajasthan) in the northeastern part of the district and maximum of 596.3 m above sea level in [Bandikui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandikui) in northern part of the district.[^cite_note-Geo2-11]

### Climate

The climate of the district mostly remains dry except in the rainy season. The annual normal rainfall of the district is 561 mm.[^cite_note-Geo1-9]

According to the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India) Dausa district has a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 1,634,409, with a population density of 476 persons per km²,[^cite_note-districtcensus-18] roughly equal to the nation of [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md)[^cite_note-cia-19] or the US state of [Idaho](14607.md).[^cite_note-20] This gives it a ranking of 305th in India (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-districtcensus-18] The district has a population density of 476 inhabitants per square kilometre (1,230/sq mi) .[^cite_note-districtcensus-18] Its [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) over the decade 2001-2011 was 24.31%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-18] Dausa has a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 905 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) for every 1000 males,[^cite_note-districtcensus-18] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 68.16%, with male literacy at 82.98% and female literacy at 51.93%. 12.35% of the population lives in urban areas. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes make up 21.68% and 26.51% of the population respectively.[^cite_note-districtcensus-18]

2. [Dhundari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhundari_language) (6.64%)

3. [Rajasthani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthani_languages) (6.40%)

At the time of the [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), 86.77% of the population in the district spoke [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), 6.64% [Dhundari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhundari_language) and 6.40% [Rajasthani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthani_languages) as their first language.[^cite_note-language-22] The dialect of the region is Dhundari.
