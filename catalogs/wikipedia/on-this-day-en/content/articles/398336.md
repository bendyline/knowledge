This is a **list of international sports federations**, each of which serves as a [non-governmental](46539.md) [governing body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_governing_body) for a given [sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport) and administers its sport at a world level, most often crafting rules, promoting the sport to prospective [spectators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectator_sport) and [fans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_\(person\)), developing prospective players, and organizing world or [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent) championships. Some international sports federations, such as [World Aquatics](317029.md) and the [International Skating Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Skating_Union), may oversee multiple activities referred to in common parlance as separate sports: World Aquatics, for example, governs swimming, diving, synchronised swimming, and water polo as separate "disciplines" within the single "sport" of Aquatics.

International sports federations form an integral part of the Olympic and Paralympic movements. Each [Olympic sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_sport) is represented by its respective international sports federation, which in turn helps administer the events in its respective sport during the Games. For a sport to become an Olympic sport, its international sports federation must be recognized by the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md).[^cite_note-1]

Likewise, an international sports federation must be recognized by the [International Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Paralympic_Committee) for its sport to become a Paralympic sport, though in the latter case, several [Paralympic Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic_sports) are governed by a dedicated committee of the International Paralympic Committee itself, under the *World Para* branding; for example, track and field athletics for disabled athletes is governed by the IPC itself, under the name "World Para Athletics". Other Paralympic sports are governed within the structure of the able-bodied equivalent; for example, the UCI governs both able-bodied and Paralympic cycling.

## Federations recognized by the International Olympic Committee (IOC)

### Association of Summer Olympic International Federations (ASOIF)

This section is an excerpt from [Association of Summer Olympic International Federations § Members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Summer_Olympic_International_Federations#Members).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Association_of_Summer_Olympic_International_Federations\&action=edit)]

International federations may apply to become a full member if they govern a core Summer Olympic sport or govern a sport which featured in the three most recent Summer Olympics. They may apply to become an associate member if they govern a sport included on the programme for future editions of the Summer Olympic Games or govern a sport which has featured in any five editions of the Summer Olympic Games since 1988.[^cite_note-2]

As of June 2025, the Association of Summer Olympic International Federations (ASOIF) has 30 full member and six associate member international federations.[^cite_note-Association_of_Summer_Olympic_International_Federations_member-3][^cite_note-4]

## Federations recognized by SportAccord, GAISF, or AIMS

Federations whose sports are either included in the [Olympic Games](22576.md) ([ASOIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Summer_Olympic_International_Federations), [WOF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Olympic_Federations)) or recognized by the [IOC](15147.md) ([ARISF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_IOC_Recognised_International_Sports_Federations)) are also stakeholders of [SportAccord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SportAccord),[^cite_note-26] after the dissolution of the [Global Association of International Sports Federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Association_of_International_Sports_Federations) (GAISF) in 2023. Other stakeholders of [SportAccord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SportAccord) (non-IOC recognized) compose the Alliance of Independent Recognized Members of Sport (AIMS).[^cite_note-27]

## Federations recognized by the International Paralympic Committee (IPC)

There are 18 international federations recognized by the [IPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Paralympic_Committee), while the IPC itself serves as the international federation for five sports. IPC recognises also 3 International Organisations of Sports for the Disabled. And there are another 15 federations which are recognised by the IPC but are not eligible to be IPC members.[^cite_note-\:2-33]

### Sports directly governed by IPC

On 30 November 2016, the IPC adopted the "World Para" brand for all 10 of the sports that it directly governed at that time.[^cite_note-World_Para-34] At the 2021 IPC General Assembly, IPC members provided a strong mandate for the IPC to cease acting as the international federation for 10 sports by the end of 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-35] [Para-alpine skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para-alpine_skiing), [para cross country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para_cross_country_skiing) and [para snowboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para_snowboard) were transferred to [FIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Ski_Federation),[^cite_note-\:0-35] and [Para biathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para_biathlon) was transferred to [IBU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biathlon_Union) to be overseen by a joint steering committee involving IBU and FIS.[^cite_note-\:1-36] Para dance sport was transferred to [World Abilitysport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Abilitysport) as of 1 January 2024.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Currently IPC acts as the international federation for five sports:[^cite_note-39]

| --- | --- |
