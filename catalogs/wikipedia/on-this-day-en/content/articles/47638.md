**Kerch**,[^cite_note-1] also known as **Kerich**,[^cite_note-2] is a city on the [Kerch Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Peninsula) in the east of [Crimea](163045.md). It has a population of 147,033 ([2014 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Federal_District_Census_\(2014\))).[^cite_note-2014Census-3]

Founded 2,600 years ago as the [ancient Greek colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonies_in_antiquity#Greek_colonies) [Pantikapaion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantikapaion), Kerch is one of the most ancient cities in Crimea. The city experienced rapid growth starting in the 1920s and was the site of [a major battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Kerch_Peninsula) during World War II.

Today, it is one of the largest cities in Crimea and is among the area's most important industrial, transport and tourist centres. As with the rest of Crimea, it has been occupied by Russian forces since the [Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) in 2014.

## History



Ruins of [Panticapaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panticapaeum) from the 6th century BC

### Ancient times

Archeological digs at Mayak village near the city ascertained that the area had already been inhabited in the 17th–15th centuries BC. While many finds from Kerch can be found in the [Hermitage Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Museum) in St Petersburg and the local museum, a large number of antique sculptures, reliefs, bronze and glassware, ceramics and jewellery were excavated in 1855–1856 during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) by Duncan MacPherson, a surgeon from the British Army, and later donated to the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum) in London.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

Kerch as a city starts its history in the 7th century BC, when [Greek colonists](42056.md) from [Miletus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miletus) founded a city-state named [**Panticapaeum**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantikapaion)[^cite_note-7] on [Mount Mithridat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mithridat) near the mouth of the Melek-Chesme river. Panticapaeum subdued nearby cities and by 480 BC became the capital of the [Kingdom of Bosporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bosporus). Later, during the rule of [Mithradates VI Eupator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithradates_VI_Eupator), Panticapaeum for a short period of time became the capital of the much more powerful and extensive [Kingdom of Pontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pontus). The city was located at the intersection of trade routes between the [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) and [Europe](9239.md). This caused it to grow rapidly. The city's main exports were grain and salted fish; wine-making was also common. Panticapaeum minted its own coins. According to extant documents the Melek-Chesme river (small and shallow nowadays) was navigable in Bosporan times, and sea [galleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley) were able to enter the river. Much of the city's population is thought to have been ethnically [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian), later [Sarmatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatian), as implied by the large [Royal Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Kurgan) at [Kul-Oba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kul-Oba).

In the 1st century AD, Panticapaeum and the Kingdom of Bosphorus suffered from [Ostrogoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoth) raids; then the city was devastated by the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns) in AD 375.

The settlement of [Myrmekion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrmekion) was founded by [Ionians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionians) in the eastern part of what is now Kerch, some four kilometers north-east of ancient [Panticapaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panticapaeum), in the first half of the 6th century BC.[^cite_note-8]

### Middle Ages



Yenikale fortress

From the 6th century the city was under the control of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). By order of Emperor [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I), a citadel named Bospor was built there. Bospor was the centre of a [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric), the [diocese of Bosporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Bosporus) and developed under the influence of Greek [Christianity](5211.md). In 576, it withstood a siege by the [Göktürks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6kt%C3%BCrks) under Bokhan, aided by Anagai, the last [khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(title\)) (ruler) of the [Uturgurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uturgurs) (tribe of [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns)).



Church of St John the Baptist, built in the 8th century

In the 7th century, the [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) [Khazars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars) took control of Bospor, and the city was named **Karcha** from Turkic "karşı" meaning 'opposite, facing.' The main local government official during Khazar times was the [tudun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudun). During Khazar rule, Kerch was used as a major port for the [Khazar slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazar_slave_trade), through which slaves were exported to the [Black Sea slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_slave_trade).[^cite_note-9]

Christianity was a major religion in Kerch during the period of Khazar rule. Kerch's Church of [St. John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist) was founded in 717; thus, it is the oldest church in Ukraine.[^cite_note-10] The Church of the Apostles existed during the late 8th and early 9th centuries, according to the *Life of the Apostle Andrew* by [Epiphanius of Salamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis).

Following the fall of Khazaria to [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') in the late 10th century, Kerch became the centre of a Khazar [successor-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor-state). Its ruler, [Georgius Tzul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgius_Tzul), was deposed by a [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire)-[Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') expedition in 1016.

From the 10th century, the city was a [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples) settlement named Korchev, which belonged to the [Tmutarakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tmutarakan) principality. Kerch was a center of trade between Russia', [Crimea](163045.md), [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) and the [Orient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient).

In the 13th century, Crimea including Korchev was invaded by [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Rus). After the Mongols, the city became the [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) colony of Cerco (Cherkio) in 1318 and served as a sea harbour, where townspeople worked at salt works and fishery.

In 1475, the city was passed to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). During the Turkish rule Kerch fell into decay and served as a [slave-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery).[^cite_note-11] It repeatedly suffered from raids of [Zaporizhian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhian_Sich).

### 18th–20th centuries



*Kerch* by [Ivan Aivazovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Aivazovsky), 1839

In response to the strengthening of [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) military forces in [Azov](78019.md) area, the Turks built a fortress, named [Yenikale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenikale), near Kerch on the shore of [Kerch Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait). The fortress was completed by 1706. In 1771 the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) invaded [Crimea](163045.md) and approached Yenikale. The Turks decided to abandon the fortress, though reinforcements from the Ottoman Empire had arrived a few days earlier. By the Peace [Treaty of Kuchuk-Kainarji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kuchuk-Kainarji) in 1774, Kerch and Yenikale were ceded to Russia. As a result, the Turkish heritage has been almost completely wiped out.

In 1790 Russian naval forces under the command of admiral [Fyodor Ushakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Fyodorovich_Ushakov) defeated the Turkish fleet at the [Battle of Kerch Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kerch_Strait_\(1790\)).[^cite_note-12]

Because of its location, from 1821 Kerch developed into an important trade and fishing port. The state museum of ancient times and a number of educational institutions were opened in the city. The ironwork factory was built in 1846 based on a huge [iron ore](145352.md) deposit found on Kerch Peninsula.

During the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) the city was devastated by [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) forces in 1855.

In the late 19th century, mechanical and [cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement) factories were built, and tinned food and [tobacco](30942.md) factories were established. By 1900, Kerch was connected to a railroad system, and the fairway of Kerch Strait was deepened and widened. At this time, the population had reached 33,000.

After suffering a decline during the [First World War](4764461.md) and the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), the city resumed its growth in the late 1920s, with the expansion of various industries, iron ore, and metallurgy in particular, and by 1939 its population had reached 104,500.

### Kerch in World War II



[Monument of Glory in Kerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_of_Glory_in_Kerch?action=edit\&redlink=1)

On the [Eastern Front of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)) from 1941 to 1945, Kerch was the site of heavy fighting between [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) and [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) forces. After fierce fighting, the city was taken by the [Germans](11867.md) in November 1941. On 31 December 1941, the [302nd Mountain Rifle Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/302nd_Rifle_Division_\(Soviet_Union\)) recaptured the city following a naval landing operation at Kamysh Burun, to the south of the city, five days earlier.[^cite_note-13] In 1942 the Germans occupied the city again. The Red Army lost over 160,000 men, either killed or taken [POW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW) at the [Battle of the Kerch Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Kerch_Peninsula_\(1942\)). On 31 October 1943 another Soviet naval landing operation was launched. Kerch returned to Soviet control on 11 April 1944.

The German invaders killed about 15,000 citizens and deported another 14,000 during their occupation. Evidence of German atrocities in Kerch was presented in the [Nuremberg trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_trials). After the war, the city was awarded the title [Hero City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_\(Soviet_Union\)).

The [Adzhimushkay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_of_the_Adzhimushkay_quarry) [catacombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs) in the city's suburbs were the site of [guerrilla warfare](12720.md) against the occupation. Thousands of soldiers and refugees found shelter inside and were involved in counterattacks. Many of them died underground, including those who died of numerous alleged poison gas attacks. Later, a memorial was established on the site.

## Administration

The city municipality stretches over a substantial area and includes several separate neighborhoods that are part of the Kerch city: Eltigen (Heroyevskoe), Kamysh-Burun (Arshyntsevo), [Port Krym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Krym), Adzhimushkai, and [Tuzla Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuzla_Island).

## Economy

Today Kerch is home to many [metallurgists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgist), shipbuilders, and fishermen. It also has a significant tourism sector.

### Industry

The largest enterprises in the city are:

- Kerch Metallurgical Works Factory launched in 1900

- [Zaliv Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaliv_Shipbuilding_Yard) that produces and repairs tankers and cargo ships.

Construction-materials, food processing, and light industries play a significant role in the city's economy. Kerch is also a fishing fleet base and an important processing centre for numerous fish products.

### Tourism



Mithridates Staircase (19th century)



Lenina Street in Kerch

Because of its location on the shores of the [Azov](78019.md) and [Black](3386.md) seas, Kerch became a popular summer resort among people of the former [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). Also, several mud-cure sources are located near the city. Despite the seaside location, the tourist appeal of Kerch today is limited because of the industrial character of the city and associated pollution. Despite the lack of beaches in the town's area, there are a lot of them at a distance of 20 minutes' travel by bus, train or taxi.

Kerch has a number of impressive architectural and historical monuments. Ancient historical heritage of the city makes it attractive for scientific tourism. The most notable of Kerch's sights are:

- Site of ancient settlement [Pantikapaion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantikapaion) (5th century BC–3rd century AD).

- [Royal Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Kurgan) (4th century BC) – burial mound for one of the Bosporian kings

- [Church of St. John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_John_the_Baptist,_Kerch) (AD 717)

- [Fortress of Yenikale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenikale) (18th century)

- The Great Mithridates Staircase leading on top of the [Mount Mithridat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mithridat), contains 428 footsteps, built in 1833–1840 under the guidance of [Italian](14532.md) architect [Alexander Digbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Digbi?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- Obelisk of Glory on the [Mount Mithridat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mithridat), built after World War II

- [Lapidarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapidarium,_Kerch)

- Memorial of heroic [guerilla warfare in Adzhimushkay mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_of_the_Adzhimushkay_quarry)

- [Kerch Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Fortress); restricted area in Soviet times but free to enter in present days. The fortress was built by the Russian military architect [Totleben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Totleben) in the middle of 19th century.

- Sites of ancient settlements [Myrmekion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrm%C4%93kion), [Tyritake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyritak%C4%93) and [Nymphaion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphaion_\(Crimea\)). There are also some settlements which have gone underwater due to earthquakes.

- [Demeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demeter)'s Crypt, a crypt with numerous frescos dated 1st century BC.

### Transport



Private houses are common in Kerch.

Kerch has a harbour on the Kerch Strait, which makes it a key to the [Sea of Azov](78019.md). It has several railroad terminals and a small airport. The [Kerch Strait ferry line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait_ferry_line) across the [Kerch Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait) was established in 1953, connecting Crimea and the [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md) ([Port Krym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Krym) – [Port Kavkaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Kavkaz) line); (as of November 2009) there are also plans for a Kerch-[Poti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poti) ferry route.[^cite_note-23] Tavrida Highway work in progress along Kerch railway (two rails) highway (four lanes) bridge to connect Rostov Krasnodar with peninsula.

There are several ports in Kerch, including Kerch Maritime Trading Port, Kerch Maritime Fishing Port, [Port Krym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Krym) (ferry crossing), Kamysh-Burun Port.

The railroad terminals include: Kerch, Kerch I, Kerch Factory, Arshyntsevo, and Krym.

A bus network connects Kerch to other cities in Crimea and [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md).

#### Crimean Bridge

Main article: [Crimean Bridge](22563317.md)

On 25 April 2010, Ukrainian President [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) and Russian President [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) signed an agreement to build a bridge across the Kerch Strait. In January 2015, with Russia now in control, the contract for the construction of the bridge was awarded to [Arkady Rotenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkady_Rotenberg)'s [S.G.M. Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroygazmontazh). In May 2015 construction commenced, and the road section of the bridge was opened to traffic in May 2018. The rail section was opened in 2019, with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) taking the first train across on 23 December.[^cite_note-24] It was [heavily damaged by explosions](71948884.md) on 8 October 2022, and in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Crimean_Bridge_explosion) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Crimean_Bridge_explosion).

### Archaeology



Excavations in Pantikapaeum

Archaeological digs in Kerch were launched under Russian auspices in the middle of the 19th century. Since then the site of ancient [Panticapaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panticapaeum) city on [Mount Mithridat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mithridat) has been systematically excavated. Located nearby are several ancient burial mounds ([kurgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan)) and excavated cities. Kerch takes part in [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s "[Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road)" programme.\
&#x20;Treasures and historical findings of Kerch adorn the [Lapidarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapidarium,_Kerch) in Kerch and the collections of major museums around the world. Such as: the [Hermitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Museum), the Louvre, the British Museum, the Berlin Museum, the Moscow State Museum of fine arts and many others.\
&#x20;Currently, excavations at ancient fortresses of Kerch are led by scientists from Russia, Ukraine, and Poland.
