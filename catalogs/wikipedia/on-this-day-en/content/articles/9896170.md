The **Iran women's national football team**, nicknamed the **Lionesses**, represents [Iran](14653.md) in international [women's football](191466.md) and is governed by the [Football Federation Islamic Republic of Iran](1262861.md) (FFIRI). The team played its first international match in May 1971 against [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_women's_national_football_team) and its first FIFA-recognised international in September 2005 against [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_women's_national_football_team).

## History

### Early years: 1969–1979

In 1969, a group of Iranian women attended [FIFA](11049.md) coaching classes for women's football in South Korea, Singapore, and India. During their time in those countries they also watched women's football matches. Upon their return home, [Iran Football Federation](1262861.md) after many meetings decided to kick start women's football in Iran. Soon after, clubs such as [Taj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteghlal_F.C.), [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_F.C.), Deyhim, [Oghab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oghab_Tehran_F.C.?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [PAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAS_Tehran_F.C.) formed women's football teams and a women's football league was established in [Tehran](57654.md). Persepolis women football team was coached by [Alan Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Rogers_\(football_manager\)), assisted by [Ali Parvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Parvin).[^cite_note-3]

On 7 May 1971, visitors to Tehran, [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_women's_national_football_team), took to the field and won against Iranian club side [Taj S.C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteghlal_F.C.)[^cite_note-4] 2-0. The goals were scored by Medri and Bertolo. The Italians also missed a penalty kick by Nonni. It was the first time an Iranian women's team faced a foreign opponent. Two days later there was the second match which was between Iran women's national football team and Italy, with the Azzurre scoring five goals. Nonni, [Gerwien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Grazia_Gerwien), who scored a brace, Pesenti and Gualdi scored.[^cite_note-5]

Both matches were organisedby [Pari Abasalti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pari_Abasalti), editor-in-chief of *[Ettelaat-e Banuvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettelaat-e_Banuvan)* magazine, who was also the president of Iran's Damsels and Ladies Association. The venue was [Amjadieh stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiroudi_Stadium) in Tehran. Iran women's national team comprised players selected from Taj, Persepolis, Deyhim, Oghab, and PAS. Some of the players who played were Hengameh Afshar, Goli Rahani, and Effat Mohammadi. The team's head coach was [Aziz Asli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aziz_Asli).[^cite_note-6]

Women's football was abandoned after the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md).

### Re-establishment in 2004

Refounded in 2004,[^cite_note-7] the team reached second place at the [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_West_Asian_Football_Federation_Women's_Championship) [West Asian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asian_Football_Federation) [Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WAFF_Championship#Women's_tournaments) in [Amman](45007.md), [Jordan](7515964.md) held in September and October 2005.

In May 2006, the women's team hosted their first foreign visitors when a club from Berlin, Germany called BSV Al-Dersimspor played out a 2–2 draw in [Ararat Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ararat_Stadium) in Tehran.

The team won second place again at the [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_West_Asian_Football_Federation_Women's_Championship) and [2011 West Asian Football Federation Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_West_Asian_Football_Federation_Women's_Championship).[^cite_note-8]

### 2010s

See also: [Women's rights in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_rights_in_Iran) and [Zahra Khajavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahra_Khajavi)

Iran were briefly banned by FIFA from international competition in 2011 for wearing [hijabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijabs). This caused Iran to forfeit its bid to qualify for the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The ban was lifted in 2012.[^cite_note-11]

In 2015 an official claimed that eight players on the team were males awaiting sex change operations. The claims arose from random gender checks introduced in 2014 after it had been revealed that four of the players were either men who had not completed sex change operations, or were women suffering from sexual development disorders.[^cite_note-12] The FIFA-appointed doctor in Iran denied the claim, saying that all of the women had been checked. [^cite_note-13]

Iran vs [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_women's_national_football_team) friendly in 2019

On 27 December 2019, [Zahra Khajavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahra_Khajavi) broke the [clean sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Sheet) record of Iranian football by not conceding any goal for 953 minutes.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Also, she, along with [Alireza Biranvand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alireza_Beiranvand), are known as the record holders of the longest hand throws in Iranian football.[^cite_note-\:0-17] As one of the best goalkeepers in the history of Iranian football, Khajavi was able to once again draw the attention of the domestic media to the Iranian women's national football team. Her achievements played a key role in the progress of the women's national football team in these years.[^cite_note-\:0-17]

Iran qualified for their first ever [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md) when they won against Jordan on penalties in the [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_qualification) for the [2022 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup) to be hosted in India.[^cite_note-19] In Iran's debut, the team held India goalless, but following India's withdrawal due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Iran's only point was lost, and thus Iran was left vulnerable to China and Chinese Taipei, losing 0–7 and 0–5 in process, and was eliminated as the worst third-placed team.

In 2023, a report on [BBC Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Persian_Television) reported the lack of facilities and friendly matches for the team. This report suggested that the Iranian Football Federation was unwilling to have a women's team.[^cite_note-20] In the [2024 AFC Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_AFC_Women's_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament), [Maryam Azmoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_Azmoon)'s team had one draw and two defeats (in the second round of the tournament). Their loss against powerful [Australia](30864460.md) was predictable for the fans.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

#### 2025–2026 protests and defections in Australia

Main article: [Defection of Iran women's national football team](82634971.md)

Following the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md) and the [large-scale crackdown in which thousands of protesters were killed](82036668.md), a number of players resigned from the national team in solidarity with the protesters.[^cite_note-24] The [Football Federation Islamic Republic of Iran](1262861.md) reportedly threatened resigning players with multi-year bans from professional football, judicial action, and prison sentences.[^cite_note-25]

During the [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup](67991141.md) in Australia, members of the squad refused to sing the national anthem before their opening match against [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_football_team) on 2 March 2026, in an act widely interpreted as solidarity with the ongoing protests and victims of the crackdown in Iran.[^cite_note-26] Iranian state television subsequently labelled the players "wartime traitors", with one broadcaster stating the designation was punishable by death.[^cite_note-27] In their subsequent matches, the players were seen singing the anthem, after reports emerged of threats from security officials accompanying the delegation.[^cite_note-28]

Following the team's elimination from the tournament, five players — [Fatemeh Pasandideh](81929593.md), [Zahra Ghanbari](64417617.md), [Zahra Sarbali](64381444.md), [Atefeh Ramezanizadeh](74434878.md) and [Mona Hamoudi](64417968.md) — accepted [humanitarian visas](50128538.md) from the [Australian Government](1222540.md) on 9 March 2026 and were taken to a safe location.[^cite_note-29] Player Mohaddeseh Zolifi and staff member Zahra Soltan Meshkeh Kar also initially chose to remain, though one of the two subsequently returned to Iran with the rest of the squad.[^cite_note-30] As the team's bus departed the hotel, it was briefly blocked by protesters chanting "Save our girls!".[^cite_note-31]

Australian Home Affairs Minister [Tony Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Burke) confirmed that six players ultimately received permanent humanitarian visas to remain in Australia.[^cite_note-32] [FFIRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FFIRI) president Mehdi Taj accused Australian authorities of kidnapping the players and stated the episode cast further doubt on Iran's participation in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-33] However on Saturday March 14th, three members of the football team, players [Mona Hamoudi](64417968.md), [Zahra Sarbali](64381444.md) and a team support staff member returned to Iran after attempting to seek asylum.[^cite_note-34]

Two of the Iranian women's football team that found asylum in Australia, Fatemeh Pasandideh and Atefeh Ramezanisadeh, made their first public appearance on Tuesday, March 17, while practicing with [a professional team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_Roar_FC_\(women\)) in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane).[^cite_note-35]

## Team image

### Nicknames

The Iran women's national football team have been known or nicknamed as the "Iranian Lionesses" (in [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): *Shirzanan*).[^cite_note-\:2-36] The team has been widely nicknamed the Persian stars since 2010. "Persian ladies" are the other nickname of the team in Asian football.[^cite_note-\:2-36]

### Media coverage and promotion

In the decades after [1979](347268.md), the media coverage of women's football in Iran had problems, but the matches of the women's national team are usually followed by Iranian men and women in the national and foreign media.[^cite_note-\:1-18]

Persian-language documentaries and sports programs are made about the team's winning potential and history.[^cite_note-\:1-18]

### Home stadium

Iran plays their home matches at the [Ararat Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ararat_Stadium).

### Attendance of women in football matches

On 9 November 2018 [Fatma Samoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatma_Samoura), Secretary General of International Federation of Football Association [FIFA](11049.md) said she would ask Iranian government to end ban on women’s entry to sport stadiums.[^cite_note-38]

In September 2019, it was reported that Iranian female football fan [Sahar Khodayari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahar_Khodayari), dubbed the "blue girl", died after self-immolating in front of a court in Tehran when she found out she could face a two year sentence for attempting to enter a football stadium to watch her favourite team play.[^cite_note-39]

### Players of Iranian heritage in other national teams

Unfortunately, due to strict FIFA rules, Iran women's team is unable to acquire experienced footballers who have many caps for other national teams. Two players of Iranian heritage in other national teams are [Sara Doorsoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Doorsoun) ([Germany](11867.md) [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team)), and [Natasha Shirazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Shirazi) ([Uganda](31816.md) [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_women's_national_football_team)).
