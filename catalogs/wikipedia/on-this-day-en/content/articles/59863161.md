# Match fixing in tennis

&#x20; The issue of **match fixing in tennis** is an ongoing problem. First reported on by The Sunday Telegraph in 2003,[^cite_note-1] an organisation called the [Tennis Integrity Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Integrity_Unit) was set up in 2008 following an investigation into the problem.[^cite_note-2] In 2011, [Daniel Köllerer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_K%C3%B6llerer) became the first player to receive a lifetime ban from the sport due to match fixing.[^cite_note-3] Later that year, the organisers of the Wimbledon tournament were provided a list of people suspected of involvement in the issue.[^cite_note-4] In 2016 the BBC reported on "evidence of widespread suspected match-fixing at the top level of world tennis, including at Wimbledon",[^cite_note-5] and in February 2019 the BBC said that tennis was a "sport riddled with corruption".[^cite_note-6]

In 2021 the International Governing Bodies of professional tennis established the [International Tennis Integrity Agency](66396342.md) as a further step to combat corruption in the sport.[^cite_note-7]

## Examples

- In 2003, The Sunday Telegraph newspaper in London carried a front-page story entitled, "Tennis players are throwing matches for financial gain". Its investigations included references to numerous matches, including one that involved a top 10-ranked player. As a result of the Telegraph's investigations, "Internet odds exchange Betfair announce\[d] that it signed a memorandum of understanding with the Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP)"

  [^cite_note-8]

- In 2007, French tennis player [Arnaud Clément](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaud_Cl%C3%A9ment) claimed he was offered a bribe to fix a match, which he turned down, but added, "I won't say where or under what circumstances". Clément feared divulging more details on the bribe would have negative consequences on his career.

  [^cite_note-9]

- In 2008, the [Association of Tennis Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals) cleared Russian tennis player [Nikolay Davydenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Davydenko) from allegations that he fixed a match against [Martín Vassallo Argüello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_Vassallo_Arg%C3%BCello) in Poland in 2007.

  [^cite_note-10]

  &#x20;In 2016, an investigation found that several millions of dollars were placed on the match from Russian-based accounts. Leaked files to the joint [BuzzFeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuzzFeed) and [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Broadcasting_Corporation) investigators found 82 instances where Davydenko had sent or received [text messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_message) from the suspected head of an Italian [sports betting](1328050.md) syndicate.

  [^cite_note-11]

- In January 2016, a joint [BuzzFeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuzzFeed) and [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Broadcasting_Corporation) investigation reported alleged widespread match-fixing, which involve Northern Italian, Sicilian, and Russian betting syndicates, which included suspicious betting at major tournaments such as [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon). The reporters examined betting incidents on a total of 26,000 matches.

  [^cite_note-12]

- In June 2018, [Argentinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentinian) tennis player [Nicolás Kicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Kicker) was banned from the sport for at least three years for match-fixing. According to an investigation by the [Tennis Integrity Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Integrity_Unit), Kicker knowingly participated in at least two fixed matches in 2015. The ruling prohibits Kicker from competing in or attending a sanctioned tennis match.

  [^cite_note-13]

- In July 2018, Egyptian tennis player [Karim Hossam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Hossam) received a lifetime ban for match fixing.

  [^cite_note-14]

- In August 2019, Egyptian tennis player [Issam Haitham Taweel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issam_Haitham_Taweel) was suspended for five years for match-fixing and other corruption offences.

  [^cite_note-15]

- In September 2019, Brazilian tennis player Diego Matos received a lifetime ban for match fixing.

  [^cite_note-16]

- In May 2020, Egyptian tennis player [Youssef Hossam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youssef_Hossam) received a lifetime ban for match fixing, as his brother Karim had two years prior.

  [^cite_note-opencourt1-17]

- In June 2021, Russian tennis player [Yana Sizikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yana_Sizikova) was arrested at Roland Garros after her [French Open doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles) first-round loss, amid a match fixing investigation from [previous year's tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles).

  [^cite_note-18]

  &#x20;The prosecutor's office said her arrest was for "sports bribery and organized fraud for acts likely to have been committed in September 2020."

  [^cite_note-19]

  &#x20;The case was opened by a French police unit specializing in betting fraud and match-fixing, and centered on suspicions about one match at Roland Garros.

  [^cite_note-20]

- In 2023, a large match-fixing ring in the lower levels of professional tennis, centered around gambling, was broken. At least 181 players were involved.

  [^cite_note-21]
