**Teterboro Airport** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **TEB[^cite_note-3]**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **KTEB**, [FAA](11186.md) [LID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_identifier): **TEB**) is a [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) [relief airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_airport) situated in the [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(New_Jersey\)) of [Teterboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teterboro,_New_Jersey), [Moonachie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonachie,_New_Jersey), and [Hasbrouck Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbrouck_Heights,_New_Jersey) in [Bergen County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_County,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](21648.md).[^cite_note-Location-4] It is owned and managed by the [Port Authority of New York and New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey), and operated by AFCO AvPORTS Management. The airport is in the [New Jersey Meadowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Meadowlands), 12 miles (19 km) north-northwest of [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), making it popular for private and corporate aircraft. The airport has a weight limit of 100,000 pounds (45,000 kg) on aircraft, making it nonviable for any commercial service.

The airport takes up almost all of Teterboro and consists of 827 acres (3.35 km<sup>2</sup>): 90 acres (0.36 km<sup>2</sup>) for aircraft hangar and [offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office), 408 acres (1.65 km<sup>2</sup>) for aeronautical use and [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway), and 329 acres (1.33 km<sup>2</sup>) undeveloped. The airport has more than 1,137 [employees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employee), of whom more than 90% work full-time.

In April 2009, the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) reported that the airport had the third-highest rate of [wildlife strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike) of any airport in the United States, based on takeoffs and landings (43 per 100,000).[^cite_note-5]

Teterboro is home to many private aviation charter companies flying both nationally and globally.[^cite_note-6]

## History

Teterboro Airport is the oldest operating airport in the [New York City area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area). Walter C. Teter (1863–1929[^cite_note-7]) acquired the property in 1917,[^cite_note-8] and [North American Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation) operated a manufacturing plant on the site during [World War I](4764461.md). After the war, the airport served as a base of operations for [Anthony Fokker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Fokker), the Dutch aircraft designer. The first flight from the present airport site was made in 1919. In 1926, [Colonial Air Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Air_Transport) at Teterboro was the first private company to deliver mail by air.[^cite_note-9]

During [World War II](32927.md), the [United States Army](32087.md) operated at the airport. The [Port of New York Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey) came to a deal with Frederic Wehran on August 12, 1948 to purchase the airport for \$3.015 million. At the time of the signed agreement, the Port Authority gave Wehran \$100,000. The rest, official as of April 2, 1949, would be \$1.4 million in bonds and \$548,000 in cash. The rest would go to discharging the \$967,000 mortgage on the airport.[^cite_note-10] The Port Authority later leased it to [Pan American World Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_World_Airways) (and its successor organization [Johnson Controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Controls)) for 30 years until December 1, 2000, when the Port Authority assumed full responsibility for the operation of Teterboro.[^cite_note-11]

Since 1977, aircraft greater than 100,000 pounds (45 t) are not allowed to take off from Teterboro, effectively banning commercial service. In 2003, U.S. Congressman [Steve Rothman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Rothman) helped authorize a federal bill codifying the ban, citing excessive noise in the surrounding residential areas.[^cite_note-12]

In 2019, Teterboro Airport trailed [Republic Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Airport) in total number of aircraft operations by 46,047 (173,625 at TEB vs 219,672 at FRG), making it the second busiest general aviation airport in the region and fifth busiest airport when including operations from [Kennedy Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Airport) (463,198), [Newark Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Airport) (449,543), and [LaGuardia Airport](248547.md) (374,539).[^cite_note-OPSNET-1]

In 2020, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the airport saw a significant drop in total number of aircraft operations, with a reduction in traffic to 86,465 (difference in 87,160 flights); however, Teterboro leads [Republic Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Airport) in transient flights, while Republic has more local general aviation traffic, with 165,250 flights during the pandemic.[^cite_note-OPSNET-1]

## Facilities

Teterboro Airport covers 827 acres (335 ha) at an elevation of 8.4 feet (2.6 m).[^cite_note-FAA-2]

### Air traffic control

The airport's first [air control tower](48563.md) opened in 1975. In October 2025, a new \$73.4 million 157 feet (48 m) tower opened on top of a 19,000 sq ft (1,800 m<sup>2</sup>) base.[^cite_note-18]

### Runways

[Runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway) 6-24 is 6,013 feet (1,833 m) long and 150 feet (46 m) wide, with [High Intensity Runway Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway_Edge_Lights) (HIRL). Runway 6 approach has an [Instrument Landing System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_Landing_System) (ILS), and a Medium Approach Lighting System-R (MALS-R). Runway 24 approach is equipped with both a [Precision Approach Path Indicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_Approach_Path_Indicator) (PAPI) and [Runway End Identification Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway_End_Identification_Lights) (REIL) systems. Runway 6-24 underwent complete overlay and grooving in 1987.

Runway 1-19 is 7,000 feet (2,100 m) long and 150 feet (46 m) wide, with HIRL. Both runways 1 and 19 are equipped with REIL systems. Runway 1 approach is equipped with a [VASI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_Approach_Slope_Indicator) system. Runway 19 approach has an [ILS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_Landing_System), and a [Precision Approach Path Indicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_Approach_Path_Indicator) (PAPI). Runway 1-19 was overlaid and grooved in the summer of 2000, and included the installation of centerline and touchdown zone lighting. Runway 19 is the preferred runway for noise [abatement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_mitigation) procedures.

### Aircraft

In 2017, the airport had 178,369 aircraft operations, averaging 488 per day: 65.6% [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation), 34% [air taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_taxi), 0.3% military, and <1% airline. 121 aircraft were then based at this airport: 81% jet, 10.7% [helicopter](8286923.md), 6.6% single-engine, and 1.7% multi-engine.[^cite_note-FAA-2]

### Law enforcement

Since the [September 11, 2001, terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001,_terrorist_attacks), law enforcement at Teterboro Airport has been in the hands of the [Newark Liberty International Airport](248492.md) Command of the [Port Authority of New York and New Jersey Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey_Police_Department). In addition to normal uniformed patrol of terminals, concourses, and parking lots, the PAPD provides anti-crime plainclothes units, criminal investigative detective squads, counter-terrorism units, high-value cargo escorts and patrols, dignitary protection, passenger screening point protection and security, Aircraft Rescue Fire Fighting and community outreach.[^cite_note-19] The PAPD operates alongside partner agencies, including the [Transportation Security Administration](172933.md) (TSA), [U.S. Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) (CBP), and the [New Jersey State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_State_Police).

### Other

The [Aviation Hall of Fame of New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Hall_of_Fame_of_New_Jersey) is on the airport grounds. Founded in 1972, it is the first state aviation hall of fame in the nation, honoring the men and women who brought outstanding aeronautical achievements to the state. The museum offers visitors an opportunity to view historic air and space equipment and artifacts, photographs, fine art and an extensive model collection. The library has more than 4,000 volumes and hundreds of aviation video tapes.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Public transportation

Teterboro Airport can be reached from the [Port Authority Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_Bus_Terminal) in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan) on [New Jersey Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Transit) bus routes [161](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/161_\(New_Jersey_bus\)) (regular service), [165](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/165_\(New_Jersey_bus\)) (limited weekday service) and [144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/144_\(New_Jersey_bus\)) (peak periods weekdays).[^cite_note-Location-4] The [Teterboro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teterboro_\(NJT_station\)) is the closest rail station along [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit)'s [Pascack Valley Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascack_Valley_Line), but [Wood-Ridge station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood-Ridge_\(NJT_station\)), along the same route, is also close to the southwest of the airport.

## Notable incidents

- In 1956 and again in 1958, [Thomas Fitzpatrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fitzpatrick_\(pilot\)) flew stolen aircraft from Teterboro and landed them along city streets in the [Washington Heights, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Heights,_Manhattan) neighborhood.

- In June 1966, in Hasbrouck Heights, a two-engine [Piper Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_Aztec) going to Teterboro Airport crashed, striking a tree and narrowly missing homes on Burton Avenue near [U.S. Route 46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_46) (US 46). The pilot sustained injuries including a skull fracture and was taken to Hackensack Hospital by ambulance. He was carrying film for Eastman Kodak.

- On September 23, 1981, a Ronson Aviation Bell 206B helicopter and a Seminole Air Charier Piper PA-34 airplane collided in flight over [East Rutherford](136878.md), about 2 nautical miles (3.7 km; 2.3 mi) south of Teterboro Airport. The airplane had a flight plan to Teterboro from [Syracuse, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York). The helicopter was inbound to Teterboro from Woodbridge, NJ. The two collided at an altitude of about 650 feet (200 m). The helicopter fell into the [Meadowlands Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Sports_Complex) parking lot, and both persons aboard were killed. The airplane, with about 8 feet (2.4 m) of its left wing and its right engine missing, made a gear-up landing in a marsh about 0.7 nautical miles (1.3 km; 0.81 mi) east of the collision point. The pilot was seriously injured, and the passenger received minor injuries.

The two pilots were seriously injured, as were two occupants in a vehicle. The cabin aide, eight passengers, and one person in the building received minor injuries. Five people remained hospitalized, one of them gravely injured. A 66-year-old Paterson man who was riding in a car the jet struck was on life support, authorities said.
