**Edinburgh**[^cite_note-6] is the [capital city](181337.md) of [Scotland](26994.md) and one of its 32 [council areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_areas_of_Scotland). It is located in southeast Scotland and is bounded to the north by the [Firth of Forth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firth_of_Forth) and to the south by the [Pentland Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentland_Hills). Edinburgh had a population of 506,520 in 2020,[^cite_note-NRS_Locality_Estimate-7] making it the [second-most-populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_towns_and_cities_in_Scotland_by_population) city in Scotland (behind [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow)) and the [seventh-most-populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md). The [wider metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_urban_area) had a population of 912,490 in the same year.[^cite_note-8]

Recognised as the capital of Scotland since at least the 15th century, Edinburgh is the seat of the [Scottish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government), the [Scottish Parliament](61188.md), the [highest courts in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Scotland), and the [Palace of Holyroodhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Holyroodhouse), the [official residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_residence) of the [British monarch](47063.md) in Scotland. It is also the annual venue of the [General Assembly of the Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_of_the_Church_of_Scotland). The city has long been a centre of education, particularly in the fields of medicine, [Scottish law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_literature), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_philosophy), the sciences and engineering. The [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) was founded in 1582 and is now one of four universities in the city. The [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre) of Scotland, Edinburgh was ranked as the second-largest financial centre in the United Kingdom, the fourth-largest in Europe, and the thirteenth-largest in the world in the [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index) in 2020.[^cite_note-9]

The city is a cultural centre, and is the home of institutions including the [National Museum of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Scotland), the [National Library of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Scotland), and the [Scottish National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_National_Gallery).[^cite_note-10] The city is also known for the [Edinburgh International Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Festival) and [the Fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Festival_Fringe), the latter being the world's largest annual international arts festival. Historic sites in Edinburgh include [Edinburgh Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle), the Palace of Holyroodhouse, [St Giles' Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Giles'_Cathedral), [Greyfriars Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyfriars_Kirk), [Canongate Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canongate_Kirk) and the extensive Georgian New Town built in the 18th and 19th centuries. The [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_Edinburgh) and the [New Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Town,_Edinburgh) are together listed as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md),[^cite_note-www\.edinburgh.org_world-heritage-site-11] and the site has been managed by [Edinburgh World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_World_Heritage) since 1999. The city's historical and cultural attractions have made it Britain's second-most-visited tourist destination, attracting 5.3 million visits, including 2.4 million from overseas, in 2023.[^cite_note-12]

Edinburgh is governed by the [City of Edinburgh Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Edinburgh_Council), a unitary authority. The City of Edinburgh council area had an estimated population of 531,370 in 2025,[^cite_note-popstats-2] and includes outlying towns and villages. The city is in the [Lothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothian) region and was historically part of the [shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shires_of_Scotland) of [Midlothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlothian) (also called Edinburghshire).

## Etymology

Main article: [Etymology of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Edinburgh)

"Edin", the root of the city's name, derives from *_[Eidyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidyn)_*, the name for the region in [Cumbric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbric), the [Brittonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittonic_languages) [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) language formerly spoken there. The name's meaning is unknown.[^cite_note-Gelling-13] The district of Eidyn was centred on the stronghold of Din Eidyn, the [dun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dun_\(fortification\)) or [hillfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillfort) of Eidyn.[^cite_note-Gelling-13] This stronghold is believed to have been located at [Castle Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Rock,_Edinburgh),[^cite_note-14] now the site of [Edinburgh Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle). A siege of Din Eidyn by [Oswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_of_Northumbria), king of the [Angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angles_\(tribe\)) of [Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria) in 638 marked the beginning of three centuries of Germanic influence in south east Scotland that laid the foundations for the development of [Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_language), before the town was ultimately subsumed in 954 by the kingdom known to the English as Scotland.[^cite_note-Driscoll229-15] As the language shifted from Cumbric to [Northumbrian Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbrian_Old_English) and then [Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_language), the Brittonic *din* in Din Eidyn was replaced by *[burh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burh)*, producing *Edinburgh*. In [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic) *din* becomes *dùn*, producing modern *Dùn Èideann*.[^cite_note-Gelling-13][^cite_note-16]

## Nicknames

The city is nicknamed ***Auld Reekie***,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Scots for *Old Smoky*, for the views from the country of the smoke-covered Old Town. A note in a collection of the works of the poet [Allan Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Ramsay_\(poet\)) explains, "Auld Reeky...A name the country people give Edinburgh, from the cloud of smoke or reek that is always impending over it."[^cite_note-19] In [Walter Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Scott)'s 1820 novel *[The Abbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Abbot)*, a character observes that "yonder stands Auld Reekie—you may see the smoke hover over her at twenty miles' distance".[^cite_note-20] [Thomas Carlyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Carlyle) comments on the phenomenon: "Smoke cloud hangs over old Edinburgh, for, ever since [Aeneas Silvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_II)'s time and earlier, the people have the art, very strange to Aeneas, of burning a certain sort of black stones, and Edinburgh with its chimneys is called 'Auld Reekie' by the country people".[^cite_note-21] The 19th-century historian [Robert Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Chambers_\(publisher_born_1802\)) asserted that the [sobriquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobriquet) could not be traced before the reign of [King Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) in the late 17th century. He attributed the name to a [Fife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fife) laird, Durham of Largo, who regulated the bedtime of his children by the smoke rising above Edinburgh from the fires of the tenements. "It's time now bairns, to tak' the beuks, and gang to our beds, for yonder's Auld Reekie, I see, putting on her nicht-cap!".[^cite_note-22]

Edinburgh has been popularly called the *Athens of the North* since the early 19th century.[^cite_note-Lowrey-23] References to [Athens](1216.md), such as *Athens of Britain* and *Modern Athens*, had been made as early as the 1760s. The similarities were seen to be topographical but also intellectual. Edinburgh's Castle Rock reminded returning [grand tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour) of the [Athenian](1216.md) [Acropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acropolis,_Athens), as did aspects of the [neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) and layout of [New Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Town,_Edinburgh).[^cite_note-Lowrey-23] In 1818, naturalist [Edward Daniel Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Daniel_Clarke) called Edinburgh "a very correct model of a Grecian city", pointing out perceived similarities between both cities and their [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) (respectively, [Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith) and [Piraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piraeus)).[^cite_note-Lowrey-23] Intellectually, the [Scottish Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Enlightenment), with its [humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism) and [rationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalism) outlook, was influenced by [Ancient Greek philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_philosophy).[^cite_note-24] In 1822 the Scottish landscape painter [Hugh William Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_William_Williams) organised an exhibition that showed his paintings of Athens alongside views of Edinburgh, and the idea of a direct parallel between both cities quickly caught the popular imagination.[^cite_note-25] When plans were drawn up in the early 19th century to architecturally develop [Calton Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calton_Hill), the design of the [National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Monument_of_Scotland) directly copied Athens' [Parthenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon).[^cite_note-McKee-26] [Tom Stoppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Stoppard)'s character Archie of *[Jumpers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumpers_\(play\))* said, perhaps playing on [Reykjavík](25798.md) meaning "smoky bay", that the "Reykjavík of the South" would be more appropriate.[^cite_note-27]

The city has also been known by several [Latin names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Latin_place_names_in_Britain#Cities_and_towns_in_Scotland), such as *Edinburgum*, while the adjectival forms *Edinburgensis* and *Edinensis* are used in educational and scientific contexts.[^cite_note-Grässe1909-28][^cite_note-29]

*Edina* is a late 18th-century poetical form used by the Scottish poets [Robert Fergusson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fergusson) and [Robert Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Burns). "Embra" or "Embro" are colloquialisms from the same time,[^cite_note-30] as in [Robert Garioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Garioch)'s *Embro to the Ploy*.[^cite_note-31]

[Ben Jonson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Jonson) described it as "Britaine's other eye",[^cite_note-32] and Sir Walter Scott referred to it as "yon Empress of the North".[^cite_note-33] [Robert Louis Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Louis_Stevenson), also a son of the city, wrote that Edinburgh "is what Paris ought to be".[^cite_note-34]

## History

Main article: [History of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Edinburgh)

### Early history



[Surgeons' Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgeons'_Hall), one of the [Greek Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Revival_architecture) buildings that earned Edinburgh the nickname "Athens of the North"

The earliest known human habitation in the Edinburgh area was at [Cramond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramond), where evidence was found of a [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) camp site dated to c. 8500 BC.[^cite_note-35] Traces of later [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) settlements have been found on Castle Rock, [Arthur's Seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur's_Seat), [Craiglockhart Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiglockhart_Hill), and the [Pentland Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentland_Hills).[^cite_note-36]

When the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) arrived in Lothian at the end of the 1st century AD, they found a [Brittonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Britons) Celtic tribe whose name they recorded as the [Votadini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votadini).[^cite_note-37] The Votadini transitioned into the [Gododdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gododdin) kingdom in the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages), with Eidyn serving as one of the kingdom's districts. During this period, the Castle Rock site, thought to have been the stronghold of Din Eidyn, emerged as the kingdom's major centre.[^cite_note-38] The medieval [Welsh-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) poem *[Y Gododdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Gododdin)* describes a war band from across the Brittonic world who gathered in Eidyn before a fateful raid; this may describe a historical event around 600AD.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In 638 the Gododdin stronghold was besieged by forces loyal to [King Oswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_of_Northumbria) of [Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria), and around this time, control of Lothian passed to the [Angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angles_\(tribe\)). Their influence continued for the next three centuries until around 950, when, during the reign of [Indulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indulf), son of [Constantine II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_II_of_Scotland), the "burh" (fortress), named in the 10th-century *[Pictish Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictish_Chronicle)* as *oppidum Eden*,[^cite_note-42] was abandoned to the Scots. It thenceforth remained, for the most part, under their jurisdiction.[^cite_note-43]

The [royal burgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_burgh) was founded by [King David I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_I_of_Scotland) in the early 12th century on land belonging to the Crown, though the date of its charter is unknown.[^cite_note-44] The first documentary evidence of the medieval [burgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgh) is a [royal charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_charter), c. 1124–1127, by King David I granting a [toft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgage) in *burgo meo de Edenesburg* to the [Priory of Dunfermline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunfermline_Abbey).[^cite_note-45] The [shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shires_of_Scotland) of Edinburgh seems also to have been created during David's reign, possibly covering all of Lothian at first, but by 1305 the eastern and western parts of Lothian had become [Haddingtonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Lothian) and [Linlithgowshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lothian), leaving Edinburgh as the county town of a shire covering the central part of Lothian, which was called Edinburghshire or [Midlothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlothian) (the latter name being an informal, but commonly used, alternative until the county's name was legally changed in 1947).[^cite_note-Chalmers-46][^cite_note-47]

During the 12th century, human pressures on woodland resources left the land surrounding Edinburgh almost treeless, and the burgh had to resort to [peat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat) for fuel.[^cite_note-48]



Edinburgh, showing Arthur's Seat, one of the earliest known sites of human habitation in the area

Edinburgh was largely under English control from 1291 to 1314 and from 1333 to 1341, during the [Wars of Scottish Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_Scottish_Independence). When the [English invaded Scotland in 1298](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_invasion_of_Scotland_\(1298\)), [Edward I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England) chose not to enter Edinburgh but passed by it with his army.[^cite_note-49]

In the middle of the 14th century the French chronicler [Jean Froissart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Froissart) described it as the capital of Scotland (c. 1365), and [James III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_III_of_Scotland) (1451–1488) referred to it in the 15th century as "the principal burgh of our kingdom".[^cite_note-50] In 1482 James III "granted and perpetually confirmed to the said Provost, Bailies, Clerk, Council, and Community, and their successors, the office of [Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriff_principal) within the Burgh for ever, to be exercised by the Provost for the time as Sheriff, and by the Bailies for the time as Sheriffsdepute conjunctly and severally; with full power to hold Courts, to punish transgressors not only by banishment but by death, to appoint officers of Court, and to do everything else appertaining to the office of Sheriff; as also to apply to their own proper use the fines and escheats arising out of the exercise of the said office."[^cite_note-51] Despite being [burnt by the English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_of_Edinburgh_\(1544\)) in 1544, Edinburgh continued to develop and grow,[^cite_note-52] and was at the centre of events in the 16th-century [Scottish Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Reformation)[^cite_note-53] and 17th-century [Wars of the Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenanter).[^cite_note-54] In 1582 Edinburgh's town council was given a [royal charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_charter) by [King James VI and I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_VI_and_I) permitting the establishment of a university;[^cite_note-55] founded as *Tounis College* (Town's College), the institution developed into the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), which contributed to Edinburgh's central intellectual role in subsequent centuries.[^cite_note-56]

### 17th century



Edinburgh in the 17th century

In 1603 [King James VI of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_VI_and_I) succeeded to the English throne, uniting the crowns of Scotland and England, an event known as the [Union of the Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_the_Crowns), though the two kingdoms remained separate realms governed in [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union).[^cite_note-57] In 1638 [King Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England)'s attempt to introduce [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion) church forms in Scotland encountered stiff [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism) opposition, culminating in the conflicts of the [Wars of the Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Three_Kingdoms).[^cite_note-58] Subsequent Scottish support for [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England)'s restoration to the throne of England resulted in Edinburgh's occupation by [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell)'s [Commonwealth of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_England) forces – the [New Model Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army) – in 1650.[^cite_note-59]

In the 17th century Edinburgh's boundaries were still defined by the city's defensive [town walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_town_walls). As a result, the city's growing population was accommodated by increasing the height of the houses. Buildings of 11 storeys or more were common,[^cite_note-60] and have been described as forerunners of the modern-day skyscraper.[^cite_note-Council_on_Tall_Buildings_and_Urban_Habitat-61][^cite_note-62] Most of these old structures were replaced by the predominantly [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) buildings seen in today's Old Town. In 1611 an act of parliament created the [High Constables of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Constables_of_Edinburgh) to keep order in the city, thought to be the oldest statutory police force in the world.[^cite_note-63]

### 18th century



A painting showing Edinburgh characters (based on [John Kay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kay_\(caricaturist\))'s caricatures) behind St Giles' Cathedral in the late 18th century

Following the [Treaty of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Union) in 1706, the Parliaments of England and Scotland passed [Acts of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707) in 1706 and 1707 respectively, uniting the two kingdoms in the [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) effective from 1 May 1707.[^cite_note-64] As a consequence, the [Parliament of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Scotland) merged with the [Parliament of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_England) to form the [Parliament of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain), which sat at [Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster) in London. The Union was opposed by many Scots, resulting in riots in the city.[^cite_note-65]

By the first half of the 18th century, Edinburgh was described as one of Europe's most densely populated, overcrowded, and unsanitary towns.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Visitors were struck by the fact that the social classes shared the same urban space, even inhabiting the same [tenement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenement) buildings; although here a form of social segregation did prevail, whereby shopkeepers and tradesmen tended to occupy the cheaper-to-rent cellars and garrets, while the more well-to-do professional classes occupied the more expensive middle storeys.[^cite_note-68]

During the [Jacobite rising of 1745](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobite_rising_of_1745), Edinburgh was briefly occupied by the Jacobite "Highland Army" before its march into England.[^cite_note-69] After its eventual defeat at [Culloden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Culloden), there followed a period of reprisals and pacification, largely directed at the rebellious [clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_clan).[^cite_note-70] In Edinburgh, the Town Council, keen to emulate London by initiating city improvements and expansion to the north of the castle,[^cite_note-71] reaffirmed its belief in the Union and loyalty to the [Hanoverian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hanover) monarch [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom) by its choice of names for the streets of the New Town: for example, [Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_rose) Street and [Thistle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thistle#Scottish_thistle) Street; and for the royal family, [George Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Street,_Edinburgh), [Queen Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Street,_Edinburgh), Hanover Street, Frederick Street and [Princes Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_Street) (in honour of George's two sons).[^cite_note-princeshist-72] The consistently geometric layout of the plan for the extension of Edinburgh was the result of a major competition in urban planning staged by the Town Council in 1766.[^cite_note-73]

In the second half of the century, the city was at the heart of the [Scottish Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Enlightenment),[^cite_note-74] when thinkers like [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume), [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith), [James Hutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hutton) and [Joseph Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Black) were familiar figures in its streets. Edinburgh became a major intellectual centre, earning it the nickname "Athens of the North" because of its many [neo-classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) buildings and reputation for learning, recalling ancient Athens.[^cite_note-75] In the 18th-century novel *[The Expedition of Humphry Clinker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expedition_of_Humphry_Clinker)* by [Tobias Smollett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Smollett) one character describes Edinburgh as a "hotbed of genius".[^cite_note-76] Edinburgh was also a major centre for the Scottish book trade. The highly successful London bookseller [Andrew Millar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Millar_\(publisher\)) was apprenticed there to James McEuen.[^cite_note-77]

From the 1770s onwards, the professional and business classes gradually deserted the Old Town in favour of the more elegant "one-family" residences of the New Town, a migration that changed the city's social character. According to the foremost historian of this development, "Unity of social feeling was one of the most valuable heritages of old Edinburgh, and its disappearance was widely and properly lamented."[^cite_note-78]

### 19th and 20th centuries

An aerial photo of Edinburgh with an aeroplane visible

Edinburgh, c. 1920

Despite an enduring myth to the contrary,[^cite_note-79] Edinburgh became an industrial centre[^cite_note-80] with its traditional industries of printing, brewing and distilling continuing to grow in the 19th century and joined by new industries such as [rubber works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Boot_Ltd), [engineering works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) and others. By 1821 Edinburgh had been overtaken by [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) as Scotland's largest city.[^cite_note-81] The city centre between Princes Street and George Street became a major commercial and shopping district, a development partly stimulated by the arrival of railways in the 1840s. The Old Town became an increasingly dilapidated, overcrowded slum with high mortality rates.[^cite_note-82] Improvements carried out under Lord Provost [William Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Chambers_\(publisher\)) in the 1860s began the transformation of the area into the predominantly [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture) Old Town seen today.[^cite_note-83] More improvements followed in the early 20th century as a result of the work of [Patrick Geddes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Geddes),[^cite_note-84] but relative economic stagnation during the two world wars and beyond saw the Old Town deteriorate further before major [slum clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum_clearance_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the 1960s and 1970s began to reverse the process. New construction, such as of [Argyle House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argyle_House,_Edinburgh) near the castle and the university building developments, which transformed the [George Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Square,_Edinburgh) and Potterrow areas, proved highly controversial.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-h568-86]



Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) and [Donald Dewar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Dewar) at the opening of the [Scottish Parliament](61188.md), 1999

Since the 1990s a new "financial district", including the [Edinburgh International Conference Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Conference_Centre), has grown mainly on demolished railway property to the west of the castle, stretching into [Fountainbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountainbridge), a run-down 19th-century industrial suburb which has undergone radical change since the 1980s with the demise of industrial and brewery premises. This ongoing development has enabled Edinburgh to maintain its place as the United Kingdom's second largest financial and administrative centre after London.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Financial services now account for a third of all commercial office space in the city.[^cite_note-W_Rae,_Edinburgh_1994,_p.164-89] The development of [Edinburgh Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Park), a new business and technology park covering 38 acres (15 ha), 4 mi (6 km) west of the city centre, has also contributed to the District Council's strategy for the city's major economic regeneration.[^cite_note-W_Rae,_Edinburgh_1994,_p.164-89]

In 1998 the [Scotland Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Act_1998), which came into force the following year, established a [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution) Scottish Parliament and Scottish Executive (renamed the Scottish Government since September 2007[^cite_note-90]). Both based in Edinburgh, they are responsible for governing Scotland while [reserved matters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_and_excepted_matters) such as defence, foreign affairs, and some elements of income tax remain the responsibility of the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](13964.md) in London.[^cite_note-91]

### 21st century

In 2022 Edinburgh was affected by the [2022 Scotland bin strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Scotland_bin_strikes).[^cite_note-92] In 2023 Edinburgh became the first capital city in Europe to sign the global [Plant Based Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant-based_action_plan#Plant_Based_Treaty), which was introduced at [COP26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) in 2021 in Glasgow.[^cite_note-93] The [Scottish Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Greens) councillor Steve Burgess introduced the treaty. The Scottish Countryside Alliance and other farming groups called the treaty "anti-farming".[^cite_note-94]

In 2026, Edinburgh experienced a series of [pro-Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Palestinian) demonstrations linked to the [Israel–Gaza conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Gaza_conflict). On 17 January, a National Palestine Demonstration was held in the city, drawing thousands of participants. The march proceeded through the city centre and concluded near the [Scottish Parliament](61188.md).[^cite_note-source1-95]

&#x20;Later in January, pro-Palestinian activists blockaded a Leonardo UK defence facility in Edinburgh, calling on the company to respond to demands made by [Palestine Action](67777543.md).[^cite_note-source2-96][^cite_note-source3-97] a group proscribed under the [Terrorism Act 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Act_2000).[^cite_note-source4-98] The protests involved around 100 activists and required the presence and intervention of [Police Scotland](34423036.md).[^cite_note-source5-99]

## Geography

### Location



Victoria Street in October 2021

Situated in Scotland's [Central Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Belt), Edinburgh lies on the southern shore of the Firth of Forth. The city centre is 2+1⁄2 mi (4.0 km) southwest of the shoreline of [Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith) and 26 mi (42 km) inland, as the crow flies, from the east coast of Scotland and the [North Sea](21179.md) at [Dunbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar).[^cite_note-100] While the early burgh grew up near the prominent Castle Rock, the modern city is often said to be built on [seven hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hills_in_Edinburgh), namely [Calton Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calton_Hill), [Corstorphine Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corstorphine_Hill), Craiglockhart Hill, [Braid Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_Hills), [Blackford Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackford_Hill), Arthur's Seat and the Castle Rock,[^cite_note-101] giving rise to allusions to the [seven hills of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_hills_of_Rome).[^cite_note-102]

### Cityscape



View from Calton Hill

Occupying a narrow gap between the Firth of Forth to the north and the [Pentland Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentland_Hills) and their outrunners to the south, the city sprawls over a landscape which is the product of early volcanic activity and later periods of intensive glaciation. [^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> Igneous activity between 350 and 400 million years ago, coupled with [faulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)), led to the creation of tough [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) [volcanic plugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_plug), which predominate over much of the area.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> One such example is the Castle Rock which forced the advancing ice sheet to divide, sheltering the softer rock and forming a 1 mi-long (1.6 km) tail of material to the east, thus creating a distinctive [crag and tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crag_and_tail) formation.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> Glacial erosion on the north side of the crag gouged a deep valley later filled by the now drained [Nor Loch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nor_Loch). These features, along with another hollow on the rock's south side, formed an ideal natural strongpoint upon which Edinburgh Castle was built.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> Similarly, Arthur's Seat is the remains of a volcano dating from the [Carboniferous period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous_period), which was eroded by a glacier moving west to east during the ice age.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> Erosive action such as [plucking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plucking_\(glaciation\)) and [abrasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrasion_\(geology\)) exposed the rocky crags to the west before leaving a tail of deposited glacial material swept to the east.[^cite_note-104] This process formed the distinctive [Salisbury Crags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_Crags), a series of [teschenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theralite#Teschenites) cliffs between Arthur's Seat and the location of the early burgh.[^cite_note-105] The residential areas of [Marchmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchmont) and [Bruntsfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruntsfield) are built along a series of [drumlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drumlin) ridges south of the city centre, which were [deposited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_deposition) as the glacier receded.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup>

Other prominent landforms, such as [Calton Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calton_Hill) and [Corstorphine Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corstorphine_Hill), are also products of glacial erosion.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> The Braid Hills and Blackford Hill are a series of small summits to the south of the city centre that command expansive views looking northwards over the urban area to the Firth of Forth.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 64–65 </sup>

Edinburgh aerial photograph panorama taken from Princes Street, city centre.

Panorama of Edinburgh, 2025

Edinburgh is drained by the river named the [Water of Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_of_Leith), which rises at the Colzium Springs in the Pentland Hills and runs for 18 miles (29 km) through the south and west of the city, emptying into the Firth of Forth at Leith.[^cite_note-waterofleith-106] The nearest the river gets to the city centre is at [Dean Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Village) on the north-western edge of the New Town, where a deep gorge is spanned by [Thomas Telford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Telford)'s [Dean Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Bridge), built in 1832 for the road to [Queensferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Queensferry). The [Water of Leith Walkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_of_Leith_Walkway) is a mixed-use [trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail) that follows the course of the river for 19.6 km (12.2 mi) from Balerno to Leith.[^cite_note-walkway-107]

Excepting the shoreline of the Firth of Forth, Edinburgh is encircled by a [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt), designated in 1957, which stretches from [Dalmeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmeny) in the west to [Prestongrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestongrange) in the east.[^cite_note-greenbelt-108] With an average width of 3.2 km (2 mi) the principal objectives of the green belt were to contain the outward expansion of the city and to prevent the agglomeration of urban areas.[^cite_note-greenbelt-108] Expansion affecting the green belt is strictly controlled but developments such as [Edinburgh Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Airport) and the [Royal Highland Showground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Highland_Showground) at [Ingliston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingliston) lie within the zone.[^cite_note-greenbelt-108] Similarly, suburbs such as [Juniper Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniper_Green) and Balerno are situated on green belt land.[^cite_note-greenbelt-108] One feature of the Edinburgh green belt is the inclusion of parcels of land within the city which are designated green belt, even though they do not connect with the peripheral ring. Examples of these independent wedges of green belt include [Holyrood Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holyrood_Park) and Corstorphine Hill.[^cite_note-greenbelt-108]

### Areas



Map showing the areas of central Edinburgh

#### Early settlements

Edinburgh includes former towns and villages that retain much of their original character as settlements in existence before they were absorbed into the expanding city of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.[^cite_note-109] Many areas, such as [Dalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalry,_Edinburgh), contain residences that are multi-occupancy buildings known as [tenements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenement), although the more southern and western parts of the city have traditionally been less built-up with a greater number of detached and semi-detached villas.[^cite_note-110]

The historic centre of Edinburgh is divided into two by the broad green swathe of [Princes Street Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_Street_Gardens). To the south, the view is dominated by Edinburgh Castle, built high on Castle Rock, and the long sweep of the Old Town descending towards Holyrood Palace. To the north lie Princes Street and the New Town.

The [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End,_Edinburgh) includes the financial district, with insurance and banking offices as well as the Edinburgh International Conference Centre.

#### Old and New Towns



Skyline of the [Edinburgh Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Old_Town)

Edinburgh's Old and New Towns were listed as a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 1995 in recognition of the unique character of the Old Town with its medieval street layout and the planned Georgian New Town, including the adjoining Dean Village and Calton Hill areas. There are over 4,500 [listed buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building_\(United_Kingdom\)) within the city,[^cite_note-www\.edinburgh.org_world-heritage-site-11][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] a higher proportion relative to area than any other city in the United Kingdom.

The castle is perched on top of a rocky crag (the remnant of an extinct volcano), and the [Royal Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Mile) runs down the crest of a ridge from it, terminating at Holyrood Palace. Minor streets (called closes or [wynds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wynd)) lie on either side of the main spine, forming a herringbone pattern.[^cite_note-113] Due to space restrictions imposed by the narrowness of this landform, the Old Town became home to some of the earliest "high rise" residential buildings. Multi-storey dwellings known as *lands* were the norm from the 16th century onwards, with ten and eleven storeys being typical, and one even reaching fourteen or fifteen storeys.[^cite_note-114] Vaults below street level were inhabited to accommodate the influx of incomers, particularly [Irish immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_migration_to_Great_Britain), during the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution). The street has several fine public buildings such as St Giles' Cathedral, the [City Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_City_Chambers) and the [Law Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Edinburgh). Other places of historical interest nearby are [Greyfriars Kirkyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyfriars_Kirkyard) and [Mary King's Close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_King's_Close). The [Grassmarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassmarket), running deep below the castle, is connected by the steep double terraced Victoria Street. The street layout is typical of the old quarters of many Northern European cities.

&#x20;The New Town was an 18th-century solution to the problem of an increasingly crowded city, which had been confined to the ridge sloping down from the castle. In 1766 a competition to design a "New Town" was won by [James Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Craig_\(architect\)), a 27-year-old architect.[^cite_note-115] The plan was a rigid, ordered grid, which fitted in well with [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) ideas of rationality. The principal street was to be [George Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Street,_Edinburgh), running along the natural ridge to the north of what became known as the "Old Town". To either side of it are two other main streets: Princes Street and Queen Street. Princes Street has become Edinburgh's main shopping street and now has few of its [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_architecture) buildings in their original state. The three main streets are connected by a series of streets running perpendicular to them. The east and west ends of George Street are terminated by [St Andrew Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrew_Square,_Edinburgh) and [Charlotte Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Square) respectively.&#x20;



Skyline of the [Edinburgh New Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_New_Town)

&#x20;The latter, designed by [Robert Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Adam), influenced the architectural style of the New Town into the early 19th century.[^cite_note-116] [Bute House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bute_House), the official residence of the [First Minister of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland), is on the north side of Charlotte Square.[^cite_note-117]

The hollow between the Old and New Towns was formerly the [Nor Loch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nor_Loch), which was created for the town's defence but came to be used by the inhabitants for dumping their [sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage). It was drained by the 1820s as part of the city's northward expansion. Craig's original plan included an ornamental canal on the site of the loch,[^cite_note-princeshist-72] but this idea was abandoned.[^cite_note-118] Soil excavated while laying the foundations of buildings in the New Town was dumped on the site of the [loch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loch) to create the slope connecting the Old and New Towns known as [The Mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mound).

In the middle of the 19th century the [National Gallery of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Scotland) and the [Royal Scottish Academy Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Scottish_Academy_Building) were built on The Mound, and tunnels for the railway line between [Haymarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_railway_station) and [Waverley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Waverley_railway_station) stations were driven through it.

#### Southside

Main article: [Southside, Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southside,_Edinburgh)

The Southside is a residential part of the city, which includes the districts of [St Leonards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Leonard's,_Edinburgh), [Marchmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchmont), [Morningside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningside,_Edinburgh), [Newington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newington,_Edinburgh), [Sciennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciennes), [the Grange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grange,_Edinburgh) and [Blackford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackford,_Edinburgh). The Southside is broadly analogous to the area covered formerly by the [Burgh Muir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgh_Muir), and was developed as a residential area after the opening of the [South Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bridge,_Edinburgh) in the 1780s. The Southside is particularly popular with families (many state and private schools are here), young professionals and students (the central University of Edinburgh campus is based around [George Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Square,_Edinburgh) just north of Marchmont and [the Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meadows_\(park\))), and [Napier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napier_University) (with major campuses around Merchiston and Morningside). The area is also well provided with hotels and "bed and breakfast" accommodation for visiting festival-goers. These districts often feature in works of fiction. For example, [Church Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Hill,_Edinburgh) in Morningside, was the home of [Muriel Spark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_Spark)'s Miss Jean Brodie,[^cite_note-119] and [Ian Rankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Rankin)'s [Inspector Rebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_Rebus) lives in Marchmont and works in St Leonards.[^cite_note-120]

#### Leith



The Shore, Leith

[Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith) was historically the port of Edinburgh, an arrangement of unknown date that was confirmed by the royal charter Robert the Bruce granted to the city in 1329.[^cite_note-121] The port developed a separate identity from Edinburgh, which to some extent it still retains, and it was a matter of great resentment when the two burghs merged in 1920 into the City of Edinburgh.[^cite_note-122] Even today, the parliamentary seat is known as "Edinburgh North and Leith". The loss of traditional industries and commerce (the [last shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Robb) closed in 1983) resulted in economic decline.[^cite_note-123] The Edinburgh Waterfront development has transformed old dockland areas from Leith to Granton into residential areas with shopping and leisure facilities and helped rejuvenate the area. With the redevelopment, Edinburgh has gained the business of cruise liner companies, which now provide cruises to Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Germany, and the Netherlands.

The coastal suburb of [Portobello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portobello,_Edinburgh) is characterised by Georgian villas, Victorian tenements, a beach and [promenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade), and cafés, bars, restaurants and independent shops. There are rowing and sailing clubs, a restored Victorian swimming pool, and [Victorian Turkish baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Turkish_baths).

#### Urban area

The [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_towns_and_cities_in_Scotland_by_population#Settlements) of Edinburgh is almost entirely within the [City of Edinburgh Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_City_of_Edinburgh_Council) boundary, merging with [Musselburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musselburgh) in East Lothian. Towns within easy reach of the city boundary include [Inverkeithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverkeithing), [Haddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haddington,_East_Lothian), [Tranent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranent), [Prestonpans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestonpans), [Dalkeith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalkeith), [Bonnyrigg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnyrigg), [Loanhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanhead), [Penicuik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicuik), [Broxburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broxburn,_West_Lothian), [Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingston,_West_Lothian) and [Dunfermline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunfermline). Edinburgh lies at the heart of the Edinburgh & South East Scotland City region, with a population in 2014 of 1,339,380.[^cite_note-NRS_LA_Estimate-124][^cite_note-City_Region_Definition-125]

### Climate



Sunshine in [Princes Street Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_Street_Gardens)

Like most of Scotland, Edinburgh has a cool [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate) [maritime climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_climate) (*Cfb*) which, despite its northerly latitude, is milder than places which lie at similar latitudes such as [Moscow](19004.md) and [Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador).[^cite_note-temperature-126] The city's proximity to the sea mitigates any large variations in temperature or extremes of climate. Winter daytime temperatures rarely fall below freezing while summer temperatures are moderate, rarely exceeding 22 °C (72 °F).[^cite_note-temperature-126] The highest temperature recorded in the city was 31.6 °C (88.9 °F) on 25 July 2019[^cite_note-temperature-126] at Gogarbank, beating the previous record of 31 °C (88 °F) on 4 August 1975 at Edinburgh Airport.[^cite_note-127] The lowest temperature recorded in recent years was −14.6 °C (5.7 °F) during December 2010 at Gogarbank.[^cite_note-128]

Given Edinburgh's position between the coast and hills, it is renowned as "the windy city", with the prevailing wind direction coming from the south-west, which is often associated with warm, unstable air from the [North Atlantic Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Current) that can give rise to rainfall – although considerably less than cities to the west, such as Glasgow.[^cite_note-temperature-126] Rainfall is distributed fairly evenly throughout the year.[^cite_note-temperature-126] Winds from an easterly direction are usually drier but considerably colder, and may be accompanied by [haar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haar_\(fog\)), a persistent coastal fog. Vigorous Atlantic depressions, known as [European windstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_windstorm), can affect the city between October and April.[^cite_note-temperature-126]

Located slightly north of the city centre, the weather station at the [Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Botanic_Garden_Edinburgh) (RBGE) has been an official weather station for the [Met Office](319671.md) since 1956. The Met Office operates its own weather station at Gogarbank on the city's western outskirts, near [Edinburgh Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Airport).[^cite_note-129] This slightly inland station has a slightly wider temperature span between seasons, is cloudier and somewhat wetter, but differences are minor.

Temperature and rainfall records have been kept at the Royal Observatory since 1764.[^cite_note-130]

## Governance

Main articles: [Politics of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Edinburgh) and [City of Edinburgh Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Edinburgh_Council)

### Governance



[Edinburgh City Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_City_Chambers) is the headquarters of the [City of Edinburgh Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Edinburgh_Council).

Following local government reorganisation in 1996, the City of Edinburgh Council constitutes one of the [32 council areas of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_areas_of_Scotland).[^cite_note-act-137] Like all other [local authorities of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_authorities_of_Scotland), the council has powers over most matters of local administration such as housing, planning, [local transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Edinburgh), parks, economic development and regeneration.[^cite_note-act1-138] The council comprises 63 elected [councillors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Councillor), returned from 17 [multi-member electoral wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wards_and_electoral_divisions_of_the_United_Kingdom) in the city.[^cite_note-councillors-139] Following the [2007 City of Edinburgh Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_City_of_Edinburgh_Council_election) the incumbent [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Labour) lost majority control of the council following 23 years to a [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Liberal_Democrats)/[SNP](28887.md) coalition.[^cite_note-140]

Following the 2017 election, the SNP and Labour formed a coalition administration, which lasted until the next election in 2022. The [2022 City of Edinburgh Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_City_of_Edinburgh_Council_election) resulted in the most politically balanced council in the UK, with 19 SNP, 13 Labour, 12 Liberal Democrat, 10 Green, and 9 Conservative councillors. A minority Labour administration was formed, being voted in by Scottish Conservative and Scottish Liberal Democrat councillors. The SNP and Greens presented a coalition agreement, but could not command a majority in the council. This caused controversy amongst the Scottish Labour Party group for forming an administration supported by Conservatives, and led to the suspension of two Labour councillors on the council for abstaining on the vote to approve the new administration.[^cite_note-141] The city's [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Edinburgh) was registered by the [Lord Lyon King of Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Lyon_King_of_Arms) in 1732.[^cite_note-142]

### Politics

Scottish Parliament

The [Scottish Parliament](61188.md) is located in the [Holyrood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holyrood_Park) area of the city.

St Andrew's House

The HQ of the [Scottish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government) is located at [St Andrew's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrew's_House) in the city.

Edinburgh, like all of Scotland, is represented in the [Scottish Parliament](61188.md), situated in the [Holyrood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holyrood,_Edinburgh) area of the city. For electoral purposes, the city is divided into six constituencies, which, along with 3 seats outside of the city, form part of the [Lothian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothian_\(Scottish_Parliament_electoral_region\)).[^cite_note-elections-143] Each constituency elects one [Member of the Scottish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Scottish_Parliament) (MSP) by the [first past the post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_past_the_post) system of election, and the region elects seven [additional MSPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional-member_system) to produce a result based on a form of proportional representation.[^cite_note-elections-143]

As of September 2026, the Scottish National Party have three MSPs: [Ben Macpherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Macpherson_\(politician\)) for [Edinburgh Northern and Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Northern_and_Leith), [Simita Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simita_Kumar) for [Edinburgh South Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_South_Western) and [Kate Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Campbell_\(politician\)) for [Edinburgh Eastern, Musselburgh and Tranent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Eastern,_Musselburgh_and_Tranent) constituencies. [Alex Cole-Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Cole-Hamilton), the leader of the [Scottish Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Liberal_Democrats) represents [Edinburgh North Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_North_Western) alongside his fellow party member [Sanne Dijkstra-Downie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanne_Dijkstra-Downie) for [Edinburgh Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Northern), while [Daniel Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Johnson_\(Scottish_politician\)) of the [Scottish Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Labour) represents [Edinburgh Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Southern) constituency and [Lorna Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorna_Slater) of the [Scottish Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Greens). In addition, the city is also represented by seven regional MSPs representing the Lothian electoral region: The Scottish Greens have three regional list MSPs: [Kate Nevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Nevens), [Q Manivannan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_Manivannan) and [Kayleigh Kinross-O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayleigh_Kinross-O'Neill), while Labour have two: [Irshad Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irshad_Ahmed_\(politician\)) and [Katherine Sangster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Sangster). [Angela Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Ross) of [Reform UK](59757202.md) and [Miles Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Briggs) of the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) make up the 7 regional list MSPs.

Edinburgh is also represented in the [House of Commons](13828.md) by five [Members of Parliament](3776360.md). The city is divided into [Edinburgh North and Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_North_and_Leith_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Edinburgh East and Musselburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_East_and_Musselburgh_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Edinburgh South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_South_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Edinburgh South West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_South_West), and [Edinburgh West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_West_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), each constituency electing one member by the first past the post system. Since the [2024 General election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election), Edinburgh is represented by four [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Labour) MPs ([Tracy Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Gilbert) in Edinburgh North and Leith, [Chris Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Murray_\(politician\)) in Edinburgh East and Musselburgh, [Ian Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Murray_\(Scottish_politician\)) in Edinburgh South, and [Scott Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Arthur) in Edinburgh South West), and one [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Liberal_Democrats) MP in Edinburgh West ([Christine Jardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Jardine)).

## Demography

### Current



Population density map

The most recent official population estimates (2020) are 506,520 for the locality (includes [Currie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currie,_Edinburgh)),[^cite_note-NRS_Locality_Estimate-7] and 530,990 for the Edinburgh settlement (includes [Musselburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musselburgh)).[^cite_note-NRS_Locality_Estimate-7]

Edinburgh has a high proportion of young adults, with 19.5% of the population in their 20s (exceeded only by Aberdeen) and 15.2% in their 30s which is the highest in Scotland. The proportion of Edinburgh's population born in the UK fell from 92% to 84% between 2001 and 2011, while the proportion of White Scottish-born fell from 78% to 70%. Of those Edinburgh residents born in the UK, 335,000 or 83% were born in Scotland, with 58,000 or 14% being born in England.[^cite_note-edinburgh.gov.uk-144]

Some 16,000 people or 3.2% of the city's population are of [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Polish) descent. 77,800 people or 15.1% of Edinburgh's population class themselves as Non-White, which is an increase from 8.2% in 2011 and 4% in 2001. Of the Non-White population, the largest group by far is [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian-Scots), totalling about 44 thousand people. Within the Asian population, people of [Chinese](1255834.md) descent are now the largest sub-group, with 15,076 people, amounting to about 2.9% of the city's total population. The city's population of [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian) descent amounts to 12,414 (2.4% of the total population), while there are some 7,454 of [Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pakistanis) descent (1.5% of the total population). Although they account for only 2,685 people or 0.5% of the city's population, Edinburgh has the highest number and proportion of people of [Bangladeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Bangladeshi) descent in Scotland. Close to 12,000 people were born in African countries predominantly in [Nigeria](21383.md) and [South Africa](17416221.md) (2.3% of the total population) and over 13,000 in the Americas. With the notable exception of Inner London, Edinburgh has a higher number of people born in the United States (over 6,500) than any other city in the UK.[^cite_note-2022census_Scot-149]

A census by the Edinburgh presbytery in 1592 recorded a population of 8,003 adults spread equally north and south of the High Street which runs along the spine of the ridge sloping down from the Castle.[^cite_note-156] In the 18th and 19th centuries, the population expanded rapidly, rising from 49,000 in 1751 to 136,000 in 1831, primarily due to migration from rural areas.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 9 </sup> As the population grew, problems of overcrowding in the Old Town, particularly in the cramped [tenements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenement) that lined the present-day Royal Mile and the [Cowgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowgate), were exacerbated.[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 9 </sup> Poor sanitary arrangements resulted in a high incidence of disease,[^cite_note-Edwards2005-103]<sup>: 9 </sup> with outbreaks of [cholera](7591.md) occurring in 1832, 1848, and 1866.[^cite_note-157]

The construction of the New Town from 1767 onwards witnessed the migration of the professional and business classes from the difficult living conditions in the Old Town to the lower-density, higher quality surroundings taking shape on land to the north. [^cite_note-etmoac46-158] Expansion southwards from the Old Town saw more tenements being built in the 19th century, giving rise to [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) suburbs such as [Dalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalry,_Edinburgh), Newington, Marchmont and Bruntsfield.[^cite_note-etmoac46-158]

Early 20th-century population growth coincided with lower-density suburban development. As the city expanded to the south and west, detached and semi-detached villas with large gardens replaced tenements as the predominant building style. Nonetheless, the 2001 census revealed that over 55% of Edinburgh's population were still living in tenements or blocks of flats, a figure in line with other Scottish cities, but much higher than other British cities, and even central London.[^cite_note-dwellings-159]

From the early to mid 20th century, the growth in population, together with slum clearance in the Old Town and other areas, such as [Dumbiedykes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumbiedykes), [Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith), and [Fountainbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountainbridge), led to the creation of new estates such as [Stenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenhouse,_Edinburgh) and [Saughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saughton), [Craigmillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigmillar) and [Niddrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niddrie,_Edinburgh), [Pilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilton,_Edinburgh) and [Muirhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muirhouse), [Piershill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piershill), and [Sighthill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sighthill,_Edinburgh).[^cite_note-160]

### Religion



The High Kirk of Edinburgh, also known as [St Giles' Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Giles'_Cathedral)

As per the 2022 census, 13% of the population belonged to the Church of Scotland and 10% to the Catholic Church.[^cite_note-161]

[Saint Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Giles) is historically the patron saint of Edinburgh.[^cite_note-162] [St Cuthbert's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Cuthbert's_Church,_Edinburgh), situated at the west end of Princes Street Gardens in the shadow of Edinburgh Castle and St Giles' can lay claim to being the oldest Christian sites in the city,[^cite_note-163] though the present St Cuthbert's, designed by [Hippolyte Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippolyte_Blanc), was dedicated in 1894.[^cite_note-164]

Other Church of Scotland churches include [Greyfriars Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyfriars_Kirk), the [Canongate Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canongate_Kirk), [The New Town Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh:_New_Town_Church) and the [Barclay Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Barclay_Church). The [Church of Scotland Offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland_Offices) are in Edinburgh,[^cite_note-165] as is the [Assembly Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_Hall_of_the_Church_of_Scotland) where the annual [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_of_the_Church_of_Scotland) is held.[^cite_note-166]

The [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of St Andrews and Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_St_Andrews_and_Edinburgh) has 27 parishes across the city.[^cite_note-167] The [archbishop of St Andrews and Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_St_Andrews_and_Edinburgh) has his official residence in [Greenhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhill,_Edinburgh),[^cite_note-168] the diocesan offices are in nearby [Marchmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchmont),[^cite_note-169] and its cathedral is [St Mary's Cathedral, Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Cathedral,_Edinburgh_\(Catholic\)). The [Diocese of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Edinburgh) of the [Scottish Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Episcopal_Church) has over 50 churches, half of them in the city.[^cite_note-170] Its centre is the late 19th-century [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture) style [St Mary's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Cathedral,_Edinburgh_\(Episcopal\)) in the West End's Palmerston Place.[^cite_note-171] Orthodox Christianity is represented by Pan, [Romanian](384458.md) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) Orthodox churches, including [St Andrew's Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrew's_Orthodox_Church,_Edinburgh), part of the [Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Thyateira and Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Archdiocese_of_Thyateira_and_Great_Britain).[^cite_note-172] There are several independent churches in the city, both [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant), including [Charlotte Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Chapel_\(Edinburgh\)), [Carrubbers Christian Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrubbers_Christian_Centre), [Bellevue Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue_Chapel) and [Sacred Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Heart,_Edinburgh).[^cite_note-173] There are also churches belonging to [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker_Meeting_House,_Edinburgh), [Christadelphians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christadelphians),[^cite_note-174] [Seventh-day Adventists](28632.md), [Church of Christ, Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ,_Scientist), [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (LDS Church) and [Elim Pentecostal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elim_Pentecostal_Church).

Muslims have several places of worship across the city. Edinburgh has over 39 [graveyards and cemeteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_graveyards_and_cemeteries_in_Edinburgh), many of which are listed and of historical character, including several former church burial grounds.[^cite_note-Golledge-189] Examples include [Old Calton Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Calton_Burial_Ground), [Greyfriars Kirkyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyfriars_Kirkyard) and [Dean Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Cemetery).[^cite_note-OldCarltonBurial-190][^cite_note-Greyfriars-191][^cite_note-DeanCemetery-192]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Edinburgh)

Further information: [Economy of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Scotland)



The [Bank of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Scotland)'s head office in central Edinburgh



Headquarters of [Rockstar North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_North) situated in Edinburgh

Edinburgh has the second-strongest economy of any city in the United Kingdom behind [London](17867.md) and the highest percentage of professionals in the UK with 43% of the population holding a degree-level or professional qualification.[^cite_note-Work_In_Edinburgh_-_Jobs,_Employment,_Careers_And_Recruitment_Information-193] According to the Centre for International Competitiveness, it is the second most competitive major city in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-194] In 2023, its [gross domestic product per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product_per_capita) of £69,809 surpassed London's for the first time.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] It also had the highest [gross value added](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_value_added) per capita in Scotland, at £65,500 in 2023.[^cite_note-197] It was named European Best Large City of the Future for Foreign Direct Investment and Best Large City for Foreign Direct Investment Strategy in the *[Financial Times](136566.md)* magazine in 2012.[^cite_note-198]

As the centre of [Scotland's government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government) and [legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law), the public sector plays a central role in Edinburgh's economy. Many departments of the Scottish Government are in the city, including the headquarters of the government at [St Andrew's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrew's_House), the official residence of the [First Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland) at [Bute House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bute_House) and Scottish Government offices at [Victoria Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Quay,_Edinburgh). Other major sectors across the city include administrative and support services, the education sector, public administration and defence, the health and social care sector, scientific and technical services, and construction and manufacturing.[^cite_note-199] When the £1.3bn Edinburgh & South East Scotland City Region Deal[^cite_note-200] was signed in 2018, the region's Gross Value Added (GVA) contribution to the Scottish economy was cited as £33bn, or 33% of the country's output. The City Region Deal funds a range of "Data Driven Innovation" hubs which are using data to innovate in the region, recognising the region's strengths in technology and data science, the growing importance of the data economy, and the need to tackle the digital skills gap, as a route to social and economic prosperity.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]

Tourism is also an important element in the city's economy. As a World Heritage Site, tourists visit historical sites such as Edinburgh Castle, the Palace of Holyroodhouse, and the Old and New Towns. Their numbers are augmented in August each year during the [Edinburgh Festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Festival), which attract over 4 million visitors, and generate over £400M for the local economy.[^cite_note-\:2-204] In May 2024, unemployment in Edinburgh was at 3.5%, in line with the Scottish average of 3.7%.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206] In 2022 Edinburgh was the second most visited city in the United Kingdom, behind London, by overseas visitors.[^cite_note-207]

In July 2026 Edinburgh introduced a visitor levy, popularly known as a tourist tax, the first city in Scotland to do so. The tax is charged at a rate of 5% on overnight stays, in hotels, self-catering accommodation, short-term holiday lets such as [Airbnb](32058290.md), bed and breakfasts, guest houses and hostels. It is only payable for the first five nights of the stay.[^cite_note-208]

## Culture

### Festivals and celebrations

#### Edinburgh festivals

Main article: [List of Edinburgh festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Edinburgh_festivals)



[Edinburgh Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle) illuminated during the [Edinburgh Military Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Military_Tattoo)

The city hosts a series of festivals that run between the end of July and early September each year. The best known of these events are the [Edinburgh Festival Fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Fringe), the [Edinburgh International Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Festival), the [Edinburgh Military Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Military_Tattoo), the [Edinburgh Art Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Art_Festival), the [Edinburgh International Book Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Book_Festival) and [Edinburgh International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-209]

The longest established of these festivals is the Edinburgh International Festival (EIF) and Edinburgh International Film Festival (EIFF). Both festivals were first held in 1947.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] EIF consists mainly of a programme of high-profile theatre productions and classical music performances, featuring international directors, conductors, theatre companies and orchestras.[^cite_note-212] Edinburgh International Film Festival is the world's oldest continually running [film festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_festival). EIFF is globally renowned for world, international and UK premieres, high-profile film talent, significant filmmaking competitions and prominent industry guests.[^cite_note-213]

The Edinburgh Fringe, which began as a programme of marginal acts alongside the "official" Festival, has become the world's largest performing arts festival. In 2023, over 3700 different shows were staged in 300 venues across the city.[^cite_note-\:2-204][^cite_note-214] Comedy has become one of the mainstays of the Fringe, with many comedians getting their first 'break' there, often by being chosen to receive the [Edinburgh Comedy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Comedy_Awards).[^cite_note-215] The Edinburgh Military Tattoo occupies the Castle Esplanade every night for three weeks each August, with massed [pipe bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_band) and [military bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_band) drawn from around the world. Performances end with a short fireworks display.

As well as the summer festivals, [many other festivals are held during the rest of the year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Edinburgh_festivals), including [Edinburgh International Science Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Science_Festival).[^cite_note-216] The summer of 2020 was the first time in its 70-year history that the Edinburgh festival was not run, being cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-217] This affected many of the tourist-focused businesses in Edinburgh which depend on the various festivals over summer to return an annual profit.[^cite_note-218]

#### Edinburgh's Hogmanay

Main article: [Edinburgh's Hogmanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh's_Hogmanay)



A Viking [longship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longship) being burnt during Edinburgh's annual [Hogmanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogmanay) celebrations

The annual Edinburgh [Hogmanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogmanay) celebration was originally an informal street party focused on the [Tron Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tron_Kirk) in the Old Town's High Street. Since 1993, it has been officially organised with the focus moved to Princes Street. In 1996, over 300,000 people attended, leading to ticketing of the main street party in later years up to a limit of 100,000 tickets.[^cite_note-Hogmanay-219] Hogmanay now covers four days of processions, concerts and fireworks, with the street party beginning on Hogmanay. Alternative tickets are available for entrance into the Princes Street Gardens concert and [Cèilidh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A8ilidh), where well-known artists perform and ticket holders can participate in traditional Scottish cèilidh dancing. The event attracts thousands of people from all over the world.[^cite_note-Hogmanay-219]

#### Beltane and other festivals

On the night of 30 April the [Beltane Fire Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltane_Fire_Festival) takes place on Calton Hill, involving a procession followed by scenes inspired by [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism) old spring fertility celebrations.[^cite_note-220] At the beginning of October each year the [Dussehra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navratri) Hindu Festival is also held on Calton Hill.[^cite_note-221]

### Music, theatre and film



The [Usher Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usher_Hall)

Outside the festival season, Edinburgh supports several theatres and production companies. The [Royal Lyceum Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Lyceum_Theatre) has its own company, while the [King's Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Theatre,_Edinburgh), [Edinburgh Festival Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Festival_Theatre) and [Edinburgh Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Playhouse) stage large touring shows. The [Traverse Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traverse_Theatre) presents a more contemporary repertoire. [Amateur theatre companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_amateur_theatre) productions are staged at the [Bedlam Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedlam_Theatre), [Church Hill Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Hill_Theatre) and [King's Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Theatre,_Edinburgh) among others.[^cite_note-222] The [Usher Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usher_Hall) is Edinburgh's premier venue for classical music, as well as occasional popular music concerts.[^cite_note-223] It was the venue for the [Eurovision Song Contest 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1972). Other halls staging music and theatre include [The Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hub_\(Edinburgh\)), the [Assembly Rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_Rooms_\(Edinburgh\)) and the [Queen's Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Hall,_Edinburgh). The [Scottish Chamber Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Chamber_Orchestra) is based in Edinburgh.[^cite_note-224]



[Traverse Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traverse_Theatre)

Edinburgh has two repertory cinemas, [The Cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cameo,_Edinburgh) and the [Edinburgh Filmhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Filmhouse), as well as the independent [Dominion Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Cinema) and a range of [multiplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplex_\(cinema\)).[^cite_note-225] Large concerts are occasionally staged at [Murrayfield Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield_Stadium) and [Meadowbank Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowbank_Stadium), while mid-sized events take place at smaller venues such as [O2 Academy Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Corn_Exchange). In 2010, [PRS for Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRS_for_Music) listed Edinburgh among the UK's top ten 'most musical' cities.[^cite_note-226] Several city pubs are well known for their live performances of [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music).[^cite_note-227]

Nightclub venues within the city host [electronic dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music) events.[^cite_note-228]

### Museums, libraries, and galleries



[National Gallery of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_National_Gallery)



[National Museum of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Scotland)

Edinburgh has many museums and libraries. These include the [National Museum of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Scotland), the [National Library of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Scotland), [National War Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_War_Museum), the [Museum of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Edinburgh), [Surgeons' Hall Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgeons'_Hall), the [Writers' Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers'_Museum), the [Museum of Childhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Childhood_\(Edinburgh\)) and [Dynamic Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_Earth_\(Edinburgh\)). The [Museum on The Mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_on_the_Mound) has exhibits on money and banking.[^cite_note-229] [Edinburgh Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Zoo), covering 82 acres (33 ha) on Corstorphine Hill, is the second most visited paid tourist attraction in Scotland,[^cite_note-begin-230] and was previously home to two [giant pandas](12713.md), Tian Tian and Yang Guang, on loan from the People's Republic of China. Edinburgh is also home to [The Royal Yacht Britannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMY_Britannia), decommissioned in 1997 and now a five-star visitor attraction and evening events venue permanently berthed at [Ocean Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Terminal,_Edinburgh).

Edinburgh contains Scotland's three [National Galleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Galleries_Scotland) of Art as well as smaller art galleries.[^cite_note-231] The national collection is housed in the [Scottish National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_National_Gallery), located on The Mound, comprising the linked National Gallery of Scotland building and the [Royal Scottish Academy building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Scottish_Academy_building). Contemporary collections are shown in the [Scottish National Gallery of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_National_Gallery_of_Modern_Art), which occupies a split site at Belford. The [Scottish National Portrait Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_National_Portrait_Gallery) on Queen Street focuses on portraits and photography. The council-owned [City Art Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Art_Centre) in Market Street mounts regular art exhibitions. Across the road, [The Fruitmarket Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruitmarket_Gallery) offers world-class exhibitions of contemporary art, featuring work by British and international artists with both emerging and established international reputations.[^cite_note-232]

The city hosts several of Scotland's galleries and organisations dedicated to contemporary visual art. Significant strands of this infrastructure include [Creative Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Scotland), [Edinburgh College of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_College_of_Art), [Talbot Rice Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talbot_Rice_Gallery) (University of Edinburgh), [Collective Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Gallery) (based at the [City Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Observatory)) and the [Edinburgh Annuale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Annuale). Many small private shops/galleries provide space to showcase works from local artists.[^cite_note-233]

### Shopping

The locale around [Princes Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_Street) is the main shopping area in the city centre, with souvenir shops, chain stores such as [Boots the Chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boots_the_Chemist), [Edinburgh Woollen Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Woollen_Mill), and [H\&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H\&M).[^cite_note-shopping-234] [George Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Street,_Edinburgh), north of Princes Street, has several upmarket shops and independent stores.[^cite_note-shopping-234] At the east end of Princes Street, the redeveloped [St James Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James_Quarter) opened its doors in June 2021,[^cite_note-235] while next to the [Balmoral Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmoral_Hotel) and Waverley Station is [Waverley Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waverley_Market). [Multrees Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multrees_Walk) is a pedestrian shopping district, dominated by the presence of [Harvey Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Nichols), and other names including [Louis Vuitton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Vuitton), [Mulberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulberry_\(company\)) and [Michael Kors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kors).[^cite_note-shopping-234]

Edinburgh also has substantial retail parks outside the city centre. These include [The Gyle Shopping Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gyle_Shopping_Centre) and Hermiston Gait in the west of the city, [Cameron Toll Shopping Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Toll_Shopping_Centre), Straiton Retail Park (actually just outside the city, in Midlothian) and [Fort Kinnaird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Kinnaird) in the south and east, and [Ocean Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Terminal,_Edinburgh) in the north on the [Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith) waterfront.[^cite_note-236]

## Media

The main local newspaper is the *[Edinburgh Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Evening_News)*. It is owned and published alongside its sister titles *[The Scotsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scotsman)* and *[Scotland on Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_on_Sunday)* by [JPIMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPIMedia).[^cite_note-237] Student newspapers include, *[The Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_\(student_newspaper\))* Scotland wide Universities, and *[The Student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Student_\(newspaper\))* [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) which was founded in 1887. Community newspapers include *The Spurtle* from Broughton, *Spokes Bulletin*, and *The Edinburgh Reporter*.

The city has many commercial radio stations including [Forth 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forth_1), a station which broadcasts mainstream chart music, Greatest Hits Edinburgh on DAB which plays classic hits and Edge Radio.[^cite_note-238] [Capital Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Scotland) and [Heart Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_Scotland) also have transmitters covering Edinburgh. Along with the UK national radio stations, [BBC Radio Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Scotland) and the Gaelic language service [BBC Radio nan Gàidheal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_nan_G%C3%A0idheal) are also broadcast. DAB digital radio is broadcast over two local multiplexes. [BFBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFBS) Radio broadcasts from studios on the base at Dreghorn Barracks across the city on 98.5FM as part of its UK Bases network. Small-scale DAB started in October 2022 with community stations on board.

Television, along with most radio services, is broadcast to the city from the [Craigkelly transmitting station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigkelly_transmitting_station) situated in Fife on the opposite side of the Firth of Forth[^cite_note-239] and the [Black Hill transmitting station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hill_transmitting_station) in [North Lanarkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Lanarkshire) to the west. There are no television stations based in the city. Edinburgh Television existed in the late 1990s to early 2003[^cite_note-240] and [STV Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STV_Edinburgh) existed from 2015 to 2018.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Edinburgh)

### Air



[Edinburgh Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Airport) is the busiest airport in Scotland and [sixth busiest in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_in_the_United_Kingdom).

[Edinburgh Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Airport) is Scotland's busiest airport and the principal international gateway to the capital, handling over 14.7 million passengers; it was also the [sixth-busiest airport in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busiest_airports_in_the_United_Kingdom_by_total_passenger_traffic) by total passengers in 2019.[^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244] In anticipation of rising passenger numbers, the former operator of the airport [BAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAA_Limited) outlined a draft masterplan in 2011 to provide for the expansion of the airfield and the terminal building. In June 2012, [Global Infrastructure Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Infrastructure_Partners) purchased the airport for £807 million.[^cite_note-245] The possibility of building a second runway to cope with an increased number of aircraft movements has also been mooted.[^cite_note-246]

### Buses



A Lothian Bus on [North Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bridge,_Edinburgh)

Travel in Edinburgh is undertaken predominantly by bus. [Lothian Buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothian_Buses), the successor company to Edinburgh Corporation Transport Department, operates the majority of [city bus services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_bus_service) within the city and to surrounding towns and villages, with most routes running via Princes Street. Services further afield operate from the [Edinburgh bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_bus_station) off [St Andrew Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrew_Square,_Edinburgh) and Waterloo Place and are operated by [Stagecoach South Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_South_Scotland), [Scottish Citylink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Citylink), [National Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Express) and [Borders Buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_Buses).

Lothian Buses and [McGill's Scotland East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill's_Scotland_East) operate the city's branded public [tour buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_bus_service). The [night bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_bus) service and airport buses are mainly operated by Lothian Buses.[^cite_note-247] In 2019, Lothian Buses recorded 124.2 million passenger journeys.[^cite_note-248]

To tackle [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion), Edinburgh is now served by six [park & ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_&_ride) sites on the periphery of the city at Sheriffhall (in Midlothian), [Ingliston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingliston), [Riccarton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccarton,_Edinburgh), [Inverkeithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverkeithing) (in Fife), [Newcraighall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcraighall) and [Straiton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straiton,_Loanhead) (in Midlothian). A [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_congestion_charge#Referendum) of Edinburgh residents in February 2005 rejected a proposal to introduce [congestion charging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_congestion_charge) in the city. [^cite_note-249]

### Railway



A train preparing to depart from Edinburgh Waverley station

[Edinburgh Waverley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Waverley_railway_station) is the second-busiest railway station in Scotland, with only [Glasgow Central](314616.md) handling more passengers. On the evidence of passenger entries and exits between April 2015 and March 2016, Edinburgh Waverley is the fifth-busiest station in the United Kingdom outside London; it is also the UK's second biggest station in terms of the number of platforms and area size.[^cite_note-250] Waverley is the terminus for most trains arriving from [London King's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_King's_Cross_railway_station) and the departure point for many [rail services within Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Scotland) operated by [ScotRail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ScotRail).

To the west of the city centre lies [Haymarket station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_railway_station), which is an important commuter stop. Opened in 2003, [Edinburgh Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Park_railway_station) station serves the Gyle business park in the west of the city and the nearby [Gogarburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gogarburn) headquarters of the Royal Bank of Scotland. The [Edinburgh Crossrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Crossrail) route connects Edinburgh Park with Haymarket, Edinburgh Waverley and the suburban stations of [Brunstane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunstane_railway_station) and [Newcraighall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcraighall_railway_station) in the east of the city.[^cite_note-251] There are also commuter lines to [Edinburgh Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Gateway), [South Gyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Gyle) and Dalmeny, the latter serving [South Queensferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Queensferry) by the Forth Bridges, and to [Wester Hailes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wester_Hailes) and [Curriehill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriehill) in the south-west of the city.

### Trams



Edinburgh Tram heading for the city centre

[Edinburgh Trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Trams) became operational on 31 May 2014. The city had been without a tram system since [Edinburgh Corporation Tramways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Corporation_Tramways) ceased on 16 November 1956.[^cite_note-252] Following parliamentary approval in 2007, construction began in early 2008. The first stage of the project was expected to be completed by July 2011[^cite_note-operational-253] but, following delays caused by extra utility work and a long-running contractual dispute between the council and the main contractor, [Bilfinger SE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilfinger_SE), the project was rescheduled.[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256] The line opened in 2014 but had been cut short to 8.7 mi (14.0 km) in length, running from [Edinburgh Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Airport_tram_stop) to [York Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Place,_Edinburgh) in the east end of the city.

The line was later extended north onto [Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith) and [Newhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhaven,_Edinburgh), opening a further eight stops to passengers in June 2023. The York Place stop was replaced by a new island stop at [Picardy Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenside,_Edinburgh). The original plan would have seen a second line run from Haymarket through [Ravelston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravelston) and [Craigleith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigleith,_Edinburgh) to [Granton Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granton,_Edinburgh) on the [Waterfront Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfront_Edinburgh). This was shelved in 2011 but in 2025 is once again [under consideration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposals_for_new_tram_lines_in_Edinburgh), as is another line potentially linking the south of the city and the [Bioquarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_BioQuarter).[^cite_note-257] There were also long-term plans for lines running west from the airport to [Ratho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratho) and [Newbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbridge,_Edinburgh) and another connecting [Granton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granton,_Edinburgh) to Newhaven via Lower Granton Road. In January 2026, a map showing a [potential future network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposals_for_new_tram_lines_in_Edinburgh#Other_proposals:_Edinburgh_South_Suburban_Railway) using the [Edinburgh South Suburban Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_South_Suburban_Railway) was published by the council.[^cite_note-\:22-258] Lothian Buses and Edinburgh Trams are both owned and operated by [Transport for Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_Edinburgh).

Despite its modern transport links, in January 2021, Edinburgh was named the most congested city in the UK for the fourth year running, though it has since fallen to 7th place in 2022.[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260]

## Education

### Schools

There are 18 nursery, 94 primary and 23 secondary [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Edinburgh) administered by the City of Edinburgh Council.[^cite_note-primaryschools-261] Edinburgh is home to [The Royal High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_High_School,_Edinburgh), one of the [oldest schools in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_oldest_schools_in_the_United_Kingdom) and [the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_oldest_schools_in_the_world). The city also has several [independent, fee-paying schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_school_\(UK\)#Independent_schools_in_Scotland) including [Edinburgh Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Academy), [Fettes College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fettes_College), [George Heriot's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot's_School), [George Watson's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watson's_College), [Merchiston Castle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchiston_Castle_School), [Stewart's Melville College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart's_Melville_College) and [The Mary Erskine School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mary_Erskine_School). In 2009, the proportion of pupils attending independent schools was 24.2%, far above the Scottish national average of just over 7% and higher than in any other region of Scotland.[^cite_note-SCISstats-262] In August 2013, the City of Edinburgh Council opened the city's first stand-alone Gaelic primary school, [Bun-sgoil Taobh na Pàirce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bun-sgoil_Taobh_na_P%C3%A0irce).[^cite_note-263]

### College and university



[Old College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_College,_Edinburgh) of the University of Edinburgh

There are three universities in Edinburgh: the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), [Heriot-Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University), and [Edinburgh Napier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Napier_University). Established by royal charter in 1583, the University of Edinburgh is one of Scotland's [ancient universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_universities_of_Scotland) and is the fourth oldest in the country after [St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) and [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen).[^cite_note-Universityhistory-264] Originally centred on [Old College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_College,_University_of_Edinburgh) the university expanded to premises on The Mound, the Royal Mile and George Square.[^cite_note-Universityhistory-264] Today, the [King's Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Buildings) in the south of the city contain most of the schools within the College of Science and Engineering. In 2002, the [medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh_Medical_School) moved to purpose-built accommodation adjacent to the new [Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Infirmary_of_Edinburgh) at [Little France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_France). The university was placed 16th in the QS World University Rankings for 2022.[^cite_note-University_Rankings-265]

Heriot-Watt University is based at the [Riccarton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccarton,_Edinburgh) campus in the west of Edinburgh. Originally established in 1821 as the world's first [mechanics' institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics'_Institutes), it was granted university status by royal charter in 1966. It has other campuses in the Scottish Borders, Orkney, the United Arab Emirates and Putrajaya in Malaysia. It takes the name *Heriot-Watt* from Scottish inventor [James Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watt) and Scottish philanthropist and goldsmith [George Heriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot). Heriot-Watt University has been named International University of the Year by *The Times and Sunday Times Good University Guide 2018*. In the latest Research Excellence Framework, it was ranked overall in the Top 25% of UK universities and 1st in Scotland for research impact.



The Craiglockhart campus, [Edinburgh Napier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Napier_University)

Edinburgh Napier University was originally founded as Napier College, which was renamed Napier Polytechnic in 1986 and gained university status in 1992.[^cite_note-Napierhistory-266] Edinburgh Napier University has campuses in the south and west of the city, including the former [Merchiston Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchiston_Tower) and [Craiglockhart Hydropathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiglockhart_Hydropathic).[^cite_note-Napierhistory-266] It is home to the [Screen Academy Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Academy_Scotland). [Queen Margaret University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Margaret_University) was located in Edinburgh before it moved outside the city boundary to a new campus in the county of [East Lothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Lothian) on the outskirts of [Musselburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musselburgh) in 2008.[^cite_note-267] Until 2012, further education colleges in the city included [Jewel and Esk College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewel_and_Esk_College) (incorporating [Leith Nautical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith_Nautical_College) founded in 1903), [Telford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh's_Telford_College), opened in 1968, and [Stevenson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevenson_College_\(Edinburgh\)), opened in 1970. These have now been amalgamated to form [Edinburgh College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_College). [Scotland's Rural College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland's_Rural_College) also has a campus in South Edinburgh. Other institutions include the [Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Surgeons_of_Edinburgh) and the [Royal College of Physicians of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians_of_Edinburgh), which were established by royal charter in 1506 and 1681, respectively. The [Trustees' Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trustees'_Academy) of Edinburgh, founded in 1760, became the [Edinburgh College of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_College_of_Art) in 1907.[^cite_note-268]

## Healthcare



The Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh is the main public hospital for the city.

See also: [List of hospitals in Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Scotland#City_of_Edinburgh)

The main [NHS Lothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS_Lothian) hospitals serving the Edinburgh area are the [Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Infirmary_of_Edinburgh), which includes the [University of Edinburgh Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh_Medical_School), and the [Western General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_General_Hospital),[^cite_note-hospitals-269] which has a large cancer treatment centre and nurse-led Minor Injuries Clinic.[^cite_note-270] The [Royal Edinburgh Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Edinburgh_Hospital) in Morningside specialises in mental health. The [Royal Hospital for Children and Young People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Hospital_for_Children_and_Young_People), colloquially referred to as *the Sick Kids*, is a specialist [paediatrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paediatrics) hospital.

There are two private hospitals: Murrayfield Hospital in the west of the city and Shawfair Hospital in the south; both are owned by [Spire Healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spire_Healthcare).[^cite_note-hospitals-269]

## Sport

### Football



[Tynecastle Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tynecastle_Park)

Edinburgh has four [football](10568.md) clubs that play in the [Scottish Professional Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Professional_Football_League) (SPFL): [Heart of Midlothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_Midlothian_F.C.), founded in 1874, [Hibernian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibernian_F.C.), founded in 1875, [Edinburgh City F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_City_F.C.), founded in 1966 and [Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartans_F.C.), founded in 1951. Heart of Midlothian and Hibernian are known locally as "Hearts" and "Hibs", respectively. Both play in the [Scottish Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Premiership).[^cite_note-271] They are the oldest city rivals in Scotland and the [Edinburgh derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_derby) is one of the oldest derby matches in world football. Both clubs have won the [Scottish league championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Scottish_football_champions) four times. Hearts have won the [Scottish Cup](883238.md) eight times and the [Scottish League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_League_Cup) four times. Hibs have won the Scottish Cup and the Scottish League Cup three times each. [Edinburgh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_City_F.C.) were promoted to [Scottish League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_League_Two) in the 2015–16 season, becoming the first club to win promotion to the SPFL via the pyramid system playoffs.

Edinburgh was also home to four other [former Scottish Football League clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_former_Scottish_Football_League_clubs): the original [Edinburgh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_City_F.C._\(1928\)) (founded in 1928), [Leith Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith_Athletic_F.C.), [Meadowbank Thistle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowbank_Thistle_F.C.) and [St Bernard's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bernard's_F.C.). Meadowbank Thistle played at [Meadowbank Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowbank_Stadium) until 1995, when the club moved to [Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingston,_West_Lothian) and became [Livingston F.C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingston_F.C.). The [Scottish national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_men's_national_football_team) has very occasionally played at [Easter Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Road) and [Tynecastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tynecastle_Park), although its normal [home stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team_home_stadium) is [Hampden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden_Park) in Glasgow. St Bernard's [New Logie Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Logie_Green) was used to host the [1896 Scottish Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896_Scottish_Cup_Final), the only time the match has been played outside Glasgow.[^cite_note-UD-272] The city also plays host to [Lowland Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland_Football_League) clubs [Civil Service Strollers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Service_Strollers_F.C.), [Edinburgh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_University_A.F.C.) and [Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartans_F.C.), as well as [East of Scotland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Scotland_Football_League) clubs [Craigroyston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigroyston_F.C.), [Edinburgh United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_United_F.C.), [Heriot-Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University_F.C.), [Leith Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith_Athletic_F.C.), [Lothian Thistle Hutchison Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothian_Thistle_Hutchison_Vale_F.C.), and [Tynecastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tynecastle_F.C.).

In women's football, [Hearts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearts_Ladies_F.C.), [Hibs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibernian_L.F.C.) and [Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartans_W.F.C.) play in the [SWPL 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Women's_Premier_League).[^cite_note-273] [Hutchison Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothian_Thistle_Hutchison_Vale_F.C.#Women) and [Boroughmuir Thistle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughmuir_Thistle_F.C.) play in the [SWPL 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Women's_Premier_League).[^cite_note-274]

### Rugby



[Murrayfield Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield_Stadium)

The [Scotland national rugby union team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team) play at [Murrayfield Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield_Stadium), and the professional [Edinburgh Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Rugby) team play at the adjacent [Edinburgh Rugby Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Rugby_Stadium); both are owned by the [Scottish Rugby Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Rugby_Union) and are also used for other events, including music concerts. Murrayfield is the largest capacity stadium in Scotland, seating 67,144 spectators.[^cite_note-275] Edinburgh is also home to [Scottish Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Premiership_\(rugby\)) teams [Boroughmuir RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughmuir_RFC), [Currie RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currie_RFC), the [Edinburgh Academicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Academicals), [Heriot's Rugby Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot's_Rugby_Club) and [Watsonians RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watsonians_RFC).[^cite_note-276]

The Edinburgh Academicals ground at [Raeburn Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raeburn_Place) was the location of the world's first international rugby game on 27 March 1871, between Scotland and England.[^cite_note-277] [Rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) is represented by the [Edinburgh Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Eagles) who play in the [Rugby League Conference Scotland Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_League_Conference_Scotland_Division). Murrayfield Stadium has hosted the [Magic Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Weekend) where all [Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_League) matches are played in the stadium over one weekend.

### Other sports

The [Scottish cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_cricket_team), which represents Scotland internationally, plays its home matches at the [Grange cricket club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grange_Club).[^cite_note-278] The [Edinburgh Capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Capitals) are the latest of a succession of [ice hockey](14790.md) clubs in the Scottish capital. Previously, Edinburgh was represented by the [Murrayfield Racers (2018)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield_Racers_\(2018\)), the original [Murrayfield Racers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield_Racers) *(who folded in 1996)*, and the Edinburgh Racers. The club plays their home games at the [Murrayfield Ice Rink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield_Ice_Rink) and have competed in the eleven-team professional [Scottish National League (SNL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_National_League_\(ice_hockey\)) since the 2018–19 season.[^cite_note-279]

[Caledonia Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonia_Pride) is the only women's professional basketball team in Scotland. Established in 2016, the team competes in the UK-wide [Women's British Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_British_Basketball_League) and plays their home matches at the [Oriam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriam) National Performance Centre. Edinburgh also has several men's basketball teams within the Scottish National League. [Boroughmuir Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughmuir_Blaze), [City of Edinburgh Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Edinburgh_Kings), and [Edinburgh Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Lions) all compete in Division 1 of the National League, and [Pleasance B.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasance_B.C.) compete in Division 2. The [Edinburgh Diamond Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Diamond_Devils) is a baseball club that won its first Scottish Championship in 1991 as the "Reivers". 1992 saw the team repeat the achievement, becoming the first team to do so in league history. The same year saw the start of their first youth team, the Blue Jays. The club adopted its present name in 1999.[^cite_note-280]

Edinburgh has also hosted national and international sports events including the [World Student Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Student_Games), the [1970 British Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_British_Commonwealth_Games),[^cite_note-cwgs-281] the [1986 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Commonwealth_Games)[^cite_note-cwgs-281] and the inaugural 2000 Commonwealth Youth Games.[^cite_note-282] For the 1970 Games the city built Olympic standard venues and facilities including Meadowbank Stadium and the [Royal Commonwealth Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commonwealth_Pool). The Pool underwent refurbishment in 2012 and hosted the Diving competition in the [2014 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Commonwealth_Games), which were held in Glasgow.[^cite_note-283]

The [New Year Sprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year_Sprint) is a 110 metre athletics handicap sprint race that has been run continuously since 1870.[^cite_note-284]

In [American football](18951490.md), the [Scottish Claymores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Claymores) played [WLAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_League_of_American_Football)/[NFL Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Europe) games at Murrayfield, including their [World Bowl 96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bowl_IV) victory. From 1995 to 1997, they played all their games there, from 1998 to 2000 they split their home matches between Murrayfield and Glasgow's Hampden Park, then moved to Glasgow full-time, with one final Murrayfield appearance in 2002.[^cite_note-285] The city's most successful non-professional team are the [Edinburgh Wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Wolves) who play at Meadowbank Stadium.[^cite_note-286] The [Edinburgh Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Marathon) has been held annually in the city since 2003 with more than 16,000 runners taking part on each occasion.[^cite_note-287] Its organisers have called it "the fastest marathon in the UK" due to the elevation drop of 40 m (130 ft).[^cite_note-288] The city also organises a half-marathon, as well as [10 km](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_metres) (6.2 mi) and [5 km](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5000_metres) (3.1 mi) races, including a 5 km (3 mi) race on 1 January each year.

Edinburgh has a [speedway](230182.md) team, the [Edinburgh Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Monarchs), which, since the loss of its stadium in the city, has raced at the Lothian Arena in [Armadale, West Lothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadale,_West_Lothian). The Monarchs have won the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League_\(speedway\)) championship five times in their history, in 2003[^cite_note-289] and again in 2008,[^cite_note-290] 2010, 2014 and 2015. Edinburgh also has Scotland's first onshore artificial open air [surfing pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Shore_Surf_Resort), located at former Craigpark quarry in [Ratho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratho).[^cite_note-291]

Edinburgh will also host the [**Grand Départ**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Tour_de_France_Grands_D%C3%A9parts) of the 2027 [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France).[^cite_note-292]

## Notable people

Main article: [List of people from Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Edinburgh)

Edinburgh has a literary tradition, which became especially evident during the [Scottish Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Enlightenment). This heritage and the city's literary life in the present led to it being declared the first [UNESCO City of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_City_of_Literature) in 2004.[^cite_note-Urbanism-293][^cite_note-294] Authors who have lived in Edinburgh include the economist [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith), born in [Kirkcaldy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkcaldy) and author of *[The Wealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wealth_of_Nations)*,[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] [James Boswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Boswell), biographer of [Samuel Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_of_Samuel_Johnson); Sir [Walter Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Scott), creator of the historical novel and author of works such as *[Rob Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Roy_\(novel\))*, *[Ivanhoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanhoe)*, and *[Heart of Midlothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Heart_of_Midlothian)*; [James Hogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hogg), author of *[The Private Memoirs and Confessions of a Justified Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Private_Memoirs_and_Confessions_of_a_Justified_Sinner)*; [Robert Louis Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Louis_Stevenson),[^cite_note-296] creator of *[Treasure Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Island)*, *[Kidnapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapped_\(novel\))*, and *[Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Case_of_Dr_Jekyll_and_Mr_Hyde)*; [Sir Arthur Conan Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Conan_Doyle), the creator of [Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes); [Muriel Spark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_Spark), author of *[The Prime of Miss Jean Brodie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prime_of_Miss_Jean_Brodie_\(novel\))*; [Irvine Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Welsh), author of *[Trainspotting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainspotting_\(novel\))*, whose novels are mostly set in the city and often written in colloquial [Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_language); [^cite_note-297] [Ian Rankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Rankin), author of the [Inspector Rebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detective_Inspector_John_Rebus) series of crime thrillers, [Alexander McCall Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_McCall_Smith), author of the [No. 1 Ladies' Detective Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._1_Ladies'_Detective_Agency) series,[^cite_note-298] and [J. K. Rowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._K._Rowling), author of [Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter), who moved to the city in 1993 and wrote much of her first book in Edinburgh coffee shops.[^cite_note-299]

Edinburgh produced figures in science and engineering. [John Napier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Napier), inventor of [logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithm), was born in [Merchiston Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchiston_Tower) and lived and died in the city.[^cite_note-300] His house now forms part of the original campus of Napier University which was named in his honour. He lies buried under St. Cuthbert's Church. [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell), founder of the modern theory of [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism), was born at 14 India Street (now the home of the [James Clerk Maxwell Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell_Foundation)) and educated at the [Edinburgh Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Academy) and the University of Edinburgh,[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] as was the engineer and telephone pioneer [Alexander Graham Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Graham_Bell).[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] [James Braidwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Braidwood_\(firefighter\)), who organised Britain's first municipal fire brigade, was also born in the city and began his career there.

Other names connected with the city include physicist [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born), a principle founder of [Quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics) and [Nobel laureate](52497.md);[^cite_note-301] [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin), the biologist who propounded the theory of [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection);[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume), philosopher, economist and historian;[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] [James Hutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hutton), regarded as the "Father of Geology";[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] [Joseph Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Black), the chemist who discovered [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium) and [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide), and one of the founders of [Thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamics);[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] pioneering medical researchers [Joseph Lister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Lister,_1st_Baron_Lister) and [James Young Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Young_Simpson);[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] chemist and discoverer of the element [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen) [Daniel Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Rutherford); [Colin Maclaurin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Maclaurin), mathematician and developer of the [Maclaurin series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maclaurin_series),[^cite_note-302] and [Ian Wilmut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Wilmut), the geneticist involved in the cloning of [Dolly the sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_\(sheep\)) just outside Edinburgh, at the [Roslin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roslin_Institute).[^cite_note-famouspeoplecouncil-295] The stuffed carcass of Dolly the sheep is now on display in the National Museum of Scotland.[^cite_note-303] The latest in a long line of science celebrities associated with the city is theoretical physicist, [Nobel laureate](52497.md) and professor emeritus at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) [Peter Higgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Higgs), born in Newcastle but resident in Edinburgh for most of his academic career, after whom the [Higgs boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_boson) particle has been named.[^cite_note-304]

Edinburgh has been the birthplace of actors like [Alastair Sim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alastair_Sim) and Sir [Sean Connery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Connery), the [first cinematic James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrayal_of_James_Bond_in_film#Sean_Connery),[^cite_note-305] the comedian and actor [Ronnie Corbett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Corbett), one of [The Two Ronnies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Two_Ronnies),[^cite_note-306] and the impressionist [Rory Bremner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_Bremner). Artists from the city include the portrait painters Sir [Henry Raeburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Raeburn), Sir [David Wilkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wilkie_\(artist\)), and [Allan Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Ramsay_\(artist\)). The city has produced or been home to musicians [Ian Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Anderson), front man of the band [Jethro Tull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jethro_Tull_\(band\)), [The Incredible String Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Incredible_String_Band), the folk duo [The Corries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Corries), [Wattie Buchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wattie_Buchan), lead singer and founding member of punk band [The Exploited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exploited), [Shirley Manson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Manson), lead singer of the band [Garbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbage_\(band\)), the [Bay City Rollers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_City_Rollers), [The Proclaimers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Proclaimers), [Swim School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swim_School), [Boards of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boards_of_Canada) and [Idlewild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlewild_\(band\)). Edinburgh is the birthplace of former British prime minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) who attended the city's [Fettes College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fettes_College).[^cite_note-307]

Criminals from Edinburgh's past include [Deacon Brodie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon_Brodie), head of a trades guild and Edinburgh city councillor by day but a burglar by night, who is said to have been the inspiration for [Robert Louis Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Louis_Stevenson)'s story, the *[Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Case_of_Dr_Jekyll_and_Mr_Hyde)*,[^cite_note-308] and murderers [Burke and Hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke_and_Hare) who delivered fresh corpses for dissection to the famous anatomist [Robert Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Knox_\(surgeon\)).[^cite_note-309]

Another Edinburgh resident was [Greyfriars Bobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyfriars_Bobby). The small [Skye Terrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skye_Terrier) reputedly kept vigil over his dead master's grave in [Greyfriars Kirkyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyfriars_Kirkyard) for 14 years in the 1860s and 1870s, giving rise to a story of canine devotion which plays a part in attracting visitors to the city.[^cite_note-310]

## International relations

### Twin towns and sister cities

See also: [List of twin towns and sister cities in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_twin_towns_and_sister_cities_in_Scotland)

The City of Edinburgh has entered into 14 international [twinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_twinning) arrangements since 1954.[^cite_note-Edinburgh_twinning-311] Most of the arrangements are styled as *Twin Cities* but the agreement with [Kraków](16815.md) is designated as a *Partner City*,[^cite_note-Edinburgh_twinning-311] and the agreement with [Kyoto Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prefecture) is officially styled as a *Friendship Link*, reflecting its status as the only region to be twinned with Edinburgh.[^cite_note-Edinburgh_twinning-311]

In June 2024, the City of Edinburgh Council shelved plans for a friendship arrangement with [Kaohsiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaohsiung), [Taiwan](25734.md), after concerns were raised that the agreement could hurt relations with China and heighten the risk of cyber attacks. A few weeks before the decision, the Chinese consul general, Zhang Biao, met with Scottish government minister [Angus Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_Robertson) to protest against the potential agreement. In a letter to the city council, the Chinese representative said signing a sister city agreement "will [hurt the feeling of the Chinese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurting_the_feelings_of_the_Chinese_people) and bring about serious consequences to … bilateral relations".[^cite_note-312]
