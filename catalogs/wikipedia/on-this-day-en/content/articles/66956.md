**Ho Chi Minh City** (**HCMC**; [Vietnamese](32511.md): *Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh*, [\[tʰaɲ˨˩ fow˦˥ ho˨˩ cɪj˦˥ mɨn˧˧\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Vietnamese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/24/Thanh_Pho_Ho_Chi_Minh.ogg/Thanh_Pho_Ho_Chi_Minh.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/24/Thanh_Pho_Ho_Chi_Minh.ogg/Thanh_Pho_Ho_Chi_Minh.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), also known as **Saigon** (*Sài Gòn*, [\[ʂaj˨˩ ɣɔŋ˨˩\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Vietnamese)),[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] is the most populous city and [centrally-governed city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Vietnam) of [Vietnam](202354.md), with a population of more than 14 million in 2025.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-pop_2025-3] Its geography is defined by rivers and canals, of which the largest is the [Saigon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_River).

As the biggest [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre) of Vietnam, Ho Chi Minh City has the largest [gross regional domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_domestic_product) out of all Vietnamese provinces and municipalities,[^cite_note-10] contributing around a quarter of the country's total [GDP](12594.md).[^cite_note-11] It is the busiest international [transport hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub) in Vietnam, with [Tân Sơn Nhất International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Son_Nhat_International_Airport) accounting for nearly half of all international arrivals to Vietnam, and the [Port of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Port) among the [busiest container ports in Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Ho Chi Minh City harbors various [historic buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historic_buildings_in_Ho_Chi_Minh_City) and modern landmarks, including the [Independence Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Palace), [Bitexco Financial Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitexco_Financial_Tower), [Landmark 81 Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmark_81), the [War Remnants Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Remnants_Museum), and [Bến Thành Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%BFn_Th%C3%A0nh_Market). The city is known for its [alleyways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hem_\(alleyway\)) and [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife), most notably [Phạm Ngũ Lão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%E1%BA%A1m_Ng%C5%A9_L%C3%A3o_Street) and [Bùi Viện Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B9i_Vi%E1%BB%87n_Street).

The area was initially part of [Cambodian](334751.md) polities until it became part of the Vietnamese [Nguyễn lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_lords) in 1698, as a result of [Đại Việt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BA%A1i_Vi%E1%BB%87t)'s expansionist policy of *[Nam tiến](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_ti%E1%BA%BFn)*. It served as the capital of the Nguyễn lords in their final years before the establishment of the [Nguyễn dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_dynasty) in 1802. After the fall of the [Citadel of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_Saigon) during the [Cochinchina campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochinchina_campaign) in 1859, it became the capital of [French Cochinchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cochinchina) from 1862 to 1949. It was also the capital of [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina) from 1887 to 1902, and again from 1945 until its cessation in 1954. After France [recognized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lys%C3%A9e_Accords) Vietnam's independence and unity,[^cite_note-15] it was the capital of the [State of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Vietnam) from 1949 to 1955. Following the [1954 partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Geneva_Conference), it became the capital of [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam) until it was [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon) by [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam), leading to a unified [communist state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state) in 1976. The city was subsequently renamed after North Vietnamese leader [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh), while *Saigon* remains in use informally and has been the official name of the [city's administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City) since 2025. In 2025, the provinces of [Bình Dương](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ACnh_D%C6%B0%C6%A1ng_province) and [Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A0_R%E1%BB%8Ba%E2%80%93V%C5%A9ng_T%C3%A0u_province) were merged into Ho Chi Minh City, allowing the city to inherit the two former provinces' [industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_city) and [coastal metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C5%A9ng_T%C3%A0u).

## Etymology

Main article: [Names of Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Ho_Chi_Minh_City)

The first known human habitation in the area was a [Cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chams) settlement called *Baigaur*.[^cite_note-16] The [Cambodians](5430.md) then took over the Cham village of Baigaur and renamed it *Prey Nokôr* (ព្រៃនគរ, [\[prɛi̯ nɔkor\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Khmer)), a fishing village.[^cite_note-SaigonHis2-17][^cite_note-salkin-962-18] Later, Vietnamese settlers arrived in this region in the early 17th century adopted and Vietised the Khmer name as *Sài Gòn* ([chữ Hán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%AF_H%C3%A1n): 柴棍, [\[ʂaj˨˩ ɣɔŋ˨˩\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Vietnamese), *pr* spirantised to *ʂ*, *k* > *ɣ*).[^cite_note-19]

Over time, under the control of the Vietnamese, it was officially renamed [Gia Định](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_%C4%90%E1%BB%8Bnh) ([嘉](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%98%89)[定](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%AE%9A)) in 1698, a name that was retained until the time of the [French conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochinchina_Campaign) in the 1860s, when it adopted the name *Sài Gòn*, [francized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francization) as *Saïgon*,[^cite_note-salkin-962-18] while the city was still indicated as [嘉](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%98%89)[定](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%AE%9A) on Vietnamese maps written in [chữ Hán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%AF_H%C3%A1n) until at least 1891.[^cite_note-mapofvietnam18912-20] An old name of [Gia Định](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_%C4%90%E1%BB%8Bnh_province) was *Phan Yên* 藩安, which was later glossed as *Phiên An*.[^cite_note-21]

The name Ho Chi Minh City was given after [reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_Day) in 1976 to honour the late [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh).[^cite_note-resolution-23] The informal name of *Sài Gòn* remains in daily speech. There is a technical difference between the two terms: *Sài Gòn* is used to refer to the city centre, aptly named [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City) and the adjacent neighborhoods of [Bến Thành](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%BFn_Th%C3%A0nh), [Tân Định](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A2n_%C4%90%E1%BB%8Bnh,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City), [Xuân Hòa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu%C3%A2n_H%C3%B2a,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City) while *Ho Chi Minh City* refers to all of its urban and rural area.[^cite_note-salkin-962-18]

### Saigon



Saigon is written here as 柴棍 along with other Southern Vietnamese cities. (On the left of the page, first row after "城庯三")

The original toponym behind Sài Gòn was attested earliest as 柴棍, with two phonograms whose Sino-Vietnamese readings are "*sài*" and "*côn*" respectively.

In Lê Quý Đôn's "*Miscellaneous Chronicles of the Pacified Frontier*" (撫邊雜錄, *Phủ biên tạp lục* c. 1776), wherein he relates that, in 1674, Cambodian prince [Ang Nan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Nan) was installed as [uparaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uparaja) in 柴棍 (Sài Gòn) by Vietnamese forces.

柴棍 also appears later in Trịnh Hoài Đức's "*Comprehensive Records about the Gia Định Citadel*" (嘉定城通志, *Gia Định thành thông chí*, c. 1820), "*Textbook on the Geography of the Southern Country*" (南國地輿教科書, *Nam quốc địa dư giáo khoa thư*, 1908),[^cite_note-\:0-24] etc.

Adrien Launay's *Histoire de la Mission de Cochinchine* (1688–1823), "*Documents Historiques II: 1728 – 1771*" (1924: [190](https://books.google.com/books?id=zZ6AAAAAIAAJ\&pg=PA190)) cites 1747 documents containing the toponyms: provincia *Rai-gon, Rai-gon thong* (for \**Sài Gòn thượng* "Upper Saigon"), & *Rai-gon-ha* (for \**Sài Gòn hạ* "Lower Saigon"). It is probably a transcription of Khmer ព្រៃនគរ (*Prey Nokôr*),[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-TruongVinhKy2-26][^cite_note-28] or Khmer ព្រៃគរ (*Prey Kôr*).

There is a proposal that Sài Gòn is from the non-Sino-Vietnamese reading of Chinese 堤岸 ("*embankment*", *tai4 ngon6*, South Vietnamese: *đê ngạn*).[^cite_note-30]

### Ho Chi Minh City

The name commemorates [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh), the first leader of the [Democratic Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam). This name, while not his given name, was one he favored throughout his later years. It combines a Vietnamese surname (Hồ, [胡](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%83%A1)) with a given name meaning "enlightened will" (from [Sino-Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_vocabulary), [志](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%BF%97)[明](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%98%8E); *Chí* meaning 'will' or 'spirit', and *Minh* meaning 'light'), in essence, meaning "light bringer".[^cite_note-bbc-bacho2-31] "Saigon" is still used as a semi-official name for the city, in some cases being used interchangeably with Ho Chi Minh City, partly due to its longer history and familiarity.[^cite_note-\:1-32]

## History

### Early settlement

The earliest settlement in the area was a [Funan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funan) temple at the location of what later is the Phụng Sơn Buddhist temple, founded in the [4th century AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_century_AD).[^cite_note-HistDict2-33] A settlement called Baigaur was established on the site in the 11th century by the [Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa).[^cite_note-HistDict2-33] Baigaur was renamed Prey Nokor after conquest by the [Khmer Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Empire) around 1145,[^cite_note-HistDict2-33] Prey Nokor grew on the site of a fishing village and area of forest.[^cite_note-34]

The first [Vietnamese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people) crossed the sea to explore this land completely without the organisation of the [Nguyễn Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Lords). Thanks to the marriage between Princess [Nguyễn Phúc Ngọc Vạn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Ph%C3%BAc_Ng%E1%BB%8Dc_V%E1%BA%A1n) – daughter of Lord [Nguyễn Phúc Nguyên](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Ph%C3%BAc_Nguy%C3%AAn) – and the King of Cambodia [Chey Chettha II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chey_Chettha_II) in 1620, the relationship between Vietnam and Cambodia became smooth, and the people of the two countries could freely move back and forth. In exchange, Chey Chettha II gifted Prei Nokor to the [Nguyễn lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_lords).[^cite_note-Song22-35]

### Nguyễn dynasty



*[Thành Bát Quái](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_Saigon)* (Citadel of Eight [Trigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagua)) or *Thành Quy* (Citadel of Tortoise) in 1795.



Map of Gia Định in 1815

In 1679, Lord [Nguyễn Phúc Tần](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Ph%C3%BAc_T%E1%BA%A7n) allowed a group of Chinese refugees from the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) to settle in [Mỹ Tho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%E1%BB%B9_Tho_\(city\)), [Biên Hòa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bien_Hoa) and Saigon to seek refuge. In 1698, [Nguyễn Hữu Cảnh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_H%E1%BB%AFu_C%E1%BA%A3nh), a Vietnamese noble, was sent by the Nguyễn rulers of [Huế](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu%E1%BA%BF) by sea to establish Vietnamese administrative structures in the area, thus detaching the area from Cambodia, which was not strong enough to intervene. He is credited with the expansion of Saigon into a significant settlement.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] King [Chey Chettha IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chey_Chettha_IV) of Cambodia tried to stop the Vietnamese and was defeated by Nguyễn Hữu Cảnh in 1700. In February 1700, he invaded Cambodia from [An Giang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Giang). In March, the Vietnamese expedition under Cảnh and a Chinese general [Trần Thượng Xuyên](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%E1%BA%A7n_Th%C6%B0%E1%BB%A3ng_Xuy%C3%AAn) (Chen Shangchuan) defeated the main Cambodian army at Bích Đôi citadel, king Chey Chettha IV took flight while his nephew [Ang Em](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Em) surrendered to the invaders, as the Vietnamese marched onto and captured Cambodia's capital [Phnom Penh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phnom_Penh).[^cite_note-Song23-38] As a result, Saigon and [Long An](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_An) were officially and securely obtained by the Nguyễn, more Vietnamese settlers moved into the newly conquered lands.[^cite_note-Song23-38]

In 1788, [Nguyễn Ánh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_Long) captured the city, and used it as a centre of resistance against Tây Sơn.[^cite_note-39] Two years later, a [Vauban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vauban) citadel called [Gia Định](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_Saigon), or *Thành Bát Quái* ("Eight Diagrams") was built by Victor [Olivier de Puymanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_de_Puymanel), one of the [Nguyễn Ánh's French mercenaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_assistance_to_Nguy%E1%BB%85n_%C3%81nh).[^cite_note-40] The citadel was captured by [Lê Văn Khôi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%AA_V%C4%83n_Kh%C3%B4i) during [his revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%AA_V%C4%83n_Kh%C3%B4i_revolt) of 1833–35 against emperor [Minh Mạng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minh_M%E1%BA%A1ng). Following the revolt, Minh Mạng ordered it to be dismantled, and a new citadel, called *Phụng Thành*, was built in 1836.[^cite_note-\:2-41] In 1859, the citadel was destroyed by the French following the [Battle of Kỳ Hòa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ky_Hoa).[^cite_note-\:2-41] Initially called Gia Định, the Vietnamese city became Saigon in the 18th century.[^cite_note-HistDict2-33]

### French colony



Thủ Ngữ flag pole, Saigon taken in 1866 by [Émile Gsell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Gsell).

Ceded to France by the 1862 [Treaty of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saigon_\(1862\)),[^cite_note-42] the city was planned by the French to transform into a town for [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization). During the 19th and 20th centuries, construction of French-style buildings began, including a [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Zoo_and_Botanical_Gardens), the [Norodom Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norodom_Palace), [Hotel Continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Continental_Saigon), [Notre-Dame Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_Cathedral_Basilica_of_Saigon), and [Bến Thành Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%BFn_Th%C3%A0nh_Market), among others.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] In April 1865, [Gia Định Báo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_Dinh_Bao) was established in Saigon, becoming the first newspaper published in Vietnam.[^cite_note-45] During the French colonial era, Saigon became known as "Pearl of the Orient" (*_Hòn ngọc Viễn Đông_*),[^cite_note-46] or "Paris of the Extreme Orient".[^cite_note-47]

On 27 April 1931, a new [région](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9gion) called **Saigon–Cholon** consisting of Saigon and Cholon was formed; the name Cholon was dropped after South Vietnam gained independence from France in 1955.[^cite_note-48] From about 256,000 in 1930,[^cite_note-\:3-49] Saigon's population rose to 1.2 million in 1950.[^cite_note-\:3-49]

### State of Vietnam, and Republic of Vietnam

On 14 June 1949, 10 days after France returned [Cochinchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cochinchina) to Vietnam, former emperor [Bảo Đại](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%A3o_%C4%90%E1%BA%A1i) made Saigon the capital of the [State of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Vietnam) within the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union) with himself as head of state.[^cite_note-History-50] The state was proclaimed in July. In July 1954, the [Geneva Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Geneva_Conference) partitioned Vietnam [along](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Demilitarized_Zone) the [17th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_parallel_north) ([Bến Hải River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%BFn_H%E1%BA%A3i_River)), with the [Việt Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Minh), under [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh), gaining complete control of [the northern half of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam), while the southern half remained under the rule of the State of Vietnam.[^cite_note-\:4-51]

The State officially became the [Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Vietnam) when Bảo Đại was deposed by his Prime Minister [Ngô Đình Diệm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C3%B4_%C4%90%C3%ACnh_Di%E1%BB%87m) in the 1955 [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_State_of_Vietnam_referendum),[^cite_note-\:4-51] with Saigon as its capital.[^cite_note-\:5-52] On 22 October 1956, the city was given the official name, *_Đô thành Sài Gòn_* ("Metropolitan city of Saigon").[^cite_note-\:6-53] After the decree of 27 March 1959 came into effect, Saigon was divided into eight districts and 41 wards.[^cite_note-\:6-53]

In December 1966, two wards from the old An Khánh Commune of Gia Định, were formed into District 1, then seceded later to become District 9.[^cite_note-\:8-54] In July 1969, District 10 and District 11 were founded, and by 1975, the city's area consisted of eleven districts, [Gia Định](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_%C4%90%E1%BB%8Bnh_Province), [Củ Chi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%E1%BB%A7_Chi_District) ([Hậu Nghĩa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BA%ADu_Ngh%C4%A9a_Province)), and [Phú Hòa District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%C3%BA_H%C3%B2a_District) ([Bình Dương](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ACnh_D%C6%B0%C6%A1ng_Province)).[^cite_note-\:8-54]

Saigon served as the financial, industrial and transport centre of the Republic of Vietnam.[^cite_note-55] In the 1950s, with the U.S. providing nearly \$2 billion in aid to the Diệm regime, the country's economy grew more rapidly under the [capitalist model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism);[^cite_note-\:6-53] by 1960, over half of South Vietnam's factories were located in Saigon.[^cite_note-56] Beginning in the 1960s, Saigon experienced economic downturn and higher inflation, as it was completely dependent on U.S. aid and imports from other countries.[^cite_note-\:6-53] As a result of urbanisation, with the population reaching 3.3 million by 1970, the city was described by the [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) as being turned "into a huge slum".[^cite_note-57] The city had "prostitutes, drug addicts, corrupt officials, beggars, orphans, and Americans with money", and according to [Stanley Karnow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Karnow), it was "a black-market city in the largest sense of the word".[^cite_note-\:5-52]

On 28 April 1955, the [Vietnamese National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_National_Army) launched [an attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Saigon_\(1955\)) against [Bình Xuyên](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ACnh_Xuy%C3%AAn) military force in the city. The battle lasted until May, killing an estimated 500 people and leaving about 20,000 homeless.[^cite_note-\:5-52][^cite_note-58] Ngô Đình Diệm then later turned on other paramilitary groups in Saigon, including the [Hòa Hảo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B2a_H%E1%BA%A3o) Buddhist reform movement.[^cite_note-\:5-52] On 11 June 1963, Buddhist monk [Thích Quảng Đức](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Qu%E1%BA%A3ng_%C4%90%E1%BB%A9c) self-immolated in the city, in protest of the Diệm regime. On 2 November of the same year, Diệm was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest_and_assassination_of_Ngo_Dinh_Diem) in Saigon, in a successful coup by [Dương Văn Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C6%B0%C6%A1ng_V%C4%83n_Minh).[^cite_note-\:5-52]

During the 1968 [Tet Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tet_Offensive), communist forces launched a failed [attempt to capture the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Saigon_\(1968\)). Seven years later, on 30 April 1975, [Saigon was captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon), ending the [Vietnam War](32611.md),[^cite_note-59] and the city came under the control of the [Vietnamese People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_People's_Army).[^cite_note-\:5-52]

### Socialist Republic of Vietnam



Ho Chi Minh City during sunset (November 2017)

In July 1976, upon the establishment of the unified [Socialist Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Vietnam), the city of Saigon (including the Cholon area), the province of Gia Ðịnh and two suburban districts of two other nearby provinces were combined to create Ho Chi Minh City, in honour of the late Communist leader Ho Chi Minh.[^cite_note-resolution-23] At the time, the city covered an area of 1,295.5 square kilometres (500.2 sq mi) with eight districts and five rurals: [Thủ Đức](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%E1%BB%A7_%C4%90%E1%BB%A9c_\(urban_district\)), [Hóc Môn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B3c_M%C3%B4n_District), [Củ Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%E1%BB%A7_Chi_District), [Bình Chánh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ACnh_Ch%C3%A1nh_District), and [Nhà Bè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nh%C3%A0_B%C3%A8_District).[^cite_note-\:8-54] Since 1978, administrative divisions in the city have been revised multiple times,[^cite_note-\:8-54] including in 2020, when [District 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_2,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City), [District 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_9,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City), and [Thủ Đức District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%E1%BB%A7_%C4%90%E1%BB%A9c_\(urban_district\)) were consolidated to form a [municipal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%E1%BB%A7_%C4%90%E1%BB%A9c_\(city\)).[^cite_note-\:9-60] On 29 October 2002, 60 people died and 90 were injured in the [International Trade Center building fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Ho_Chi_Minh_City_ITC_fire) in Ho Chi Minh City.[^cite_note-61]

Ho Chi Minh City, along with its surrounding provinces and municipalities, is regarded as "the manufacturing hub" of Vietnam, and "an attractive business hub".[^cite_note-62] In terms of cost, it was ranked the 178th-most expensive major city in the world according to the Mercer Cost Of Living 2024 survey of 226 cities.[^cite_note-63] In terms of international connectedness, as of 2024, the city was classified as a "Beta+" city by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-64]

## Geography



Population density and elevation above sea level in the city (2010). Ho Chi Minh City is vulnerable to [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).

The city is located in [Southeast Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Vietnam). The average elevation is 5 m (16 ft) above sea level for the city centre and 16 m (52 ft) for the suburb areas.[^cite_note-65] It borders the [Tây Ninh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A2y_Ninh_Province) province and [Đồng Nai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BB%93ng_Nai_province) municipality to the north, [Đồng Nai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BB%93ng_Nai_Province) and [Lâm Đồng](2416067.md) province to the east, [Tây Ninh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A2y_Ninh_province) to the west, and [Đồng Tháp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BB%93ng_Th%C3%A1p_province) province and the [South China Sea](74209.md) to the south with a coast 89 km (55 mi) long. The city covers an area of 6.772 km<sup>2</sup> (2.615 sq mi) or 2% of the surface area of Vietnam, extending up to Minh Thạnh commune (20 km or 12 mi from the [Memot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memot_District) in [Cambodia](334751.md)) and down to [Côn Đảo special administrative region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4n_%C4%90%E1%BA%A3o_special_administrative_region). The municipality also extends far to the east to [Bình Châu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ACnh_Ch%C3%A2u) (110 km (68 mi) from the city center). Due to its location on the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta), the city is fringed by tidal flats that have been modified for agriculture.[^cite_note-66]

### Flooding

During the rainy season, a combination of tide, rains, flow volume in the [Saigon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_River) and [Đồng Nai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Nai_River) and land subsidence results in flooding in parts of the city.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] A once-in-100 year flood would cause 23% of the city to undergo flooding.[^cite_note-69]

The population of the city, as of the 1 October 2004 census, was 6,117,251 (of which 19 inner districts had 5,140,412 residents and five suburban districts had 976,839 inhabitants).[^cite_note-Statistical_office-80]

In 2007, the city's population was 6,650,942 – with the 19 inner districts home to 5,564,975 residents and the five suburban districts containing 1,085,967 inhabitants. The result of the 2009 Census shows that the city's population was 7,162,864 people,[^cite_note-gso.gov.vn-81] about 8.34% of the total population of Vietnam, making it the highest population-concentrated city in the country. As of the end of 2012, the total population of the city was 7,750,900 people, an increase of 3.1% from 2011.[^cite_note-gso-82]

As an administrative unit, its population is the largest at the provincial level. According to the 2019 census, Ho Chi Minh City has a population of over 8.9 million within the [city proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_proper) and over 21 million within its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-2019census-83]

In August 2017, the city's mayor, Nguyễn Thành Phong, admitted that previous estimates of 8–10 million were drastic underestimations.[^cite_note-84] The actual population (including those who have not officially registered) was estimated 13 million in 2017.[^cite_note-trithuctre-85] The [Ho Chi Minh City Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metropolitan_Area), a metropolitan area covering the [southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_\(Vietnam\)) region plus [Tiền Giang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti%E1%BB%81n_Giang_Province) and [Long An Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_An_Province) in [southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_\(Vietnam\)) under planning, will have an area of 30,000 km<sup>2</sup> (12,000 sq mi) with a population of 20 million inhabitants by 2020.[^cite_note-86]

### Ethnic groups

The majority of the population are ethnic Vietnamese ([Kinh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinh)) at about 93.52%. Ho Chi Minh City's largest minority ethnic group are the Chinese ([Hoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoa_people)) with 5.78%. [Cholon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholon,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City) is home to the largest Chinese community in Vietnam. The *Hoa* (Chinese) speak a number of [varieties of Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Chinese), including [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese), [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_dialect), [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien), [Hainanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainanese), and [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese); smaller numbers also speak [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese). Other ethnic minorities include [Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_people) with 0.34%, [Cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chams) with 0.1%, as well as a small group of [Baweans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawean_people) from [Bawean Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawean_Island) in Indonesia (about 400; as of 2015), they occupy city center of [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City) and [Bến Thành](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%BFn_Th%C3%A0nh).[^cite_note-87] Other nationalities including [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans), [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people), [Americans](19792942.md), [Russians](59510.md), [South Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africans), [Filipinos](2298105.md), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [Britons](19097669.md) reside in Ho Chi Minh City as [expatriate workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate). The highest concentration of which are in [Thủ Đức](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%E1%BB%A7_%C4%90%E1%BB%A9c_\(city\)) and [Phú Mỹ Hưng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%C3%BA_M%E1%BB%B9_H%C6%B0ng_urban_area).[^cite_note-88]

### Religion

As of 2025, the city reported more than 2.4 million religious adherents. [Buddhism](3267529.md) remained the largest religion in the city, with about 1.6 million followers, followed by [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) with more than 640,000 followers, [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) with more than 65,000 followers, [Caodaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caodaism) with more than 48,000 followers, and [Islam](6037917.md) with about 5,000 followers.[^cite_note-89] In 2009, city reporting also listed smaller communities including [Hòa Hảo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B2a_H%E1%BA%A3o) with 4,894 followers, [Tịnh độ cư sĩ Phật hội Việt Nam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E1%BB%8Bnh_%C4%91%E1%BB%99_c%C6%B0_s%C4%A9_Ph%E1%BA%ADt_h%E1%BB%99i_Vi%E1%BB%87t_Nam?action=edit\&redlink=1) with 1,387 followers, [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) with 395 followers, [Đạo Tứ ấn hiếu nghĩa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BA%A1o_T%E1%BB%A9_%E1%BA%A5n_hi%E1%BA%BFu_ngh%C4%A9a?action=edit\&redlink=1) with 298 followers, [Minh Sư Đạo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minh_S%C6%B0_%C4%90%E1%BA%A1o?action=edit\&redlink=1) with 283 followers, [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) with 192 followers, [Bửu Sơn Kỳ Hương](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BB%ADu_S%C6%A1n_K%E1%BB%B3_H%C6%B0%C6%A1ng) with 89 followers, [Minh Lý Đạo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MinhL%C3%BD%C4%90%E1%BA%A1o?action=edit\&redlink=1) with 67 followers.[^cite_note-90]

## Economy

The city contains 20.2% of Vietnam's GDP, 27.9% of industrial output and 34.9% of the [FDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment) projects in the country in 2005.[^cite_note-91] In 2005, it had 4,344,000 labourers, of whom 130,000 are over the labour age norm (in Vietnam, 60 for male and 55 for female workers).[^cite_note-92] In 2009, [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) reached \$2,800, compared to the country's average level of \$1,042.[^cite_note-93]

### Sectors



[Saigon Hi-tech Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Hi-tech_Park), located in [Tăng Nhơn Phú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%83ng_Nh%C6%A1n_Ph%C3%BA), is one of Vietnam's two national hi-tech parks.

The city and its ports are part of the [21st Century Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Maritime_Silk_Road) that runs from the Chinese coast via the [Suez Canal](29323.md) to the Mediterranean, there to the Upper Adriatic region of [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) with its rail connections to [Central](5188.md) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] As of June 2006, the city had three export processing zones and twelve industrial parks, in addition to Quang Trung Software Park and Ho Chi Minh City hi-tech park. [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) has invested about 1 billion dollars in a factory in the city. More than fifty banks with hundreds of branches and about 20 insurance companies are also located inside the city. The [Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Stock_Exchange), the first stock exchange in Vietnam, was opened in 2001. Ho Chi Minh City has 66 shopping malls, about 300 supermarkets and 405 traditional markets, along with thousands of convenience stores and stabilized price points.[^cite_note-106]

On Vietnam's Provincial Competitiveness Index 2023, a tool for evaluating the business environment in Vietnam's provinces, Ho Chi Minh City received a score of 67.19.[^cite_note-107] This was a fall from 2022 in which the province received a score of 65.86. In 2023, the province received its highest scores on the 'Time Costs' and 'Law and Order' criterion and lowest on 'Access To Land' and 'Policy Bias'.[^cite_note-108]

### Urbanisation



Ho Chi Minh City has a higher concentration of skyscrapers as a result of urbanisation. The [Landmark 81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmark_81) is the tallest building in Vietnam.

With a population of 8,382,287 (as of Census 2010 on 1 April 2010)[^cite_note-109] (registered residents plus migrant workers and a metropolitan population of 10 million), the city needs increased public infrastructure.[^cite_note-Statistical_office-80] In 2007, three million foreign tourists, about 70% of the total number of tourists to Vietnam, visited the city. Total cargo transport to city's ports reached 50.5 million [tonnes](31185.md).[^cite_note-110]

## Cityscape

### Architecture

Ho Chi Minh City has buildings from styles and time periods. French influence during the colonial era can be seen throughout the city, especially in the city center where a number of buildings can be found. Buildings of [French colonial architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_architecture) include the [Ho Chi Minh City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Hall), [Saigon Central Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Central_Post_Office), [Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_Cathedral_Basilica_of_Saigon) and [Bến Thành Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%BFn_Th%C3%A0nh_Market).[^cite_note-FrenchArchitecture-111] Ho Chi Minh City is also home to a number of buildings inspired by [Chinese architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_architecture), which are mostly found in [Chợ Lớn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%A3_L%E1%BB%9Bn), the city's [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) quarter. These include the [Thien Hau Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thien_Hau_Temple_\(Cholon\)), which was first built around 1760.[^cite_note-112]

During the Republic of Vietnam era, Vietnamese modernist architecture began to develop in the city. Buildings which were commissioned during this time include the [Independence Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Palace), replacing the former [Independence Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Governor's_Palace) which was of [Baroque Revival architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Revival_architecture).[^cite_note-113]

### Parks and gardens



[Tao Đàn Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Dan_Park) is one of the largest and oldest parks in Ho Chi Minh City.

Ho Chi Minh City has parks, including the [Tao Đàn Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Dan_Park), located next to the [Independence Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Palace).[^cite_note-114] Other parks in downtown include the [September 23rd Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_23rd_Park) and [30/4 Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_30).[^cite_note-115]

The [Saigon Zoo and Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Zoo_and_Botanical_Gardens) is located on the northern end of [Saigon quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City). It contains a collection of over 600 rare animals and about 4,000 plant species, some of which are over 100 years in age.[^cite_note-116]

### Pedestrian zones



[Nguyễn Huệ Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Hu%E1%BB%87_Boulevard).

[Nguyễn Huệ Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Hu%E1%BB%87_Boulevard) was the first [pedestrian street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian_zone) in Ho Chi Minh City. It opened to the public in April 2015, and is a spot for locals and visitors to gather.[^cite_note-117] Events are held in the precinct throughout the year, including the annual flower festival during [Tết](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E1%BA%BFt).[^cite_note-118]

[Bùi Viện Walking Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B9i_Vi%E1%BB%87n_Street) is known for its status as a hub for western backpackers and tourists.[^cite_note-119] Bùi Viện Street, also known as "Western Street", is a tourism hub in Ho Chi Minh City that offers restaurants, coffee shops, hotels, live music pubs, and rooftop bars. Before becoming a walking street, Bùi Viện Street was a destination for backpackers to have fun, try unfamiliar cuisines, and explore new places during their trip to Ho Chi Minh City.[^cite_note-120]

## &#x20;Transport

### Air



Tân Sơn Nhất International Airport is the busiest airport in Vietnam.

The city is served by [Tân Sơn Nhất International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Son_Nhat_International_Airport), the largest airport in Vietnam in terms of passengers handled (with an estimated number of over 15.5 million passengers per year in 2010, accounting for more than half of Vietnam's air passenger traffic).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-autogenerated2-122]

[Long Thành International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Thanh_International_Airport) is scheduled to begin operating in 2026. Based in [Long Thành, Đồng Nai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Th%C3%A0nh,_%C4%90%E1%BB%93ng_Nai), about 40 km (25 mi) east of Ho Chi Minh City, Long Thành Airport will serve international flights, with a maximum traffic capacity of 100 million passengers per year when fully completed; Tân Sơn Nhất Airport will serve domestic flights.[^cite_note-airportnews-123]

### Rail

The city is a terminal for [Vietnam Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Railways) train routes in the country. The [Reunification Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Railway_\(Vietnam\)) (*tàu Thống Nhất*) runs from Saigon to Hanoi from [Saigon Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Railway_Station) near the [Nhieu Loc Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nhieu_Loc%E2%80%93Thi_Nghe_Channel), with stops at cities and provinces along the line.[^cite_note-124] Within the city, the two main stations are Sóng Thần and Sài Gòn. There are several smaller stations such as [Dĩ An](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%A9_An_station), Thủ Đức (demolished), [Bình Triệu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ACnh_Tri%E1%BB%87u_station), [Gò Vấp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B2_V%E1%BA%A5p_station). Rail transport comprises 0.6% of passenger traffic and 6% of goods shipments.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-125]

### Water

Traffic between Ho Chi Minh City and Vietnam's southern provinces has steadily increased over the years; the Đôi and Tẻ Canals, some of the routes to the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta), receive 100,000 waterway vehicles every year, representing around 13 million tons of cargo. A project to dredge these routes has been approved to facilitate transport, to be implemented in 2011–14.[^cite_note-waterway-expand-126] In 2017, the [Saigon Waterbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Waterbus) launched, connecting down town to [Thủ Đức](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%E1%BB%A7_%C4%90%E1%BB%A9c_\(city\)).[^cite_note-127] In 2026 the local government approved the Can Gio International transshipment port project at the mouth of the Cai Mep River, which is designed to have a 4.8M TEU capacity by 2030, reaching >16M TEUs by 2047.[^cite_note-128]

### Public

The [Ho Chi Minh City Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metro), a [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) network, is being built in stages. [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metro\)) was opened in 2024.[^cite_note-129] The line connects [Bến Thành](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Thanh_station) to [Suối Tiên Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suoi_Tien_Amusement_Park) in Tăng Nhơn Phú, with a depot in [Long Bình](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Binh_Depot). Planners expect the route to serve more than 160,000 passengers daily.[^cite_note-railwaytech-130] A line between Bến Thành and Tham Lương has been approved by the government,[^cite_note-secondlineapproved-131] and several more lines (Lines [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metro\)), [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metro\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_4_\(Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metro\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_5_\(Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metro\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_6_\(Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Metro\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)) are the subject of feasibility studies.[^cite_note-railwaytech-130]

### Private

For short trips, "hug vehicle" motorcycle taxis are available throughout the city, usually congregating at a major intersection. You can also book motorcycle and car taxis through ride-hailing apps like [Grab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grab_\(company\)) and [Gojek](51272668.md). An activity for tourists is a tour of the city on [cyclos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_rickshaw), which allow for longer trips at a more relaxed pace. For years, cars have become more popular.[^cite_note-132] There are approximately 340,000 cars and 3.5 million motorcycles in the city, which is almost double compared with Hanoi.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-125] The growing number of cars tend to cause gridlock and contribute to air pollution. The government has called out motorcycles as the reason for the congestion and has developed plans to reduce the number of motorcycles and to improve public transport.[^cite_note-133]

See also: [List of historic buildings in Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historic_buildings_in_Ho_Chi_Minh_City)



The opera house is located in the city centre.



Bùi Viện Walking Street is lined with hotels, coffee shops and bars catering to tourists.

Tourist attractions in the city may relate to periods of French colonisation and the Vietnam War. The city's centre has some American-style boulevards, and [French colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial) buildings. The majority of these tourist spots are located down town. Structures in the city centre include the [Reunification Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_Palace) (*_Dinh Thống Nhất_*), City Hall (*_Ủy ban nhân dân Thành phố_*), [Municipal Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Theatre,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City) (*_Nhà hát thành phố_*, also known as the Opera House), City Post Office (*_Bưu điện thành phố_*), State Bank Office (*_Ngân hàng Nhà nước_*), City People's Court (*_Tòa án nhân dân thành phố_*), and [Notre-Dame Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_Cathedral_Basilica_of_Saigon) (*_Nhà thờ Đức Bà Sài Gòn_*), which was constructed between 1863 and 1880. Some of the historic hotels include the [Hotel Majestic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Majestic_\(Saigon\)), dating from the French colonial era, and the [Rex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rex_Hotel) and [Caravelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravelle_Hotel) hotels, both of which are former hangouts for American officers and war correspondents in the 1960s & '70s.[^cite_note-146]

&#x20;The city offers restaurants serving Vietnamese dishes, including [phở](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%E1%BB%9F) and [rice vermicelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_vermicelli). The area around [Phạm Ngũ Lão Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%E1%BA%A1m_Ng%C5%A9_L%C3%A3o_Street) and [Bùi Viện Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B9i_Vi%E1%BB%87n_Street) in down town is referred to as the Backpackers’ Quarter.[^cite_note-147] [Saigon Hotpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Hotpot) is a volunteer student group that gives international tourists free tours of the city.[^cite_note-Vi2009-01-28-148]



A [dragon dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_dance) performance, done to welcome [Tết Nguyên Đán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E1%BA%BFt_Nguy%C3%AAn_%C4%90%C3%A1n) in Ho Chi Minh City, 2009

It was approximated that 4.3 million tourists visited Vietnam in 2007, of which 70%, approximately 3 million tourists, visited the city.[^cite_note-thongtinthuongmaivietnam1-149] Ho Chi Minh City welcomed 6 million tourists in 2017.[^cite_note-150] According to [Mastercard](378791.md)'s 2019 report, the city is the country's second most visited city (18th in Asia Pacific), with 4.1 million overnight international visitors in 2018 (after [Hanoi](56667.md) with 4.8 million visitors).[^cite_note-VNExpress-151] In H12025, HCMC welcomed over 22.1 million visitors (3.8 million international and 18.3 million domestic), generating approximately ₫118 trillion (\~US \$4.6 billion), a +27.3% year-over-year growth.[^cite_note-152] Over the Tết 2025 holiday, the city led all Vietnamese provinces in tourism receipts with \~\$303 million, up 17% from 2024.[^cite_note-153] In September 2025, HCMC is hosting ITE HCMC 2025 which focuses on sustainable tourism and trade networking and is the 19th of its kind.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

### Sport



[Thống Nhất Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%E1%BB%91ng_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_Stadium) is home to the [V.League 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V.League_1) association football club [Ho Chi Minh City F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_F.C.)

As of 2005, Ho Chi Minh City was home to 91 association football fields, 86 swimming pools, and 256 gyms.[^cite_note-159] The largest stadium in the city is the 15,000-seat [Thống Nhất Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%E1%BB%91ng_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_Stadium), located on Đào Duy Từ Street, Diên Hồng ward. The next largest is [Military Region 7 Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%C3%A2n_khu_7_Stadium), located near [Tan Son Nhat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Son_Nhat_Airport) in [Tân Sơn Hòa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A2n_S%C6%A1n_H%C3%B2a). The Military Region 7 Stadium was of the venues for the [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup) finals. [Phú Thọ Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%C3%BA_Th%E1%BB%8D_Horse_Racing_Ground), another sporting venue established during colonial times, was the only racetrack in Vietnam, now it is place for [Phú Thọ Indoor Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%C3%BA_Th%E1%BB%8D_Indoor_Stadium) and Phú Thọ Circus.[^cite_note-160]

The city is home to a number of association football clubs. [Ho Chi Minh City F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_F.C.) is based at Thống Nhất Stadium, formerly as *Cảng Sài Gòn*, it was four-time champions of Vietnam's [V.League 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V.League_1) (in 1986, 1993–94, 1997, and 2001–02). [Navibank Saigon F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navibank_Saigon_F.C.), founded as *Quân Khu 4*, was also based at Thống Nhất Stadium, emerged as champions of the First Division in the 2008 season, and was promoted to the V-League in 2009, the club has since been dissolved during a corruption scandal.[^cite_note-161]

In 2011, the city was awarded an expansion team for the [ASEAN Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Basketball_League).[^cite_note-162] [Saigon Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon_Heat) was the first ever international professional basketball team to represent Vietnam.[^cite_note-163] In 2016, a second professional basketball team was created, [Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City_Wings), playing in the domestic [Vietnam Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Basketball_Association). The city hosts international sport events throughout the year, such as the [AFF Futsal Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFF_Futsal_Championship) and the [Vietnam Vertical Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Vertical_Run). Other sports are represented by teams in the city, such as Irish (Gaelic) Football, rugby, cricket,[^cite_note-164] volleyball, basketball, chess, athletics, and table tennis.[^cite_note-165]
