The **Bureau of Corrections** (**BuCor** [/ˈbjʊ.kɔːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Kawanihan ng Koreksiyon*;[^cite_note-4] formerly the **Bureau of Prisons** from 1905 to 1989) is an agency of the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice_\(Philippines\)) which is charged with the custody and rehabilitation of national offenders, commonly known as Persons Deprived of Liberty (PDL), who have been [sentenced](870351.md) to three years of [imprisonment](213806.md)[^cite_note-Department_of_Justice-5] or more. The agency has its headquarters in the [New Bilibid Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Bilibid_Prison) Reservation in [Muntinlupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntinlupa).[^cite_note-6]

## History

### Spanish colonial era



Old Bilibid Prison circa 1900

The Old Bilibid Prison which was located on Oroquieta Street in [Manila](184334.md) was established in 1847 and by a [Royal Decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Decree) formally opened on April 10, 1866. On August 31, 1870, the San Ramon Prison and Penal Farm was established in [Zamboanga City](25167165.md) for Muslim and political prisoners opposed to the rule of [Spain](26667.md).

### American colonial era



National historical marker installed in 2005

The Iuhit penal Settlement now known as [Iwahig Prison and Penal Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwahig_Prison_and_Penal_Farm) was established in 1904 by the Americans in 28,072 hectares of land. The land areas expanded to 40,000 hectares in the late 1950s.[^cite_note-history-7] and expanded again to 41,007 hectares by virtue of Executive Order No. 67 issued by Governor Newton Gilbert on October 15, 1912.

The Bureau of Prisons was established on November 1, 1905 under the Reorganization Act of the [Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commission) as an agency under the Department of Commerce and Police.[^cite_note-history-7][^cite_note-agency-profile-8] The Reorganization Act also re-established the San Ramon Prison in 1907 which was destroyed during the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) in 1898. The prison was placed under the Bureau of Prisons and receive prisoners in Mindanao.[^cite_note-history-7]

The [Correctional Institution for Women](46469171.md) was founded on November 27, 1929, by virtue of Act No. 3579 as the first and only prison for women in the Philippines.[^cite_note-history-7] Later, on January 21, 1932, the bureau opened the Davao Penal Colony in Southern Mindanao.[^cite_note-history-7]

The New Bilibid Prison was established in 1935 in [Muntinlupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntinlupa) due to the increased rate of prisoners.[^cite_note-history-7]

### Contemporary era

Proclamation No. 72 issued on September 26, 1954, established the Sablayan Prison and Penal Farm in [Occidental Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occidental_Mindoro), and the Leyte Regional Prison was established on January 16, 1973, under Proclamation No. 1101.[^cite_note-history-7]

The Administrative Code of 1987 and Proclamation No. 495, issued on November 22, 1989, changed the agency's name from Bureau of Prisons to "Bureau of Corrections".[^cite_note-history-7]

President [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) signed into law on May 24, 2013, Republic Act no. 10575 or the Bureau of Corrections Act which enables the government to upgrade and professionalize the country's penal system.[^cite_note-agency-profile-8][^cite_note-9]

During the administration of President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) in the 2020s, a proposal has been made to merged the BuCor with the [Bureau of Jail Management and Penology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Jail_Management_and_Penology) to align the Philippines' penal system with its Southeast Asian neighbors.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-bigger-roles-11]

## Function

The Bureau of Corrections is an agency under the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice_\(Philippines\)). It manages prisons in the Philippines such as the [New Bilibid Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Bilibid_Prison). It shares roles with the [Bureau of Jail Management and Penology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Jail_Management_and_Penology) (BJMP) which is under the [Department of the Interior and Local Government](4718691.md) although there are proposals to merge the two bodies.[^cite_note-bigger-roles-11]

The BuCor maintains the detention of inmates or what it calls "persons deprived of liberty" (PDLs) serving sentences more than three years of imprisonment. While the BJMP deals with convicts serving sentences less than three years or suspects awaiting trial.[^cite_note-12]

## Logo



The old Bureau of Corrections Logo

The logo of the bureau represents the government agency's mandate, the rehabilitation of inmate. The logo focuses on the man in prison as the main concern of rehabilitation. It presents man behind bars, but who looks outwards with the hope of rejoining the free community. The 7 rays of the sun represent the 7 operating prisons and penal farms who carry out the reformation programs of the bureau. The color green symbolizes hope. The color orange is symbolic of happiness. The bar of justice represents the Philippine justice system.
