**Sindh**[^cite_note-10] is a [province](446017.md) of [Pakistan](23235.md), located in the [southeastern region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pakistan) of the country. It is the third-largest Pakistani province by land area and [second-largest by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Pakistan). It is bounded by the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) to the south and borders the provinces of [Balochistan](4687.md) to the west and [Punjab](24751.md) to the north; in addition to sharing an [international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_border) with the [Indian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_states) of [Gujarat](53707.md) and [Rajasthan](26291.md) to the east. [Karachi](17123.md), located along the southern coast, is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Sindh_by_population). Sindh's landscape consists mostly of [alluvial plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain) flanking the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River), the [Thar Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert_of_Sindh) in the eastern portion of the province along the international border with India, and the [Kirthar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirthar_Mountains) in the western portion along the provincial border with Balochistan.

The first [settled life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) in [South Asia](21566765.md) emerged around Sindh and Punjab 9,000 years ago along the [Indus River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River_Basin), which gradually developed into the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation). The [Aryans migrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_migrations) from [Central Asia](6742.md) after 2000 BCE as the Indus Valley Civilisation gradually declined. Around 1000 BCE, the [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryans) kingdom of [Sindhu-Sauvīra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhu-Sauv%C4%ABra) emerged in Sindh which lasted until 518 BCE when the [Achaemenids captured the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_conquest_of_the_Indus_Valley), followed by the [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_campaign_of_Alexander_the_Great), the [Mauryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryans), the [Indo-Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greeks), the [Indo-Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Scythians), the [Guptas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guptas), and finally the [Sasanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hind_\(Sasanian_province\)) in the 3rd century CE. Sindh emerged as an independent kingdom under the [Rai dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_dynasty) in the 5th century, which was succeeded by the [Chach dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chach_dynasty) in the 7th century. The [Arabs conquered Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_conquest_of_Sindh) in 712 CE under the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) and established it as a [caliphal province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_\(caliphal_province\)) — laying the foundations of the [Muslim period in South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_period_in_the_Indian_subcontinent), which lasted over a millennia.

The [Habbari Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habbari_Emirate) emerged in the 9th century which nominally pledged allegiance to the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate). Between the 11th and the 16th centuries, the [Soomra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soomra_dynasty), [Samma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samma_dynasty), and [Arghun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghun_dynasty) dynasties ruled Sindh respectively — mostly under the vassalage of the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate). The [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) captured Sindh in the late 16th century, and directly ruled it until 1701, when the [Kalhora dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalhora_dynasty) became the local rulers first under Mughal, and later [Afsharid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_Empire) and [Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire), suzerainty. The rule of the [Talpur dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talpur_dynasty) began in 1783 and lasted until 1846 when the [East India Company captured Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_conquest_of_Sindh), with a branch of the dynasty continuing to rule [Khairpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khairpur_\(princely_state\)) under British suzerainty. Initially a [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Division) in the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency), Sindh became a separate province in 1936 under the [Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League)'s [demands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points_of_Jinnah) raised by [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah). Following the passing of the [Lahore Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Resolution) in 1940, Sindh became the first province to [officially endorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Resolution_in_Sindh_assembly) the [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement), and became part of the [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan) upon [its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Pakistan\)) in 1947. It was amalgamated into [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) in 1955 until being restored to provincehood, with the inclusion of its historic territories, Khairpur and Karachi, in 1970.

The [economy of Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Sindh) is the second-largest in Pakistan; its provincial capital Karachi is the [most-populous city in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Pakistan_by_population) as well as its main financial hub. Sindh is home to a large portion of [Pakistan's industrial sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_of_Pakistan) and contains two of the country's busiest commercial seaports: [Port Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Qasim) and the [Port of Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Karachi). The remainder of Sindh consists of an [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Pakistan)-based economy and produces fruits, consumer items and vegetables for other parts of the country.[^cite_note-The_Nation,_2014-11][^cite_note-SALU_Press-12][^cite_note-Dawn_News,_2007-13]

Sindh is sometimes referred to as the *Bāb-ul Islām* (transl. 'Gateway of [Islam](6037917.md)'), or simply *Mehrān* (transl. 'Gateway'), as it was one of the first regions of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) to become a part of [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The province is well known for its [distinct culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Sindh), which is strongly influenced by [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism), an important marker of Sindhi identity for both [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_Muslims) and [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_Hindus).[^cite_note-WakabayashiKothari2009-16] Sindh is prominent for its history during the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) under the Indus Valley civilization, and is home to two [UNESCO](21786641.md)-designated [World Heritage Sites](44940.md): the [Makli Necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makli_Necropolis) and [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro).[^cite_note-17]

## Etymology

The [Greeks](42056.md) who conquered Sindh in 325 BC under [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) referred to the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River) as *[Indós](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%B8%CE%BD%CE%B4%CF%8C%CF%82#Ancient_Greek)*, hence the modern *Indus*. The [ancient Iranians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Iranians) referred to everything east of the river Indus as *hind*.[^cite_note-Now_or_Never-18][^cite_note-Ikram1995-19] The word *Sind* is a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) derivative of the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) term *Sindhu,* meaning "river," a reference to vast Indus River.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPhiroze_Vasunia20136-20]

The previous [Perso-Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perso-Arabic) spelling *Sind* (سند) was discontinued in favour of Sindh in 1988 by an amendment passed in the [Sindh Assembly](7664955.md).[^cite_note-21]

Sindh and surrounding areas contain the ruins of the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation). There are remnants of ancient cities and structures, with a notable example in Sindh being that of [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro). Built around 2500 BCE, it was one of the largest settlements of the ancient Indus Civilisation, with features such as standardized bricks, street grids, and covered sewerage systems.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] It was one of the world's earliest major cities, contemporaneous with the civilisations of [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), [Minoan Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization), and [Caral-Supe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caral-Supe_civilization). Mohenjo-daro was abandoned in the 19th century BCE as the Indus Valley Civilisation declined, and the site was not rediscovered until the 1920s. Significant excavation has since been conducted at the site of the city, which was designated a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 1980.[^cite_note-mohenjodaro.net-24] The site is currently threatened by erosion and improper restoration.[^cite_note-25] A gradual [drying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aridification) of the region during the 3rd millennium BC may have been the initial stimulus for its urbanisation.[^cite_note-26] Eventually it also reduced the water supply enough to cause the civilisation's demise and to disperse its population to the east.[^cite_note-Note-Brooke-27]

During the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), the territory of Sindh was known as [Sindhu-Sauvīra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhu-Sauv%C4%ABra), covering the lower Indus Valley,[^cite_note-Raychaudhuri2-28] with its southern border being the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) and its northern border being the [Pañjāb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) around [Multān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJain1974\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsinernetdli201572385pagen227mode2up_209\]-\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsinernetdli201572385pagen227mode2up_210\]-29] The capital of Sindhu-Sauvīra was named Roruka and Vītabhaya or Vītībhaya, and corresponds to the medieval [Arohṛ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aror) and the modern-day [Rohṛī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohri).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJain1974\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsinernetdli201572385pagen227mode2up_209\]-\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsinernetdli201572385pagen227mode2up_210\]-29][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESikdar1964501–502-30][^cite_note-Raychaudhuri-31] The [Achaemenids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) conquered the region in the 6th century BCE and established the satrapy of [Hindush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindush). The territory may have corresponded to the area covering the lower and central [Indus Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Basin) (present-day Sindh and the southern Punjab in Pakistan).[^cite_note-32] Alternatively, some authors consider that *Hindush* may have been located in the Punjab with its capital at [Taxila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxila).[^cite_note-33] These areas remained under Persian control until the [invasion by Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_campaign_of_Alexander_the_Great).[^cite_note-34]

Alexander initially appointed a native dynast [Sambus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambus_\(satrap\)) in present-day Sindh, but after the latter's rebellion replaced him with his general [Peithon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peithon_\(son_of_Agenor\)) as governor. He constructed a harbour at the city of [Patala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regio_Patalis) in Sindh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDani198137-35][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEggermont197513-36] After [a war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid%E2%80%93Mauryan_War) with [Seleucus I Nicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucus_I_Nicator), who had succeeded Alexander in Persia, [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya) forced him to cede all territories west of the Indus River in 303 BCE, while gifting him 500 elephants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThorpe200933-37]

Following a century of [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan) rule, by 180 BC the region had come under the [Indo-Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greeks), followed by the [Indo-Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Scythians), who ruled from their capital at [Minnagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnagara).[^cite_note-38] Later on, [Sasanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanians) from the reign of [Shapur I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_I) claimed control of the Sindh in their inscriptions, calling it as [Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hind_\(Sasanian_province\)).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-NS-40]

By the 5th century, Sasanian control, which in any case was tenuous as apparent from the [distinct local coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_coinage_of_Sindh), weakened further with local ruler [Ranaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranaditya_Satya) minting his own coins. The native [Rai dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_dynasty) emerged in Sindh and reigned for a period of 144 years, concurrent with the [Huna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huna_people) invasions of northern India.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWink1996133,_152–153-41] [Aror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aror) was noted to be the capital.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWink1996133,_152–153-41][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsif201665,_81–82,_131–134-42] The [Chach dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chach_dynasty) succeeded the Rai dynasty.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWink1996151-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-Gier-45][^cite_note-Naik-46] Most of the information about its existence comes from the *[Chachnama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachnama)*, a historical account of the dynasty.[^cite_note-47] After the dynasty's fall in 712, its members administered parts of Sindh under the Umayyad [wilayat of Sind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_\(caliphal_province\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWink1991152–153-48]

### Medieval period

After the death of the Islamic prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad), the Arab expansion towards the east reached Sindh beyond Persia.[^cite_note-UNESCO-49] The connection between the Sindh and Islam was established by the initial Muslim invasions during the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate). The first clash with the Rai kings of Sindh took place in 636 (15 AH) under caliph [Umar ibn al-Khattab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar_ibn_al-Khattab) with the governor of Bahrain, [Uthman ibn Abu-al-Aas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman_ibn_Abu-al-Aas), dispatching naval expeditions against [Thane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thane), [Bharuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharuch) and [Debal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debal).[^cite_note-50] [Al-Baladhuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Baladhuri) states they were victorious at Debal but does not mention the results of other two raids. However, the *Chachnama* states that the raiders of Debal were defeated and its governor killed the leader of the raids.[^cite_note-51] These raids were thought to be triggered by a later pirate attack on Umayyad ships.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETripathi1967337-52] Al-Baladhuri adds that this stopped any more incursions until the reign of [Uthman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsif201635-53] Al-Hakim ibn Jabalah al-Abdi, who attacked [Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makran) in the year 649, was an early partisan of [Ali ibn Abu Talib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali).[^cite_note-MacLean,_Derryl_N._1989_pp._1262-54] During the caliphate of Ali, a number of [Jats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jats) of the region (known in Arabic sources as [Zuṭṭ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zu%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%AD)) came under the influence of [Shi'ism](26961.md)[^cite_note-55] with some participating in the [Battle of Camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Camel) for Ali.[^cite_note-MacLean,_Derryl_N._1989_pp._1262-54] Under the Arab Umayyads (661–750), many Shi'as sought asylum in the region of Sindh, to live in relative peace in the remote area.[^cite_note-56]

In 712, [Muhammad ibn al-Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_al-Qasim) defeated the last Chach maharaja [Dahir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahir_of_Aror) who died fighting at the [Battle of Aror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aror), and annexed it to the Umayyad Caliphate. The [Habbari dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habbari_dynasty) ruled much of Sindh as a semi-independent *emirate* from 854 to 1024. Beginning with the rule of 'Umar bin Abdul Aziz al-Habbari in 854, the region became semi-independent from the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) in 861, while continuing to nominally pledge allegiance to the Abbasid Caliph in [Baghdad](4492.md).[^cite_note-Singh2-57][^cite_note-Maclean2-58] The Habbaris ruled Sindh until they were defeated by [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) [Mahmud Ghaznavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) in 1026, who then went on to destroy the old Habbari capital of [Mansura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansura,_Sindh), and annex the region to the [Ghaznavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavid), thereby ending the Arab rule in Sindh.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Later chroniclers like [Ali ibn al-Athir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_ibn_al-Athir) (c. late 12th c.) and [Ibn Khaldun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldun) (c. late 14th c.) attributed the fall of Habbarids to Mahmud of Ghazni, lending credence to the argument of Hafif being the last Habbarid.[^cite_note-Collinet-2008a-61]

The [Soomra dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soomra_dynasty) was a local Sindhi Muslim dynasty that ruled between early 11th century and the 14th century.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-IJDL-2007-63][^cite_note-Dani-2007-64] They appear to have established themselves as a regional power in the power vacuum after the fall of Habbaris.[^cite_note-Collinet-2008a-61][^cite_note-Boivin-2008a-65] The [Ghurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_dynasty) and Ghaznavids continued to rule parts of Sindh throughout the eleventh and early twelfth century alongside Soomras.[^cite_note-Collinet-2008a-61] The precise delineations are not yet known but Soomras were probably centered in lower Sindh.[^cite_note-Collinet-2008a-61] Some of them were adherents of [Isma'ilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isma'ilism).[^cite_note-Boivin-2008a-65] One of their kings Shimuddin Chamisar had submitted to [Iltutmish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iltutmish), the [Sultan of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Delhi), and was allowed to continue on as a vassal.[^cite_note-Ray201932-66]

[Makli Necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makli_Necropolis)









The [Makli Necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makli_Necropolis) at [Thatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatta) is one of the largest funerary sites in the world.

[^cite_note-67]

The [Sammas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samma_\(tribe\)) overthrew the Soomras soon after 1335. The last Soomra ruler took shelter with the governor of [Gujarat](53707.md), under the protection of [Muhammad bin Tughluq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Tughluq), the sultan of Delhi.[^cite_note-(Pakistan)Latif197622-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-guj2-70] Mohammad bin Tughlaq made an expedition against Sindh in 1351 and died at Sondha, possibly in an attempt to restore the Soomras. With this, the Sammas became independent. The next sultan, [Firuz Shah Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuz_Shah_Tughlaq) attacked Sindh in 1365 and 1367, unsuccessfully, but with reinforcements from Delhi he later obtained Banbhiniyo's surrender. For a period the Sammas were therefore subject to Delhi again. Later, as the Sultanate of Delhi collapsed they became fully independent.[^cite_note-panhwar.com22-71] Jam Unar was the founder of Samma dynasty as mentioned by [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta).[^cite_note-panhwar.com22-71] The Samma dynasty contributed significantly to the evolution of the [Indo-Islamic architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Islamic_architecture) style. The city of [Thatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatta) is famous for its necropolis of erstwhile royals, the [Makli Necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makli_Necropolis), which covers 10 square km on the [Makli Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makli_Hill).[^cite_note-72] The Sammas have left a mark on Sindh with magnificent structures in Thatta.[^cite_note-(Pakistan)Latif19763-73][^cite_note-74] They were later overthrown by the Turko-Mongol [Arghuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghun_dynasty) in the late 15th century.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-Bosworth_p._329-76]

### Modern period

Main articles: [Thatta Subah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatta_Subah) and [Sind State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_State)



Elaborately illustrated map of the Thatta Subah of the Mughal Empire, commissioned by Jean Baptiste Joseph Gentil, ca.1770

In the late 16th century, Sindh was annexed into the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) by [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar), himself born in the [Sodha kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Amarkot) in [Umerkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umerkot) in Sindh.[^cite_note-Cambridge-77][^cite_note-78] In 1591–1593, Akbar sent an army to conquer lower Sindh from the [Tarkhan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarkhan_dynasty) after defeating the last Tarkhan ruler, Mirza Jani Beg; Jani Beg and his son [Mirza Ghazi Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Ghazi_Beg).[^cite_note-Davies,_p._628-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-SS-81]

Mughal rule from their provincial capital of Thatta was to last in lower Sindh until the early 18th century, while upper Sindh was ruled by the indigenous [Kalhora dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalhora_dynasty) holding power, consolidating their rule from their capital of [Khudabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khudabad), before shifting to [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_Sindh) from 1768 onwards.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

The [Talpurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talpur_dynasty) succeeded the Kalhoras and four branches of the dynasty were established.[^cite_note-85] One ruled lower Sindh from the city of [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_Sindh), another ruled over upper Sindh from the city of [Khairpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khairpur), a third ruled around the eastern city of [Mirpur Khas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur_Khas), and a fourth was based in [Tando Muhammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tando_Muhammad_Khan). They were ethnically [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people),[^cite_note-Solomon-2006-86] and for most of their rule, they were subordinate to the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) and were forced to pay tribute to them.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

They ruled from 1783, until 1843, when they were in turn defeated by the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) at the [Battle of Miani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Miani) and [Battle of Dubbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dubba).[^cite_note-Talpur-2002-89] The northern Khairpur branch of the Talpur dynasty, however, continued to maintain a degree of sovereignty during British rule as the [princely state of Khairpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khairpur_\(princely_state\)),[^cite_note-Solomon-2006-86] whose ruler elected to join the new [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan) in October 1947 as an autonomous region, before being fully amalgamated into [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) in 1955.

#### British Raj

See also: [Sind Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Division) and [Sind Province (1936–1955)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Province_\(1936%E2%80%931955\))



Sindh became part of the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency) in 1909.

The British conquered Sindh in 1843. General [Charles Napier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_James_Napier) is said to have reported victory to the Governor General with a one-word telegram, namely *"[Peccavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peccavi)"* – or *"I have sinned"* ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin)).[^cite_note-90] The British had two objectives in their rule of Sindh: the consolidation of British rule and the use of Sindh as a market for British products and a source of revenue and raw materials. With the appropriate infrastructure in place, the British hoped to utilise Sindh for its economic potential.[^cite_note-LongSingh2015-91] The British incorporated Sindh, some years later after annexing it, into the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency). Distance from the provincial capital, Bombay, led to grievances that Sindh was neglected in contrast to other parts of the Presidency. The merger of Sindh into Punjab province was considered from time to time but was turned down because of British disagreement and Sindhi opposition, both from Muslims and Hindus, to being annexed to Punjab.[^cite_note-LongSingh2015-91]

Later, desire for a separate administrative status for Sindh grew. At the annual session of the Indian National Congress in 1913, a Sindhi Hindu put forward the demand for Sindh's separation from the Bombay Presidency on the grounds of Sindh's unique cultural character. This reflected the desire of Sindh's predominantly Hindu commercial class to free itself from competing with the more powerful Bombay's business interests.[^cite_note-LongSingh2015-91] Meanwhile, Sindhi politics was characterised in the 1920s by the growing importance of Karachi and the [Khilafat Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khilafat_Movement).[^cite_note-Malik1999-92] A number of Sindhi [pirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_\(Sufism\)), descendants of Sufi saints who had proselytised in Sindh, joined the Khilafat Movement, which propagated the protection of the Ottoman Caliphate, and those pirs who did not join the movement found a decline in their following.[^cite_note-Minault1982-93] The pirs generated huge support for the Khilafat cause in Sindh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsari199277-94] Sindh came to be at the forefront of the Khilafat Movement.[^cite_note-Society2007-95]

Although Sindh was less [sectarian](648554.md) than other parts of India, the province's Muslim elite and emerging Muslim middle class demanded separation of Sindh from Bombay Presidency as a safeguard for their own interests. In this campaign, local Sindhi Muslims identified 'Hindu' with Bombay instead of Sindh. Sindhi Hindus were seen as representing the interests of Bombay instead of the majority of Sindhi Muslims. Sindhi Hindus, for the most part, opposed the separation of Sindh from Bombay.[^cite_note-LongSingh2015-91] Although Sindh had a culture of religious syncretism, communal harmony and tolerance due to Sindh's strong [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) culture in which both Sindhi Muslims and Sindhi Hindus partook,[^cite_note-auto-96] both the Muslim landed elite, *waderas*, and the Hindu commercial elements, *[banias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bania_\(caste\))*, collaborated in oppressing the predominantly Muslim peasantry of Sindh who were economically exploited.[^cite_note-Jalal20022-97] Sindhi Muslims eventually demanded the separation of Sindh from the Bombay Presidency, a move opposed by Sindhi Hindus.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsari199277-94][^cite_note-LongSingh20152-98][^cite_note-Society20072-99]

In Sindh's first provincial election after its separation from Bombay in 1936, economic interests were an essential factor of politics informed by religious and cultural issues.[^cite_note-Jalal2002-100] Due to British policies, much land in Sindh was transferred from Muslim to Hindu hands over the decades.[^cite_note-SinghIyer2016-101] Religious tensions rose in Sindh over the Sukkur Manzilgah issue where Muslims and Hindus disputed over an abandoned mosque in proximity to an area sacred to Hindus. The Sindh Muslim League exploited the issue and agitated for the return of the mosque to Muslims. Consequentially, a thousand members of the Muslim League were imprisoned. Eventually, due to panic the government restored the mosque to Muslims.[^cite_note-Jalal2002-100] The separation of Sindh from Bombay Presidency triggered Sindhi Muslim nationalists to support the Pakistan Movement. Even while the Punjab and North-West Frontier Province were ruled by parties hostile to the Muslim League, Sindh remained loyal to Jinnah.[^cite_note-Ahmed2016-102] Although the prominent Sindhi Muslim nationalist [G. M. Syed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._M._Syed) left the All India Muslim League in the mid-1940s and his relationship with Jinnah never improved, the overwhelming majority of Sindhi Muslims supported the creation of Pakistan, seeing in it their deliverance.[^cite_note-Malik1999-92] Sindhi support for the Pakistan Movement arose from the desire of the Sindhi Muslim business class to drive out their Hindu competitors.[^cite_note-Kukreja2003-103] The Muslim League's rise to becoming the party with the strongest support in Sindh was in large part linked to its winning over of the religious pir families.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsari1992115-104] Although the Muslim League had previously fared poorly in the 1937 elections in Sindh, when local Sindhi Muslim parties won more seats,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsari1992115-104] the Muslim League's cultivation of support from local pirs in 1946 helped it gain a foothold in the province,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsari1992122-105] it didn't take long for the overwhelming majority of Sindhi Muslims to campaign for the creation of Pakistan.[^cite_note-Malik19992-106][^cite_note-Kukreja20032-107]

#### Partition (1947)

In 1947, violence did not constitute a major part of the Sindhi partition experience, unlike in Punjab. There were very few incidents of violence on Sindh, in part due to the Sufi-influenced culture of religious tolerance and in part that Sindh was not divided and was instead made part of Pakistan in its entirety. Sindhi Hindus who left generally did so out of a fear of persecution, rather than persecution itself, because of the arrival of Muslim refugees from India. Sindhi Hindus differentiated between the local Sindhi Muslims and the migrant Muslims from India. A large number of Sindhi Hindus travelled to India by sea, to the ports of Bombay, Porbandar, Veraval and Okha.[^cite_note-108]

### Post independence

In the early years, Karachi acted as the capital of Pakistan with the provincial capital being relocated to Hyderabad. In 1955, all provinces, including Sindh, were abolished and merged to form [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) as a counterweight to the demographically superior [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan). This produced grievances in the other regions and finally, in 1970, old provinces were re-established. In 1960, capital was transferred to the newly built [Islamabad](51153.md) with Karachi being returned to the province in 1970. In the 1970 elections, [Z. A. Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z._A._Bhutto) won in Sindh and Punjab, later serving as prime minister of the country from 1973 to 1977. His [Pakistan People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_People's_Party) (PPP) has continued to heavily dominate the politics of Sindh since 1970s. The import of the so-called [Kalashnikov culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalashnikov_culture) following the [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in 1979 and the influx of [Afghan refugees](3657017.md) deepened the already felt ethnic tensions in Sindh. The rise of [MQM militancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQM_militancy) gripped Karachi in the 1990s and 2000s and caused severe human and economic loss to the city. Since 2008, PPP has led the provincial government.

### Religion

See also: [Sufism in Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism_in_Sindh) and [Hinduism in Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Sindh)

4. [Ahmaddiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmaddiya) (0.03%)

Islam in Sindh has a long history, starting with the capture of Sindh by Muhammad Bin Qasim in 712. Over time, the majority of the population in Sindh converted to Islam, especially in rural areas. Today, Muslims make up 90% of the population, and are more dominant in urban than rural areas. Islam in Sindh has a strong Sufi ethos with numerous Muslim saints and mystics, such as the Sufi poet [Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abdul_Latif_Bhittai), having lived in Sindh historically. One popular legend that highlights the strong Sufi presence in Sindh is that 125,000 Sufi saints and mystics are buried on [Makli Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makli_Hill) near [Thatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatta).[^cite_note-121] The development of Sufism in Sindh was similar to the development of Sufism in other parts of the Muslim world. In the sixteenth century two Sufi *tareeqat* (orders) – Qadria and Naqshbandia – were introduced in Sindh.[^cite_note-122] [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) continues to play an important role in the daily lives of Sindhis.[^cite_note-123]

In 1941, the last census conducted prior to the partition of India, the total population of Sindh was 4,840,795 out of which 3,462,015 (71.5%) were [Muslims](19541.md), 1,279,530 (26.4%) were [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) and the remaining were Tribals, [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs), [Christians](18337522.md), [Parsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsis), [Jains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism), [Jews](25955086.md), and [Buddhists](3267529.md).[^cite_note-sindh1941-110]<sup>: 28 </sup>[^cite_note-124]

Sindh also has Pakistan's highest percentage of [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Pakistan) overall, accounting for 8.8% of the population, roughly around 4.9 million people,[^cite_note-2017_Census-125] and 13.3% of the province's rural population as per 2023 Pakistani census report. These numbers also include the [scheduled caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) population, which stands at 1.7% of the total in Sindh (or 3.1% in rural areas),[^cite_note-religioninpakistan-126] and is believed to have been under-reported, with some community members instead counted under the main Hindu category.[^cite_note-127] Although, [Pakistan Hindu Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Hindu_Council) claimed that there are 6,842,526 Hindus living in Sindh Province covering around 14.29% of the region's population.[^cite_note-128] Umerkot district in the Thar Desert is Pakistan's only Hindu-majority district. The [Shri Ramapir Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramapir_Temple_Tando_Allahyar) in Tandoallahyar whose annual festival is the second largest Hindu pilgrimage in Pakistan is in Sindh.[^cite_note-129] Sindh is also the only province in Pakistan to have a separate law for governing [Hindu marriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_marriage_laws_in_Pakistan).[^cite_note-Shahid-130]

2020 community estimates indicated the Sikh population in Sindh stood at approximately 10,000,[^cite_note-Tunio-131] while the 2023 census indicated a population of 5,182 Sikhs.[^cite_note-sindh2023-132]

## Geography and nature



Peninsula of Manora

Sindh is in the western corner of South Asia, bordering the [Iranian plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau) in the west. Geographically it is the third largest province of Pakistan, stretching about 579 kilometres (360 mi) from north to south and 442 kilometres (275 mi) (extreme) or 281 kilometres (175 mi) (average) from east to west, with an area of 140,915 square kilometres (54,408 sq mi) of Pakistani territory. Sindh is bounded by the [Thar Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert) to the east, the [Kirthar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirthar_Mountains) to the west and the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) and [Rann of Kutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rann_of_Kutch) to the south. In the centre is a fertile plain along the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River).

Sindh is divided into three main geographical regions: [Siro (Region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siro_\(Region\)) ("upper country"), aka Upper Sindh, which is above [Sehwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sehwan), [Vicholo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichol_\(Region\)) ("middle country"), or Middle Sindh, from Sehwan to [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_Sindh), and [Lāṟu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larr_\(Region\)) or Larr ("sloping, descending country"), or Lower Sindh, mostly consisting of the [Indus Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Delta) below Hyderabad.[^cite_note-Haig_1894-154]

### Flora

Sindhri mangoes is among top 10 mango varieties in the world

[Sindhri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhri) is among top 10 [mango varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango_varieties) in the world.

[^cite_note-155]

The province is mostly arid with scant vegetation except for the irrigated Indus Valley. The dwarf palm, *Acacia rupestris* (kher), and *[Tecomella undulata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecomella_undulata)* ([lohirro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohida)) trees are typical of the western hill region. In the Indus valley, the *[Acacia nilotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia_nilotica)* (babul) (babbur) is the most dominant and occurs in thick forests along the Indus banks. The *[Azadirachta indica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadirachta_indica)* (neem) (nim), *Zizyphys vulgaris* (bir) (ber), *Tamarix orientalis* (jujuba lai) and *[Capparis aphylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capparis_aphylla)* (kirir) are among the more common trees.

Mango, date palms and the more recently introduced banana, guava, orange and [chiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiku) are the typical fruit-bearing trees. The coastal strip and the creeks abound in semi-aquatic and aquatic plants and the inshore Indus delta islands have forests of *Avicennia tomentosa* (timmer) and *[Ceriops candolleana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceriops_tagal)* (chaunir) trees. Water lilies grow in abundance in the numerous lakes and ponds, particularly in the lower Sindh region.

### Fauna

Main article: [Fauna of Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Sindh)

Among the wild [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal), the [Sindh ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh_ibex) (sareh), [blackbuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbuck), wild sheep ([Urial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urial) or gadh) and [wild bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_black_bear) are found in the western rocky range. The [leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard) is now rare and the [Asiatic cheetah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_cheetah) extinct. The [Pirrang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_cat) (large tiger cat or fishing cat) of the eastern desert region is also disappearing. [Deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkara) occur in the lower rocky plains and in the eastern region, as do the [Striped hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena) (charakh), [jackal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal), [fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox), [porcupine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcupine), [common gray mongoose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongoose) and [hedgehog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedgehog). The Sindhi phekari, red lynx or Caracal cat, is found in some areas. Phartho (hog deer) and wild bear occur, particularly in the central inundation belt. There are bats, lizards and reptiles, including the cobra, lundi (viper) and the mysterious Sindh [krait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krait) of the Thar region, which is supposed to suck the victim's breath in his sleep. Some unusual sightings of Asian cheetah occurred in 2003 near the [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_\(Pakistan\)) border in [Kirthar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirthar_Mountains). The rare [Houbara bustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houbara_bustard) finds Sindh's warm climate suitable to rest and mate. Unfortunately, it is hunted by locals and foreigners.

[Crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile) are rare and inhabit only the backwaters of the Indus, eastern Nara channel and Karachi backwater. Besides a large variety of marine fish, the plumbeous [dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin), the beaked dolphin, rorqual or blue whale and skates frequent the seas along the Sindh coast. The Pallo (Sable fish), a marine fish, ascends the Indus annually from February to April to spawn. The [Indus river dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_river_dolphin) is among the most endangered species in Pakistan and is found in the part of the Indus river in northern Sindh. [Hog deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_hog_deer) and [wild bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_black_bear) occur, particularly in the central inundation belt.

Although Sindh has a [semi arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi_arid) climate, through its coastal and riverine forests, its huge fresh water lakes and mountains and deserts, Sindh supports a large amount of varied wildlife. Due to the semi-arid [climate of Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Sindh) the left out forests support an average population of jackals and snakes. The [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks) established by the Government of Pakistan in collaboration with many organizations such as [World Wide Fund for Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature) and [Sindh Wildlife Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh_Wildlife_Department) support a huge variety of animals and birds. The [Kirthar National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirthar_National_Park) in the Kirthar range spreads over more than 3000 km<sup>2</sup> of desert, stunted tree forests and a lake. The KNP supports [Sindh ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh_ibex), [wild sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_sheep) (urial) and [black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_black_bear) along with the rare leopard. There are also occasional sightings of The Sindhi phekari, ped lynx or Caracal cat. There is a project to introduce [tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger) and [Asian elephants](379035.md) too in KNP near the huge Hub Dam Lake. Between July and November when the [monsoon](57630.md) winds blow onshore from the ocean, giant [olive ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_ridley) turtles lay their eggs along the seaward side. The turtles are protected species. After the mothers lay and leave them buried under the sands the SWD and WWF officials take the eggs and protect them until they are hatched to keep them from predators.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Sindh)



Lansdowne Railway Bridge

Sindh lies in a [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) to [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical) region; it is hot in the summer and mild to warm in winter. Temperatures frequently rise above 46 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (115 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)) between May and August, and the minimum average temperature of 2 °C (36 °F) occurs during December and January in the northern and higher elevated regions. The annual rainfall averages about seven inches, falling mainly during July and August. The southwest monsoon wind begins in mid-February and continues until the end of September, whereas the cool northerly wind blows during the winter months from October to January.

Sindh lies between the two [monsoons](57630.md)—the southwest monsoon from the Indian Ocean and the northeast or retreating monsoon, deflected towards it by the [Himalayan mountains](37966.md)—and escapes the influence of both. The region's scarcity of rainfall is compensated by the inundation of the Indus twice a year, caused by the spring and summer melting of Himalayan snow and by rainfall in the monsoon season.

Sindh is divided into three climatic regions: Siro (the upper region, centred on [Jacobabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobabad)), Wicholo (the middle region, centred on [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_Sindh)), and Lar (the lower region, centred on [Karachi](17123.md)). The [thermal equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_equator) passes through upper Sindh, where the air is generally very dry. Central Sindh's temperatures are generally lower than those of upper Sindh but higher than those of lower Sindh. Dry hot days and cool nights are typical during the summer. Central Sindh's maximum temperature typically reaches 43–44 °C (109–111 °F). Lower Sindh has a damper and humid maritime climate affected by the southwestern winds in summer and northeastern winds in winter, with lower [rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainfall) than Central Sindh. Lower Sindh's maximum temperature reaches about 35–38 °C (95–100 °F). In the Kirthar range at 1,800 m (5,900 ft) and higher at [Gorakh Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorakh_Hill) and other peaks in [Dadu District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadu_District), temperatures near freezing have been recorded and brief snowfall is received in the winters.

The [Provincial Assembly of Sindh](7664955.md) is a [unicameral](204304.md) and consists of 168 seats, of which 5% are reserved for non-Muslims and 17% for women. The provincial capital of Sindh is [Karachi](17123.md). The [provincial government](3584314.md) is led by [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Sindh) who is directly elected by the [popular and landslide votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Pakistan); the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Sindh) serves as a ceremonial representative nominated and appointed by the [President of Pakistan](419460.md). The administrative boss of the province who is in charge of the bureaucracy is the [Chief Secretary Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Secretary_Sindh), who is appointed by the [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md). Most of the influential Sindhi tribes in the province are involved in [Pakistan's politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_families_of_Pakistan).

In addition, Sindh's politics leans towards the [left-wing](18499.md) and its political culture serves as a dominant place for the [left-wing spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_Pakistan) in the country.[^cite_note-Rug_Pandits,_Yasir-161] The province's trend towards the [Pakistan Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Peoples_Party) (PPP) and away from the [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)) can be seen in nationwide [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Pakistan), in which Sindh is a stronghold of the PPP.[^cite_note-Rug_Pandits,_Yasir-161] The PML(N) has a limited support due to its [centre-right agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Pakistan).[^cite_note-News_International-162]

In metropolitan cities such as [Karachi](17123.md) and [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_Sindh), the [MQM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muttahida_Qaumi_Movement_%E2%80%93_Pakistan) (another party of the left with the support of [*Muhajirs*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Muslims_in_Pakistan)) has a considerable vote bank and support.[^cite_note-Rug_Pandits,_Yasir-161] Minor leftist parties such as the [People's Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_Tehreek) also found support in rural areas of the province.[^cite_note-Tanqeed,_Sodhar-163]

### Divisions

Main article: [Divisions of Sindh, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Sindh,_Pakistan)

In 2008, after the public elections, the new government decided to restore the structure of Divisions of all provinces.[^cite_note-164] In Sindh after the lapse of the Local Governments Bodies term in 2010 the Divisional Commissioners system was to be restored.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]

In July 2011, following excessive [violence in the city of Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2011_Karachi_target_killings) and after the political split between the ruling PPP and the majority party in Sindh, the MQM and after the resignation of the MQM Governor of Sindh, PPP and the Government of Sindh decided to restore the commissionerate system in the province. As a consequence, the five divisions of Sindh were restored – namely Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur, Mirpurkhas and Larkana with their respective districts. Subsequently, a new division was added in Sindh, the Nawab Shah/Shaheed Benazirabad division.[^cite_note-168]

## Culture

Main article: [Sindhi culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_culture)



Children in a rural area of Sindh, 2012



[Sant Nenuram Ashram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Nenuram_Ashram)

The rich culture, art and architectural landscape of Sindh have fascinated historians. The culture, folktales, art and music of Sindh form a mosaic of human history.[^cite_note-187]

### Cultural heritage



Archaeological ruins at Moenjodaro, Sindh, Pakistan



The ruins of an ancient mosque at [Bhambore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhambore)



Sindhi women collecting water from a reservoir on the way to Mubarak Village

The work of Sindhi artisans was sold in ancient markets of Damascus, Baghdad, Basra, Istanbul, Cairo and Samarkand. Referring to the lacquer work on wood locally known as Jandi, T. Posten (an English traveller who visited Sindh in the early 19th century) asserted that the articles of Hala could be compared with exquisite specimens of China. Technological improvements such as the spinning wheel ([charkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charkha_\(spinning_wheel\))) and treadle (pai-chah) in the weaver's loom were gradually introduced and the processes of designing, dyeing and printing by block were refined. The refined, lightweight, colourful, washable fabrics from Hala became a luxury for people used to the woollens and linens of the age.[^cite_note-188]

Non-governmental organisations (NGOs) such as the World Wildlife Fund, Pakistan, play an important role to promote the culture of Sindh. They provide training to women artisans in Sindh so they get a source of income. They promote their products under the name of "Crafts Forever". Many women in rural Sindh are skilled in the production of caps. Sindhi caps are manufactured commercially on a small scale at New Saeedabad and Hala New. Sindhi people began celebrating Sindhi Topi Day on 6 December 2009, to preserve the historical culture of Sindh by wearing Ajrak and Sindhi topi.[^cite_note-189]

## Tourism

This section is an excerpt from [Tourism in Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Sindh).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tourism_in_Sindh\&action=edit)]



[Mazar-e-Quaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-e-Quaid) in [Karachi](17123.md)

[Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Sindh) is a province in [Pakistan](23235.md).

The province includes a number of important historical sites. The [Indus Valley civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilization) (IVC) was a [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization) (mature period 2600–1900 BC) which was centred mostly in the Sindh.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Sindh_tribune.com.pk-190] Sindh has numerous tourist sites with the most prominent being the ruins of [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro) near the city of [Larkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larkana).[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Sindh_tribune.com.pk-190] Islamic architecture is quite prominent as well as colonial and post-partition sites. Natural sites, like [Manchar Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchar_Lake) have increasingly been a source of [sustainable tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_tourism) in the province.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Sindh_\:0-191]
