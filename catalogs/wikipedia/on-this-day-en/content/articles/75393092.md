In the [Gaza war](74998519.md), as part of the [bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Gaza) and [invasion of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_invasion_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) has conducted numerous airstrikes in densely populated [Palestinian refugee camps](400347.md) in both the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) and [West Bank](33209.md).[^cite_note-1]

## Gaza Strip

### Al-Mawasi

On 4 December 2024, the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) executed two sequential strikes on al-Mawasi beginning prior to 19\:20 [PSST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_the_State_of_Palestine). The attacks ignited numerous makeshift tents housing [displaced civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_evacuations), prompting immediate responses from [Palestinian Civil Defence](49984885.md) crews who attempted to control the fires while conducting rescue operations. Many displaced civilians were still in their tents when the resulting fire began to spread.[^cite_note-\:04-2][^cite_note-\:12-3]

The death toll progressively increased throughout the afternoon. Initial reports indicated 20 fatalities, with Civil Defense spokespersons confirming that children were among the victims. The attacks also resulted in dozens of injuries, with medical teams being overwhelmed in treating severely wounded survivors due to [limited medical resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Gaza_Strip_healthcare_collapse) and insufficient staff. As a result, many initial survivors died due to [severe burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn) or from severe injuries caused by the explosions.[^cite_note-\:04-2][^cite_note-\:12-3]

The attacks caused severe damage to a wide array of refugee shelters and accommodations, with many being completely destroyed.[^cite_note-\:04-2]

### Jabaliya

Main article: [Attacks on Jabalia refugee camp (2023–present)](75014301.md)

#### 9–22 October



Mass graves found in Jabalia camp

Following Israeli airstrikes on other areas of Gaza, internally displaced Gazans fled to the [Jabaliya camp](18169472.md).[^cite_note-\:0-4] At the time of the attack, the market was full, with customers and vendors stocking up on goods.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The airstrike hit the al-Trans area of the Jabalia market, one of the most populated areas of Jabaliya.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:2-7] Survivors of the attack, speaking to [France 24](1263244.md), stated that the airstrike "hit in the heart of the market", and in the immediate aftermath, there were many dead. Many of the bodies were unable to be recovered in the days following the airstrike, due to a lack of equipment.[^cite_note-8]

A rescue worker speaking to the *New York Times* stated that the death toll was over sixty, and the entire market and surrounding buildings were destroyed.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Gaza's health ministry declined to give a full estimate, but reported "dozens" of dead and wounded.[^cite_note-9] Gaza's interior ministry also claimed that the airstrike initially targeted a residential building belonging to the Abu Eshkayyah family.[^cite_note-10]

The Israeli government claimed that the Jabalia airstrike targeted elements of Hamas located in a mosque in the Jabalia camp.[^cite_note-11] [Amnesty International](18947898.md) investigated the airstrike in its report on the [Gaza genocide](75668427.md). The report said there was no evidence of a military objective and that the "Israeli military made contradictory remarks and failed to substantiate them." The Amnesty report concluded the strike was a likely war crime.[^cite_note-12]

On 12 October, a second airstrike on the Jabalia camp hit a residential building, destroying several apartments, and killing people from two families.[^cite_note-13] Gaza's Interior Ministry reported 45 people were killed, and at least four others injured.[^cite_note-14] Some were sheltering there after being displaced from [Beit Hanoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Hanoun).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

On 19 October, the camp was hit by a third airstrike, killing 18 refugees.[^cite_note-17] On 22 October, the camp was bombed for a fourth time during a particularly intense bombardment of Gaza. Multiple homes were bombed and at least 30 bodies have been recovered from the rubble.[^cite_note-18]

#### 31 October

Main article: [31 October 2023 Jabalia refugee camp airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_October_2023_Jabalia_refugee_camp_airstrike)

On 31 October, the camp was bombed again by Israeli fighter jets.[^cite_note-19] The IDF said the attack was meant to target a key leader of the [7 October attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamas_attack_on_Israel) Ibrahim Biari, as well as a "vast underground tunnel complex" beneath the camp that according to the IDF Biari was commanding operations from.[^cite_note-20] Hamas denied the presence of any commander and said Israel was using these claims as an excuse for the attack.[^cite_note-\:03-21]

&#x20;An eyewitness interviewed by CNN spoke of "apocalyptic scenes":&#x20;

> "Children were carrying other injured children and running, with grey dust filling the air. Bodies were hanging on the rubble, many of them unrecognized. Some were bleeding and others were burnt. ... I saw women screaming and confused. They didn't know whether to cry for losing their children or run and look for them, especially since many children were playing in the neighborhood."[^cite_note-CNN1031-22]

Atef Abu Seif, Minister of Culture of the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) of President [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md) and a well-known critic of Hamas, likewise spoke of "apocalyptic" scenes to *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)*, saying more than 50 houses were "smashed, crushed. In each house there were dozens of people, families and relatives who had fled here from outside because their areas were bombed ... They bombed the center, the heart of the refugee camp. No place in all of Palestine is probably as densely populated as this. Now we can't even make out where which building began and ended."[^cite_note-23]

*[Al Jazeera](30204052.md)* reporter Anas Al Shareef was on the scene, stating, "It's a massive massacre. It is hard to count the number of buildings that have been destroyed here."[^cite_note-24] Nebal Farsakh, a spokesperson for the [Palestinian Red Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Red_Crescent), described the scene as "absolutely horrific".[^cite_note-25] More than a hundred people were reported missing beneath the rubble.[^cite_note-26] The [Gaza Interior Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Minister_of_the_Gaza_Strip) stated the camp had been "completely destroyed", with preliminary estimates of about 400 wounded or dead.[^cite_note-27] The director of the [Indonesia Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_Hospital) reported more than 50 dead.[^cite_note-28]

IDF spokesperson [Daniel Hagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Hagari) confirmed that Israeli fighter jets attacked the refugee camp.[^cite_note-29] IDF spokesman [Richard Hecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hecht) described civilian deaths as a tragic consequence of war and accused Hamas of using the local population as human shields, noting that civilians had been warned to move south.[^cite_note-CNN1031-22]

#### 1–2 November

Less than a day after the 31 October airstrike which killed at least 50 people, Jabalia was bombed again.[^cite_note-30] The Civil Defense in Gaza described the airstrike as a "second massacre" which destroyed several buildings surrounding the camp and killed at least 80 people and wounded hundreds more.[^cite_note-31] The IDF said the strike killed "the head of \[Hamas's] anti-tank missile unit, Muhammad A'sar".[^cite_note-32]

On 2 November, another airstrike in the Jabalia refugee camp hit the [UNRWA](32270.md)-sponsored Abu Hussein school, home to many displaced Gazans.[^cite_note-33]

On 4 November, a UNRWA spokeswoman confirmed reports that Israel had conducted another airstrike against a UN-run school in the Jabalia refugee camp.[^cite_note-ReutersUNSchool1104-34] According to the Gaza health ministry, [the attack on the Al-Fakhoura school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fakhoora_school_airstrike) killed fifteen and wounded dozens more.[^cite_note-ReutersUNSchool1104-34] Reuters reported having obtained a video of a boy crying in despair: "I was standing here when three bombings happened, I carried a body and another decapitated body with my own hands. God will take my vengeance."[^cite_note-ReutersUNSchool1104-34] According to UNRWA, at least one strike hit the schoolyard where displaced families had set up their tents.[^cite_note-ReutersUNSchool1104-34] In response to the strike, *[Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English)* remarked Israel was "trying to eliminate all sources of survival for the civilian population to force the evacuation to the southern part of Gaza."[^cite_note-35]

A second airstrike on the school occurred in the early hours of the morning on 18 November. Journalists on the scene reported dead bodies everywhere, suggesting the strike may have been an Israeli message to civilians to flee to the southern Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-Dozens_killed_as_Israeli_forces_attack-36] The second strike killed at least 50 people.[^cite_note-Hamas_Health_Ministry_Says-37] A video clip surfacing following what has been described as a "massacre" depicts a man walking through several rooms where dozens of corpses can be seen, and distress can be heard throughout the school.[^cite_note-38]

#### 13–23 November

Further information: [Abu Hussein School airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hussein_School_airstrike)

On 13 November, Israel bombed the camp, destroying twelve houses and killing more than 30 people.[^cite_note-Responders_cannot_reach-39] The civil defence team reported being unable to rescue injured people from the rubble due to a lack of equipment.[^cite_note-Responders_cannot_reach-39] On 14 November, Israeli airstrikes killed volleyball players Hassan Zuaiter and [Ibrahim Qusaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Qusaya) at the camp.[^cite_note-40] On 17 November, Israel hit several residential buildings, killing and wounding an unknown number.[^cite_note-41] Residents and rescue workers reportedly used axes, hammers, and their bare hands to try to find survivors.[^cite_note-42] On 23 November, an Israeli [airstrike on the Abu Hussein School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hussein_School_airstrike) killed at least 27 people, according to the Gaza Health Ministry.[^cite_note-43]

#### December

On 5 December, the IDF stated they had the entire Jabalia camp surrounded.[^cite_note-44] On 6 December, airstrikes on the camp reportedly intensified.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

### Al-Shati

Main articles: [Al-Shati refugee camp airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shati_refugee_camp_airstrike) and [July 2024 al-Shati refugee camp attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2024_al-Shati_refugee_camp_attack)

#### 2023

On 9 October 2023, during the [Gaza war](74998519.md), the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) conducted an airstrike on [al-Shati refugee camp](16296150.md) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), destroying four mosques. According to Palestinian media, the attack killed people inside. The camp is Gaza's third-largest refugee camp, with a population of more than 90,000 refugees.[^cite_note-48] A second strike was conducted on 12 October, killing 13 people.[^cite_note-oct-12-49]

The al-Gharbi mosque, Yassin mosque, and al-Sousi mosque were totally destroyed according to satellite footage, with local news reporting an unspecified number of people killed inside.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] The [Palestinian Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Ministry_of_Health) described the situation as "a massacre".[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

#### 2024

Ten people were killed in an airstrike on 29 January.[^cite_note-56] Two people were killed and four wounded in an airstrike on 24 February.[^cite_note-57] By 6 March 2024, aerial footage showed that the Shati camp, which had been one of the world's most densely populated areas before the war, was in complete ruins.[^cite_note-58] Several casualties were reported following an Israeli bombing of the camp on 22 March.[^cite_note-59] [Wafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafa) reported at least four people were killed by an Israeli attack on 18 April.[^cite_note-60] The government media office stated 24 were killed by an air raid on 22 June 2024.[^cite_note-61] An Israeli drone strike killed five children on 13 October 2024, according to Gaza's civil defence agency.[^cite_note-62] On 19 October 2024, an Israeli airstrike killed at least 73 people at the Asmaa School, according to Gaza's Civil Defense.[^cite_note-63] On 7 November, an Israeli airstrike targeted a school for displaced people, killing 12 people.[^cite_note-64] On 16 November, 10 people were killed and at least 20 others were injured by an Israeli strike on a UN-run school.[^cite_note-65] On 16 December, five Palestinian civilians were killed and 15 others were injured by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-66] On 20 December, another Israeli airstrike killed at least 10 people.[^cite_note-67]

#### 2025

On 15 January 2025, three Palestinians were killed by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-68] On 1 April, three Palestinian civilians were killed and several others injured by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-69]

On 19 June, an Israeli drone attacked a makeshift tent at the camp, killing at least 13 Palestinians.[^cite_note-70] On 9 July, ten people, including two children, were killed and at least 30 others were injured when Israeli forces attacked the Jouda family home in west of the refugee camp.[^cite_note-71] On 12 July, at 11 people were killed and more than 40 others were injured by Israeli attacks in Al-Balakhia, west of the refugee camp.[^cite_note-72] On 15 July, nine people including five children were killed while 25 others were injured in an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-73] On 22 July, Israeli forces targeted tents in the refugee camp, killing 16 people and injuring more than 25 others.[^cite_note-74] On 7 August, three people were killed after Israeli forces targeted a residential apartment. [^cite_note-renamed_from_20250807_on_20250808015939-75] On 6 September, six people were killed in an Israeli attack on a house in the camp.[^cite_note-76] On 27 September, four people were killed by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-euronews-77]

### Al-Bureij

#### October – December 2023

An airstrike by Israeli forces on the [Al-Bureij refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bureij_refugee_camp) on 17 October 2023 killed at least 12 people and injured dozens.[^cite_note-78]

On 2 November 2023, a residential building in the camp was bombed, killing 15 people.[^cite_note-79] On 5 November 2023, residential structures in the camp were bombed again, killing at least 20 people.[^cite_note-80]

A drone bombing killed two people on 22 December.[^cite_note-81] Four were killed in a 23 December bombing.[^cite_note-82]

#### January – May 2024

Attacks reported intensified on 5 January 2024.[^cite_note-83] A bombing on 1 March killed four people.[^cite_note-84] An airstrike on 14 March killed nine people from a single family.[^cite_note-85] At least two people were killed by an Israeli airstrike on 27 March, with the Palestinian Red Crescent stating it had recovered one body and three wounded people.[^cite_note-86] Local media reported an Israeli strike killed four people on 31 May 2024.[^cite_note-87]

#### June – December 2024

Three people were reportedly killed by an airstrike on 3 June 2024.[^cite_note-88] The camp underwent "constant bombardment" in June.[^cite_note-89] Six people were killed by an Israeli bombing on 8 June.[^cite_note-90] Nine people were killed by an Israeli strike on the camp on 16 June.[^cite_note-91] Nine people, including five children, were reportedly killed in an Israeli attack on 9 July.[^cite_note-92] In July 2024, nine men were reportedly killed while attempting to transport the deceased from Al-Bureij to Nuseirat.[^cite_note-93] On 8 August, at least 12 people, including children were killed when an Israeli fighter jet targeted a home in the camp.[^cite_note-94] On 10 September, a Palestinian was killed and several others were injured by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-95] On 8 October, at least 17 people were killed by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-96] Five people were reportedly killed by an Israeli drone attack in 2 November.[^cite_note-97] On 17 November, at least eight people, including a child were killed by Israeli airstrikes.[^cite_note-98]

#### January – June 2025

On 4 January 2025, four people were killed and dozens were injured when Israeli forces targeted the Abu Helou School that houses displaced Palestinians.[^cite_note-99] On 21 April, one Palestinian was killed and five others were injured in an Israeli airstrike on the camp.[^cite_note-100] On 2 May 2025, at least nine Palestinians were killed by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] On 6 May, 49 people were killed and dozens were injured when Israeli airstrikes struck Abu Hamisa and Al-Karama schools for displaced persons at the camp.[^cite_note-103] On 29 May, at least 23 people were killed by Israeli attacks on residential buildings in the camp.[^cite_note-104]

#### July 2025 – present

On 14 July, at least four people were killed when Israeli forces targeted a commercial centre of the camp, according to [Wafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafa).[^cite_note-105] On 20 September, eight people were killed by an Israeli strike in front of a clinic in the camp.[^cite_note-106] On 2 December, a Palestinian man was killed by Israeli gunfire in the eastern part of the camp, according to [Al-Awda Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Awda_Hospital).[^cite_note-107]

### Al-Maghazi

Main article: [Israeli attacks on Al-Maghazi refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_attacks_on_Al-Maghazi_refugee_camp)

#### 17 October (UNRWA school)

On 17 October 2023, an airstrike conducted by [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) struck a [United Nations](31769.md) Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East ([UNRWA](32270.md)) school in the [Al-Maghazi refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghazi_\(camp\)) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).[^cite_note-UNRWA-108][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-109][^cite_note-Reuters-110] Six people were killed by the airstrike.[^cite_note-Reuters-110][^cite_note-TOI-111][^cite_note-112] The UNRWA said that at least 4,000 people had taken refuge in the school since the start of the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-UNRWA-108] On 22 October, UNRWA confirmed that 29 of their staff had been killed since 7 October, half of whom were UNRWA teachers. The agency further added that as of 21 October nearly 180 civilians sheltering at UNRWA schools had been injured and 12 had been killed, while 38 UNRWA installations had been impacted by strikes since 7 October.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

#### 5 November

On 5 November 2023, during the [Israeli invasion of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_invasion_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) conducted an airstrike in the [al-Maghazi refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghazi_\(camp\)) in the central [Gaza Strip](12047.md). According to the [Gaza Health Ministry](52186562.md), at least 45 people were killed, who were mostly women and children. The IDF did not confirm that the camp had been hit with an Israeli airstrike and said its airstrikes were "specific intelligence-based strikes, specifically against terrorist elements".[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] The airstrike caused severe damage to neighboring homes and infrastructure. The Gaza Health Ministry stated that more than 30 dead people arrived in [Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital](75493386.md) in [Deir Al-Balah](1470463.md) following the airstrike.[^cite_note-119]

#### 6 December

#### 24 and 25 December

#### 9 January 2024

### Nuseirat



A Palestinian father with his child who was injured during Israeli shelling of Nuseirat Camp in October 2023

The [Nuseirat refugee camp](18168164.md) has been bombed repeatedly.

#### October – December 2023

On 18 October 2023, the Grand Nuseirat Mosque was bombed and destroyed by Israeli airstrikes, killing at least 30.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

On 4 November, four Palestinians were killed by an airstrike.[^cite_note-140] An airstrike on 17 November was the third such strike on the area, and killed 18 people.[^cite_note-141] On 21 November, Israeli forces bombed the camp again, targeting homes including those of a nurse and civil defense staff, killing at least 20 people.[^cite_note-142]

On 3 December, at least thirteen were killed in an airstrike.[^cite_note-143] At least ten were reported killed in an airstrike on 5 December.[^cite_note-144] Six were reported killed in an airstrike on 6 December.[^cite_note-145] Several people were reported killed on 18 December.[^cite_note-146] On 17 or 18 December,[^cite_note-\:22-147][^cite_note-\:7-148] karateka [Nagham Abu Samra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagham_Abu_Samra) was injured in an Israeli airstrike that also killed her sister Roseanne. #### January 2024

## Reactions

In response to an Israeli airstrike on the Nuseirat camp on 14 March 2024, the Malaysian [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Malaysia\)) condemned the attack, stating, "This reprehensible act of aggression against innocent civilians underscores the flagrant disregard for human rights and international law by the Israeli authorities".[^cite_note-290][^cite_note-291] In June 2024, [OHCHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHCHR) condemned several Israeli attacks on refugee camps in Gaza, stating, "These attacks also appear to be disproportionate in that they would be expected to cause incidental loss of civilian life, injury to civilians and damage to civilian objects excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated."[^cite_note-292]
