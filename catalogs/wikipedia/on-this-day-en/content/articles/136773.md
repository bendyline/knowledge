**Laredo** (English pronunciation: [/ləˈɹeɪdoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/04/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/04/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; Spanish pronunciation: [\[laˈɾeðo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ed/LL-Q1321\_%28spa%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav/LL-Q1321\_%28spa%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ed/LL-Q1321_%28spa%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav/LL-Q1321_%28spa%29-Noaius_Paticus-Laredo.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a city in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Texas](29810.md) and the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) and largest city of [Webb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webb_County,_Texas), on the north bank of the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande) in [South Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas), across from [Nuevo Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Laredo), [Tamaulipas](222254.md), [Mexico](3966054.md). Founded in 1755, Laredo grew from a village to the capital of the short-lived [Republic of the Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Rio_Grande) to the largest inland port on the [Mexican border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border). Laredo's economy is primarily based on international trade with Mexico, and as a major hub for three areas of transportation: land, rail, and air cargo. The city is on the southern end of [I-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35), which connects manufacturers in northern Mexico through Interstate 35 as a major route for trade throughout the U.S. It has [four international bridges and two railway bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_bridges_in_Laredo,_Texas).

According to the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the city's population was 255,205, making it the [11th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Texas_by_population) city in Texas and third-most populated U.S. city on the Mexican border, after [San Diego, California](28504.md), and [El Paso, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas).[^cite_note-9] Its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_metropolitan_area) is the [178th-largest in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_metropolitan_areas) and includes all of Webb County, with a population of 267,114. Laredo is also part of the cross-border [Laredo-Nuevo Laredo metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo%E2%80%93Nuevo_Laredo) with an estimated population of 636,516.[^cite_note-10]

Laredo's [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) proportion of 95.15% is one of [the highest proportion of Hispanic Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._cities_with_large_Hispanic_populations) of any city in the United States outside of [Puerto Rico](23041.md).[^cite_note-2020Hispanic-11]

[Texas A\&M International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_International_University) and [Laredo College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_College) are in Laredo. [Laredo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_International_Airport) is within the Laredo city limits, while the [Quetzalcoatl International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetzalcoatl_International_Airport) is nearby in Nuevo Laredo on the Mexican side.

The biggest festival, [Washington's Birthday Celebration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington's_Birthday_Celebration), is held during the later part of January and the majority of February, attracting hundreds of thousands of tourists.[^cite_note-12]

## History

The Spanish colonial settlement of Villa de San Agustín de Laredo was founded in 1755 by [Don Tomás Sánchez de la Barrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_S%C3%A1nchez_\(captain\)), while the area was part of the [Nuevo Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Santander) region in the Spanish viceroyalty of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain). Villa de San Agustín de Laredo was named after [Laredo, Cantabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo,_Cantabria), Spain and in honor of Saint [Augustine of Hippo](2030.md). In 1840, Laredo was the capital of the independent [Republic of the Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Rio_Grande), set up in opposition to [Antonio López de Santa Anna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_L%C3%B3pez_de_Santa_Anna); it was brought back into Mexico by military force.

In 1846 during the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War), the town was occupied by the [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Ranger_Division). After the war, the [Treaty of Guadalupe-Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe-Hidalgo) ceded the land to the United States. A referendum was taken in the town, which voted to petition the American military government in charge of the area to return the town to Mexico. When this petition was rejected, many who had been in the area for generations, moved across the river into Mexican territory, where they founded [Nuevo Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Laredo). Many others, especially original land grantees on the north side of the Rio Grande remained, becoming Texans in the process. In 1849, the United States Army set up [Fort McIntosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McIntosh_\(Texas\)) (originally Camp Crawford). Laredo was rechartered as a city in 1852.

Laredo is one of the oldest [crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_crossing) points along the Mexico–United States border, and the nation's largest inland port of entry. In 2005, Laredo celebrated the 250th anniversary of its founding.

The etymology of the name for the Spanish town of Laredo is unclear. Some scholars say the name stems from *[glaretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaretum?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, which means "sandy, rocky place". Others state Laredo stems from a [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) word meaning "beautiful pastures".[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Laredo might also stem from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *[larida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laridae)*, which means [gull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gull).[^cite_note-16]

Cellist [Yo-Yo Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo-Yo_Ma) brought his [Bach Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspired_by_Bach) to the Juarez–Lincoln International Bridge in April 2019.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Geography



[NASA](18426568.md) satellite image of Laredo and Nuevo Laredo (2007)

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 102.6 square miles (265.7 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 1.5 square miles (3.9 km<sup>2</sup>) (1.37%) are covered by water.[^cite_note-19]

### Location

Laredo is on the west end of the Rio Grande Plains, south of the Edwards Plateau, west of the Coastal Plains, and east of the Mexican Mountains. The area consists of a few hills and flat land covered with grasses, oaks, and mesquite.

### Bodies of water

Notable geographic features are the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande) and [Chacon Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chacon_Creek)'s man-made reservoir, [Lake Casa Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Casa_Blanca), in Lake Casa Blanca International State Park. The lake is 371 acres (1.5 km<sup>2</sup>) of land and 1,650 acres (7 km<sup>2</sup>) of water. The six major creeks are Chacon Creek, [San Ildefonso Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ildefonso_Creek), [San Ygnacio Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ygnacio_Creek), [Santa Isabel Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Isabel_Creek), [Sombrerillito Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sombrerillito_Creek), and [Zacate Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacate_Creek), all of which drain into the Rio Grande. Several man-made reservoirs include the [San Ildefonso Creek Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ildefonso_Creek_Lake?action=edit\&redlink=1) (second-largest reservoir), and the [Sombrerillito Creek Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sombrerillito_Creek_Lake?action=edit\&redlink=1) (third-largest reservoir).

### Climate

Laredo is considered to have a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), *BSh*). with sweltering temperatures in the summer and mild temperatures during the winter. Its weather is affected by the [Sierra Madre Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Oriental) mountains to the west, the Gulf of Mexico to the east, and the [Chihuahuan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuahuan_Desert) of Northern Mexico and West Texas. Moisture from the Pacific is cut off by the Mexican mountain range.

The normal monthly mean temperature ranges from 57.6 °F (14.2 °C) in January to 89.1 °F (31.7 °C) in August; official record temperatures range from 11 °F (−11.7 °C) on December 30, 1983, up to 115 °F (46.1 °C) on May 7, 1927, June 17, 1908, and June 19, 2023.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Corpus_Christi,_TX_(CRP)-20] On average, temperatures reach 100 °F (37.8 °C) or higher on 74.2 afternoons annually, and fall to or below the freezing mark on 4.6 mornings, although, in five seasons,[^cite_note-21] the annual minimum temperature was above freezing.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Corpus_Christi,_TX_(CRP)-20][^cite_note-NOAA-22]

Rainfall averages 19.7 in (500 mm) annually, with higher amounts typically occurring from May to October, with peaks in May and September separated by a secondary minimum due to a westward shift of the [subtropical anticyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_anticyclone) and divergence between the [westerlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerlies) and [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds).[^cite_note-23] Actual annual rainfall has ranged from 6.65 inches or 168.9 millimetres in 2011 to 42.28 inches or 1,073.9 millimetres in 1971. The highest monthly rainfalls have been 12.94 inches or 328.7 millimetres in September 1923, 12.45 inches or 316.2 millimetres in July 1919, 12.29 inches or 312.2 millimetres in June 1973 and 11.54 inches or 293.1 millimetres in September 1971. The heaviest daily rainfall has been 7.55 inches or 191.8 millimetres on July 28, 1903. In contrast, not even a trace fell for 108 days from May 12 to August 28 of 1917.

Measurable snow in Laredo has occurred in only five seasons since records began in 1902:[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Corpus_Christi,_TX_(CRP)-20]

1. on December 27–28, 1925 and January 22–23, 1926, totalling 3.7 inches (0.094 m)

2. on December 14, 1947, with 1.0 inch (0.025 m)

3. on January 9, 1967, with 2.7 inches (0.069 m)

4. on Christmas Eve 2004, with 1.1 inches (0.028 m)

5. on December 7–8, 2017, with 1.3 inches (0.033 m)

### Trade



View across the Rio Grande at Laredo, Texas (postcard, c. 1909)

More than 47% of United States international trade headed for Mexico and more than 36% of Mexican international trade crosses through [the Laredo port of entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_World_Trade_Port_of_Entry).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Laredo's economy revolves around commercial and industrial warehousing, import, and export. ### Labor market information

As of October 2007, Laredo's labor market was in the following industries by percentage of number employed: Trade, Transportation, and Utilities (32%), Information (1%), Financial Activity (5%), Professional and Business Services (6%), Education and Health Services (15%), Leisure and Hospitality (10%), Government (23%), Mining and Construction (5%), Manufacturing (2%), and Other Services (2%).

Laredo has increased the number of nonagricultural jobs from 55,100 in January 1996 to 86,600 in October 2007. Laredo has had a higher job growth rate (2%–6.5%) than the state as a whole because of expanded international trade through NAFTA. In 2007, Laredo experienced a job growth rate of 2.5% with the unemployment rate as of October 2007, standing at 4.1% or 3,700 unemployed persons, as compared to 3.9% in Texas statewide. This is a significant drop since the mid-1990s, when Laredo's unemployment was over 15%.

Laredo has had positive job market growth since the mid-1990s; setbacks in the mining (oil/gas) industry shifted a few thousand workers to other industries such as international trade and construction. Many large employers in the oil and gas industries shut down operations in Laredo and across Texas, and shifted to foreign countries. The same effect occurred in the garment industry (Levis and Haggar) along the Texas border area. Laredo lost its only garment-producing company (Barry), costing the jobs of about 300 workers. Laredo's strong job growth rate in retail and transportation services limited the adverse effects of long-term unemployment from the few massive layoffs of the late 1990s. Laredo's success with international trade is also a vulnerability; it depends on changes to Mexico's economy, that status of immigration laws (along with daily border crossings: shoppers and commercial trade), and terrorism.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

### Agriculture

Laredo is a major center for the [cattle ranching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_ranching) in the state.[^cite_note-surveillance-49] Cattle here suffer from the cattle fever tick, *[Rhipicephalus microplus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhipicephalus_microplus)* (syn. *Boophilus microplus*).[^cite_note-surveillance-49] Researchers and ranchers are concerned about [pyrethroid resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrethroid_resistance) developing and spreading here, as it has in nearby areas of the state and neighboring [Tamaulipas](222254.md) state.[^cite_note-surveillance-49] Because the situation is so severe, the main office of the country's Cattle Fever Tick Eradication Program is located here.[^cite_note-sitrep-50] This program is operated by [USDA APHIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA_APHIS).[^cite_note-sitrep-50] The Deutch Strain of this tick was collected here by Davey *et al.*, 1980 and is now a commonly used [laboratory strain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_strain) negative for pyrethroid resistance.[^cite_note-melt-51]

## Arts and culture

### Annual celebrations

The [Washington's Birthday Celebration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington's_Birthday_Celebration),[^cite_note-52] a month-long event that celebrates George Washington's birthday, is the largest annual celebration of its kind in the United States, with 400,000 attendees. It was founded in 1898 by the [Improved Order of Red Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improved_Order_of_Red_Men), local chapter Yaqui Tribe No. 59. The first celebration was a success, and its popularity grew rapidly; in 1923, it received its state [charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter). In 1924, the celebration held its first colonial pageant, which featured 13 girls from Laredo, representing the 13 original colonies. The celebration includes parades, a carnival, an air show, fireworks, live concerts, and a citywide [prom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prom) during which many of Laredo's elite dress in very formal attire. The related [Jalapeño](475861.md) Festival is one of the United States' top 10 eating festivals.

Jamboozie is held in late January in downtown Laredo as part of the Washington's birthday celebrations. Similar to New Orleans' [Mardi Gras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardi_Gras), the Jamboozie is a colorful event, with many people dressed in beads, masks, and flamboyant outfits.

### Museums



[Republic of the Rio Grande Capitol Building Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Rio_Grande_Capitol_Building_Museum)

The **[Republic of the Rio Grande Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Rio_Grande_Museum)**[^cite_note-53] is in the downtown historical district next to the historic La Posada Hotel.[^cite_note-54] What was once the capitol building now showcases memorabilia from the short lived [Republic of the Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Rio_Grande). It displays pictures, books, and furniture from the 19th century Laredo area, and offers guided tours for school-aged children and adults year-round. Because of this Republic, Laredo had flown seven flags instead of the traditional Six Flags over Texas.

The **Laredo Water Museum,**[^cite_note-\:0-55] opened in 2017,[^cite_note-56] is a facility operated by the Laredo Utilities Department which focuses on educating the public about Laredo's water infrastructure. The museum is located at 2702 Anna St. next to the Jefferson Water Treatment Plant; likewise the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande). Some topics include: [water treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_treatment), [river ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_ecosystem), history along the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande), and [water footprints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_footprint).[^cite_note-57] Admission is free, and operation hours can be found on their homepage.[^cite_note-\:0-55]

The **Imaginarium of South Texas**[^cite_note-58] (formerly Laredo Children's Museum), located in a restored building on the [Laredo College Fort McIntosh Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_College), is a [children's museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_museum) that provides a hands-on experience with science, technology, and art for Laredo's youth. A second museum was planned on the [Texas A\&M International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_International_University) campus; however there has been no further development.[^cite_note-59]

The [Nuevo Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Santander) Museum Complex is composed of restored buildings of [Fort McIntosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McIntosh,_Texas), a historical collection of photographs of the fort, the main guardhouse, which has World War I (1914–1918) memorabilia, and a science and technology museum.

### Churches and architecture

Most of Laredo's architecture is of [Spanish Colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_architecture), [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_the_United_States), and Mexican flavor. Most of Laredo's Spanish Colonial-style buildings are in downtown Laredo. More modern American architecture can be seen along Interstate Highway 35, as well as in the downtown area[^cite_note-80]

Our Lady of Guadalupe is an imposing structure in [Romanesque Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_Revival) Lombard (North Italian) style. It was designed by [Leo M. J. Dielmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_M._J._Dielmann) of San Antonio, a popular architect of Catholic buildings, and built for a Mexican-American and Hispanic congregation in the inner city, at San Jorge Avenue and Callaghan St. Dielmann was commissioned by church authorities to design churches for similar congregations in Houston and San Antonio. He also did the San Agustin parish school, and may have had a hand in the San Agustin church, itself.[^cite_note-81]

Both the First United Methodist Church, in 1949, and the Christ Church Episcopal, were designed by [Henry Steinbomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Steinbomer?action=edit\&redlink=1), a popular San Antonio architect known for designing more than 100 churches and related buildings during the 1940s and 50s.[^cite_note-82]

Other Laredo churches include [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist), [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_of_America), [Lutheran](23371382.md), [Assembly of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_God), [Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon), and nondenominational congregations.

#### Music

Laredo has been the subject of several songs in popular culture. One of the most popular songs is the "[Streets of Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streets_of_Laredo_\(song\))", originally known as "A Cowboy's Lament" and written by [Frank H. Maynard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_H._Maynard?action=edit\&redlink=1), who lived mostly in [Colorado](5399.md). It has been recorded by artists such as [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash), [Marty Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Robbins), [Waylon Jennings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waylon_Jennings), [John Cale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cale), [Roy Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Rogers), and [Prefab Sprout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefab_Sprout) (who also made a lyrical reference to Laredo in an early song, "Cue Fanfare"), and is even featured in a *[Charlie's Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie's_Angels)* episode ("Pretty Angels all in a Row", season two, episode three). On October 28, 1958, in the episode "The Ghost" of the ABC/WB Western series, *[Sugarfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarfoot)*, "The Streets of Laredo" is performed by child actor [Tommy Rettig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Rettig).[^cite_note-90] Another song is "[Laredo Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_Tornado)" from the English rock band [Electric Light Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_Light_Orchestra).

The first song on Marty Robbins' 1966 LP *The Drifter* was "Meet Me Tonight in Laredo".[^cite_note-91]

From 1959 to 1972, the six-member singing group, The Rondels, were part the musical scene in Laredo. Members were Carlos Saenz Landin, Humberto Donovan, Roberto Alonzo, Sammy Ibarra, Joe Lee Vera and Noe Adolfo Esparza. With their disbanding, a community member recalls The Rondels "left a large void that will never be forgotten."[^cite_note-92]

The song [Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_\(Band_of_Horses_song\)) from the [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock) [band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_\(rock_and_pop\)) [Band of Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Horses), contained in their [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy)-nominated album [Infinite Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Arms), was placed at number 28 in [Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone) magazine's top 50 songs of [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010).[^cite_note-93]

#### Shirley Field

The original Shirley Field was next to the Civic Center and R\&T Martin High School on San Bernardo Avenue. It was built in 1937, along with Martin High School. Shirley Field was the location for outdoor athletics for Laredo Independent School District and also hosts the annual Border Olympics events. It seats up to about 6,000 fans with additional seating at the 2 endzones. Professional Mexican soccer teams have played various exhibition games here, noting the real grass allows for "better" soccer games. The various sports played on the stadium are football, soccer and track & field events. Major renovations are slated for this historic stadium. In November 2009 Shirley Field was demolished and was rebuilt by the 2011 football season. The total cost of the reconstruction was \$12,000,000 and it now seats 8,000 fans and features artificial turf.[^cite_note-107]

## Government

### Municipal government



Administrative headquarters of the Laredo Fire Department are on Del Mar Boulevard across from St. Patrick's Catholic Church.

The Laredo city government is a [strong city council – weak mayor system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government#Weak-mayor_form). The mayor presides over the eight-member city council, but only votes to break a tie. City Council elections are based on single-member districts and campaigns have no party affiliations. Municipal elections are now held in November (formerly in May) of even-numbered years. The municipal government is administered by the city manager hired by the city council. All city elected offices have a four-year term and are [nonpartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-partisan_democracy) though most officials have a Democratic party preference or affiliation.

City council meetings are held on Mondays and can be viewed on the [public-access television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public-access_television) cable TV channel or live online at Public Access Channel live stream.[^cite_note-119]

### State and federal representation

The [United States District Court for the Southern District of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_Texas) Laredo division is a relatively new building adjacent to the Webb County Courthouse.

The [United States Border Patrol](567453.md) Laredo Sector Headquarters is at 207 W. Del Mar Blvd, Laredo, Texas.

The [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) operates its main Post Office at 2700 East Saunders Street south of [Laredo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_International_Airport).[^cite_note-124] Postal branches are downtown and at 2395 East Del Mar Boulevard.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

The [Texas Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Army_National_Guard) armory is at 6001 E. Bob Bullock Loop 20 Laredo, Texas.

The Colburn Memorial United States Army Reserve Center is at 1 W End Washington St, Laredo, Texas.

The [Texas Department of Criminal Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Department_of_Criminal_Justice) (TDCJ) operates the Laredo Parole Office.[^cite_note-127]

The private prison operator [GEO Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEO_Group) runs the [Rio Grande Detention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_Detention_Center) in Laredo, which opened in 2008 and holds a maximum of 1900 federal detainees.[^cite_note-128]

## Education

### Elementary and secondary



Newman Elementary School at 1300 Alta Vista Blvd. is named for the late businessman [B. P. Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.P._Newman?action=edit\&redlink=1) and his widow, Peggy.

Two school districts, the [Laredo Independent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_Independent_School_District) and the [United Independent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Independent_School_District), and eight private schools serve Laredo.

The **Laredo Independent School District**[^cite_note-129] (LISD) serves the areas in central Laredo. The LISD high schools are [Cigarroa High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cigarroa_High_School), [Martin High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_High_School_\(Laredo,_Texas\)), [J. W. Nixon High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._W._Nixon_High_School) and the [Laredo Early College High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_college_high_school). LISD also has three [magnet schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet): [Dr. Dennis D. Cantu Health Science Magnet School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_High_School_\(Laredo,_Texas\)), [LISD Magnet for Engineering and Technology Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cigarroa_High_School), and [Vidal M. Trevino School of Communications and Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidal_M._Trevino_School_of_Communications_and_Fine_Arts).

The **United Independent School District** serves the rest of Laredo and northern [Webb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webb_County). The UISD high schools are [John B. Alexander High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Alexander_High_School), [Lyndon B. Johnson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson_High_School_\(Laredo,_Texas\)), Laredo Early College High School, [United High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_High_School_\(Laredo,_Texas\)), and [United South High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_South_High_School). Alexander Health Science Magnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Alexander_High_School), [United Engineering Magnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_High_School_\(Laredo,_Texas\)), and the [United South Business Magnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_South_High_School). There are thirty-nine schools within UISD and more are under construction or development. United ISD is one of the state's fastest growing districts, serving almost forty thousand students and covering an area the physical size of [Rhode Island](25410.md).

### Colleges and universities

Laredo is home to three higher education institutions [Laredo College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_College) (LC),[^cite_note-131] [Texas A\&M International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_International_University) (TAMIU),[^cite_note-132] and the [University of Texas Education and Research Center at Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Education_and_Research_Center_at_Laredo) (UT Center at Laredo).[^cite_note-133]

Laredo College is a two-campus institution which offers two-year [Associate's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Degree) degrees. The main campus, Laredo College Fort McIntosh Campus, is at the western end of downtown Laredo near the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande), on the site of the former [Fort McIntosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McIntosh,_Texas). This fort played a major role in the development of Laredo, as it protected the community from Indian raids in its early history. Several of the old buildings at the fort were converted into classrooms, but after renovation programs nearly all of the campus structures are now modern. The smaller, newer second campus, [Laredo College South Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_College_South_Campus?action=edit\&redlink=1), is in south Laredo along [U. S. Route 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U._S._Route_83).



Texas A\&M International University Library

[Texas A\&M International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_International_University), one of the member schools of the [Texas A\&M University System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_University_System), offers [bachelor's](188874.md), [master's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_Degree), and doctoral degrees. On April 22, 2004,[^cite_note-134] the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board in Austin, Texas approved Texas A\&M International University to grant its first PhD in International Business Administration.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] The university was an extension of Texas A\&I-Kingsville and later the former Laredo State University.

The [UT Center at Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Education_and_Research_Center_at_Laredo) is located north of [Business U.S. Highway 59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_routes_of_U.S._Route_59) at the intersection of McPherson Road and Bustamante Street and neighbors the [Laredo Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_Medical_Center?action=edit\&redlink=1) hospital. The center offers graduate certificates, bachelors degrees, and doctoral degrees in various health related fields from five UT System institutions.

### Television

According to [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research), the Laredo region (which includes [Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webb_County,_Texas) and [Zapata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapata_County,_Texas) counties) is ranked 185th market by population size in the United States.[^cite_note-138] The first station to broadcast in Laredo was [KGNS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGNS-TV) in 1956, followed by [KVTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVTV) in 1973, then KJTB (now [KLDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLDO-TV)) in 1985.

The only notable television network missing from Laredo's airwaves is [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) or [Cozi TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozi_TV). Laredo had a full-power local [The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW) affiliate, KGNS-DT2, but on July 3, 2014, the affiliation switched to [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company). Prior to that KJTB channel 27, from January 1985 to October 1988 was Laredo's ABC affiliate. KJTB was later bought by [Entravision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entravision) and affiliated the station to [Telemundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemundo) and changed its callsign to [KLDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLDO-TV). Today KLDO is affiliated to [Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision). Before KJTB, [KGNS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGNS-TV), an [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) affiliate had a secondary affiliation to ABC from its founding in 1956 through KJTB's founding in 1985. On November 6, 2013, KGNS reached an agreement to add the ABC affiliation. The ABC affiliate launched in July 2014 when KGNS dropped The CW programming and added ABC programming.[^cite_note-139] In October 2015 KVTV now [KYLX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KYLX-LD) started broadcasting The CW Programming on its digital subchannel 13.2.[^cite_note-140]

In December 2014, all Nuevo Laredo stations turned off [analog television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_television) broadcasting and started broadcasting [digitally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_television) only.[^cite_note-141]

## Infrastructure

### Health care



Laredo Medical Center, formerly Mercy Hospital, is the largest hospital in Laredo.



Doctor's Hospital in Laredo



Laredo Specialty Hospital, near the Laredo Medical Center, handles certain patients requiring long-term care.

In addition to the [University of Texas Health Science Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Health_Science_Center) branch, there are five other principal medical centers in Laredo: the [Laredo Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_Medical_Center?action=edit\&redlink=1), Doctor's Hospital, Gateway Community Health Center, Providence Surgical & Medical Center, and the Laredo Specialty Hospital.

Doctors Hospital[^cite_note-145] is Laredo's second-largest medical center. The hospital complex is over 250,000 square feet (23,000 m<sup>2</sup>), with 180 licensed beds on a 58-acre (23 ha) campus. Affiliated with [Universal Health Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Health_Services), it is on [Loop 20](11272733.md) in north Laredo. The Doctors Regional Cancer Treatment Center offers comprehensive cancer services.

The Providence Surgical & Medical Center[^cite_note-146] is an ambulatory health care center in north-central Laredo and also owned by Universal Health Services.

The Gateway Community Health Center[^cite_note-147] is the third-largest medical center in Laredo. The health center's main building is 64,000 square feet (5,900 m<sup>2</sup>). The Medical center moved to its new \$11,000,000 building in 2006. The main Gateway Community Health Center is in East Laredo, close to [U.S. Highway 59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Highway_59_\(Texas\)). It also has three branches in the Laredo area: the South Clinic, El Cenizo Community Center, and Quad City Community Center.

The Laredo Specialty Hospital[^cite_note-148] is the fourth-largest medical center in Laredo. It is owned by [Ernest Health Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Health_Inc.?action=edit\&redlink=1) and was founded by Elmo Lopez Jr. on May 22, 2006. It admitted its first patient within hours of operation. The grand opening took place in March 2007.

### Transportation

In 2016, 82.3 percent of working Laredo residents commuted by driving alone, 10.2 percent carpooled, 0.9 percent used public transportation, and 1.9 percent walked. About 2 percent of working Laredo residents commuted by all other means, including taxi, bicycle, and motorcycles. About 2.6 percent worked at home.[^cite_note-149]

In 2015, 6.5 percent of city of Laredo households were without a car, which decreased slightly to 5.9 percent in 2016. The national average was 8.7 percent in 2016. Laredo averaged 1.85 cars per household in 2016, compared to a national average of 1.8 per household.[^cite_note-150]

#### Air



Laredo International Airport



Tex-Mex Railway International Bridge view from Laredo

Laredo is served by the [Laredo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_International_Airport). Daily flights are available to Houston ([George Bush Intercontinental Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bush_Intercontinental_Airport)) and to [Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas/Fort_Worth_International_Airport). Tri-weekly flights to [Las Vegas, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas,_Nevada) are available. After Laredo Air Force Base closed in the mid-1970s, the federal government handed over the old air force base and property to the City of Laredo for a new municipal airport. From the mid-1970s until the mid-1990s, the airport used a small terminal for passenger airline service and several old hangars for air cargo and private aircraft. A new state-of-the art passenger terminal was built along the then newly constructed Loop 20 to accommodate larger jets and to increase passenger air travel through Laredo. Expansion of air cargo facilities, taxiways and aprons, air cargo carriers such as DHL, FedEx, UPS, BAX, and others have responded by adding commercial air cargo jet services. Laredo also has two medical [helipads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helipad), at Laredo Medical Center and Doctor's Hospital.

#### Mass transit

[El Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Metro_Transit) is the public transit system that operates in the city with 21 fixed routes and Paratransit services, with approximately 4.6 million passengers per year. El Metro works with a fleet of over 47 fixed route buses, 2 trolleys and 18 Paratransit/El Lift vans. The El Metro hub is in downtown Laredo at El Metro Transit Center. The center also houses [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) and provides fee-based daily parking for downtown shoppers and workers.

#### Rural transit

Rural transportation is provided by the [Webb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webb_County) operated "El Aguila Rural Transportation" (the Eagle) bus services. El Aguila serves fixed daily routes from rural communities ([Bruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruni,_Texas), [El Cenizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cenizo,_Texas), [Mirando City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirando_City,_Texas), [Oilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oilton,_Texas), and [Rio Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Bravo,_Texas)) to the downtown El Metro Transit Center.

## Sister cities

During the month of July, Laredo sponsors the Laredo International Sister Cities Festival, which was founded in 2003. The festival is an international business, trade, tourism, and cultural expo. All of Laredo's [sister cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_City) are invited to participate. In 2004, the Laredo International Sister Cities Festival received the best overall Program award from the [Sister Cities International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_Cities_International).[^cite_note-158]
