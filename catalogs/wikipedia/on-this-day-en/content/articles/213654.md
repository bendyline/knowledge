# Shipyard

This article is about the ship repair and construction yard. For other uses, see [Shipyard (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipyard_\(disambiguation\)).



[Constanța Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba_Shipyard), Romania



[Turku Repair Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku_Repair_Yard), Finland

A **shipyard**, also called a **dockyard** or **boatyard**, is a place where [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) are [built](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) and repaired. These can be [yachts](34291.md), military vessels, [cruise liners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_liner) or other cargo or passenger ships. Compared to shipyards, which are sometimes more involved with original construction, dockyards are sometimes more linked with maintenance and basing activities. The terms are routinely used interchangeably, in part because the [evolution](#History) of dockyards and shipyards has often caused them to change or merge roles.

Countries with large shipbuilding industries include [Australia](4689264.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [China](5405.md), [Croatia](5573.md), [Denmark](76972.md), [Finland](10577.md), [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md), [India](14533.md), [Ireland](14560.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Japan](15573.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md), [Norway](21241.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), [Poland](22936.md), [Romania](25445.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Spain](26667.md), [Sweden](5058739.md), [Taiwan](25734.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Ukraine](31750.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), the United States and [Vietnam](202354.md). The shipbuilding industry is more fragmented in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Europe) than in [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Asia) where countries tend to have fewer, larger companies. Many naval vessels are built or maintained in shipyards owned or operated by the national government or navy.

Shipyards are constructed near the sea or tidal rivers to allow easy access for their ships. The [United Kingdom](31717.md), for example, has shipyards on many of its rivers.

The site of a large shipyard will contain many specialised [cranes](318378.md), [dry docks](213739.md), [slipways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipway), dust-free warehouses, painting facilities and extremely large areas for fabrication of the ships. After a ship's useful life is over, it makes its final voyage to a [ship-breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship-breaking) yard, often on a [beach](40630.md) in [South Asia](21566765.md). Historically ship-breaking was carried out in drydock in developed countries, but high wages and environmental regulations have resulted in movement of the industry to third-world regions.

## History

The oldest structure sometimes identified as a dockyard[^cite_note-2] was built c. 2400 BC by the [Indus Valley civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilisation) in the [Harappan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappan_architecture) port city of [Lothal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothal#Dockyard) (in present-day [Gujarat, India](53707.md)).[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-4] Lothal engineers accorded high priority to the creation of a dockyard and a [warehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warehouse) to serve the purposes of maritime trade. The name [Naupactus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naupactus), an ancient Greek city on the Gulf of Corinth, means "shipyard" (combination of the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) words ναύς *naus*: "ship, boat"; and πήγνυμι *pêgnumi*, *pegnymi*: "builder, fixer").[^cite_note-t347-5] Naupactus' reputation in this field extended back into legendary times – the site is traditionally identified by Greek authors such as [Ephorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephorus) and [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) as the place where a fleet was said to have been built by the legendary [Heraclidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclidae)[^cite_note-6] Other early historical shipyards include [Tel Abu Saifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Abu_Saifi?action=edit\&redlink=1), in the Northern [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula), a 4th-century BCE, Ptolemaic Era, Egyptian dockyard, with two dry docks.[^cite_note-7] [Narni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_shipyard_of_Stifone_\(Narni\)) was a shipyard of [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome).

In the Spanish city of [Barcelona](4443.md), the [Drassanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Royal_Shipyard) shipyards were active from at least the mid-13th century until the 18th century, although at times they served as a barracks for troops as well as an arsenal. During their time of operation the Drassanes were continuously changed, rebuilt and modified, but two original towers and part of the original eight construction-naves remain today. From the 14th century, several hundred years before the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), ships were the first items to be manufactured in a [factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory) – in the [Venice Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Arsenal) of the [Venetian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Republic) in present-day [Italy](14532.md). The Arsenal apparently [mass-produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production) nearly one ship every day using [pre-manufactured parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_system_of_manufacturing) and [assembly lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line). At its height in the 16th century the enterprise employed 16,000 people. Spain built component ships of the [Great Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armada) of 1588 at ports such as [Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras) or [Málaga](184763.md).[^cite_note-8] In the 17th and 18th centuries, shipyards developed in complexity, with yards such as [Blackwall Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwall_Yard) (1614 to 1987), the [Scotts Shipbuilding and Engineering Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotts_Shipbuilding_and_Engineering_Company) in Greenock, Scotland (1711–1984) and the [Kraljevica Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraljevica_Shipyard) (1729 and still operating) being established. [Havana](49719.md) was long the only dockyard in the [Caribbean](18956035.md) during the colonial period, the *[Santísima Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_ship_Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_de_la_Sant%C3%ADsima_Trinidad)*, the largest warship of its time, was built there in 1769. [Royal Naval Dockyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Dockyards) in the [UK](31717.md) also expanded at this time, (including [Woolwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolwich_Dockyard), [Deptford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deptford), [Chatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Dockyard), [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Dockyard) and [Devonport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNB_Devonport)), [Gibraltar](7607314.md), [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay), [Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Dockyard,_Bermuda), [Hong Kong](13404.md) and elsewhere worldwide. Similarly, other countries in this period include the Nantes-Indret yard in France (established in 1771 it built ships for the American Revolution including the *Deane*), Charlestown Navy Yard, later [Boston Navy Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Navy_Yard), [Boston](24437894.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md) (1800 to 1974), the [Navy Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Island), [Ontario](22218.md), Canada (French in the 18th century, then British 1763 to [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812)), the [Philadelphia Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Naval_Shipyard) (1799 to 1995), at two locations, and the [Portsmouth Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Shipyard), located on [Maine](19977.md)-[New Hampshire](21134.md) border (1800 to present, making it the oldest continuously operating shipyard of the US Navy).

The [Industrial revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_revolution) saw the creation of many new shipyards around the world. In the UK, these included [Thames Ironworks and Shipbuilding Co. Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Ironworks_and_Shipbuilding_Co._Ltd) (1837 to 1912), [William Denny and Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Denny_and_Brothers) in Dumbarton, Scotland (1840 to 1963), [John Brown & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_&_Company), Scotland (1851 to 1972), [Swan Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Hunter) (1880 to 2006), [Harland & Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harland_&_Wolff) – (1861 - still a working yard) and [Cammell Laird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cammell_Laird) (1828, still a working yard). In Europe, other examples include [Blohm+Voss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blohm+Voss) (1877) where *Bismarck* was constructed (still a major yard). [Ulstein Verft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulstein_Verft) in Norway was established in 1917 (still a working yard under the [Ulstein Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulstein_Group)). In France, [Chantiers de l'Atlantique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantiers_de_l'Atlantique) (STX France) was established in 1861 (and is still a working yard). [3. Maj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3._Maj) was one of the largest shipyard in the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean), established in 1892 in [Rijeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijeka) (it is still a working yard). [SLKB Komarno (Komárno) – Slovak Shipyard Komárno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLKB_Komarno_\(Kom%C3%A1rno\)_%E2%80%93_Slovak_Shipyard_Kom%C3%A1rno?action=edit\&redlink=1) was another European shipyard on the [Danube](21209639.md) (established in 1898). [Jean Street Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Street_Shipyard) (1843–present) is the oldest continually operated shipyard in the U.S. Located on the [Hillsborough River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_River_\(Florida\)) in [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida). [Gloucester Marine Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucester_Marine_Railways) in the US (1859–present) is the oldest working shipyard in New England, being located on [Rocky Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Neck,_Gloucester) in [Gloucester, Massachusetts](116744.md).

During the late industrial revolution, British shipyards were among the largest in the world, including [Harland & Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harland_&_Wolff) in Belfast, [John Brown & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_&_Company) at Clydebank (Glasgow) and [Swan Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Hunter) at Wallsend (Tyne).[^cite_note-e757-9][^cite_note-g015-10] By the 20th century, large shipyards were built during conflicts such as the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) and [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). The [Sun Shipbuilding & Drydock Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Shipbuilding_&_Drydock_Co.) in [Chester, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester,_Pennsylvania) was the largest shipyard in the world by 1945, employing some 40,000 workers and building hundreds of ships during the Second World War.[^cite_note-m712-11][^cite_note-a437-12] Other examples of historical US yards include [Mare Island Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Island_Naval_Shipyard), [Mare Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Island), [California](5407.md) (1854 to 1996), New York Naval Shipyard (NYNSY), also known as the Brooklyn Navy Yard, the [New York Navy Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Navy_Yard), and United States Navy Yard, New York (1801 to 1966), [San Francisco Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Naval_Shipyard), later Hunters Point Naval Shipyard, then Treasure Island Naval Station Hunters Point Annex (1941 to 1994) and [Long Beach Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Naval_Shipyard) (1943 to 1997).

New shipyards were established after the war, a prominent example being the [Gdańsk Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk_Shipyard) in 1945, the birthplace of [Solidarity Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Solidarity) – (still a working yard). In the late 20th century, shipbuilding in countries such as the US and UK declined, with yards closing and new shipyards instead expanding in countries such as [Japan](15573.md), [South Korea](27019.md) and [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-e757-9][^cite_note-v785-13] By the early 21st century, China became the worlds leading shipbuilder, with approximately 50% of global tonnage build at Chinese shipyards in 2023.[^cite_note-h362-14] Since the early 2020s, shipyard capacity, design and infrastructure is changing in light of technological change and as the result of regulatory changes from the [International Maritime Organization](14986.md) requiring ships to be built to operate more efficiently and with less pollution.[^cite_note-c717-15]

## Shipyard work



Donjon shipyard with fully enclosed assembly building in [Erie, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie,_Pennsylvania)

Work in shipyards typically involves the construction, modification, retrofitting and repair of ships.[^cite_note-c766-16] This varies according to the type of shipyard and if there are [drydocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drydock) in the shipyard.[^cite_note-c776-17] It may also involve [ship breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_breaking), although in the 21st century, most ship recycling takes place at the [Alang Ship Breaking Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alang_Ship_Breaking_Yard) in [India](14533.md), the [Chittagong Ship Breaking Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Ship_Breaking_Yard) in [Bangladesh](3454.md) and the [Gadani Ship Breaking Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadani_Ship_Breaking_Yard) in [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-h741-18] Shipyards normally have industrial facilities for the production, assembly, and installation of materials. Shipyards normally have quays, jetties and slipways that include specific areas for launching, for repair and for outfitting work.[^cite_note-c766-16] The work in the shipyard will typically involve activities such as the [welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welding) and cutting of [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel), the use of [cutting tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_tool_\(machining\)) and other [machine tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_tool) plumbing, electrical work, and the application, removal or renewal of paints and coatings.[^cite_note-i517-19]

Work in the shipyard typically falls under the relevant national domestic health and safety legislation. Examples around the world include the US Shipyard Industry Standards, part of [Occupational Safety and Health Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_Safety_and_Health_Administration), the UK workplace regulations of the UK [Health and Safety Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_and_Safety_Executive) and the Industrial Safety and Health Act of Ministry of Employment and Labor in South Korea.[^cite_note-t292-20][^cite_note-j836-21][^cite_note-n521-22] Work in shipyards can at times be considered dangerous.[^cite_note-r319-23] Accidents in shipyards may involve falls from height,[^cite_note-n521-22] as well as injuries from the use of tools and equipment, and from other hazards such as fire, explosion and pollution.[^cite_note-r319-23] In the late 20th century, many shipyard workers have been affected by the legacy of [asbestos](21492663.md) use within shipyards, although the use of the material is often prohibited following greater understanding of the effects of [asbestosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asbestosis).[^cite_note-u653-24] Hazards may also come from factors such as slips, trips, excessive-noise, high-pressure tools and impact tools such as [needleguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needlegun) and [grinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grinding_machine).[^cite_note-k771-25] To mitigate the dangers and hazards of shipyard work, many employ safe systems of work based on regulation, best practice and guidance, typically involve the control of processes and the use of risk assessments and similar methods of work.[^cite_note-c776-17]

## Prominent dockyards and shipyards

### Africa

- [Alexandria shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_shipyard), the oldest shipyard in Africa founded in 1831 located in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Egypt](8087628.md).

### North America



Aerial view of [Norfolk Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Naval_Shipyard)

- [Davie Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davie_Shipbuilding), (formerly Chantier Davie Canada Inc) in Lévis, Québec, is the oldest continually operating shipbuilder in North America.

- [Newport News Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News_Shipbuilding), (formerly Northrop Grumman Newport News) is the largest private ship builder in the [US](3434750.md) and the one best known for its unique capacity to build the [*Nimitz*-class aircraft carriers](181160.md).

- [Ingalls Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingalls_Shipbuilding), part of [Huntington Ingalls Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Ingalls_Industries), located in [Pascagoula, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascagoula,_Mississippi), repaired the [USS *Cole*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Cole_\(DDG-67\)) and builds [offshore drilling rigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_drilling_rig), [cruise ships](314855.md) and naval vessels.

- [National Steel and Shipbuilding Company (NASSCO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Steel_and_Shipbuilding_Company) shipyard in [San Diego](28504.md), California, part of [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics); is the primary shipbuilding location on the west coast of the United States.

- [Norfolk Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Naval_Shipyard) in [Portsmouth, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Virginia), is one of the largest shipyards in the world; specializing in repairing, overhauling and modernizing naval ships and submarines. It's the oldest and largest industrial facility that belongs to the [United States Navy](20518076.md)

- [Electric Boat Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_Electric_Boat) (EBDiv) of [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics) in [Groton, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groton,_Connecticut), with an accessory facility in [Quonset Point, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quonset_Point,_Rhode_Island), builder of many Naval [submarines](28825.md) over the past 100 years, with some types built only here.

- [Bath Iron Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_Iron_Works) (BIW), subsidiary of General Dynamics, is a major American shipyard located on the [Kennebec River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennebec_River) in [Bath, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Maine).

- [Puget Sound Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound_Naval_Shipyard_and_Intermediate_Maintenance_Facility) in [Bremerton, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerton,_Washington), is also owned by the U.S. Navy. It services ships and submarines from the West Coast.

- [Vigor Marine Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigor_Marine_Group), a shipbuilding and ship repair company headquartered in [Portland, Oregon](23503.md), with shipyards in Oregon, Washington, California, and Virginia as of 2025.

- [Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard and Intermediate Maintenance Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor_Naval_Shipyard) is operated by the US Navy. It services surface ships and submarines from the Pacific region
