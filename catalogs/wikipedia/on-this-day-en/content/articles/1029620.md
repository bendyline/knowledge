The **Ghana national football team** represents [Ghana](12067.md) in men's international [association football](10568.md).[^cite_note-4] It is nicknamed the **Black Stars** after the [Black Star of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Star_of_Africa) in the [flag of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ghana).[^cite_note-5] The team is governed by the [Ghana Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Football_Association).[^cite_note-6] Prior to 1957, it played as the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)).[^cite_note-7]

Ghana qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) for the first time in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] It has won the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) four times ([1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_African_Cup_of_Nations), [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_African_Cup_of_Nations), [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_African_Cup_of_Nations), and [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_African_Cup_of_Nations)), while finishing as runners-up five times ([1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_African_Cup_of_Nations), [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_African_Cup_of_Nations), [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_African_Cup_of_Nations), [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations)).[^cite_note-10] Ghana have qualified for the [CHAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Nations_Championship) four times, finishing as runners-up twice ([2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_African_Nations_Championship) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_African_Nations_Championship)).[^cite_note-11]

## History

On 19 August 1962 at the [Accra Sports Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_Sports_Stadium), Ghana hosted [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid), who were at the time [Spanish champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish_football_champions), drawing 3–3.[^cite_note-12]

[Charles Kumi Gyamfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kumi_Gyamfi) became coach in 1961, leading Ghana to successive [African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Cup_of_Nations) titles, in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_African_Cup_of_Nations). The Black Stars achieved their record win, 13–2 away to [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_national_football_team), in the latter.[^cite_note-13] It reached the final of the tournament in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_African_Cup_of_Nations), losing 1–0 on both occasions, to [DR Congo](1030243.md) and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_national_football_team) respectively.[^cite_note-podcasts.musixmatch.com-14] Its domination of the tournament earned it the nickname "the Black Stars of Africa" in the 1960s.[^cite_note-Ghana_ready_to_fulfil_their_destiny-15]

Fortunes changed for the Black Stars after it failed to qualify for three successive AFCON's in the 1970s.[^cite_note-16] In the 1980s, with emerging talents such as [Abedi Pele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abedi_Pele), the Black Stars defeated [1982 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_African_Cup_of_Nations) hosts [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_African_Cup_of_Nations_final) to win its fourth continental title.[^cite_note-17] Fortunes changed again, as in the 1984 tournament, it was knocked out in the group stages, before failing to qualify for the 1986, 1988 and 1990 tournaments.[^cite_note-podcasts.musixmatch.com-14] In 1992, the Black Stars would finish runners-up to the [Ivory Coast](1039856.md) in a penalty shootout after a goalless draw, which saw every player on the pitch take a penalty, in which they were beaten 11-10,[^cite_note-18] with [African Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Footballer_of_the_Year) Abedi Pele suspended for the final.[^cite_note-19]

Tensions among the squad led to the [parliamentary](3166296.md) and [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ghana) to intervene and settle issues between players Abedi Pele and [Tony Yeboah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Yeboah). In the 1990s, this may have played some part in the failure of the team to build on the successes of the national underage teams. The generation of Black Stars players who went to the [2001 FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) final became the "core" of the team at the [2002 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations), going undefeated for a year in 2005 and [qualifying for the final tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) of the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup). The Black Stars started by succumbing to a [2–0 defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E#Italy_vs_Ghana) to eventual champions [Italy](362466.md), and wins over [Czech Republic](729739.md) ([2–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E#Czech_Republic_vs_Ghana)) and the [United States](582488.md) ([2–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E)). This saw it advance through to the second round, where it lost [3–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Brazil_vs_Ghana) to [Brazil](149286.md).[^cite_note-2006_World_Cup_–_Part_2-20]

Under head coach [Milovan Rajevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milovan_Rajevac), the Black Stars went on to secure a 100% win record in its [qualification campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)), winning its group and becoming the first African team to qualify for the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md). In the final tournament, it was placed in Group D with [Germany](250204.md), [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team) and [Australia](4792249.md), advancing to the round of 16 after finishing second in their group. Ghana played the United States, winning 2–1 in extra time to become only the third African nation to reach the World Cup quarter-finals, before losing to [Uruguay](679782.md) in a [penalty shootout](511302.md) in the quarter-finals, after Uruguayan forward [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)) blocked a header with his hand in the penalty box in extra time and was sent off. [Asamoah Gyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asamoah_Gyan) missed the penalty given for the handball, with the score 1–1. Ghana went on to lose the penalty shootout 4–2.[^cite_note-21]

In 2013, Ghana became the first team to reach four consecutive African Cup of Nations semi-finals twice, with the first occasion between 1963 and 1970.[^cite_note-22]

The Black Stars won their [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_second_round) group of [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) qualifying, and defeated [Egypt](1022953.md) 7–3 on aggregate in a two-legged play-off,[^cite_note-Black_Stars_win_through-23] qualifying for the finals. It was drawn in [Group G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) for the finals, where it faced [Germany](250204.md), [Portugal](576328.md), and the [United States](582488.md).[^cite_note-2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Final_Draw-24] It exited in the group stage recording one draw against eventual champions Germany and two losses, and was the only team to not lose to Germany in the tournament, and the only team to hold onto a lead against the Germans at any point.

In the [2015 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Ghana reached the final, being denied the title on penalties against [Ivory Coast](1039856.md). While its [2017 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) campaign ended in a 4th-place finish, it finished behind [Egypt](1022953.md) and [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team) in its final group [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md). At the [2019 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Ghana was eliminated by [Tunisia](1029953.md) in the round of 16. In 2021, manager Rajevac was brought back, and the Black Stars ended up failing to win a match at the [AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) where it lost 2–3 to debutants [the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_national_football_team) after an [André Ayew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Ayew) red card to finish bottom of their group, failing to progress beyond the group stage for the first time since 2006. Ghana drew 0–0 in a match against [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) and drew 1–1 in Nigeria to qualify for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) on [away goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_goals_rule).[^cite_note-25] During the 2022 FIFA World Cup, it lost its first match against [Portugal](576328.md) 3–2. Ghana earned a victory in its second match against [South Korea](1018627.md) by the same scoreline. A victory against [Uruguay](679782.md) was required for a spot in the round of 16 in a rematch of the 2010 quarter-final; Ghana instead lost 2–0 and thus finished bottom, while Uruguay was eliminated as well, as a result of South Korea upsetting Portugal.

Ghana failed to qualify for the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md), for the first time since 2004, as it finished bottom of the group below [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team), [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_national_football_team), and [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_national_football_team).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

### Organisation and finance

The Black Stars had no official head because of "corrupt" practices[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] by the then president, [Kwesi Nyantakyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwesi_Nyantakyi)[^cite_note-37] and vice-president George Afriyie,[^cite_note-38] with Frank Davis as [director of football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_football), and Edward Bawa as treasurer.[^cite_note-Kwesi_Nyantakyi-39] The Ghana Football Association (GFA) signed a [CN¥](66958.md)92.2 million (US\$15 million) deal with Ghanaian state-run [oil and gas exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas_exploration) corporation, [Ghana National Petroleum Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_National_Petroleum_Corporation) (GNPC), to [sponsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponsor_\(commercial\)) the Black Stars and the renewable contract saw the oil and gas exploration corporation become the global headline sponsor of the Black Stars, with a yearly Black Stars player salary wage bill,[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] following the [gold mining](1291393.md) corporations [Ashanti Goldfields Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_Goldfields_Corporation) and Goldfields Ghana Limited (GGL), which had been sponsoring the Black Stars since 2005.

On 28 August 2013, the GFA launched a [TV channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_channel) and named [GFA TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GFA_TV). The channel has the [exclusive rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_right) to broadcast all the Black Stars' matches.[^cite_note-Ghana_Football_Association_launches_GFA_TV-42] In November 2013, the Black Stars signed a 2013–2015 CN¥30.6 million (US\$5 million) and an additional [classified](252857.md) multi-million [private bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_bank) sponsorship deal with the Ghanaian state-run [private banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_banking) institution [UniBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniBank).[^cite_note-43]

### Supporters



Ghanaian supporters at the 2015 AFCON match between Ghana and Guinea

The Black Stars maintain an average stadium match attendance of over 60,000, with 84,017 watching the [2010 FIFA World Cup quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Uruguay_vs_Ghana) against [Uruguay](679782.md).[^cite_note-Ghana_1_–_1_Uruguay-44] Its match against [England](9904.md) on 29 March 2011 had the largest away following for any association football national team since the re-opening of [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) in 2007.[^cite_note-Tickets_For_Ghana_And_England_Maiden_International_Friendly_Sold_Out-45] The match was watched by 700 million people around the world.[^cite_note-Tickets_For_Ghana_And_England_Maiden_International_Friendly_Sold_Out-45]

Following the team's appearances at the [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2010 World Cup](168079.md) tournaments, Ghana were greeted by some hundred fans dancing and singing at [Accra International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_International_Airport) in Accra.[^cite_note-Ghana_gives_Black_Stars_heroes'_welcome_after_World_Cup-46]

### Rivalries

Main article: [Jollof derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jollof_derby)



Ghana against [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) in the [2008 Africa Cup of Nations quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Africa_Cup_of_Nations#Quarter-finals)

Ghana is rivals with [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team). The "Battle of Supremacy on the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea)" is between two of the "most successful teams on the African continent".[^cite_note-Rivals_herald_African_awakening_(Ghana_vs_Nigeria)-47] The proximity of the two countries to each other, a dispute between the different association football competitions and [wider diplomatic competition for influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana%E2%80%93Nigeria_relations) across West Africa add to this rivalry.[^cite_note-Rivals_herald_African_awakening_(Ghana_vs_Nigeria)-47][^cite_note-48] The match between these two countries is called the [Jollof derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jollof_derby).[^cite_note-49]

During the [scheduled qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the [2014 World Cup](656933.md) national broadcaster [GTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTV_\(Ghana\)), a sub-division of the [Ghana Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Broadcasting_Corporation) (GBC), broadcast to the Ghanaian public home qualifiers with away qualifiers broadcast by the satellite television broadcasting corporation [Viasat 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasat_1). Gyamfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Gyamfi) led it to 3 Africa Cup of Nations titles – in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_African_Cup_of_Nations), [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_African_Cup_of_Nations) – making Gyamfi the "joint most successful coach" in the competition's history.[^cite_note-62] [Fred Osam Duodu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Osam-Duodu) led the Black Stars to their [1978 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) title;[^cite_note-63] [Ratomir Dujković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratomir_Dujkovi%C4%87), [Milovan Rajevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milovan_Rajevac), [James Kwesi Appiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Kwesi_Appiah) and [Otto Addo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Addo) have led the Black Stars to World Cup qualification,[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-Kwesi_Appiah_challenges_his_former_bosses_statistically-65] with the first two being [Serbs](29265.md).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] [Carlos Queiroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Queiroz) was appointed in April 2026 as Ghana's head coach ahead of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), replacing Otto Addo after a run of four consecutive losses which culminated in a loss to the German national team in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MHPArena).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Former Ghana international Anthony Yeboah later expressed support for Queiroz's squad selection ahead of the tournament, backing the coach's decisions for the 2026 FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-76]

### Local team

Main article: [Ghana A' national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_A'_national_football_team)

The football association of Ghana (GFA) administers national teams at levels, including one for the local national team. The team is restricted to players who only play in the local league, thus the [Ghana Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Premier_League). It is nicknamed *Local Black Stars*.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-\:3-81][^cite_note-\:4-82]

## Competitive record

### FIFA World Cup

Main article: [Ghana at the FIFA World Cup](40256544.md)



Top: At the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in Germany.\
&#x20;Center and bottom: Ghana against [Uruguay](679782.md) in the [2010 tournament](168079.md) at [Soccer City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_City), [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa.

Ghana have qualified for 5 [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) tournaments, in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](168079.md), [2014](656933.md), [2022](17742072.md) and [2026](36581929.md). In 2006, it was the only African side to advance to the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage) in Germany, and was the sixth nation in a row from Africa to progress beyond the group stages of the World Cup.[^cite_note-Ghana_2-1_USA-93] Ghana had the youngest team in the [2006 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#Ghana) with an average age of 23 years and 352 days,[^cite_note-Ghana_2-1_USA-93] and were praised for their improving performance.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] [FIFA](11049.md) ranked Ghana 13th out of the 32 countries who competed in the tournament.[^cite_note-96]

In the [2010 World Cup](168079.md), Ghana reached the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Uruguay_vs_Ghana) where they were eliminated by [Uruguay](679782.md), after [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_\(Uruguayan_footballer\))'s handball prevented a possible winning goal.[^cite_note-97] Of the 32 countries that participated in the 2010 edition, FIFA ranked Ghana 7th.[^cite_note-98]

After beating [Egypt](1022953.md) 7–3 on aggregate in November 2013, Ghana qualified for the [2014 World Cup](656933.md) in Brazil.[^cite_note-99] For the first time, Ghana were eliminated in the group stage, drawing 2–2 against Germany, and losing to the United States and Portugal 2–1.[^cite_note-100] Eight years later, Ghana failed to advance past the group stage again, at the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar.[^cite_note-101]

Ghana qualified for the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md) as the lowest-ranked African team in the tournament, sitting 74th in the last [FIFA ranking](719095.md) before the competition.[^cite_note-102] They were eliminated in the round of 32 after losing 1–0 to Colombia.[^cite_note-103]

### Africa Cup of Nations

Main article: [Ghana at the Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_at_the_Africa_Cup_of_Nations)



At the 2015 AFCON vs. Guinea in the quarter-finals.

The *Black Stars* of Ghana have won the Africa Cup of Nations four times: in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_African_Cup_of_Nations), [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_African_Cup_of_Nations), [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_African_Cup_of_Nations), and [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_African_Cup_of_Nations), bettered only by [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) and [Egypt](1022953.md). As the first winner of three AFCON tournaments, Ghana obtained the right to permanently hold the trophy in 1978.[^cite_note-BBC-Newtrophy-104]

In November 2024, following a 1–1 draw against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team), Ghana failed to qualify for the [2025 AFCON](57061770.md), missing the tournament for the first time since 2004.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]
