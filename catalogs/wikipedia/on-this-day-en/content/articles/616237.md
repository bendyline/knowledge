# Swabia

"Schwaben" and "Suevia" redirect here. For the naval ship, see [SMS *Schwaben*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Schwaben). For other uses, see [Suevia (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suevia_\(disambiguation\)) and [Swabia (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabia_\(disambiguation\)).



Today's Swabia within Germany. The [Schwarzwald-Baar-Kreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzwald-Baar-Kreis) (yellow) is at the transitional area of the Swabian, Upper Rhenish and Lake Constance dialects of Alemannic. The western [Bodenseekreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodenseekreis) district is not considered a part of modern Swabia. The dividing line is between [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md) (west) and [Bavaria](3764.md) (east).



The [coat of arms of Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg): *Or, three lions passant sable*, the arms of the [Duchy of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia), in origin the arms of the [House of Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenstaufen). Also used for Swabia (and [Württemberg-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg-Baden), 1945–1952) are the three antlers of the [coat of arms of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg).

**Swabia**[^cite_note-1] is a cultural, [historical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_region) and linguistic region in southwestern [Germany](11867.md). The name is ultimately derived from the medieval [Duchy of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia), one of the German [stem duchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_duchy), representing the historic settlement area of the [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) tribe alliances named [Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) and [Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi).

This territory would include all of the [Alemannic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) area, but the modern concept of Swabia is more restricted, due to the collapse of the duchy of Swabia in the thirteenth century. Swabia as understood in modern ethnography roughly coincides with the [Swabian Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Circle) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) as it stood during the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period), now divided between the states of [Bavaria](3764.md) and [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md).

[Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabians) (*Schwaben*, singular *Schwabe*) are the natives of Swabia and speakers of [Swabian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_German). Their number was estimated at close to 800,000 by [SIL Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIL_Ethnologue) as of 2006, out of a total population of 7.5 million in the regions of [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCbingen_\(region\)), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_\(region\)) and [Bavarian Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabia_\(Bavaria\)).

### Early history

Further information: [Alamannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamannia), [Raetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia), and [Germania Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_Superior)

Like all of [Southern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Germany), what is now Swabia was part of the [La Tène culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_T%C3%A8ne_culture); as such, it has a [Celtic (Gaulish)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls) substrate. In the Roman era, it was part of the [Raetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia) province.

The name *Suebia* is derived from that of the *[Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi)*. It is used already by [Tacitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_\(Tacitus\)) in the 1st century, albeit in a different geographical sense: He calls the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) the *Mare Suevicum* ("Suebian Sea") after the [Suiones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suiones), and ends his description of the Suiones and [Sitones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitones) with "Here Suebia ends" (*Hic Suebiae finis*).[^cite_note-Tacitus45-2] By the mid-3rd century, groups of the Suebi form the core element of the new tribal alliance known as the [Alamanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamanni), who expanded towards the [Roman Limes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Limes) east of the Rhine and south of the Main. The Alamanni were sometimes referred to as Suebi even at this time, and their new area of settlement came to be known as Suebia. In the [migration period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_period), the Suebi (Alamanni) [crossed the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_of_the_Rhine) in 406 and some of them established the [Kingdom of the Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Suebi) in Galicia. Another group settled in parts of [Pannonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia), after the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns) were defeated in 454 in the [Battle of Nedao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nedao).

The Alemanni were ruled by independent kings throughout the 4th to 5th centuries but fell under [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) domination in the 6th ([Battle of Tolbiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tolbiac) 496).[^cite_note-3] By the late 5th century, the area settled by the Alemanni extended to [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace) and the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau), bordering on the [Bavarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarii) to the east, the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) to the north, the remnants of [Roman Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Gaul) to the west, and the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) and [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths), united in the [Kingdom of Odoacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Odoacer), to the south.

The name *Alamannia* was used by the 8th century, and from the 9th century, *Suebia* was occasionally used for *Alamannia*, while *Alamannia* was increasingly used to refer to [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace) specifically. By the 12th century, *Suebia* rather than *Alamannia* was used consistently for the territory of the [Duchy of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia).[^cite_note-4]

### Duchy of Swabia

Main articles: [Duchy of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia) and [Dukes of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukes_of_Swabia)

Swabia was one of the original [stem duchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_duchy) of [East Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Francia), the later [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), as it developed in the 9th and 10th centuries. Due to the foundation of the important abbeys of [St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Gallen) and [Reichenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichenau_Abbey), Swabia became an important center of [Old High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_German) literary culture during this period.

In the later [Carolingian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_period), Swabia became once again de facto independent, by the early 10th century mostly ruled by two dynasties, the [Hunfriding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunfriding) counts in [Raetia Curiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia_Curiensis) and the [Ahalolfings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahalolfing) ruling the [Baar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baar_\(region\)) estates around the upper [Neckar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neckar) and Danube rivers. The conflict between the two dynasties was decided in favour of Hunfriding [Burchard II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burchard_II,_Duke_of_Swabia) at the [Battle of Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Winterthur_\(919\)) (919).[^cite_note-5] Burchard's rule as duke was acknowledged as such by the newly elected king [Henry the Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Fowler), and in the 960s the duchy under [Burchard III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burchard_III,_Duke_of_Swabia) was incorporated in the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) under [Otto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor).

The [Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen) dynasty, which ruled the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in the 12th and 13th centuries, arose out of Swabia, but following the execution of [Conradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conradin), the last Hohenstaufen, on 29 October 1268, the duchy was not reappointed during the [Great Interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregnum_\(HRE\)). In the following years, the original duchy gradually broke up into many smaller units.

[Rudolf I of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_I_of_Germany), elected in 1273 as emperor, tried to restore the duchy, but met the opposition of the higher nobility who aimed to limit the power of the emperor. Instead, he confiscated the former estates of the Hohenstaufen as imperial property of the Holy Roman Empire, and declared most of the cities formerly belonging to Hohenstaufen to be [Free Imperial Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Imperial_City), and the more powerful abbeys within the former duchy to be Imperial Abbeys.

The rural regions were merged into the [Imperial Shrievalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogt) (*Reichslandvogtei*) of Swabia, which was given as Imperial Pawn to Duke [Leopold III of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_III,_Duke_of_Austria) in 1379 and again to [Sigismund, Archduke of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund,_Archduke_of_Austria), in 1473/1486. He took the title of a "Prince of Swabia" and integrated the Shrievalty of Swabia in the realm of [Further Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_Austria).

### Later medieval period

Further information: [Interregnum (HRE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregnum_\(HRE\))

The [Swabian League of Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_League_of_Cities) was first formed on 20 November 1331, when twenty-two [imperial cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city) of the former Duchy of Swabia banded together in support of the [Emperor Louis IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Louis_IV), who in return promised not to mortgage any of them to any imperial [vassal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal). Among the founding cities were [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg), [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn), [Reutlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reutlingen), and [Ulm](189455.md). The counts of [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg), [Oettingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Oettingen-Wallerstein), and [Hohenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Hohenberg_\(Swabia\)) were induced to join in 1340.

The defeat of the city league by Count [Eberhard II of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberhard_II,_Count_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg) in 1372 led to the formation of a new league of fourteen Swabian cities on 4 July 1376. The emperor refused to recognise the newly revitalised Swabian League, seeing it as a rebellion, and this led to an "[imperial war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_war)" against the league. The renewed league defeated an imperial army at the Battle of [Reutlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reutlingen) on 14 May 1377. Burgrave [Frederick V of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_V,_Burgrave_of_Nuremberg) finally defeated the league in 1388 at [Döffingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grafenau,_W%C3%BCrttemberg). The next year the city league disbanded according to the resolutions of the [Reichstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_\(Holy_Roman_Empire\)) at [Eger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheb).

The major dynasties that arose out of medieval Swabia were the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg) and the [Hohenzollerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenzollern), who rose to prominence in Northern Germany. Also stemming from Swabia are the local dynasties of the dukes of [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg) and the [margraves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrave) of [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Baden). The [Welf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Welf) family went on to rule in [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) and [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hanover), and are ancestral to the [British royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family) that has ruled since 1714. Smaller feudal dynasties eventually disappeared, however; for example, branches of the [Montforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montfort_of_Vorarlberg) and [Hohenems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenems) lived until modern times, and the [Fürstenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCrstenberg_\(princely_family\)) survive still. The region proved to be one of the most divided in the empire, containing, in addition to these principalities, numerous [free cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city), ecclesiastical territories, and fiefdoms of lesser [counts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count) and [knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight).

### Early modern history



Map of the Swabian Circle (1756)

A new [Swabian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_League) (*Schwäbischer Bund*) was formed in 1488, opposing the expansionist [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bavaria) dukes from the [House of Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach) and the revolutionary threat from the south in the form of the [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaffan1975198-6] In 1519, the League conquered Württemberg and sold it to [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) after its duke [Ulrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich,_Duke_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg) seized the Free Imperial City of Reutlingen during the interregnum that followed the death of Maximilian I. It helped to suppress the [Peasants' Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Peasants'_War) in 1524–26 and defeat an alliance of [robber barons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robber_baron_\(feudalism\)) in the [Franconian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconian_War). The [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation) caused the league to be disbanded in 1534.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaffan1975198-6]

The territory of Swabia as understood today emerges in the early modern period. It corresponds to the [Swabian Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Circle) established in 1512. The [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy) was *de facto* independent from Swabia from 1499 as a result of the [Swabian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_War), while the [Margraviate of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Baden) had been detached from Swabia since the twelfth century.

Fearing the power of the greater princes, the cities and smaller secular rulers of Swabia joined to form the [Swabian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_League) in the fifteenth century. The League was quite successful, notably expelling the [Duke of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg) in 1519 and putting in his place a Habsburg governor, but the league broke up a few years later over religious differences inspired by the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), and the Duke of Württemberg was soon restored.

The region was quite divided by the Reformation. While secular princes such as the Duke of Württemberg and the Margrave of [Baden-Durlach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Durlach), as well as most of the Free Cities, became [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant), the ecclesiastical territories (including the [bishoprics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) of [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Augsburg), [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Constance) and the numerous [Imperial abbeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Imperial_abbeys)) remained [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church), as did the territories belonging to the Habsburgs ([Further Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_Austria)), the [Sigmaringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern#Swabian_branch) branch of the [House of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern), and the Margrave of [Baden-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Baden).

### Modern history

In the wake of the territorial reorganization of the empire of 1803 by the *[Reichsdeputationshauptschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsdeputationshauptschluss)*, the shape of Swabia was entirely changed. All the ecclesiastical estates were secularized, and most of the smaller secular states, and almost all of the free cities, were [mediatized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_mediatization), leaving only the [Kingdom of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg), the [Grand Duchy of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Baden), and the Principality of [Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen) as sovereign states. Much of Eastern Swabia became part of [Bavaria](3764.md), forming what is now the [Swabian administrative region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabia_\(Bavaria\)) of Bavaria. The [Kings of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) assumed the title *Duke in Swabia*, with the *in* indicating that only parts of the Swabian territory was ruled by them, unlike their other title *[Duke of Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Franconia)* which made clear that the whole of Franconia had become part of their kingdom.&#x20;

In contemporary usage, *Schwaben* is sometimes taken to refer to Bavarian Swabia exclusively, correctly however it includes the larger Württemberg part of Swabia. Its inhabitants attach great importance to calling themselves Swabians. Baden, historically part of the duchy of Swabia and also of the Swabian Circle, is no longer commonly included in the term. Baden's residents mostly refer to themselves as [Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) (versus the [Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabians)).

## Geography

Like many cultural regions of Europe, Swabia's borders are not clearly defined. However, today it is normally thought of as comprising the former [Swabian Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Circle), or equivalently the former state of [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg) (with the Prussian [Hohenzollern Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenzollern_Province)), or the modern districts of [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCbingen_\(region\)) (excluding the former Baden regions of the [Bodenseekreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodenseekreis) district), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_\(region\)), and the administrative region of [Bavarian Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Swabia).

In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), the term Swabia indicated a larger area, covering all the lands associated with the [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francia) stem duchy of [Alamannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamannia) stretching from the [Vosges Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vosges_Mountains) in the west to the broad [Lech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_\(river\)) river in the east. This also included the region of [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace) and the later [Margraviate of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Baden) on both sides of the [Upper Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Rhine) Valley, as well as modern German-speaking Switzerland, the Austrian state of [Vorarlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorarlberg) and the principality of [Liechtenstein](17810.md) in the south.

## Swabian people

Main article: [Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabians)

### Language

Main article: [Swabian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_German)



The traditional distribution area of Western Upper German ( = Alemannic) dialect features in the nineteenth and twentieth century

[SIL Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIL_Ethnologue) cites an estimate of 819,000 Swabian speakers as of 2006. This corresponds to roughly 10% of the total population of the Swabian region, or roughly 1% of the total population of Germany.

As an ethno-linguistic group, Swabians are closely related to other speakers of [Alemannic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German), i.e. [Badeners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden), [Alsatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace), and [German-speaking Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-speaking_Swiss).[^cite_note-7]

Swabian German is traditionally spoken in the upper [Neckar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neckar) basin (upstream of [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn)), along the upper [Danube](21209639.md) between [Tuttlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuttlingen) and [Donauwörth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donauw%C3%B6rth), in [Upper Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Swabia), and on the left bank of the [Lech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_\(river\)), in an area centered on the [Swabian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Alps) roughly stretching from [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) to [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg).

Many Swabian surnames end with the suffixes *-le*, *-(l)er*, *-el*, *-ehl*, and *-lin*, typically from the [Middle High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_High_German) [diminutive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminutive) suffix *-elîn* (Modern Standard German *-lein*). Examples would be: *Schäuble*, *Egeler*, *Rommel*, and *Gmelin*. The popular German surname *Schwab* as well as *Svevo* in Italy are derived from this area, both meaning literally "Swabian".
