**Severe weather** is any dangerous [meteorological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorological) phenomenon with the potential to cause damage, serious social disruption, or loss of life.[^cite_note-WMO-1][^cite_note-NSSL-2] These vary depending on the [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude), [altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude), [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography), and [atmospheric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric) conditions. High [winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind), [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail), excessive [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)), and [wildfires](56106.md) are forms and effects, as are [thunderstorms](70807.md), [downbursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downbursts), [tornadoes](37530.md), [waterspouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterspout), [tropical cyclones](8282374.md), and [extratropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone). Regional and seasonal phenomena include [blizzards](4548.md), [snowstorms](244878.md), [ice storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_storms), and [duststorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duststorm).[^cite_note-AMS1-3]

Severe weather is one type of [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather), which includes unexpected, unusual, severe, or unseasonal [weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather) and is by definition rare for that location or time of the year.[^cite_note-\:8-4] Due to the [effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change), the frequency and intensity of some of the extreme weather events are increasing, for example, [hot airflow direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airflow), [heatwaves](293270.md) and [droughts](37335.md).[^cite_note-5]<sup>: 9 </sup>

## Terminology

Meteorologists have generally defined *severe weather* as any aspect of the weather that poses risks to life or property or requires the intervention of authorities. A narrower definition of *severe weather* is any weather phenomenon relating to [severe thunderstorms](70807.md).[^cite_note-AMS1-3][^cite_note-Glossary2009-6]

According to the [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md) (WMO), severe weather can be categorized into two groups: general severe weather and localized severe weather.[^cite_note-WMO-1] [Nor'easters](402179.md), European wind storms, and the phenomena that accompany them form over wide geographic areas. These occurrences are classified as *general severe weather*.[^cite_note-WMO-1] Downbursts and tornadoes are more localized and therefore have a more limited geographic effect. These forms of weather are classified as *localized severe weather*.[^cite_note-WMO-1]

The term *severe weather* is technically not the same phenomenon as [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather). Extreme weather describes unusual weather events that are at the extremes of the historical distribution for a given area.[^cite_note-7]

## Causes



This graphic shows the conditions favorable for certain organized thunderstorm complexes, based upon [CAPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convective_available_potential_energy) and vertical [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) values.

See also: [Extreme weather § Causes and attribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather#Causes_and_attribution)

Organized severe weather occurs under the same conditions that generate ordinary thunderstorms: atmospheric moisture, lift (often from [thermals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal)), and [instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_instability).[^cite_note-SPC_FAQ-8] A wide variety of conditions cause severe weather. Several factors can convert thunderstorms into severe weather.  For example, a pool of cold air aloft may aid in the development of large hail from an otherwise innocuous-appearing thunderstorm. The most severe hail and tornadoes are produced by [supercell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercell) thunderstorms, and the worst [downbursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downburst) and [derechos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derecho) (straight-line winds) are produced by [bow echoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_echo). Both of these types of storms tend to form in environments with high [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear).[^cite_note-SPC_FAQ-8]

Floods, hurricanes, tornadoes, and thunderstorms are considered to be the most destructive weather-related [natural disasters](58921.md). Although these weather phenomena are all related to [cumulonimbus clouds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulonimbus_cloud), they form and develop under different conditions and geographic locations. The relationship between these weather events and their formation requirements is used to develop [models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_model) to predict the most frequent and possible locations. This information is used to notify affected areas and save lives.

## Categories



Diagram showing ingredients needed for severe weather. The red arrow shows the position of the low level jet stream, while the blue arrow shows the location of the upper-level jet stream.

Severe thunderstorms can be assessed in three different categories. These are "approaching severe", "severe", and "significantly severe".

**Approaching severe** is defined as hail between 1⁄2 to 1 inch (13 to 25 mm) diameter or winds between 50 and 58 mph (50 knots, 80–93 km/h). In the United States, such storms will usually warrant a [Significant Weather Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Significant_Weather_Alert).[^cite_note-9]

**Severe** is defined as hail 1 to 2 inches (25 to 51 mm) diameter, winds 58 to 75 miles per hour (93 to 121 km/h), or a tornado.[^cite_note-WhyOne-10]

**Significant severe** is defined as hail 2 inches (51 mm) in diameter or larger, winds 75 mph (65 knots, 120 km/h) or more, or a [tornado](37530.md) of strength EF2 or stronger.[^cite_note-WMO-1][^cite_note-OnlineSevere2007-11]

Both *severe* and *significant severe* events warrant a [severe thunderstorm warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_thunderstorm_warning) from the United States [National Weather Service](286989.md) (excludes flash floods), the [Environment Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_Canada), the [Australian Bureau of Meteorology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Bureau_of_Meteorology), the [Meteorological Service of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorological_Service_of_New_Zealand) and the Meteorological Office UK, if the event occurs in those countries. If a tornado is occurring (a tornado has been seen by [spotters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_spotter)) or is imminent ([Doppler weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_weather_radar) has observed [strong rotation in a storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_vortex_signature), indicating an incipient tornado), the severe thunderstorm warning will be superseded by a *[tornado warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_warning)* in the United States and Canada.[^cite_note-spc-faq-12]

A severe weather outbreak is typically considered to be when ten or more tornadoes, some of which will likely be long-tracked and violent, and *many* large hail or damaging wind reports occur within one or more consecutive days. Severity is also dependent on the size of the geographic area affected, whether it covers hundreds or thousands of square kilometers.[^cite_note-13]

## High winds

Main articles: [Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind) and [Beaufort scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_scale)



Panorama of a strong [shelf cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelf_cloud), which can precede the onset of high winds

High winds are known to cause damage, depending upon their strength.

Wind speeds as low as 23 knots (43 km/h) may lead to power outages when tree branches fall and disrupt power lines.[^cite_note-LightningPrinciples2009-14] Some species of trees are more vulnerable to winds. Trees with shallow roots are more prone to uproot, and brittle trees such as [eucalyptus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus), sea [hibiscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiscus), and [avocado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avocado) are more prone to branch damage.[^cite_note-Derek2006-15]

Wind gusts may cause poorly designed [suspension bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_bridge) to sway. When wind gusts [harmonize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony) with the frequency of the swaying bridge, the bridge may fail as occurred with the [Tacoma Narrows Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma_Narrows_Bridge_\(1940\)) in 1940.[^cite_note-Grazulis2001-16]

Hurricane-force winds, caused by individual thunderstorms, thunderstorm complexes, derechos, tornadoes, extratropical cyclones, or tropical cyclones can destroy mobile homes and structurally damage buildings with foundations. Winds of this strength due to downslope winds off terrain have been known to shatter windows and sandblast paint from cars.[^cite_note-boulder-17]

Once winds exceed 135 knots (250 km/h) within strong tropical cyclones and tornadoes, homes completely collapse, and significant damage is done to larger buildings. Total destruction to man-made structures occurs when winds reach 175 knots (324 km/h). The [Saffir–Simpson scale](255313.md) for cyclones and [Enhanced Fujita scale](3724479.md) ([TORRO scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TORRO_scale) in Europe) for tornadoes were developed to help estimate wind speed from the damage they cause.[^cite_note-NHC_SSHS-18][^cite_note-EF_SPC-19]

### Tornado

Main article: [Tornado](37530.md)



The F5 tornado that struck [Elie, Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie,_Manitoba), Canada, in 2007

A dangerous rotating column of air in contact with both the surface of the earth and the base of a [cumulonimbus cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulonimbus_cloud) (thundercloud) or a [cumulus cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulus_cloud), in rare cases. Tornadoes come in many sizes but typically form a visible [condensation funnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funnel_cloud) whose narrowest end reaches the earth and surrounded by a cloud of [debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debris) and [dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust).[^cite_note-Renno-20]

Tornadoes' wind speeds generally average between 40 miles per hour (64 km/h) and 110 miles per hour (180 km/h). They are approximately 250 feet (76 m) across  and travel a few miles (kilometers) before dissipating. Some attain wind speeds in excess of 300 miles per hour (480 km/h), may stretch more than two miles (3.2 km) across, and maintain contact with the ground for dozens of miles (more than 100 km).[^cite_note-autogenerated2-21][^cite_note-fastest_wind-22][^cite_note-widest_tornado-23] The [Enhanced Fujita Scale](3724479.md) and the [TORRO Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TORRO_Scale) are two examples of scales used to rate the strength, intensity and/or damage of a tornado.

Tornadoes, despite being one of the most destructive weather phenomena, are generally short-lived. A long-lived tornado generally lasts no more than an hour, but some have been known to last for 2 hours or longer (for example, the [Tri-State Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-State_Tornado)). Due to their relatively short duration, less information is known about the development and formation of tornadoes.[^cite_note-Tornadoes2008-24]

### Waterspout

Main article: [Waterspout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterspout)



Formation of numerous waterspouts in the Great Lakes region

Waterspouts are generally defined as tornadoes or non-[supercell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercell) tornadoes that develop over bodies of water.[^cite_note-Glossary-25]

Waterspouts typically do not do much damage because they occur over open water, but they are capable of traveling over land. Vegetation, weakly constructed buildings, and other infrastructure may be damaged or destroyed by waterspouts. Waterspouts do not generally last long over terrestrial environments as the friction produced easily dissipates the winds. Strong horizontal winds will cause waterspouts to dissipate as they disturb the vortex.[^cite_note-erh.noaa.gov-26] While not generally as dangerous as "classic" tornadoes, waterspouts can overturn boats, and they can cause severe damage to larger ships.[^cite_note-spc-faq-12]

### Downburst and derecho

Main articles: [Downburst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downburst) and [Derecho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derecho)

&#x20;Downbursts are created within thunderstorms by significantly rain-cooled air, which, upon reaching ground level, spreads out in all directions and produce strong winds. Unlike winds in a [tornado](37530.md), winds in a downburst are not rotational but are directed outwards from the point where they strike land or water.



Illustration of a microburst. The air moves in a downward motion until it hits ground level. It then spreads outward in all directions.

&#x20;"Dry downbursts" are associated with [thunderstorms](70807.md) with very little precipitation,[^cite_note-micro-27] while **wet downbursts** are generated by thunderstorms with large amounts of rainfall. [Microbursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbursts) are very small downbursts with winds that extend up to 2.5 miles (4 km) from their source, while [macrobursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroburst) are large-scale [downbursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downbursts) with winds that extend in excess of 2.5 miles (4 km).[^cite_note-Microburst-28] The **heat burst** is created by vertical currents on the backside of old [outflow boundaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outflow_boundary) and [squall lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squall_line) where rainfall is lacking. Heat bursts generate significantly higher temperatures due to the lack of rain-cooled air in their formation.[^cite_note-Oklahomaheat1999-29] **[Derechos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derecho)** are longer, usually stronger, forms of downburst winds characterized by straight-lined windstorms.[^cite_note-noaa-30][^cite_note-EW-31]

Downbursts create vertical [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) or [microbursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microburst), which are dangerous to aviation.[^cite_note-National1992-32] These [convective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_convection) **downbursts** can produce damaging winds, lasting 5 to 30 minutes, with wind speeds as high as 168 mph (75 m/s), and cause tornado-like damage on the ground. Downbursts also occur much more frequently than tornadoes, with ten downburst damage reports for every one tornado.[^cite_note-columbia-33]

### Squall line



[Cyclonic vortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) over Pennsylvania with a trailing squall line

Main articles: [Squall line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squall_line), [Severe thunderstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_thunderstorm), [Bow echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_echo), and [Derecho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derecho)

A squall line is an elongated line of [severe thunderstorms](70807.md#Severe_thunderstorms) that can form along or ahead of a [cold front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front).[^cite_note-Glossary2009a-34][^cite_note-Glossary2009b-35]  The squall line typically contains heavy [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)), [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail), frequent [lightning](61344.md), strong straight line winds, and possibly [tornadoes](37530.md) or [waterspouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterspouts).[^cite_note-Office2008-36] Severe weather in the form of strong straight-line winds can be expected in areas where the squall line forms a [bow echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_echo), in the farthest portion of the bow.[^cite_note-Glossary2009c-37] [Tornadoes](37530.md) can be found along waves within a line echo wave pattern (LEWP) where mesoscale [low-pressure areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) are present.[^cite_note-Glossary2009d-38] Intense bow echoes responsible for widespread, extensive wind damage are called [derechos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derecho), and move quickly over large territories.[^cite_note-noaa-30] A [wake low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_low) or a mesoscale low-pressure area forms behind the rain shield (a high pressure system under the rain canopy) of a mature squall line and is sometimes associated with a [heat burst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_burst).[^cite_note-Glossary2009e-39]

Squall lines often cause severe straight-line wind damage, and most non-tornadic wind damage is caused from squall lines.[^cite_note-Louisville-40] Although the primary danger from squall lines is straight-line winds, some squall lines also contain weak tornadoes.[^cite_note-Louisville-40]

### Tropical cyclone



[Hurricane Isabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Isabel) (2003) as seen from orbit during Expedition 7 of the International Space Station

Main article: [Tropical cyclone](8282374.md)

Very high winds can be caused by mature tropical cyclones (called *hurricanes* in the United States and Canada and *typhoons* in eastern Asia). A tropical cyclone's heavy [surf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_wave) created by such winds may cause harm to marine life either close to or upon the surface of the water, such as [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reefs).[^cite_note-Dan2004-41] Coastal regions usually take more serious wind damage than inland, due to rapid dissipation upon landfall, though heavy rain from their remnants may flood either.

### Strong extratropical cyclones



[GOES-13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOES-13) Imagery of an intense Nor'Easter that impacted the North East US on 26 March 2014 and produced recorded gusts of 101mph+

Main article: [Extratropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone)

Severe local windstorms in Europe that develop from winds off the North Atlantic. These windstorms are commonly associated with the destructive extratropical cyclones and their low pressure frontal systems.[^cite_note-EuropeanWindstorms-42] European windstorms occur mainly in the seasons of autumn and winter.[^cite_note-EuroWind-43] Severe European windstorms are often characterized by heavy precipitation as well.

A [synoptic-scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synoptic_scale_meteorology) extratropical storm along the upper [East Coast of the United States](89126.md) and [Atlantic Canada](71095.md) is called a [Nor'easter](402179.md). They are so named because their winds come from the [northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_direction), especially in the coastal areas of the [Northeastern United States](431669.md) and Atlantic Canada. More specifically, it describes a [low-pressure area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) whose center of [rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation) is just off the upper East Coast and whose leading winds in the left forward quadrant rotate onto land from the northeast. Nor'easters may cause [coastal flooding](31546082.md), [coastal erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion), heavy rain or snow, and [hurricane-force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_Scale) winds. The precipitation pattern of Nor'easters is similar to other mature [extratropical storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone). Nor'easters can cause heavy rain or snow, either within their comma-head precipitation pattern or along their trailing cold or stationary front. Nor'easters can occur at any time of the year but are mostly known for their presence in the winter season.[^cite_note-disc3-44]

### Dust storm

Main article: [Dust storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm)

A dust storm is an unusual form of windstorm that is characterized by the existence of large quantities of sand and dust particles carried by the wind.[^cite_note-Glossary_a-45] Dust storms frequently develop during periods of droughts, or over arid and semi-arid regions.&#x20;



A massive dust storm cloud ([Haboob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haboob)) is close to enveloping a military camp as it rolls over [Al Asad Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Asad_Airbase), [Iraq](7515928.md), just before nightfall on 27 April 2005.

Dust storms have numerous hazards and are capable of causing deaths. Visibility may be reduced dramatically, so risks of vehicle and aircraft crashes are possible. Additionally, the particulates may reduce oxygen intake by the lungs,[^cite_note-Duststorms-46] potentially resulting in suffocation. Damage can also be inflicted upon the eyes due to abrasion.[^cite_note-DustStorm-47]

Dust storms cause many issues for agricultural industries as well. Soil erosion is one of the most common hazards and decreases [arable lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land). Dust and sand particles can cause severe weathering of buildings and rock formations. Nearby bodies of water may be polluted by settling dust and sand, killing aquatic organisms. Decrease in exposure to sunlight can affect plant growth, as well as decrease in infrared radiation may cause decreased temperatures.

### Wildfires

Main article: [Wildfire](56106.md)

See also: [Dry lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_lightning)



Wildfire in [Yellowstone National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_National_Park) produces a pyrocumulus cloud.

The most common cause of wildfires varies throughout the world. In the United States, Canada, and Northwest China, lightning is the major source of ignition. In other parts of the world, human involvement is a major contributor. For instance, in Mexico, Central America, South America, Africa, Southeast Asia, Fiji, and New Zealand, wildfires can be attributed to human activities such as [animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry), agriculture, and land-conversion burning. Human carelessness is a major cause of wildfires in China and in the [Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin). In Australia, the source of wildfires can be traced to both lightning strikes and human activities such as machinery sparks and cast-away cigarette butts."[^cite_note-Krock-48] Wildfires have a rapid *forward rate of spread* (FROS) when burning through dense, uninterrupted fuels.[^cite_note-49] They can move as fast as 10.8 kilometers per hour (6.7 mph) in forests and 22 kilometers per hour (14 mph) in grasslands.[^cite_note-50] Wildfires can advance tangential to the main front to form a *flanking* front, or burn in the opposite direction of the main front by *backing*.[^cite_note-51]

Wildfires may also spread by *jumping* or *spotting* as winds and vertical [convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection) columns carry *firebrands* (hot wood embers) and other burning materials through the air over roads, rivers, and other barriers that may otherwise act as [firebreaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firebreak).[^cite_note-underfire-52][^cite_note-53] Torching and fires in tree canopies encourage spotting, and dry ground fuels that surround a wildfire are especially vulnerable to ignition from firebrands.[^cite_note-54] Spotting can create *spot fires* as hot embers and firebrands ignite fuels downwind from the fire. In Australian bushfires, spot fires are known to occur as far as 10 kilometers (6 mi) from the fire front.[^cite_note-55] Since the mid-1980s, earlier snowmelt and associated warming has also been associated with an increase in length and severity of the wildfire season in the [Western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States).[^cite_note-Westerling2006-56]

## Hail

Main articles: [Hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail) and [List of costly or deadly hailstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_costly_or_deadly_hailstorms)



A large hailstone with concentric rings

Any form of thunderstorm that produces precipitating [hailstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailstones) is known as a hailstorm.[^cite_note-Glossary_b-57] Hailstorms are generally capable of developing in any geographic area where [thunderclouds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thundercloud) ([cumulonimbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulonimbus)) are present, although they are most frequent in tropical and monsoon regions.[^cite_note-Factsabout2009-58] The updrafts and downdrafts within cumulonimbus clouds cause water molecules to freeze and solidify, creating hailstones and other forms of solid precipitation.[^cite_note-Hailstorms-59] Due to their larger density, these hailstones become heavy enough to overcome the density of the cloud and fall towards the ground. The downdrafts in cumulonimbus clouds can also cause increases in the speed of the falling hailstones. The term *hailstorm* is usually used to describe the existence of significant quantities or size of hailstones.

Hailstones can cause serious damage, notably to [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile), aircraft, skylights, glass-roofed structures, [livestock](25160767.md), and [crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crops).[^cite_note-Nolanhail-60] Rarely, massive hailstones have been known to cause [concussions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concussion) or fatal head [trauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_trauma). Hailstorms have been the cause of costly and deadly events throughout history. One of the earliest recorded incidents occurred around the 12th century in [Wellesbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesbourne), Britain.[^cite_note-HailstormsExtremes-61] The largest hailstone in terms of maximum circumference and length ever recorded in the United States fell in 2003 in [Aurora, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora,_Nebraska), USA. The hailstone had a diameter of 7 inches (18 cm) and a circumference of 18.75 inches (47.6 cm).[^cite_note-62]

## Heavy rainfall and flooding



A flash flood caused by a severe thunderstorm

Main articles: [Rain](19009110.md), [Flood](50482.md), and [Flash flood](244391.md)

Heavy rainfall can lead to a number of hazards, most of which are floods or hazards resulting from floods. Flooding is the inundation of areas that are not normally under water. It is typically divided into three classes: River flooding, which relates to rivers rising outside their normal banks; flash flooding, which is the process where a landscape, often in urban and arid environments, is subjected to rapid floods;[^cite_note-Glossary2009f-63] and coastal flooding, which can be caused by strong winds from tropical or non-tropical cyclones.[^cite_note-64] [Meteorologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorology), excessive rains occur within a plume of air with high amounts of moisture (also known as an [atmospheric river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_river)), which is directed around an upper level [cold-core low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold-core_low) or a tropical cyclone.[^cite_note-Boniface2010-65]

Flash flooding can frequently occur in slow-moving thunderstorms and are usually caused by the heavy liquid precipitation that accompanies it. Flash floods are most common in densely populated urban environments, where less plants and bodies of water are presented to absorb and contain the extra water. Flash flooding can be hazardous to small infrastructure, such as bridges, and weakly constructed buildings. Plants and crops in agricultural areas can be destroyed and devastated by the force of raging water. Automobiles parked within experiencing areas can also be displaced. [Soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil) erosion can occur as well, exposing risks of [landslide](37208.md) phenomena. Like all forms of flooding phenomenon, flash flooding can also spread and produce [waterborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterborne_disease) and [insect-borne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect-borne_disease) diseases cause by microorganisms. Flash flooding can be caused by extensive rainfall released by tropical cyclones of any strength or the sudden thawing effect of [ice dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_jam).[^cite_note-WeatherEye2007-66][^cite_note-srh.noaa.gov-67]

### Monsoons

Main article: [Monsoon](57630.md)

Seasonal wind shifts lead to long-lasting [wet seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season), which produce the bulk of annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) in areas such as Southeast Asia, Australia, Western Africa, eastern South America, Mexico, and the Philippines. Widespread flooding occurs if rainfall is excessive,[^cite_note-68] which can lead to landslides and mudflows in mountainous areas.[^cite_note-69] Floods cause rivers to exceed their capacity with nearby buildings becoming submerged.[^cite_note-70] Flooding may be exacerbated if there are fires during the previous dry season. This may cause soils that are sandy or composed of [loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loam) to become hydrophobic and repel water.[^cite_note-71]

Government organizations help their residents deal with wet-season floods though [floodplain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodplain) mapping and information on erosion control. Mapping is conducted to help determine areas that may be more prone to flooding.[^cite_note-72] Erosion control instructions are provided through outreach over the telephone or the internet.[^cite_note-73]

Flood waters that occur during monsoon seasons can often host numerous [protozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist), [bacterial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria), and [viral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus) microorganisms.[^cite_note-PreventingDisease-74] Mosquitoes and flies will lay their eggs within the contaminated bodies of water. These disease agents may cause infections of foodborne and waterborne diseases. Diseases associated with exposure to flood waters include [malaria](20423.md), [cholera](7591.md), [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid), [hepatitis A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_A), and the [common cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_cold).[^cite_note-Monsoondiseases2009-75] Possible [trench foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_foot) infections may also occur when personnel are exposed for extended periods of time within flooded areas.[^cite_note-76]

### Tropical cyclone

Main article: [Tropical cyclone](8282374.md)



The damage caused by [Hurricane Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Andrew) is a good example of the damage caused by a category 5 Tropical cyclone

A tropical cyclone is a rapidly rotating storm system characterized by a low-pressure center, a closed low-level atmospheric circulation, strong winds, and a spiral arrangement of thunderstorms that produce heavy rain or squalls. A tropical cyclone feeds on heat released when moist air rises, resulting in [condensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensation) of [water vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_vapor) contained in the moist air. Tropical cyclones may produce torrential rain, high waves, and damaging [storm surge](449744.md).[^cite_note-oxfo-77] Heavy rains produce significant inland flooding. Storm surges may produce extensive coastal flooding up to 40 kilometres (25 mi) from the coastline.

Although [cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone) take an enormous toll in lives and personal property, they are also important factors in the precipitation regimes of areas they affect. They bring much-needed precipitation to otherwise dry regions.[^cite_note-2005_EPac_outlook-78] Areas in their path can receive a year's worth of rainfall from a tropical cyclone passage.[^cite_note-Jack2005-79] Tropical cyclones can also relieve [drought](37335.md) conditions.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-80] They also carry heat and energy away from the tropics and transport it toward [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate) [latitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitudes), which makes them an important part of the global [atmospheric circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_circulation) mechanism. As a result, tropical cyclones help to maintain equilibrium in the Earth's [troposphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troposphere).

## Severe winter weather

### Heavy snowfall

Main articles: [Blizzard](4548.md), [Lake-effect snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake-effect_snow), [Snow](28191.md), and [Winter storm](244878.md)



Damage caused by [Lake Storm "Aphid"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Storm_%22Aphid%22) in October 2006

When extratropical cyclones deposit heavy, wet snow with a snow-water equivalent (SWE) ratio of between 6\:1 and 12\:1 and a weight in excess of 10 pounds per square foot (\~50 kg/m<sup>2</sup>)[^cite_note-Historic_snowfall_for_the_Niagara_Frontier-81] piles onto trees or electricity lines, significant damage may occur on a scale usually associated with strong tropical cyclones.[^cite_note-Historic_Lake_Effect_Snow_Storm_of_October_12–13,_2006-82] An [avalanche](73321.md) can occur with a sudden thermal or mechanical impact on snow that has accumulated on a mountain, which causes the snow to rush downhill suddenly. Preceding an avalanche is a phenomenon known as an avalanche wind caused by the approaching avalanche itself, which adds to its destructive potential.[^cite_note-Glossary2009g-83] Large amounts of snow that accumulate on top of man-made structures can lead to structural failure.[^cite_note-Gershon2009-84] During snowmelt, acidic precipitation that previously fell in the snow pack is released and harms marine life.[^cite_note-Samuel1995-85]

Lake-effect snow is produced in the winter in the shape of one or more elongated bands. This occurs when cold winds move across long expanses of warmer lake water, providing energy and picking up [water vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_vapor), which then freezes and is deposited on the [lee shores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_shore).[^cite_note-Glossary2009h-86] For more information on this effect see the main article.

Conditions within blizzards often include large quantities of blowing snow and strong winds that may significantly reduce visibility. Reduced viability of personnel on foot may result in extended exposure to the blizzard and increase the chance of becoming lost. The strong winds associated with blizzards create [wind chill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_chill) that can result in [frostbites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostbite) and [hypothermia](146879.md). The strong winds present in blizzards are capable of damaging plants and may cause power outages, frozen pipes, and cut off fuel lines.[^cite_note-Blizzards-87]

### Ice storm

Main article: [Ice storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_storm)



Trees that have been destroyed by an ice storm.

Ice storms are also known as a *Silver storm*, referring to the color of the freezing precipitation.[^cite_note-amsglossary.allenpress.com-88] Ice storms are caused by liquid [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) which freezes upon cold surfaces and leads to the gradual development of a thickening layer of ice.[^cite_note-amsglossary.allenpress.com-88] The accumulations of ice during the storm can be extremely destructive. Trees and vegetation can be destroyed and in turn may bring down power lines, causing the loss of heat and communication lines.[^cite_note-Glossary2009i-89] Roofs of buildings and automobiles may be severely damaged. Gas pipes can become frozen or even damaged causing gas leaks. [Avalanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalanches) may develop due to the extra weight of the ice present. Visibility can be reduced dramatically. The aftermath of an ice storm may result in severe flooding due to sudden thawing, with large quantities of displaced water, especially near lakes, rivers, and bodies of water.[^cite_note-WinterStormsp-90]

## Heat and drought

### Drought

Main article: [Drought](37335.md)



Crops in Australia that have failed due to drought conditions

Another form of severe weather is drought, which is a prolonged period of persistently dry weather (that is, absence of precipitation).[^cite_note-drought-91] Although droughts do not develop or progress as quickly as other forms of severe weather,[^cite_note-creeping-92] their effects can be just as deadly; in fact, droughts are classified and measured based upon these effects.[^cite_note-drought-91] Droughts have a variety of severe effects; they can cause crops to fail,[^cite_note-creeping-92] and they can severely deplete water resources, sometimes interfering with human life.[^cite_note-drought-91] A drought in the 1930s known as the [Dust Bowl](59749.md) affected 50 million acres of farmland in the central United States.[^cite_note-drought-91] In economic terms, they can cost many billions of dollars: a drought in the United States in 1988 caused over \$40 billion in losses,[^cite_note-93] exceeding the economic totals of [Hurricane Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Andrew), the [Great Flood of 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mississippi_and_Missouri_Rivers_Flood_of_1993), and the [1989 Loma Prieta earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Loma_Prieta_earthquake).[^cite_note-creeping-92] In addition to the other severe effects, the dry conditions caused by droughts also significantly increase the risk of wildfires.[^cite_note-drought-91]

### Heat waves

Main article: [Heat wave](293270.md)

See also: [Heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index)



A map indicating above-normal temperatures in Europe in 2003

Although official definitions vary, a heat wave is generally defined as a prolonged period with excessive heat.[^cite_note-aussie1-94] Although heat waves do not cause as much economic damage as other types of severe weather, they are extremely dangerous to humans and animals: according to the United States National Weather Service, the average total number of heat-related fatalities each year is higher than the combined total fatalities for floods, tornadoes, lightning strikes, and hurricanes.[^cite_note-heatwave-95] In Australia, heat waves cause more fatalities than any other type of severe weather.[^cite_note-aussie1-94] The dry conditions that may accompany heat waves can also severely affect plant life as the plants lose moisture and die.[^cite_note-aussie2-96] Heat waves are often more severe when combined with high humidity.[^cite_note-heatwave-95]
