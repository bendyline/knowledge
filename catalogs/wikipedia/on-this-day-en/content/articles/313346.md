# Shutout

For other uses, see [Shut Out (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shut_Out_\(disambiguation\)).

Not to be confused with [Penalty shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout).



Goaltender [Martin Brodeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Brodeur) holds several [individual National Hockey League records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NHL_records_\(individual\)) for shutouts.

In team sports, a **shutout** ([U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_English)) or **clean sheet** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_English)) is a game in which the losing team fails to score. While possible in most major sports, they are highly improbable in some sports, such as [basketball](3921.md). Shutouts are usually seen as a result of effective defensive play even though a weak opposing offense may be as much to blame. Some sports credit individual players, particularly [goalkeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goalkeeper) and [starting pitchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_pitcher), with shutouts and keep track of them as statistics; others do not.

## American football

Shutouts in [American football](18951490.md) are uncommon. Keeping an opponent scoreless in American football requires a team's defense to be able to consistently shut down both pass and run offenses over the course of a game. The difficulty of completing a shutout is compounded by the many ways a team can score in the game. For example, teams can attempt field goals, which have a high rate of success. The range of NFL caliber kickers makes it possible for a team with a weak offense to get close enough to the goalposts (within "[field goal range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_range)") to kick a field goal. Of 2,544 regular season NFL games from 2000 to 2009, 89 (3.5%) were shutouts.

There are at least five instances in American football in which a team had been shut out throughout an entire season, and four in which a team has shut out all of their opponents in the season (the longest of these being the ten-game [perfect season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_season) in which the 1933 [Providence Huskies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Huskies) did not concede a single point).[^cite_note-1]

In college football, the [Tennessee Volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Volunteers_football) hold the record for most consecutive shutout wins with 17. The streak started against [Tennessee-Chattanooga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattanooga_Mocs_football) on November 30, 1938 and ended with a 27–12 loss against Alabama on October 19, 1940.[^cite_note-2]

The achievement of a shutout is much more difficult in [Canadian football](6963.md), where scoring and offensive movement is generally more frequent and a [single point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_\(football\)) can be scored by kicking the ball into the [end zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_zone) such that the other team does not, or cannot, return it or kick it out of the end zone.

## Association football



Goalkeeper [Ray Clemence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Clemence) recorded 537 "clean sheets" during his career.

In [association football](10568.md) and other sports with a [goalkeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goalkeeper), the goalkeeper may be said to "keep a **clean sheet**" if they prevent their opponents from scoring during an entire match. Because football is a relatively low-scoring game, it is common for one team, or even both teams, to score no goals.[^cite_note-wg_cs-3] A theory as to the term's origin is that sports reporters used separate pieces of paper to record the different statistical details of a game. If one team did not allow a goal, then that team's "details of goals conceded" page would appear blank, leaving a clean sheet. If a game ends with a final score of 0–0, both sides are considered to have kept a clean sheet.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## Baseball



[Nolan Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolan_Ryan) recorded 61 shutouts during his career as a major-league pitcher.

Main article: [Shutout (baseball)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shutout_\(baseball\))

In [Major League Baseball](38776.md), a shutout (denoted statistically as **ShO** or **SHO**[^cite_note-6]) refers to the act by which a single [pitcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcher) pitches a [complete game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_game) and does not allow the opposing team to score a [run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_\(baseball\)). If two or more pitchers combine to complete this act, no pitcher will be awarded a shutout, although the team itself can be said to have "shut out" the opposing team. The only exception to this is when a pitcher enters a game before the opposing team scores a run or makes an out and then completes the game without allowing a run to score. That pitcher is then awarded a shutout, although not a complete game.

The all-time career leader in shutouts is [Walter Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Johnson), who pitched for the [Washington Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Senators_\(1901%E2%80%9360\)) from 1907 to 1927. He accumulated 110 shutouts,[^cite_note-7] which is 20 more than second placed [Grover Cleveland Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland_Alexander).[^cite_note-8] The most shutouts recorded in one season was 16, which was a feat accomplished by both Grover Alexander (1916) and [George Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bradley) (1876).[^cite_note-9] These records are considered among the most secure records in baseball, as pitchers today rarely earn more than one or two shutouts per season with a heavy emphasis on [pitch count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_count) and [relief pitching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_pitcher). Complete games themselves have also become rare among [starting pitchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_pitcher). As of 2021, the current active leader in shutouts is [Clayton Kershaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton_Kershaw) of the [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md), whose 15 shutouts ties him for 463rd all time. Only four pitchers whose entire careers were in the post-1920 [live-ball era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-ball_era) threw as many as 60 career shutouts, with [Warren Spahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Spahn) leading those pitchers with 63.[^cite_note-BaseballRefSHO-10]

## Ice hockey



Goaltender [George Hainsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hainsworth) recorded 22 shutouts during the [1928–29 NHL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928%E2%80%9329_NHL_season).

In [ice hockey](14790.md), a shutout (**SO**) is credited to a [goaltender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goaltender) who successfully stops the other team from scoring during the entire game. In regular season games, if the score is 0–0 with the game going to a penalty shootout, both goaltenders are credited with a shutout.[^cite_note-11] The record holder for most regular-season career shutouts in the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) is [Martin Brodeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Brodeur) with 125 (see the [all-time regular season shutout leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NHL_statistical_leaders#Regular_season_shutouts)). For a single NHL season, the most shutouts recorded by a goaltender is 22, by [George Hainsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hainsworth) during the 1928–29 season. The modern-day record for a team being shut out in a season is held by the [Columbus Blue Jackets](73128.md) at 16, during the [2006–07 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_NHL_season).

In the event that a shutout is accomplished by a team using more than one goaltender in the game, the shutout is credited to the team, and no goaltender is awarded a shutout. This has happened several times in NHL history:

- On April 3, 1983, the [Washington Capitals](73125.md) won 3–0 against the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) with [Al Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jensen) in goals for the first two periods and [Pat Riggin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Riggin) for the last period.

  [^cite_note-12]

- On November 23, 2006, the [Nashville Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Predators) won 6–0 against the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) with goaltender [Tomáš Vokoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Vokoun) being replaced at the start of the third period by [Chris Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Mason_\(ice_hockey\)) due to injury.

  [^cite_note-13]

- On December 1, 2009, the [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) won 3–0 against the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md). [Jonas Gustavsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Gustavsson) started in goal but was replaced after the first period by [Joey MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_MacDonald) because of a cardiac problem.

  [^cite_note-14]

- On March 26, 2013, the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) won 1–0 against the Montreal Canadiens with [Marc-André Fleury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Andr%C3%A9_Fleury) starting the game and being replaced because of injury by Tomáš Vokoun for the third period.

  [^cite_note-15]

- On December 3, 2021, the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) won 1–0 against the [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) after [Igor Shesterkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Shesterkin) was injured and replaced by [Alexandar Georgiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandar_Georgiev) in the third period.

- On March 19, 2023, the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) won 3–0 against the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) after [Joel Hofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Hofer) had an equipment malfunction and was replaced by [Thomas Greiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Greiss) for 2\:36 in the 2nd period. Hofer then returned after fixing his equipment.

- On March 10, 2024, the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) won 4–0 against the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) after [Calvin Pickard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Pickard) was replaced with [Stuart Skinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Skinner) for the last 1\:16 of the second period and did not face a shot. Pickard then returned for the third period.

  [^cite_note-16]

## Rugby

Clean sheets are not common in either [rugby union](25405.md) or [league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league), since it is relatively simple to score a penalty kick. The 2005 Gillette [Rugby League Tri-Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Rugby_League_Tri-Nations#Final_2) final was the first time that [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_national_rugby_league_team) had been "nilled" since 1981. There is no alternative term for the occurrence of a team failing to score, except to say that the team scored "nil" (or "zero" or "nothing" in North America). For example, the December 2006 [Celtic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro14) match between [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Rugby) and [Connacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht_Rugby) ended 13–0 to Munster;[^cite_note-17] it was, therefore, said that Munster won "thirteen–nil."

Recent examples of clean sheets in international rugby union include England vs Scotland in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Six_Nations_Championship), France vs Italy in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Six_Nations_Championship), France vs Argentina in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_June_rugby_union_tests), Scotland vs Italy in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Six_Nations_Championship), New Zealand vs South Africa in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Rugby_Championship), New Zealand vs Australia in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup_warm-up_matches), and Wales vs Italy in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Six_Nations_Championship).

Generally, a team that is well-disciplined defensively, as well as behaviorally (not giving away penalty kicks), is most likely to not concede scores. This may also occur if there is a significant difference in class between the two teams, for example, when [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team) beat [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_rugby_union_team) (who were playing in their only Rugby World Cup) 48–0 in the [1999 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Rugby_World_Cup),[^cite_note-18] or when Australia beat [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_rugby_union_team) 142–0 in the [2003 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup). [^cite_note-19]
