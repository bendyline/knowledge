**Gospić** (pronounced [\[ɡǒːspitɕ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian)) is a town in [Lika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lika), [Croatia](5573.md). It is the seat of the [Lika-Senj County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lika-Senj_County). It is located in the mountainous region of Lika, one of the least densely populated areas of the country, and lies along important inland transport routes connecting northern and southern Croatia.&#x20;

The city serves as a local economic, cultural, and administrative hub for the surrounding rural settlements. Gospić has historical significance dating back to earlier periods of regional development in Lika, and today it is known for its quiet environment, natural surroundings, and proximity to national parks and mountainous landscapes.

## History

The area of Gospić and its surroundings has been inhabited since prehistoric times. This is evidenced by numerous archaeological finds, especially those of the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) tribe of the [Iapodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iapodes).

Even before the arrival of the [Croats](69801.md), this area was inhabited by an indigenous population consisting of descendants of the Iapods and Roman settlers who ruled Lika, which was part of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire).

The first organised inhabitation of the area was recorded in 1263 as *Kaseg* or *Kasezi*. The name *Gospić* is first mentioned in 1604, which likely originates from the [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language) word for "lady" (*gospa*) or another archaic form, *gospava*.

During the [Ottoman wars in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_wars_in_Europe), Gospić was for a time ruled by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) as part of [Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak) of Lika initially in [Rumeli Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumeli_Eyalet) (1528–1580), later in [Bosnia Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_Eyalet) (1580–1686). Today's town was built around two [Ottoman](22278.md) forts (the towers of [Aga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_\(Ottoman_Empire\)) Senković and of Aga Alić).

The Turkish incursion was repelled by the end of the 17th century and Gospić became an administrative centre of the [Lika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lika) region within the [Croatian Military Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Military_Frontier), a section of the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) organized as defense against the Ottomans. Before 1850, there are references to the town as *Gospich* in German. Since this time, there's been a significant population of [ethnic Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Croatia) in the Gospić region.

Politician and writer [Ante Starčević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ante_Star%C4%8Devi%C4%87) was born in 1823 in the village of [Veliki Žitnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliki_%C5%BDitnik) near Gospić. In Croatia he has been referred to as [Father of the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_of_the_Nation).

Scientist and inventor [Nikola Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Tesla), of Serb descent, was born in the nearby village of [Smiljan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiljan,_Croatia) and grew up in Gospić between 1862 and 1870, when he moved to [Karlovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovac). He again lived in Smiljan between 1873 and 1879, when he briefly returned to Gospić until 1880 when he went to [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz).

In the 19th century, Gospić was the headquarters of Military Frontier "Likaner Regiment" N° I.[^cite_note-3] Following the [compromise of 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1867), it became part of the [Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Croatia-Slavonia). In the late 19th century and early 20th century, Gospić was part of the [Lika-Krbava County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lika-Krbava_County).

In 1926, an [HKD Napredak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HKD_Napredak) branch was founded by the [Lička štedionica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C4%8Dka_%C5%A1tedionica?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Gospić.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatić20041185-4]

During the [Genocide of Serbs in the Independent State of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Serbs_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia) in [WWII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWII), the district of Gospić experienced the first large-scale massacres which occurred in the Lika region, as some 3,000 Serb civilians were killed between late July and early August 1941.[^cite_note-Biondich-5] A [concentration camp was established in Gospić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospi%C4%87_concentration_camp) in which (together with other camps that belonged to the same complex) the Ustaše might have killed between 24,000-42,000 people, most of them being [Serbs](59512.md) and [Jews](25955086.md), but some of the prisoners were also [Croatian](69801.md).[^cite_note-Israeli2013-6][^cite_note-7]

On April 4, 1945, [Yugoslav Partisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans) units killed several hundred people without trial after entering the city.[^cite_note-8] The remains of 253 Croatian victims of World War II and the post-war period were buried in a common grave in 2024, where 102 victims killed at locations in the area of the city of Gospić have already been buried in 2021.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In the Gospić area, the [Chetniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetniks) were extremely strong during the war. After the end of WWII Yugoslav authority undertook radical actions to destroy remaining Chetnik groups, especially in Lika area. One of the radical methods was [forced displacement](477535.md) of Serbs from the area of Gospić.[^cite_note-11]

In the 1990s, during the course of the [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence), Gospić suffered greatly during the [Battle of Gospić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gospi%C4%87). After [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar), Gospić was the most devastated Croatian city.[^cite_note-\:0-12] On February 3, 1991, preparations for the conquest of Gospić began. A "rally of all Serbs of Lika and the world" was held in Gospić; it was a gathering of rebel Serbs (about 5,000 of them) and preparations for the capture of Gospić.[^cite_note-\:1-13] The town was held by Croatian government forces throughout the war, while the rebel [Serb forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Army_of_Krajina) of the [Republic of Serbian Krajina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Serbian_Krajina) occupied positions directly to the east and often bombarded the town from there. In addition to the [Yugoslav People's Army](536643.md) (JNA) and local rebel Serbs, the "[White Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Eagles_\(paramilitary\))" and special units from [Niš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C5%A1) were active in the city itself.[^cite_note-\:1-13] On August 5, 1991, a [Chetnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetniks) group from Raduče took five people from [Lovinac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovinac,_Lika-Senj_County) in an unknown direction. After a week, the police found their butchered bodies along the railway line to Gospić. In the occupied territories, massacres of Croatian civilians continued, civilians were killed in Lovinac, Perušićka Kosa, [Široka Kula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iroka_Kula_massacre), Urije and Lički Osik. To this day, no one has been held accountable for the crimes committed.[^cite_note-14] After the fall of the [Perušić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%C5%A1i%C4%87) barracks on 14 September, then on 18 September the barracks in Gospić, the Croatian troops had something to defend themselves with.[^cite_note-\:0-12]

In late 1991, the town was the site of the [Gospić massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospi%C4%87_massacre), where between 100-120 predominantly Serb civilians were killed by Croatian military units.[^cite_note-Schäuble-15] [Gospić Cathedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Annunciation,_Gospi%C4%87)l originally built in 1781 was damaged in Serb attacks and burned down on September 15, 1991.[^cite_note-16] In February 1992, a statue of Nikola Tesla in downtown Gospić was destroyed in an explosion. The local [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church), originally built in 1790 and previously demolished by the [Ustaše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e) regime during World War II, was reconstructed in 1964 but again destroyed in 1992.[^cite_note-Škiljan2025-17] The perpetrators were never apprehended.[^cite_note-Balkan_Insight-18] Control of the area finally devolved to the Croatian government with the success of [Operation Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Storm) in August 1995.[^cite_note-Schäuble-15]

Gospić is the third smallest [seat of a county government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) in Croatia. Its status as the county capital helped to spur some development in it, but the town as well as the entire region have suffered a constant decrease in population over the last several decades.&#x20;

Gospić is also the site of one of the regional branches of the [Croatian State Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_State_Archives), the *Državni arhiv Gospić*, at Kaniška 17. It was founded 30 September 1999 and officially opened 1 September 2000 in a renovated building and now houses historical documents of relevance to the Lika-Senj region which were formerly housed in the Regional Archive at Karlovac.

In 2013, Croatian [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Croatia) [Zoran Milanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoran_Milanovi%C4%87) urged the town's authorities to allow for a replica of the Tesla statue that had been destroyed in 1992 to be reinstated. The mayor of Gospić at the time, Milan Kolić, refused to give his approval for such a move and instead vowed to erect a statue of wartime Croatian President [Franjo Tuđman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franjo_Tu%C4%91man) on the spot where the Tesla statue had once stood.[^cite_note-Balkan_Insight-18] In 2021, under mayor Karlo Starčević, the town renamed a square after Tesla, and the [Croatian Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Ministry_of_Culture) sponsored a new statue on the square, a replica of the [Frano Kršinić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frano_Kr%C5%A1ini%C4%87) original.[^cite_note-19]

## Geography

Gospić is located in the mountainous and sparsely populated region of Lika. It is the [administrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government) center of [Lika-Senj County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lika-Senj_County). Gospić is located near the [Lika River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lika_\(river\)) in the middle of a [karst field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst_field) ([Ličko Polje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C4%8Dko_Polje_\(Lika\))).

## Climate

Gospić has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate), Dfb by Köppen climate classification, with mean temperatures varying from −0.9 °C (30.4 °F) in January to 18.1 °C (64.6 °F) in July. Being situated higher than 500 metres (1,640 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level), the area experiences high diurnal ranges, especially in summer, and frost has been recorded in every month except for July. The record low and high temperatures are −33.5 °C (−28.3 °F) and 38.7 °C (101.7 °F), respectively. Gospić is also quite a rainy city, with a slight summer minimum, but it experiences plentiful precipitation all year long, with the maximum being in autumn. During winter, Gospić can get strong blizzards, with on average 5.1 days a year when more than 50 cm (20 in) falls, and 16.1 days when more than 30 cm (12 in) falls. Its record snow cover was 285 cm (112.2 in), and it was measured in February 1916.

Since records began in 1872, the highest temperature recorded at the local weather station at an elevation of 564 metres (1,850 ft) was 38.7 °C (101.7 °F), on 30 July 1947.[^cite_note-20] The coldest temperature was −33.5 °C (−28.3 °F), on 17 February 1956.[^cite_note-21]

## Politics

### Minority councils and representatives

Directly elected minority councils and representatives are tasked with consulting tasks for the local or regional authorities in which they are advocating for minority rights and interests, integration into public life and participation in the management of local affairs.[^cite_note-T-Portal-28] At the [2023 Croatian national minorities councils and representatives elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Croatian_national_minorities_councils_and_representatives_elections) [Serbs of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Croatia) fulfilled legal requirements to elect 15 members minority council of the Town of Gospić with only 13 members being elected in the end.[^cite_note-LSŽ-Manjine-2023-29]

## Sports

The local chapter of the [HPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Mountaineering_Society) is *HPD "Visočica"*, which had 284 members in 1936 under the Ivan Gojtan presidency. At the time, it had a [ski](28478.md) section, a [caving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caving) section and a [photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography) section.[^cite_note-30] Membership rose to 291 in 1937.[^cite_note-31]<sup>: 244 </sup> Membership fell to 217 in 1938.[^cite_note-32]<sup>: 244 </sup> In August 1941, the Minister of Sport and Mountaineering of the [NDH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDH), [Miško Zebić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C5%A1ko_Zebi%C4%87?action=edit\&redlink=1), named Ivan Banić as the state *povjerenik* of the *HPD "Visočica"*, and designated as the chapter's advisory board: Josip Krišković, Vinko Mataija, Ivan Štimac, Antun Pichler and Juraj Vukšinić.[^cite_note-33]<sup>: 195 </sup> The *HPD "Visočica"* was officially renamed *Hrvatsko planinarsko društvo u Gospiću* in March 1942,[^cite_note-34]<sup>: 52 </sup> but Gospić did not reply to the order.[^cite_note-35]
