**Tell** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): تلّ), pronounced **Till<sub>,</sub>** is a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories) town in the [Nablus Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Governorate) in northern [West Bank](33209.md), located five kilometers southwest of [Nablus](233369.md). According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS), the town had a population of 5,162 inhabitants in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] Most of the town's laborers work in agriculture, with figs and olives being the major source of income.[^cite_note-3]

[Mohammad Shtayyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Shtayyeh), a Palestinian economist and politician, was born in Tell.

## History

Ceramics from the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) era have been found here.[^cite_note-4]

### Ottoman era

In 1517, the village was included in the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) with the rest of [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Palestine#Ottoman_period), and it appeared in the 1596 [tax-records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) as *Till*, located in the *[Nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* of Jabal Qubal of the *[Liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* of [Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Sanjak). The population was 46 households, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). They paid a fixed tax rate of 33.3% on agricultural products, such as wheat, barley, summer crops, olive trees, goats and beehives, in addition to occasional revenues, a press for olive oil or grape syrup, and a fixed tax for people of Nablus area; a total of 5,100 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-5]

In 1838, *Till* was located in the District of *Jurat 'Amra*, south of Nablus.[^cite_note-Robinson127-6]

In 1863, [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) found it to have a population of one thousand inhabitants. It was divided into several districts, each administered by a different [sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh). He further noted: "Some houses are large and fairly well built. Around the village grow, in pens, beautiful plantations of fig and pomegranate trees."[^cite_note-7]

In 1870/1871 (1288 [AH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Hegirae)), an Ottoman census listed the village in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiyah)* (sub-district) of Jamma'in al-Thani, subordinate to Nablus.[^cite_note-8]

In 1882, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* described *Till* as: "A village of moderate size on low ground, with a high mound behind it on the south; it has a [well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_well) and a few trees, and on the west a pool in winter; the hills to the north are bare and white, but terraced to the very top."[^cite_note-9]

### British mandate era

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), *Tel* had a population of 567 Muslims,[^cite_note-Census1922-10] increasing in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to 803 Muslims, in 209 houses.[^cite_note-11]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) the population was 1,060 Muslims,[^cite_note-1945p19-12] while the total land area was 13,766 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam), according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-13] Of this, 1,056 dunams were for plantations and irrigable land, 7,023 for cereals,[^cite_note-14] while 55 dunams were classified as built-up areas.[^cite_note-15]

### Jordanian era

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Tell came under [Jordanian occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank).

The Jordanian census of 1961 found 1,539 inhabitants.[^cite_note-16]

### Israeli occupation

Since the Six-Day War in 1967, Tell has been held under [Israeli military occupation](59076253.md). Several Israeli settlements and outposts have been established on farmland and pastureland privately owned by Palestinians from Tell and nearby villages between Nablus and Qalqiliya. One of these is Havat Gilad (also known as Gilad Farm), which was established in 2002 as an outpost of Kedumim.[^cite_note-17] On February 2018, the Israeli government authorized the retroactive approval of the outpost and decided to connect it to the electricity and water networks and pave an access road.[^cite_note-18]

A report, This Is Ours – And This, Too, by B’Tselem and Kerem Navot, documented how agricultural outposts in the central West Bank have contributed to the loss of Palestinian access to farmland and pastureland, including through restrictions imposed around settlements and outposts.[^cite_note-19] B'Tselem documented that the presence of Havat Gilad had contributed to restrictions on Palestinian access to agricultural land and settler attacks on Palestinian property. On October 2018, for example, farmers from Tell found that settlers had harvested olives from their trees and damaged olive saplings; the military restricted the farmers' access to their land for most of the year, allowing access for cultivation on only a limited number of pre-arranged days.[^cite_note-20]

On 17 May 2022, Israeli settlers planted trees on privately owned Palestinian land in Tell and assaulted the landowner and residents who came to assist him. According to [B'Tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Tselem), Israeli soldiers subsequently attacked the Palestinian residents and detained several of them.[^cite_note-21]

On 26–27 July 2022: Settlers attacked residents on village land, injuring five Palestinians; one of them was also beaten and detained for several hours by Israeli soldiers. The settlers burned a mobile structure owned by Muhammad Ahmad Hamdan and smashed a vehicle of Tell village council.[^cite_note-22]

On 1 November 2022: Settlers stole olives from Palestinian-owned trees in Tell, alongside figs, and almonds.[^cite_note-23]

On 13 October 202&#x33;*,* in the aftermath of the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), settlers, accompanied by Israeli soldiers, attacked Palestinian farmers harvesting olives in the Karkaba area west of Tell.[^cite_note-24]

On 28 June 2025, Israeli settlers, accompanied by Israeli forces, attacked Palestinian homes in the Kafrūr area of Tell. Residents confronted the settlers, after which the IDF fired live ammunition and tear-gas canisters but no injuries were reported.[^cite_note-25]

On September 2025, Peace Now reported that the Israeli Civil Administration had declared 455.58 dunams of land belonging to the villages of Tell, Jit and Far'ata as "state land" for the purpose of legalizing Havat Gilad.[^cite_note-26] The underlying Civil Administration declaration, issued on 1 September 2025, designated the 455.58 dunams under Declaration of Government Property No. 2/25.[^cite_note-27]

#### 2026

Further information: [July 2026 West Bank unrest](83779267.md)

On 23 February 2026, Israeli settlers torched the Abu Bakr al-Ṣiddiq Mosque between Tell and Surra. The entrance was burned and racist slogans were sprayed on its exterior walls.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

On 6 April 2026: The [United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs](1602284.md) reported that settlers burned an agricultural structure and damaged water tanks, tools, and other water infrastructure in Tell and neighboring [Asira al-Qibliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asira_al-Qibliya).[^cite_note-30]

On 18 July 2026, Israeli settlers [set fire to an inhabited Palestinian house](47246246.md) and its surroundings in Tell. Palestinian [firefighters](146303.md) reported that Israeli forces delayed their access to the area; by the time they arrived, the house had been completely destroyed. No injuries were reported.[^cite_note-31]

Six days later, on the early morning hours of 24 July 2026, four Palestinian civilians were killed and four others wounded, three critically, when armed settlers allegedly attempted to burn a mosque, prompting residents to confront them, after which Israeli troops entered the village in aid of the settlers and fired live ammunition. During the altercation, a Palestinian villager, Farouq Ramadan, took the weapon of an Israeli settler and opened fire. The settler, who opened fire with a handgun, was killed, as well as a commander of the Israeli military force that had arrived. Farouq was then killed by the Israeli troops, as were two brothers and a cousin of Farouq.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Israeli officials claimed that the settlers were on a hike when a Palestinian man stole a weapon from local security and opened fire.[^cite_note-\:0-34] Palestinian officials stated that the men were defending their village from armed trespassers.[^cite_note-\:0-34]

A video recording of the events revealed that during a confrontation, one of the settlers kicked a Palestinian man and yelled at him to back away. Settler minors recorded the chaos while cheering, whistling, and shouting: *"An Arab is wounded, yes! Come on, revenge!".*[^cite_note-35] The Israeli government declared the incident as a "terror attack" and vowed retribution, including demolition of Ramadan's family home, expanding the construction of new [settler outposts](15123.md) and "separating traffic routes and [checkpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_checkpoint) between Israelis and Palestinians".[^cite_note-36] The measures are an escalation of the widely documented discriminatory policies of [Israeli apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_apartheid) in the West Bank as well as [collective punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_punishment_in_the_occupied_west_bank) by demolishing the family of the villager’s home, while the Israeli government disputes this designation.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

On July 31st, the IDF shot a Palestinian who threw rocks as settlers marched through the village in memory of the 2 Israelis killed in the shootout a week prior. The IDF also said it received a report of settlers vandalizing a cemetery in the town.[^cite_note-40]
