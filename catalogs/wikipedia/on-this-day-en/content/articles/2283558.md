**Crans-Montana** (French: [\[kʁɑ̃ mɔ̃tana\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Switzerland) and ski resort in the predominantly French-speaking district of [Sierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierre_\(district\)) in the [canton](180752.md) of [Valais](198789.md), [Switzerland](26748.md). The municipality was formed through the merger of [Chermignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chermignon), [Mollens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollens,_Valais), [Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana,_Switzerland) and [Randogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randogne) municipalities on 1 January 2017.[^cite_note-1] The ski resort Crans-Montana was created through the fusion of the two centres of Crans and Montana, and is split between three municipalities, namely Crans-Montana, [Icogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icogne), and [Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens,_Valais).

## History

### Chermignon

Chermignon is first mentioned in 1228 as *Chermenon* and *Chirminon*.[^cite_note-HDS_Chermignon-2] It became an independent municipality in 1905 when it separated from [Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens,_Valais).[^cite_note-Fusion-3]

### Mollens

Mollens is first mentioned about 1250 as *Molaen*. In 1286 it was mentioned as *Moleing*. The municipality was formerly known by its German name *Molei*; however, that name is no longer used.[^cite_note-HDS_Mollens-4]

### Montana

Montana is first mentioned in 1243 as *Montana*.[^cite_note-HDS_Montana-5] In 1905 it separated from [Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens,_Switzerland) to form an independent municipality.[^cite_note-Fusion-3]

### Randogne

Randogne is first mentioned in 1224 as *Randonni*.[^cite_note-HDS_Randogne-6]

### 2026 New Year's Eve disaster

Main article: [2026 Crans-Montana bar fire](82004998.md)

During the night of 31 December 2025 to 1 January 2026, at around 2026-10-03T14\:33 [UTC+01\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+01\:00) \[[refresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crans-Montana\&action=purge)] a.m., a [fire at the *Le Constellation* bar](82004998.md) killed 41 people (including many underage individuals) and injured 115, most of them suffering severe burns.[^cite_note-7]

The resort is [one of many](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ski_areas_and_resorts_in_Switzerland) ski resorts in the [Swiss Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Alps). It is located in the [French-speaking part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romandie) of the canton of [Valais](198789.md) on a plateau above [Sierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierre) at an elevation of about 1,500 m (4,920 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_mean_sea_level), with a panoramic view over the [Valais Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valais_Alps) and [Weisshorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weisshorn) in particular. The resort is a fusion of the two centres of Crans and Montana and belongs to six municipalities (Chermignon, Icogne, Lens, Mollens, Montana and Randogne).

The skiing area of Crans-Montana has 140 km (87 mi) of [pistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piste), and includes the [Plaine Morte Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaine_Morte_Glacier). It is topped by the [Pointe de la Plaine Morte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe_de_la_Plaine_Morte) at 2,927 m (9,600 ft).

Crans-Montana is famous in [alpine ski racing](39576.md) for the [1987 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_FIS_Alpine_World_Ski_Championships) and is often on the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) schedule, usually for women's speed events.

On 1 January 2026, at around 1\:30 am local time, [a fire broke out at the Constellation Bar](82004998.md) during a [New Year's Eve](277922.md) celebration, killing 41 people and injuring more than 116 others.[^cite_note-8]

## Other activities

The area hosts the winter mountain pop rock festival, the [Caprices Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caprices_Festival?action=edit\&redlink=1), and a professional golf tournament, the [European Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Tour)'s [Omega European Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_European_Masters), which takes place each September. The area has also been frequently used for bicycle racing, hosting stage finishes of the [Tour de Suisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_Suisse) seven times and of the [Tour de Romandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_Romandie) eight times as of 2013. In addition, Crans-Montana also hosted the finish of the 20th stage of the [1984 Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Tour_de_France), won by [Laurent Fignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Fignon), who also took the overall race win that year.[^cite_note-9]

In 2017, Crans-Montana was the site of [United Nations](31769.md)–mediated [diplomatic talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%932017_Cyprus_talks) between Greek and Turkish ministers in an attempt to resolve disputes over their [claims in Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_problem). The talks, which also included representatives of the Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot groups, ended stormily and without reaching a solution, with U.N. Secretary General [António Guterres](471806.md) participating in heated discussions towards the close of the diplomatic summit.[^cite_note-10]

The [Golf-Club Crans-sur-Sierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf-Club_Crans-sur-Sierre?action=edit\&redlink=1) has four courses, one designed by [Seve Ballesteros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seve_Ballesteros) and another named after [Jack Nicklaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus).[^cite_note-11]

The internationally renowned [Les Roches International School of Hotel Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Roches_International_School_of_Hotel_Management) is located in Crans-Montana.[^cite_note-12]

## Geography



Aerial view of Crans-Montana



Panoramic view of the surrounding mountains around Crans-Montana

Crans-Montana has an area, as of 2009, of 59.66 km<sup>2</sup> (23.03 sq mi).[^cite_note-Arealstatistik-13]

Before the 2017 merger, the territory of present-day Crans-Montana was divided among the former municipalities of [Chermignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chermignon), [Mollens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollens,_Valais), Montana (Valais) and [Randogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randogne). Several settlements and resort areas extended across the boundaries of more than one of these municipalities.[^cite_note-14]

The former municipality of [Chermignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chermignon) comprised [Ollon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ollon), Chermignon-d'en-Bas, Chermignon-d'en-Haut and part of the [Crans-sur-Sierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crans-sur-Sierre) resort. The hamlet of Champzabé was historically divided between Chermignon, Montana and [Sierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierre).[^cite_note-15]

The former municipality of [Mollens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollens,_Valais) included Mollens, Cordona, Conzor, Laques, Saint-Maurice-de-Laques and the high-altitude resort of [Aminona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminona).[^cite_note-16]

The former municipality of Montana (Valais) included Montana-Village, [Corin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corin) and part of Champzabé, while Diogne was divided between Montana and Chermignon. [Montana-Vermala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana-Vermala) developed across the territories of Montana and the former municipality of [Randogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randogne).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The former municipality of [Randogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randogne) comprised the village of Randogne together with [Loc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loc), part of Darnona, Bluche, most of [Montana-Vermala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana-Vermala) and Montana-Station.[^cite_note-19]

## Climate

Main article: [Geography of Switzerland § Climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Switzerland#Climate)

Crans-Montana has a warm summer [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate), (*Dfb*) according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification). Between [1981 and 2010](42778673.md), Montana had on average 101 days of rain or snow per year and received on average 692 mm (27.2 in) of [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)). Crans Montana has fairly evenly distributed precipitation throughout the year, as is common in this type of climate. As with other locations in the canton of [Valais](198789.md), annual precipitation rates are quite low due to its location in the [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) of some of the tallest mountains in the Alps.[^cite_note-MeteoSchweiz-23]

## Education

Crans-Montana is one of the participating municipalities in the Écoles des Villages organization, which maintains six school sites.[^cite_note-25] School sites in the municipality include Corin,[^cite_note-26] Martelles,[^cite_note-27] Montana-Village,[^cite_note-28] and Randogne-Mollens.[^cite_note-29]

The Bibliothèque de Crans-Montana is the municipality's library.[^cite_note-30]

## Sports

### Winter sports



Scenic view of skiing in Crans-Montana

Crans-Montana is a prominent ski resort that developed from the early 20th century (in the Crans-sur-Sierre area) and specializes in alpine skiing.[^cite_note-31] It has hosted the 1987 World Championships, the 2011 Junior World Championships, and numerous stages of the Alpine Skiing World Cup and the European Cup. The resort offers approximately 140 km of slopes.[^cite_note-32]
