The **Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin** (Charité – University Medicine Berlin; French: [\[ʃaʁite\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/78/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Ltrlg-charit%C3%A9.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Ltrlg-charit%C3%A9.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/78/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Ltrlg-charit%C3%A9.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Ltrlg-charit%C3%A9.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) medical school and hospital in [Berlin](3354.md), Germany. It is the [largest university hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_by_capacity) in Europe. The medical school is affiliated with [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) and [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin).[^cite_note-3]

The Charité traces its origins to 1710. The complex is spread over four campuses and comprises around 3,000 beds, 15,500 staff, 8,000 students, and more than 60 operating theaters, and has a turnover of two billion euros annually.[^cite_note-report-1] It is one of the ten best hospitals in the world.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

The modern history of medicine has been significantly influenced by scientists who worked at the Charité. [Rudolf Virchow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Virchow) was the founder of cellular pathology, while [Robert Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Koch) developed vaccines for anthrax, cholera, and tuberculosis.[^cite_note-6] For his life's work Koch is seen as one of the founders of modern medicine.[^cite_note-7] More than half of all German [Nobel Prize winners in Physiology or Medicine](52502.md), including [Emil von Behring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_von_Behring), [Robert Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Koch), and [Paul Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich), have worked at the Charité.&#x20;

In 2010–2011 the medical schools of [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) and [Freie Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freie_Universit%C3%A4t_Berlin) were united under the roof of the Charité. The admission rate of the reorganized medical school was 3.9% for the 2019–2020 academic year.[^cite_note-8]

## History



Historic and modern buildings at Campus Charité Mitte (CCM)



Locations of the four campuses of Charité in Berlin

Complying with an order of King [Frederick I of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I_of_Prussia) from 14 November 1709, the hospital was established north of the Berlin city walls in 1710 in anticipation of an outbreak of the [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) that had already depopulated [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia). After the plague spared the city, it came to be used as a charity hospital for the poor; so on 9 January 1727, King [Frederick William I of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_William_I_of_Prussia) gave it the French name "Charité".[^cite_note-9]

The construction of an [anatomical theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomical_theatre) in 1713 marks the beginning of the medical school, then supervised by the *collegium medico-chirurgicum* of the [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^cite_note-Einhäup-Ganten2010-10]

In the 19th century, after the University of Berlin (today [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin)) was founded in 1810, the dean of the medical college [Christoph Wilhelm Hufeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Wilhelm_Hufeland) integrated the Charité as a teaching hospital in 1828. During this time it became home to such notable medical pioneers as [Rudolf Virchow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Virchow), known as "the father of modern [pathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathology)"[^cite_note-11] and whose name is given to the eponymous "Virchow's Method" of autopsy;[^cite_note-RG-12] the Swiss psychiatrist and neurologist [Otto Binswanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Binswanger), whose work in vascular [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia) led to the discovery of [Binswanger's Disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binswanger's_Disease)—so coined by his colleague [Alois Alzheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Alzheimer);[^cite_note-NPOO-13] [Robert Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Koch), who identified the specific causative agents of [tuberculosis](30653.md), [cholera](7591.md), and [anthrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthrax); and [Emil von Behring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_von_Behring), widely known as a "saviour of children"[^cite_note-NPO-14] for his 1894 discovery of a [diphtheria](58937.md) [antitoxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antitoxin) at a time when diphtheria was a major cause of child death (among many others).

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the Charité endured the [Battle of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin) and Berlin was taken by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) on 2 May 1945. Though the majority of its original and pre-war structure was damaged or destroyed during the war, it was nevertheless used as a Red Army hospital. The Charité remained in the [Soviet Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_zone) of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Berlin) until the formation of the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic), the GDR (German: *Deutsche Demokratische Republik, DDR*) in 1949, more commonly called [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany). Under the [Communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism), standards were largely maintained, and it became a showpiece for [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) propaganda during the [Cold War](325329.md). Corpses of [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall) victims were taken here for autopsies.

In 1990, with the [reunification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Germany), and in the years following, Charité once again became one of the world's leading research and teaching hospitals.

In 2001, the Helios Clinics Group acquired the hospitals in Buch with their 1,200 beds.[^cite_note-16] Still, the Charité continues to use the campus for teaching and research and has more than 200 staff members located there. The Charité encompasses more than 100 clinics and scientific institutes, organized in 17 different departments, referred to as *Charité Centers* (CC):

- CC 1: Health and Human Sciences

- CC 2: Basic Sciences (First Year)

- CC 3: Dental, Oral and Maxillary Medicine

- CC 4: Biomedicine

- CC 5: Diagnostic Laboratory and Preventative Medicine

- CC 6: Diagnostic and Interventional Radiology and Nuclear Medicine

- CC 7: Anesthesiology, Operating-Room Management and Intensive Care Medicine

- CC 8: Surgery

- CC 9: Traumatology and Reconstructive Medicine

- CC 10: Charité Comprehensive Cancer Center

- CC 11: Global Health

- CC 12: Internal Medicine and Dermatology

- CC 13: Internal Medicine, Gastroenterology and Nephrology

- CC 14: Tumor Medicine

- CC 15: Neurology, Neurosurgery, Psychiatry

- CC 16: Audiology/Phoniatrics, Ophthalmology and Otolaryngology

- CC 17: Gynecology, Perinatal, Pediatric and Adolescent Medicine with Perinatal Center & Human Genetics

Overall, 12 of those centers focus on patient care, while the rest focuses on research and teaching. On 1 January 2023, Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin and the Deutsches Herzzentrum Berlin – Stiftung des bürgerlichen Rechts (DHZB) have merged their cardiac medical facilities to form the Deutsches Herzzentrum der Charité (DHZC). The DHZC is located at the three clinical campuses of Charité at Campus Virchow-Klinikum, Campus Charité Mitte and Campus Benjamin Franklin. It comprises a total of eight clinics and institutes with around 2,500 employees and has around 470 beds. It is one of the largest cardiac centres in Germany for the treatment of all cardiovascular diseases in patients of all ages

The *Medical History Museum Berlin* has a history dating back to 1899. The museum in its current form opened in 1998 and is famous for its pathological and anatomical collection.[^cite_note-17]

In 2003 the Berlin city and state House of Representatives passed an interim law unifying the medical faculties of both [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) and [Freie Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freie_Universit%C3%A4t_Berlin) under the roof of the Charité.[^cite_note-19] Since 2010–2011 all new medical students have been enrolled in the New Revised Medical Curriculum Programme with a length of 6 years.[^cite_note-20] The Charité is together with [Heidelberg University Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University_School_of_Medicine) Germany's most competitive medical school (2020).[^cite_note-21] 3.17% of all Charité Medical School students are supported by the [German Academic Scholarship Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studienstiftung), one of the highest percentages of all public German universities. The [Erasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Programme) Exchange Programme offered to Charité Medical School students includes 72 universities and is the largest in Europe.[^cite_note-22] Charité students can spend up to a year at a foreign medical school with exchange partners such as the [Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institute), [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen), [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University), [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University), [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), and the [University of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Z%C3%BCrich). Students are also encouraged to participate in research projects, complete a dissertation, or join Charité affiliated social projects.

In 2021, the **Berlin Institute of Health** (BIH) became the translational research unit of Charité, making the Charité the first university clinic that receives direct and annual financial support by the federal state of Germany.[^cite_note-23] Together with private charity donors like the [Johanna Quandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johanna_Quandt)'s private excellence initiative or the [Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_&_Melinda_Gates_Foundation), as well as financing by the [State of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Berlin), the new direct federal investments will become the third financial foundation for research at the Charité.[^cite_note-24] In addition, it is part of the [Berlin University Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_University_Alliance), receiving funding from the [German Universities Excellence Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Universities_Excellence_Initiative) in 2019.

## Einstein Foundation

Main article: [Einstein Foundation Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_Foundation_Berlin)

The Charité is one of the main partners of the Einstein Foundation, which was established by the city and state of Berlin in 2009. It is a "foundation that aims to promote science and research of top international caliber in Berlin and to establish the city as a centre of scientific excellence".[^cite_note-27] Research fellows include:

- [Thomas Südhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._S%C3%BCdhof) – biochemist *([Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](52502.md) in 2013)*

- [Brian Kobilka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Kobilka) – chemist *([Nobel Prize in Chemistry](25416893.md) in 2012)*

- [Edvard Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Moser) – neuroscientist *(Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2014)*
