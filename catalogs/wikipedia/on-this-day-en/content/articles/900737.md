**Tabuk** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): تَبُوك *_Tabūk_*, Arabic: [\[taˈbuːk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)) is a city in northwestern [Saudi Arabia](349303.md),[^cite_note-2] and the capital of [Tabuk Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuk_Province). It is located near the [Jordan–Saudi Arabia border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_border).[^cite_note-3]

## History and prehistory

See also: [History of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Saudi_Arabia) and [Midian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midian)

The [1068 Near East earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1068_Near_East_earthquakes) were a pair of major earthquakes that occurred in 1068; the first of which whose epicentre was in the area of Tabuk.[^cite_note-Ambraseys-4]

[Tabuk Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuk_Province) is rich in antiquities and archaeological sites such as [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph), inscriptions, forts, palaces, sections of the [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Hajj#Syrian_route) and [Egyptian pilgrimage (Hajj) routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Hajj#Egyptian_route), as well as remains of the [Hejaz railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_railway), an important station of which is located in Tabuk.

Hundreds of localities with rock art and inscriptions dating to different archaeological periods ranging from the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) to the Islamic period were recorded at Wadi Dam and the region west of Tabuk. A study of the art revealed rich stylistic variability with representations of both human and animal figures. Numerous sites in the area with [Thamudic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamudic), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) and [Nabataean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabataean_Arabic) inscriptions have been found.

The ancient region of [Midian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midian) roughly corresponds to what is now the Tabuk Province.[^cite_note-Edwards2013-5] According to tradition, a fortified place belonging to the *Aṣ-ḥāb al-Aykah* ("Companions of the Wood"), the term used in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) for the [Midianites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midian),[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] is identified with Tabuk or even its fort, although the extant one is less than five centuries old.

2nd-century [Alexandrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) geographer [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy) mentioned a place by the name 'Tabawa', at the northwestern corner of [Arabia](47858.md). This name may be a reference to 'Tabuka' or 'Tabuk'. If this is true, the town may be as old as Ptolemy's time. [Pre-Islamic Arab poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_poetry#Pre-Islamic_poetry) such as [Antra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarah_ibn_Shaddad) and [Nabiqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nabigha) mention the mountain 'Hasmi' in their poems.

Tabuk became famous for the [Expedition of Tabuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_Tabuk) in 630, during the period of the [Islamic](6037917.md) [prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophets_and_messengers_in_Islam) [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad). Since then, it remained a gateway to North Arabia.

Ain Sukkrah is an ancient *‘ayn* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عَيْن, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *spring*) dating back to *[Pre-Islamic Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Islamic_Arabia)* ("Era of Ignorance"). It is remembered that Muhammad camped for more than ten days near the spring during the expedition of Tabuk, and drank from its water.[^cite_note-10]



The 16th-century [Ottoman Hajj fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuk_Castle)

The [Hajj fort of Tabuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuk_Castle) seen today dates back to the 16th century, during the [Ottoman period](22278.md) and has been restored many times since. Several forts and stations were built along the [Syrian Hajj (pilgrimage) route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Hajj#Syrian_route), from [Damascus](8914.md) to [Medina](36636.md), to welcome and protect the [pilgrims](23763.md) performing the [Hajj](23487999.md). The fort consists of two floors built around an open courtyard with a mosque, a well, and a stairway leading to the watch towers used by the guards. The Tabuk fort is considered a historical landmark of the region and is open to visitors.

Tabuk was visited by [Charles Montagu Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Montagu_Doughty) in 1877.

Tabuk was captured by the Arab forces in 1918, three weeks after the British [capture of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Damascus_\(1918\)).[^cite_note-11]

Tabuk became a centre of military activity during the [1991 Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) as the city faced threats from [Iraqi Scud missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile) and air attacks.

## Education

See also: [List of universities and colleges in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Saudi_Arabia) and [Education in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Saudi_Arabia)

### University of Tabuk

The [University of Tabuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tabuk) (UT) was established in 2006 as part of a national effort to expand higher-education access across the Kingdom. Since its founding, the university has grown to include multiple colleges and specialized research centers, offering undergraduate and graduate programs in fields such as Medicine, Engineering, Business Administration, Computer Science, and Education.[^cite_note-12]

UT also operates several branch campuses across the province, including in [Umluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umluj), [Haql](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haql), [Tayma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tayma), and [Duba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duba,_Saudi_Arabia), providing educational opportunities to students outside the city of Tabuk.

### Other Universities and Institutions

Tabuk is home to additional higher-education institutions, most notably [Fahd bin Sultan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahd_bin_Sultan_University) (FBSU), a private university offering programs in Engineering, Business, Computer Science, and Humanities.[^cite_note-13] The province also hosts a branch of the [Saudi Electronic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Electronic_University), offering flexible online and on-campus bachelor's and graduate programs.[^cite_note-14]

### Technical and Vocational Education

Technical and vocational training in Tabuk Province is overseen by the [Technical and Vocational Training Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_and_Vocational_Training_Corporation) (TVTC). Institutions for male students include Tabuk Technical College, Umluj Technical College, Haql Technical College, Al-Wajh Technical College, and branches in Tayma and Duba. For female students, the Technical College for Girls in Tabuk provides diploma programs and vocational training in a range of technical and administrative fields.

### General Education

Public schools in Tabuk—covering elementary, intermediate, and secondary levels—operate under the supervision of the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Saudi_Arabia\)), serving communities throughout the province.

## Transportation

### Air

[Prince Sultan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Sultan_bin_Abdulaziz_Airport) serves as the primary airport for the city, offering both domestic and international flights that connect Tabuk to major destinations across Saudi Arabia (such as Riyadh and Jeddah) and abroad.[^cite_note-ASN_110772-18][^cite_note-DOD_2021-19]
