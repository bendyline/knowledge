The **Dassault Falcon 50** is a French super-midsize, long-range [business jet](247003.md), featuring a [trijet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trijet) layout with an [S-duct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-duct) air intake for the central engine. It has the same fuselage cross-section and similar capacity as the earlier twin-engined [Falcon 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_20), but was a new design that is [area ruled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_rule) and includes a more advanced wing design.[^cite_note-Janes_88_p75-2]

## Design and development

The first prototype flew on 7 November 1976, with French [airworthiness certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) on 27 February 1979, followed by U.S. [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) certification on 7 March 1979.[^cite_note-Janes_88_p75-2] Dassault developed a maritime surveillance and environmental protection version as the **Gardian 50**.[^cite_note-3]

The Falcon 50 was later updated as the Falcon 50EX, the first of which flew in 1996,[^cite_note-4] and the last of which was delivered in 2008.[^cite_note-flightglobal.com-1] The Falcon 50EX features improved engines and other enhancements to give further range improvements to an already long-range jet. The Falcon 50EX designation applies to serial numbers 253–352, which marks the end of the production line for the Falcon 50/50EX.

Successors to the Falcon 50 are the [Falcon 7X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_7X)[^cite_note-5] and the [Falcon 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_900) featuring a larger fuselage and the same [three-engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trijet) arrangement. Dassault announced in January 2008 what is essentially a replacement aircraft for the Falcon 50, codenamed the "[SMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_5X)" (Super Mid Size). The basic design process, including engine selection, was supposed to be completed by early 2009. However, in a June 2009 press conference, CEO Charles Edelstenne said that all design choices had been reopened and the goal was extended to the end of the year.

Dassault and [Aviation Partners Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Partners_Inc.) have developed and certified High Mach blended [winglets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingtip_device) for the Falcon 50 & 50EX as a retrofit kit.

By 2018, Falcon 50s from the mid-late 1980s were priced at \$0.879 to \$1.6 million while 1998-2003 Falcon 50EXs can be had for \$2.95 to \$3.95 million.[^cite_note-AINdec2018-6]

## Variants

Falcon 50

Basic initial variant with [Honeywell TFE 731-3-1C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_TFE731) engines and optional [auxiliary power unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_power_unit) (APU); 252 manufactured, with one serving as a prototype for the Falcon 50EX.

Falcon 50EX

Marketing name for Falcon 50 with 3 DEEC (Digital Electronic Engine Control) controlled [TFE 731-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_TFE731) engines; an APU installed as standard equipment; changes to the [rudder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudder) control system; updated avionics; and other improvements; 100 manufactured, plus one modified Falcon 50

[^cite_note-TCDSp4-7]

[^cite_note-TCDSp16-8]

Falcon 50 "Susanna"

Single Falcon 50 for Iraq modified with a Cyrano IV-C5 radar and hardpoints to carry two [AM-39 Exocet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AM-39_Exocet) antiship missiles. Used for training Mirage F1 crews and possibly carried out the [attack on the USS *Stark*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Stark_incident) on May 17, 1987. This aircraft was flown to Iran during the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War) and was not returned.

## Accidents and incidents

- 6 April 1994 (1994-04-06): Rwandan President [Juvénal Habyarimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana) and Burundian President [Cyprien Ntaryamira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprien_Ntaryamira) were killed when the Falcon 50 they were travelling in (registration 9XR-NN) [was shot down near Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira), Rwanda, triggering the [Rwandan genocide](676659.md).

- 20 October 2014 (2014-10-20): [Falcon 50EX F-GLSA collided with a snow plow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Vnukovo_Airport_Falcon_50_crash) while taking off from Moscow Vnukovo Airport, Russia, killing all four people on board, including the CEO of Total oil and gas company [Christophe de Margerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_de_Margerie).

- 27 September 2018: a runway overrun by chartered Falcon 50 *N114TD*, at [Greenville, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenville,_South_Carolina), USA, resulted in the death of the pilot and copilot, as well as serious injuries to two passengers. The pilots had reportedly violated several FAA regulations, in undertaking the flight that led to the incident.

  [^cite_note-15]

- 23 December 2025: a Falcon 50 [crashed](81917460.md) near [Kesikkavak, Haymana](34139920.md), Turkey, killing eight people. Among the deceased was the commander of the [Libyan Armed Forces](35021184.md), [Mohammed Ali Ahmed al-Haddad](81918412.md).

  [^cite_note-16]
