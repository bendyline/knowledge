**Colombians in the United Kingdom** or **Colombian Britons** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Colombianos en el Reino Unido*) include [British citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_nationality_law) or residents who are of [Colombian](5222.md) ancestry. According to the [2011 UK Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_UK_Census), the Colombian-born population of [England](9316.md) was 25,016, [Wales](69894.md) 166,[^cite_note-2011CensusEngWal-1] [Scotland](26994.md) 507[^cite_note-2011CensusScot-2] and [Northern Ireland](21265.md) 72.[^cite_note-2011CensusNI-3]

## History

See also: [History of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Colombia) and [Immigration to the United Kingdom since 1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_the_United_Kingdom_since_1922)

Latin American migration to Europe has been growing rapidly since the mid-1960s. The first migrants consisted of [Chilean](5489.md) and [Argentine](18951905.md) refugees, these were followed by Colombians and many other Latin American groups in the early 1970s.[^cite_note-PIZZ-4] Many Colombians came to work in the UK on visas mainly in the domestic services and [catering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catering) industries. Colombians in the UK tend to originate from the country's urban areas such as [Bogotá](211271.md), [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali), [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn) and [Palmira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmira,_Valle_del_Cauca), although some come from the smaller towns and countryside. There are roughly three stages of Colombian migration to the UK,[^cite_note-5] between 1975 and 1979 many came under the work permit system to take up jobs in low pay domestic services predominantly. Between 1980 and 1986 many relatives and families of the early settlers came to the UK, many of them soon became illegal immigrants due to themselves overstaying their visas. Another stage where significant numbers of asylum seekers came to the UK was between 1986 and 1997 when thousands came to the UK due to [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla) and [paramilitary](146730.md) violence in Colombia.[^cite_note-6] This is the period where the largest number of Colombians came to the UK, although significant numbers were still migrating in 2009 as the Colombian British population expands into its second generation.[^cite_note-ICAR-7] Historically, the United Kingdom has, in fact, proven the most popular European destination for Colombian migrants despite the lack of historical links that are evident with [Spain](26667.md).[^cite_note-COMPAS-8] Spain alongside several other European nations has formerly been dubbed a "reservoir of migrant labour".[^cite_note-COMPAS-8] However, since the turn of the millennium, Spain has become a key destination within Europe for Colombian migrants and is now home to the large majority of the Colombian community in the European continent, with over 350,000 Colombian-born residents in Spain as of 2015.[^cite_note-9] Significantly smaller communities are present in the United Kingdom, France and Italy.[^cite_note-COMPAS-8]

## Population

See also: [Demographics of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Colombia) and [Demographics of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_United_Kingdom)

The [2001 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Census_2001) recorded 12,331 Colombian-born people living in the UK.[^cite_note-OECD-10] The [2011 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Census_2011) recorded 25,016 Colombian-born residents in [England](9316.md), 166 in [Wales](69894.md),[^cite_note-2011CensusEngWal-1] 507 in [Scotland](26994.md),[^cite_note-2011CensusScot-2] and 72 in [Northern Ireland](21265.md).[^cite_note-2011CensusNI-3] The [Office for National Statistics](324029.md) estimates that in 2020, 39,000 Colombian-born people were residing in the UK.[^cite_note-2020_estimates-11]

According to one estimate, Colombians now make up the second largest sub-group of Latin American Britons behind [Brazilian Britons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Brazilian), numbering up to 160,000.[^cite_note-Buchuck-12] An article published by the [Migration Policy Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Policy_Institute) estimates the Colombian population in the UK to be 90,000 as of 2003.[^cite_note-MPI-13] The overwhelming majority of Colombians in the UK live in [London](17867.md), although within the capital they are fairly well dispersed. Despite this, the largest numbers can be found in the boroughs of [Lambeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Lambeth), [Islington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Islington), [Southwark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Southwark) and [Camden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Camden).[^cite_note-COMPAS-8] Outside of London, concentrations of Colombians can also be found in the [Northern English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England) cities of [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Sunderland), [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Leeds) and [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne).[^cite_note-ICAR-7] The vast majority are [Roman Catholic](606848.md).

### Asylum seekers

Applications for [asylum](1621432.md) from Colombians in the UK started to rise in 1996, peaked in 1997 and has been falling since then (with the exception of 1999 which saw a sharp increase). The sudden rise in asylum applications between 1996 and 1997 could have been linked to paramilitary and guerrilla violence in Colombia. Also the sharp drop in applications in 1998 could be associated with the introduction of visa restrictions. After the increase in 1999, the start of the 21st century has seen a more or less stable number of applications by Colombians for asylum.[^cite_note-ICAR-7] The table below is a breakdown for applications received for asylum in the UK between 1993 and 2002.[^cite_note-ICAR-7]

### Culture

See also: [Culture of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Colombia)

Colombian culture and cultural activities in the UK are virtually non-existent compared to places such as [Miami](53846.md) or [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-GUA-27] This is due to the [United States](3434750.md) having a much larger Colombian community, as well as stronger links between the two countries. The few Colombian events that occur across the country are [music festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_festivals) (usually in the [summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer)). Many popular Colombian artists and musicians come to the UK to play in these festivals.[^cite_note-ICAR-7] The British capital, London is home to several Colombian [clubs](19321330.md), [restaurants](26437.md) and [cafes](370815.md)[^cite_note-28] as well as many shops and independent businesses, there is also a significant presence of Colombian media in the UK, consisting mainly of a few newspapers and radio stations. A similarity between Colombia and the United Kingdom is that [football](10568.md) is an extremely popular sport in both countries; this easily helps link communities and today many Colombians play in the Latin American leagues of London as well as for local teams.[^cite_note-ICAR-7] Despite this, some of these activities are seen more as entrepreneurial than cultural activities. Besides Colombian football and music in the UK, the other aspect that contributes to the culture of Colombians in the UK is food. [Sancocho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sancocho) is a traditional Colombian dish that has become popular in mainstream London and to an extent [British culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_culture). Colombian students who are studying in the UK often organise events that mainly involve debates and conferences about the situation back home in their native Colombia.[^cite_note-ICAR-7][^cite_note-29]

Many new Colombian immigrants including asylum seekers and refugees in the UK find finding work extremely difficult, it has become increasingly complicated over time to gain visas and working permits in the UK and there is also the likely chance of a claim for asylum being denied. Despite this, there are hundreds if not thousands of new Colombian immigrants to the UK per annum, with the vast majority of these having little knowledge of the [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) at all. This makes it hard to gain access to public services and ultimately more complicated to get a job, language skills are extremely important, and if [Spanish](26825.md) is the only language spoken by and individual, they are bound to be beaten to the post by a fluent or more conversational English speaker. As stated earlier many new immigrants work in minimum wage jobs such as in [domestic service](636781.md) and [catering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catering) industries. Lack of English skills is also a negative aspect for Colombians in the UK outside of the world of work, for example it may be hard to gain required information from local councils as well as legal advice. As a response to this many new Latin American set up groups have been founded in London to help Colombians and other Hispanics to improve their language skills and/or find work.[^cite_note-ICAR-7] According to [Institute for Public Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Public_Policy_Research) findings, 49.96 per cent of new Colombian immigrants in [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) are employed, compared to 66.48 per cent of settled Colombian immigrants.[^cite_note-30]

### Housing

Housing is often identified as one of the main problems facing new Colombian immigrants and asylum seekers to the UK. A study of the housing situation of these people carried out in 1995 highlighted the problems of homelessness and poor housing. The situation is worst for single people, but also affects families. No studies have yet been performed to determine the housing trends of settled Colombian immigrants and second generation British born people of Colombian descent.[^cite_note-CARILA-31]

### Social issues

Many Colombians come to the UK as asylum seekers, and in certain circumstances it can be hard for them to integrate, especially since the lack of Colombian culture and influence in the UK can be all too obvious.[^cite_note-ICAR-7] In June 2000, there were 118 Colombian nationals imprisoned in the UK (103 male and 15 female);[^cite_note-HOPRISON-32] many of them are involved in the Colombian [drug trade](218449.md) (the UK is one of the largest buyers of Colombian cocaine, millions of hectares of rainforest are being destroyed each year in Colombia to supply drug users in countries such as the UK. Drug mules are also a problem, with many Colombians risking their lives by transporting drugs inside of them to get into the UK[^cite_note-BBCN-33]). This is the largest number of foreign nationals in British prisons originating anywhere in [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Americas), although in comparison to the number of certain foreign nationals from Europe, Asia, Africa and elsewhere it is fairly small.[^cite_note-HOPRISON-32] As the Colombian British population grows larger, so do the problems within the community; it is believed that the differences within the community are now more visible and unity is less evident. Many of these differences mirror situations in Colombia where differences in social class, political views and immigration status all contribute to an ever fragmenting community. The most obvious difference seems to be between those who immigrated between 1970 and the early 1990s when political persecution was more evident, whilst more recent immigrants (particularly asylum seekers) have had greater difficulty in proving their status as political refugees, partly down to the current complicated situation in Colombia. Out of a survey of several Colombians in London, the majority stated their frustration with the overall Colombian British community as when it comes to organising cultural events, attendance can often be very poor. The perception is that settled Colombian Brits are too busy working and to an extent lose their political awareness and interests of Colombian culture in the UK and at home in Colombia.[^cite_note-ICAR-7]

Further information: [Category\:British people of Colombian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:British_people_of_Colombian_descent)

Several Colombian immigrants to the UK as well as British born people of Colombian descent, have gained notable success, some examples are listed here. [Jorge Castano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Castano) is a Cali born [professional wrestler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestler) who has made a new life and career for himself in the UK,[^cite_note-34] [Fernando Montano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Montano) is the first Colombian to dance with the Royal Ballet and has been a member of the company since 2006, [Phil Manzanera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Manzanera) a musician and record producer who was the lead guitarist with [Roxy Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxy_Music) was born in London to a Colombian mother and English father.[^cite_note-35] [Colombian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_American) journalist, Sonia Uribe studied in and now lives in London.[^cite_note-36] [José Fernández Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Madrid) was a former ambassador to the UK for Colombia who lived in the UK for a number of years where he subsequently died.[^cite_note-37] Another notable expatriate in the UK of Colombian origin is [Australian](4689264.md) actor [Adam Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Garcia) who has found fame in many US and British films.[^cite_note-38] Colombian-born author and award-winning academic [Dr Camila Devis-Rozental](https://staffprofiles.bournemouth.ac.uk/display/cdevisrozental) is a recognised figure in Higher Education. She was included in the 2022 and 2023 [Shaw Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Trust) Disability Power 100 list[^cite_note-39] which recognises the 100 most influential disabled people in the UK. In 2010 Miss Great Britain winner [Tara Hoyos-Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_Hoyos-Mart%C3%ADnez) of Colombian parentage became the first Latina to represent the country in the global [Miss Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Universe_2010) pageant.[^cite_note-40]
