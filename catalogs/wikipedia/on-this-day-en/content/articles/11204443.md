**Oumar Mariko** (born 4 February 1959) is a [Malian](19127.md) politician, doctor and noted former student activist. He is the Secretary-General of [African Solidarity for Democracy and Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Solidarity_for_Democracy_and_Independence) (SADI), a [left-wing](18499.md) political party, and has three times run for President of Mali, in 2002, 2007 and 2013.[^cite_note-Bama-1]

## Biography

Born in [Bafoulabé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafoulab%C3%A9), his engagement in politics began in his student days. He worked as part of the [National Union of Students and Pupils of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Students_and_Pupils_of_Mali) (UNEEM) at the [Dioila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioila) [lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e) from 1976 to 1977 and the [Badalabougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badalabougou?action=edit\&redlink=1) lycée from 1978 to 1979. He was a member of the union's organisation bureau from 1979 to 1980. A founding member of the [Mali Students and Pupils' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Students_and_Pupils'_Association?action=edit\&redlink=1), he was its [general secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_secretary) from 1990 to 1992.[^cite_note-Bama-1] Mariko led protests that eventually led to the overthrow of [Moussa Traoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Traor%C3%A9) in 1991[^cite_note-Count-2] and participated in the [Transitional Committee for the Salvation of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Committee_for_the_Salvation_of_the_People?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CTSP) representing the Student Association.[^cite_note-Bama-1]

Oumar Mariko is a [medical doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_doctor) and the founding director of the Mah Doumbia surgical clinic at [Bamako](56606.md). He has also been the director general of the private radio network [Kayira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayira?action=edit\&redlink=1) since 1995 and is the external relations secretary of the [URTEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/URTEL), the [trade union](17626.md) representing radio and television broadcasting in Mali.[^cite_note-Bama-1]

## Political career

Together with [Cheick Oumar Sissoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheick_Oumar_Sissoko), in 1996 Mariko founded a political party, African Solidarity for Democracy and Independence (SADI),[^cite_note-3] in opposition to [President](430390.md) [Alpha Oumar Konaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Oumar_Konar%C3%A9). He is the Secretary-General of SADI,[^cite_note-Porte-4] which is avowedly [leftist](18499.md).

Mariko was chosen as the party's candidate for the [April 2002 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Malian_presidential_election) at its first ordinary congress in March 2002.[^cite_note-5] In the election, he finished in 12th place with .88% of the vote.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-DB-7] In the [July 2002 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Malian_parliamentary_election), Mariko was a candidate in [Kolondiéba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolondi%C3%A9ba) constituency.[^cite_note-8]

At the end of a SADI national conference, on February 24, 2007 Mariko was designated as the party's candidate for the [April 2007 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Malian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-Porte-4] He campaigned on the platform of opposition to the current economic, social, education and healthcare policies, declaring his wish for a "democratic and popular state". He opposed the privatisation of the *[Compagnie malienne pour le développement du textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_malienne_pour_le_d%C3%A9veloppement_du_textile)* (CMDT), the *[Huilerie cotonnière du Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huilerie_cotonni%C3%A8re_du_Malo?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (Huicoma), and called for the re-nationalisation of the railways. He also opposed the alleged "favouritism" of President [Amadou Toumani Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_Toumani_Tour%C3%A9) in social housing. Throughout the campaign, Mariko declared that "elections will be neither transparent, nor sincere and even less credible", accusing the [Citizen's Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Movement_\(Mali\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), which supported Touré, of attempting fraud. He finished in fourth place with 2.72% of the vote.[^cite_note-DB-7][^cite_note-Court-9] In a press conference on May 29, Mariko denounced the electoral commission and the Constitutional Court for their handling of the election, saying that the former should be dissolved.[^cite_note-10] He was also sharply critical of the main opposition coalition, the [Front for Democracy and the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_for_Democracy_and_the_Republic), in which he did not participate.[^cite_note-11]

He won a seat in the [National Assembly of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Mali) in the [July 2007 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Malian_parliamentary_election) on a SADI list in Kolondiéba; the other candidate on the list was [Moussa Coumbéré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Coumb%C3%A9r%C3%A9?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Results-12] The SADI list took second place, with 21.28% of the vote, in the first round, behind the [National Congress for Democratic Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_for_Democratic_Initiative) (CNID) list, which received 25.29%.[^cite_note-13] However, the SADI list won in the second round with 61.76% of the district's vote.[^cite_note-Results-12]

Mariko is a critic of [economic globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_globalization) and the [Bretton Wood Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Monetary_and_Financial_Conference).[^cite_note-Count-2]

As of 2007–2008, Mariko is President of the Commission of Foreign Affairs, Malians Living Abroad, and African Integration in the National Assembly.[^cite_note-14]

### 2012 military coup d'etat

See also: [2012 Malian coup d'etat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Malian_coup_d'etat)

Following the coup d'état that suspended Mali's democratic institutions on the night of 21–22 March 2012, Oumar Mariko and his SADI political party created MP22, the *Mouvement populaire du 22 mars* (22 March People's Movement) supporting the coup plotters.[^cite_note-15]

In the [July–August 2013 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Malian_parliamentary_election), Mariko was re-elected to the National Assembly. He subsequently stood as a candidate for the post of President of the National Assembly, but in the vote held on 22 January 2014, the governing party's candidate, [Issaka Sidibé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issaka_Sidib%C3%A9), won easily, with 115 votes against 11 for Mariko.[^cite_note-16]
