**Barcelona**[^cite_note-8] is a city on the northeastern coast of [Spain](26667.md). It is the capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Catalonia) of the [autonomous community](55002.md) of [Catalonia](6822.md), as well as the [second-most populous municipality of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish_municipalities_by_population) after [Madrid](41188263.md). With a population of 1.7 million within city limits,[^cite_note-population-3] its urban area extends to numerous neighbouring municipalities within the [province of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Barcelona) and is home to around 5.7 million people,[^cite_note-demographia-4] making it the [fifth most populous urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_European_Union) of the [European Union](9317.md) after [Paris](22989.md), the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) area, Madrid, and [Milan](36511.md).[^cite_note-demographia-4] It is one of the largest metropolises on the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), located on the coast between the mouths of the rivers [Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llobregat) and [Besòs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bes%C3%B2s_\(river\)), and bounded to the west by the [Serra de Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Collserola) mountain range.

According to tradition, Barcelona was founded by either the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) or the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginians), who had trading posts along the Catalonian coast.[^cite_note-britannica.com-9] In the Middle Ages, Barcelona became the capital of the [County of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Barcelona). After joining with the [Kingdom of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aragon) to form the composite monarchy of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon), Barcelona, which continued to be the capital of the [Principality of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Catalonia), became the most important city in the Crown of Aragon and its main economic and administrative centre, only to be overtaken by [Valencia](63861.md), wrested from [Moorish control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) by the Catalans, shortly before the dynastic union between the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) and the Crown of Aragon in 1516. Barcelona became the centre of [Catalan separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_separatism), briefly becoming part of [France](5843419.md) during the 17th century [Reapers' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reapers'_War) and again from 1812 to 1814 under [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon).

Experiencing industrialization and several [workers movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_movement) during the 19th and early 20th century, it became the capital of [autonomous Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_of_Catalonia_\(1931%E2%80%931939\)) in 1931 and was the epicenter of the [revolution experienced by Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Catalonia) during the [Spanish Revolution of 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Revolution_of_1936), until its [capture by the fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Barcelona) in 1939. After the [Spanish transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy) in the 1970s, Barcelona once again became the capital of an autonomous Catalonia. Barcelona has a rich cultural heritage and is today an important cultural centre and a major tourist destination. Particularly renowned are the architectural works of [Antoni Gaudí](31566384.md) and [Lluís Domènech i Montaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llu%C3%ADs_Dom%C3%A8nech_i_Montaner), which have been designated UNESCO [World Heritage Sites](44940.md). The city is home to two of the most prestigious universities in Spain: the [University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Barcelona) and [Pompeu Fabra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeu_Fabra_University). The headquarters of the [Union for the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Mediterranean) are located in Barcelona. The city is known for hosting the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics) as well as world-class [conferences and expositions](#Trade_fair_and_exhibitions). In addition, many [international sport tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Barcelona#International_tournaments) have been played here.

Barcelona is a major cultural, economic, and financial centre in [southwestern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula),[^cite_note-10] as well as the main [biotech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotech) hub in Spain.[^cite_note-11] As a leading world city, Barcelona's influence in global socio-economic affairs qualifies it for [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) status (Beta +).[^cite_note-12] Barcelona is a [transport hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub), with the [Port of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Barcelona), one of Europe's principal seaports and the busiest European passenger port,[^cite_note-StatServ2014-13] an international airport, [Barcelona–El Prat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona%E2%80%93El_Prat_Airport), which handles over 50 million passengers per year,[^cite_note-14] an [extensive motorway network](#Roads_and_highways), and a [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan%E2%80%93Barcelona_high-speed_rail_line) line with links to France and the rest of Europe.[^cite_note-15]

## Names

The name *Barcelona* comes from the ancient Iberian *Baŕkeno*, attested in an ancient coin inscription found on the right side of the coin in [Iberian script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_scripts) as Barkeno in Levantine Iberian script,[^cite_note-16] in [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) sources as Βαρκινών, *Barkinṓn*;[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Schütte1917-18] and in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) as *Barcino*,[^cite_note-19] *Barcilonum*[^cite_note-20] and *Barcenona*.[^cite_note-Parthey1848-21][^cite_note-WesselingGrammarian1735-22][^cite_note-Cook1910-23]

Other sources suggest that the city may have been named after the [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) general [Hamilcar Barca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilcar_Barca), who was supposed to have founded the city in the 3rd century BC,[^cite_note-britannica.com-9][^cite_note-24] but there is no evidence that its name in antiquity, *Barcino*, was connected with the [Barcid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcid) family of Hamilcar.[^cite_note-Corporation1957-25] During [the Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), the city was variously known as *Barchinona*, *Barçalona*, *Barchelonaa*, and *Barchenona*.

An abbreviated form sometimes used by locals for the city is *Barna*. *Barça* is only applied to the local football club [FC Barcelona](68187.md), not to the city. Another common abbreviation is 'BCN', which is also the [IATA airport code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code) of the [Barcelona-El Prat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona-El_Prat_Airport).

The city is referred to as the *Ciutat Comtal* in [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) and *Ciudad Condal* in Spanish (i.e., "Comital City" or "City of Counts"), owing to its past as the seat of the [Count of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Barcelona).[^cite_note-26]

### Legendary founding

The origin of the earliest settlement at the site of present-day Barcelona is unclear. The ruins of an early settlement have been found, including different tombs and dwellings dating to earlier than 5000 BC.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-MontañaCamposFarré2008-28] In Greek mythology, the founding of Barcelona had been attributed to the mythological [Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules).

### Punic Barcelona

According to tradition, Barcelona was founded by Punic ([Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia)) settlers, who had trading posts along the Catalonian coast.[^cite_note-britannica.com-9][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] In particular, some historians attribute the foundation of the city directly to the historical [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) general, [Hamilcar Barca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilcar_Barca), father of [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal), who supposedly named the city *Barcino* after his family in the 3rd century BC,[^cite_note-britannica.com-9][^cite_note-31] but this theory has been questioned.[^cite_note-Corporation1957-25] Iberia and Barcelona was controlled by the [Carthaginian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Empire) and was an important location for trade due to its proximity to Punic [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza). Archeological evidence in the form of coins from the 3rd century BC have been found on the hills at the foot of Montjuïc with the name Bárkeno written in the Iberian script in the [Iberian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_language). Thus, some have argued that the [Laietani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laietani), an ancient [Iberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberians) (pre-Roman) people of the Iberian peninsula, who inhabited the area occupied by the city of Barcelona around 3–2 BC after the Punic withdrawal from Iberia, called the area Bàrkeno, which means "The Place of the Plains" (Barrke = plains/terrace).[^cite_note-32]

### Roman Barcelona

See also: [Roman Sepulchral way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Sepulchral_way) and [Roman walls of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_walls_of_Barcelona)



A marble plaque in the Museu d'Història de la Ciutat de Barcelona, dated from around 110–130 AD and dedicated to the Roman colony of Barcino

In about 15 BC, the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) redrew the town as a *[castrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra)* (Roman military camp) centred on the "*Mons Taber*", a little hill near the [Generalitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalitat) (Catalan Government) and city hall buildings. The Roman Forum, at the crossing of the [Cardo Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardo_Maximus) and [Decumanus Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decumanus_Maximus), was approximately placed where current [Plaça de Sant Jaume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pla%C3%A7a_de_Sant_Jaume) is. Thus, the political centre of the city, [Catalonia](6822.md), and its domains has remained in the same place for over 2,000 years.

Under the Romans, it was a colony with the surname of *Faventia*,[^cite_note-33] or, in full, *Colonia Faventia Julia Augusta Pia Barcino*[^cite_note-34] or *Colonia Julia Augusta Faventia Paterna Barcino*. [Pomponius Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomponius_Mela)[^cite_note-35] mentions it among the small towns of the district, probably as it was eclipsed by its neighbour *[Tarraco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarragona)* (modern [Tarragona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarragona)), but it may be gathered from later writers that it gradually grew in wealth and consequence, favoured as it was with a beautiful situation and an excellent harbour.[^cite_note-36] It enjoyed immunity from imperial burdens.[^cite_note-37] The city minted its own coins; some from the era of [Galba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galba) survive.

Important Roman vestiges are displayed in [Plaça del Rei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pla%C3%A7a_del_Rei) underground, as a part of the [Barcelona City History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_City_History_Museum) (MUHBA); the typically Roman grid plan is still visible today in the layout of the historical centre, the *[Barri Gòtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_G%C3%B2tic)* (Gothic Quarter). Some remaining fragments of the [Roman walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_walls_of_Barcelona) have been incorporated into the cathedral.[^cite_note-38] The cathedral, Catedral Basílica Metropolitana de Barcelona, is also sometimes called *La Seu*, which simply means cathedral (and see, among other things) in Catalan.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] It is said to have been founded in 343.

### Medieval Barcelona

The city was conquered by the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) in the early 5th century, becoming for a few years the capital of all [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania). After being conquered by the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) in the early 8th century, it was conquered after a [siege in 801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Barcelona_\(801\)) by [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne)'s son [Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_the_Pious), who made Barcelona the seat of the [Carolingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire) "[Hispanic March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marca_Hispanica)" (*Marca Hispanica*), a [buffer zone](489870.md) ruled by the [Count of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Barcelona).[^cite_note-41]



The remaining section of the medieval walls

The Counts of Barcelona became increasingly independent and expanded their territory to include much of modern [Catalonia](6822.md), although in 985, Barcelona was [sacked](209811.md) by the army of [Almanzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almanzor).[^cite_note-42] The sack was so traumatic that most of Barcelona's population was either killed or enslaved.[^cite_note-43] In 1137, [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aragon) and the [County of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Barcelona) merged in [dynastic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynastic_union)[^cite_note-bisson_31-44][^cite_note-45] by the marriage of [Ramon Berenguer IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Berenguer_IV,_Count_of_Barcelona) and [Petronilla of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronilla_of_Aragon), their titles finally borne by only one person when their son [Alfonso II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_II_of_Aragon) ascended to the throne in 1162. His territories were later to be known as the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon), which conquered many overseas possessions and ruled the western [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) with outlying territories [in Naples](55880.md) and Sicily and as far as [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Athens) in the 13th century.

Barcelona also had a substantial [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Catalonia) at the time, then the largest Jewish community in the Crown of Aragon. Called "[the Call](https://www.barcelona.cat/museuhistoria/en/itineraris/el-call-de-barcelona)," for the many small streets that defined the area, it later became enclosed. [Montjuïc or Montjuich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc), in medieval Latin and Catalan, meaning "Jewish Mountain" and the birthplace of the city, is the site of a medieval Jewish cemetery, Jews continued to live in Barcelona until the [Massacre of 1391](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_1391) diminished their numbers. The [Spanish Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Inquisition) forced the remaining Jews who refused to convert to Christianity to be burned at the stake, or sell their property and leave.

Barcelona was the leading [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery) centre of the Crown of Aragon up until the 15th century, when it was eclipsed by [Valencia](63861.md).[^cite_note-46] It initially fed from eastern and Balkan slave stock later drawing from a [Maghribian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) and, ultimately, [Subsaharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsaharan) pool of slaves.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGonzález_Arévalo201917-47]

The Bank or [Taula de canvi de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taula_de_canvi_de_Barcelona), often viewed as the oldest public bank in Europe, was established by the city [magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate) in 1401. It originated from necessities of the state, as did the [Bank of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici_Bank#Rise) (1402) and the [Bank of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Saint_George) (1407).[^cite_note-48]

### Barcelona under the Spanish monarchy



Barcelona in 1563 by [Anton van den Wyngaerde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_van_den_Wyngaerde)

In the beginning of the early modern period, Barcelona lost political primacy, but the economy managed to achieve a balance between production capacity and imports.[^cite_note-49] In the context of the wider early recovery of Catalonia from the [17th-century crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_General_Crisis) in the second half of the century, increasing maritime activity since 1675 doubled traffic in the port of Barcelona compared to figures from the beginning of the 17th century.[^cite_note-50] In the late 17th and early 18th century, Barcelona repeatedly endured the effects of war, including the 1691 bombing, the sieges of 1697, 1704, 1705, 1706, and the 1713 blockade and ensuing 1714 siege and assault.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArranzGrau1991135–136-51] In the 18th century, the population grew from 30,000 to about 100,000 inhabitants, as the city became one of the key mercantile centres in the Western Mediterranean, with inland influence up to [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza), and to the south up to [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante).[^cite_note-52] A fortress was built at [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc) that overlooked the harbour. Much of Barcelona was negatively affected by the [Napoleonic wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War), but the start of industrialization saw the fortunes of the province improve.

During the 19th century, Catalan business interests, many of them based in Barcelona, became increasingly involved in the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md), taking advantage of the void left by the [British abolition of the trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_Trade_Act_1807) in 1807. Catalan immigrants in Spanish America also became heavily involved in owning and running [slave plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_plantation) in Cuba and Puerto Rico. Between 1817 and 1867, Catalans were involved in the transportation of 700,000 slaves from West Africa to the Caribbean, which "financed much of the industrialisation of Catalonia and the 19th-century building boom in Barcelona." Although the Spanish government had abolished slave trading in 1817, it turned a blind eye to illicit slave trading. When slavery was abolished in the Spanish Empire in 1886, many Catalans returned to Barcelona and invested their newfound fortunes in constructing opulent mansions in areas such as [La Rambla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rambla,_Barcelona).[^cite_note-Burgen2023-53]

### Transforming the city

Main article: [Cerdà Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerd%C3%A0_Plan)



Barcelona before the Cerdà Plan (1857)

In the mid-1850s, Barcelona was struggling with population density as it became an industrial, [port city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_city) and European capital. The city's density was at 856 people per hectare, more than double that of Paris. Mortality rates were on the rise and any outbreaks of disease would devastate the population. To solve the issue, a civil engineer named [Ildefons Cerdà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ildefons_Cerd%C3%A0) proposed a [plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerd%C3%A0_Plan) for a new district known as the [Eixample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eixample). The citizens of Barcelona had begun to demolish the medieval wall surrounding and constricting the city. Cerdà thought it best to transform the land outside the walls into an area characterized by a scientific approach to [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization). His proposal consisted of a grid of streets to unite the old city and surrounding villages. There would also be wide streets to allow people to breathe clean air, gardens in the centre of each [street block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_block), integration of rich and poor giving both groups access to the same services, and smooth-flowing traffic.

Urban quality, egalitarianism, hygiene, sunlight, and efficiency were all major keys for Cerdà's vision. Not everything he imagined would be realized within the Eixample district, but the iconic octagonal superblocks with chamfered corners for better visibility are his direct brainchild and remain immensely helpful even 170 years later. The district and its ideals were not appreciated at the time. The city council awarded the design of the extension plan to another architect. The Spanish government was the one to step in and impose Cerdà's plan, laying the groundwork for many more tensions between the Spanish and Catalan administrations. Regardless, some of the upper class citizens of Barcelona were excited by the new plan and began a race to build "the biggest, tallest, most attractive house" in the district. Their interest and money fueled the rich diversity that we now see in the district's architecture. In the end, Cerdà's ideas would have a lasting impact on Barcelona's development, earning it international recognition for its highly efficient approach to urban planning and design.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

### Spanish Civil War and Franco period



*[Woman training for a Republican militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman_training_for_a_Republican_militia)* by [Gerda Taro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerda_Taro), [Somorrostro beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somorrostro) (1936)

Main article: [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War)

Shortly after the establishment of the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic), in 1931, it became the capital of the [autonomous region of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_of_Catalonia_\(1931%E2%80%931939\)). During the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), the city, and Catalonia in general, were resolutely Republican. Many enterprises and public services were [collectivized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_ownership) by the [CNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederaci%C3%B3n_Nacional_del_Trabajo) and UGT unions. As the power of the Republican government and the Generalitat diminished, much of the city was under the effective control of anarchist groups. The anarchists lost control of the city to their own allies, the Communists and official government troops, after the street fighting of the Barcelona [May Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Days).

Barcelona was one of the last places in Spain to fall under [Francoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain) control, and became the capital of the Second Spanish Republic in November 1937.



Aerial photograph of the [bombing of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Barcelona) (1938)

From 16 to 18 March 1938, [Barcelona was bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Barcelona) several times by [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy) and [Nazi Germany](21212.md), in support of the Francoist-led army. It left at least 1,300 people killed and 2,000 wounded. The airstrikes intended to destroy industrial areas and demoralize the republicans, however civil buildings were also destroyed.

The [fall of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Barcelona_\(1939\)) on 26 January 1939, caused a mass exodus of civilians who fled to the French border. The resistance of Barcelona to [Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco)'s *coup d'état* was to have lasting effects after the defeat of the Republican government. The autonomous institutions of [Catalonia](6822.md) were abolished,[^cite_note-56] and the use of the [Catalan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) in public life was suppressed. Barcelona remained the second largest city in Spain, at the heart of a region which was relatively industrialized and prosperous, despite the devastation of the civil war. The result was a large-scale immigration from poorer regions of Spain (particularly [Andalusia](2736.md), [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia) and [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\))), which in turn led to rapid urbanization.

During the [francoist period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain), municipal elections were suspended through all Spain and the mayor was appointed by the central government. Pressure for change was particularly strong in Barcelona, which considered that it had been punished during nearly forty years of Francoism for its support of the Republican government.[^cite_note-Mock2011-57] After Franco's death in 1975, massive peaceful demonstrations on 11 September 1977 assembled over a million people in the streets of Barcelona to call for the restoration of Catalan autonomy. It was granted less than a month later.[^cite_note-Friend2012-58] After the adoption of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Spain), municipal elections were held again ([1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Barcelona_municipal_election)).

### Recent history

The development of Barcelona was promoted by two events in 1986: [Spanish accession to the European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_European_Communities_\(1973-1993\)), and particularly Barcelona's designation as host city of the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-ParkinSharma1999-59][^cite_note-AbebeBolton2013-60] The process of [urban regeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_regeneration) has been rapid, and accompanied by a greatly increased international reputation of the city as a tourist destination. The increased cost of housing has led to a slight decline (−16.6%) in the population over the last two decades of the 20th century as many families move out into the suburbs. This decline has been reversed since 2001, as a new wave of immigration (particularly from Latin America and from [Morocco](19291.md)) has gathered pace.[^cite_note-61]

In 1987, an [ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_\(separatist_group\)) [car bombing](155282.md) at Hipercor [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipercor_bombing) 21 people.

&#x20;In 1992, Barcelona hosted the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics). The after-effects of this are credited with driving major changes in what had, up until then, been a largely industrial city. As part of the preparation for the games, industrial buildings along the sea-front were demolished and 3 km (2 mi) of beach were created. New construction increased the road capacity of the city by 17%, the sewage handling capacity by 27% and the amount of new green areas and beaches by 78%. Between 1990 and 2004, the number of hotel rooms in the city doubled. Perhaps more importantly, the outside perception of the city was changed making, by 2012, Barcelona the 12th most popular city destination in the world and the 5th amongst European cities.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]



A woman leaves flowers in a [Guàrdia Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A0rdia_Urbana_de_Barcelona) van after the attack

The year 2017 was an intense year in Barcelona. On August 17, Barcelona suffered [a terrorist attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Barcelona_attacks) in the iconic [Rambla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rambla,_Barcelona), where a van drove into pedestrians, killing 13 people and injuring at least 130 others. The perpetrator fled on foot, killing another person to steal his car, and a [large police operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronos_Operation?action=edit\&redlink=1) was started. Massive demonstrations in the city center took place the days after the attack, with the motto "*No tinc por*" ("I am not afraid").[^cite_note-67]

Other attacks took place elsewhere in Catalonia. The Prime Minister of Spain, [Mariano Rajoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Rajoy), called the attack in Barcelona a [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) attack. [Amaq News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaq_News_Agency) attributed indirect responsibility for the attack to the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

In October 2017, after several years of growing of the [Catalan independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_independence_movement), with massive demonstrations along the decade, an [independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Catalan_independence_referendum) took place. It was declared unconstitutional by the [Spanish Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Spain), however the [Catalan Government](294461.md) ignored it and celebrated it. This led to another court ordering the Spanish [Civil Guard](325008.md) and [National Police](1608789.md) to dismantle voting centers, however their intervention was not pacific. Barcelona was the scenario of several police oppression and chaos, with sources that reported at least 1,066 injured requiring medical attention.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Several [NGOs](46539.md) condemned the police's violence and reported an excessive use of force.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

In July 2023, Barcelona was announced as the UNESCO-UIA World Capital of Architecture for the 2024–2026 term. This means it will be the hub for discussion around global challenges including culture, heritage, urban planning and architecture. In addition to being the capital through 2026, it will also host the UIA World Congress of Architects for that year. The honour is befitting of Barcelona, as its history is peppered with architectural achievement and various iconic styles and influences. From its ancient Roman roots, to the Gothic and Modernisme movements, Barcelona has thrived through the way it ties together architecture and culture.[^cite_note-76]

## Geography



A panoramic view of Barcelona

### Location



Barcelona as seen by the [European Space Agency](10363.md)'s [Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus_Programme) [Sentinel-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-2) mission



Map of [Barcelona metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_metropolitan_area)

Barcelona is located on the northeast coast of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), facing the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), on a [plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_of_Barcelona) approximately 5 km (3 mi) wide limited by the mountain range of [Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Collserola), the [Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llobregat) river to the southwest and the [Besòs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bes%C3%B2s_\(river\)) river to the north.[^cite_note-gec_3-77] This plain covers an area of 170 km<sup>2</sup> (66 sq mi),[^cite_note-gec_3-77] of which 101 km<sup>2</sup> (39.0 sq mi)[^cite_note-guies_estadistiques-78] are occupied by the city itself. It is 120 km (75 mi) south of the [Pyrenees](24707.md) and the [Catalan](6822.md) border with France.

[Tibidabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibidabo), 512 m (1,680 ft) high, offers striking views over the city[^cite_note-79] and is topped by the 288.4 m (946.2 ft) [Torre de Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_de_Collserola), a [telecommunications tower](2044496.md) that is visible from most of the city. Barcelona is peppered with small hills, most of them urbanized, that gave their name to the neighbourhoods built upon them, such as [Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Carmel) (267 m or 876 ft), [Putxet](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardines%20del%20Tur%C3%B3%20del%20Putxet) (es) (181 m or 594 ft) and [Rovira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tur%C3%B3_de_la_Rovira) (261 m or 856 ft). The escarpment of [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc) (173 m or 568 ft), situated to the southeast, overlooks the harbour and is topped by [Montjuïc Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc_Castle_\(Barcelona\)), a fortress built in the 17–18th centuries to control the city as a replacement for the Ciutadella. Today, the fortress is a museum and Montjuïc is home to several sporting and cultural venues, as well as Barcelona's biggest [park and gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_gardens_of_Barcelona).

The city borders on the municipalities of [Santa Coloma de Gramenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Coloma_de_Gramenet) and [Sant Adrià de Besòs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Adri%C3%A0_de_Bes%C3%B2s) to the north; the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) to the east; [El Prat de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Prat_de_Llobregat) and [L'Hospitalet de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Hospitalet_de_Llobregat) to the south; and [Sant Feliu de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Feliu_de_Llobregat), [Sant Just Desvern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Just_Desvern), [Esplugues de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esplugues_de_Llobregat), [Sant Cugat del Vallès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Cugat_del_Vall%C3%A8s), and [Montcada i Reixac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montcada_i_Reixac) to the west. The municipality includes two small sparsely-inhabited [exclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) to the north-west.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Barcelona)

According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Barcelona has a hot summer [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa*), with mild winters and warm to hot summers,[^cite_note-80] while the rainiest seasons are autumn and spring. The rainfall pattern is characterized by a short (3 months) [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season) in summer, as well as less winter rainfall than in a typical Mediterranean climate. However, both June and August are wetter than February, which is unusual for the Mediterranean climate. This subtype, labelled as "Portuguese" by the French geographer George Viers after the climate classification of [Emmanuel de Martonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_de_Martonne)[^cite_note-81] and found in the NW Mediterranean area (e.g. [Marseille](40888948.md#Climate)), can be seen as transitional to the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate#Europe) (*Cfa*) found in inland areas.

Barcelona is densely populated, thus heavily influenced by the [urban heat island effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island_effect). Areas outside of the urbanized districts can have as much as 2 °C of difference in temperatures throughout the year.[^cite_note-82] Its average annual temperature is 21.2 °C (70.2 °F) during the day and 15.1 °C (59.2 °F) at night. The average annual temperature of the sea is about 20 °C (68 °F). In the coldest month, January, the temperature typically ranges from 12 to 18 °C (54 to 64 °F) during the day, 6 to 12 °C (43 to 54 °F) at night and the average sea temperature is 13 °C (55 °F).[^cite_note-weather2travel-83] In the warmest month, August, the typical temperature ranges from 27 to 31 °C (81 to 88 °F) during the day, about 23 °C (73 °F) at night and the average sea temperature is 26 °C (79 °F).[^cite_note-weather2travel-83] Generally, the summer or "holiday" season lasts about six months, from May to October. Two months – April and November – are transitional; sometimes the temperature exceeds 20 °C (68 °F), with an average temperature of 18–19 °C (64–66 °F) during the day and 11–13 °C (52–55 °F) at night. December, January and February are the coldest months, with average temperatures around 15 °C (59 °F) during the day and 9 °C (48 °F) at night. Large fluctuations in temperature are rare, particularly in the summer months. Because of the proximity to the warm sea plus the [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island), frosts are very rare in the city of Barcelona. Snow is also very infrequent in the city of Barcelona, but light snowfalls can occur yearly in the nearby [Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collserola) mountains, such as in the [Fabra Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabra_Observatory) located in a nearby mountain.[^cite_note-84]

Barcelona averages 78 rainy days per year (≥ 1 mm), and annual average relative humidity is 72%, ranging from 69% in July to 75% in October. Rainfall totals are highest in late summer and autumn (September–November) and lowest in early and mid-summer (June–August), with a secondary winter minimum (February–March). Sunshine duration is 2,524 hours per year, from 138 (average 4.5 hours of sunshine a day) in December to 310 (average 10 hours of sunshine a day) in July.[^cite_note-Meteorología-85]

As of 2024, Barcelona has a population of 1,686,208,[^cite_note-population-3] on a land area of 101.4 km<sup>2</sup> (39 sq mi). It is the main component of an [administrative area of Greater Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%80rea_Metropolitana_de_Barcelona), with a population of 3,218,071 in an area of 636 km<sup>2</sup> (246 sq mi) (density 5,060 inhabitants/km<sup>2</sup>). The population of the urban area was 4,840,000.[^cite_note-demographia-4] It is the central nucleus of the [Barcelona metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_metropolitan_area), which relies on a population of 5,474,482.[^cite_note-eurostat-5]

In 1900, Barcelona had a population of 533,000,[^cite_note-gec_3-77] which grew steadily but slowly until 1950, when it started absorbing a high number of people from other less-industrialized parts of Spain. Barcelona's population peaked in 1979 at 1,906,998, and fell throughout the 1980s and 1990s as more people sought a higher [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life) in outlying cities in the Barcelona Metropolitan Area. After bottoming out in 2000 with 1,496,266 residents, the city's population began to rise again as younger people started to return, causing a great increase in housing prices.[^cite_note-96]

### Languages spoken

Catalan is the "[own language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language)"[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] of Catalonia and Barcelona, and therefore its the main language used by the City Council, in schools and it dominates the [linguistic landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_landscape). All [toponymy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy) of Barcelona, including the stops of the [metro system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Metro), are exclusively in the [Catalan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language). Despite this, Spanish is the most spoken language in Barcelona (according to the linguistic census held by the Government of Catalonia in 2013) and it is understood almost universally. [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) is also very commonly spoken in the city: it is understood by 95% of the population, while 72.3% can speak it, 79% can read it, and 53% can write it.[^cite_note-99] Knowledge of Catalan has increased significantly in recent decades thanks to a [language immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_immersion) educational system.

After Catalan and Spanish, the most spoken languages in Barcelona are those from North Africa, such as [Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), followed by [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language), [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language), English, Russian and [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechuan_languages), according to data collected by the University of Barcelona.[^cite_note-100]

### Population density



Aerial view of the centre and the Eixample, with the [Serra de Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Collserola) at the background

Barcelona is one of the most densely populated cities in Europe. In 2008, the city council calculated the population to 1,621,090 living in the 102.2 km<sup>2</sup> sized municipality, giving the city an average population density of 15,926 inhabitants per square kilometre with [Eixample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eixample) being the most populated district.[^cite_note-council-101]

In the case of Barcelona though, the land distribution is extremely uneven. Half of the municipality or 50.2 km<sup>2</sup>, all of it located on the municipal edge is made up of the ten least densely populated neighbourhoods containing less than 10% of the city's population, the uninhabited [Zona Franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zona_Franca_\(Barcelona\)) industrial area and [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc) forest park. Leaving the remaining 90% or slightly below 1.5 million inhabitants living on the remaining 52 km<sup>2</sup> (20 sq mi) at an average density close to 28,500 inhabitants per square kilometre.[^cite_note-council-101]

Of the 73 neighbourhoods in the city, 45 had a population density above 20,000 inhabitants per square kilometre with a combined population of 1,313,424 inhabitants living on 38.6 km<sup>2</sup> at an average density of 33,987 inhabitants per square kilometre. The 30 most densely populated neighbourhoods accounted for 57.5% of the city population occupying only 22.7% of the municipality, or in other words, 936,406 people living at an average density of 40,322 inhabitants per square kilometre. The city's highest density is found at and around the neighbourhood of [la Sagrada Família](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagrada_Fam%C3%ADlia,_Barcelona) where four of the city's most densely populated neighbourhoods are located side by side, all with a population density above 50,000 inhabitants per square kilometre.[^cite_note-council-101]

### Age structure

In 1900, almost a third (28.9 percent) of the population were children (aged younger than 14 years). In 2017, this age group constituted only 12.7% of the population. In 2017, people aged between 15 and 24 years made up 9 percent of the population; those aged between 25 and 44 years made up 30.6 percent of the population; while those aged between 45 and 64 years formed 56.9% of all Barcelonans. In 1900, people aged 65 and older made up just 6.5 percent of the population. In 2017, this age group made up 21.5 percent of the population.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Barcelona)



[Temple Expiatori del Sagrat Cor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Expiatori_del_Sagrat_Cor)

In 2007, most of the inhabitants stated they are [Roman Catholic](606848.md) (208 churches).[^cite_note-LMBCN-109] In a 2011 survey conducted by InfoCatólica, 49.5% of Barcelona residents of all ages identified themselves as [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic).[^cite_note-cbn.com-110] This was the first time that more than half of respondents did not identify themselves as Catholic Christians.[^cite_note-cbn.com-110] The numbers reflect a broader trend in Spain whereby the numbers of self-identified Catholics have declined.[^cite_note-cbn.com-110] In 2019, a survey by [Centro de Investigaciones Sociológicas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_de_Investigaciones_Sociol%C3%B3gicas) showed that 53.2% of residents in Barcelona identified themselves as [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) (9.9% practising Catholics, 43.3% non-practising Catholics).[^cite_note-C-I-S-111]

The province has the largest [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) community in Spain, 322,698 people in Barcelona province are of Muslim religion.[^cite_note-observatorio.hispanomuslim.es-112] A considerable number of Muslims live in Barcelona due to immigration (169 locations, mostly professed by [Moroccans in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccans_in_Spain)).[^cite_note-LMBCN-109] In 2014, 322,698 out of 5.5 million people in the province of Barcelona identified themselves as Muslim,[^cite_note-observatorio.hispanomuslim.es-112] which makes 5.6% of the total population.

## Economy

### General information



[Torre Glòries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Gl%C3%B2ries) and the [22@Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22@Barcelona) business and innovation district

The Barcelona metropolitan area comprises over 66% of the people of Catalonia, one of the richer regions in Europe and the fourth richest region per capita in Spain, with a GDP per capita amounting to €28,400 (16% more than [the EU average](1598533.md#Richest_and_poorest_NUTS_regions_\(GDP_PPP_2010\))). The greater Barcelona metropolitan area had a [GDP amounting to \$177 billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP) (equivalent to \$34,821 in per capita terms, 44% more than the EU average), making it the 4th most economically powerful city by gross GDP in the European Union, and 35th in the world in 2009.[^cite_note-pricewater-116] Barcelona city had a very high GDP of €80,894 per head in 2004, according to [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat).[^cite_note-urbanaudit.org-117] Furthermore, Barcelona was Europe's fourth best business city and fastest improving European city, with growth improved by 17% per year as of 2009.[^cite_note-BEBC-118]

Barcelona was the [24th most "livable city" in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_most_livable_cities#Most_Liveable_Cities_Index) in 2015 according to lifestyle magazine *[Monocle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocle_\(2007_magazine\))*.[^cite_note-119] Similarly, according to Innovation Analysts 2thinknow, Barcelona occupies 13th place in the world on *Innovation Cities™ Global Index*.[^cite_note-120] At the same time it is according to the Global Wealth and Lifestyle Report 2020 one of the most affordable cities in the world for a luxury lifestyle.[^cite_note-121]

Barcelona has a long-standing mercantile tradition. Less well known is that the city industrialized early, taking off in 1833, when Catalonia's already sophisticated textile industry began to use steam power. It became the first and most important industrial city in the Mediterranean basin. Since then, manufacturing has played a large role in its history.

[Borsa de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borsa_de_Barcelona) (Barcelona Stock Exchange) is the main stock exchange in the northeastern part of the Iberian Peninsula.

Barcelona was recognized as the Southern European City of the Future for 2014/15, based on its economic potential,[^cite_note-122] by *[FDi Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDi_Magazine)* in their bi-annual rankings.[^cite_note-123]

### Trade fair and exhibitions



The [World Trade Center Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_Barcelona)



The Palau de Congressos de Barcelona

It also has several congress halls, notably [Fira de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fira_de_Barcelona) – the second largest trade fair and exhibition centre in Europe, that host a quickly growing number of national and international events each year (at present above 50). The total exhibition floor space of Fira de Barcelona venues is 405,000 m<sup>2</sup> (41 ha), not counting [Gran Via centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Via_\(Barcelona\)) on the [Plaza de Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_Europa). However, the [Eurozone crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone_crisis) and deep cuts in business travel affected the council's positioning of the city as a convention centre.

An important business centre, the [World Trade Center Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_Barcelona), is located in Barcelona's [Port Vell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Vell) harbour.

The city is known for hosting well as world-class conferences and expositions, including the 1888 *[Exposición Universal de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposici%C3%B3n_Universal_de_Barcelona)*, the [1929 Barcelona International Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Barcelona_International_Exposition) (Expo 1929), the [2004 Universal Forum of Cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Universal_Forum_of_Cultures) and the 2004 [World Urban Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Urban_Forum).[^cite_note-124]

### Tourism



Part of the beach promenade and the beach of *La Barceloneta* towards [Port Olímpic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Ol%C3%ADmpic)



Beaches of Barcelona

In 2023, Barcelona was the 9th most visited city in the world by international visitors and the fifth most visited city in Europe after Paris, Madrid, Amsterdam, and Rome, with around 9 million international arrivals.[^cite_note-125] Barcelona is an internationally renowned tourist destination, with numerous recreational areas, one of the best beaches in the world,[^cite_note-NationalGeographic-126][^cite_note-discoverychannel-127] mild and warm climate, historical monuments, including eight UNESCO [World Heritage Sites](44940.md), 519 hotels as of March 2016[^cite_note-128] including 35 five-star hotels,[^cite_note-129] and developed tourist infrastructure.

Due to its large influx of tourists each year, Barcelona, like many other tourism capitals, has to deal with pickpockets, with wallets and passports being commonly stolen items. Despite its moderate pickpocket rate, Barcelona is considered one of the safest cities in terms of security and personal safety,[^cite_note-130] mainly because of a sophisticated policing strategy that has dropped crime by 32% in just over three years and has led it to be considered the 15th safest city in the world by *[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider)* in 2016.[^cite_note-131]



Street art rejecting tourists

While tourism produces economic benefits, according to one report, the city is "overrun \[by] hordes of tourists". In early 2017, over 150,000 protesters warned that tourism is destabilizing the city. Slogans included "Tourists go home", "Barcelona is not for sale" and "We will not be driven out". By then, the number of visitors had increased from 1.7 million in 1990 to 32 million in a city with a population of 1.62 million, increasing the cost of rental housing for residents and overcrowding the public places. While tourists spent an estimated €30 billion in 2017, they are viewed by some as a threat to Barcelona's identity.[^cite_note-132]

A May 2017 article in the British online daily *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* included Barcelona among the "Eight Places That Hate Tourists the Most" and included a comment from Mayor [Ada Colau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Colau), "We don't want the city to become a cheap souvenir shop", citing Venice as an example.[^cite_note-coffey-133] To moderate the problem, the city has stopped issuing licenses for new hotels and holiday apartments; it also fined AirBnb €30,000. The mayor has suggested introducing a new tourist tax and setting a limit on the number of visitors.[^cite_note-coffey-133] One industry insider, Justin Francis, founder of the [Responsible Travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibletravel.com) agency, stated that steps must be taken to limit the number of visitors that are causing an "overtourism crisis" in several major European cities. "Ultimately, residents must be prioritised over tourists for housing, infrastructure and access to services because they have a long-term stake in the city's success", he said.[^cite_note-134] "Managing tourism more responsibly can help", Francis later told a journalist, "but some destinations may just have too many tourists, and Barcelona may be a case of that".[^cite_note-135] In April 2024 protests against overtourism began in the city, reaching its peak in July 2024. These protests caught further attention again in July 2025, as similar protests took place in [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) and included [Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca), and [Lisbon](18091.md), organized by the Southern Europe Network Against Touristification (SET).[^cite_note-136] During the protests some protesters used large water guns to spray water on tourists.[^cite_note-137]

### Manufacturing sector

Industry generates 21% of the total gross domestic product (GDP) of the region,[^cite_note-138] with the energy, chemical and metallurgy industries accounting for 47% of industrial production.[^cite_note-139] The Barcelona metropolitan area had 67% of the total number of industrial establishments in Catalonia as of 1997.[^cite_note-140]

Barcelona has long been an important European automobile manufacturing centre. Formerly there were automobile factories of [AFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFA_\(automobile\)), [Abadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abadal), Actividades Industriales, Alvarez, [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_\(Spanish_automobile\)), Artés de Arcos, Balandrás, Baradat-Esteve, [Biscúter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisc%C3%BAter), J. Today, the headquarters and a large factory of [SEAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEAT) (the largest Spanish automobile manufacturer) are in one of its suburbs. There is also a [Nissan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Motors) factory in the [logistics and industrial area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zona_Franca_\(Barcelona\)) of the city.[^cite_note-143] The factory of [Derbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbi), a large manufacturer of motorcycles, scooters and mopeds, also lies near the city.[^cite_note-144]

As in other modern cities, the [manufacturing sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) has long since been overtaken by the services sector, though it remains very important. The region's leading industries are textiles, chemical, [pharmaceutical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical), motor, electronic, printing, logistics, publishing, in telecommunications industry and culture the notable [Mobile World Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_World_Congress), and information technology services.

### Fashion



&#x20;[The Brandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brandery) fashion show of 2011

Main article: [Fashion in Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_in_Barcelona)

The traditional importance of textiles is reflected in Barcelona's drive to become a major fashion centre. There have been many attempts to launch Barcelona as a [fashion capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_in_Barcelona), notably *Gaudi Home*.

Beginning in the summer of 2000, the city hosted the [Bread & Butter urban fashion fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_&_Butter_\(tradeshow\)) until 2009, when its organizers announced that it would be returning to [Berlin](3354.md). This was a hard blow for the city as the fair brought €100 m to the city in just three days.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

From 2009, *[The Brandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brandery)*, an urban fashion show, was held in Barcelona twice a year until 2012. According to the [Global Language Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Language_Monitor)'s annual ranking of the world's top fifty fashion capitals Barcelona was named as the seventh most important [fashion capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_capital) of the world right after [Milan](36511.md) and before [Berlin](3354.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-147]

### Technology

## Government and administrative divisions

See also: [Municipal elections in Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_elections_in_Barcelona) and [List of mayors of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Barcelona)



&#x20;[Palau de la Generalitat de Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_de_la_Generalitat_de_Catalunya)

As the capital of the autonomous community of [Catalonia](6822.md), Barcelona is the seat of the Catalan government, known as the *[Generalitat de Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalitat_of_Catalonia)*; of particular note are the [executive branch](294461.md), the [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Catalonia), and the [High Court of Justice of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice_of_Catalonia). The city is also the capital of the [Province of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Barcelona) and the [Barcelonès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelon%C3%A8s) [comarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarques_of_Catalonia) (district).

Barcelona is governed by a city council formed by 41 city councillors, [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_elections_in_Barcelona) for a four-year term by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage). As one of the two biggest cities in Spain, Barcelona is subject to a special law articulated through the *Carta Municipal* (Municipal Law). A first version of this law was passed in 1960 and amended later, but the current version was approved in March 2006.[^cite_note-ley_1/2006-149] According to this law, Barcelona's city council is organized in two levels: a political one, with elected city councillors, and one executive, which administers the programs and executes the decisions taken on the political level.[^cite_note-150] This law also gives the local government a special relationship with the central government and it also gives the mayor wider prerogatives by the means of municipal executive commissions.[^cite_note-151] It expands the powers of the city council in areas like telecommunications, city traffic, [road safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_traffic_safety) and [public safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_safety). It also gives a special economic regime to the city's treasury and it gives the council a veto in matters that will be decided by the central government, but that will need a favourable report from the council.[^cite_note-ley_1/2006-149]



The City Hall of Barcelona

The *Comissió de Govern* (Government Commission) is the executive branch, formed by 24 councillors, led by the [Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Barcelona), with 5 lieutenant-mayors and 17 city councillors, each in charge of an area of government, and 5 non-elected councillors.[^cite_note-152] The plenary, formed by the 41 city councillors, has advisory, planning, regulatory, and fiscal executive functions.[^cite_note-153] The six *Commissions del Consell Municipal* (City council commissions) have executive and controlling functions in the field of their jurisdiction. They are composed by a number of councillors proportional to the number of councillors each political party has in the plenary.[^cite_note-154] The city council has jurisdiction in the fields of [city planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning), transportation, municipal taxes, public highways security through the *[Guàrdia Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A0rdia_Urbana_de_Barcelona)* (the municipal police), city maintenance, [gardens, parks and environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_gardens_of_Barcelona), facilities (like schools, nurseries, sports centres, libraries, and so on), culture, sports, youth and [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_spending). Some of these competencies are not exclusive, but shared with the Generalitat de Catalunya or the central [Spanish government](1306113.md). In some fields with shared responsibility (such as public health, education or social services), there is a shared Agency or Consortium between the city and the Generalitat to plan and manage services.[^cite_note-155]



The Saló de Cent, in the City Hall of Barcelona

The executive branch is led by a Chief Municipal Executive Officer which answers to the Mayor. It is made up of departments which are legally part of the city council and by separate legal entities of two types: autonomous public departments and public enterprises.[^cite_note-156]

The seat of the city council is on the Plaça de Sant Jaume, opposite the seat of Generalitat de Catalunya. Since the [coming of the Spanish democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy), Barcelona [had been governed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_elections_in_Barcelona) by the [PSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialists'_Party_of_Catalonia), first with an [absolute majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority) and later in coalition with [ERC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Left_of_Catalonia) and [ICV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiative_for_Catalonia_Greens). After the May 2007 election, the ERC did not renew the coalition agreement and the PSC governed in a minority coalition with ICV as the junior partner.

After 32 years, on 22 May 2011, [CiU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_and_Union) gained a plurality of seats at the municipal election, gaining 15 seats to the PSC's 11. The [PP](404727.md) hold 8 seats, ICV 5 and ERC 2.

### Public safety

Public safety in Barcelona is the duty of the City Council and the Government of Catalonia, who provide it through 2 police forces: the *[Guàrdia Urbana de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A0rdia_Urbana_de_Barcelona)* and the *[Mossos d'Esquadra](294120.md)*, respectively. Before 2008, public safety was also conducted by the [National Police Corps](1608789.md), however their duties are now delegated to documental and immigration purposes.[^cite_note-157]

Medical emergencies are attended by the [System of Medical Emergencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_of_Medical_Emergencies?action=edit\&redlink=1), while fire and rescue emergencies are attended by the *[Bombers de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombers_de_Barcelona)*. Maritime rescue is provided by the [Maritime Safety and Rescue Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Safety_and_Rescue_Society), the [Civil Guard](325008.md) and *Mossos* (with the support of *Guàrdia Urbana* during the summer).

The protection of the wildlife of the part of [Collserola Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Collserola) inside municipal limits is the duty of the [Rural Agents Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Agents_Corps?action=edit\&redlink=1).

## Education

Main article: [Education in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Spain)



Main hall of the [University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Barcelona)



Roger de Llúria building at Campus de la Ciutadella, [Pompeu Fabra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeu_Fabra_University)

Barcelona has a well-developed higher education system of [public universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university). Most prominent among these are the [University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Barcelona) (established in 1450) and the more modern [Pompeu Fabra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeu_Fabra_University).[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] Barcelona is also home to the [Polytechnic University of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Catalonia), and in the private sector the [EADA Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADA_Business_School) (founded in 1957), which became the first Barcelona institution to run manager training programmes for the business community. [IESE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IESE) Business School, as well as the largest private educational institution, the [Ramon Llull University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Llull_University), which encompasses schools and institutes such as the [ESADE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESADE) Business School. The [Autonomous University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Barcelona), another public university, is located in [Bellaterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellaterra), a town in the [Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%80mbit_metropolit%C3%A0_de_Barcelona). [Toulouse Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse_Business_School) and the [Open University of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University_of_Catalonia) (a private Internet-centred open university) are also based in Barcelona.

The city has a network of public schools, from nurseries to high schools, under the responsibility of a consortium led by city council (though the curriculum is the responsibility of the Generalitat de Catalunya). There are also many private schools, some of them Roman Catholic. Most such schools receive a public subsidy on a per-student basis, are subject to inspection by the public authorities, and are required to follow the same curricular guidelines as public schools, though they charge tuition. Known as *escoles concertades*, they are distinct from schools whose funding is entirely private (*escoles privades*).

The language of instruction at public schools and *escoles concertades* is Catalan, as stipulated by the 2009 Catalan Education Act. Spanish may be used as a language of instruction by teachers of Spanish literature or language, and foreign languages by teachers of those languages. An experimental partial immersion programme adopted by some schools allows for the teaching of a foreign language (English, generally) across the curriculum, though this is limited to a maximum of 30% of the school day. No public school or *escola concertada* in Barcelona may offer 50% or full immersion programmes in a foreign language, nor does any public school or *escola concertada* offer International Baccalaureate programmes.

## Culture



A café in Barcelona

Main article: [Culture of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Barcelona)

Barcelona's cultural roots go back 2000 years. Since the [arrival of democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy), the Catalan language (very much repressed during the [dictatorship of Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_State)) has been promoted, both by recovering works from the past and by stimulating the creation of new works. Barcelona is designated as a [world-class city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) by the Globalization and World Cities Study Group and Network.[^cite_note-160] It has also been part of the [UNESCO Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) as a City of Literature since 2015.[^cite_note-161]

[FC Barcelona](68187.md) is a [sports club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_club) best known worldwide for its [football](10568.md) team, one of the largest and the [wealthiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte_Football_Money_League) in the world.[^cite_note-166] It has 74 national trophies (while finishing 46 times as runners-up) and 17 continental prizes (with being runners-up 11 times), including five [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) trophies out of eight finals and three [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) wins out of four finals. ### Squatter movement

Barcelona is also home to numerous [social centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_center) and [illegal squats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting) that effectively form a shadow society mainly made up of the unemployed, immigrants, dropouts, [anarchists](12.md), [anti-authoritarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-authoritarianism) and [autonomists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism).[^cite_note-169] [Peter Gelderloos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gelderloos) estimates that there around 200 squatted buildings and 40 social centres across the city with thousands of inhabitants, making it one of the largest squatter movements in the world. He notes that they pirate electricity, internet and water allowing them to live on less than one euro a day. He argues that these squats embrace an [anarcho-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-communism) and [anti-work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-work) philosophy, often freely fixing up new houses, cleaning, patching roofs, installing windows, toilets, showers, lights and kitchens. In the wake of austerity, the squats have provided a number of [social services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_services) to the surrounding residents, including bicycle repair workshops, carpentry workshops, [self-defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defense) classes, free libraries, [community gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_gardening), free meals, computer labs, language classes, theatre groups, free medical care and legal support services.[^cite_note-170] The squats help elderly residents avoid eviction and organize various protests throughout Barcelona. Notable squats include [Can Vies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_Vies), [Can Masdeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_Masdeu), [La Cinètika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Cin%C3%A8tika?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Àgora Juan Andrés Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%80gora_Juan_Andr%C3%A9s_Ben%C3%ADtez) and [Kasa de la Muntanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa_de_la_Muntanya).

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Barcelona)

### Air



&#x20;[Barcelona–El Prat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona%E2%80%93El_Prat_Airport) as seen from the air

Barcelona is served by [Barcelona-El Prat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona-El_Prat_Airport), about 17 km (11 mi) south-west of the centre of Barcelona. It is the second-largest airport in Spain, and the largest on the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) coast, which handled more than 50.17 million passengers in 2018, showing an annual upward trend.[^cite_note-171] It is a main hub for [Vueling Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vueling_Airlines) and [Ryanair](262878.md), and also a focus for [Iberia](98613.md) and [Air Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Europa). The airport mainly serves domestic and European destinations, although some airlines offer destinations in Latin America, Asia and the United States. The airport is connected to the city by highway, metro ([Airport T1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_T1_\(Barcelona_Metro\)) and [Airport T2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_T2_station) stations), commuter train ([Barcelona Airport railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroport_railway_station_\(el_Prat\))) and scheduled bus service. A new terminal (T1) has been built, and entered service on 17 June 2009.

Some low-cost airlines, also use [Girona-Costa Brava Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girona-Costa_Brava_Airport), about 90 km (56 mi) to the north, [Reus Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reus_Airport), 77 km (48 mi) to the south, or [Lleida-Alguaire Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lleida-Alguaire_Airport), about 150 km (93 mi) to the west, of the city. [Sabadell Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabadell_Airport) is a smaller airport in the nearby town of [Sabadell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabadell), devoted to [pilot training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_training), aerotaxi and private flights.

The city has several heliports, mainly at the top of hospitals or [tall buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Barcelona). However, only one heliport is public, the [Port of Barcelona Heliport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Barcelona_Heliport).

### Seaport



The [Port of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Barcelona)

The [Port of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Barcelona) has a 2000-year-old history and a great contemporary commercial importance. It is Europe's ninth largest [container](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization) port, with a trade volume of 1.72 million TEU's in 2013.[^cite_note-traffic_statistics_2013-172] The port is managed by the Port Authority of Barcelona. Its 10 km<sup>2</sup> (4 sq mi) are divided into three zones: [Port Vell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Vell) (the old port), the commercial port and the logistics port ([Barcelona Free Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Free_Port)). The port is undergoing an enlargement that will double its size thanks to diverting the mouth of the [Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llobregat) river 2 km (1 mi) to the south.[^cite_note-173]



[Port Vell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Vell) in winter

The Barcelona harbour is the leading European cruiser port and a very important Mediterranean turnaround base.[^cite_note-174] In 2013, 3.6 million pleasure cruise passengers used the Port of Barcelona.[^cite_note-traffic_statistics_2013-172]

The Port Vell area also houses the Maremagnum (a commercial mall), a multiplex cinema, the [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX) Port Vell and one of Europe's largest [aquariums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium) – [Aquarium Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium_Barcelona), containing 8,000 fish and 11 sharks contained in 22 basins filled with 4 million litres of sea water. The Maremagnum, being situated within the confines of the port, is the only commercial mall in the city that can open on Sundays and public holidays.

### National and international rail



The main railway station [Estació de Sants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Sants_railway_station)

Barcelona is a major hub for the Spanish rail network. The city's main [Inter-city rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) station is [Barcelona Sants railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Sants_railway_station), whilst [Estació de França](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estaci%C3%B3_de_Fran%C3%A7a) terminus serves a secondary role handling suburban, regional and medium distance services. Freight services operate to local industries and to the Port of Barcelona.

RENFE's [AVE](523382.md) [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) system, which is designed for speeds of 310 km/h (193 mph), was extended from [Madrid](41188263.md) to Barcelona in 2008 in the form of the [Madrid–Barcelona high-speed rail line](16106142.md). A shared RENFE-SNCF high-speed rail connecting Barcelona and France (Paris, [Marseille](40888948.md) and [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), through [Perpignan–Barcelona high-speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan%E2%80%93Barcelona_high-speed_rail_line)) was launched in 2013. Both these lines serve Barcelona Sants terminal station.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

### Metro and regional rail

Main article: [Barcelona Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Metro)



Barcelona Metro

Barcelona is served by an extensive local public transport network that includes a [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system, a bus network, a [regional railway](6286.md) system, trams, [funiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular), [rack railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rack_railway), a [Gondola lift](298681.md) and [aerial cable cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_tramway). These networks and lines are run by a number of different operators but they are integrated into a coordinated fare system, administered by the [Autoritat del Transport Metropolità](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoritat_del_Transport_Metropolit%C3%A0) (ATM). The system is divided into fare zones (1 to 6) and various Integrated Travel Cards are available.[^cite_note-177]

The [Barcelona Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Metro) network comprises twelve lines, identified by an "L" followed by the line number as well as by individual colours. The Metro largely runs underground; eight Metro lines are operated on dedicated track by the [Transports Metropolitans de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transports_Metropolitans_de_Barcelona) (TMB), whilst four lines are operated by the [Ferrocarrils de la Generalitat de Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarrils_de_la_Generalitat_de_Catalunya) (FGC) and some of them share tracks with RENFE commuter lines.

In addition to the city Metro, several regional rail lines operated by [Renfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe)'s [Rodalies de Catalunya](27342438.md) and FGC run across the city, providing connections to outlying towns in the surrounding region.

### Tram

Main article: [Trams in Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Barcelona)



Barcelona Tram at Avinguda Diagonal

The city currently has two modern tram systems, [Trambaix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trambaix) and [Trambesòs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trambes%C3%B2s), are operated by [TramMet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TramMet).[^cite_note-178] Both go through the [Avinguda Diagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avinguda_Diagonal), one at each end of the avenue, but they do not meet. Since 2020, there is an ongoing project to unify the two systems and allow the tram to connect both ends of the city.[^cite_note-179]

A [heritage tram line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_streetcar), the [Tramvia Blau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramvia_Blau), also operates between the metro Line 7 and the [Funicular del Tibidabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular_del_Tibidabo).[^cite_note-180]

### Funicular and cable car

Barcelona's metro and rail system is supplemented by several aerial [cable cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_tramway), [funiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular) and [rack railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rack_railway) that provide connections to mountain-top stations.

FGC operates the [Funicular de Tibidabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular_de_Tibidabo) up the hill of [Tibidabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibidabo) and the [Funicular de Vallvidrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular_de_Vallvidrera), while TMB runs the [Funicular de Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular_de_Montju%C3%AFc) up [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc). The city has two aerial cable cars: the [Montjuïc Cable Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc_Cable_Car), which serves Montjuïc castle, and the [Port Vell Aerial Tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Vell_Aerial_Tramway) that runs via [Torre Jaume I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Jaume_I) and [Torre Sant Sebastià](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Sant_Sebastia) over the port.

### Bus

Main article: [Buses in Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_Barcelona)

Buses in Barcelona are a major form of public transport, with extensive local, interurban and night bus networks. Most local services are operated by the TMB, although some other services are operated by a number of private companies, albeit still within the ATM fare structure. There are 21 night lines, 19 of them go through Plaça de Catalunya, where you can change to other lines. The frequency is 15/20 minutes between 22\:20 and 06\:00. A separate private bus line, known as Aerobús, links the airport with the city centre, with its own fare structure.

The [Estació del Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estaci%C3%B3_del_Nord_Sports_Hall) (Northern Station), a former railway station which was renovated for the 1992 Olympic Games, now serves as the terminus for long-distance and regional bus services.

### Taxi



Two typical Barcelona taxis

Barcelona has a [metered taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) fleet governed by the Institut Metropolità del Taxi (Metropolitan Taxi Institute), composed of more than 10,000 cars. Most of the licences are in the hands of [self-employed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-employment) drivers. With their black and yellow livery, Barcelona's taxis are easily spotted, and can be caught from one of many [taxi ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_rank), hailed on street, called by telephone or via app.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

On 22 March 2007,[^cite_note-183] Barcelona's City Council started the [Bicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicing) service, a bicycle service understood as a public transport. Once the user has their user card, they can take a bicycle from any of the more than 400 stations spread around the city and use it anywhere the urban area of the city, and then leave it at another station.[^cite_note-184] The service has been a success, with 50,000 subscribed users in three months.[^cite_note-185]

### Roads and highways



[B-20 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-20_motorway_\(Spain\)) in Barcelona

Barcelona lies on three international routes, including [European route E15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E15) that follows the Mediterranean coast, [European route E90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E90) to Madrid and Lisbon, and [European route E09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E09) to Paris. It is also served by a comprehensive network of motorways and highways throughout the metropolitan area, including [A-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_A-2), [A-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_A-7)/[AP-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopista_AP-7), [C-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-16_highway_\(Spain\)), [C-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17_highway_\(Spain\)), [C-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-31_highway_\(Spain\)), [C-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-32_highway_\(Spain\)), [C-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopista_C-33), [C-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autovia_C-60).

The city is circled by three half [ring roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltway) or bypasses, [Ronda de Dalt (B-20)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B20_motorway_\(Spain\)) (on the mountain side), [Ronda del Litoral (B-10)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B10_motorway_\(Spain\)) (along the coast) and [Ronda del Mig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronda_del_Mig) (separated into two parts: [Travessera de Dalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travessera_de_Dalt) in the north and the [Gran Via de Carles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Via_de_Carles_III?action=edit\&redlink=1)), two partially covered[^cite_note-186] fast highways with several exits that bypass the city.

The city's main arteries include [Diagonal Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avinguda_Diagonal,_Barcelona), which crosses it diagonally, [Meridiana Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avinguda_Meridiana,_Barcelona) which leads to [Glòries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pla%C3%A7a_de_les_Gl%C3%B2ries_Catalanes,_Barcelona) and connects with Diagonal Avenue and [Gran Via de les Corts Catalanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Via_de_les_Corts_Catalanes,_Barcelona), which crosses the city from east to west, passing through its centre. The famous boulevard of [La Rambla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rambla,_Barcelona), whilst no longer an important vehicular route, remains an important pedestrian route.

## Main sights



[Sagrada Família](59545.md) church at night, designed by [Gaudí](31566384.md)



[Palau de la Música Catalana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_de_la_M%C3%BAsica_Catalana)

The *[Barri Gòtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_G%C3%B2tic)* (Catalan for "Gothic Quarter") is the centre of the old city of Barcelona. Many of the buildings date from medieval times, some from as far back as the Roman settlement of Barcelona. Catalan *[modernista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernisme)* architecture (related to the movement known as [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) in the rest of Europe) developed between 1885 and 1950 and left an important legacy in Barcelona. Several of these buildings are [World Heritage Sites](44940.md). Especially remarkable is the work of architect [Antoni Gaudí](31566384.md), which can be seen throughout the city. His best-known work is the immense but still unfinished church of the [Sagrada Família](59545.md), which has been under construction since 1882 and is still financed by private donations. As of 2015, completion is planned for 2026.[^cite_note-187]

Barcelona was also home to [Mies van der Rohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Mies_van_der_Rohe)'s [Barcelona Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Pavilion). Designed in 1929 for the [International Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Barcelona_International_Exposition) for Germany, it was an iconic building that came to symbolize modern architecture as the embodiment of van der Rohe's aphorisms "less is more" and "God is in the details".[^cite_note-Rybczynski2001-188] The Barcelona pavilion was intended as a temporary structure and was torn down in 1930 less than a year after it was constructed. A modern re-creation by Spanish architects now stands in Barcelona, however, constructed in 1986.

Barcelona won the 1999 [RIBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_British_Architects) [Royal Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gold_Medal) for its architecture,[^cite_note-189] the first (and as of 2015, only) time that the winner has been a city rather than an individual architect. Barcelona is the home of many points of interest declared [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md):[^cite_note-190]

### Museums

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of museums in Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Barcelona).



The [National Museum of Art of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_Nacional_d'Art_de_Catalunya) stands out for its collection of [Romanesque painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_painting).



[Barcelona Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Museum_of_Contemporary_Art)

Barcelona has many museums, which cover different areas and eras. The [National Museum of Art of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_Nacional_d'Art_de_Catalunya) possesses a well-known collection of [Romanesque art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_art), while the [Barcelona Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Museum_of_Contemporary_Art) focuses on [post-1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war) Catalan and [Spanish art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_art). The [Fundació Joan Miró](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundaci%C3%B3_Joan_Mir%C3%B3), [Picasso Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_Picasso), and [Fundació Antoni Tàpies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundaci%C3%B3_Antoni_T%C3%A0pies) hold important collections of these world-renowned artists, as well as the [Can Framis Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_Framis_Museum), focused on post-1960 Catalan Art owned by [Fundació Vila Casas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundaci%C3%B3_Vila_Casas). Several museums cover the fields of history and archaeology, like the [Barcelona City History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_City_History_Museum) (MUHBA), the [Museum of the History of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_History_of_Catalonia), the [Archeology Museum of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_Museum_of_Catalonia), the [Maritime Museum of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Museum_of_Barcelona), the [Music Museum of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_de_la_M%C3%BAsica_de_Barcelona) and the privately owned [Egyptian Museum of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Museum_of_Barcelona). The Erotic museum of Barcelona is among the most peculiar ones, while [CosmoCaixa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CosmoCaixa_Barcelona) is a [science museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_museum) that received the [European Museum of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Museum_of_the_Year_Award) in 2006.[^cite_note-192]

The [Museum of Mathematics of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Mathematics_of_Catalonia) is a place where mathematics is approached from a practical and playful point of view. The [Museum of Natural Sciences of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Natural_Sciences_of_Barcelona) was founded in 1882 under the name of "Museo Martorell de Arqueología y Ciencias Naturales"[^cite_note-134-anys-dhistoria-193][^cite_note-Miradas-194] (Spanish for "Martorell Museum of Archaeology and Natural Sciences"). In 2011 the Museum of Natural Sciences ended up with a merge of five institutions: the Museum of Natural Sciences of Barcelona (the main site, at the [Forum Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_Building)), the [Martorell Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martorell_Museum?action=edit\&redlink=1) (the historical seat of the Museum, opened to the public from 1924 to 2010 as a geology museum), the *[Laboratori de Natura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratori_de_Natura?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, at the [Castle of the Three Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_of_the_Three_Dragons) (from 1920 to 2010: the Zoology Museum), the [Historical Botanical Garden of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Botanical_Garden_of_Barcelona?action=edit\&redlink=1), founded 1930, and the [Botanical garden of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jard%C3%AD_Bot%C3%A0nic_de_Barcelona), founded 1999. Those two gardens are a part of the [Botanical Institute of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_Institute_of_Barcelona) too.

The [FC Barcelona Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona_Museum) is the third most popular tourist attraction in Catalonia, with 1,51 million visitors in 2013.[^cite_note-195]

### Parks

Main article: [Parks and gardens of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_gardens_of_Barcelona)



[Park Güell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_G%C3%BCell)



&#x20;[Parc de la Ciutadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_de_la_Ciutadella) north of [La Barceloneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Barceloneta)

Barcelona contains sixty municipal parks, twelve of which are historic, five of which are thematic (botanical), forty-five of which are [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_park), and six of which are forest.[^cite_note-196] They range from vest-pocket parks to large recreation areas. The urban parks alone cover 10% of the city (549.7 ha or 1,358.3 acres).[^cite_note-guies_estadistiques-78] The total park surface grows about 10 ha (25 acres) per year,[^cite_note-197] with a proportion of 18.1 m<sup>2</sup> (195 sq ft) of park area per inhabitant.[^cite_note-198]

Of Barcelona's parks, [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc) is the largest, with 203 [ha](24919513.md) located on the mountain of the same name, and includes the [Botanical Garden of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jard%C3%AD_Bot%C3%A0nic_de_Barcelona), the [Mossèn Costa i Llobera Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss%C3%A8n_Costa_i_Llobera_Gardens), and more.[^cite_note-guies_estadistiques-78] It is followed by [Parc de la Ciutadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_de_la_Ciutadella) (which occupies the site of the old [citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_Barcelona) and which houses the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Catalonia) building, the [Barcelona Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Zoo), and several museums); 31 ha or 76.6 acres including the zoo), the [Guinardó Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinard%C3%B3_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) (19 ha or 47.0 acres), [Park Güell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_G%C3%BCell) (designed by [Antoni Gaudí](31566384.md); 17.2 ha or 42.5 acres), [Oreneta Castle Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oreneta_Castle_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) (also 17.2 ha or 42.5 acres), [Diagonal Mar Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_Mar_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) (13.3 ha or 32.9 acres, inaugurated in 2002), [Nou Barris Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nou_Barris_Central_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) (13.2 ha or 32.6 acres), [Can Dragó Sports Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_Drag%C3%B3_Sports_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Poblenou Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poblenou_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) (both 11.9 ha or 29.4 acres), the [Labyrinth Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_del_Laberint_d'Horta) (9.10 ha or 22.5 acres), named after the garden maze it contains.[^cite_note-guies_estadistiques-78] There are also several smaller parks, for example, the [Parc de Les Aigües](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_de_Les_Aig%C3%BCes?action=edit\&redlink=1) (2 ha or 4.9 acres). A part of the Collserola Park is also within the city limits. [PortAventura World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PortAventura_World), one of the largest resort in Europe, with 5,837,509 visitors per year, is located one hour's drive from Barcelona.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200] Also, within the city lies [Tibidabo Amusement Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibidabo_Amusement_Park), a smaller amusement park in Plaza del Tibidabo, with the [Muntanya Russa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntanya_Russa_\(Tibidabo_Amusement_Park\)) amusement ride.

Besides the parks managed by the town hall, there exist several self-managed green spaces and gardens such as Jardí del Silenci in Gracia,[^cite_note-201] the [Ágora Juan Andrés Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%80gora_Juan_Andr%C3%A9s_Ben%C3%ADtez) in Raval,[^cite_note-202] and L'Horta Alliberada in Sants.[^cite_note-203]

### Beaches



[La Barceloneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Barceloneta)

Barcelona beach was listed as number one in a list of the top ten city beaches in the world according to *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_\(magazine\))*[^cite_note-NationalGeographic-126] and *[Discovery Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Channel)*.[^cite_note-DiscoveryChannel-204] Barcelona contains seven beaches, totalling 4.5 km (2+3⁄4 mi) of coastline. [Sant Sebastià](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Sebasti%C3%A0), [Barceloneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Barceloneta,_Barcelona) and [Somorrostro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somorrostro) beaches, both 1,100 m (3,610 ft) in length,[^cite_note-guies_estadistiques-78] are the largest, oldest and the most-frequented beaches in Barcelona.

The [Olympic Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Harbour) separates them from the other city beaches: Nova Icària, Bogatell, Mar Bella, Nova Mar Bella and Llevant. These beaches (ranging from 400 to 640 m (1,310 to 2,100 ft) were opened as a result of the city restructuring to host the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics), when a great number of industrial buildings were demolished. At present, the beach sand is artificially replenished given that storms regularly remove large quantities of material. The [2004 Universal Forum of Cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Universal_Forum_of_Cultures) left the city a large concrete bathing zone on the eastmost part of the city's coastline. Most recently, Llevant is the first beach to allow dogs access during summer season.
