The **Community of Madrid**[^cite_note-4] is one of the seventeen [autonomous communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_communities) and 50 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain) of [Spain](26667.md). It is located at the heart of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) and [Central Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meseta_Central) (*Meseta Central*); its capital and largest municipality is [Madrid](41188263.md). The Community of Madrid is bounded to the south and east by [Castilla–La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilla%E2%80%93La_Mancha) and to the north and west by [Castile and León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n). It was formally created in 1983, in order to address the particular status of the city of Madrid as the capital of the Spanish State and in urban hierarchy.[^cite_note-5] Its boundaries are coextensive with those of the province of Madrid, which was until then conventionally included in the historical region of [New Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Castile_\(Spain\)) (*Castilla la Nueva*).

The Community of Madrid is the 3rd most populous in Spain with 7,113,886 inhabitants as of 2025,[^cite_note-population-2] roughly a seventh of the national total, mostly concentrated in the [metropolitan area of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area_of_Madrid).[^cite_note-6] It is also the most densely populated autonomous community. Madrid has both the largest nominal [GDP](12594.md), slightly ahead of that of [Catalonia](6822.md),[^cite_note-7] and the highest GDP per capita in the country.[^cite_note-8] Madrid's economy is highly [tertiarised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy),[^cite_note-9] having a leading role in Spain's logistics and transportation.

The Community of Madrid is almost entirely comprised in the [Tagus Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus_Basin), from the [Central System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_Central) (*Sistema Central*) reliefs in the north and northwest to the [Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus) River bed in the southern border. The climate is generally temperate, ranging from [mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) to [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate), except in the Central System highlands. It contains four [World Heritage Sites](44940.md): the [Monastery and Royal Site of El Escorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Escorial), the university and historic centre of [Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares), the cultural landscape of [Aranjuez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranjuez) and the [Paseo del Prado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paseo_del_Prado) and [Buen Retiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_del_Buen_Retiro,_Madrid) park in Madrid City. In addition, the [Montejo Beech Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montejo_Beech_Forest?action=edit\&redlink=1) is part of the transnational [Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_and_Primeval_Beech_Forests_of_the_Carpathians_and_Other_Regions_of_Europe) world heritage site.

## History

### From Prehistory to Late Antiquity



Prehistoric vessel from [Ciempozuelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciempozuelos), exhibited at the [National Archaeological Museum of Spain in Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archaeological_Museum_of_Spain)

The territory of the Community of Madrid has been populated since the [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic), mainly in the valleys between the rivers of [Manzanares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manzanares_\(river\)), [Jarama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarama), and [Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henares), where several archaeological findings have been made.

Some notable discoveries of the region the bell-shaped vase of Ciempozuelos (between 1970 and 1470 BCE),[^cite_note-10] from the [Bell beaker culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_beaker_culture).

The territory of the current-day region currently roughly belonged to the wider territory of [Carpetania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpetania).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMangas_ManjarrésAzcárraga_CámaraMärtens_Alfaro2017680-11] Under Roman rule, urban centres in the area clearly included [Complutum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutum) (northeast) and [Titulcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titulcia) (south). Other urban locations of moot identification in the Carpetania within the bounds of the current-day region might be Mantua and [Miaccum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miaccum?action=edit\&redlink=1) (tentatively ascribed to, respectively, the southwest and the northwest) as well as another possible urban-level settlement in the Dehesa de la Oliva site (near [Patones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patones)) in the north.[^cite_note-12]



A mosaic of the House of Dionysus in [Complutum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutum)

During the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the region was part of the [Citerior Tarraconese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citerior_Tarraconese?action=edit\&redlink=1) province, except for the south-west portion of it, which belonged to [Lusitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitania). It was crossed by two important Roman roads, the *via xxiv-xxix* (joining [Astorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astorga,_Spain) to [Laminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminium) and *via xxv* (which joined [Emerita Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerita_Augusta) and [Caesaraugusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesaraugusta)), and contained some important conurbations.

During the period of the [Visigothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom), the region lost its importance. The population was scattered amongst several small towns. Complutum was designated the bishopric seat in the 5th century by orders of Asturio, archbishop of [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Toledo), but this event was not enough to bring back the lost splendor of the city.

### Middle ages

The centre of the peninsula (the [Middle March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_March) of [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) or *aṯ-Ṯaḡr al-Awsaṭ*) became a frontier march for the Umayyads rulers of [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain), who developed a defensive system of fortresses and towers all across the region with which they tried both to prevent attacks of the Christian northern kingdoms and to control [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Spain). Part of the current-day province was under the political control of the [Banū Sālim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban%C5%AB_S%C4%81lim) lineage, in the so-called Taġr Banū Sālim, that also included territories of the current day provinces of [Soria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Soria) and [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Guadalajara).

Both *Mayrit* ([Madrid](41188263.md)) and [Talamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talamanca_de_Jarama) were founded towards 860 under Umayyad rule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartín_Viso200821-13] Later foundations, dated around 940, include the fortresses of Calatalifa ([Villaviciosa de Odón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villaviciosa_de_Od%C3%B3n)) and Saktan (tentatively identified with Peña Muñana in [Cadalso de los Vidrios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadalso_de_los_Vidrios)).[^cite_note-14] Likewise, the castle of [Qal'-at'-Abd-Al-Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_la_Vieja?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Alcalá la Vieja) replaced the old Complutum.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartín_Viso200821-13] Meanwhile, the population along the [Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus) valley probably developed from scattered rural communities (*[alquerías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alquer%C3%ADa)*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartín_Viso200821–22-15] The economy was based in agricultural activities (cereal, grapevines, and olive) in the plains near the Guadarrama, Manzanares, and Henares, and cattle in the mountains.[^cite_note-16] Water infrastructures were built to increase crop productivity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorán_Alonso202313-17]

The region passed to Christian control in the context of the Conquest of the [Taifa of Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Toledo) by Alfonso VI. Madrid and Talamanca were recorded among the list of the most important places of the Kingdom of Toledo to have passed to Christian control.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartín_Viso200823-18] [Almoravid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid) pusback lead to the Muslim recovery of Alcalá by 1109 and a 1110 attack on Madrid.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartín_Viso200823-18] *[Razzias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razzia_\(military\))* damaged many of the pre-existing water infrastructures in the 11th and 12th centuries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorán_Alonso202313-17] The entire centre of the peninsula became a [war theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_theater) and Christian occupation of the territory did not fully consolidate until the collapse of the Almoravid threat towards 1145–47.[^cite_note-19] Alcalá became a estate of the [Mitre of Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Toledo) for centuries to come after a 1125 donation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartín_Viso200833-20] Talamanca followed suit in 1188.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMonsalvo_Antón200385-21] Organized originally through the so-called *[comunidades de villa y tierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comunidad_de_Villa_y_Tierra?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, human use of the land in the region expanded from the 12th to the 15th century, with the bulk of the current-day settlements' origins dating back to this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorán_Alonso202314-22]

A key feature of the middle ages in the territory of the current-day region was the constant strife of the town of Madrid with the powerful council of [Segovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segovia), whose jurisdiction extended south of the [Sistema Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_Central) (the *Transierra*); particularly along the [Guadarrama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadarrama_River), holding possessions such as the [sexmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexmo) of Valdemoro, the sexmo of Casarrubios, and the sexmo of Valdelozoya. Both councils vied for the control over [El Real de Manzanares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Real_de_Manzanares?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Monte de El Pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_de_El_Pardo).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESegura_Graiño19957–8-23] In addition to the town councils, the territory also featured some presence of the [military orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_order_\(religious_society\)), particularly the [Order of Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Santiago), that established a network of [commanderies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanderies) in the Tagus valley area (south of the current-day region) underpinned by their control of the fortresses in Alarilla, Alboer, Oreja, Malsobaco, and Biedma,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartín_Viso200834–35-24] in addition to the control of [Paracuellos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracuellos_del_Jarama) in the centre of the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVera_Yagüe1996672–673-25] Competing interests, primarily economic, led to rocky relationships between the Order and the Archdiocese of Toledo.[^cite_note-26] Likewise, there was also a monastic estate (under the [Abbey of Santa María de Valdeiglesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery_of_Pelayos)) in the west.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVera_Yagüe1996672-27]

The aftermath of the [Castilian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilian_Civil_War_of_1351%E2%80%931369) and subsequent onset of the [Trastamara dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Trast%C3%A1mara) in 1369 brought the gift of much territory in the *Transierra* hitherto held by royal-demesne councils to the nobility, with the [House of Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Mendoza) being, by far, the biggest beneficiary.[^cite_note-28]

### Early modern period



Panoramic view of Madrid, a 16th-century work by [Anton van den Wyngaerde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_van_den_Wyngaerde)

The town of Madrid, which was one of the eighteen cities with the right to vote in the [Cortes of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortes_of_Castile), was seat of the Courts themselves on several occasions and was the residence of several monarchs, amongst them the emperor [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Spain) who reformed and expanded the *Alcázar* or Castle of the city. Alcalá de Henares grew in importance as cultural center since the foundation by the [Cardinal Cisneros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Jim%C3%A9nez_de_Cisneros) of [its university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares).



[Philip II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) supervises the works on [El Escorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Escorial) (by [Luca Giordano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Giordano)).

In 1561, King [Philip II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) made Madrid the capital of the [Hispanic Monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_empire). The surrounding territories became economically subordinated to the town itself, even beyond the present day limits of the Community of Madrid. But it was not a unified region as several lords and churches had jurisdiction over their own autonomous territories.

During the 18th century, the fragmented administration of the region was not solved despite several attempts. During the reign of [Philip V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Spain), the *intendencia* was created as a political and administrative division. Nonetheless, the *intendencia* of Madrid did not fully solve the problem, and the region was still fragmented into several small dominions even though some processes were centralized. This territorial dispersion had a negative effect on its economic growth; while the town of Madrid received economic resources from the entire country as the capital, the surrounding territories—in hands of noblemen or the clergy—became impoverished.

During the eighteenth century, the town of Madrid was transformed through several grandiose buildings and monuments as well as through the creation of many social, economic, and cultural institutions, some of which are still operating. Madrid grew to a population of 156,672 inhabitants by the end of the eighteenth century.

### Province



Manufacturing of big clay pots in [Colmenar de Oreja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colmenar_de_Oreja) (by [Ulpiano Checa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulpiano_Checa))

The current territory of the region was roughly defined with the [1833 reorganization of Spain into provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1833_territorial_division_of_Spain) promoted by [Javier de Burgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_de_Burgos), in which the province of Madrid was classified in the region of [New Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Castile_\(Spain\)) (lacking the later any sort of administrative institution at the regional level nonetheless). The government institution at the provincial level was the [deputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_deputation_\(Spain\)) (*diputación*). In addition to the former body, another provincial political authority was the [civil governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_governor?action=edit\&redlink=1) discretionarily designated by the central government. Two modest changes to the 1833 provincial boundaries that concerned Madrid took place shortly before 1845, when Aranjuez (187 km<sup>2</sup>) left the [province of Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Toledo) and joined that of Madrid, and in 1850, when the small municipality of [Valdeavero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdeavero) (19 km<sup>2</sup>), until then part of the [province of Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Guadalajara), joined the province of Madrid.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurgueño1990403,_406-29]



Construction of the bridge-aqueduct of the chasm, part of the [Canal de Isabel II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_de_Isabel_II) in 1854 (by [Charles Clifford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Clifford_\(photographer\)))

One of the limits so far for the growth of the capital, water supply, experienced a substantial change in 1858 following the arrival to the city of Madrid of water from the [Lozoya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozoya_River) with the inauguration of the bringing of the [Canal de Isabel II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_de_Isabel_II).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBahamonde_MagroFernández_García2008489-30]



Female workers in a phone-line factory managed by [Ericsson](9424.md) in Getafe (1924)

In decadence since the middle 18th century, the city of [Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares), experienced a relative demographic and economic upturn in the second half of the 19th century, based on its newly acquired condition of military outpost, to which an embryonic industrial nucleus was also added.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGómez_Mendoza2008625–628-31]

During the reign of [Ferdinand VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_VII_of_Spain) the south of the province was made up of small agricultural settlements of limited population. Among them, Getafe stood out in population,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Alcalá2008650-32] and became the seat of a [judicial district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_districts_of_Spain) in 1834,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Alcalá2008652-33] with the main economic activity of the former jurisdiction still being non-irrigated agriculture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Alcalá2008652-33] [Rail transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) arrived in 1851, with the [Strawberry train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry_train), the railway connecting Madrid and [Aranjuez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranjuez).



Ruins of the headquarters of the provincial deputation in 1939

During the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) (1936–1939), the territory was divided by the battlefront, with the southwest of the province controlled by the [rebel faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_faction_\(Spanish_Civil_War\)), and the capital as well as a great part of the rest of the province by the [side loyal to the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_faction_\(Spanish_Civil_War\)). The city of Madrid was target of many bombings during the conflict, becoming the first big city in Europe to suffer such systematic and massive air attacks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESánchez_Pérez2008555-34]

Since the 1970s, a process of a population transfer from the capital to the rest of municipalities of the metropolitan area emerged. This process accelerated when the autonomous community was founded, and it took place along a strong decrease of birth rates.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFernández_García2008733-35]

### Autonomous community

The creation of the contemporary Community of Madrid was preceded by an intense political debate. Autonomous communities were to be created by one or more provinces with a distinct regional identity. Since the 1833 provincial organization, Madrid was part of the [historical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_region) of [New Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Castile_\(Spain\)) along with the provinces of Guadalajara, Toledo, Cuenca and Ciudad Real. Thus, it was first planned that the province of Madrid would be part of the future community of [Castile–La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile%E2%80%93La_Mancha) (which was roughly similar to New Castile, with the addition of [Albacete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Albacete)) but with some special considerations as the home of the national capital.[^cite_note-sinopsis-36] The other provinces that were to become part of Castile–La Mancha expressed fears of inequality if Madrid were associated with them. These provinces opposed such a special status, and after considering other options for Madrid—like its inclusion in the community of Castile and León or its constitution as an entity similar to a [federal district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_district)[^cite_note-sinopsis-36]—it was decided that the province of Madrid would become a single-province autonomous community by virtue of Article 144 of the Constitution, which empowers the Cortes to create an autonomous community in the "nation's interest" even if it did not satisfy the requirement of having a distinct historical identity. Thus, in 1983, the Community of Madrid was constituted and a [Statute of Autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy) was approved taking over all the competences of the old "*Diputación Provincial*" and the new ones the Statute considered.



Sunset in Las Tablas in 2015

During the first 25 years of the "autonomic" period, this autonomous community accounted for the biggest [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) in Spain, becoming a platform for the internationalisation of the [Spanish economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_economy),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_DelgadoCarrera_Troyano2008753-37] featuring a marked preponderance of the [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_DelgadoCarrera_Troyano2008756–757-38] By the turn of the 21st century, a strong boost to the [construction sub-sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction) also took place.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_DelgadoCarrera_Troyano2008757–758-39] During this period the Community of Madrid stood out due to its role as centre for welcoming immigration,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_DelgadoCarrera_Troyano2008761-40] due to its condition as transport node vis-à-vis the Spanish geography,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_DelgadoCarrera_Troyano2008762-41] and due to its condition as scientific and cultural centre of the country.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_DelgadoCarrera_Troyano2008763–764-42]

## Geography

Relief map of the Community of Madrid.

Despite the existence of a large urban area of nearly 7 million people, the Community of Madrid still retains some remarkably unspoiled and diverse habitats and landscapes. Madrid is home to mountain peaks rising above 2,000 m, [holm oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_rotundifolia) dehesas and low-lying plains. The slopes of the [Guadarrama mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadarrama_mountain_range) are cloaked in dense forests of [Scots pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_pine) and Pyrenean [oak](39696.md). The [Lozoya Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozoya_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1) supports a large [black (monk) vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_black_vulture) colony, and one of the last bastions of the [Spanish imperial eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_imperial_eagle) in the world is found in the Park Regional del Suroeste in dehesa hills between the Gredos and Guadarrama ranges. The recent possible detection of the existence of [Iberian lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_lynx) in the area between the [Cofio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofio_River) and [Alberche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberche) rivers is testament to the biodiversity of the area. Taking advantage of the orography, there are several reservoirs and local dams, with the [Santillana reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santillana_reservoir) being the largest.



[Peñalara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1alara): the [Guadarrama mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadarrama_mountain_range)'s highest peak.

The province of Madrid is shaped approximately like an equilateral triangle, with the city of Madrid located near its center. First, by the western side, it borders the "[Sistema Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_Central)" (the [Guadarrama mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadarrama_mountain_range)), the southern border features a protrusion following the [Tagus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus) in order to include the royal site of [Aranjuez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranjuez) in the region; the eastern edge of the triangle comes from the rupture of the fluvial river basins. This autonomous community is located in the basin of the Tagus River. The Tagus passes through the southern border of the Autonomy in its path west toward the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), draining the waters of the [Jarama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarama_River) (collecting in turn the waters of the [Lozoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozoya_River), the [Guadalix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalix_River), the [Manzanares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manzanares_River), the [Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henares_River) and the [Tajuña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taju%C3%B1a_River)), the [Alberche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberche) and the [Guadarrama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadarrama_River) in the Community.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGallego_Calvo2011414-43]



The [Iberian ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capra_pyrenaica) (*Capra pyrenaica*) in [La Pedriza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pedriza).

This autonomous community also includes the exclave of [Dehesa de la Cepeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehesa_de_la_Cepeda) (part of the municipality of [Santa María de la Alameda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_la_Alameda)), a mostly open-area geographically located between the provinces of [Ávila](143517.md) and [Segovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segovia) in the autonomous community of [Castile and León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_and_Leon).

The Province of Madrid occupies a surface area of approximately 8,028 km<sup>2</sup> (3,100 sq mi) (1.6% of all Spanish territory). More specifically, the exact position of Madrid is 3° 40' of longitude west of [Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich), England, and 40° 23' north of the equator.

Most of the province lies between 600 and 1,000 m above sea level. However, there the altitude ranges from 2,428 metres of [Peñalara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1alara) to 430 metres at the Alberche river when it leaves [Villa del Prado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_del_Prado) into the province of Toledo.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAyllón_LópezBarbera_Luna201158-44] Other considerable heights, as well as being famous, are the [Bola del Mundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bola_del_Mundo) ("Ball of the World") in [Navacerrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navacerrada), at a height of 2,258 m, the [Siete Picos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siete_Picos) ("Seven Peaks") in [Cercedilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercedilla), at 2,138 m, and the [Peña Cebollera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1a_Cebollera) (2,129 m) at the northernmost end of the province, a [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) between the Madrid region and the provinces of Segovia and Guadalajara.

Among the protected species of birds nesting in the region stand out the [Spanish imperial eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_imperial_eagle), the [golden eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_eagle), the [Bonelli's eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonelli's_eagle), the [cinereous vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinereous_vulture), the [peregrine falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_falcon) and the [black stork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_stork).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELara_ZabíaMontoro_LouvierMingot_Martín2011362-45]

Exotic invasive species of birds and mammals in the region include the [red-eared slider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachemys_scripta_elegans), the [monk parakeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myiopsitta_monachus), the [common snapping turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelydra_serpentina), the [rose-ringed parakeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psittacula_krameri), the [American mink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neovison_vison) and the [raccoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procyon_lotor).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECobos_SuárezMartín_Sanz201188–90-46] Species described as "out of place" and with an increasing population include the [black-headed gull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larus_ridibundus), the [lesser black-backed gull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larus_fuscus), the [great cormorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalacrocorax_carbo) and [Eurasian collared dove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptopelia_decaocto),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECobos_SuárezMartín_Sanz201190–91-47] while the emblematic [iberian ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_ibex) is presented as a case of a species "gone out of control" in [La Pedriza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pedriza) following its [re-introduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-introduction) in the region in 1990 after roughly a century disappeared from the Madrilenian mountains.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECobos_SuárezMartín_Sanz201192-48]

### Vegetation



The summer drought is characteristic of the Madrid region's climate. [Montejo Beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montejo_Beech?action=edit\&redlink=1) (part of the transnational [Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_and_Primeval_Beech_Forests_of_the_Carpathians_and_Other_Regions_of_Europe) world heritage site) is a relict forest featuring a particular case of microclimate, allowing for Eurosiberian species that do not grow in the region in normal conditions.

In the vicinity of the mountain peaks, oromediterranean vegetation such as *[Agrostula truncatula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrostula_truncatula)*, *[Armeria caespitosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armeria_caespitosa)*, *[Festuca indigesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festuca_indigesta)*, *[Jasione crispa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasione_crispa?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Jurinea humilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurinea_humilis?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Minuartia recurva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minuartia_recurva)*, *[Pilosella vahlii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilosella_vahlii?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Plantago holosteum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantago_holosteum)* and the *[Thymus praecox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymus_praecox)* is common.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrijalbo_Cervantes201131-55] Below the summit line, shrubby species such as the [Cytisus oromediterraneus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytisus_oromediterraneus) and the [common juniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniperus_communis) as well as the [Scots pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_sylvestris) take over.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrijalbo_Cervantes201132-56] There are also masses of [black pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_nigra) and the [pyrenean oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_pyrenaica) situated above the domain of the holm oak.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrijalbo_Cervantes201134-57]



Olive tree orchards in [Arganda del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arganda_del_Rey).

Eurosiberian flora is not common in the region, and species such as the [moor birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betula_alba) and the [silver birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betula_pendula) are restricted to very specific humid valley areas with special climate conditions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrijalbo_Cervantes201135-58] The climax vegetation in the *campiña* is the [holly oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_ilex). Some of the species that take over when the holly oak forest degrades are the ["sticky shrub"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistus_ladanifer), the [Retama sphaerocarpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retama_sphaerocarpa), the [French lavender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavandula_pedunculata), the *[Thymus mastichina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymus_mastichina)* and the *[Thymus zygis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymus_zygis)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrijalbo_Cervantes201139-59]

The lower reaches of Guadarrama Mountain Range are populated by species such as the *[Juniperus thurifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniperus_thurifera)*, the [maritime pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_pinaster), the [Portuguese oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_faginea), the [stone pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_pinea); only in the somewhat more humid westernmost end of the region, near the [Alto del Mirlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto_del_Mirlo?action=edit\&redlink=1), there are forests of [chestnut trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castanea_sativa).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrijalbo_Cervantes201136–38-60]

54,4% of the surface of the region is soil categorised as forest areas of which the 51.4% (27.7% of the total of the region) it is already covered by forests, so there is room for tree re-population.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESanjuanbenito_García2011163-61] The first modest efforts towards tree re-population were taken in the Lozoya Valley in the late 19th century intending to achieve a purer water from the river,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESanjuanbenito_García2011161-62] that provided the capital with water for consumption. However, the bulk of the process took place after the Spanish Civil War, with a largely successful repopulation with several species of [conifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifers).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESanjuanbenito_García2011162-63]

### Climate

See also: [Climate of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Madrid)

The Madrid region features a climate marked by dry summers, while average temperature varies with altitude, marking different climate subtypes. Most of the region has a climate intermediate between a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csa*) and a [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Cold_semi-arid_climates) (Köppen *BSk*), with a dry summer and a moderate to low amount of rain primarily distributed throughout the rest of the year (in the case of the capital, roughly an equinoctial pattern of precipitation maximums), as well as summer temperature averages over 22 °C (with daily maximums consistently surpassing 30 °C in July and August). The capital has a cold semi-arid climate (*BSk*) and in the west it borders the Mediterranean climate (*Csa*). The areas at a higher altitude close to the [Sierra de Guadarrama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_Guadarrama) feature a colder climate, also generally with more precipitation (particularly in the winter), with climate subtypes ranging from the *Csa* to the warm-summer mediterranean climate (Köppen: *Csb*) and the [dry summer continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) (Köppen: *Dsb*) on the peaks of the mountain range, with temperature averages below freezing point during January and February in the later case. It is the [European capital](9239.md) with the lowest average annual precipitation.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

## Government and politics

### Autonomous institutions of government

See also: [Assembly of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Madrid), [President of the Community of Madrid](608649.md), and [Government of the Community of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Community_of_Madrid)



[Hemicycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicycle) of the Assembly of Madrid, the autonomous legislature



A meeting of the [Council of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Community_of_Madrid) presided by former President [Ángel Garrido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Garrido)

Like the rest of autonomous communities, the Community of Madrid is organized politically within a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system); that is, the [head of government](37570.md)—known as the "president"—is dependent on the direct support from the autonomous [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power), whose members elect him by a [majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority).

The [Statute of Autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy) of the Madrid Autonomous Community is the fundamental organic law in conjunction with (and subordinated to) the [Spanish constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_constitution). The Statute of Autonomy establishes that the powers through which the self-government of the autonomous community is exercised are the following institutions:

- The [Assembly of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Madrid),

  [^cite_note-67]

  &#x20;a directly elected body, represents the people of Madrid and exercises the [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) of the community in approving and supervising the budget and in coordinating and controlling the actions of the government. The seat of the Assembly is located in Madrid, in the district of [Puente de Vallecas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_de_Vallecas). The members of the legislature (currently 132) are elected through [proportional representation](23298.md) with closed-party lists and a 5% [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) in a single region-wide constituency.

  [\[n. 1\]](#cite_note-69)

  &#x20;The [last election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Madrilenian_regional_election) took place on 26 May 2019.

- The [President of the Community of Madrid](608649.md) is the supreme representative of the autonomous community and the ordinary representation of the State. It presides and heads the activities of the Madrilenian autonomous government, designates and dismisses the vice-presidents and the ministers (*consejeros*) which conform an executive cabinet. The investiture of the regional president, who is nominated as candidate by the [Speaker of the Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_the_Madrid_Assembly) among its members after holding consultations, is voted by a qualified majority of the plenary of the legislature (or, failing to achieve the former, a simple majority of 'yes' votes in a second round voting 48 hours later) and then formally appointed by the King through a [Royal Decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Decree). The seat of the Presidency is the [Real Casa de Correos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_House_of_the_Post_Office) located at the [Puerta del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerta_del_Sol) at the center of Madrid.

  [^cite_note-70]

  &#x20;Since 2019, the president is [Isabel Díaz Ayuso](59693424.md), of the [People's Party](404727.md) (PP).

- The [Government of the Community of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Community_of_Madrid) is the [collegiate body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_body) that heads the politics and the [executive and administrative powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) of the community. The incumbent Council of Government comprises the President, the vice-president (assuming additional competences) and twelve more ministers.

### Delegation of the Central Government

Since the creation of autonomous communities, the [Government of Spain](1306113.md) appoints a special representative to each autonomous community, the [Government Delegate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Delegation_\(Spain\)), part of the Peripheral State Administration. Unlike other single-province autonomous communities, the Government also appoints the Government Sub-delegate, the successor office to the provincial civil governor. The seats of both the delegation and the subdelegation are located at the [Borghetto Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borghetto_Palace?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Madrid.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

The Community of Madrid, following the long-standing form of [local government in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Spain), is divided administratively into 179 [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Spain) (featuring 801 towns and entities). Its municipalities comprise 2.2% of the Spanish territory (8,110). It is ranked 23rd amongst Spanish provinces in number of municipalities, which is slightly above average.[^cite_note-73]

The *[ayuntamiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayuntamiento_\(Spain\))*, presided by its *alcalde* (Mayor) is the formal institution charged with the government and administration of most municipalities. The municipal councillors forming the deliberative assembly of the *ayuntamiento* are directly elected through [proportional representation](23298.md) with closed party lists and a 5% [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold). In turn, the councillors are charged with electing from among themselves (by default candidates are the head of each electoral list) the Mayor presiding over the *ayuntamiento*.

There are twenty [judicial districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_districts_of_Spain) (*partidos judiciales*), whose seats correspond to the municipalities of [Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares), [Alcobendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcobendas), [Alcorcón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcorc%C3%B3n), [Aranjuez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranjuez), [Arganda del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arganda_del_Rey), [Collado Villalba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collado_Villalba), [Colmenar Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colmenar_Viejo), [Coslada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coslada), [Fuenlabrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuenlabrada), [Getafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe), [Leganés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legan%C3%A9s), [Madrid](41188263.md), [Majadahonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majadahonda), [Móstoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B3stoles), [Navalcarnero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navalcarnero), [Parla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parla), [San Lorenzo de El Escorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Lorenzo_de_El_Escorial), [Torrejón de Ardoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrej%C3%B3n_de_Ardoz), [Torrelaguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrelaguna), [Valdemoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemoro), and [Valdaracete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdaracete) (the historical judicial district of San Martín de Valdeiglesias is no longer a judicial district as of 1985). These jurisdictions relate to the judicial administration, with their seat having at least one [court of first instance](21123697.md#Courts_of_first_instance).

As of 2025, the population is 7,113,886, of which 47.9% are male, and 52.1% are female. Minors make up 16.6% of the population, and seniors make up 18.9%.[^cite_note-population-2]

Madrid also has the greatest population density in Spain. Its inhabitants are mainly concentrated in the capital and in a series of municipalities ([Móstoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B3stoles), [Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares), [Fuenlabrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuenlabrada), [Leganés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legan%C3%A9s), [Alcorcón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcorc%C3%B3n), [Getafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe), [Torrejón de Ardoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrej%C3%B3n_de_Ardoz), and [Alcobendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcobendas)), as opposed to in rural areas with low population density. It is a focus of attraction for those migrating for reasons of employment. Population growth in Madrid is mainly due to the arrival of foreigners.[^cite_note-75]

As of 1 January 2024, the region's population included 1,038,671 people born in Spanish-speaking countries from the Americas, up from 81,552 in 1999.[^cite_note-76]

The Community of Madrid is the [EU-Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUTS_regions) with the highest average [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) at birth. The average life expectancy was 82.2 years for males and 87.8 for females in 2016.[^cite_note-77]&#x20;

## Economy



*Distrito Telefónica*, the main headquarters of [Telefónica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telef%C3%B3nica), one of the multinational corporations located in the region

Madrid is the autonomous community with the highest [income per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_per_capita) in Spain, at €38,435 in 2022 – significantly above the national average and ahead of the [Basque Country](20514897.md), with €35,832, [Navarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarra), €33,798, and [Catalonia](6822.md), €32,550.[^cite_note-ine.es-78] In that year, the GDP per capita growth was 8.6%.[^cite_note-ine.es-78] Madrid has a [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) of €230.8 billion (\$281 billion) as of 2018; making it the largest economy of Spain, ahead of [Catalonia](6822.md), where regional GDP amounted to €228.7 billion and the most populated Spanish region, [Andalusia](2736.md) (€160.6 billion).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]



Airbus A330-A340 horizontal stabilizer near the Getafe Airbus factory

In 2005, the Community of Madrid was the main receptor of [foreign investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) in the country, at 34.3% of the total. The community ranks 34th amongst all European regions (evaluated in 2002), and 50th amongst the most competitive cities-regions worldwide,[^cite_note-81] ahead of [Barcelona](4443.md) and [Valencia](63861.md), the other two largest [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_areas) of Spain. The strengths of the economy of the community are its low unemployment rate, its high investment in research, its high development, and the added-value services therein performed. Its weaknesses include the low penetration of broadband and new technologies of information and an unequal male to female occupation.[^cite_note-82]



Madrid Trade Fair

The service, construction, and industry sectors are prominent in Madrid's commercial productive structure. According to the *Directorio Central de Empresas* (Central Companies Directory of the INE), Madrid's active businesses stand in third place nationally in terms of numbers as at 1 January 2006. The branches of activity with most active businesses are other business activities, retail trade, construction, wholesale trade, hospitality, property activities, land transport, and pipeline transport.

Madrid's levels of industrial activity set it at fourth place in Spain. The following areas predominate in terms of business numbers: publishing and graphic arts, manufacture of metal products (except machinery and equipment), manufacture of furniture and other manufacturing industries, wearing apparel and fur industry, and food product industry. The province also boasts a higher concentration of high and medium technology activities and services than the rest of Spain. This is the case in the following areas: manufacture of office machinery and IT equipment; manufacture of electronic products, manufacture of radio equipment, and devices; manufacture of medical and surgical, precision, optical and timekeeping equipment and instruments; post and telecommunications; IT activities; and research and development.[^cite_note-83]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Spain)

State Education in Spain is free and compulsory from six to sixteen years of age. The current education system is called LOMLOE (Ley Orgánica 3/2020, de 29 de diciembre, por la que se modifica la Ley Orgánica 2/2006, de 3 de mayo, de Educación).[^cite_note-88]

### Levels



Secondary school in [Torrejón de Ardoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrej%C3%B3n_de_Ardoz)

- From three to six years – Educación Infantil (Preparatory School)

- From six to twelve years – Educación Primaria (Primary School), years first through sixth

- From twelve to sixteen years – Educación Secundaria Obligatoria (Compulsory Secondary School), years first through fourth

- From sixteen to seventeen years – Bachillerato (Post-Compulsory School), years first and second

Children from three to five years old in Spain have the option of attending the [pre-school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-school) stage, which is non-compulsory and free for all students. It is regarded as an integral part of the education system with infantil classes in almost every primary school. There are some separate Colegios Infantiles or nursery schools.

Spanish students aged six to sixteen undergo [primary](25058.md) and [secondary school](554992.md) education, which are compulsory and free of charge. Successful students are awarded a Secondary Education Certificate, which is necessary for entering further (optional) education as is Bachillerato for their University or Formación Profesional (vocational studies). Once students have finished their Bachillerato, they can take their University Entrance Exam (Pruebas de Acceso a la Universidad, popularly called *Selectividad*) which differs greatly from region to region.

The secondary stage of education is normally referred to by its initials, e. g., ESO or Educación Secundaria Obligatoria for secondary education.

[EducaMadrid](https://www.educa2.madrid.org/educamadrid/) is the educational platform that offers teachers and students in these and other non-university studies (professional studies, arts, languages, adult education and others) a virtual environment with all the necessary Internet services, in compliance with [GDPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation). It is safe, free, sustainable and based on [Open source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software).

### Universities

Madrid is home to a large number of public and private universities.



Faculty of Medicine of the Complutense University of Madrid, in the [Ciudad Universitaria campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City_of_Madrid)

The [Complutense University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid) is one of the most prestigious, and the largest, university in Spain and one of the oldest universities in the world. It has 10,000 staff members and a student population of 117,000. Nearly all academic staff are Spanish. It is located on two campuses, in the university quarter Ciudad Universitaria at Moncloa in Madrid, and in Somosaguas.[^cite_note-Universidad_Complutense-89] The Complutense University of Madrid was founded in [Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares), old Complutum, by [Cardinal Cisneros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Cisneros) in 1499. Nevertherless, its real origin dates back from 1293, when King Sancho IV of Castile built the General Schools of Alcalá, which would give rise to Cisnero's Complutense University. During the course of 1509–1510 five schools were already operative: Artes y Filosofía (Arts & Philosophy), Teología (Theology), Derecho Canónico (Canonical Laws), Letras (Liberal Arts) and Medicina (Medicine). In 1836, during the reign of [Isabel II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_II_of_Spain), the university was moved to Madrid, where it took the name of Central University and was located at San Bernardo Street. Subsequently, in 1927, a new [University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City_of_Madrid) ("Ciudad Universitaria") was planned to be built in the district of Moncloa-Aravaca. The [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) turned the University City into a war zone, with several faculties sustaining severe damage during the conflict. By 1943 the Central University started to be known as the University of Madrid.[^cite_note-90]

In 1970 the University of Madrid was renamed to Complutense University of Madrid. It was then when the new campus at Somosaguas was created in order to house the new School of Social Sciences. The old Alcalá campus was reopened as the independent [University of Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alcal%C3%A1) in 1977.[^cite_note-Universidad_Complutense_de_Madrid-91]



The Severo Ochoa Centre for Molecular Biology, in the campus of the [Autonomous University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Madrid)

Another important university is the [Autonoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Madrid), perhaps Spain's best university for research along with the Complutense, was instituted under the leadership of the famous physicist, [Nicolás Cabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Cabrera_\(physicist\)). The Autonoma is widely recognised for its research strengths in theoretical physics. Known simply as *la Autónoma* in Madrid, its main site is the Cantoblanco Campus, situated 15 kilometers to the north of the capital (M-607) and close to the municipal areas of Madrid, namely [Alcobendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcobendas), [San Sebastián de los Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_de_los_Reyes), [Tres Cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tres_Cantos) and [Colmenar Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colmenar_Viejo). Located on the main site are the Rectorate building and the Faculties of Science, Philosophy and Fine Arts, Law, Economic Science and Business Studies, Psychology, Higher School of Computing Science and Engineering, and the Faculty of Teacher Training and Education. The Medical School is located outside the main site and beside the Hospital Universitario La Paz.[^cite_note-Universidad_Autónoma-92]



The *[Magerit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magerit)* supercomputer, located in the [Supercomputing and Visualization Center of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputing_and_Visualization_Center_of_Madrid), part of the Technical University of Madrid and located in Pozuelo de Alarcón

Other local universities, among many others, are the [Technical University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Madrid), as the result of merging the different Technical Schools of Engineering; the [Universidad de Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alcal%C3%A1), founded in 1499; the [Carlos III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Carlos_III_de_Madrid), whose philosophy is to create responsible free-thinking people with a sensitivity to social problems and an involvement in the concept of progress based on freedom, justice and tolerance and the [Universidad Pontificia Comillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comillas_Pontifical_University), involved in a number of academic exchange programmes, work practice schemes and international projects with over 200 Higher Education Institutions in Europe, South America, North America, and Asia.

Other universities in Madrid: [Rey Juan Carlos University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey_Juan_Carlos_University) (public), [Alfonso X El Sabio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_X_El_Sabio_University), [Universidad Antonio de Nebrija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Antonio_de_Nebrija?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Universidad Camilo José Cela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Camilo_Jos%C3%A9_Cela), [Universidad Francisco de Vitoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Francisco_de_Vitoria), [Universidad Europea de Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Europea_de_Madrid), and [Universidad San Pablo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_San_Pablo?action=edit\&redlink=1) (all of them private).

Madrid is also home to the [Escuela Superior de Música Reina Sofía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escuela_Superior_de_M%C3%BAsica_Reina_Sof%C3%ADa), the [Real Conservatorio Superior de Música de Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Conservatory), and many other private educational institutions.

## Transport

### Air

Main article: [Barajas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barajas_International_Airport)



Barajas Airport

Madrid is served by [Barajas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barajas_International_Airport). Barajas is the main [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub) of [Iberia Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberia_Airlines) and consequently serves as the main gateway to the Iberian peninsula from Europe, the Americas, and the rest of the world. Current passenger volumes range upwards of 52 million passengers per year, putting it in the [top 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airport) busiest airports in the world.[^cite_note-93] Given annual increases close to 10%, a new fourth terminal has been constructed. It has significantly reduced delays and doubled the capacity of the airport to more than 70 million passengers per year. Two additional runways have also been constructed, making Barajas a fully operational four-runway airport.

### Commuter rail

Main article: [Cercanías Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas_Madrid)



Commuter rail station in [Parla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parla)

Cercanías Madrid is the [commuter rail](6286.md) service that serves [Madrid](41188263.md), the capital of Spain, and its metropolitan area. It is operated by [Cercanías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas), the commuter rail division of [Renfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe), the former monopoly of rail services in Spain. The system is infamous for being the target of [11 March 2004 Madrid train bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_March_2004_Madrid_train_bombings). The attacks triggered a small reduction in the ridership of the system, but it is still the most used and most profitable[^cite_note-94] (by 2004) of the commuter rail services in Spain. The total length spans 339.1 km (210.7 mi).

Spain's railway system, the *Red Nacional de Ferrocarriles Españoles* ([Renfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe)), operates the vast majority of Spain's railways. In Madrid, the main rail terminals are [Atocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Atocha_railway_station) in the south and [Chamartín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamart%C3%ADn_\(Madrid\)) in the north.

### High-speed rail



AVE trains in Atocha

Main article: [AVE](523382.md)

The crown jewel of Spain's next decade of infrastructure construction is the Spanish high-speed rail network, Alta Velocidad Española [AVE](523382.md). Currently, an ambitious plan includes the construction of a 7,000-kilometre (4,300 mi) network, centered on Madrid. The overall goal is to have all important provincial cities be no more than four hours away from Madrid and no more than six hours away from [Barcelona](4443.md). As of 2008, [AVE](523382.md) [high-speed trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_trains) link Madrid-Atocha station to [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville), [Málaga](184763.md), and [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Spain) in the south, Valencia, Albacete, Cuenca and Alicante in the east, and to [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza), [Tarragona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarragona), Girona, Leida, Huesca and [Barcelona](4443.md) in the north-east. [AVE](523382.md) trains also arrive to [Segovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segovia), [Valladolid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valladolid), Zamora and León.

Already connected by tunnels used by conventional rail lines, a tunnel link connecting the Atocha and Chamartín stations with high-speed rail services is finished but, as of August 2019, yet to be inaugurated.[^cite_note-95]

### Metro



Madrid's metro map

Main article: [Madrid Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Metro)

Serving the city's population of some six million, the [Madrid Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Metro) is one of the most extensive and fastest-growing [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) networks in the world.[^cite_note-Metro_de_Madrid-96] With the addition of a loop serving suburbs to Madrid's south-west "Metrosur", it is now the second largest metro system in [Western Europe](33800.md), second only to London's [Underground](17839.md). In 2007, Madrid's metro system was expanded, and it currently runs over 322 kilometers (200 mi) of line. The province of Madrid is also served by an extensive [commuter rail](6286.md) network called [Cercanías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas).

Metro fees are regulated by the [Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consorcio_Regional_de_Transportes_de_Madrid) (CRTM) jointly with fees for commuter rail, bus transport and light-rail.

### Religion



Catholic procession of the Virgen de Gracia at the [plaza de la Cebada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_la_Cebada?action=edit\&redlink=1), Madrid (c. 1741)

The majority of the religious population is [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic), [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) is, by far, the largest religion in Community of Madrid. According to a 2019 CIS poll, 18.9% of the surveyed people in the region identified as practising Catholic and 43.0% as non-practising Catholic.[^cite_note-CIS2019Madrid-103] The most important religious minorities are evangelicals, Jews and Muslims.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_GarcíaRamírez_FernándezHerrero_GalianoKirhlani200722-104]

Jehovah's Witnesses literature in Madrid

Since the second half of the 20th century the Jewish population in the region grew due to both [Sephardi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) that came from the [MENA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MENA), as well as exiles from [Latin America](18524.md) (mostly Argentinians) primordially [Ashkenazim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_GarcíaRamírez_FernándezHerrero_GalianoKirhlani200764-109]

There are also Greek, Romanian and Russian orthodox Christians, [Jehovah Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah_Witnesses) (15,031 according to 2001 estimations) and [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons) (6,700 according to 2007 estimations).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_GarcíaRamírez_FernándezHerrero_GalianoKirhlani200722;_72;_75-110] There are some buddhists (the majority of which have Spanish citizenship and are from the middle to upper middle class),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_GarcíaRamírez_FernándezHerrero_GalianoKirhlani200782-111] and small minorities of believers of religions of vedic origin: [hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) (primordially [Sindhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_GarcíaRamírez_FernándezHerrero_GalianoKirhlani200776-112] [sikhism](27964.md), [Hare Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Krishna_Consciousness) and [Brahma Kumaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Kumaris). There are a scarce amount of believers of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith). Other confessions, often derided as "cults" (*sectas*) in the country, such as the [Unification movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_movement) and [Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientology), have a marginal presence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_GarcíaRamírez_FernándezHerrero_GalianoKirhlani200786–91-113]

### Feasts



Official feasts of the 2 de Mayo

The regional day is the 2 May, commemorating the [Dos de Mayo Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dos_de_Mayo_Uprising) of the citizens of Madrid against the French occupation in 1808 that triggered the wave of insurrections marking the beginning of the [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War). It is a [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday) in the Community of Madrid since 1984, when it was approved by the regional legislature and sanctioned as law.[^cite_note-114]

A floral tribute is traditionally offered to the fallen "heroes" by the regional authorities. The ceremony of presentation of commemorative medals to stand out individuals also take place on this day in the [Royal House of the Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_House_of_the_Post_Office).[^cite_note-115]

## Sports

See also: [Sport in Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Madrid)



Practice of padel tennis in Madrid

According to a 2010 study by the [National Sports Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consejo_Superior_de_Deportes) (CSD), madrilenians led the country in terms of grassroots sports practice.[^cite_note-tm-116]

Roughly a 52% of the regional population between 15 and 75 years old practised one sports modality, while a 10% of the population between 15 and 75 years old practised two or more sports.[^cite_note-tm-116] The most practised sports modalities were: fitness gymnastics (43.6%), [football](10568.md) (22.1%), [swimming](27999.md) (20.7%), [cycling](5931.md) (19.6%), [jogging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogging)/running (16.2%), [padel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padel_\(sport\)) (9.9%), [athletics](43719.md) (8.3%), [basketball](3921.md) (6.9%), other football modalities (6.6%), [hiking](147313.md) (6.1%), [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_arts) (4.5%), [body-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body-building) (3.5%), [shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_sports)/[hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting) (0.9%), and [recreational fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_fishing) (0.2%).[^cite_note-tm-116]

Association football is the most popular sport in Spain in terms of passive following. The [Madrid Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Football_Federation) is the governing body of the sport of football in the region. The Community of Madrid has its own autonomous team, the [Madrid autonomous football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_autonomous_football_team), taking part in [friendly fixtures](977090.md). It currently has 2 top flight men's football teams: [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid) and [Atlético Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid). The first of them, Real Madrid, has become one of the most valuable sports teams in the planet.[^cite_note-117]

The regional administration had its own big track and field stadium, "[La Peineta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitano_Stadium)", inaugurated in 1994. It was later transferred to the Madrid City Council, becoming the center of two unsuccessful bids of the city of Madrid to the Summer Olympics.
