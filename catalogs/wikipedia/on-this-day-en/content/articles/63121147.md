**Carlos Alcaraz Garfia**[^cite_note-6] (born 5 May 2003) is a Spanish professional [tennis](29773.md) player. He has been ranked [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players) in men's singles by the [Association of Tennis Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals) (ATP) for 66 weeks, and finished as the [year-end No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players#Year-end_No._1_players) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_ATP_Tour) and [2025](75429008.md). Alcaraz has won 26 [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour)–level singles titles, including seven [majors](197638.md#Tournaments) and eight [ATP Masters 1000](30872553.md) titles. He is the ninth and youngest man in history to complete the [career Grand Slam](2092499.md#Career_Grand_Slam) in singles.[^cite_note-guinness-7]

A teen phenomenon, Alcaraz won the [Next Gen ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Next_Gen_ATP_Finals) and was the 2020 [ATP Newcomer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Newcomer_of_the_Year). In 2022, Alcaraz won his first major title at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). He became the youngest man in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era) to be ranked world No. 1, at 19 years, 130 days old, and finished the year as the youngest year-end No. 1 in [ATP rankings](13480983.md) history. Alcaraz claimed the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) in 2023, defeating seven-time champion [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) in [an epic final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_final).

In 2024, Alcaraz won his first [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) and defended his title at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), becoming the youngest man to win the "[Channel Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Slam)". In 2025, he defeated [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in [a historic final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_final) to win the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), as well as the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and finished as the year-end world No. 1 for a second time. In 2026, Alcaraz won his seventh major at the [Australian Open](81715568.md), and became the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam, at 22 years, 272 days old.[^cite_note-8] He is known for his aggressive playing style and varied shotmaking.

## Early and personal life

Carlos Alcaraz Garfia was born on 5 May 2003, in [El Palmar, Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Palmar,_Murcia), Spain, to parents Carlos Alcaraz González and Virginia Garfia Escandón. Alcaraz has one older brother, Álvaro, and two younger brothers, Sergio and Jaime.[^cite_note-\:4-9]

Alcaraz started playing tennis at the age of four at the Real Sociedad Club de Campo de Murcia, where his father was a tennis coach and club administrator.[^cite_note-10] His mother worked as a sales assistant at [IKEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA).[^cite_note-11] Alcaraz's father had played tennis but stopped pursuing a professional career as a teenager, as he could not afford it.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] In 2013, at age 10, Alcaraz signed his first contract with [Babolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babolat).[^cite_note-14] After watching him compete at age 11, [IMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMG_\(company\)) agent Albert Molina recognized Carlos as a standout talent and sought to persuade his parents to work with him; his father ultimately agreed when Carlos was 12.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

Alcaraz is frequently accompanied to tournaments by his father and by his brother Álvaro, who works as his [hitting partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitting_partner) and assistant coach. During the tennis off-season, he lives at his parents' home in [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia).[^cite_note-18] His friends and family call him "Carlitos" or "Charly".[^cite_note-19] He is [Catholic](606848.md), and has received blessings from priests before important tournaments.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Junior career

At the age of 10, Alcaraz competed outside Spain for the first time at the under-10 World Championship in Croatia,[^cite_note-22] where he lost in the final.[^cite_note-23] That same year, he became an official member of the [Babolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babolat) team, later joining the brand's international roster at age 13.[^cite_note-24]

In 2015, Alcaraz won the under-12 division of the Rafa Nadal Tour Masters, and the following year captured the competition's under-14 title.[^cite_note-25]

In 2017, Alcaraz enjoyed a breakthrough season at the under-14 level. He won the XIV Taça Internacional Maia Jovem, defeating [Daniel Rincón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Rinc%C3%B3n_\(tennis\)) in the final,[^cite_note-26] and later captured the Babolat Cup.[^cite_note-27] That summer, he helped Spain win the 14-and-under European Summer Cup[^cite_note-28] and was part of the Spanish team that finished runner-up to Switzerland at the [ITF World Junior Tennis Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_World_Junior_Tennis_Finals).[^cite_note-29]

Alcaraz continued his rise in 2018. In July, he won the Dutch Junior Open, defeating Filip Kolasinski in the final,[^cite_note-30] and weeks later captured the [European 16-and-under Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_European_Junior_Championships#European_Junior_Championships_16_&_Under) by defeating [Elmer Møller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_M%C3%B8ller).[^cite_note-31] Later that year at the [Junior Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Davis_Cup_and_Junior_Billie_Jean_King_Cup) in Budapest, he played a key role in Spain's title run, saving match point while trailing against [Harold Mayot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Mayot) in singles before returning to win the decisive doubles match after France had taken an early lead.[^cite_note-ChildhoodCoach-32][^cite_note-33]

In March 2019, Alcaraz won the J300 Villena, defeating [Illya Beloborodko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illya_Beloborodko) in the final,[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] and was ranked No. 1 on the [Tennis Europe Junior Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Europe_Junior_Tour) during the year.[^cite_note-36] As a junior, he achieved a career-high world ranking of No. 22.[^cite_note-37]

## Professional career

### 2018–2020: Pro debut

In February 2018, aged 14, Alcaraz played his first professional tennis event as a qualifier at the Spain F5 [ITF Futures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Men's_World_Tennis_Tour) in Murcia. He knocked out second seed and world No. 292 [Federico Gaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Gaio) in the first round, and ultimately reached the quarterfinals. He collected his first two ATP points from this tournament, and entered the rankings as world No. 1414 on 26 February 2018.[^cite_note-38]

In April 2019, aged 15, Alcaraz made his [Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Challenger_Tour) debut at the [JC Ferrero Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JC_Ferrero_Challenger_Open) in Villena. He won his first round match against a 17-year old [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md), who was on a sixteen-match winning streak.[^cite_note-39] With this victory, Alcaraz became the first player born in 2003 to win a Challenger match.[^cite_note-40] He was defeated by eighth seed [Lukáš Rosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luk%C3%A1%C5%A1_Rosol) in the second round. One week later at the [Murcia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Murcia_Open), he defeated fifth seed and world No. 140 [Pedro Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Mart%C3%ADnez_\(tennis\)) in the second round.[^cite_note-41] Alcaraz went on to win four Challenger titles, three of them before the age of 18. He was the first player born in 2003 to reach a Challenger title match.[^cite_note-42]

In February 2020, aged 16, Alcaraz made his ATP main-draw debut at the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Rio_Open) after receiving a wild card for the singles main draw.[^cite_note-43] He defeated world No. 41 [Albert Ramos Viñolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ramos_Vi%C3%B1olas) in a marathon three-setter that lasted three hours and 37 minutes.[^cite_note-44] Alcaraz became the first player born in 2003 to win an ATP Tour match, and also achieved this feat before anyone born in 2002.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] He was defeated in the second round by [Federico Coria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Coria).

In September 2020, aged 17, Alcaraz played his first qualifying round at a major tournament at the postponed [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_qualifying). He held two match points to defeat [Aleksandar Vukic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Vukic) in straight sets, but ended up losing in three.[^cite_note-47]

### 2021: First ATP title, Next Gen champion

Alcaraz made his debut in the [main draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_draw_\(tennis\)) of a major as a [qualifier](2086074.md) at the [2021 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open). He was the youngest man to qualify for the tournament since [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) in 2005.[^cite_note-\:023-48] As the main draw was postponed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Alcaraz played the [Great Ocean Road Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Great_Ocean_Road_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) in early February, where he was defeated by [Thiago Monteiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Monteiro_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-49] In his first main draw match at the Australian Open, Alcaraz defeated fellow qualifier [Botic van de Zandschulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botic_van_de_Zandschulp) in straight sets. He was the only teenager in the draw to progress past the first round, and the first person born in 2003 to win a match at a major.[^cite_note-\:023-48] In the second round, Alcaraz was defeated by [Mikael Ymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Ymer).



Alcaraz at the [2021 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open)

Alcaraz played a mix of Challenger and ATP Tour-level tournaments throughout the spring, including the [Gran Canaria Challenger II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Gran_Canaria_Challenger_II_%E2%80%93_Singles) and the [Open Occitanie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Open_Sud_de_France_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] He received [wildcards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_card_\(tennis\)) at the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles), where he lost to eventual champion [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) in the first round;[^cite_note-52] at the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles), where he lost to [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe) in the first round;[^cite_note-53] and at the [Andalucía Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Andaluc%C3%ADa_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where he defeated [Nikola Milojevic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Milojevi%C4%87_\(tennis\)), [Feliciano Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feliciano_L%C3%B3pez), and third seed [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud) to make the deepest ATP tournament run of his career. He lost to compatriot [Jaume Munar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaume_Munar) in the semifinal.[^cite_note-54]

Alcaraz received a wildcard at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where he lost in the first round to [Emil Ruusuvuori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Ruusuvuori). This was his first appearance at a ATP Masters 1000 tournament.[^cite_note-55] He was given another ATP 1000 wildcard at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). Alcaraz became the youngest match winner in tournament history after his first round defeat of [Adrian Mannarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Mannarino), breaking a record set by [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) in 2004. He then met Nadal for their first career meeting, and lost to him in two short sets on the day of his eighteenth birthday.[^cite_note-56] Alcaraz entered the [Challenger Oeiras III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Open_de_Oeiras_III_%E2%80%93_Singles) as an unseeded player, and defeated [Facundo Bagnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facundo_Bagnis) in the final to claim the fourth Challenger title of his career.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] On 24 May 2021, he rose from 114th to 94th in the ATP rankings. He became the youngest player inside the top 100, as well as the youngest inside the top 500.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-\:133-60]

Alcaraz easily qualified for the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) and reached the third round, where he was defeated by [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff). He was the youngest man to reach the third round at Roland Garros in 29 years, and the youngest to do so at any major since Nadal in 2004.[^cite_note-61] Alcaraz then made his debut at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) via a wildcard. In the first round, he defeated [Yasutaka Uchiyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasutaka_Uchiyama) in the first five-set match of his career.[^cite_note-\:133-60] He was defeated in the second round in straight sets by world No. 2 [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev).[^cite_note-62] In July, Alcaraz won the [Umag Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Croatia_Open_Umag_%E2%80%93_Singles), defeating [Richard Gasquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gasquet) to claim the first ATP title of his career.[^cite_note-63] He was the youngest ATP champion since [Kei Nishikori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_Nishikori) in 2008.[^cite_note-64]

In the third round of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz defeated world No. 3 [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas) in a dramatic fifth-set tiebreak on [Arthur Ashe Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe_Stadium).[^cite_note-65] At 18 years and four months, Alcaraz became the youngest man to beat a top 3 ranked-player in singles at the US Open since the [ATP rankings](13480983.md) began in 1973. He also became the youngest man in a major fourth round since 17-year-old [Andrei Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Medvedev_\(tennis\)) at the [1992 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and the youngest man in the US Open fourth round since 17-year-old [Michael Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chang) in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Alcaraz then reached the quarterfinals by defeating qualifier [Peter Gojowczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gojowczyk). He became the youngest US Open men's quarterfinalist in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era), the youngest all-time since [Thomaz Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomaz_Koch) in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_U.S._National_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and the youngest at any major since Michael Chang at the [1990 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). He retired against [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime) in the quarterfinals after injuring his leg.[^cite_note-68]

At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz was seeded (30th) for the first time at an ATP Masters 1000 event, but lost in the first round to [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray).[^cite_note-69] At the [Vienna Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Erste_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) two weeks later, Alcaraz met Murray for a rematch and defeated him.[^cite_note-70] He then defeated world No. 7 [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini) to claim a second career top 10 win, and entered the top 35 on 1 November.[^cite_note-71] He lost to [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) in the semifinal.[^cite_note-72] At the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles), Alcaraz took a third top 10 win against eighth seed [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in their first ATP meeting.[^cite_note-73] However, in the round of 16, Alcaraz was unable to cope with a raucous home crowd backing Frenchman [Hugo Gaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Gaston) and lost in straight sets.[^cite_note-74] Alcaraz ended his season at the [Next Gen ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Next_Generation_ATP_Finals); he had qualified as third seed behind Sinner and Auger-Aliassime, but became first seed after their withdrawals. He dominated the draw to win the title, dropping just one set.[^cite_note-75]

### 2022: US Open champion, world No. 1

Main article: [2022 Carlos Alcaraz tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Carlos_Alcaraz_tennis_season)

Alcaraz was seeded for the first time in a major at the [Australian Open](69399815.md). He reached the third round, where he was defeated in a fifth set tiebreak by [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini).[^cite_note-76] This would later prove to be an unusual blemish on Alcaraz's standout five-set winning record.[^cite_note-77] At the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Rio_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), Alcaraz won his first [ATP 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_500_tournaments) title by defeating [Diego Schwartzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Schwartzman) in the final.[^cite_note-78] He became the youngest winner of an ATP 500 event since the category was created in 2009.[^cite_note-79] He entered the ATP top 20 for the first time on 21 February 2022.&#x20;

At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz defeated defending champion [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie) to reach his first ATP Masters 1000 quarterfinal and semifinal, which he lost to [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal).[^cite_note-80] Two weeks later, at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz defeated [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud) to win his first 1000 title. He was the youngest men's singles winner in tournament history, as well as the youngest men's ATP Masters 1000 champion since Rafael Nadal at [Monte-Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Monte_Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles) in 2005.[^cite_note-81] He was also the first Spaniard to win Miami. At [Monte-Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles), Alcaraz was upset by [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda).[^cite_note-82] Alcaraz beat top seed [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas) in the quarterfinals of the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles) to enter the world top 10 for the first time on 25 April 2022.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] He became the youngest man to break into the top 10 since Nadal on 25 April 2005, exactly 17 years earlier.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Alcaraz defeated [Pablo Carreño Busta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Carre%C3%B1o_Busta) to win the Barcelona title.[^cite_note-87]

The day after his 19th birthday, Alcaraz defeated five-time champion, world No. 4 and third seed [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) in the quarterfinals of the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-88] This was Alcaraz's first victory against Nadal, and their final Tour-level meeting. The following day, Alcaraz faced world No. 1 [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) for their first meeting. After three hours and thirty-six minutes, Alcaraz prevailed in a tight third set tiebreak. He became the youngest male player to win a match against a world No. 1 since Nadal in 2004, and the only player ever to defeat Nadal and Djokovic back-to-back on clay.[^cite_note-89] In the final, Alcaraz dismantled defending champion and world No. 3 [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) in an hour and four minutes.[^cite_note-BBCMadrid2022-90] He became the youngest champion in the tournament's history at 19 years, 3 days.[^cite_note-91] On 9 May 2022, Alcaraz climbed to a career-high ranking of world No. 6.[^cite_note-92]

Alcaraz was widely projected to be one of the favorites for the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) title.[^cite_note-93] In his second round match against [Albert Ramos Viñolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ramos_Vi%C3%B1olas), he was taken to five sets and forced to save match point.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] He lost to [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) in the quarterfinal.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-RG2022QF2-98] At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz lost to [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in the fourth round.[^cite_note-99] At the [Hamburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Hamburg_European_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz recorded his first-ever defeat in an ATP final against [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti).[^cite_note-100] Nevertheless, this result took him to a new career-high ranking of world No. 5 on July 25; he became the youngest male player to enter the top 5 since Nadal in 2005.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] Alcaraz reached and lost a second consecutive final at the [Croatia Open Umag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Croatia_Open_Umag_%E2%80%93_Singles), in another defeat by Jannik Sinner. He became world No. 4 on 1 August.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]



Alcaraz at the [2022 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships)

&#x20;Alcaraz's North American swing did not start well: he was upset by [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_\(tennis\)) in his debut at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles),[^cite_note-105] and [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie) in the quarterfinals of the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-106] Prior to the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_\(tennis\)), Alcaraz, [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud), [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev), and [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas) were all in contention to take over the [world No. 1 ranking](13480983.md). In the fourth round, Alcaraz defeated former champion [Marin Čilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87) in five sets, and became the youngest man to reach back-to-back US Open quarterfinals in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era).[^cite_note-107] In the quarterfinals, Alcaraz saved a match point in the fourth set before recovering to win in five sets against [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md). The match lasted five hours and fifteen minutes, and recorded the latest finish in the history of the tournament at 2\:50AM EST.[^cite_note-108] Alcaraz played a third consecutive five-setter to defeat [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe).[^cite_note-109]



Carlos Alcaraz, Swiss Indoors Basel 2022.

Alcaraz then met fifth seed [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud) for his first major final, a match which doubled as a shoot-out for the world No. 1 ranking. Alcaraz won the match in four sets to claim his first major title. He became the youngest No. 1 in the history of the [ATP Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Rankings) at 19 years, 130 days, breaking [Lleyton Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt)'s record, and the [second youngest all-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-time_tennis_records_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) behind [Lew Hoad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Hoad).[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-ATPWorldNo1-111][^cite_note-\:0-112] He also became the youngest men's major champion since Nadal at the [2005 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), the youngest [US Open](82729.md) champion since [Pete Sampras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sampras) in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and the first man born in the 2000s to win a major singles title.

Alcaraz's season ended more flatly. He lost his first singles match as world No. 1 to [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime) at the [2022 Davis Cup Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Davis_Cup_Finals).[^cite_note-113] He lost to [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin) in the first round of the [Astana Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Astana_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), and lost again to Auger-Aliassime in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Swiss_Indoors_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-114] Alcaraz reached the quarterfinals of the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles), but retired down a set to [Holger Rune](60992715.md).[^cite_note-115] A day later, he announced he had suffered an abdominal tear requiring a six-week layoff, forcing him to end his season early.[^cite_note-116] At 19 years and 214 days, Alcaraz ended the year as youngest and first teenage world No. 1 in the ATP era, and [second youngest of all-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-time_tennis_records_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) behind Hoad.[^cite_note-\:0-112][^cite_note-auto2-117]

### 2023: Wimbledon champion

Main article: [2023 Carlos Alcaraz tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Carlos_Alcaraz_tennis_season)



Alcaraz at the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open)

On January 7, Alcaraz announced his withdrawal from the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open) due to a right hamstring injury sustained while training.[^cite_note-118] He had held the world No. 1 ranking for 20 weeks, but lost it after the tournament to champion [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md).[^cite_note-119] Alcaraz began his season at the [Argentina Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Argentina_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where he was playing for the first time. He defeated [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie) in the final in straight sets to claim his first title since the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-120] The next week, Alcaraz reached the final of the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rio_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where he was defending champion, also against Norrie; however, he aggravated his leg injury during the match and lost in three sets.[^cite_note-121] Alcaraz withdrew from the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Open_\(tennis\)), citing the same injury.[^cite_note-122]

At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open), Alcaraz recorded his 100th career win against 31st seed [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor) in the third round, making him the second fastest player to reach this milestone after [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe).[^cite_note-123] He defeated [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in the semifinal and [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev) in the final to lift the trophy.[^cite_note-124] He became the first male player to win Indian Wells without dropping a set since [Roger Federer](262376.md) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and the youngest man to have won both legs of the [Sunshine Double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Double). As a result, he regained the world No. 1 ranking on 20 March 2023.[^cite_note-125] Alcaraz hoped to complete the Sunshine Double in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where he was the defending champion. He reached the semifinals without dropping a set, where he lost to Jannik Sinner in three sets.[^cite_note-126] With the loss of his title points, Alcaraz dropped back to No. 2 in the world rankings.[^cite_note-127]

Alcaraz was scheduled to play at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Monte-Carlo_Masters) the following week, but withdrew due to post-traumatic arthritis in his left hand and muscular discomfort in his spine.[^cite_note-128] He defeated [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas) in the final of the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles) to defend his title without dropping a set.[^cite_note-129] He also defended his title at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), defeating [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff) to claim his tenth career title and cement his dominance on Spanish clay.[^cite_note-130] At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz recovered the No. 1 ranking from Djokovic just by playing his second round match. However, he was upset in the third round by world No. 135 [Fabian Marozsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Marozsan) in straight sets, in what [Jon Wertheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Wertheim) labelled the biggest upset of the year.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

Alcaraz only dropped one set before the semifinals of [Roland-Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where he met [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) for their second career meeting. This was a highly anticipated showdown, as the two men had not played a match in over a year despite their ongoing fight over the world No. 1 ranking.[^cite_note-SiDjokovicSemi-133][^cite_note-GuardianDjokovicSemi-134] The match was competitive until Alcaraz faltered at the start of the third set due to cramps from mental pressure and physical intensity; Djokovic won in four sets, going on to win the tournament and regain the No. 1 ranking.[^cite_note-CrampVsDjokovic-135]



Alcaraz holding the Gentlemen's Singles Challenge Cup after winning the [2023 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships)

&#x20;At the [Queen's Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Queen's_Club_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles), Alcaraz claimed his first title on grass in just his third Tour-level grass tournament, beating [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur) in the final. This victory also restored him as world No. 1.[^cite_note-136] Despite relatively muted expectations for Alcaraz at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), he only dropped two sets en route to the final, where he faced off against seven-time champion and four-time defending champion Djokovic. Alcaraz was [breadsticked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadstick) in the first set but held his nerve to win the match in five sets. It lasted four hours and forty-two minutes, and was instantly acclaimed as a modern epic.[^cite_note-NYTimesWimbledon-137] This was his first Wimbledon title, and his second major title overall.[^cite_note-138] Alcaraz also became the only man outside the [Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(tennis\)#Big_Four_era) to claim the Wimbledon singles title since 2002, before he himself had been born.[^cite_note-auto12-139]

Alcaraz lost in the quarterfinals of [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) to [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-140] He then met Djokovic again for the [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) final. Alcaraz lost in three sets, despite having a championship point in the second-set tiebreak. It was the longest ever best-of-three-sets ATP Masters 1000 final and the longest match in the tournament's history, at 3 hours and 49 minutes; Djokovic praised it as "one of the toughest matches of \[his] life".[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] Alcaraz entered the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) as defending champion, and reached the semifinals for the loss of just one set. However, he lost there to [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) champion [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev) in four sets.[^cite_note-143] Following the tournament, Alcaraz lost the No. 1 ranking once again to eventual champion Djokovic, who had not been defending any points.[^cite_note-144]

Alcaraz entered the autumn-winter hardcourt season with the goal of seizing the year-end No. 1 ranking.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] However, just like 2022, he experienced an end-of-year slump as the tour moved back to fast [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt). He lost to [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in the semifinals of [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and then to [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov) in the round of 16 at [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rolex_Shanghai_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] He withdrew from [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Swiss_Indoors) due to injury,[^cite_note-149] and lost in his opening round at the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles) to [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin).[^cite_note-150] At the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), Alcaraz lost to [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) but defeated [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev) and [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev) in the round robin stage. He qualified for the semifinals, where he lost decisively to eventual champion Djokovic.[^cite_note-151] Ultimately, Alcaraz ended the year ranked No. 2.[^cite_note-152]

### 2024: Channel Slam, Olympic silver

Main article: [2024 Carlos Alcaraz tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Carlos_Alcaraz_tennis_season)



Alcaraz holding the [Coupe des Mousquetaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_des_Mousquetaires) following the [men's singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) final at the [2024 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open)

Alcaraz began his 2024 campaign at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). He produced a dominant performance against [Miomir Kecmanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miomir_Kecmanovi%C4%87) to reach the quarterfinals for the first time in his career;[^cite_note-153] however, he lost there to [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) in four sets.[^cite_note-154] In February, Alcaraz chose to play on South American clay courts for the third year in a row. He entered the [Argentina Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Argentina_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) as defending champion, but lost to [Nicolás Jarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Jarry) in the semifinal.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] The following week, he retired from the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rio_Open) two games into his first match after twisting his right ankle.[^cite_note-RioOpenAnkle2-157] He announced that he had suffered a lateral sprain, but that he would miss just "a few days" of training before [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-RioOpenAnkle2-157]

As defending champion on some of his favorite courts in Indian Wells, Alcaraz came back into form.[^cite_note-158] In the semifinals, he defeated [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) to end his 19-match winning streak.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-atptour.com2-160] He then defeated [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev) in the final, earning him his first title in eight months.[^cite_note-161] In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz reached the quarterfinals without dropping a set but lost there to [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov).[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] Following the tournament, he dropped to world No. 3 behind [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) and Miami champion Sinner.



Alcaraz receiving his silver medal following the [men's singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) final at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics)

Alcaraz's clay season was disrupted by injury. He withdrew from the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Monte-Carlo_Masters) and the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell) due to a problem with his right forearm.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] In [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Open_\(tennis\)), Alcaraz continued to struggle with the injury, which hampered his ability to hit his signature forehand at full power. He lost in the quarterfinals to eventual champion [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev),[^cite_note-\:14-166] and withdrew from [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-\:14-166] However, Alcaraz recovered in time to play the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) in June. Despite some shaky early matches he reached the semi-final, where he defeated second seed [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in five gruelling sets to advance to his first Roland Garros final. Alcaraz defeated [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) in another five-setter to claim his first Roland Garros title. At 21 years and 35 days, he became the youngest male player in history to win a major title on three different surfaces.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

In July, Alcaraz defended his [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships) title by defeating [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) in straight sets in a rematch of their [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships) final.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] At 21 years and 70 days, Alcaraz became the youngest male player in the Open Era to complete the [Channel Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Slam) (winning the French Open and Wimbledon in the same season).[^cite_note-171] Alcaraz then entered the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) in both doubles and singles. Partnering Rafael Nadal, he reached the quarterfinals in doubles. In singles, Alcaraz reached the final without dropping a set. He was the heavy favorite for the gold medal; however, he lost to Djokovic in two tightly contested sets during which neither man dropped serve.[^cite_note-172] He became the youngest-ever silver medalist in men's singles.

Alcaraz skipped the [2024 Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), citing fatigue. He played at the [2024 Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), but was upset by [Gaël Monfils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils) in the opening round.[^cite_note-173] This match marked the first time Alcaraz broke a racket on court, which generated significant media coverage and drove him to deliver a public apology.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-RacquetSmash-176] He next played the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where he suffered a shocking second-round defeat in straight sets to [Botic van de Zandschulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botic_van_de_Zandschulp). This was his earliest loss in a major since Wimbledon 2021.[^cite_note-177]

Alcaraz recovered his morale with successes at the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Davis_Cup) and [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Laver_Cup) team events. Afterwards, he defeated [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in the final of the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). This was Alcaraz's third encounter with Sinner in 2024, and his third victory.[^cite_note-178] With this win, Alcaraz reclaimed the world No. 2 ranking from [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md).[^cite_note-179] He also became the first player in ATP Tour history to win an [ATP 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_500) singles title on every surface – [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court), [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_court) and [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt).[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-ChinaOpenSinner-181] However, he delivered an otherwise shaky autumn for the third year in a row. He lost in the quarterfinals of the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Shanghai_Masters_\(tennis\)) to [Tomáš Macháč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Mach%C3%A1%C4%8D), and in the round of 16 at the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rolex_Paris_Masters) to [Ugo Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Humbert).[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] He was unwell during the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_ATP_Finals) but did not withdraw, beating [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev) but losing to [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud) and Alexander Zverev in straight sets to exit the tournament at the round robin stage.[^cite_note-184] Alcaraz reunited with the [Spanish Davis Cup team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_Davis_Cup_team) in [Málaga](184763.md) for the quarterfinals. They were knocked out by the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Davis_Cup_team), marking the end of [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal)'s career.[^cite_note-185] Alcaraz became the first man to finish a season ranked as low as world No. 3 after winning two majors.

### 2025: French and US Open titles, return to No. 1

Main article: [2025 Carlos Alcaraz tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Carlos_Alcaraz_tennis_season)

Alcaraz began his 2025 season at the [2025 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where he was vying to become the youngest man to complete a [Career Grand Slam](2092499.md#Career_Grand_Slam).[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] However, he was defeated by [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) in the quarterfinals, in what was widely analysed as a nervous match from his side of the net.[^cite_note-BBCDjokovicAO2025-188][^cite_note-189] Alcaraz opted to play on European [hardcourts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt) in February for the first time in his career. He won the first indoor hardcourt title of his career at the [Rotterdam Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ABN_AMRO_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where he defeated [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur) in the final.[^cite_note-190] At the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Qatar_ExxonMobil_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), Alcaraz lost in the quarterfinals to [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka), despite breaking Lehečka's serve first in every set.[^cite_note-191] As the two-time defending champion at [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open), Alcaraz lost to [Jack Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Draper) in the semifinals despite [bagelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel_\(tennis\)) him in the second set. This was Alcaraz's first loss beyond the quarterfinal stage of a tournament in over a year.[^cite_note-192] In dire form, Alcaraz then lost his first match in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Open) to world No. 55 [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin), marking just his third career opening-round defeat at an ATP 1000 tournament.[^cite_note-193]



Alcaraz serving at the [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open)

Alcaraz's clay season was much more successful. He began it at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles), a tournament where he had previously never won a match.[^cite_note-194] Still initially in shaky form, Alcaraz fought through a tight three-setter with [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils) in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-195] In the final, he defeated [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti) to claim his first ATP Masters 1000 title in thirteen months. Alcaraz comfortably reached the final of the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles), where he lost to [Holger Rune](60992715.md). Following the final, he announced his withdrawal from the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), with injuries to both legs.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] Alcaraz defeated world No. 1 [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) to claim his first [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) title. He became the third man to win every modern big title on clay (French Open, Monte-Carlo, Madrid, and Rome) after [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) and [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md).[^cite_note-198]

Alcaraz met Sinner for a second consecutive big [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_final) at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). After winning the first two sets, Sinner had three [championship points](2086074.md#Match_point) on Alcaraz's serve in the fourth set. Alcaraz held from 0–40 and then broke Sinner's own serve to win the set. This forced a fifth set, which Alcaraz won with a dominant [super tiebreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_tiebreak), thereby claiming his fifth major title. At 5 hours 29 minutes, it was the longest French Open final ever played, and the second longest major final of all time.[^cite_note-199] It was also the first time that Alcaraz had rallied from two sets to love down,[^cite_note-ATPRG2025-200] and he became the ninth male player to achieve this feat to win a major singles final.[^cite_note-ATPRG2025-200]. Several writers and sports analysts declared it one of the greatest matches of all time.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]

Alcaraz began his grass season at the [Queen's Club Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Queen's_Club_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). His semifinal win against [Roberto Bautista Agut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Bautista_Agut) was his 250th ATP victory; he became the third-fastest man to achieve this milestone in the Open Era.[^cite_note-206] Alcaraz defeated [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka) in the final to claim his third consecutive title, and fifth title of the season.[^cite_note-207] In the first round of [Wimbledon](80007709.md), Alcaraz was unexpectedly taken to five sets by [Fabio Fognini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Fognini), who had already announced his retirement and had not won a match all season.[^cite_note-208] Alcaraz dropped a set each to [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff), [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev), and [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz), but ultimately still reached his sixth consecutive final. He lost to [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) in four sets, his first loss in a slam final and his first loss to Sinner since 2023. This also ended Alcaraz's 24-match win streak, the longest of his career.[^cite_note-209]



Alcaraz in the final of the [2025 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_\(tennis\))

Alcaraz and Sinner faced off in yet another big final at the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). Sinner struggled physically, and retired while Alcaraz was leading 5–0 in the first set. Alcaraz therefore claimed his third ATP 1000 title of the season.[^cite_note-ATPCincinnati2025-210] At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), Alcaraz swept through the first six rounds of competition, [bagelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel_\(tennis\)) both [Mattia Bellucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattia_Bellucci) and [Luciano Darderi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Darderi) on the way.[^cite_note-211] In the semifinal, he recorded his first win over [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) on [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt).[^cite_note-NYTUSOpen2025-212] Alcaraz reached the final without dropping a single set, the first man to do so at the US Open since [Roger Federer](262376.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-213] This was his eighth consecutive tour-level final, and for a third consecutive slam final, he faced [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md). Alcaraz defeated him in four sets to claim his sixth major in one of the most dominant runs of all time.[^cite_note-214] He became the fourth and youngest man in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era) to win multiple slam titles on each surface.[^cite_note-215] With this victory, Alcaraz also regained the [world No. 1 ranking](13480983.md) for the first time in two years.[^cite_note-ATPNo1Return2025-216]

Alcaraz then made his debut at the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Japan_Open_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles). In the first set of his first round match against [Sebastián Báez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_B%C3%A1ez), he twisted his ankle.[^cite_note-217] Despite the injury, he kept playing to reach his ninth consecutive final, where he faced [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz). Having lost to Fritz at the [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Laver_Cup) just 10 days prior, Alcaraz swept past him to secure his eighth title of the year.[^cite_note-218] Alcaraz then announced his withdrawal from the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rolex_Shanghai_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles) due to injury.[^cite_note-219] He lost his first match at the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles) to [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie) in three sets, ending his streak of consecutive finals.[^cite_note-220] At the [ATP Finals](81434533.md), Alcaraz defeated [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur), Taylor Fritz, and [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti) to top his round robin group. With the win against Musetti, he clinched the year-end No. 1 ranking for the second time. Alcaraz defeated [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime) in the semifinal, making only three unforced errors in the first set. He progressed to yet another final against [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md), which he lost in straight sets.[^cite_note-bbc.co.uk-221] Alcaraz then announced his withdrawal from the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Davis_Cup_Finals) due to a hamstring injury, ending his season.[^cite_note-222]

## Rivalries

### Jannik Sinner

Main article: [Alcaraz–Sinner rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcaraz%E2%80%93Sinner_rivalry)

Carlos Alcaraz and [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) have faced each other 17 times on the ATP Tour, with Alcaraz leading 10–7.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]

Notable early matches include the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) quarterfinal, which Alcaraz won en route to the title in five sets after [saving a match point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Grand_Slam_and_related_tennis_records#Won_a_title_after_saving_match_points).[^cite_note-248] The pair met three times in 2024, with Alcaraz winning all three matches, including the tightly contested final of the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-ChinaOpenSinner-181] Sinner lost as many matches to Alcaraz in 2024 as he did to all other players on tour combined.

In 2025, Alcaraz and Sinner met in three consecutive major finals. Alcaraz won their [first major final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_final) at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open), despite Sinner holding three [championship points](2086074.md#Match_point) in the fourth set. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* acclaimed it as "one of the greatest finals ever played, in any sport."[^cite_note-249] They met again at [Wimbledon](80007709.md), where Sinner won in four sets, dethroning defending champion Alcaraz; and at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where Alcaraz won in four sets, dethroning defending champion Sinner.[^cite_note-ATPNo1Return2025-216] For a second consecutive year, they split the season's slams at two-apiece.

They also contested two ATP Masters 1000 finals in 2025, at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) and the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cincinnati_Open), both won by Alcaraz;[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-ATPCincinnati2025-210] and the final of the [2025 ATP Finals](81434533.md), where Sinner defeated Alcaraz in straight sets.[^cite_note-bbc.co.uk-221] Sinner defeated Alcaraz in straight sets to claim the title at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles) in their first meeting in 2026. Their rivalry has been described as "potentially era-defining".[^cite_note-251]

### Alexander Zverev

Carlos Alcaraz and [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) have faced off thirteen times, with Alcaraz leading 7–6.[^cite_note-252]

They met twice in 2021, with Zverev dismantling a 17–18 year old Alcaraz in both matches. Their first notable match was the final of the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) in 2022, where Alcaraz swept past defending champion Zverev in one hour and four minutes.[^cite_note-BBCMadrid2022-90] A few weeks later they had their first major meeting in the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) quarterfinals, where Zverev defeated Alcaraz in four sets.[^cite_note-253] They had further high profile encounters in the quarterfinals of the [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), won by Alcaraz, and the quarterfinals of the [2024 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), won by Zverev.&#x20;

In 2024 they met for the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) final, where Zverev was hunting for his first major title. Zverev took a two-sets-to-one lead, but Alcaraz outlasted him to win in five sets and claim his first French Open title.[^cite_note-254]

In the semifinals of the [2026 Australian Open](81715568.md), Alcaraz took a two-set lead before struggling due to cramps. Zverev won the next two sets in a pair of tiebreaks, but Alcaraz recovered in time to win the fifth set, proceeding to his maiden Australian Open final. At 5 hours and 27 minutes, this was the longest Australian Open semifinal in history.[^cite_note-ATPZverevAO2026-225]

### Novak Djokovic



Carlos Alcaraz and Novak Djokovic after the 2023 Wimbledon final

Carlos Alcaraz and [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) have faced off ten times; despite a 16-year age gap, their rivalry has remained competitive, with the head-to-head tied at 5–5.[^cite_note-255] Nine of their ten matches have been in the semifinals or finals of "big" tournaments: at all four majors, two ATP Masters 1000, the ATP Finals and the Olympics.

Their first meeting was at the [2022 Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) semifinals, where Alcaraz prevailed in a deciding set tiebreaker, becoming the only man ever to secure back-to-back victories against Nadal and Djokovic on clay. Despite their ongoing fight over the world No. 1 ranking, they did not meet again for over a year. They finally faced off in the semifinals of the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), a highly anticipated showdown heavily promoted by the media.[^cite_note-SiDjokovicSemi-133][^cite_note-GuardianDjokovicSemi-134][^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257] Djokovic won in four sets, with the match competitive until Alcaraz faltered at the start of the third set due to cramps.[^cite_note-CrampVsDjokovic-135]

The duo soon met for a rematch in the [2023 Wimbledon final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_final), in which Alcaraz defeated four-time defending champion Djokovic in an epic five-setter that lasted 4 hours and 42 minutes.[^cite_note-NYTimesWimbledon-137] One month later, they fought another epic at the [2023 Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) final, with Djokovic prevailing in three tightly contested sets after saving a championship point. At 3 hours and 49 minutes, this was the longest best-of-three-sets [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour) final in history, the longest match in the tournament's history, and was immediately heralded as one of the best matches ever.[^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259] They then played a relatively uncompetitive match at the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles), where Djokovic overpowered Alcaraz in straight sets.[^cite_note-260]

Djokovic and Alcaraz met twice in the summer of 2024, both in high-profile finals. On July 14, Alcaraz cruised to victory in straight sets against Djokovic in [a rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_final) of the previous year's Wimbledon final. Three weeks later, on August 4, Djokovic defeated Alcaraz in [the Olympics final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) on [Court Philippe Chatrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_Philippe_Chatrier). Neither player was broken on serve across two sets, with Djokovic winning both tiebreaks.[^cite_note-261] Sports historian [Steve Flink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Flink) called it "the best two-set match I have ever seen".[^cite_note-262] *[Tennis.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_\(magazine\))* listed it as its ATP match of the year for 2024.[^cite_note-263]

Djokovic and Alcaraz met twice in 2025. In their first quarterfinal-level meeting at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open), Djokovic prevailed in four sets to retain his unbeaten record against Alcaraz on hardcourt and deny his hopes of achieving the [Career Grand Slam](197638.md#Career_Grand_Slam).[^cite_note-BBCDjokovicAO2025-188] They met for a rematch in the semifinals of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where Alcaraz took revenge in straight sets to record his first win over Djokovic on hardcourt.[^cite_note-NYTUSOpen2025-212] Tickets to the match were the most expensive in US Open history.[^cite_note-264] With this match, Alcaraz and Djokovic completed a set of meetings at each of the four majors, something that the [Federer–Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federer%E2%80%93Nadal_rivalry) rivalry never achieved.[^cite_note-265]

Djokovic and Alcaraz have met once in 2026, in the final of the [Australian Open](81715568.md), which Alcaraz won in four sets to end Djokovic's undefeated 10–0 record in Australian Open finals and become the youngest male player to complete the Career Grand Slam.[^cite_note-266] Notably, Alcaraz (along with [Stan Wawrinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Wawrinka)) is one of two tennis players to hold a perfect major final record against Djokovic.

## Playing style

Alcaraz is an [all-court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-court_player) tennis player, but primarily employs an aggressive baseline style of play. He is known for his exceptional court coverage and defensive retrieval ability, often turning defensive positions into offensive opportunities during rallies. His straight-armed [forehand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forehand) is typically his most potent shot: he can either hit it flat and fast for winners from every court position, or add a great amount of topspin and margin over the net. He plays with a flatter and lower two-handed [backhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backhand). Due to his aggression, Alcaraz typically generates a high count of both [winners](2086074.md#winner) and [unforced errors](2086074.md#unforced_error) off his groundstrokes, particularly his forehand.

Alcaraz has a powerful first serve for his height; commonly around 115 to 120 mph and as high as 135 mph, but hit with average placement.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268] Alcaraz often adds topspin to his second serve in order to generate a high bounce, which either pushes his opponents back or forces a weak return. This serve typically reaches 150 to 170 km/h (93 to 106 mph).[^cite_note-269] Due mostly to its lack of targeted placement, Alcaraz's serve is often described as a relative weak spot in his game.[^cite_note-270] He is elite as a returner, particularly of first serves. In 2024, he won nearly a third of his opponents' service games.[^cite_note-271]

Alcaraz has an impressive net-game with excellent drop volleys and drive volleys, and frequently serves-and-volleys on crucial points.[^cite_note-272] His greatly disguised [drop shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_shot) is key to his game. The heaviness of his groundstrokes pushes his opponents to the back of the court, which then makes his drop shots difficult to reach. His slight preference for forehand over backhand drop shots has been noted as unusual.[^cite_note-273] [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick) has said that the Alcaraz drop shot is "the best in the history of tennis".[^cite_note-274] Some players and commentators have credited Alcaraz as responsible for rising drop shot usage across both professional tours in recent years.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276]

Alcaraz has earned acclaim for his athletic and physical traits. In particular, his direct sprints, counterattacking abilities, and an extremely high peak [footspeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footspeed) have garnered him comparisons to [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal). He has been compared to [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) for his assured lateral movement and court-coverage, aided by physical splits and sliding through the court on defense, particularly on the backhand side. Like [Roger Federer](262376.md), exceptional touch and aggression have been crucial to his success.[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] Djokovic has said Alcaraz combines "the best of all three worlds" from his game, Federer's, and Nadal's.[^cite_note-279]

Alcaraz's losses come more frequently in shorter matches, particularly in the best-of-three format. He has sometimes been criticised for a lack of consistency, across matches and across seasons.[^cite_note-280] Alcaraz himself claims he is unable to play well without enjoying himself on court.[^cite_note-281]

Alcaraz is frequently lauded for his on-court creativity and showmanship.[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283] He has been described many times as a "human highlight reel".[^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286] In 2023, he said, "Obviously, I want to win every match that I play, but at the same time, I want to have fun, try different things, make the people enjoy watching tennis and watching my matches. Sometimes I talk to myself about what is most important: if I win or doing great things."[^cite_note-287]

## Coaches and team

Alcaraz's childhood coaches were Kiko Navarro and Carlos Santos.[^cite_note-ChildhoodCoach-32] In September 2018, fifteen-year-old Alcaraz moved to [Villena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villena) to begin training at the [Ferrero Tennis Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrero_Tennis_Academy) under the direction of [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero), who became his personal coach.[^cite_note-\:2-288] Ferrero turned down several coaching requests from top players in order to coach Alcaraz.[^cite_note-289][^cite_note-290] Their partnership was widely praised: Ferrero won the [ATP Coach of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Awards) in 2022 and 2025. In December 2025, Alcaraz unexpectedly announced that he and Ferrero had parted ways after seven years.[^cite_note-291]

In December 2024, it was announced that [Samuel López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_L%C3%B3pez_\(tennis_coach\)), a former member of Ferrero's own coaching team, would be joining Alcaraz's team to work under Ferrero as a second coach.[^cite_note-292] Following Ferrero's departure, he became Alcaraz's head coach for the 2026 season.[^cite_note-Jurejko-223]

Alcaraz has maintained largely the same staff since he arrived on tour. His team includes physical trainer Alberto Lledó, physiotherapist Juanjo Moreno, doctor Juanjo López, and agent Albert Molina. His brother Álvaro works as his longtime [hitting partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitting_partner) and, as of 2026, assistant coach.[^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294] In 2025, Alcaraz appointed childhood friend Fran Rubio as an additional physiotherapist.[^cite_note-295] Alcaraz also works with sports psychologist Isabel Balaguer.[^cite_note-296]

### Endorsements

In June 2025, Alcaraz became an ambassador for multinational food company [Danone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danone).[^cite_note-307] The same month, he was announced as an ambassador for French mineral water brand [Evian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evian), which is owned by Danone.[^cite_note-308] In July 2025, he was announced as the brand ambassador for YoPRO, a high protein yoghurt brand also under Danone's portfolio, and its sister brand Oikos.[^cite_note-309][^cite_note-310] In February 2026, Alcaraz's longtime sponsor Babolat announced a commercial range of "Carlitos Junior" racquets and bags for young tennis players.[^cite_note-311] In March 2026, Alcaraz became a brand ambassador for [Sunreef Yachts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunreef_Yachts), and purchased an Ultima 88 catamaran, on the advice of fellow brand ambassador [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal).[^cite_note-312] In April 2026, he was announced as a brand ambassador for Indian multinational technology company [Infosys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys), which also supplies tennis data to the ATP.[^cite_note-313] In May 2026, he became an ambassador for [Ant International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant_International), the Singapore-based payments and [fintech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_technology) arm of China-based [Ant Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant_Group).[^cite_note-314]

In August 2025, [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes) estimated that Alcaraz was the highest-paid active tennis player, combining endorsements and prize money, for the second year running.[^cite_note-315][^cite_note-Forbes2025-316] [Sportico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportico) estimated that Alcaraz was the world's tenth highest-paid athlete from endorsements (excluding prize money) in 2025.[^cite_note-317]

### In the media and popular culture

Alcaraz made appearances in both seasons of Netflix's *[Break Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_Point_\(2023_TV_series\))* docuseries,[^cite_note-318] and played against [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) in *The Netflix Slam*, a live-streamed tennis match hosted by MGM Resorts International on March 3, 2024.[^cite_note-319] Alcaraz was the subject of the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) show *[Carlos Alcaraz: My Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz:_My_Way)*, which documented his 2024 season and was released April 23, 2025.[^cite_note-320] The three-part series focused on conflict between Alcaraz and his team as he attempted to balance his ambition to make tennis history with the normal life of a 21-year-old. It included appearances by Rafael Nadal, [Roger Federer](262376.md), [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza), [Martina Navratilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Navratilova), [Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi), [Björn Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Borg), and [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe) alongside other tennis greats.[^cite_note-321]

In April 2025, Barney Ronay for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* described Alcaraz as "arguably the most interesting athlete in the world right now".[^cite_note-322] He is known for his wide smile and charisma on the court.[^cite_note-323][^cite_note-324] He has received significant media attention off the court for his personal life, particularly his trips to [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza).[^cite_note-325][^cite_note-326] Alcaraz's style choices, particularly his unplanned [buzz cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_cut) at the [2025 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_\(tennis\)) and his subsequent blonde dye job, have also generated media attention.[^cite_note-327][^cite_note-328] He is known for receiving tattoos to commemorate his slam victories.[^cite_note-329] The *[New York Times](30680.md)* selected him as one of its "most stylish people" of 2025, and *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ)* as one of the most stylish male tennis players of all time.[^cite_note-330][^cite_note-331] At the [2026 US Open](80928175.md), [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) launched the [Cactus Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus_Jack_Records) x Carlos Alcaraz Performance Collection in collaboration with Alcaraz and [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott), which Alcaraz wore on court.[^cite_note-332]

In 2024, a media controversy arose when Alcaraz publicly broke a racket for the first time during a match in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-333] He afterwards delivered a public apology.[^cite_note-RacquetSmash-176] Alcaraz is also known for his willingness to discuss mental health with the media. After his loss in the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), he stated, "I've taken steps backwards, like I'm not doing well mentally, I'm not strong. I don't know how to control myself when faced with problems and I don't know how to handle it."[^cite_note-334] Following a loss at [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open) in 2025, he admitted to having felt "nervous the whole day", and following a win in [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles) a month later, that he had been "struggling stepping on the court" and had considered taking an extended break from tennis.[^cite_note-335][^cite_note-336] Various commentators, including [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick), [Boris Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Becker), and [Andrea Petkovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Petkovic), have highlighted his unusual candidness about mental health.[^cite_note-337][^cite_note-338][^cite_note-339]

Alcaraz has often advocated for professional tennis players' welfare. In 2024, he spoke out several times against the length of the tennis calendar, stressing the frequency of injuries and joking that "probably they are going to kill us in some way".[^cite_note-340][^cite_note-341] In 2025, he supported fellow Spanish player [Sara Sorribes Tormo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Sorribes_Tormo)'s decision to take a break from the sport for her health.[^cite_note-342] He also signed two letters alongside other top men's and women's tennis players asking that the four major tournaments distribute a greater share of their revenue to all competing players.[^cite_note-343][^cite_note-344] In 2026, after returning from injury, he once against criticised the tennis schedule and announced his intention to take one or two month breaks during future seasons.[^cite_note-345]

Alcaraz has often been compared to his compatriot Rafael Nadal, particularly in their native Spain. As a teenager, he was dubbed "baby Rafa" or "young Nadal".[^cite_note-346] However, he and Nadal have both dismissed these comparisons at length.[^cite_note-347][^cite_note-348]

Alcaraz was featured on the [Time 100 Next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100_Next) list in 2022.[^cite_note-349] In December 2025, he was included on the *50 Names in the [Boardroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boardroom_\(company\))* list.[^cite_note-350]

### Philanthropy

In April 2024, Alcaraz announced the launch of the Carlos Alcaraz Garfia Foundation, with the aim of improving the lives of disadvantaged children. It is headquartered in [El Palmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Palmar,_Murcia) and operates in partnership with local schools.[^cite_note-351][^cite_note-352] Alcaraz's foundation provided buses to transport volunteers after the [flash floods in Valencia in 2024](78242552.md), during which Alcaraz and [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero) also ran a charity auction to raise funds for those affected.[^cite_note-353] On December 12, 2024, Alcaraz inaugurated his foundation's exhibition "Los Pies en la Tierra" in Murcia, highlighting the importance of equal opportunities for children.[^cite_note-354] The exhibition travelled to other areas of Spain, including Madrid and Seville, in 2025 and 2026.[^cite_note-355]

### Involvement in other sports

Alcaraz is a football fan who supports the Spanish club [Real Madrid](26413.md).[^cite_note-356] In 2026 he presented the [FIFA World Cup Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy) for [Louis Vuitton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Vuitton) prior to the [Spain–Argentina final](75845879.md).[^cite_note-357] He is known for his vocal support of many other Spanish athletes, including [Formula One](10854.md) driver [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) and Georgian-Spanish [MMA](228344.md) fighter [Ilia Topuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilia_Topuria).[^cite_note-358][^cite_note-359] Alcaraz is also a keen player of golf and of chess. In 2023, [Chess.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess.com) created a dedicated Carlos Alcaraz bot that any user can challenge virtually on the platform.[^cite_note-360]
