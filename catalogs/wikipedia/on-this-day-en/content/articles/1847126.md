The **brown skua** (***Stercorarius antarcticus***), also known as the **Antarctic skua**, **Subantarctic skua**, **southern great skua**, **southern skua**, or **hākoakoa** ([Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language)), is a large [seabird](147019.md) that breeds in the [subantarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subantarctic) and [Antarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic) zones and moves farther north when not breeding. Its [taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_\(biology\)) is highly complex and a matter of dispute, with some [splitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpers_and_splitters) it into two or three species: Falkland skua (*S. antarcticus*), Tristan skua (*S. hamiltoni*), and subantarctic skua (*S. lonnbergi*). To further confuse, it [hybridizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_\(biology\)) with both the [south polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_polar_skua) and [Chilean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_skua) skuas, and the entire group has been considered to be a [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies) of the [great skua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_skua), a species otherwise restricted to the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere).

## Description



Brown skua eyeing a [king penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_penguin) carcass

This is the heaviest species of [skua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skua) and rivals the largest [gulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gull), the [great black-backed gull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_black-backed_gull) and [glaucous gull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucous_gull), as the heaviest species in the [shorebird order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charadriiformes) although not as large in length or wingspan.[^cite_note-\:0-2] It is 52–64 cm (20–25 in) in length, 126–160 cm (50–63 in) in wingspan and has a body mass of 1.2–2.18 kg (2.6–4.8 lb).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-CRC-4] *S. a. hamiltoni* measured on [Gough Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gough_Island), weighed an average of 1.43 kg (3.2 lb) in 9 males and 1.65 kg (3.6 lb) in 9 females. *S. a. lonnbergi* measured in the [Chatham Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands) weighed an average of 1.73 kg (3.8 lb) in 30 males and an average of 1.93 kg (4.3 lb) in 32 females. The latter is one of the highest colony mean body mass for any living species of shorebird.[^cite_note-CRC2-5]

A study in 2016 reported that brown skuas can identify individual human beings, possibly indicating high cognitive abilities.[^cite_note-Lee-6]

Brown skuas have been noted for sometimes bonding with humans who live for extended periods in Antarctica, such as the [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) clergymen at [Trinity Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Church_\(Antarctica\)), and engaging in playful or apparently mischievous behavior with them.[^cite_note-7]

## Taxonomy

There are three accepted subspecies:[^cite_note-8]

- *S. a. lonnbergi.* Range: subantarctic.

- *S. a. antarcticus.* Range: Falkland Islands.

- *S. a. hamiltoni.* Range: [Tristan da Cunha](31361.md).

## Ecology

### Diet

It feeds on [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) (often via [kleptoparasitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleptoparasitism)), penguin chicks and other [seabirds](147019.md), small [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal), eggs and [carrion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrion).[^cite_note-\:0-2]
