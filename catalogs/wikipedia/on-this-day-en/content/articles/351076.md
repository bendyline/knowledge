The **West Midlands** is one of nine official [regions of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_England) at the [first level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITL_1_statistical_regions_of_England) of [International Territorial Level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Territorial_Level) for [statistical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics) purposes. It covers the western half of the area known traditionally as the [Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands_\(England\)). The region consists of the [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_england) of [Herefordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herefordshire), [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire), [Staffordshire](58733.md), [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire), [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\)) and [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire). The region has seven cities: [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry), [Hereford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereford), [Lichfield](159948.md), [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent), [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton) and [Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_England).

The West Midlands region is geographically diverse, from the urban central areas of the [West Midlands conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation) to the rural counties of Herefordshire, Shropshire which border [Wales](69894.md), and Worcestershire. The region is landlocked; however, the longest river in the UK, the [River Severn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Severn), traverses the region south-eastwards, flowing through the [county towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) of [Shrewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury) and Worcester, and the [Ironbridge Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironbridge_Gorge), a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md). Staffordshire is home to the industrialised [Potteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Potteries) conurbation, including the city of [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent) and the [Staffordshire Moorlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Moorlands) area, which borders the south-eastern [Peak District National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District_National_Park) near [Leek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leek,_Staffordshire). The region also encompasses five [Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areas_of_Outstanding_Natural_Beauty): the [Wye Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wye_Valley), [Shropshire Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire_Hills), [Cannock Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase), [Malvern Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern_Hills) and parts of the [Cotswolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotswolds). Warwickshire is home to the towns of [Stratford upon Avon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford_upon_Avon), birthplace of writer [William Shakespeare](32897.md); [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_Warwickshire), the birthplace of [Rugby football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football); and [Nuneaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuneaton), birthplace to author [George Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Eliot).

## Geography

The official region contains the [ceremonial counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) of [Herefordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herefordshire), [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire), [Staffordshire](58733.md), [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire), [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\)) and [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire).

There is some confusion in the use of the term "West Midlands", as the name is also used for the much smaller [West Midlands county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\)) and [West Midlands conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation) which is in the central belt of the Midlands and on the eastern side of the West Midlands Region. It is also still used by various organisations within that area, such as [West Midlands Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Police) and [West Midlands Fire Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Fire_Service).

The highest point in the region is [Black Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mountain_\(hill\)), at 703 metres (2,307 ft) in west Herefordshire on the border with [Powys, Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powys,_Wales). The region contains five [Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areas_of_Outstanding_Natural_Beauty) (AONBs), including the [Shropshire Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire_Hills), [Malvern Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern_Hills) and [Cannock Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase), and parts of the [Wye Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wye_Valley) and [Cotswolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotswolds). The [Peak District National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District_National_Park) also stretches into north [Staffordshire](58733.md).

## Modern history

Further information: [Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercia)

### Second World War

The [RAF Fauld explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Fauld_explosion) on 27 November 1944 in east Staffordshire produced a 100-foot deep crater, and is the UK's largest explosion, being caused by around 4,000 tonnes of [high explosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_material), and may be the [world's largest non-nuclear explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_artificial_non-nuclear_explosions).

Birmingham was the third [most bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Blitz) city in the UK after London and [Liverpool](18081.md); Spitfires were built in Castle Bromwich, [Lancasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lancaster) at Austin's works in [Longbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longbridge_plant) at [Cofton Hackett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofton_Hackett), and the [Birmingham Small Arms Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Small_Arms_Company) at [Small Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Heath,_Birmingham) produced the [M1919 Browning machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1919_Browning_machine_gun). [Boulton Paul Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulton_Paul_Aircraft) had their main aircraft factory in the north of Wolverhampton. [RAF Defford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Defford), in the south of Worcestershire between Pershore and [Croome Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croome_Court), was where many important [airborne radars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control) were developed, such as [H2S (radar)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H2S_\(radar\)) and anti-submarine radars.

### Scientific heritage

[Thomas Wedgwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Wedgwood_\(photographer\)), son of Josiah Wedgwood, discovered the first [photo-sensitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographic_paper) (light-sensitive) chemicals – [silver nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_nitrate) and [silver chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_chloride) in the 1790s.

Sir [Norman Lockyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Lockyer) of [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_Warwickshire) discovered [helium](13256.md) in 1868, for which he used electromagnetic [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy).

[Edward Weston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Weston_\(chemist\)) of Oswestry, who emigrated to the US, built the first accurate [voltmeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltmeter) in the late 1880s, and the [Weston cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston_cell) in 1893.

[Francis W. Aston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_William_Aston) of Harborne, educated at the University of Birmingham, developed [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry) in 1919, which helped him to identify the first [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope), receiving the [Nobel Prize for Chemistry](25416893.md) in 1922.

[Dennis Gabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Gabor) invented holography at [British Thomson-Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Thomson-Houston) in Rugby in 1947, receiving the [Nobel Prize for Physics](52497.md) in 1971.

[James Glaisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Glaisher) in 1862 took a record balloon flight with [Henry Tracey Coxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Tracey_Coxwell) for the [BAAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Science_Association) near Wolverhampton. They reached 29,000 feet (8,800 m) the composition of the Earth's atmosphere until then was not understood; the altitude records for the UK have not been exceeded since; [Project Excelsior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Excelsior) in the US in 1960 would later reach 20 miles (110,000 ft).

[Philip Lawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Lawley) of Burton upon Trent was first person to realise that [chemical damage to DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_tobacco) caused cancer (at the Chester Beatty Research Institute in London) in the early 1960s.

[Francis Galton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Galton) (d. 1911) of the [Darwin–Wedgwood family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin%E2%80%93Wedgwood_family)'s Birmingham branch was an early [eugenicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics) rooted in improving animal breeding stock and examining heredity. He invented terms eugenics and *[nature versus nurture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_versus_nurture)*. His limited calls for human eugenics were widened by the [German Society for Racial Hygiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Society_for_Racial_Hygiene) in 1905 founded by [Alfred Ploetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Ploetz), which coupled with the racial superiority fallacies of [Aryanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan_race) reached its nadir in [genocidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust) [antisemitism](1078.md). Moral teachings and [inherent repulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deontological_ethics) towards human eugenics were overcome by a minority of those in power espousing [racial equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_equality); European media and leaders lamented [loss of Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_imperialism), advocated [ultranationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalism) and prized military physical advantage; Galton saw human eugenics as part of all means to *do better*.

### Industrial heritage

See also: [Science and invention in Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_invention_in_Birmingham) and [Architecture of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Birmingham)



The [cast iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast_iron) Iron Bridge at [Coalbrookdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalbrookdale), opened in January 1781, was the first large-scale object made out of cast iron; but cast iron is not reliably strong due to impurities. [Wrought iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrought_iron), where the carbon is hammered to remove the carbon and impurities is much stronger; the first large-scale wrought iron bridge was the [Britannia Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia_Bridge) over the [Menai Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menai_Strait), only possible due to its innovative [box girder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_girder) design by [Robert Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Stephenson).&#x20;

Much of the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) in the United Kingdom began in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) and the [Black Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Country) area of West Midlands. The Industrial Revolution is thought to have begun when [Abraham Darby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Darby_I) substituted [coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coke_\(fuel\)) in the place of charcoal to smelt iron, at his Old Furnace. The Black Country may be regarded as the world's first industrial landscape,[^cite_note-6] while nearby [Ironbridge Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironbridge_Gorge) claims to be the Birthplace of Industry.[^cite_note-7] [The world's first cast iron bridge in 1779](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Iron_Bridge) spans the Gorge. The first self-propelled locomotive to run on rails in 1803 at Coalbrookdale, was built by [Richard Trevithick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Trevithick). The [first iron rails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_way_\(history\)) for horse-drawn transport, were made at Coalbrookdale in 1768 by [Richard Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Reynolds_\(ironmaster\)) at [Ketley Ironworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketley_Ironworks). Iron rails only became widely successful in 1820 when made out of [wrought iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrought_iron) at [Bedlington Ironworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedlington_Ironworks) in north-east England.



*Coalbrookdale by Night*, of the [Madeley Wood Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeley_Wood_Company), painted by [Philip James de Loutherbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_James_de_Loutherbourg) in 1801

Birmingham's industrial development was triggered by discussions at the [Lunar Society of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Society_of_Birmingham) at [Soho House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soho_House), Boulton's house, and products were carried along the [BCN Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCN_Main_Line) canal. [Soho Manufactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soho_Manufactory) was the first man-made-powered factory in world. [Chance Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chance_Brothers) of Smethwick built the glass for [The Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crystal_Palace) in 1851. [Smethwick Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smethwick_Engine), now at [Thinktank, Birmingham Science Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinktank,_Birmingham_Science_Museum), is the oldest working steam engine, made in 1779, and is the oldest working engine in the world. Smethwick was a main centre for making [lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse) [lanterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantern).

[Valor Fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_&_Valor) in Erdington developed the first [radiant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_heating) [gas fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_heater) in 1967, a balanced [flue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flue) fire in 1973, and a natural flame gas fire in 1978. The Erdington site, owned by Iceland's [BDR Thermea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BDR_Thermea), closed in May 2012. The company also built [gas cookers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_stove); since 2011 the company has been part of [Glen Dimplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Dimplex), who have a site at [Cooper's Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper's_Bank?action=edit\&redlink=1), south of [Gornalwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gornal,_West_Midlands).



Boulton, Watt and Murdoch, a 1956 statue on Broad Street in Birmingham; the [SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) unit of power is the watt, most commonly found as the kW, a replacement for the imperial measurement of [horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower).&#x20;

[Ditherington Flax Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditherington_Flax_Mill) in Shrewsbury was the first [iron-framed building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_frame) in the world in 1797. [Thomas Bolton & Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bolton_&_Sons?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Froghall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Froghall), Staffordshire, made the world's first [transatlantic telegraph cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_telegraph_cable) in 1857, having supplied a [submarine cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_communications_cable) across the English Channel in 1850. On 10 July 1890, a trunk circuit telephone line was opened between London and Birmingham by the [National Telephone Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Telephone_Company); for the first time this allowed phone calls between the London and the north.[^cite_note-8] The world's first [coaxial cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxial_cable) was laid between London and Birmingham in 1936 to give 40 channels for telephone traffic.[^cite_note-9] and brought into use in 1938, later extended to Manchester in 1940.

[Alexander Parkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Parkes) invented the first man-made plastic ([thermoplastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoplastic)) in Birmingham in 1856. Princess Square, Wolverhampton, was the site of Britain's first traffic lights in 1927. Infrared cameras were developed at the Royal Radar Establishment in Malvern (with EMI Electronics) in 1967. The world's first [Maglev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maglev) train operated at [Birmingham Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirRail_Link) in 1983. The tallest freestanding structure in the region was the chimney of [Ironbridge power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironbridge_power_stations) at 673 ft. [John Baskerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Baskerville) of Birmingham, a former stone carver, largely invented fonts, or [typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface), for printing.

See also: [Automotive industry in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_the_United_Kingdom)

Much of the UK's car industry would be centred in Coventry and Birmingham; most of this has now gone. [Midland Motor Cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Motor_Cylinder?action=edit\&redlink=1) (part of [Birmid Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmid_Industries?action=edit\&redlink=1)) of Smethwick was the largest producer of automobile [cylinder blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_block) in Europe. [Fort Dunlop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Dunlop) was Europe's largest tyre plant. [Metro-Cammell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Cammell) in Birmingham made most of the 1970s and 1980s [London Underground](17839.md) trains. [MG Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MG_Rover_Group) (a company of Rover) closed in 2005 (from 1885), The [Ryton plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryton_plant), which made the [Peugeot 206](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_206), closed at the end of 2006, with production moving to [Trnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trnava) in Slovakia, and some to a plant at [Kolín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kol%C3%ADn) in the [Czech Republic](5321.md). [Alfred Herbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Herbert_\(company\)) of Coventry was the largest [machine-tool manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_builder?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the UK for many decades; it was brought down in the 1970s by advancing technology overseas, and complacent strategic decisions of the management, finally closing in 1982; many Midlands manufacturing companies followed similar fates in the 1970s and 1980s.



1817 Boulton & Watt beam blowing engine re-erected on the [Dartmouth Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Circus) roundabout, on the [A4540 Middleway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4540_Middleway) and the A38(M)

[Henry Wiggin & Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wiggin_&_Co?action=edit\&redlink=1) of Hereford developed the metal [alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy) necessary for other Midlands' (and beyond) automotive and aerospace companies – [Inconel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inconel), [Incoloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incoloy) and [Nimonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimonic). It was the lack of [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium) for [high-melting point alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-temperature_corrosion), caused by Royal Navy action, that prevented German [Me 262](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Me_262) engines being serviceable; had German Second World War engineers had a greater supply of vanadium and [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum), the engine life (around 12 hours maximum, from entering service in April 1944 to the end of the war) of their [jet engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Jumo_004) would have increased much more, which would have been significant to the war's outcome. [Bristol Siddeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Siddeley) developed the rocket engines for [Black Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Arrow) at Ansty; in fact all of R-R's rocket engines were developed and built there at R-R's Industrial and Marine Gas Turbine Division; Britain's smaller rocket engines for missiles were built by [Bristol Aerojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Aerojet) in what is now [North Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Somerset). High Duty Alloys at Redditch constructed (forged) the compressor and turbine blades for Whittle's first engines, and many of the early jet engines; it made [Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde)'s airframe from the [Hiduminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiduminium) R.R.58 [aluminium alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_alloy).

[Maxaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxaret), the world's first [ABS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-lock_braking_system) [braking](65423.md) system, was invented in Coventry by Dunlop in the early 1950s for aircraft; [John Boyd Dunlop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boyd_Dunlop) was a Scottish vet who had first produced the first pneumatic [tyres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tire) in 1889. [Matthew Piers Watt Boulton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Piers_Watt_Boulton), grandson of Matthew Boulton, and born in the area, invented the [aileron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileron), an important [flight control surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_control_surfaces) in 1868, decades before the first actual flight. [Triumph Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumph_Engineering) was a famous motorbike firm in Meriden. About a quarter of all British WWI planes were built in Coventry. The [Jensen Interceptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Interceptor) FF was the first production four-wheel-drive car in the world, designed by Major [Tony Rolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Rolt), and built at their factory in West Bromwich.

Cadbury launched *[Dairy Milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadbury_Dairy_Milk)* in 1905, *[Bournville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournville_\(chocolate_bar\))* in 1906, *[Fruit & Nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadbury_Dairy_Milk_Fruit_&_Nut)* in 1928, *Whole Nut* in 1930, *[Cadbury Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadbury_Roses)* in 1938, and the *[Cadbury Creme Egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadbury_Creme_Egg)* in 1971. [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Cadbury) and [Richard Cadbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Cadbury) built their factory in 1879 and [Bournville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournville) in 1893, named after the [Bourn brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournbrook). [Iceland (supermarket)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_\(supermarket\)) opened its first store in Oswestry in 1970 – heralding the onset of frozen food in the UK. [Alfred Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Bird) invented egg-free custard in 1837 in Birmingham – accidentally given to guests at his home, being created as his wife had an allergy to eggs; he then invented [baking powder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking_powder) in 1843 as his wife also had an allergy to [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast).

### Culture

[J. R. R. Tolkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._R._Tolkien) grew up in Birmingham, Kings Heath, then part of Worcestershire, and was inspired by [Moseley Bog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moseley_Bog) and [Sarehole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarehole), and perhaps by the [Perrott's Folly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perrott's_Folly). [Philip Larkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Larkin) came from Coventry. [Rowland Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowland_Hill) (stamps) was from Kidderminster. The writer [George Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Eliot) came from [Nuneaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuneaton). [Anthony E. Pratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_E._Pratt) from Birmingham invented [Cluedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluedo).

[Frederick Gibberd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Gibberd) of Coventry designed [Liverpool Metropolitan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Metropolitan_Cathedral). [Edward Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Cave) from Rugby made Britain's first magazine in 1731 – [The Gentleman's Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gentleman's_Magazine). [Philip Astley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Astley) from Newcastle-under-Lyme invented the modern day circus in 1768 – [Astley's Amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astley's_Amphitheatre).

The [Castlemorton Common Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlemorton_Common_Festival) in May 1992 near Malvern, led to the [Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Justice_and_Public_Order_Act_1994).

The [Nowka Bais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowka_Bais) is a [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_culture) [boat racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat_racing) festival which takes place annually in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham). It is a cultural event in the West Midlands, [United Kingdom](31717.md) attracting not only the [Bangladeshi diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_diaspora) but a variety of cultures.[^cite_note-10] It is also the largest kind of [boat race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat_race) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-11]

### Teenage pregnancy

For top-tier authorities in the West Midlands, Stoke-on-Trent has the highest [teenage pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_pregnancy_and_sexual_health_in_the_United_Kingdom) rate. For council districts, [Nuneaton and Bedworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuneaton_and_Bedworth) in Warwickshire has the highest rate closely followed by [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth,_Staffordshire). For top-tier authorities, Shropshire has the lowest rate, and for council districts [Malvern Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern_Hills_\(district\)) has the lowest rate.

### Social deprivation

The region, from studies of multiple deprivation, shows similarities with [Yorkshire and the Humber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_and_the_Humber), and is more deprived than the neighbouring East Midlands. From the [Indices of deprivation 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indices_of_deprivation_2007), it can be seen that, in common with [Northern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England), the region has more [Lower Area Super Output Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Output_Areas) in the 20% most deprived districts than in the 20% least deprived districts.[^cite_note-20] The region's most deprived council districts, in descending order, are Birmingham (10th highest in England), Sandwell (14th), Stoke-on-Trent (16th), Wolverhampton (28th), Walsall (45th), Coventry (61st), and Dudley (100th).[^cite_note-21]

The least deprived districts in 2007 (before Shropshire became a unitary authority in 2009) were Bromsgrove, South Staffordshire, Warwick, Wychavon, and Lichfield. At county level, the least deprived areas, in descending order, were Warwickshire, Worcestershire, Solihull, Staffordshire, and Shropshire.

In March 2011 the region had the second highest overall [unemployment claimant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jobseeker's_Allowance) count in England at 4.7%, second to [North East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_England). The highest in the region was Wolverhampton at 7.7%, the joint second highest (with Manchester) unemployment rate in England. Next is Sandwell with 7.1%, Birmingham with 7.0%, and Walsall with 6.4%. The lowest rate in the region is the district of [Stratford-on-Avon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford-on-Avon_\(district\)), with 1.6% – one of the lowest unemployment rates in England.[^cite_note-22]

### Regional assembly

The official representative body of the region is the [West Midlands Leaders Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Leaders_Board) which has limited administrative functions such as regional planning and economic development. The board is not an elected body, but is made up of members appointed from local councils across the region and is known as a [quango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quango). It is based on *Edward Street* in Birmingham, near the [National Indoor Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Indoor_Arena). From March 2010, the funding decisions at regional level were taken over by [Advantage West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advantage_West_Midlands), the [Regional Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Development_Agency).

### Elections

In the [2015 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_United_Kingdom_general_election), the Conservatives gained the largest share of the region by popular vote and took control of the number of seats, with 42% of the region's electorate voting Conservative, 33% Labour, 16% UKIP, 6% Liberal Democrat and 3% Green. The Conservatives gained 2 seats with virtually no swing from Labour to Conservative.[^cite_note-electoralcalculus2015-24]



General Election results in 2017

In the [2017 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_United_Kingdom_general_election), [South Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Staffordshire_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) ([Gavin Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Williamson)) had the second-highest Conservative vote proportion in the UK – 69.8%. [David Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Firth_\(statistician\)), at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), invented the BBC election [exit poll](665947.md). 6 ft 9 [Daniel Kawczynski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kawczynski), a former Shropshire MP, was the tallest MP ever.

As of the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election), the Labour Party have the most seats in the region with 38 and won 34.0% of the vote. The Conservatives lost 29 and hold 15 seats, almost halving their vote share to 27.6%. The Liberal Democrats gained two and the Green Party gained one, both from zero. There is also an independent MP representing [Birmingham Perry Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Perry_Barr).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_R._Ehrlich) about population growth in *[Playboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy)* magazine.[^cite_note-28] In 1975, it became the Ecology Party[^cite_note-29] and then the Green Party in 1985.[^cite_note-30]

### Secondary education

Selective schools are in low numbers as follows: Birmingham (8), Walsall (2), Wolverhampton (1), Warwickshire (6), Stoke-on-Trent (1), and Telford and Wrekin (2). The highest proportion per head therefore is Warwickshire (its population is between 550,000 and 600,000 people). The other counties and metropolitan boroughs have none, their public education systems are comprehensive in intake. The grammar and independent schools tend to produce pass-rate examination results among the top twenty ranked regionally. Many pupils compete for entrance examinations to attend such long-established [Grammar Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar_schools_in_England) and most have significant parent sponsorship. In 2016 two of the top ten such schools nationally were in Warwickshire, where in the [CV37 postal district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CV_postcode_area) prices were 34% higher than the county as a whole.[^cite_note-35]

Around 275,000 secondary schools are in the region, the greatest number after the [South East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_England), Greater London and [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_England).

At [GCSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCSE) based on % of entrants' pass rates, the best performing local government area in 2010 was [Solihull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solihull), closely followed by Warwickshire and Shropshire. Dudley, Herefordshire, Telford and Wrekin, Birmingham and Staffordshire (in descending order) are above the English average, at which rate, is approximately Worcestershire. The area consistently having fewest passes is [Sandwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwell), followed by Stoke-on-Trent. Struggling pupils in Wolverhampton and Walsall also attain fewer passes than the English average in most GCSE years, sometimes by a very narrow margin. For metropolitan boroughs, Solihull then Dudley perform best. Dudley is the best metropolitan borough at A-level passes and has a consistent post-2000 history of being better than Solihull.

According to *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, schools have been [off-rolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-rolling) pupils.[^cite_note-36] Pupils likely to perform poorly in examinations are expelled before the examinations to improve the school performance in league tables. Expelled pupils then disproportionately get involved in gangs and in crime. Knife crime in the West Midlands is the highest outside London.[^cite_note-37]

In 2010, regionally in persistent [truancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truancy) at secondary school, Sandwell had the highest rate at 6.9%; [Bromsgrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromsgrove_\(district\)) had the lowest at 2.3%.[^cite_note-38]

### Tertiary education

There are thirty-seven FE colleges (FECs).[^cite_note-39] There are six LSCs for the area (which fund FECs), and the [Learning and Skills Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_and_Skills_Council) head office is based in [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry). The five largest FE colleges in the region – [Bournville College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournville_College), [North Warwickshire and Hinckley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Warwickshire_and_Hinckley_College), [Solihull College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solihull_College), [South & City College Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_&_City_College_Birmingham) and [Stoke-on-Trent College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent_College) – each have more than 25,000 students.

School children in Shropshire and Solihull are most likely to attend university, followed by Herefordshire, Worcestershire and Warwickshire.

The University of Birmingham is the main university in the region[^cite_note-41] and has the most funding. It has a large research grant, as does the University of Warwick, which is the next largest in terms of funding. Birmingham and Warwick are members of the [Russell Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Group) of public research universities. Keele and Aston have a moderate research grant, but none of the other universities do. Keele, although having the largest campus in the UK (by area), is one of the smallest universities in the region. There are medical schools at [Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_Medical_School), [Keele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keele_University_Medical_School) and [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham_Medical_School). Birmingham and Warwick receive more than twice as much total income than any other university in the region – around £400 million each.

Around 45% of students are from the region, and 35% from other parts of the UK, while 20% are from overseas. The region attracts students from [South East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_England) owing to good access via the [M40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M40_motorway) and the [West Coast Main Line](182538.md), but there is a good mix from other regions too, except the North East (especially) and Yorkshire. Students native to the West Midlands are most likely to study in the region (40%), then the [East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands) (12%), the [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_England) (11%), and then Yorkshire (9%). Very few go to the [East of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_England) or the [North East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_England). The region has a net export of university students to other regions.

At time of graduation in 2010 almost 60% of graduates remained in the West Midlands, with 10% going to London, 7% to the South-East, and around 5% to the East Midlands. Very few go to Yorkshire, the North-East, or even (neighbouring) Wales.

## Transport

### Railways

Served by many lines in the urban areas such as the [West Coast Main Line](182538.md) and branches. The [Welsh Marches Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Marches_Line) and the [Cotswold Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotswold_Line) transect the region as well as the [Cross Country Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Country_Route) and [Chiltern Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiltern_Line). There are plans to reopen the [Gloucestershire Warwickshire Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire_Warwickshire_Railway). The [HS2 (High Speed Two) project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Speed_2) is planned to connect London to Birmingham. The opening date is uncertain, but beyond 2033.[^cite_note-42]

### Roads

Several notable roads pass through the region, with most converging around the central conurbation. The [M5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M5_motorway) motorway, which connects [South West England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_England) to the region, passes through [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire) and [Gloucestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire), and through the [West Midlands county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_county), past [West Bromwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich), with its northern terminus at its junction with the [M6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_motorway) outside [Staffordshire](58733.md). The M6, which has its southern terminus just outside the southeast of the region at its junction with the [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway) motorway, and which connects the region to [North West England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_England), passes [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_Warwickshire) and [Nuneaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuneaton) in Warwickshire, [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry) and [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), and [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford) and [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent) in Staffordshire. The [M6 toll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_toll) provides an alternative route to the M6 between [Coleshill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleshill,_Warwickshire) and [Cannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock), passing north of [Sutton Coldfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Coldfield) and just south of [Lichfield](159948.md).

The [M45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M45_motorway) motorway connects the region through [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire) to the [East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands), giving access to the north of the [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway) motorway and, with its eastern terminus at its junction with the [A45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A45_road) road near the Warwickshire-Northamptonshire border; it passes close to [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_Warwickshire) and [Dunchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunchurch). The M42 connects the M5 at [Bromsgrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromsgrove), passing around the south and east of Birmingham, joining the M40 and M6, passing [Fordbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordbridge), [Kingshurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingshurst) and [Castle Bromwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Bromwich), to [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth,_Staffordshire), northeast of Birmingham. The [M50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M50_motorway_\(Great_Britain\)) connects the M5 from near [Tewkesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tewkesbury) to [Ross-on-Wye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross-on-Wye) in the southwest. The [M54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M54_motorway) connects [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington,_Shropshire) in the west, passing [Telford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telford), to the M6 near [Cannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock). The [A5 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_road_\(Great_Britain\)) traverses the region northwest–southeast, passing through [Shrewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury), [Telford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telford), [Cannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock), [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth,_Staffordshire) and [Nuneaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuneaton), also giving access to [Hinckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinckley).

The [A40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A40_road_\(Great_Britain\)), the longest A- road in [Zone 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_road_numbering_scheme#Zoning_system), passes through [Ross-on-Wye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross-on-Wye) alongside the [A449](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A449), which goes to [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford) and [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Wales), and [M50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M50_motorway_\(Great_Britain\)) motorway.

The longest elevated road viaduct in the UK is the 3 miles (4,779 m) section from Gravelly Hill to Castle Bromwich on the M6, opened on 24 May 1972; the 3.5 miles (5.6 km) [Bromford Viaduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromford_Viaduct) is the longest viaduct in the UK. The section of the A45 in Coventry from [Willenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willenhall,_Coventry) to Allesley in 1939 was one of the UK's first ever large planned road schemes; road schemes on that scale had not been previously built, with few large road schemes outside of London, or were piecemeal.

Princes Square in Wolverhampton had Britain's first automatic traffic lights on 5 November 1927. On 13 January 2012, 34-year-old Ben Westwood of Wednesfield, was caught by the police, when speeding at 180 mph, in an [Audi RS5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A5) with a [Lamborghini](18271.md) engine, from Wolverhampton up to Stafford on the M6, and back again. He was travelling so fast that he was outpacing the [Central Counties Air Operations Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Counties_Air_Operations_Unit) [Eurocopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC135) helicopter. He and the vehicle had been in fifteen [smash and grab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smash_and_grab) raids and he was jailed for nine years at [Wolverhampton Crown Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Crown_Court) in August 2012.[^cite_note-43]

### Transport policy

As part of the transport planning system, the Regional Assembly is under statutory requirement to produce a [regional transport strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_transport_strategy?action=edit\&redlink=1) (RTS) to provide long term planning for transport in the region. This involves region-wide transport schemes such as those carried out by [Highways England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_England) and [Network Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_Rail).[^cite_note-GoWM1-44]

Within the region, the local transport authorities carry out transport planning through the use of a [local transport plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_transport_plan) (LTP) which outlines their strategies, policies and implementation programme.[^cite_note-DfT1-45] The most recent LTP is that for the period 2006–11. In the West Midlands region, the following transport authorities have published their LTP online: [Herefordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herefordshire),[^cite_note-HLTP-46] [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire) U.A.,[^cite_note-ShLTP-47] [Staffordshire](58733.md),[^cite_note-StLTP-48] [Telford and Wrekin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telford_and_Wrekin) U.A.,[^cite_note-T\&WLTP-49] [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire),[^cite_note-WrLTP-50] [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\))[^cite_note-WMLTP-51] and [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire).[^cite_note-WcLTP-52] The transport authority of [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent) U.A. publishes a joint local transport plan in partnership with Staffordshire County Council to cover the North Staffordshire Major Urban Area, which includes Stoke-on-Trent and the more urban parts of [Newcastle-under-Lyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle-under-Lyme) and [Staffordshire Moorlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Moorlands).[^cite_note-SoTLTP-53]

## Sport

See also: [Sport in Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Birmingham)



St George's Park is the training ground of the [England national football team](9904.md).

The [National Sports Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sports_Centre_\(Sport_England\)) at [Lilleshall Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilleshall_Hall) is in [Sheriffhales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriffhales), Shropshire, north-east of Telford; it was largely established by the [Central Council of Physical Recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_and_Recreation_Alliance) in 1949 as a National Recreation Centre; the south of England had theirs at [Bisham Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisham_Abbey) on the Thames. [St George's Park National Football Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George's_Park_National_Football_Centre) is at [Tatenhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatenhill) near [Byrkley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byrkley_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Needwood Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needwood_Forest), near former the [RAF Tatenhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Tatenhill) off the [A515](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A515_road), four miles west of Burton upon Trent.

The [Tough Guy Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tough_Guy_Competition), now a widespread sport competition in the US, began in 1987 on a farm at [Perton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perton) in Staffordshire. The [Olympic Movement](15147.md) started at [Much Wenlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Much_Wenlock), and also to the east of region, where [Baron de Coubertin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_de_Coubertin) formulated his ideas for sport and the Olympics at [Rugby School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_School) in 1883, with the headmaster [Thomas Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Arnold), whose son would be the famous poet [Matthew Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Arnold) and whose great-grandson would be [Aldous Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldous_Huxley).

### Tennis

See also: [History of tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tennis)

Britain's first tennis club was founded in 1872 in [Leamington Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Leamington_Spa). The modern rules of lawn tennis were developed in 1874 by Leamington Tennis Club. Tennis was pioneered in Edgbaston in 1859, and [Edgbaston Archery and Lawn Tennis Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgbaston_Archery_and_Lawn_Tennis_Society) also claims to be the oldest tennis club in the world, where tennis was invented by Major [Harry Gem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Gem) and the Spaniard [Augurio Perera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augurio_Perera).
