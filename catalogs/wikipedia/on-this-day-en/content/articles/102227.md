The ***New York Post*** (***NY Post***), founded as the ***New York Evening Post*** (originally ***New-York Evening Post***), is an American [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States)[^cite_note-3] daily [tabloid newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_\(newspaper_format\)) published in [New York City](645042.md). The *Post* also operates three online sites: NYPost.com;[^cite_note-4] *[Page Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_Six)*, a gossip site; and [*Decider*](#Decider), an entertainment site. The newspaper was founded in 1801 by [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton), a [Federalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_Party) and [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States) who was appointed the nation's first [secretary of the treasury](43338.md) by [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington). Its most notable 19th-century editor was [William Cullen Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cullen_Bryant). The newspaper became a respected [broadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadsheet) in the 19th century.[^cite_note-5]

In the mid-20th century, the newspaper was owned by [Dorothy Schiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Schiff), who developed the tabloid format that has been used by the newspaper since. In 1976, [Rupert Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch)'s [News Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corp) bought the *Post* for US\$30.5 million (equivalent to \$173 million in 2025).[^cite_note-inflation-US-6][^cite_note-hollywoodreporter2005-7] Since its acquisition by News Corp, the *Post* has been frequently criticized over the years for its controversial headlines and editorial choices along with accusations of bias in its political coverage.[^cite_note-CJR-8]

As of 2026, the *New York Post* is the [third-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_United_States#Top_10_newspapers_by_subscribers_and_print_circulation) newspaper by print circulation among all U.S. newspapers.[^cite_note-9]

## History

### 19th century

Further information: [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton)



The *New York Post* was founded in 1801 by [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton), a [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Father_of_the_United_States) who [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) appointed as the nation's first [Secretary of the Treasury](43338.md).



Hamilton appointed [William Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Coleman_\(editor\)) as the newspaper's first editor in 1801; Coleman served in that capacity until his death in 1829.



[William Cullen Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cullen_Bryant), the *Post*'s most notable 19th-century editor

The *Post* was founded by Alexander Hamilton with about \$10,000, equivalent to \$193,500 in 2025,[^cite_note-inflation-US-6] from a group of investors in the autumn of 1801 as the *New-York Evening Post*,[^cite_note-10] a [broadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadsheet). Hamilton's co-investors included other New York members of the [Federalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_Party), including [Robert Troup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Troup) and [Oliver Wolcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Wolcott)[^cite_note-11] who were dismayed by the election of [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson) as [U.S. president](24113.md) and the rise in popularity of the [Democratic-Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-Republican_Party).[^cite_note-emery-12]<sup>: 74 </sup> At a meeting held at [Archibald Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Gracie)'s weekend villa, which is now [Gracie Mansion](1206268.md), Hamilton recruited the first investors for the new paper.[^cite_note-13] Hamilton chose [William Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Coleman_\(editor\)) as his first editor.[^cite_note-emery-12]<sup>: 74 </sup>

The most notable 19th-century *Evening Post* editor was the [abolitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States) and poet [William Cullen Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cullen_Bryant).[^cite_note-emery-12]<sup>: 90 </sup> So well respected was the *Evening Post* under Bryant's editorship, it received praise from the English philosopher [John Stuart Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stuart_Mill), in 1864.[^cite_note-14] "Except for the [*Tribune*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New-York_Tribune), it had more effect upon public policies than any other journal in the 1840's and 1850's".[^cite_note-15] In addition to literary and drama reviews, [William Leggett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Leggett_\(writer\)) began to write political editorials for the *Post*. Leggett espoused a fierce opposition to [central banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_banking) and support for the organization of labor unions. He was a member of the [Equal Rights Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locofocos). In 1831, he became a co-owner and editor of the *Post*,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:8-17] eventually working as sole editor of the newspaper while Bryant traveled in Europe in 1834 and 1835.[^cite_note-\:8-17]

One of the co-owners of the paper during this period was [John Bigelow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bigelow).[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-19] Born in [Malden-on-Hudson, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malden-on-Hudson,_New_York), Bigelow graduated in 1835 from Union College, where he was a member of the [Sigma Phi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Phi) Society and the Philomathean Society,[^cite_note-20] and was admitted to the bar in 1838.[^cite_note-\:0-18] From 1849 to 1861, he was one of the editors and co-owners of the *Evening Post*.[^cite_note-\:0-18] Another owner with Bryant and Bigelow was Isaac Henderson.[^cite_note-\:6-21] In 1877, this led to the involvement of his son [Isaac Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Henderson) Jr., who became the paper's publisher, stockholder, and member of its board, just five years after graduating from college.[^cite_note-\:7-22] Henderson Sr.'s 33-year tenure with the *Evening Post* ended in 1879, when it was learned that he had defrauded Bryant the entire time.[^cite_note-\:6-21] Henderson Jr. sold his interest in the newspaper in 1881.[^cite_note-\:7-22]

In 1881, [Henry Villard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Villard) took control of the *Evening Post* and *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation)*, which became the *Post*'s weekly edition. With this acquisition, the paper was managed by the triumvirate of [Carl Schurz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Schurz), [Horace White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_White_\(writer\)), and [Edwin L. Godkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Lawrence_Godkin).[^cite_note-23] When Schurz left the paper in 1883, Godkin became editor-in-chief.[^cite_note-24] White became editor-in-chief in 1899, and remained in that role until his retirement in 1903.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] In 1897, both publications passed to the management of Villard's son, [Oswald Garrison Villard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Garrison_Villard),[^cite_note-27] a founding member of the [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP);[^cite_note-28] he was also a founder of the [American Anti-Imperialist League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Anti-Imperialist_League).[^cite_note-emery-12]<sup>: 257 </sup>

### 20th century



A [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) passenger reading the *New York Post* in April 1974

Villard sold the newspaper in 1918 following widespread allegations of pro-[German](21212.md) sympathies during [World War I](4764461.md) hurt the newspaper's circulation. The new owner was [Thomas Lamont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_W._Lamont), a senior partner in the [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street) firm of [J.P. Morgan & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.P._Morgan_&_Co.) Unable to stem the paper's financial losses, he sold it to a [consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium) of 34 financial and reform political leaders, headed by [Edwin Francis Gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Francis_Gay), dean of the [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School), whose members included [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt). In 1924, conservative [Cyrus H. K. Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_H._K._Curtis),[^cite_note-29] publisher of the *[Ladies Home Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies_Home_Journal)*, purchased the *Evening Post*,[^cite_note-ketupa-30] and briefly turned it into a non-sensational [tabloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_journalism) nine years later, in 1933.[^cite_note-ketupa-30]

In 1928, [Wilella Waldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilella_Waldorf) became drama editor at the *Evening Post*. She was one of the first women to hold an editorial role at the newspaper,[^cite_note-31] During her time at the *Evening Post*, she was the only female first-string critic on a New York newspaper.[^cite_note-32] She was preceded by [Clara Savage Littledale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Littledale), the first woman reporter ever hired by the *Post* and the editor of the woman's page in 1914.[^cite_note-NAW-33] In 1934, [J. David Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._David_Stern) purchased the paper, changed its name to the *New York Post*,[^cite_note-ketupa-30] and restored its broadsheet size and liberal perspective.[^cite_note-emery-12]<sup>: 292 </sup> For four months of that same year, future [U.S. Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senator) from [Alaska](624.md) [Ernest Gruening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Gruening) was an editor of the paper. In 1939, [Dorothy Schiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Schiff) purchased the paper. Her husband [George Backer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Backer) was named editor and publisher.[^cite_note-34] Her second editor and third husband [Ted Thackrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Thackrey) became co-publisher and co-editor with Schiff in 1942.[^cite_note-35] Together, they recast the newspaper into its modern-day tabloid format.[^cite_note-emery-12]<sup>: 556 </sup>

In 1945, *[The Bronx Home News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx_Home_News)* merged with it.[^cite_note-36] In 1949, [James Wechsler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wechsler) became editor of the paper, running both the news and the editorial pages. In 1961, he turned over the news section to Paul Sann and stayed on as editorial page editor until 1980. Under Schiff's tenure the *Post* was seen to have liberal tilt, supporting trade unions and social welfare, and featured some of the most popular columnists of the time, such as [Joseph Cookman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cookman), [Drew Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Pearson_\(journalist\)), [Eleanor Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Roosevelt), [Max Lerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Lerner), [Murray Kempton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Kempton), [Pete Hamill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Hamill), and [Eric Sevareid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Sevareid), theater critic [Richard Watts Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Watts_Jr.), and gossip columnist [Earl Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Wilson_\(columnist\)). In November 1976, it was announced that Australian [Rupert Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch) had bought the *Post* from Schiff with the intention that Schiff would be retained as a consultant for five years.[^cite_note-\:9-37] In 2005, it was reported that Murdoch bought the newspaper for US\$30.5 million.[^cite_note-hollywoodreporter2005-7]

The *Post* at this point was the only surviving afternoon daily in New York City and its circulation under Schiff had grown by two-thirds, particularly after the failure of the competing *[World Journal Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_World_Journal_Tribune)*; however, the rising cost of operating an afternoon daily in a city with worsening daytime traffic congestion, combined with mounting competition from expanded local radio and TV news cut into the *Post*'s profitability, although it made money from 1949 until Schiff's final year of ownership, when it lost \$500,000. The paper has lost money ever since.[^cite_note-emery-12]<sup>: 74 </sup> In late October 1995, the *Post* announced plans to change its Monday through Saturday publication schedule and begin issuing a Sunday edition,[^cite_note-38] which it last published briefly in 1989.[^cite_note-39] On April 14, 1996, the *Post* delivered its new Sunday edition at the cost of 50 cents per paper by keeping its size to 120 pages.[^cite_note-Orlando-40] The amount, significantly less than Sunday editions from *[The New York Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Daily_News)* and *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, was part of the *Post*'s efforts "to find a niche in the nation's most competitive newspaper market".[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-Orlando-40]

Because of the institution of federal regulations limiting media [cross-ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_ownership) after Murdoch's purchase of WNEW-TV, which is now [WNYW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNYW), and four other stations from [Metromedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metromedia) to launch the [Fox Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company), Murdoch was forced to sell the paper for \$37.6 million in 1988, equivalent to \$102 million in 2025,[^cite_note-inflation-US-6] to [Peter S. Kalikow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_S._Kalikow), a real-estate magnate with no experience in the media industry.[^cite_note-MomentTruth-42] In 1988, the *Post* hired [Jane Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Amsterdam), founding editor of *[Manhattan, inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan,_inc.)*, as its first female editor, and within six months the paper had toned down the sensationalist headlines.[^cite_note-43] Within a year, Amsterdam was forced out by Kalikow, who reportedly told her "credible doesn't sell...Your big scoops are great, but they don't sell more papers."[^cite_note-44]

In 1993, after Kalikow declared bankruptcy,[^cite_note-MomentTruth-42] the paper was temporarily managed by [Steven Hoffenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Hoffenberg),[^cite_note-MomentTruth-42] a financier who later pleaded guilty to [securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_\(finance\)) [fraud](58790.md),[^cite_note-45] and for two weeks by [Abe Hirschfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Hirschfeld),[^cite_note-46] who made his fortune building parking garages. Following a staff revolt against the Hoffenberg-Hirschfeld partnership, which included publication of an issue whose front page featured the iconic masthead picture of founder [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton) with a single teardrop running down his cheek,[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] the *Post* was again purchased in 1993 by Murdoch's News Corporation. This came about after numerous political officials, including Democratic governor of New York [Mario Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Cuomo), persuaded the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) to grant Murdoch a permanent waiver from the cross-ownership rules that had forced him to sell the paper five years earlier. Without this FCC ruling, the paper would have shut down.[^cite_note-MomentTruth-42]

### 21st century

In December 2012, Murdoch announced that Jesse Angelo had been appointed publisher.[^cite_note-49] Various branches of Murdoch's media groups, [21st Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Fox)'s [Endemol Shine North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemol_Shine_North_America), and [News Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corp)'s *New York Post* created a *Page Six TV* nightly gossip show based on and named after the *Post*'s gossip section. A test run in July would occur on [Fox Television Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Television_Stations).[^cite_note-50] The show garnered the highest ratings of a nationally syndicated entertainment newsmagazine in a decade when it debuted in 2017.[^cite_note-51] With *Page Six TV*'s success, the *New York Post* formed New York Post Entertainment, a scripted and unscripted television entertainment division, in July 2018 with Troy Searer as president.[^cite_note-52]

In 2017, the *New York Post* was reported to be the preferred newspaper of President [Donald Trump](4848272.md),[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-haberman-2017-54] who maintains frequent contact with its owner Murdoch.[^cite_note-haberman-2017-54] The *Post* promoted Trump's celebrity since at least the 1980s.[^cite_note-55] In October 2020, the *Post* endorsed Trump for re-election, citing his "promises made, promises kept" policy.[^cite_note-56] Weeks after Trump was defeated and [sought to overturn the election results](65905202.md), the *Post* published a front-page editorial, asking Trump to "stop the insanity", stating that he was "cheering for an undemocratic coup", writing, "If you insist on spending your final days in office threatening to burn it all down, that will be how you are remembered. Not as a revolutionary, but as the anarchist holding the match." The *Post* characterized Trump attorney [Sidney Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Powell) as a "crazy person", and his former national security advisor [Michael Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Flynn)'s suggestion to declare martial law as "tantamount to treason."[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

In January 2021, Keith Poole, a top editor at *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_\(United_Kingdom\))*, another Murdoch-owned tabloid, was appointed as the editor in chief of the *New York Post* Group.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-Grynbaum-61] Around the same time, at least eight journalists had left the paper.[^cite_note-Grynbaum-61] In January 2025, [Tubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubi) released a *New York Post* documentary about [Luigi Mangione](78560779.md) titled *New York Post Presents: Luigi Mangione Monster or Martyr?*[^cite_note-O'Neill_2025-62][^cite_note-Vena_2025-63]

The *Post* has been criticized since the beginning of Murdoch's ownership for [sensationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensationalism), blatant [advocacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy), and [conservative bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_bias). In 1980, the *[Columbia Journalism Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Journalism_Review)* stated that the "*New York Post* is no longer merely a journalistic problem. It is a social problem—a force for evil."[^cite_note-CJR-8] In 2007, former Page Six reporter Ian Spiegelman alleged that the *Post's* Page Six section had shaped its coverage to suit Murdoch's business needs, including by suppressing unfavorable stories about [the Clintons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_family) and the government of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md), where Murdoch has invested heavily in satellite television. The Post rejected the allegations as false.[^cite_note-PageSix-64][^cite_note-65]

In a 2019 article in *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)*, [Ken Auletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Auletta) wrote that Murdoch "doesn't hesitate to use the *Post* to belittle his business opponents", and went on to say that Murdoch's support for [Edward I. Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I._Koch) while he was running for mayor of New York "spilled over onto the news pages of the *Post*, with the paper regularly publishing glowing stories about Koch and sometimes savage accounts of his four primary opponents."[^cite_note-66]

According to *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, [Ronald Reagan](25433.md)'s campaign team credited Murdoch and the *Post* for his victory in New York in the [1980 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_U.S._presidential_election).[^cite_note-67] Reagan later "waived a prohibition against owning a television station and a newspaper in the same market", allowing Murdoch to continue to control the *New York Post* and *[The Boston Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Herald)* while expanding into television.

In 1997, *Post* executive editor [Steven D. Cuozzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_D._Cuozzo) responded to criticism by saying that the *Post* "broke the elitist media stranglehold on the national agenda."[^cite_note-68] In a 2004 survey conducted by [Pace University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pace_University), the *Post* was rated the least-credible major news outlet in New York, and the only news outlet to receive more responses calling it "not credible" than credible (44% not credible to 39% credible).[^cite_note-69]

The *Post* commonly publishes news reports based entirely on reporting from other sources without independent corroboration. In January 2021, the paper forbade the use of [CNN](62028.md), [MSNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC), *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*, and *The New York Times* as sole sources for such stories.[^cite_note-70] Susan Mulcahy and Frank DiGiacomo's 2024 history was titled "*Paper of Wreckage*" after staffers' nickname for the *Post*, which was a pun on the term "[paper of record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_of_record)".[^cite_note-s205-71]

### Sports coverage

The *Post* has gained notoriety over the years for its sports coverage. In contrast to the *Daily News*, the *Post* has been known to retain longstanding writers for decades. Writers like [Steve Serby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Serby) (since 1972) and [Phil Mushnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Mushnick) (1973) have spent almost their entire careers with the paper.[^cite_note-72]

### Style

"Headless body in topless bar" redirects here. For the film inspired by the story, see [*Headless Body in Topless Bar*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headless_Body_in_Topless_Bar).



The April 15, 1983, edition of the *New York Post* featured one of the newspaper's most famous headlines.

Murdoch imported the style of many of his Australian and British newspapers, such as *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_\(United_Kingdom\))*, which remains one of the highest selling daily newspapers in the United Kingdom. This style, known as [tabloid journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_journalism),[^cite_note-73] is exemplified by the *Post*'s famous headlines such as "Headless body in topless bar" (written by [Vincent Musetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Musetto)); like its rival, the *[Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Daily_News)*, the paper has since become known for its many puns, particularly on its back and/or front covers. In its 35th-anniversary edition, *[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_\(magazine\))* magazine listed this as one of the greatest headlines. It also has five other *Post* headlines in its "Greatest Tabloid Headlines" list.[^cite_note-74] The *Post* has also been criticized for incendiary front-page headlines, such as one referring to the co-chairmen of the [Iraq Study Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Study_Group)—[James Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Baker) and [Lee Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_H._Hamilton)—as "[surrender monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese-eating_surrender_monkeys)",[^cite_note-75] and another on the murder of landlord [Menachem Stark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Menachem_Stark) reading "[Slumlord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slumlord) found burned in dumpster. Who *didn't* want him dead?"[^cite_note-76]

### Page Six

Main article: [Page Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_Six)

Not to be confused with [Page 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_6).

The *Post*'s influential gossip section **Page Six** began in 1977.[^cite_note-digiacomo200412-77] Created by [James Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brady_\(columnist\)),[^cite_note-78] it was famous for its [blind items](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_item). Beginning in 1985, columnist [Richard Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Johnson_\(columnist\)) edited Page Six for 25 years[^cite_note-79] before British journalist [Emily Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Smith_\(editor\)) replaced him in 2009.[^cite_note-80] In June 2022, Smith was replaced by her deputy, Ian Mohr.[^cite_note-81] February 2006 saw the debut of *Page Six Magazine*, distributed free inside the paper. In September 2007, it started to be distributed weekly in the Sunday edition of the paper. In January 2009, publication of *Page Six Magazine* was cut to four times a year.[^cite_note-82]

Beginning with the 2017–18 television season, a daily [syndicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_syndication) series known as ***Page Six TV*** came to air, produced by [20th Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Television), which was part of the [21st Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Fox) side of Rupert Murdoch's holdings, and [Endemol Shine North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemol_Shine_North_America). The show was originally hosted by comedian [John Fugelsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fugelsang), with contributions from Page Six and *Post* writers (including Carlos Greer), along with regular panelists [Elizabeth Wagmeister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Wagmeister) from *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* and [Bevy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bevy_Smith). In March 2018, Fugelsang left the show, with the expectation that a new host would be named, although by the end of the season it was announced that Wagmeister, Greer and Smith would be retained as equal co-hosts.[^cite_note-83] In April 2019, it was confirmed that the series would end after May 2019; by then, it was last in average viewership out of all U.S. syndicated newsmagazine programs, behind the similar tabloid-inspired program *[Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mail) TV*.[^cite_note-84]

### Erroneous reporting and defamation cases arising from bombings

[Richard Jewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Jewell), a security guard wrongly suspected of being the [Centennial Olympic Park bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_Olympic_Park_bombing), sued the *Post* in 1998, alleging that the newspaper had libeled him in several articles, headlines, photographs, and [editorial cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_cartoon). U.S. District Judge [Loretta Preska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Preska) largely denied the *Post*'s motion to dismiss, allowing the suit to proceed.[^cite_note-85] The *Post* subsequently settled the case for an undisclosed sum.[^cite_note-86]

In several stories on the day of the 2013 [Boston Marathon bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon_bombing), the *Post* inaccurately reported that twelve people had died, and that a [Saudi](349303.md) national had been taken into custody as a suspect, which was denied by the [Boston Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Police_Department).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-Guardian-88] Three days later, on April 18, the *Post* featured a full-page cover photo of two young men at the Boston marathon with the headline "[Bag Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagman)" (a term that implies criminality) and erroneously claimed they were being sought by police.[^cite_note-Guardian-88][^cite_note-CJR2-89][^cite_note-90] The men, Salaheddin Barhoum and Yassine Zaimi, were not considered suspects, and the *Post* was heavily criticized for the apparent accusation.[^cite_note-CJR2-89][^cite_note-91] Then-editor [Col Allan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_Allan) defended the story, saying they had not referred to the men as "suspects".[^cite_note-CJR2-89][^cite_note-92] The two men later sued the *Post* for [libel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libel),[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] and the suit was settled in 2014 on undisclosed terms.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

### Accusations of racism

In 1989, the *Post* described the five black and Latino teenagers arrested following [the rape and assault of a white woman in Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park_jogger_case) as coming "from a world of crack, welfare, guns, knives, indifference, and ignorance ... a land of no fathers", and having set out "to smash, hurt, rob, stomp, rape" people who were "rich" and "white".[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] The teenagers' convictions were later overturned after the confession of a serial rapist, which was confirmed with DNA evidence.

In 2009, the *Post* ran a cartoon by [Sean Delonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Delonas) of a white police officer saying to another white police officer who has just shot a [chimpanzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_chimpanzee) on the street, "They'll have to find someone else to write the next [stimulus bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Recovery_and_Reinvestment_Act_of_2009)."[^cite_note-CNN021809c-102] Comparing Obama, a Black president, to a chimpanzee was criticized as [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racist),[^cite_note-CNN021809c-102] with civil rights activist [Al Sharpton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sharpton) calling the cartoon "troubling at best given the historic racist attacks of African-Americans as being synonymous with monkeys".[^cite_note-103] New York Post chairman Rupert Murdoch apologized but said the cartoon was only meant to mock a piece of legislation.[^cite_note-104] The [Public Enemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Enemy_\(band\)) song "A Letter to the *New York Post*" from their album *[Apocalypse '91...The Enemy Strikes Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse_'91...The_Enemy_Strikes_Black)* is a complaint about what they believed to be negative and inaccurate coverage Black people received from the paper.[^cite_note-105]

In 2019, the *Post* displayed an image of the World Trade Center in flames targeting Rep. [Ilhan Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar), one of the first two Muslim women to serve in Congress. The image had been displayed due to Ms. Omar's widely criticized quote "Some people did something" which was viewed by many as insensitive and minimizing the 9/11 World Trade Center attacks.[^cite_note-106] The Yemeni American Merchant Association announced a formal boycott of the paper and ten of the most prominent Yemeni [bodega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodega) owners in New York agreed to stop selling the paper. As of June 2019, the boycott had extended to over 900 individual stores.[^cite_note-107] Yemeni-Americans own about half of the 10,000 bodegas in New York City.[^cite_note-108]

### Hunter Biden laptop story

Further information: [Hunter Biden laptop controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Biden_laptop_controversy)

On October 14, 2020, three weeks before the [2020 United States presidential election](48410011.md), the *Post* published a front-page story purporting to reveal "smoking gun" emails recovered from a [laptop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laptop) abandoned by [Hunter Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Biden) at a computer repair store in [Wilmington, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware).[^cite_note-\:2-109] The only sources named in the story were Trump personal attorney [Rudy Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Giuliani) and strategy advisor [Steve Bannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bannon).[^cite_note-\:2-109] The story came under heavy criticism from other news sources and anonymous reporters at the *Post* itself for "flimsy" reporting, including questions about the reliability of its sourcing and the lack of outreach to either Hunter Biden or the [Joe Biden](145422.md) campaign for pre-publication comment.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

In October 2020, over fifty former U.S. intelligence officials signed an open letter stating that they were "deeply suspicious that the Russian government played a significant role" in the story, but emphasized that "we do not know if the emails ... are genuine or not and that we do not have evidence of Russian involvement."[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] [John Ratcliffe](42699041.md), the [Director of National Intelligence](456082.md), said during a [Fox News](11121.md) interview that "the intelligence community doesn't believe that \[the emails originated from Russian disinformation] because there is no intelligence that supports that."[^cite_note-114] Ratcliffe, a Trump loyalist, had previously made public assertions that contradicted professional intelligence assessments.[^cite_note-115]

The [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI) took possession of the laptop in late 2019 and reported that they had "nothing to add" to Ratcliffe's remarks concerning Russian disinformation.[^cite_note-Goldman-116] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported days after the *Post* story that "no concrete evidence has emerged that the laptop contains Russian disinformation". Amid mounting pressure, the FBI wrote to U.S. Senator [Ron Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Johnson), suggesting it had not found any Russian disinformation on the laptop. It was unclear what [Justice Department](52563.md) officials knew about the FBI investigation at the time.[^cite_note-Goldman-116] Fox News reported that the laptop was seized as part of an investigation into money laundering, but did not make clear if the investigation involved Hunter Biden.[^cite_note-Singman-117] On December 9, 2020, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that investigators had initially examined possible money laundering by Hunter Biden but did not find evidence to justify further investigation.[^cite_note-118]

Following the [2016 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_U.S._presidential_election), social media companies were criticized for allowing false political information to proliferate on their platforms, including from Russian intelligence, suggesting it may have assisted Trump's election.[^cite_note-119] [Twitter](9988187.md) and [Facebook](7529378.md) initially limited the spread of the 2020 *Post* story on their platforms, citing supposed policies restricting the sharing of hacked material and personal information; Twitter also temporarily suspended the *Post*'s account. This decision proved controversial, with many critics, including Republican senator [Ted Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz), deriding it as censorship.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-\:3-121] NPR reported that Twitter initially declined to comment how it reached this decision or what evidence it had supporting this.[^cite_note-\:3-121] *The New York Times* initially reported that the story had been pitched to other outlets, including Fox News, which declined to publish it due to concerns over its reliability.[^cite_note-\:1-122]

The *Times* also reported that two writers at the *Post* declined to have their names attached to the story, and ultimately the story only listed two bylines, Gabrielle Fonrouge, who "had little to do with the reporting or writing of the article" and was unaware of her byline prior to the story's publication, and Emma-Jo Morris, a former producer for Fox News's *[Hannity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannity)* who had no prior bylines with the *Post*. In response to the concerns about the veracity of the article, retired *Post* editor-in-chief and current advisor [Col Allan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_Allan) responded in an email to the *New York Times* that "the senior editors at *The Post* made the decision to publish the Biden files after several days' hard work established its merit."[^cite_note-\:1-122] Giuliani said he gave the story to the *Post* because "either nobody else would take it, or if they took it, they would spend all the time they could to try to contradict it before they put it out."[^cite_note-\:1-122] The accuracy of the Hunter Biden laptop story resulted in increased scrutiny of Twitter and Facebook limiting the spread of the story by conservatives, who argued that their actions "proves Big Tech's bias".[^cite_note-\:23-123][^cite_note-124]

On October 30, 2020, [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News) reported, "no evidence has emerged that the documents are the product of Russian disinformation, as some experts initially suggested, but many questions remain about how the materials got into the hands of Trump's lawyer [Rudy Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Giuliani), who had met with Russian agents in his effort to dig up dirt on Biden."[^cite_note-125] On March 15, 2021, [CNN](62028.md) reported that Giuliani and other Trump allies met with Ukrainian lawmaker [Andrii Derkach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrii_Derkach), who the U.S. government later assessed was a longtime Russian intelligence agent, sanctioning him for distributing disinformation about President Biden.[^cite_note-126]

On March 27, 2022, *[Vox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vox_\(website\))* reported that no evidence had emerged that "the laptop's leak was a Russian plot".[^cite_note-\:12-127] In March 2022, *The New York Times* and *The Washington Post* confirmed that some of the emails were authentic.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-\:12-127][^cite_note-129] In April 2022, the editorial board of *The Washington Post* wrote the Biden laptop story provided "an opportunity for a reckoning" by American media to ensure "accurate and relevant" stories are covered. They noted:

> The investigation adds new details and confirms old ones about the ways in which Joe Biden's family has profited from trading overseas on his name—something for which the president deserves criticism for tacitly condoning. What it does not do, despite some conservatives' insistence otherwise, is prove that President Biden acted corruptly.[^cite_note-Board_4/3/2022-130]

On April 28, 2022, [Joan Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Donovan), the research director of the [Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorenstein_Center_on_Media,_Politics_and_Public_Policy) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), said that "This is arguably the most well-known story the *New York Post* has ever published and it endures as a story because it was initially suppressed by social media companies and jeered by politicians and pundits alike".[^cite_note-\:23-123]

In 1997, a national news story concerning [Rebecca Sealfon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Sealfon)'s victory in the [Scripps National Spelling Bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_National_Spelling_Bee) circulated. Sealfon was sponsored by the *[Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_News_\(New_York\))*, a direct in-market competitor. The *Post* published a picture of her but altered the photograph to remove the name of the *Daily News* as printed on a placard she was wearing.[^cite_note-131] In 2004, the *Post* ran a full-page cover photo of 19-year-old [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) student Diana Chien jumping to her death from the twenty-fourth story of a building. University spokesman John Beckman commented "it seems to show an appalling lack of judgment and insensitivity to the young woman's family and a disregard for the feelings of students at NYU."[^cite_note-132]

In 2012, the *Post* was criticized for running a photograph of a man struggling to climb back up onto a [subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) platform as a train approached, along with the headline "DOOMED".[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] Facing questions over why he did not help the man, the photographer claimed he was not strong enough and had been attempting to use the flash on his camera to alert the driver of the oncoming train.[^cite_note-136] In December 2020, the *Post* published a story outing an [emergency medical technician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_technician) who made additional income from posting explicit photographs of herself to the subscription website [OnlyFans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OnlyFans).[^cite_note-medic-outing-rollingstone-137][^cite_note-138] The publication was widely criticized on social media as "[doxxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doxxing) someone simply for trying to earn a living."[^cite_note-medic-outing-rollingstone-137]

In April 2021, Facebook blocked users from sharing a *Post* story about home real estate purchases by [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) co-founder [Patrisse Cullors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrisse_Cullors), saying that it violated its privacy and personal information policy.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] In response, the *Post* argued that it was an arbitrary decision since other newspapers, magazines and websites highlight the real estate purchases of high status individuals.[^cite_note-141] [News Media Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Media_Alliance) CEO David Chavern also voiced criticism of the decision, saying in a prepared statement: "There is no balance of power between 'media' and 'Big Tech.'"[^cite_note-142]

In April 2021, the *Post* published a false front-page story asserting that copies of a book by Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) were being distributed to migrant children at an intake facility in Long Beach, California.[^cite_note-\:4-143] Fox News then published a story about the matter, followed by numerous Republican politicians and pundits commenting on it, in some cases speculating that taxpayers were funding the supposed book handouts for Harris's personal profit.[^cite_note-\:4-143][^cite_note-144] Responding to questions from Fox News correspondent [Peter Doocy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Doocy), White House press secretary [Jen Psaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jen_Psaki) expressed no knowledge of the matter; the *Post* then published a new story headlined "Psaki has no answers when asked about Harris' book being given to child migrants."[^cite_note-\:5-145] Four days after the original publication, the *Post* replaced the story with a new version clarifying that just one Harris book had been donated by a community member but maintained that it was an "open-arms gesture by the Biden administration", although there was no evidence of the administration's involvement.[^cite_note-\:5-145] Laura Italiano, the author of the story, resigned that day, asserting she had been "ordered" to write it.[^cite_note-Grynbaum-61][^cite_note-\:5-145]

In October 2022, a rogue employee of the *Post* published a series of racist, violent and sexually explicit headlines on its Twitter account. Shortly after these headlines appeared, a spokesperson for the *Post* stated that the "vile and reprehensible" headlines were the result of a [hack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_hacker) and were immediately removed, and that the incident was under investigation. The spokesperson later stated that "the unauthorized conduct was committed by an employee, and the employee has been terminated."[^cite_note-146] In May 2023, amid reports that a wave of migrants might soon cross the American southern border, the *Post* ran a front-page story stating that 20 homeless veterans had been ordered to vacate [upstate New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_New_York) hotels to make room for arriving migrants. Fox News and other conservative outlets sent the story viral, with numerous conservatives expressing outrage at [President Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Joe_Biden) and other Democrats. The story was soon found to have been fabricated by a local veterans advocate.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

While attending a June 2024 [G7 Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50th_G7_summit) in Italy, G7 leaders watched an exhibition of military parachutists jump from aircraft and land nearby. After the exhibition, President Joe Biden stepped away from the group to approach some parachutists to speak and give them a thumbs-up. The *Post* tweeted a cropped version of a video that did not show the parachutists, creating a false impression that Biden had wandered off in confusion. The paper ran a full front-page story the next day, asserting "Biden embarrasses US with confused wanderings at world conference". [Fox News](11121.md) ran a segment on the *Post* story, displaying the front-page on air. *The Washington Post* factchecker assigned the story [Four Pinocchios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Kessler_\(journalist\)#Washington_Post_Fact_Checker), designating it as an outright lie.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

In September 2024, the *[Milwaukee Journal Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Journal_Sentinel)* found that several of the *New York Post*'s stories about Wisconsin politics had been authored by an individual with no clear previous journalism experience and extensive ties to the [state's Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Wisconsin). These included two recent 2024 stints completing consulting work for that party; 2023 consulting work for [Dan Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kelly_\(Wisconsin_judge\)), a conservative state Supreme Court candidate; and being the campaign manager for a state [Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Assembly) campaign in 2024.[^cite_note-151]

## Oldest claim

The *New York Post* was established in 1801 by [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton) making it the oldest still published daily newspaper in the US.[^cite_note-152] However, it is not the oldest continuously published paper, as the *New York Post* halted publication during strikes in 1958 and [in 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_New_York_City_newspaper_strike). If this is considered, *[The Providence Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Providence_Journal)* is the oldest continuously published daily newspaper in the US.[^cite_note-153] The *[Hartford Courant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_Courant)* is generally understood to be the oldest newspaper in America, as it was founded in 1764; however, it was founded as a semi-weekly paper and did not begin publishing daily until 1836, 35 years after the *New York Post* began doing so.[^cite_note-154]

## Operations



The printing plant of the *New York Post* in [The Bronx](3338.md) in August 2010

The 1906 [Old New York Evening Post Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_New_York_Evening_Post_Building) is a designated landmark. It was added to the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in 1977.[^cite_note-nris-155] It occupied the building until 1926 when a new main office for the *Post* was established at 75 West Street in the [New York Evening Post Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Evening_Post_Building). The building remained in use by the *Post* until 1970, it was added to the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in 2000.[^cite_note-nris-155]

In 1967, Schiff bought 210 South Street, the former headquarters of the *[New York Journal American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Journal_American)*, which closed a year earlier. The building became an instantly recognizable symbol for the *Post*. In 1995, owner [Rupert Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch) relocated the *Post*'s news and business offices to the [News Corporation headquarters tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1211_Avenue_of_the_Americas) at 1211 [Avenue of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) (Sixth Avenue) in midtown Manhattan. The *Post* shares this building with [Fox News Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News_Channel) and *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, which are also owned by Murdoch. From 2001 until 2021, both the *Post* and the New York City edition of the *Journal* were printed at a state-of-the-art printing plant in [The Bronx](3338.md).[^cite_note-156] The [Newspaper and Mail Deliverers Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_and_Mail_Deliverers_Union) has delivered the newspaper "since the early 1900s".[^cite_note-NYTobit-157]

### Website and Internet-Exclusive Articles

In 1996, the *New York Post* launched an [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) version of the paper.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

In September 2021, the paper launched *Post Sports+*. Featuring exclusive stories only available to subscribers, the service also boasts "in-depth" team-focused newsletters and the ability to text-message writers directly.[^cite_note-160]

### *Decider*

The *New York Post* launched the website *Decider* in 2014 to provide recommendations for streaming services. The website's first and only editor-in-chief is Mark Graham.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] Graham said that this service would "strike a nice balance between visual imagery and the written word, and come from a place of pop culture omniscience."[^cite_note-163] In 2019, *Decider* signed a deal with app provider *Reelgood* to provide Reelgood widget links at the bottom of each review and would channel some advertising revenue to both companies. The value of the deal was not disclosed.[^cite_note-164]

### Sales

The daily circulation of the *Post* decreased in the final years of the Schiff era from 700,000 around 1967–68, to approximately 517,000 by the time she sold the paper to Murdoch in 1976.[^cite_note-\:9-37] Under Murdoch, the *Post* launched a morning edition to compete directly with the rival tabloid *[Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Daily_News)* in 1978, prompting the *Daily News* to retaliate with a PM edition called *Daily News Tonight*. But the PM edition suffered the same problems with worsening daytime traffic that the afternoon *Post* experienced and the *Daily News* ultimately folded *Tonight* in 1981.[^cite_note-165] By that time, circulation of the all-day *Post* soared to a peak of 962,000, the bulk of the increase attributed to its morning edition (It set a single-day record of 1.1 million on August 11, 1977, with the news of the arrest the night before of [David Berkowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Berkowitz), the infamous "Son of Sam" serial killer who terrorized New York for much of that summer).

The *Post* and the *Daily News* have been locked in a bitter circulation war ever since. A resurgence during the first decade of the 21st century saw *Post* circulation rise to 724,748 by April 2007,[^cite_note-circ-166] achieved partly by lowering the price from 50 cents to 25 cents. In October 2006, the *Post* surpassed the *Daily News* in circulation for the first time, only to see the *Daily News* overtake its rival a few months later.[^cite_note-167] In 2010, the *Post*'s daily circulation was 525,004, just 10,000 behind the *Daily News*.[^cite_note-168] As of 2026, the *Post* was the third-largest newspaper in the United States by print circulation, while the *Daily News* was no longer in the top 25.[^cite_note-169]

The *Post* has remained unprofitable since Murdoch first purchased it from Dorothy Schiff in 1976, and was on the brink of folding when Murdoch bought it back in 1993, with at least one media report in 2012 indicating that *Post* loses up to \$70 million a year.[^cite_note-170] One commentator suggested that the *Post* cannot become profitable as long as the competing *Daily News* survives, and that Murdoch may be trying to force the *Daily News* to fold or sell out, leaving the two papers in an intractable [war of attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_warfare).[^cite_note-171] In September 2022, *The New York Post* became profitable, posting a profit for the quarter and year to date.[^cite_note-172] The *Post*'s digital network reached approximately 198 million unique users in June 2022, compared to 123 million in the prior year.[^cite_note-173]
