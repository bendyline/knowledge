**Okinawa Prefecture** ([Japanese](15606.md): 沖縄県, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Okinawa-ken*; pronounced [\[o.kʲi.na.wa(ꜜ.keɴ)\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese);[^cite_note-2] [Okinawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_language): ウチナーチン, romanized: *Uchinaachin*[^cite_note-3]) is the southernmost and westernmost prefecture of [Japan](15573.md).[^cite_note-4] It consists of three main island groups—the [Okinawa Islands](2113477.md), the [Sakishima Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakishima_Islands), and the [Daitō Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dait%C5%8D_Islands)—spread across a maritime zone approximately 1,000 kilometers east to west and 400 kilometers north to south. Despite a modest land area of 2,281 km<sup>2</sup> (880 sq mi), Okinawa's territorial extent over surrounding seas makes its total area nearly half the combined size of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu), [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku), and [Kyushu](16914.md).[^cite_note-menseki-5] Of its 160 [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island), 49 are inhabited.[^cite_note-6] The largest and most populous island is Okinawa Island, which hosts the capital city, [Naha](6792923.md), as well as major urban centers such as [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_\(city\)), [Uruma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruma), and [Urasoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urasoe,_Okinawa).[^cite_note-7] The prefecture has a [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate), characterized by warm temperatures and high rainfall throughout the year.[^cite_note-8] People from the [Nansei Islands](8458939.md), including [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island), the [Sakishima Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakishima_Islands),[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] and parts of [Kagoshima Prefecture](217439.md),[^cite_note-11] are often collectively referred to as [Ryukyuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuans).[^cite_note-12] However, there are significant cultural and customary differences between individual islands and even between local communities.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

Historically the center of the [Ryukyu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Kingdom), Okinawa long served as a maritime trading hub and cultural gateway; the kingdom participated in the [Chinese tributary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China)—maintaining formal tribute relations with the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) and [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty)—and retained distinct statehood until it was incorporated into Japan as Okinawa Prefecture in 1879 following the [Ryukyu Disposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Disposition).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] After the [Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) (1945), the islands were under U.S. administration until reversion to Japan in 1972, and today host a large share of U.S. military facilities in Japan (around 70% of the land area exclusively used by U.S. forces in Japan and amounting to about 10% of the prefecture's total land area), a continuing source of local and national debate.[^cite_note-18] A small but persistent [independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_independence_movement) exists, reflecting Okinawa's distinct historical trajectory and identity.[^cite_note-19]

Over time, Okinawa developed its own distinctive traditions, cuisine, and performing arts. Today, [Okinawan music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_music), characterized by the distinctive *[sanshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanshin)* instrument,[^cite_note-20] and festivals such as *[Eisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisa_\(dance\))* have gained popularity across the country.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The islands are also the birthplace of [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate), later popularized on the Japanese mainland and worldwide.[^cite_note-23] The prefectural economy is predominantly service-based, with [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Japan) and related services as major drivers, while manufacturing plays a comparatively small role.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Prehistoric and ancient history

The prehistoric history of Okinawa differs significantly from that of mainland Japan. Prior to written records, it is generally divided into two periods: [the Paleolithic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic_era) and [the Shellmidden period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellmidden_Period) (Kaizuka period).[^cite_note-rekishi-26]<sup>: 14 </sup> The earliest evidence of human activity in Okinawa includes the [Yamashita Cave Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamashita_Cave_Man), dating back approximately 32,000 years, and the [Minatogawa Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minatogawa_Man) from around 18,000 years ago.[^cite_note-rekishi-26]<sup>: 14 </sup> In 2012, the world's oldest known fishhook was discovered in the [Sakitari Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakitari_Cave?action=edit\&redlink=1) site in [Nanjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanj%C5%8D) City, Okinawa Prefecture.[^cite_note-27]

The [Shellmidden Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellmidden_Period) in Okinawa roughly corresponds to the [Jōmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_period) through [Heian periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period) of mainland [Japan](15573.md), with a chronological gap of about 10,000 years from the Paleolithic era.[^cite_note-\:0-28]

At the [Kogachibaru Shell Mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kogachibaru_Shell_Mound?action=edit\&redlink=1), archaeological findings include pottery styles such as [Sobata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobata_style_pottery?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Ichiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichiki_style_pottery?action=edit\&redlink=1), which originate from the [Kyushu](16914.md) region, obsidian from [Koshidake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshidake_\(Japanese_mountain\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Saga Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saga_Prefecture), and jade from [Itoigawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itoigawa) in [Niigata Prefecture](181660.md) used for ornamental pendants. These discoveries indicate that close exchanges with [Kyushu](16914.md) were already taking place during this time.[^cite_note-\:0-28][^cite_note-29]

However, by the middle of the [Shellmidden Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellmidden_Period), Okinawan pottery began to exhibit clearly localized characteristics. Examples include the Iha and Ogido pottery styles, which are distinct to the region.[^cite_note-\:0-28]

In contrast to the Shellmidden culture of Okinawa Island, which was influenced primarily by mainland Japan, the prehistoric cultures of the [Miyako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyako_Island) and [Yaeyama Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaeyama_Islands) were shaped more significantly by southern cultures, including those from the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 19 </sup>

### Gusuku and Sanzan periods



Ruins of [Katsuren Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katsuren_Castle)

From the 12th century onward, Okinawa entered the [Gusuku period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusuku_Period), characterized by the development of an [agrarian society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarian_society). During this time, populations moved from coastal dunes to more fertile limestone plateaus, leading to significant population growth and the beginnings of international trade.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 24 </sup> Local chieftains, known as [Aji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aji_\(Ryukyu\)), constructed fortified residences called [Gusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusuku) to protect their territories and expand their influence through foreign trade.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 26 </sup> Gusuku sites are found throughout the Ryukyu Islands, from the Amami Islands in the north to the Yaeyama Islands in the south, with estimates ranging from 300 to 400 sites in total. Early Gusuku were generally small, covering about 1,000 square meters, but larger fortresses appeared in later periods.[^cite_note-rekishi-26]<sup>: 48 </sup>

By the 14th century, Okinawa Island was divided into three polities, marking the beginning of the [Sanzan period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanzan_period). These were the [Kingdom of Hokuzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hokuzan), centered at [Nakijin Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakijin_Castle) in the north; the [Kingdom of Chūzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABzan), centered at [Urasoe Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urasoe_Castle) in the central region; and the [Kingdom of Nanzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzan), centered at [Ōzato Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Czato_Castle) in the south.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 26 </sup>

According to official histories compiled by the royal government in [Shuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle)—such as the *Chūzan Seikan*, *Chūzan Seifu*, and *Kyūyō*—the first royal lineage of Ryukyu was the legendary [Tenson dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenson_dynasty). After internal conflict during its 25th generation, a local Aji named [Shunten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunten) from [Urasoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urasoe,_Okinawa) was supported by the people, quelled the unrest, and was crowned as the first king of the Ryukyu Kingdom.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 30 </sup> However, these early historical accounts are heavily mythologized, and even if Shunten was a real historical figure, he likely ruled only the Urasoe area as an Aji.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 30 </sup> The Shunten dynasty lasted for three generations before being overthrown by the [Eiso dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiso_dynasty), which in turn was replaced by the [Satto dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satto_dynasty) after four generations. By this time, Okinawa Island had effectively split into the three kingdoms of Hokuzan, Chūzan, and Nanzan.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 34 </sup>

In 1372, the Ming dynasty of China dispatched an envoy, [Yang Zai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Zai?action=edit\&redlink=1), to the Kingdom of Chūzan, requesting the king, [Satto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satto), to enter into a tributary relationship. Satto agreed, and soon after, the kings of Nanzan (Chōsatto) and Hokuzan (Hanishi) also began paying tribute to the Ming court, bringing all three kingdoms into the [Chinese tributary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_tributary_system).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 36 </sup>

In 1406, the Aji of Sashiki, [Shō Hashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Hashi), overthrew King [Bunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunei) of the Satto dynasty and installed his father, [Shō Shishō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Shish%C5%8D), as king, establishing the [First Shō Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sh%C5%8D_Dynasty).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 42 </sup> In 1416, Shō Hashi capitalized on dissatisfaction among the Aji of Hokuzan with their king, [Hananchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hananchi), and conquered the kingdom. The Kingdom of Nanzan, plagued by internal conflict under the rule of [Tarumoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarumoi?action=edit\&redlink=1), was defeated by Shō Hashi in 1429, completing the unification of Okinawa Island under the Chūzan Kingdom.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 42 </sup>

### Ryukyu Kingdom period

The [First Shō Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sh%C5%8D_Dynasty) experienced political instability due to the early deaths of several kings. After the death of the fifth king, [Shō Kinpuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Kinpuku), a succession dispute known as the [Shirii-Tumui rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirii-Tumui_rebellion?action=edit\&redlink=1) broke out. Order was eventually restored when [Shō Taikyū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Taiky%C5%AB) ascended as the sixth king.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 45 </sup> During his reign, another major conflict, the [Gosamaru–Amawari rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosamaru%E2%80%93Amawari_rebellion?action=edit\&redlink=1), occurred, but Shō Taikyū was able to suppress it.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 46 </sup> His successor, King [Shō Toku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Toku), was known as a tyrant. After his death, a coup led by royal officials installed the high-ranking bureaucrat Kanemaru as king.[^cite_note-rekishi-26]<sup>: 89 </sup> Kanemaru took the royal name [Shō En](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_En), founding the [Second Shō Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sh%C5%8D_Dynasty).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 48 </sup>

Under the rule of the third king of the dynasty, [Shō Shin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Shin), a centralized administration was firmly established. Shō Shin relocated powerful regional chieftains (Aji) to the capital of [Shuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri,_Okinawa) and appointed state officials to govern the provinces directly. The territorial extent of the Ryukyu Kingdom also expanded, covering the area from the [Amami Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Islands) in the north to the [Yaeyama Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaeyama_Islands) in the south.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 48 </sup> Culturally, this era was a golden age for the kingdom, with significant development in the arts, religion, and architecture.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 53 </sup>

By actively participating in the [tribute system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China) with the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), the Ryukyu Kingdom received preferential treatment and became a key intermediary trading hub in East Asia. Many of the tribute goods presented to China originated from Japan, while Chinese goods were exported to Japan through Ryukyu.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 56 </sup> Southeast Asia, China, and Japan were Ryukyu's primary trade partners,[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 59 </sup> and the kingdom also maintained trade with the [Korean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Peninsula).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 58 </sup> However, Ryukyu's significance as a trade hub declined in the 16th century with the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery), as Portuguese and Spanish merchants entered East Asia, and China gradually relaxed its maritime prohibition policies.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 60 </sup>

The [Miyako Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyako_Islands) and [Yaeyama Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaeyama_Islands) had long been politically fragmented. In 1474, local strongman [Nakasone Toyomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakasone_Toyomiya) of Miyako Island submitted to the Ryukyu Kingdom, bringing the island under centralized control.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 64 </sup> In 1500, Ryukyuan forces defeated [Oyake Akahachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyake_Akahachi), the ruler of [Ishigaki Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishigaki_Island).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 66 </sup> In 1522, Nakasone Toyomiya conquered [Yonaguni Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonaguni_Island), completing the unification of the Yaeyama Islands under Ryukyuan rule.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 68 </sup> The Amami Islands in the north also came under Ryukyuan control by 1466.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 70 </sup>

In 1609, the [Shimazu clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimazu_clan) of the [Satsuma Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Domain) invaded the Ryukyu Kingdom in what is known as the [Invasion of Ryukyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Ryukyu). King [Shō Nei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Nei) surrendered, and the kingdom became a vassal state under Satsuma's control. The Amami Islands were ceded to Satsuma as part of the settlement.[^cite_note-rekishi-26]<sup>: 133 </sup> While Ryukyu was partially integrated into Japan's feudal [han system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_system), it continued to function as a nominally independent kingdom and maintained its tributary relationship with China.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 85 </sup>Ryukyuan sovereignty was maintained since complete annexation would have created a conflict with China. The Satsuma clan earned considerable profits from trade with China during a period in which foreign trade was heavily restricted by the shogunate. Although Satsuma maintained strong influence over the islands, the Ryukyu Kingdom maintained a considerable degree of domestic political freedom for over two hundred years.[^cite_note-31]

In the mid-17th century, the Ryukyuan reformer [Haneji Chōshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneji_Ch%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB) implemented significant political and social reforms promoting pro-Japanese policies.[^cite_note-rekishi-26]<sup>: 152 </sup> In the mid-18th century, [Sai On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_On), a statesman and scholar, continued these reforms and greatly improved the internal administration of the kingdom.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 101 </sup>

In 1816, two British ships visited Ryukyu but made no demands for trade or missionary activity.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 131 </sup> In 1844, France became the first European country to officially request trade with Ryukyu.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 132 </sup> In 1853, [Commodore Matthew Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_Matthew_Perry) of the United States [East India Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Squadron) stopped in Ryukyu prior to his negotiations with the Tokugawa shogunate in Japan.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 138 </sup>

### First Okinawa Prefecture period



U.S. troops landing in Okinawa

Following the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), Japan began its modernization process by abolishing the [han system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_system) and establishing prefectures in 1871. That same year, the [Mudan Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudan_Incident) occurred when a Ryukyuan ship drifted to [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan) and its crew was killed by local indigenous people. This event became a pretext for Japan to assert control over the Ryukyu Kingdom. In 1872, Japan reclassified the kingdom as the [Ryukyu Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Domain), a move known as the [Ryukyu Disposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Disposition). To avoid backlash from the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) and Ryukyuan royalty, the Meiji government initially designated Ryukyu as a "domain" rather than a "prefecture", a designation that had already been abolished in mainland Japan.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 147 </sup>

In 1874, another Ryukyuan shipwreck incident led to the [Taiwan Expedition of 1874](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Expedition_of_1874) (the [Botan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botan_War?action=edit\&redlink=1)), in which Japan dispatched troops to Taiwan. During post-conflict negotiations, the Qing acknowledged Japan's actions as "a righteous act of protecting its people." Japan interpreted this as de facto recognition of Ryukyu as Japanese territory and subsequently ordered the Ryukyu Domain to cease its tribute missions to China. This triggered internal division within the Ryukyuan court between pro-Japan and pro-China factions.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 151 </sup>

In March 1879, the Japanese government officially abolished the Ryukyu Domain and established Okinawa Prefecture, relocating King [Shō Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Tai) to [Tokyo](30057.md). Some Ryukyuan nobles and civilians fled to China and appealed to the Qing government to restore the Ryukyu Kingdom. Resistance in the Miyako and Yaeyama Islands was especially strong, culminating in the [Kōchi Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dchi_Incident?action=edit\&redlink=1), in which locals killed a Japanese interpreter. However, the rebellion was eventually suppressed.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 151 </sup>

The Qing dynasty invited former U.S. President [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) to mediate the dispute. Grant proposed a compromise in which the Okinawa Islands would go to Japan, while the Miyako and Yaeyama Islands would be ceded to China. The Qing countered with a plan that would return the Ryukyu Kingdom to the Okinawa Islands, assign the Amami Islands to Japan, and annex the Miyako and Yaeyama Islands to China. Ultimately, the negotiations failed. After the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War) (1894–1895), the Qing ceded [Taiwan](25734.md) to Japan and lost influence in the region, silencing calls for the restoration of Ryukyu.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 154 </sup>

In the early years of direct Japanese rule, a policy known as the Old Customs Preservation Policy (旧慣温存政策, *Kyūkan Onzon Seisaku*) was implemented, maintaining Ryukyuan land and tax systems, which slowed Okinawa's modernization.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 156 </sup> After the First Sino-Japanese War, Japan replaced this policy with an assimilation strategy, accelerating Okinawa's Japanization. However, Okinawa's strategic and economic importance declined, particularly after Taiwan became Japan's new southern frontier and sugar-producing center.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 161 </sup>

In the 20th century, Japan undertook major land reforms and prioritized sugar production in Okinawa, though economic development remained far behind mainland Japan.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 164 </sup> Transportation infrastructure also modernized, with new roads, railways, and ferry routes to the Japanese mainland established in the early 1900s.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 182 </sup> During the early [Taishō era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taish%C5%8D_era), Okinawa briefly prospered as sugar prices soared due to [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 187 </sup> However, by the late Taishō and early [Shōwa era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dwa_era), the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) struck, causing widespread famine. Many impoverished farmers resorted to eating the toxic [cycad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycad) plant to survive, in what became known as the "Cycad Hell" (Sotetsu Jigoku (蘇鉄地獄)).[^cite_note-100_years-32]<sup>: 153 </sup> Many Okinawans migrated to mainland Japan or abroad. Between 1923 and 1930, Okinawans accounted for 10% of all Japanese emigrants. Remittances from overseas workers contributed 40% to 65% of the prefecture's annual budget.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 190 </sup>

In the 1930s, Japan increasingly pursued a path of militarism. By the 1940s, Okinawa Prefecture was integrated into the wartime regime. The government enforced standard Japanese language use and replaced traditional Ryukyuan name pronunciations with Japanese ones as part of a broader [imperial assimilation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanization).[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 209 </sup>

In 1943, the Japanese military began seizing land in Okinawa to build airbases. In 1944, the [32nd Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-Second_Army_\(Japan\)) was stationed in Okinawa, requisitioning resources from civilians and initiating evacuations to mainland Japan and Taiwan.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 214 </sup> In August 1944, the evacuation ship [Tsushima Maru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima_Maru), carrying about 1,700 evacuees, was sunk by an American submarine, resulting in 1,476 deaths.[^cite_note-history-30]<sup>: 215 </sup> In October that same year, Naha was bombed in the [10-10 air raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-10_air_raids?action=edit\&redlink=1), destroying 90% of the city.[^cite_note-33]

In 1945, Okinawa became the site of the largest ground battle on Japanese soil during the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War). The [Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) resulted in the deaths of approximately 12,520 American soldiers and an estimated 200,000 Japanese personnel, including military and civilians.[^cite_note-34]

The [United States military](32212.md) landed on the coast of Yomitan Village, Okinawa Island, on April 1 and quickly secured control over the northern half of the island. The Japanese forces, facing overwhelming American attacks, retreated to the southern part of the island. [Shuri Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle), which served as the headquarters of the Japanese command, was destroyed, and on June 23, organized resistance ended with the suicide of Lieutenant General Mitsuru Ushijima, the commanding officer of the Okinawan defense forces, and other high-ranking staff at Mabuni.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

Over approximately three months of intense fighting, a large number of civilians were killed as a result of American [naval bombardment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_gunfire_support) that affected residential areas, extensive [aerial bombing](323167.md), and the use of flamethrowers against natural caves (gama) and civilian air-raid shelters. Additionally, there were numerous instances of group suicides among civilians, driven in part by fear of American capture. It is estimated that one in four residents of Okinawa perished, and the island was left devastated.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-history-30]

### Postwar disposition disputes

During World War II, the [Allied powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) engaged in multiple rounds of discussions regarding the postwar status of the Ryukyu Islands. At the [Cairo Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Conference) in 1943, U.S. President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) proactively raised the issue of Ryukyu's sovereignty, suggesting that China might administer the islands after the war. However, Chinese leader [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) responded only cautiously, proposing instead a joint occupation and international trusteeship. As a result, the [Cairo Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1943_Cairo_Declaration) made no explicit reference to the Ryukyus, instead stating that territories such as [Taiwan](25734.md) and the [Pescadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pescadores)—seized by Japan—should be returned to China. Historians believe Chiang hesitated because he was unsure whether Roosevelt's offer was sincere or a diplomatic probe, and because the wartime [Nationalist government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_government) relied heavily on American support and wished to avoid a territorial dispute.

As the Pacific War progressed, the U.S. military increasingly emphasized the strategic importance of the Ryukyus. In 1944, some U.S. officials proposed exclusive control of the islands to serve as a bulwark against Soviet expansion and threats from the Asian mainland. Roosevelt reportedly expressed to [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) his support for returning the Ryukyus to China, but no formal agreements emerged from the Cairo, [Yalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Conference), or [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Conference) meetings. The [Potsdam Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Declaration) stated only that Japanese sovereignty would be limited to the islands of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu), [Kyushu](16914.md), [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku), and [Hokkaido](58092.md), while other territories—including the Ryukyus—would be subject to future decisions by the Allied powers.[^cite_note-houyi-38][^cite_note-anchengri-39]

After the war, the Nationalist government of China recognized the strategic value of the Ryukyu Islands and proposed a joint trusteeship with the United States for a period of five to ten years. Later proposals included allowing the U.S. to establish bases on some islands, indicating a willingness to compromise and an understanding that the U.S. would not readily transfer sovereignty to China. Chinese domestic opinion was divided: some called for an independent Ryukyuan state, others demanded the full incorporation of the Ryukyus into Chinese territory. Most emphasized the islands' strategic importance as a buffer zone and argued they should not fall into the hands of another power.[^cite_note-houyi-38]

In 1946, the [United States Department of State](31975.md) advocated for the return of the Ryukyus to Japan, citing anti-expansion principles and concerns over economic burdens. In contrast, the U.S. military proposed that the islands be designated as a "strategic trust territory," with Okinawa Island declared a "strategic area." Military leaders argued that the high cost of American lives during the [Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) justified permanent military governance as compensation for their sacrifice. After internal debate, the plan was formalized in SWNCC 59 / 1, which proposed placing Okinawa under U.S. military administration rather than returning it to Japan, using trusteeship arrangements to sidestep sovereignty issues. The directives [SCAPIN-677](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCAPIN-677?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [SCAPIN-841](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCAPIN-841?action=edit\&redlink=1) established the legal and administrative basis for U.S. jurisdiction south of the 29th parallel north, forming the framework for postwar American control.[^cite_note-anchengri-39]

### U.S. administration (1945–1972)

On April 1, 1945, the [U.S. Army](32087.md) and [Marine Corps](17349325.md) launched an [invasion of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) with approximately 185,000 troops. They encountered determined and intense resistance from the Japanese defenders, but the U.S. emerged victorious on June 22. During the fighting, approximately one-third of Okinawa's civilian population lost their lives. The dead, of all nationalities, are commemorated at the [Cornerstone of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornerstone_of_Peace).[^cite_note-40]

During the battle of Okinawa, U.S. fleet admiral [Chester Nimitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_Nimitz) had issued an order on 5 April 1945, declaring the suspension of the Japanese administration in Okinawa and the establishment of the [United States Military Government of the Ryukyu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Government_of_the_Ryukyu_Islands) (USMGR). The [United States Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Civil_Administration_of_the_Ryukyu_Islands) was later established in 1950 to replace the USMGR, and the U.S. administration of Okinawa was later formalized in 1952, when the [Treaty of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_San_Francisco) came into effect.[^cite_note-41]

#### Continued U.S. military buildup

During the [Korean War](16772.md), [B-29 Superfortresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress) flew bombing missions over Korea from [Kadena Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadena_Air_Base) on Okinawa. The military buildup on the island during the [Cold War](325329.md) increased a division between local inhabitants and the American military. Under the 1952 [Treaty of Mutual Cooperation and Security between the United States and Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Mutual_Cooperation_and_Security_between_the_United_States_and_Japan), [United States Forces Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Japan) (USFJ) have maintained a large military presence.

During the mid-1950s, the U.S. seized land from Okinawans to build new bases or expand currently existing ones. According to the Melvin Price Report, by 1955, the military had displaced 250,000 residents.[^cite_note-42]

#### Secret U.S. deployment of nuclear weapons

Since 1960, the U.S. and Japan have maintained an agreement that allows the U.S. to secretly bring nuclear weapons into Japanese ports.[^cite_note-NHK01-43][^cite_note-NHK02-44][^cite_note-NHK03-45] The Japanese people tended to oppose the introduction of nuclear arms into Japanese territory;[^cite_note-NHK04-46] the Japanese government's assertion of [Japan's non-nuclear policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan's_non-nuclear_weapons_policy) and a statement of the [Three Non-Nuclear Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Non-Nuclear_Principles) reflected this popular opposition. Most of the weapons were alleged to be stored in ammunition bunkers at Kadena Air Base.[^cite_note-47] Between 1954 and 1972, 19 different types of nuclear weapons were deployed in Okinawa, but with fewer than around 1,000 warheads at any one time.[^cite_note-NHK05-48] In fall 1960, U.S. commandos in [Green Light Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Light_Teams) secret training missions carried small nuclear weapons on the east coast of Okinawa Island.[^cite_note-49]

#### Vietnam War



Map of U.S. military bases on [Okinawa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island) in 1969

Between 1965 and 1972, Okinawa was a key staging point for United States in its military operations directed towards North Vietnam. Along with Guam, it presented a geographically strategic launch pad for covert bombing missions over Cambodia and Laos.[^cite_note-50] Anti-[Vietnam War](32611.md) sentiment became linked politically to the movement for reversion of Okinawa to Japan. In 1965, the U.S. military bases, earlier viewed as paternal post war protection, were increasingly seen as aggressive. The Vietnam War highlighted the differences between United States and Okinawa but showed a commonality between the islands and mainland Japan.[^cite_note-NYT65-51]

As controversy grew regarding the alleged placement of nuclear weapons on Okinawa, fears intensified over the escalation of the Vietnam War. Okinawa was perceived by some inside Japan as a potential target for China, should the communist government feel threatened by United States.[^cite_note-52] American military secrecy blocked any local reporting on what was actually occurring at bases such as Kadena Air Base. As information leaked out, and images of air strikes were published, the local population began to fear the potential for retaliation.[^cite_note-NYT65-51]

Political leaders such as [Makoto Oda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Oda), a major figure in the [Beheiren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beheiren) movement (Foundation of Citizens for Peace in Vietnam), believed that the return of Okinawa to Japan would lead to the removal of U.S. forces, ending Japan's involvement in Vietnam.[^cite_note-Havens,_T._R._H._1987_Pg_120-53] In a speech delivered in 1967, Oda was critical of Prime Minister [Eisaku Satō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisaku_Sat%C5%8D)'s unilateral support of America's war in Vietnam, claiming "Realistically we are all guilty of complicity in the Vietnam War".[^cite_note-Havens,_T._R._H._1987_Pg_120-53] The Beheiren became a more visible anti-war movement on Okinawa as the American involvement in Vietnam intensified. The movement employed tactics ranging from demonstrations to handing leaflets to soldiers, sailors, airmen and Marines directly, warning of the implications for a third World War.[^cite_note-54]

The U.S. military bases on Okinawa became a focal point for [anti-Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_United_States_involvement_in_the_Vietnam_War) sentiment. By 1969, over 50,000 American military personnel were stationed on Okinawa.[^cite_note-55] [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) began referring to Okinawa as the "Keystone of the Pacific". This slogan was imprinted on local U.S. military license plates.[^cite_note-56]

In 1969, chemicals leaked from the U.S. storage depot at Chibana in central Okinawa, under [Operation Red Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Red_Hat). Evacuations of residents took place over a wide area for two months. Even two years later, government investigators found that Okinawans and the environment near the leak were still suffering because of the depot.[^cite_note-APJ2014-57]

On May 15, 1972, the U.S. government returned the islands to Japan following the signing of the [1971 Okinawa Reversion Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Okinawa_Reversion_Agreement).[^cite_note-58]

### Post-reversion history (1972–present)



U.S. military facilities on Okinawa Island (in red)

The U.S. Marine Corps buried a massive stockpile of Agent Orange at the Futenma air station in Okinawa, seriously sickening the base's former head of maintenance and potentially contaminating nearby residents and the ground beneath the base. The barrels were abandoned in Okinawa at the end of the Vietnam War – when the U.S. government banned the dioxin-laden defoliant for health reasons — and were buried at the installation in the city of Ginowan after the Pentagon ignored repeated requests to safely dispose of them, according to the veterans who served at the installation in the 1970s and 1980s.[^cite_note-59] The [1995 kidnaping, beating, and rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Okinawa_rape_incident) of a 12-year-old girl by three U.S. servicemen triggered widespread protests in Okinawa. Reports by the local media of accidents and crimes committed by U.S. servicemen have reduced the local population's support for the U.S. military bases. A strong emotional response has emerged from certain incidents.[^cite_note-60]

Documents declassified in 1997 proved that both tactical and strategic weapons have been maintained in Okinawa.[^cite_note-APJ2014-57] In 1999 and 2002, the *Japan Times* and the *Okinawa Times* reported speculation that not all weapons were removed from Okinawa.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] On October 25, 2005, after a decade of negotiations, the governments of the U.S. and Japan officially agreed to move [Marine Corps Air Station Futenma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Futenma) from its location in the densely populated city of [Ginowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginowan,_Okinawa) to the more northerly and remote [Camp Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Schwab) in [Nago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nago) by building a heliport with a shorter runway, partly on Camp Schwab land and partly running into the sea.[^cite_note-economist-63] The move is partly an attempt to relieve tensions between the people of Okinawa and the Marine Corps.

Despite Okinawa prefecture constituting only 0.6% of Japan's land surface, in 2006 75% of all USFJ bases were located on Okinawa, occupying 18% of the main island.[^cite_note-economist-63][^cite_note-USMilitaryBases-64]

In a poll conducted by *The Asahi Shimbun* in May 2010, 43% of the Okinawan population wanted the complete closure of the U.S. bases, 42% wanted reduction, and 11% wanted to maintain the status quo. Okinawan feelings about the U.S. military are complex.[^cite_note-65]

In early 2008, U.S. Secretary of State [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice) apologized after a series of crimes involving American troops in Japan, including the rape of a young girl of 14 by a Marine on Okinawa. The U.S. military imposed a temporary 24-hour curfew on military personnel and their families to ease the anger of local residents.[^cite_note-66] Some cited statistics that the crime rate of military personnel is consistently less than that of the general Okinawan population.[^cite_note-67] However, some criticized the statistics as unreliable, since violence against women is under-reported.[^cite_note-68] Between 1972 and 2009, U.S. servicemen committed 5,634 criminal offenses, including 25 murders, 385 burglaries, 25 arsons, 127 rapes, 306 assaults and 2,827 thefts.[^cite_note-Guardian110307-69] Yet, per [Marine Corps Installations Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Installations_Pacific) data, U.S. service members are convicted of far fewer crimes than local Okinawans.[^cite_note-70]

In 2009, a new Japanese government came to power and froze the U.S. forces relocation plan but in April 2010 indicated their interest in resolving the issue by proposing a modified plan.[^cite_note-71] A study done in 2010 found that the prolonged exposure to aircraft noise around the Kadena Air Base and other military bases cause health issues such as a disrupted sleep pattern, high blood pressure, weakening of the immune system in children, and a loss of hearing.[^cite_note-72]

In 2011, it was reported that the U.S. military—contrary to repeated denials by [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon)—had kept tens of thousands of barrels of [Agent Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange) on the island. The Japanese and American governments have angered some U.S. veterans, who believe they were poisoned by Agent Orange while serving on the island, by characterizing their statements regarding Agent Orange as "dubious", and ignoring their requests for compensation. Reports that more than a third of the barrels developed leaks have led Okinawans to ask for environmental investigations, but as of 2012 both Tokyo and Washington refused such action.[^cite_note-73] [Jon Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Mitchell_\(journalist\)) has reported concern that the U.S. used American Marines as chemical-agent guinea pigs.[^cite_note-74]

On September 30, 2018, [Denny Tamaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denny_Tamaki) was elected as the next governor of Okinawa prefecture, after a campaign focused on sharply reducing the U.S. military presence on the island.[^cite_note-75]

#### Marine Corps Air Station Futenma relocation

Main article: [Relocation of Marine Corps Air Station Futenma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relocation_of_Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Futenma)

In 2006, some 8,000 U.S. Marines were removed from the island and relocated to [Guam](11974.md).[^cite_note-76] The move to [Marine Corps Base Camp Blaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Camp_Blaz) was expected to be completed in 2023 but as of January 1, 2025, is still in process. Japan paid for a majority of the cost to construct the new base.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The U.S. still maintains Air Force, Marine, Navy, and Army military installations on the islands. These bases include [Kadena Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadena_Air_Base), [Camp Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Foster), [Marine Corps Air Station Futenma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Futenma), [Camp Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Hansen), [Camp Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Schwab), [Torii Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torii_Station), [Camp Kinser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Kinser), and [Camp Gonsalves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Gonsalves). The area of 14 U.S. bases are 233 square kilometres (90 sq mi), occupying 18% of the main island. Okinawa hosts about two-thirds of the 50,000 American forces in Japan although the islands account for less than one percent of total lands in Japan.[^cite_note-USMilitaryBases-64]

Suburbs have grown towards and now surround two historic major bases, Futenma and Kadena. A sizable portion of the land used by the U.S. military is Camp Gonsalves in the north of the island.[^cite_note-79] On December 21, 2016, 10,000 acres of Camp Gonsalves were returned to Japan.[^cite_note-80] On June 25, 2018, Okinawa residents held a protest demonstration at sea against scheduled land reclamation work for the relocation of a U.S. military base within Japan's southernmost island prefecture. A protest gathered hundreds of people.[^cite_note-81]

Since the early 2000s, Okinawans have opposed the presence of American troops [helipads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helipad) in the [Takae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takae) zone of the [Yanbaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanbaru) forest near [Higashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashi,_Okinawa) and [Kunigami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunigami,_Okinawa).[^cite_note-82] This opposition grew in July 2016 after the construction of six new helipads.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

## Geography

Main article: [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md)

### Major islands



The islands of Okinawa Prefecture

The islands comprising the prefecture are the southern two thirds of the archipelago of the Ryūkyū Islands (琉球諸島, *Ryūkyū-shotō*). Okinawa's inhabited islands are typically divided into three geographical archipelagos. From northeast to southwest:

- [Okinawa Islands](2113477.md) (沖縄諸島, *Okinawa-shotō*)&#x20;

  - [Iejima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iejima) (伊江島, *Iejima*)

  - [Kume-jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumejima,_Okinawa) (久米島町, *Kumejima-chō*)

### Natural parks

Approximately 36% percent of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [natural parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan), namely the [Iriomote-Ishigaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iriomote-Ishigaki_National_Park), [Kerama Shotō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerama_Shot%C5%8D_National_Park), and [Yambaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yambaru_National_Park) National Parks; [Okinawa Kaigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Kaigan_Quasi-National_Park) and [Okinawa Senseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Senseki_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks; and [Irabu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irabu_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Kumejima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumejima_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Tarama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarama_Prefectural_Natural_Park), and [Tonaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonaki_Prefectural_Natural_Park) Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-85] The natural parks of Okinawa Prefecture were established to protect the region's tropical landscapes, including coastlines, coral reefs, forests, and island ecosystems.[^cite_note-86]

### Ecology

The [dugong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugong) is an endangered marine mammal related to the [manatee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manatee).[^cite_note-CBD-87] [Iriomote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iriomote_Island) is home to one of the world's rarest and most endangered cat species, the [Iriomote cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iriomote_cat). The region is also home to at least one [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) [pit viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pit_viper), *[Trimeresurus elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimeresurus_elegans)*. The islands of Okinawa are surrounded by some of the most abundant coral reefs found in the world.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] The world's largest colony of rare blue coral is found off [Ishigaki Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishigaki_Island).[^cite_note-90] The [sea turtles](142905.md) return yearly to the southern islands of Okinawa to lay their eggs. The summer months carry warnings to swimmers regarding venomous [jellyfish](50185.md) and other dangerous sea creatures.

Okinawa is a major producer of [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane), [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [papaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), and other tropical fruit, and the [Southeast Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Botanical_Gardens) represent tropical plant species.

### Geology

The island is largely composed of [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral), and rainwater filtering through that coral has given the island many caves, which played an important role in the Battle of Okinawa. Gyokusendo[^cite_note-91] is an extensive [limestone cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone_cave#Solutional_cave) in the southern part of Okinawa's main island. Nuchishinji-gama (Cave Okinawa) served as a place of refuge during the Battle of Okinawa from American troops.[^cite_note-92]

### Climate

The island experiences temperatures above 20 °C (68 °F) for most of the year. The climate of the islands ranges from [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*) in the north, such as [Okinawa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island), to [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) (Köppen climate classification *Af*) in the south such as [Iriomote Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iriomote_Island). Snowfall is unheard of at sea level. However, on January 24, 2016, sleet was reported in Nago for the first time on record.[^cite_note-93]

## Politics



Okinawa Prefectural Government Headquarters

Due to its unique historical background, Okinawa has a significantly stronger progressive (left-wing) presence compared to most other Japanese prefectures; this makes it one of the most politically polarized regions in the country. The [Okinawa Social Mass Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Social_Mass_Party), a local progressive political party, has played an important role in postwar Okinawan politics.[^cite_note-94]<sup>: 55–71 </sup>

In 2014, various progressive parties such as the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Japan\)), [Japanese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Communist_Party), [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Japan\)), and the Okinawa Social Mass Party formed a cross-party electoral alliance with some conservative figures who also opposed the relocation of the U.S. [Marine Corps Air Station Futenma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Futenma) to the city of [Nago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nago,_Okinawa). This movement, known as the "All-Okinawa" campaign, achieved electoral victories in the [2014 Okinawa gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Okinawa_gubernatorial_election),[^cite_note-95] the [2016 Okinawa prefectural election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Okinawa_prefectural_election),[^cite_note-96] and others.

Since Japan introduced the [Single-member district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_district) system in 1996, Okinawa has been divided into four electoral districts for the [House of Representatives](217778.md).[^cite_note-97] Among these, Okinawa's 2nd District, which hosts the highest concentration of U.S. military bases, has long been a stronghold for the progressive camp. [Kantoku Teruya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantoku_Teruya) of the Social Democratic Party held this seat from 2003 onwards. The other three districts have seen fierce competition between conservatives and progressives, with frequent changes in party control.

In the [2014 Japanese general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Japanese_general_election), all four Okinawan districts elected candidates opposed to the relocation of the Futenma base to Nago. In the 1st District, [Seiken Akamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiken_Akamine) of the Japanese Communist Party won a seat—marking the JCP's first single-member district victory in Okinawa and its first nationwide in 18 years.[^cite_note-98]

In the [2017 Japanese general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Japanese_general_election), the [Liberal Democratic Party](164231.md) (LDP) won the seat in the 4th District.[^cite_note-99] In the [2021 Japanese general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Japanese_general_election), LDP candidates won in both the 3rd and 4th Districts and also gained proportional representation seats in the 1st and 2nd Districts. The Japanese Communist Party and the Social Democratic Party each retained one seat.[^cite_note-100] In the [2026 Japanese general election](77996830.md), the LDP won a seat in each of Okinawa's four districts.[^cite_note-101]

In the [House of Councillors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Councillors_\(Japan\)), Okinawa is represented by two seats in a single at-large district. Both are currently held by politicians who oppose the Futenma base relocation.[^cite_note-102] However, in proportional representation voting, the LDP has consistently received the highest number of votes in Okinawa.[^cite_note-103]

Okinawa has also experienced multiple changes in political leadership throughout its gubernatorial history.[^cite_note-104] In 2014, [Takeshi Onaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Onaga), backed by progressive forces, was elected governor of Okinawa Prefecture.[^cite_note-105] After Onaga's death in 2018, another progressive candidate, [Denny Tamaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denny_Tamaki), was elected governor in the September 2018 election.[^cite_note-106] Tamaki was re-elected in 2022.[^cite_note-107] However, in recent years, rising tensions across the Taiwan Strait have led to growing unease among Okinawans toward the People's Republic of China, resulting in a loss of momentum for progressive forces.[^cite_note-108]

The [Okinawa Prefectural Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefectural_Assembly) consists of 48 members, with the [Liberal Democratic Party](164231.md) holding the largest number of seats at 22.[^cite_note-109]

In terms of administrative jurisdiction, the disputed [Senkaku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands) (referred to by China and Taiwan as the Diaoyu Islands) are administered by the city of [Ishigaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishigaki,_Okinawa) in Okinawa Prefecture.[^cite_note-110]

Okinawa has long experienced social and cultural frictions with mainland Japan. In 2016, controversy erupted when police officers from [Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture) Prefecture assigned to duty at U.S. military bases in Okinawa used the derogatory term "dojin" (meaning "savage" or "native") to insult local protestors.[^cite_note-111] According to a 2017 [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK) survey, only 19% of Okinawans felt that people from mainland Japan understood Okinawan sentiments, while 79.6% believed they did not. Additionally, 56.9% reported an increase in discriminatory or derogatory remarks against Okinawans over the past five years.[^cite_note-yoron-112] On the other hand, instances of discrimination against Amerasians (children of mixed Okinawan and American parentage) have also been reported within Okinawa.[^cite_note-113]

Okinawa faces chronic fiscal challenges and relies heavily on subsidies from Japan's central government. Its fiscal capacity index is only 0.29—significantly below the national average.[^cite_note-114]

### U.S. military bases



U.S. military bases in Okinawa in red

While Okinawa comprises only 0.6% of Japan's land area, it hosts approximately 74% of all U.S. military facilities in the country.[^cite_note-115] The 33 American bases in Okinawa occupy around 10% of the prefecture's land—up to 18% on Okinawa Island.[^cite_note-116] Approximately 47,300 U.S. military personnel and their families reside in the prefecture.[^cite_note-117]

Among the many military issues, the relocation of [Marine Corps Air Station Futenma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Futenma) has been the most contentious. Due to its proximity to residential areas and repeated accidents, Futenma has been labeled one of the most dangerous military bases in the world. The prefectural government has long called for its relocation outside of Okinawa.[^cite_note-118] In 2010, Japan and the U.S. agreed to relocate the base to [Camp Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Schwab) in Nago,[^cite_note-119] sparking widespread protests.

Another key issue is crime involving U.S. military personnel. Between the 1972 reversion and 2015, 5,896 criminal cases involving U.S. forces were reported in Okinawa.[^cite_note-120] In 2016, a high-profile rape and murder case involving a U.S. military contractor in [Uruma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruma,_Okinawa) triggered mass protests.[^cite_note-121]

On the other hand, U.S. bases are Okinawa's second-largest source of employment.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

According to a 2017 NHK survey, 25.7% of Okinawans wanted the complete removal of U.S. military bases, while 50.6% preferred reducing their presence to levels comparable to mainland Japan. Only 26.5% supported relocating Futenma to Nago, while 62.6% were opposed.[^cite_note-yoron-112]

On February 24, 2019, a prefectural referendum was held on land reclamation in [Henoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henoko) for the new U.S. base. With a turnout of 52.48%, 71.74% opposed the project, 18.99% supported it, and 8.70% expressed no clear opinion.[^cite_note-124]

### Independence movement

Main article: [Okinawan independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_independence_movement)



Headquarters of the [Kariyushi Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariyushi_Club), a group advocating for Ryukyuan independence.

After Japan annexed the [Ryukyu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Kingdom) in 1879 during the so-called [Ryukyu Disposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Disposition), the last king, [Shō Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Tai), was relocated to Tokyo. In the following years, Ryukyuan figures who opposed the new arrangements petitioned the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) for redress, organizing in treaty-port communities such as [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou), [Beijing](18603746.md), and [Tianjin](50821.md). Contemporary sources referred to some émigrés as *dasshinnin* (脱清人), who advocated Qing intervention to restore Ryukyuan autonomy or statehood.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] Diplomatic mediation was even sought from former U.S. President [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) in 1879–1880, but negotiations ultimately failed to produce a settlement; the issue receded after the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War).[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

Following [World War II](32927.md), the Ryukyu Islands were placed under U.S. administration (1945–1972). During this period, proposals ranging from restoration of Ryukyuan statehood to enhanced local self-government were debated among Okinawan actors and in Japanese civil society. Under the 1971 [Okinawa Reversion Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Reversion_Agreement), administrative rights returned to Japan in 1972 while U.S. bases remained under the [Treaty of Mutual Cooperation and Security between the United States and Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Mutual_Cooperation_and_Security_between_the_United_States_and_Japan).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

In contemporary politics, independence advocacy persists alongside broader autonomy movements and base-related activism. Polling generally shows limited support for full independence but greater backing for stronger local autonomy; for example, a 2022 *[Okinawa Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Times)* survey reported 3% support for independence, 48% for a "strongly empowered" local government, and 42% for maintaining the status quo.[^cite_note-133] Public debate has increasingly extended to digital platforms; Japanese media and academic outlets have reported instances of inauthentic social-media activity linked to foreign information operations concerning Okinawa and independence narratives.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

## Demographics



Okinawa prefecture population pyramid in 2020

### Ethnic groups

The indigenous [Ryukyuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuans) make up the majority of Okinawa Prefecture's population and are also the main ethnic group of the [Amami Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Islands) to the north. Large Okinawan diaspora communities persist in places such as South America[^cite_note-138] and [Hawaii](13270.md).[^cite_note-139] With the introduction of American military bases, there are an increasing number of half-American children in Okinawa, including prefecture governor [Denny Tamaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denny_Tamaki).[^cite_note-140] The prefecture also has a sizable minority of [Yamato people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_people) from mainland Japan; exact population numbers are difficult to establish, as the Japanese government does not officially recognize Ryukyuans as a distinct ethnic group from Yamatos.

The overall ethnic identity of Okinawa residents is rather split. According to a telephone poll conducted by Lim John Chuan-tiong (林泉忠), an associate professor with the [University of the Ryukyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Ryukyus), 40.6% of respondents identified as "沖縄人 (Okinawan)", 21.3% identified as "日本人 (Japanese)" and 36.5% identified as both.[^cite_note-141]

## Language

There remain six [Ryukyuan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_languages), which, although part of the same [Japonic language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japonic_languages), are mutually unintelligible with [Japanese](15606.md). [One of the Ryukyuan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_%C5%8Cshima_language) is spoken in [Kagoshima Prefecture](217439.md), rather than in Okinawa Prefecture. These languages are in decline as the younger generation of Okinawans uses Standard Japanese. Mainland Japanese and some Okinawans generally perceive the Ryukyuan languages as dialects. Standard Japanese is almost always used in formal situations. In informal situations, the everyday language among Okinawans under the age of 60 is [Okinawan Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_Japanese), which is often mistaken by non-Okinawans as the Okinawan language proper. The actual traditional Okinawan language is still used in traditional cultural activities, such as [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_music) and [folk dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumi_Odori). There is a radio-news program in the language as well.[^cite_note-146]

## Culture

Okinawan culture retains strong influences from its historical trading partners. Among these, [Kyushu](16914.md) has maintained the closest economic and cultural ties with Okinawa from ancient times to the present, and the two regions share many cultural traits. Elements of Okinawan culture can be found throughout [Kyushu](16914.md), and vice versa. For instance, Okinawan musical scales appear in [Kyushu](16914.md)'s folk songs, and there are notable similarities in cuisine and language. [Kyushu](16914.md) is also home to a traditional instrument called the *[gottan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottan)(ゴッタン)*, which closely resembles the Okinawan *[sanshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanshin)* (三線).[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

Furthermore, the customs of the Okinawan islands show strong influences from China, [Thailand](30128.md), and [Austronesian-speaking regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples).[^cite_note-149]

&#x20;One of the most famous cultural traditions of Okinawa is undoubtedly [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate). [Karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate) is a martial art that originated when [Chinese kung fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts) was introduced to the [Ryukyu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Kingdom) and then developed independently within the islands before being brought to mainland [Japan](15573.md). Today, karate is practiced around the world in various styles, including [Shotokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotokan), [Goju-ryu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C5%8Dj%C5%AB-ry%C5%AB), [Shito-ryu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shit%C5%8D-ry%C5%AB), and [Uechi-ryu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uechi-Ry%C5%AB).[^cite_note-150]&#x20;



[Awamori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awamori) pots

A cultural feature of the Okinawans is the forming of [*moais*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moai_\(social_support_groups\)). A *moai* is a community social gathering and groups that come together to provide financial and emotional support through emotional bonding, advice giving, and social funding. This provides a sense of security for the community members and as mentioned in the Blue Zone studies, may have been a contributing factor to the longevity of its people.[^cite_note-151] However, in recent decades Okinawans' life expectancy has fallen significantly (also bringing into question the general validity of the 'Blue Zones' denomination), which often has been blamed on cultural influence from the rest of Japan, as well as foreign influences on Okinawans' lifestyle.[^cite_note-152]

Two Okinawan writers have received the [Akutagawa Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akutagawa_Prize): [Eiki Matayoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiki_Matayoshi) in 1995 for *The Pig's Retribution* (豚の報い, *Buta no mukui*) and [Shun Medoruma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shun_Medoruma) in 1997 for *A Drop of Water* (*Suiteki*). The prize was also won by Okinawans in 1967 by [Tatsuhiro Oshiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsuhiro_Oshiro) for *Cocktail Party* (*Kakuteru Pāti*) and in 1971 by [Mineo Higashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineo_Higashi) for *Okinawan Boy* (*Okinawa no Shōnen*).[^cite_note-O-times-153][^cite_note-154]

A traditional craft, the fabric named [bingata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingata), is made in workshops on the main island and elsewhere.

### Music

Main articles: [Okinawan music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_music) and [Ryukyuan music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_music)



*Eisa* performer in [Naha](6792923.md), Okinawa

The music of the prefecture contains native and imported influences in both *koten* (classical) and *[min'yō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min'y%C5%8D)* (folk) styles. Okinawan instruments include the [sanshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanshin)—a three-stringed [banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo)-like instrument, closely related to the Chinese [sanxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxian), and ancestor of the Japanese [shamisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamisen). Its body is often bound with snakeskin (from [pythons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythonidae), imported from elsewhere in Asia, rather than from Okinawa's venomous [habu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_habu), which are too small for this purpose). Okinawan musical cultures integrate dance with music, such as in [eisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisa_\(dance\)), a traditional drumming dance.[^cite_note-155]

### Religion

Main article: [Ryukyuan religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_religion)

Okinawan people have inherited a traditional religious belief system known as [Ryukyuan Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_religion), which is similar to but distinct from modern [Japanese Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto). This indigenous belief system is animistic in nature, characterized by ancestor worship and a deep respect for the relationships between the living, the dead, and the gods or spirits of the natural world.[^cite_note-156]

[Shamanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism) practitioners, known as ***[Yuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuta_\(shaman\))***, continue to play an active role in Okinawan society. They perform ritual prayers, divination, spiritual consultations, and even communicate with the spirits of the deceased. For many people, *yuta* serve as important spiritual guides who offer advice and solutions to both supernatural and everyday life problems.[^cite_note-157]

Throughout Okinawa, there are sacred sites known as *[utaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utaki)*, where rituals and ceremonies are performed. Ryukyuan beliefs preserve many elements of ancient Japanese spirituality—such as those from the [Jōmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_period) and [Yayoi periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_period)—which have largely disappeared on the Japanese mainland. As such, they are considered important resources in [comparative mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_mythology) and religious studies.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

### Cuisine and diet

See also: [Okinawan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_cuisine)



[Okinawa soba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_soba)

The [Okinawan diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_diet) consists of low-fat, low-salt foods, such as whole fruits and vegetables, legumes, tofu, and seaweed. Okinawans are particularly well known for consuming purple potatoes, also known as Okinawan sweet potatoes.[^cite_note-160] Okinawans used to be known for their [longevity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longevity) compared to the rest of Japan and the world in general. This particular island is a so-called [Blue Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Zone), an area where people are purported to live longer than most others elsewhere in the world. Possible explanations for this were diet, low-stress lifestyle, caring community, activity, and spirituality of the inhabitants of the island.[^cite_note-Santrock-161]

A traditional Okinawan product that owes its existence to Okinawa's trading history is [awamori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awamori)—an Okinawan distilled spirit made from *indica* rice imported from [Thailand](30128.md).

### Architecture



[Shuri Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle) in Naha

Despite widespread destruction during World War II, there are many remains of a unique type of castle or fortress known as *[gusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusuku)*; the most [significant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Values_\(heritage\)) are inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List ([Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusuku_Sites_and_Related_Properties_of_the_Kingdom_of_Ryukyu)).[^cite_note-162] In addition, [twenty-three Ryukyuan architectural complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Cultural_Properties_of_Japan_-_structures_\(Okinawa\)) and [forty historic sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Historic_Sites_of_Japan_\(Okinawa\)) have been [designated for protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Property_\(Japan\)) by the national government.[^cite_note-163] [Shuri Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle) in Naha is a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).



Nakamura House



A [shisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shisa)

Whereas most [homes in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_in_Japan) are made from wood and allow free-flow of air to combat humidity, typical modern homes in Okinawa are made from concrete with barred windows to protect from flying plant debris and to withstand regular [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon). Roofs are designed with strong winds in mind, in which each tile is cemented on and not merely layered as seen with many homes in Japan. The Nakamura House ([中村家住宅 (沖縄県)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%AD%E6%9D%91%E5%AE%B6%E4%BD%8F%E5%AE%85_\(%E6%B2%96%E7%B8%84%E7%9C%8C\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)) is an original 18th century farmhouse in Kitanakagusuki. Many roofs also display a lion-dog statue, called a [shisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shisa), which is said to protect the home from danger. Roofs are typically red in color and are inspired by Chinese design.[^cite_note-164]

## Education

The public schools in Okinawa are overseen by the [Okinawa Prefectural Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefectural_Board_of_Education). The agency directly operates several public high schools[^cite_note-165] including [Okinawa Shogaku High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Shogaku_High_School). The U.S. [Department of Defense Dependents Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_Dependents_Schools) operates 13 schools total in Okinawa. Seven of these schools are located on Kadena Air Base.

Okinawa has many types of private schools. Some of them are [cram schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cram_school), also known as [juku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juku). Others, such as [Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_\(eikaiwa\)), solely teach language. There are 10 [colleges / universities in Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Japan), including the [University of the Ryukyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Ryukyus), the only [national university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_universities_in_Japan) in the prefecture, and the [Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology), a new international research institute. Okinawa's American military bases also host the Asian Division of the [University of Maryland University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland_Global_Campus).

#### Karate



Karate training with [Shinpan Gusukuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinpan_Gusukuma) at [Shuri Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle), c. 1938

[Karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate) originated in the Ryukyu Kingdom, under Chinese influence. Over time, it developed into several styles and sub-styles. On Okinawa, the three main styles are considered to be [Shōrin-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Drin-ry%C5%AB), [Gōjū-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C5%8Dj%C5%AB-ry%C5%AB) and [Uechi-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uechi-ry%C5%AB). Internationally, the various styles and sub-styles include [Matsubayashi-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsubayashi-ry%C5%AB), [Wadō-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wad%C5%8D-ry%C5%AB), [Isshin-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isshin-ry%C5%AB), [Shōrinkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Drinkan), [Shotokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotokan), [Shitō-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shit%C5%8D-ry%C5%AB), [Shōrinjiryū Kenkōkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Drinjiry%C5%AB_Kenk%C5%8Dkan_Karate), Shorinjiryu Koshinkai, and [Shōrinji-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Drinji-ry%C5%AB).

Following Okinawa's occupation, karate spread to the United States and the rest of the world. It is now popular across the world, and has since been [included](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate_at_the_Summer_Olympics) in the Olympic Games.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

### Harii

**[Harii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harii_\(Japanese_sport\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (ハーリー)** is a traditional rowing sport practiced mainly in [Kyushu](16914.md) and Okinawa, [Japan](15573.md).

It is typically held during events such as the Kaijin-sai (海神祭) around the 4th day of the 5th month of the lunar calendar (ユッカヌヒー), or near the lunar Obon season.

The sport features a boat race in which dozens of men board a *harii boat* and row competitively; the team that reaches the finish line first is declared the winner.[^cite_note-170]

Various *harii* races are held throughout Okinawa, with the most prominent being the **[Naha Harii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha_Harii?action=edit\&redlink=1)** in the city of [Naha](6792923.md).[^cite_note-171]

## Economy

See also: [Okinawa Island § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#Economy)

The island economy is primarily driven by tourism and the U.S. military presence.[^cite_note-182] Other significant contributors to the economy include public utilities and public works, as well as, to a lesser extent, limestone mining, cement production, agriculture, telecommunications ([Okinawa Cellular Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Cellular_Telephone?action=edit\&redlink=1)), and alcoholic beverage production ([Orion Breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_Breweries)).[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

The 34 U.S. military installations on Okinawa are financially supported by the U.S. and Japan.[^cite_note-huff-186] The bases provide jobs for Okinawans, both directly and indirectly; in 2011, the U.S. military employed over 9,800 Japanese workers in Okinawa.[^cite_note-huff-186] As of 2012 the bases accounted for up to 5% of the economy.[^cite_note-187] However, Koji Taira argued in 1997 that because the U.S. bases occupy around 20% of Okinawa's land, they impose a [deadweight loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadweight_loss) of 15% on the Okinawan economy.[^cite_note-188] The Tokyo government also pays the prefectural government around ¥10 billion per year[^cite_note-huff-186] in compensation for the American presence, including, for instance, rent paid by the Japanese government to the Okinawans on whose land American bases are situated.[^cite_note-g2mil.com-189] A 2005 report by the U.S. Forces Japan Okinawa Area Field Office estimated that in 2003 the combined U.S. and Japanese base-related spending contributed \$1.9 billion to the local economy.[^cite_note-JapanTimes-190] On January 13, 2015, in response to the citizens electing governor [Takeshi Onaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Onaga), the national government announced that Okinawa's funding will be cut, due to the governor's stance on removing the US military bases from Okinawa, which the national government does not want happening.[^cite_note-JapanTimes2-191][^cite_note-JapanTimes3-192]

The Okinawa Convention and Visitors Bureau is exploring the possibility of using facilities on the military bases for large-scale meetings, conferencing, exhibitions events.[^cite_note-193]
