**England and Wales** ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language): *Cymru a Lloegr*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Wales and England') is one of the [three legal jurisdictions](77750.md#Legal_jurisdictions) of the [United Kingdom](31717.md). It covers the two constituent countries of [England](9316.md) and [Wales](69894.md), and was formed by the [Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_in_Wales_Acts_1535_and_1542). The substantive law of the jurisdiction is [English law](85441.md). In a legal sense, the jurisdiction was simply known as **England** from 1746 to 1967.

The [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_devolution) [Senedd](238480.md) (Welsh Parliament; [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language): *Senedd Cymru*) – previously named the National Assembly for Wales – was created in 1999 under the [Government of Wales Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Wales_Act_1998) and provides a degree of [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) in Wales. The powers of the legislature were expanded by the [Government of Wales Act 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Wales_Act_2006), which allows it to pass [its own laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_law), and the Act also formally separated the [Welsh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government) from the Senedd. There is currently no [equivalent body for England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolved_English_parliament), which is directly governed by the parliament and [government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md).

## History of jurisdiction



The Roman province of [Britannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) in 410

During the [Roman occupation of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_occupation_of_Britain), the area of present-day England and Wales was administered as a single unit, except for the land to the north of [Hadrian's Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian's_Wall) – though the Roman-occupied area varied in extent, and for a time extended to the [Antonine/Severan Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonine_Wall) (situated entirely in modern-day [Scotland](26994.md)). It is thought that at that time most of the native inhabitants of [Roman Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) spoke [Brythonic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brythonic_languages), but they were all regarded as [Britons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britons_\(historical\)), divided into numerous tribes. After the conquest, the Romans administered this region as a single unit, the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain).

Long after the departure of the Romans, the Britons in what became Wales developed their [own system of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyfraith_Hywel), first codified by [Hywel Dda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hywel_Dda) (Hywel the Good; reigned 942–950) when he was king of most of present-day Wales (compare [King of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Wales)); in England [Anglo-Saxon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_law) was initially codified by [Alfred the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_the_Great#Legal_reform) in his [Legal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_book), c. 893. However, after the [Norman invasion of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_invasion_of_Wales) in the 11th century, [English law](85441.md) came to apply in the parts of Wales conquered by the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) (the [Welsh Marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Marches)). In 1283, the English, led by [Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England), with the biggest army brought together in England since the 11th century, [conquered the remainder of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Wales_by_Edward_I_of_England), then organised as the [Principality of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Wales). This was then united with the English crown by the [Statute of Rhuddlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Rhuddlan) of 1284. This aimed to replace Welsh criminal law with English law.

Welsh law continued to be used for civil cases until the annexation of Wales to England in the 16th century by the Welsh [House of Tudor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Tudor). The [Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_in_Wales_Acts_1535_and_1542) then consolidated the administration of all the Welsh territories and incorporated them fully into the legal system of the [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England).[^cite_note-Principality-2] This was in part to update outdated Welsh laws, but also to control Wales alongside England; through these acts, the Welsh could be seen as equals to the English.[^cite_note-3] This was reflected on both [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII) and [Elizabeth I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_I)'s coat of arms where the dragon represented Wales and the lion represented England.[^cite_note-4] When the Tudor dynasty ended with the death of [Elizabeth I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_I) the [red dragon of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Dragon) was replaced with the [unicorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_Scotland) of Scotland with the succession of [King James I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_VI_and_I) who demoted Wales' status on the coat of arms and on the first adaptation of the [Flag of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Great_Britain).[^cite_note-BBC_20060410-5][^cite_note-6]

Prior to 1746, it was not clear whether a reference to "England" in legislation included Wales, and so in 1746, Parliament passed the [Wales and Berwick Act 1746](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_and_Berwick_Act_1746). This specified that in all prior and future laws, references to "England" would by default include Wales (and [Berwick-upon-Tweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick-upon-Tweed)). The Wales and Berwick Act was repealed by the [Welsh Language Act 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Language_Act_1967), although the statutory definition of "England" created by that Act still applies for laws passed before 1967. In new legislation since then, what was referred to as "England" is now "England and Wales", while subsequent references to "England" and "Wales" refer to those political divisions.

## Law



The [Royal Courts of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Courts_of_Justice) of England and Wales

England and Wales are treated as a single unit for some purposes, because the two form the constitutional successor to the former Kingdom of England. The continuance of [Scots law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law) was guaranteed under the 1706 [Treaty of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Union) that led to the [Acts of Union 1707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707), and as a consequence English law—and after [1801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1800), [Irish law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Ireland)—continued to be separate. Following the two Acts of Union, Parliament can restrict the effect of its laws to part of the realm, and generally the effect of laws, where restricted, was originally applied to one or more of the former kingdoms. Thus, most laws applicable to England also applied to Wales. However, Parliament now passes laws applicable to Wales and not to England (and vice versa), a practice which was rare before the middle of the 20th century. Examples are the [Welsh Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) Acts [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Language_Act_1967) and [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Language_Act_1993) and the [Government of Wales Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Wales_Act_1998). Measures and [Acts of the Senedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Senedd_Cymru) apply in Wales, but not in England.

Following the Government of Wales Act, effective since May 2007, the [Senedd](238480.md) can legislate on matters devolved to it. Following a [referendum on 3 March 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Welsh_devolution_referendum), the Senedd gained direct law-making powers, without the need to consult Westminster. This was the first time in almost 500 years that Wales had its own powers to legislate. Each piece of [Welsh legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_law) is known as an [Act of Senedd Cymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Senedd_Cymru).

### Company registration

Main article: [Companies House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companies_House)

For a [company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company) to be [incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporation_\(business\)) in the United Kingdom, its application for registration with [Companies House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companies_House) must state "whether the company's registered office is to be situated in England and Wales (or in Wales), in Scotland or in Northern Ireland",[^cite_note-10] which will determine the law applicable to that business entity. A registered office must be specified as "in Wales" if the company wishes to use a name ending *cyfyngedig* or *cyf*, rather than *Limited* or *Ltd.* or to avail itself of certain other privileges relating to the official use of the Welsh language.

## Other bodies

Outside the legal system, the position is mixed. Some organisations combine as "England and Wales", others are separate.

- In sports, [cricket](25675557.md) has a [combined international team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_cricket_team) (simply called "England") administered by the [England and Wales Cricket Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_and_Wales_Cricket_Board), who also govern the sport across both nations, and [Glamorgan County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamorgan_County_Cricket_Club) participates in the English and Welsh [county cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_cricket) system. In [football](10568.md), [rugby union](25405.md), [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league), the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games) and other sports have separate national representative teams for each country. A few [Welsh association football clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football_in_Wales), most notably [Cardiff City F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_City_F.C.) and [Swansea City A.F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea_City_A.F.C.), play in the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system), while [The New Saints F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Saints_F.C.), which represents places on both sides of the border, plays in the [Welsh football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_football_league_system). The [Anglo-Welsh Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Welsh_Cup) was a rugby union competition contested by [Welsh regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_regional_rugby) and [English Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_Rugby) clubs between 1971 and 2018.

- Some religious denominations organise on the basis of England and Wales, most notably the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_England_and_Wales), but also small denominations, e.g. the [Evangelical Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Presbyterian_Church_in_England_and_Wales). Prior to the [disestablishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disestablishmentarianism) of the [Church in Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_in_Wales) in 1920, the [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) church in Britain operated under the jurisdiction of the [Church of England](5955.md) throughout Wales and England.

- The [Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Commission_\(United_Kingdom\)) maintains a register of political parties, organised according to where the party operates (either *England*, *Wales* or *England and Wales*). The [Green Party of England and Wales](79787.md) stands candidates only in English and Welsh constituencies; separate Green parties exist [in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Greens) and [in Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_Northern_Ireland).

- Some professional bodies represent England and Wales, for example the [Chartered Institute of Legal Executives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_Institute_of_Legal_Executives), the [Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Chartered_Accountants_in_England_and_Wales), the [General Council of the Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Council_of_the_Bar), [The Law Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Law_Society), the [National Farmers Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Farmers_Union_\(England_and_Wales\)), the [Police Federation of England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Federation_of_England_and_Wales) and the [Trades Union Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trades_Union_Congress).

- Other examples include the [Canal & River Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_&_River_Trust), the [Charity Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_Commission), the [Drinking Water Inspectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_Water_Inspectorate), the [General Register Office for England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Register_Office_for_England_and_Wales), [His Majesty's Inspectorate of Constabulary and Fire & Rescue Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Inspectorate_of_Constabulary_and_Fire_&_Rescue_Services), [HM Land Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Land_Registry), [His Majesty's Prison Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Prison_Service), [Mountain Rescue England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_rescue_in_England_and_Wales), the [NHS Business Services Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS_Business_Services_Authority), [Ofwat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofwat), the [Probation Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probation_Service_\(England_and_Wales\)), the [Solicitors Regulation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitors_Regulation_Authority), the [Worshipful Company of Chartered Accountants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Chartered_Accountants_in_England_and_Wales) and the [Youth Hostels Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_Hostels_Association_\(England_&_Wales\)).
