An annular [solar eclipse](5024887.md) occurred at the Moon’s [ascending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit on Tuesday, February 17, 2026,[^cite_note-February_17,_2026-1] with a [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitude_of_eclipse) of 0.963. A solar eclipse occurs when the [Moon](19331.md) passes between [Earth](9228.md) and the [Sun](26751.md), thereby totally or partly obscuring the image of the Sun for a viewer on Earth. An annular solar eclipse occurs when the Moon's [apparent diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_diameter) is smaller than the Sun's, blocking most of the Sun's light and causing the Sun to look like an [annulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annulus_\(mathematics\)) (ring). An annular eclipse appears as a partial eclipse over a region of the Earth thousands of kilometres wide. The Moon's apparent diameter was near the average diameter because it occurred 6.8 days after [apogee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (on February 10, 2026, at 16\:50 UTC) and 7.5 days before [perigee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (on February 24, 2026, at 23\:15 UTC).[^cite_note-2]

Annularity was visible over [Antarctica](18959138.md) only. However, the partial eclipse was visible from the very southern tip of [Argentina](18951905.md) and [Chile](5489.md), as well as in much of [southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa) (including [South Africa](17416221.md), [Mozambique](19301.md), and [Madagascar](18964.md)).

## Eclipse season

See also: [Eclipse cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_cycle)

The eclipse is part of an [eclipse season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_season), a period, roughly every six months, when eclipses occur. Only two (or occasionally three) eclipse seasons occur each year, and each season lasts about 35 days and repeats just short of six months (173 days) later; thus two full eclipse seasons always occur each year. Either two or three eclipses happen each eclipse season. In the sequence below, each eclipse is separated by a [fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnight).

### Saros 121

This eclipse is a part of [Saros series 121](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Saros_121), repeating every 18 years, 11 days, and containing 71 events. The series started with a partial solar eclipse on April 25, 944 AD. It contains total eclipses from July 10, 1070 through October 9, 1809; hybrid eclipses on October 20, 1827 and October 30, 1845; and annular eclipses from November 11, 1863 through [February 28, 2044](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_February_28,_2044). The series ends at member 71 as a partial eclipse on June 7, 2206. Its eclipses are tabulated in three columns; every third eclipse in the same column is one [exeligmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeligmos) apart, so they all cast shadows over approximately the same parts of the Earth.&#x20;

The longest duration of totality was produced by member 39 at 6 minutes, 20 seconds on June 21, 1629, and the longest duration of annularity will be produced by member 62 at 2 minutes, 27 seconds on [February 28, 2044](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_February_28,_2044). All eclipses in this series occur at the Moon’s [ascending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit.[^cite_note-5]

### Inex series

This eclipse is a part of the long period [inex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inex) cycle, repeating at alternating nodes, every 358 [synodic months](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synodic_month) (≈ 10,571.95 days, or 29 years minus 20 days). Their appearance and longitude are irregular due to a lack of synchronization with the [anomalistic month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalistic_month) (period of perigee). However, groupings of 3 inex cycles (≈ 87 years minus 2 months) comes close (≈ 1,151.02 anomalistic months), so eclipses are similar in these groupings.
