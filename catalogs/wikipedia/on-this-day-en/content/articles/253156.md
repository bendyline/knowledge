The **Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup**, commonly known as the **U.S. Open Cup** (**USOC**), is a [knockout cup competition](939680.md) in men's [soccer](579105.md) in the United States. It is the country's oldest ongoing national soccer competition.[^cite_note-1] The competition was first held during the [1913–1914 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1913%E2%80%9314_National_Challenge_Cup) as the **National Challenge Cup**, with [Brooklyn Field Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Field_Club) winning a trophy donated by [Thomas Dewar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Dewar,_1st_Baron_Dewar) for the promotion of American soccer.[^cite_note-2] For [the 1999 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_U.S._Open_Cup), it was renamed by the [United States Soccer Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation) (USSF) in honor of [Lamar Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar_Hunt), an executive of the [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(1968%E2%80%931984\)) (NASL) and [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS).

[Maccabee Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabee_Los_Angeles) of California and [Bethlehem Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Steel_F.C._\(1907%E2%80%931930\)) of Pennsylvania both won the cup a record five times, while [Greek American AA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_American_AA) of New York and [Seattle Sounders FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC) are tied for the record for most consecutive cup victories at three. Since 2008, the champion of the U.S. Open Cup has earned the right to play in the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup).

The competition is a [single-elimination tournament](939680.md) that has been contested by at least 80 teams since the [2014 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_U.S._Open_Cup). This pool consists of the American clubs in the [professional leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_soccer_league_system), which are [Major League Soccer](71802.md), [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship), and [USL League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_One). It is also [open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_\(sport\)) to amateur teams from [USL League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_Two), [National Premier Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Premier_Soccer_League), the [United States Adult Soccer Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Adult_Soccer_Association), and [US Club Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Club_Soccer).[^cite_note-2014_Format-3]

Amateur and lower-league teams play in months leading up to the formal tournament rounds, with qualifying winners advancing to face USL clubs in geographical pairings in what most recently has been termed the "second round". Pairings in each round are done by draw within geographic areas, until a final bracket of four teams each in four geographic regions is established. After the fourth round, no new teams are introduced, leading to quarterfinals in June, the semifinal round in August, and a final match to determine the champion in September. Every match, including the final, lasts 90 minutes plus any additional [stoppage time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoppage_time). If no clear winner has been determined after 90 minutes of normal time, 30 minutes of [extra time](633242.md) is played. If the score is still level after extra time then the winner is decided by a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md).[^cite_note-2014_Format-3]

## Structure

In its current format, the U.S. Open Cup is contested by approximately 100 clubs from the [professional leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_soccer_league_system) sanctioned by the USSF, MLS, the [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship) and [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_One) divisions, and [MLS Next Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Next_Pro) – as well as amateur clubs in the earlier rounds of the tournament that qualify through their respective leagues. The overall champion is awarded \$600,000 in [prize money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize_money) and a berth in the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup), while the runner-up receives \$250,000, and the furthest-advancing team from each lower-division league receives \$50,000.[^cite_note-4]

### MLS era

MLS teams have dominated the competition since the league began play in 1996. No lower division team has won the Open Cup since the [Rochester Rhinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Rhinos) in 1999, and the most recent lower division team to reach the final was [Sacramento Republic FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Republic_FC) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_U.S._Open_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-5] The most recent champions of the competition, [Nashville SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_SC), won their first title after defeating [Austin FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_FC) in the [2025 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_U.S._Open_Cup_final).[^cite_note-6]

The [2022 U.S. Open Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_U.S._Open_Cup) marked the return of the competition after the 2020 and 2021 tournaments were canceled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), ending 106 years of consecutive play.[^cite_note-Cancelled2020-7][^cite_note-8]

### Pre-2023 formats

Through the 2011 edition, eight teams from each level of the [United States soccer league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_soccer_league_system) took part in the competition proper, with each league narrowing its delegation separately in the spring before the competition officially began in the summer. In some cases, additional teams played in qualifying rounds to gain entry. One example was found with MLS clubs, as only the top six from the previous regular season received automatic bids, while the bottom U.S.-based MLS teams faced each other to qualify for the remaining two MLS slots.

Beginning in 2012, the competition was expanded from its previous 40 teams to 64, with the qualifying process radically changed. The [National Premier Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Premier_Soccer_League) received six places, plus the possibility of a seventh in a playoff against a team from the amateur [US Club Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Club_Soccer) setup. Nine clubs from the USASA earned places, as did 16 [USL Premier Development League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_Two) teams. Each of these organizations has its own qualifying process to determine its entrants. These 32 teams competed in the first round of the Cup, with the winners meeting all 16 USL Pro and NASL teams in the second round. All 16 U.S.-based MLS teams entered in the third round.

In 2013, the competition was expanded to 68 teams. All U.S.-based Division I, II and III teams participated in the tournament proper: 16 from Major League Soccer, six from the North American Soccer League and 12 from USL Pro. The remaining 34 spots in the tournament field were filled by amateur teams from the Adult Council category–16 from the Premier Development League, eight from U.S. Adult Soccer Association regional qualifying, eight from the National Premier Soccer League, one from US Club Soccer and one from the [United States Specialty Sports Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Specialty_Sports_Association).

The process for determining the site for the Open Cup tournament semifinals and final was changed in 2013. In past years, the sites for the final three matches of the tournament had been determined through a sealed-bid process, but in 2013 the hosts of those games were determined by a coin flip. Home teams throughout the entire tournament were determined by random selection.[^cite_note-100thEdition-9]

Since 2008, the champion of the U.S. Open Cup has earned the right to play in the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup). The first team to represent the U.S. as Open Cup champion was 2007's winner, [New England Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Revolution).[^cite_note-CCL2008-10]

Starting in 2016, lower-division professional clubs owned by higher-division professional clubs are no longer eligible to participate in the U.S. Open Cup. This removed the MLS reserve clubs in USL from the 2016 competition, after issues of clubs holding back players from their USL sides in 2015 in order to keep them eligible to play for the parent MLS club. Players are [only allowed to play for one club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-tied) in any US Open Cup season.[^cite_note-11] Amateur clubs remain eligible to enter even if they are owned by professional clubs. Initially, "hybrid affiliate" clubs—i.e., lower-division professional clubs that are staffed but not owned by higher-division clubs—also remained eligible, but those clubs were also banned effective with the 2016 competition. This last change was proposed by the [Houston Dynamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Dynamo), which were the senior club to [Rio Grande Valley FC Toros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_Valley_FC_Toros) in the first such arrangement in the U.S. game; that arrangement ended after the 2021 season, and those two teams would be drawn against each other in 2022.[^cite_note-12]

### Limited MLS participation (2024–2026)

On December 15, 2023, MLS announced that affiliated reserve teams from MLS Next Pro would be sent in place of MLS teams for the 2024 edition. U.S. Soccer subsequently denied the request, ruling that MLS teams must compete in the 2024 edition of the U.S. Open Cup.

For the 2025 US Open Cup, US Soccer announced that 16 MLS teams would appear in the fourth round. For the first time in the modern era, the U.S. Open Cup defending champions will not defend their crown with LAFC participating in the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup). Likewise, half of the MLS teams would be given priority hosting and half would be designated as away teams when they begin their US Open Cup appearance in the third round.[^cite_note-13]

The selection of MLS teams for the U.S. Open Cup was folded into a wider scheme to ensure that MLS clubs were represented in all the secondary competitions in which the league's teams compete, while none were overloaded with fixture congestion - The [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup), the zonal [Leagues Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Leagues_Cup), the [Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Canadian_Championship) and the [U.S. Open Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_U.S._Open_Cup). As part of that arrangement, the Division III [MLS Next Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Next_Pro) teams of most of the absent MLS teams based in the United States took part instead, ensuring their representation.

The 16 MLS first teams represented in the [2025 U.S. Open Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_U.S._Open_Cup) were chosen based on the following criteria:[^cite_note-14]

- Teams participating in CONCACAF Champions Cup are excluded from the Open Cup, including the reigning champion [Los Angeles Football Club](44255607.md).

- Teams not participating in the 2025 Leagues Cup qualify for the Open Cup, ranked based on 2024 Supporters Shield standings

- The next best teams in the Supporters’ Shield Standings that are not in the CONCACAF Champions Cup will enter the Open Cup.

- Expansion team [San Diego FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_FC) did not enter the 2025 U.S. Open Cup.

### Full return of MLS teams (from 2027)

Beginning in 2027, all MLS first teams will once again participate in the U.S. Open Cup. Following the first round in April, all MLS teams will enter in the round of 64 in July and will have hosting priority for that round.[^cite_note-15]

## History

Main article: [History of the U.S. Open Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_U.S._Open_Cup)



The original Dewar Trophy for the National Challenge Cup, used from 1914 to 1979

The competition dates back to 1913–1914, when it was known as the "National Challenge Cup". The winners of the tournament were awarded the [Dewar Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewar_Cup), donated by Sir [Thomas Dewar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Dewar) for the promotion of soccer in the United States in 1912, until it was retired due to poor condition in 1979. It was brought back into use by the [United States Adult Soccer Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Adult_Soccer_Association) in 1997, but is now back on permanent display at the [National Soccer Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Soccer_Hall_of_Fame) at [Toyota Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Stadium_\(Texas\)) in [Frisco, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisco,_Texas), and the recent winners of the tournament have been awarded a new, different trophy. In addition to this, the name of each winning club is still added to the base of the original Dewar Cup. In 1999, U.S. Soccer honored patron [Lamar Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar_Hunt) by changing the official title of the tournament to the "Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup".

The National Challenge Cup was the first truly national cup competition in the United States, as previous cups had been effectively relegated to regional status by the difficulties in coordination and travel caused by the size of the United States in the early 1900s. While U.S. Soccer had initially administered the competition, in 1985 they handed over management to the USASA. In 1995, U.S. Soccer resumed its administration of the competition, marking the beginning of the tournament's modern era.[^cite_note-16]

From the 1960s to the early 1990s, teams from top professional leagues including the [NASL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(1968%E2%80%9384\)) did not participate in the Open Cup.[^cite_note-17]

Since MLS' debut in 1996, MLS clubs have won the cup in all but one of those years. The [Rochester Rhinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Rhinos) of the 2nd division [A-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-League_\(1995%E2%80%932004\)) were surprise winners in 1999, defeating four MLS clubs, including the [Colorado Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rapids) 2–0 in the championship match. The first professional team to win in the modern era were the [Richmond Kickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Kickers) of the USISL (the predecessor to the A-League, later known as the [USL First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_First_Division), USL Pro, United Soccer League, and now as the [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship)) in 1995, one year before the start of MLS. [D.C. United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.C._United) were the first MLS team to win in 1996.

The tournament was expanded to 64 teams in 2012 with all MLS teams in the United States receiving an automatic berth; previously, MLS participants were determined through a qualifying tournament or were selected based on league standings.[^cite_note-18] A rule change enacted in 2016 removed U.S. Open Cup entries for teams that were majority-owned by another team in a higher tier, which mostly affected [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship) teams operated by MLS as reserve or developmental squads.[^cite_note-19]

The first round of the [2020 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_U.S._Open_Cup) was suspended in March 2020 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The tournament was canceled entirely in August 2020, with all qualified teams automatically qualified for the [following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_U.S._Open_Cup); however, the 2021 Open Cup was canceled as well, due to schedule congestion as an effect of the pandemic.[^cite_note-Cancelled2020-7][^cite_note-20] The tournament resumed in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_U.S._Open_Cup) with 71 professional clubs out of a total field of 103, both modern-era records.[^cite_note-Open_Cup_returns_in_2022-21]

On December 15, 2023, [Major League Soccer](71802.md) announced that its teams would no longer enter in the U.S. Open Cup starting in the [2024 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_U.S._Open_Cup), instead opting to send their [MLS Next Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Next_Pro) teams.[^cite_note-22] However, on December 20, 2023, U.S. Soccer announced that they had denied MLS the necessary waiver to allow affiliated MLS Next Pro teams to play in the tournament.[^cite_note-23] A compromise announced on March 1, 2024, allowed eight MLS teams to participate with senior squads and eleven to be represented by MLS Next Pro teams; the teams participating in the [2024 CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup) will not send teams to the Open Cup.

For the 2025 US Open Cup, US Soccer announced that 16 MLS teams would appear in the fourth round. For the first time in the modern era, the U.S. Open Cup defending champions will not defend their crown with LAFC participating in the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Broadcasting

[ESPN+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN+) had exclusive broadcast rights for the 2019 and 2022 Open Cups.[^cite_note-44] Prior to 2019, the tournament had little broadcast exposure, with the final and select matches being broadcast on ESPN networks and all other matches streamed through the USSF's [YouTube](3524766.md) channel.

On March 1, 2022, U.S. Soccer and [Turner Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Sports) announced an 8-year exclusive multimedia rights deal for the United States [men's](582488.md) and [women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team) national teams. Cindy Parlow Cone, president of U.S. Soccer, confirmed in a separate interview that the new deal would include the Open Cup.[^cite_note-2023-30_U.S._Soccer_Rights-45][^cite_note-46] Early round U.S. Open Cup matches will air on the [Bleacher Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleacher_Report) app and the Bleacher Report Football YouTube channel.[^cite_note-47]

In 2023, [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports) reached an agreement to air the semifinals and final of the Cup. The semifinals aired on the [CBS Sports Golazo Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_Golazo_Network), with the final on [CBS Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_Network) and [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+).[^cite_note-48] In 2024, [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV+), via [MLS Season Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Season_Pass), announced an agreement to air the quarterfinals, semifinals and final of the Cup.[^cite_note-49]
