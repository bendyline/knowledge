**Cagnes-sur-Mer** (French pronunciation: [\[kaɲ syʁ mɛʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French), literally *Cagnes on Sea*; [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language): *Canha de Mar*) is a [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera) commune near [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) in the [Alpes-Maritimes](80989.md) department, in the [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur) region, in southeastern [France](5843419.md). It is part of the [Métropole Nice Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tropole_Nice_C%C3%B4te_d'Azur). Its inhabitants are known as *Cagnois*.&#x20;

## Geography

Cagnes-sur-Mer is a commune in southeastern [France](5843419.md) on the shores of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) between [Saint-Laurent-du-Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Laurent-du-Var) and [Villeneuve-Loubet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeneuve-Loubet). The largest suburb of [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), it stretches along a cove offering nearly 4 kilometres (2.5 mi) of beach and is surrounded by hills, including that of the castle, which rises to 91 metres (299 ft) above sea level.

The municipality covers an area of 1,795 hectares; the elevation ranges from 0 to 187 meters. It is classified as seismic zone 4 (moderate seismicity) and some earthquakes are felt in Cagnes-sur-Mer.[^cite_note-3]

### Hydrography

A coastal river and its main tributary run through the town: the Cagne and the Malvan.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The Loup river also borders the commune and, in some places, forms the boundary with the commune of Villeneuve-Loubet.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

Cagnes-sur-Mer has a wastewater treatment plant with a capacity of 130,000 [PE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_equivalent).[^cite_note-5]

### Climate

In 2010, the municipality’s climate was classified as [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_France), according to a study by the [National Center for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) based on a method combining climate data and environmental factors (topography, land use, etc.) and data covering the period 1971–2000.[^cite_note-\:1-6] In 2020, the climate was classified as Csa (a temperate climate with hot, dry summers), according to the [Köppen-Geiger classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) for the period 1988–2017.[^cite_note-7] Also in 2020, [Météo-France](4732627.md) published a new [climate typology for metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_France) which classified the commune within the Mediterranean climate as part of the climatic region Var, Alpes-Maritimes,[^cite_note-8] which is characterized by heavy rainfall in autumn and winter (250-300mm in autumn), abundant sunshine in summer (>75% solar insolation), a mild winter (8°C), and little fog.[^cite_note-9]

For the period 1971–2000, the average annual temperature was 15.8°C, with an annual temperature range of 0°C. The average annual precipitation was 834 mm, with 6.5 days of precipitation in January and 2.1 days in July.[^cite_note-\:1-6] For the period 1991–2020, the average annual temperature recorded at the nearest Météo-France weather station, located in the town of Antibes 9km away as the crow flies, is 15.9°C, and the average annual precipitation is 879.5 mm.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The highest temperature recorded at this station is 38.5 °C, reached on July 17, 2003; the lowest temperature is −4.4 °C, reached on March 1, 2005 (based on data collected between July 1, 1988 and October 2, 2025).

## History

The [Château Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_Grimaldi_\(Cagnes\)) was established by [Rainier Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainier_I_of_Monaco,_Lord_of_Cagnes) at the top of a hill in the area now known as "Haut-de-Cagnes" in 1309.[^cite_note-12] A quite separate coastal area, known as "Cros-de-Cagnes", was developed by fishermen, who came twice a year from [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton), in the late 18th century.[^cite_note-13]

The [Hôtel de Ville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Ville,_Cagnes-sur-Mer) was established in the "Les Logis" area, which developed in the 19th century.[^cite_note-14]

The town was the retreat and final address of the [Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionist) painter [Pierre-Auguste Renoir](24546.md), who moved there in 1907 in an attempt to improve his [arthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthritis) and remained there until his death in 1919 ([Musée Renoir](84175718.md) was opened in his house). In the late 1920s, Cagnes-sur-Mer became a residence for many [American](3434750.md) renowned literary and art figures, such as [Katie Boyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Boyle), [George Antheil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Antheil), and [Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Crosby) and [Caresse Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caresse_Crosby).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The [Belgian](3343.md) author [Georges Simenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Simenon) (1903–1989), creator of the fictional detective *Commissaire [Jules Maigret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Maigret)*, lived at 98 montée de la Bourgade in the 1950s with his third wife and their three children; his initial "S" may still be seen in the wrought iron on the stairs.

The [Parisian](22989.md) artist, poet, and philosopher Georges Charaire had a home in Cagnes for many years. Creating his lithographs in the former studio of [Paul Gauguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gauguin) and also as a co-founder of the théâtre du Tertre in [Montmartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montmartre), Charaire had a great influence on French art and thought in the 20th century and helped his friend [Eugène Ionesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Ionesco) with his first plays at the théâtre du Tertre. Charaire kept his second home in Cagnes-sur-Mer until his death in 2001.

The [Belarusian-French](3457.md) artist [Chaïm Soutine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha%C3%AFm_Soutine) created powerful and fanciful landscapes of southern France. A friend of [Amedeo Modigliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amedeo_Modigliani), Soutine left colourful landscapes from Cagnes starting in 1924. The [Spanish](26667.md) [Fauvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauvist) painter [Francisco Iturrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Iturrino) also lived in the town, where he died. The town may have been an inspiration for [Henri-Edmond Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri-Edmond_Cross) (1856–1910), the [Neo-Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Impressionist) artist who painted *Cypresses at Cagnes* (1908).

## Sights

Places of interest include Renoir's estate, *Les Collettes*, surrounded by olive trees; the medieval [castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle) at *le Haut-de-Cagnes*; and the *Cros* area, which was founded by [Italian](14532.md) fishermen in the 19th century.

The town is also known for its horse-racing venue, the [Hippodrome de la Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippodrome_de_la_C%C3%B4te_d'Azur), as well as for its 4 km (2 mi) beach.

## Transport

The [Gare de Cagnes-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_de_Cagnes-sur-Mer) is a railway station that offers local services to Nice and [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes).

## International relations

in 1973, the commune [twinned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_city) with [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau) in Germany.[^cite_note-19]
