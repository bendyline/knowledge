The **Serbian Armed Forces** ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): Војска Србије, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *Vojska Srbije*) is the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) of [Serbia](29265.md).

The [President of the Republic](774382.md) acts as [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the armed forces, while administration and defence policy is carried out by the [Government](608371.md) through the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Serbia\)). The highest operational authority, in charge of the deployment and preparation of the armed forces in peace and war, is the [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_General_Staff). [Military service](938728.md) is voluntary, though [conscription](5735.md) may occur in wartime.

The Serbian Armed Forces consists of two branches: the [Serbian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Army) and the [Serbian Air Force and Air Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Air_Force_and_Air_Defence).

## History

Main article: [Military history of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Serbia)

Main article: [History of the Serbian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Serbian_Army)

Main article: [History of the Serbian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Serbian_Air_Force)

Serbia has a long military tradition dating to early medieval period. The modern Serbian military dates back to the [Serbian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_revolution) which started in 1804 with the [First Serbian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Serbian_Uprising) against [the Ottoman occupation of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Serbia). The victories in the battles of [Ivankovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ivankovac) (1805), [Mišar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mi%C5%A1ar) (August 1806), [Deligrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Deligrad) (December 1806) and [Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1806\)) (November–December 1806), led to the establishment of the [Principality of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Serbia) in 1817. The subsequent [Second Serbian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Serbian_Uprising) of 1815–1817 led to full independence and recognition of the [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia) and weakened the [Ottoman](22278.md) dominance in the [Balkans](4829.md). In November 1885 the [Serbo-Bulgarian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Bulgarian_War) occurred following [Bulgarian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_unification) and resulted in a Bulgarian victory. In 1912 the [First Balkan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Balkan_War) (1912–1913) erupted between the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and the [Balkan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_League) (Serbia, Greece, Montenegro and Bulgaria). Balkan League victories in the [Battle of Kumanovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kumanovo) (October 1912), the [Battle of Prilep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Prilep) (November 1912), the [Battle of Monastir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Monastir) (November 1912), the [Battle of Adrianople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Adrianople_\(1913\)) (November 1912 to March 1913), and the [Siege of Scutari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Scutari_\(1912%E2%80%9313\)) (October 1912 to April 1913) resulted in the defeat of the Ottoman Empire, which lost most of its remaining Balkan territories per the [Treaty of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London_\(1913\)) (May 1913). Shortly after, the [Second Balkan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Balkan_War) (June to August, 1913) broke out when Bulgaria, dissatisfied with the division of territory, declared war against its former allies, Serbia and Greece. Following a string of defeats, Bulgaria requested an [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice) and signed the 1913 [Treaty of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Bucharest_\(1913\)), formally ending the war.



Camp of the [Armed Forces of the Principality of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Principality_of_Serbia) during [Serbian–Ottoman Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian%E2%80%93Ottoman_Wars_\(1876%E2%80%931878\)), 1876

Serbia's independence and growing influence threatened neighboring [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) which led to the [Bosnian crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_crisis) of 1908–09. Consequently, from 1901, all Serbian males between the ages of 21 and 46 became liable for general mobilization.[^cite_note-7] Following the [assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria) in June 1914, Austria-Hungary implicated Serbians and [declared war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Campaign_\(World_War_I\)) on Serbia (July 1914), which marked the beginning of the [First World War](4764461.md) of 1914–1918. Serbian forces repelled three consecutive invasions by Austria in 1914, securing the first major victories of the war for the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I), but were eventually overwhelmed by the combined forces of the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) (October–November 1915) and forced to [retreat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_army's_retreat_through_Albania) through [Albania](738.md) (1915–1916) to the [Greek](12108.md) island of [Corfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corfu) (1915–1916).

Serbian military activity after World War I took place in the context of [various Yugoslav armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Army_\(disambiguation\)) until the [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia) in the 1990s and the restoration of Serbia as an [independent state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Serbia) in 2006.

## Organization

The Serbian Armed Forces are commanded by the [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_General_Staff) corp of senior [officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_officer). The general staff is led by the [Chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_General_Staff_\(Serbia\)). The [President of the Republic](774382.md) who is the [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief) appoints the Chief of the General Staff on the suggestion of the Minister of Defence.[^cite_note-8] The current Chief of the General Staff is [General](148537.md) Milan Mojsilović.



Organization of the Serbian Armed Forces

### Service branches

The armed forces consist of the following service branches:

-  [Serbian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Army)

-  [Serbian Air Force and Air Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Air_Force_and_Air_Defence)

#### Serbian Army

Main article: [Serbian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Army)

The Serbian Army (*Kopnena vojska Srbije* - *KoV*) is the land-based and the largest component of the armed forces consisting of: infantry, armoured, artillery, engineering units as well as [River Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_River_Flotilla). It is responsible for defending the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Serbia; participating in peacekeeping operations; and providing humanitarian aid and disaster relief.

#### Serbian Air Force and Air Defence

Main article: [Serbian Air Force and Air Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Air_Force_and_Air_Defence)

The Serbian Air Force and Air Defence (*Ratno vazduhoplovstvo i protivvazduhoplovna odbrana Vojske Srbije* - *RViPVO*) is the aviation and anti-aircraft defence based component of the armed forces consisting of: aviation, anti-aircraft, surveillance and reconnaissance units. Its mission is to guard and protect the sovereignty of Serbian airspace, and jointly with the Army, to protect territorial integrity.

### Command structure

Command structure of the Serbian Armed Forces is centered around General Staff as the highest [command authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_\(military\)), and three separate commands: one for each of the branches (Army Command and Air Force and Air Defence Command) and one responsible for training (Training Command).

#### General Staff

Main article: [Serbian General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_General_Staff)

The Serbian General Staff (*Generalštab Vojske Srbije*) makes strategic and tactical preparations and procedures for use during peacetime and war. Special forces ([63rd Parachute Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Parachute_Brigade) and [72nd Brigade for Special Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_Brigade_for_Special_Operations)) are under direct command of the Chief of the General Staff. Organizational units of the Armed Forces subordinated to the General Staff are: [Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_of_the_Serbian_Armed_Forces), [Signal Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Brigade_\(Serbia\)), [Central Logistics Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Logistics_Base), [224th Center for Electronic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/224th_Center_for_Electronic_Action), [Technical Testing Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_Testing_Center), [Peacekeeping Operations Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_Operations_Center), as well as the Directorate of Military Police (which includes [Criminal Investigative Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Investigative_Group) and [Detachment of the Military Police for Special Operations "Cobras"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobras_\(Serbia\))).[^cite_note-Org-9]

#### Army Command

Army Command (*Komanda Kopnene vojske*) is responsible for unitary, administrative and operational control of the Army. Army Command headquarters is in [Niš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C5%A1).

#### Air Force and Air Defence Command

Air Force and Air Defence Command (*Komanda Ratnog vazduhoplovstva i protivvazduhoplovne odbrane*) is responsible for unitary, administrative and operational control of the Air Force and Air Defence. Its headquarters is in [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun).

#### Training Command

The [Training Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_Command_\(Serbia\)) (*Komanda za obuku*) is responsible for providing basic and specialist training for soldiers, non-commissioned officers and officers of Serbian Armed Forces as well the members of foreign armies. It also serves the role of maintaining the reserve regional brigade structure of the Serbian Armed Forces.

## Personnel

See also: [Conscription in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_Serbia)



Serbian Army infantrymen during 2020 *Sadejstvo* exercise

The Serbian Armed Forces are composed entirely of professionals and volunteers following the suspension of [mandatory military service](5735.md) in 2011.&#x20;

### Active personnel

There are 22,500 active members: 4,200 officers, 6,500 non-commissioned officers, 8,200 [active-duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_duty) soldiers and 3,500 civilians in [volunteer military service](9354649.md).[^cite_note-auto-2] It breaks down as follows:

- General Staff (including attached units: special forces brigades, Guard, Signal Brigade, logistics, etc.): 4,300

- Army Command: 13,200

- Air Force and Air Defence Command: 3,000

- Training Command: 2,300

### Reserve force

The [reserve force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_force) is composed of an active reserve and passive (i.e. war-time) reserve. The **active reserve** forces have 2,000 members and they are generally required to perform 45 days of military service per year.[^cite_note-novosti-3] They are assigned to one of four reserve territorial brigades (Banat Brigade, Belgrade Brigade, Timok Brigade, and Rasina Brigade), each having active HQ, command company and logistics company predicted for rapid deployment in case of war. The **passive reserve** totals about 600,000 citizens with past military training or experience and is activated only in the events of war.[^cite_note-novosti-3]

## Equipment

Main article: [List of equipment of the Serbian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Serbian_Armed_Forces)

The Serbian Armed Forces has a wide variety of equipment, mix of new equipment, either domestically-produced from Serbian defence contractors or acquired from foreign producers (main suppliers being [France](5843419.md), [China](5405.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Russia](25391.md), and to a lesser extent [Germany](11867.md)) and older [Yugoslav](34244.md) and [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) products (dating back to the 1980s, modernized or being in the process of modernization).

The inventory of the Serbian Army includes: 242 tanks (30 Russian-made [T-72 B1MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72) and 212 Yugoslav-made [M-84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-84)), 96 self-propelled howitzers (24 domestically-produced [Nora B-52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nora_B-52) and 72 Soviet-made [Gvozdika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gvozdika)), Israeli-made [PULS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PULS_\(multiple_rocket_launcher\)) multiple rocket launcher systems (armed with [Predator Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator_Hawk) tactical ballistic missiles and [EXTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EXTRA_artillery_rocket_system) artillery rockets) and 60 Yugoslav-made [M-77 Oganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-77_Oganj) multiple rocket launcher systems, 320 Yugoslav-made [BVP M-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BVP_M-80) infantry fighting vehicles, 80 domestically-produced [Lazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_3) armoured personnel carriers, 108 Russian [BTR-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-80) amphibious armoured personnel carriers, some 130 domestically-produced MRAPs ([M20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_M20_MRAP) and [Miloš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOV_M16_Milo%C5%A1)) as well as around 30 pieces of domestically-produced [PASARS-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PASARS-16) short-range air-defence missile system (armed with French [Mistral 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(missile\)) missiles).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The Serbian Air Force and Air Defense has in operational use the following equipment: 13 Soviet-made [MiG-29](185653.md) fighter aircraft (10 of which are modernized to SM standard and armed with [R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77) missiles),[^cite_note-12] 13 Yugoslav-made [J-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soko_J-22_Orao) attack aircraft, 2 Spanish [C-295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA_C-295) transport aircraft,[^cite_note-13] 15 Russian [Mi-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-35) attack helicopter (armed with [Ataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M120_Ataka) missiles),[^cite_note-14] 15 German [H145M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC145) utility helicopters (of which ten are armed with HForce weapon system), 5 Russian [Mi-17](1493374.md) utility helicopters, Israeli [Hermes 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbit_Hermes_900) and Chinese CH-95 and [CH-92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASC_Rainbow) combat drones, Chinese [HQ-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-22) and [HQ-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-17) medium- and short-range air-defence missile systems (4 and 2 batteries respectively),[^cite_note-15] as well as one battery of Russian [Pantsir](8645085.md) short-range air-defence missile system (2 more on order but delivery postponed due to sanctions on Russia).

Over the last several years, Serbia has embarked on an ambitious program of equipment modernization and acquisition. Whenever possible, the Serbian Ministry of Defence favors products that are manufactured in Serbia, such as: [Lazar 3M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_3) infantry fighting vehicles, [Lazar 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_3) armoured personnel carriers, [M20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_M20_MRAP) and [Miloš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOV_M16_Milo%C5%A1) MRAPs, [Nora B-52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nora_B-52) self-propelled howitzers, [ALAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALAS_\(missile\)) guided missiles, [Lasta 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utva_Lasta) training aircraft, [Pegaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegaz) combat drones, [Gavran 145](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavran_145) and [Osica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osica_\(drone\)) loitering munitions, [Vrabac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrabac_\(drone\)) reconnaissance drones, and [Zastava M19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_M19) assault rifles. Largest procurement of foreign equipment included: Chinese [HQ-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-22) and [HQ-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-17) air-defence missile systems; Israeli [PULS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PULS_\(multiple_rocket_launcher\)) multiple rocket launcher systems; Airbus [H145M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC145) utility helicopters; Russian [Mi-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-35) attack helicopters; as well as various missile and radar acquisitions (Israeli [Predator Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator_Hawk) tactical ballistic missiles and [EXTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EXTRA_artillery_rocket_system) artillery rockets for PULS multiple rocket launcher systems; French surface-to-air [Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(missile\)) missiles for PASARS-16 short-range air-defence missile system; Russian [R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77) air-to-air missiles for MiG-29 fighter aircraft, [Ataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M120_Ataka) air-to-surface missiles for Mi-35 attack helicopters, and [Kornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M133_Kornet) man-portable anti-tank guided missiles; as well as French [Ground Master 400α](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Master_400) and [Ground Master 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Master_200) long- and medium-range radar systems), and drones (Israeli [Hermes 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbit_Hermes_900) and Chinese CH-95 and [CH-92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASC_Rainbow#CH-92) combat drones; Israeli SkyStriker and Lanius-X loitering munition as well as gamma of Emirati loitering munition - Shadow 25, Shadow 50, and SM2). Also, various Russian electronic warfare systems were acquired: [Krasukha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasukha), Moskva-1, and [Repellent-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repellent-1).

Significant acquisitions of military equipment are also planned in the near future. Purchase of 12 new French [Rafale](33731380.md) multirole fighter aircraft was announced in 2024 with the aim of replacing MiG-29 which will be in service until the end of the 2020s.[^cite_note-16] In 2025, a \$1.6 billion contract was signed with Israeli defence company, [Elbit Systems](1341669.md), focusing on advanced command and control systems (including intelligence solutions that encompass all operational levels, from strategic to tactical), electro-optical devices, and night vision.[^cite_note-17] Purchase of Chinese [HQ-9](5341441.md) long-range air-defence and anti-ballistic missile systems was announced in 2026.[^cite_note-18]

## Traditions

### Motto

Motto of the Serbian Armed Forces is "For Freedom and Honour of the Fatherland" (*Za slobodu i čast Otadžbine*) and is found on uniforms as well as on brigade flags.

### Armed Forces Day

Serbian Armed Forces Day (*Dan Vojske Srbije*) is marked on 23 April, the anniversary of the [Second Serbian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Serbian_Uprising). On that day in 1815, in [Takovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takovo), prominent elders met and decided to start the fight for liberation of Serbia from the [Turkish](22278.md) authorities, which eventually led to the free and independent Serbia.

### Patron saint

The patron saint (*[krsna slava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slava_\(tradition\))*) of the Serbian Armed Forces is [Saint Stefan Visoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Lazarevi%C4%87). The first celebration was held in 2023; earlier that year, the Holy Council of Bishops of the Serbian Orthodox Church decided that the patron saint of the Serbian Armed Forces should be Saint Stefan Visoki, 15th-century Serbian ruler and saint, remembered as a wise statesman and a successful military leader.[^cite_note-19]

### Marches and marching music



The high-stepping Parade March in the 2025 *Snaga jedinstva* military parade

The Serbian military was the first to pioneer the high-step as a [military step](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_step). It is similar to the [goose step](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose_step), with the difference being that the knee is bent at the top of the arc. It was used by the Royal Yugoslav Army and at the time was called the "male step". The Yugoslav People's Army abandoned it after World War II, being in use for over two decades before being replaced by high-stepping in the 1975 [Victory Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Day_Parade), to assert itself as independent from Soviet influence. High-stepping is still used today by Serbian Armed Forces.

There are several [marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_\(music\)) in use in Serbian Armed Forces. The standard one is Parade March (*Paradni marš*), while the [Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_of_the_Serbian_Armed_Forces) uses its own Guard March (*Gardijski marš*) as standard march music. Also frequently used and the most popular and recognizable by the general public in Serbia is famous [March on the Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_on_the_Drina) (*Marš na Drinu*). Other frequently used march is Vojvoda Stepa Stepanović March (*Marš vojvode Stepe Stepanovića*).
