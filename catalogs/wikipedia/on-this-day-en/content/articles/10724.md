The **French Armed Forces** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Forces armées françaises*, pronounced [\[fɔʁs aʁme fʁɑ̃sɛːz\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) are the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) of [France](5843419.md). They consist of four [service branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch): The [Army](611740.md), the [Navy](383256.md), the [Air and Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_and_Space_Force), and the [National Gendarmerie](6330819.md). The [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(France\)) serves as the French Armed Forces' [military reserve force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_force).&#x20;

Under the [French constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_France), the [president of France](24899.md) serves as [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the French military. The [Chief of the Defence Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff_\(France\)), currently [*Général d'armée aérienne*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_general_\(France\)) [Fabien Mandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabien_Mandon),[^cite_note-5] oversees all military operations.

France has the [ninth largest defense budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures) in the world and the second largest in the [European Union](9317.md) (EU). It also has the [largest military by size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_military_and_paramilitary_personnel) in the EU.[^cite_note-SIPRI-6] As of 2025, the total active personnel of the French Armed Forces are 264,000, along with 43,000 reservists; including the National Gendarmerie (which numbered 155,000 in 2024),[^cite_note-7] France's military manpower is 462,000.[^cite_note-8]

France is frequently considered a top-tier military power, often ranking among the top ten due to its global capabilities and independent nuclear umbrella; according to the 2026 Global Firepower Index, the French Armed Forces are the world's sixth most powerful military.[^cite_note-9]

The Armed Forces continuously rank as one the institutions in which the French people have the highest confidence. A 2023 [IFOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_fran%C3%A7ais_d'opinion_publique) poll showed that 89% of the French trusted their Armed Forces.[^cite_note-10]

## History

Main article: [Military history of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_France)

The military history of France encompasses an immense panorama of conflicts and struggles extending for more than 2,000 years across areas, including modern France, greater Europe, and [French territorial possessions overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire). According to British historian [Niall Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niall_Ferguson), the French participated in 50 of the 125 major European wars that have been fought since 1495; more than any other European state. They are followed by the Austrians who fought in 47 of them, the Spanish in 44 and the English (and later British) who were involved in 43. In addition, out of all recorded conflicts which occurred since the year 387 BC, France has fought in 168 of them, won 109, lost 49 and drawn 10.

The Gallo-Roman conflict predominated from 60 BC to 50 BC, with the Romans emerging victorious in the [conquest of Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallic_Wars) by [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar). After the decline of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), a [Germanic tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) known as the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) took control of [Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul) by defeating competing tribes. The "land of Francia", from which France gets its name, had high points of expansion under kings [Clovis I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_I) and [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne). In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), rivalries with England and the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) prompted major conflicts such as the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest) and the [Hundred Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Years'_War). With an increasingly centralized monarchy, the first standing army since Roman times, and the use of artillery, France expelled the English from its territory and came out of the Middle Ages as the most powerful nation in Europe, only to lose that status to Spain following defeat in the [Italian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars). The [Wars of Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Wars_of_Religion) crippled France in the late 16th century, but a major victory in the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) made France one of the most powerful nations on the continent once more. In parallel, France developed its [first colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) in Asia, Africa, and in the Americas. Under [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV), France achieved military supremacy over its rivals, but escalating conflicts against increasingly powerful enemy coalitions [checked French ambitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession) and left the kingdom bankrupt at the opening of the 18th century.

Resurgent French armies secured victories in dynastic conflicts against the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Quadruple_Alliance), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Polish_Succession), and [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession) crowns. At the same time, France was [fending off attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_Wars) on its colonies. As the 18th century advanced, global competition with Great Britain led to the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), where France lost its [North American holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France). Consolation came in the form of dominance in Europe and the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), where [extensive French aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_in_the_American_Revolutionary_War) in the form of money and arms, and the [direct participation of its army and navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-French_War_\(1778%E2%80%9383\)) led to America's independence.[^cite_note-aid-11] Internal political upheaval eventually led to 23 years of nearly continuous conflict in the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars) and the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars). France reached the zenith of its power during this period, dominating the European continent in an unprecedented fashion under [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte), but by 1815 it had been restored to its pre-Revolutionary borders. The rest of the 19th century witnessed the growth of the [Second French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_colonial_empire) as well as French interventions in [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Days'_Campaign), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Carlist_War), and [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_intervention_in_Mexico_\(disambiguation\)). Other major wars were fought against [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) in the [Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italian_War_of_Independence), and [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) within France itself.

Following defeat in the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War), [Franco-German rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French%E2%80%93German_enmity) erupted again in the First World War. France and its allies were victorious this time. Social, political, and economic upheaval in the wake of the conflict led to the Second World War, in which the Allies were defeated in the [Battle of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France) and the French government surrendered and was replaced with an [authoritarian regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France). The [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II), including the government in exile's [Free French Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French_Forces) and later a liberated French nation, eventually emerged victorious over the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers). As a result, France secured an [occupation zone in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany) and a permanent seat on the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md).

The imperative of avoiding a third Franco-German conflict on the scale of those of two world wars paved the way for [European integration](683704.md) starting in the 1950s. France became a [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_de_frappe) with its [first test of an atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerboise_Bleue) in [Algeria](358.md) in 1960.[^cite_note-12] Since the 1990s its military action is most often seen in cooperation with [NATO](21133.md) and its European partners.

## International stance

See also: [France and weapons of mass destruction](589091.md)

Today, French military doctrine is based on the concepts of national independence, nuclear deterrence (*see [Force de dissuasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_de_dissuasion)*), and military self-sufficiency. [France](5843419.md) is a charter member of [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization), and has worked actively with its allies to adapt NATO—internally and externally—to the post-[Cold War](325329.md) environment. In December 1995, France announced that it would increase its participation in NATO's military wing, including the Military Committee (France withdrew from NATO's military bodies in 1966 whilst remaining full participants in the Organisation's political Councils). France remains a firm supporter of the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) and other cooperative efforts. Paris hosted the May 1997 NATO-Russia [Summit](3271361.md) which sought the signing of the Founding Act on Mutual Relations, Cooperation and Security. Outside of NATO, France has actively and heavily participated in both coalition and unilateral peacekeeping efforts in [Africa](5334607.md), the [Middle East](19323.md), and the [Balkans](4829.md), frequently taking a lead role in these operations. France has undertaken a major restructuring to develop a professional military that will be smaller, more rapidly deployable, and better tailored for operations outside of mainland France. Key elements of the restructuring include: reducing personnel, bases and headquarters, and rationalisation of equipment and the [armaments industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_industry_in_France).

Since the end of the [Cold War](325329.md), France has placed a high priority on arms control and non-proliferation. French Nuclear testing in the [Pacific](23070.md), and the [sinking of the *Rainbow Warrior*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Rainbow_Warrior) strained French relations with its Allies, South Pacific states (namely [New Zealand](4913064.md)), and world opinion. France agreed to the [Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Non-Proliferation_Treaty) in 1992 and supported its indefinite extension in 1995. After conducting a controversial final series of six nuclear tests on [Mururoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mururoa) in the [South Pacific](23070.md), the French signed the [Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Test_Ban_Treaty) in 1996. Since then, France has implemented a moratorium on the production, export, and use of anti-personnel [landmines](18172.md) and supports negotiations leading toward a universal ban. The French are key players in the adaptation of the Treaty on Conventional Armed Forces in Europe to the new strategic environment. France remains an active participant in: the major programs to restrict the transfer of technologies that could lead to the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction: the Nuclear Suppliers Group, the [Australia Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Group) (for chemical and biological weapons), and the [Missile Technology Control Regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Technology_Control_Regime). France has also signed and ratified the [Chemical Weapons Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Weapons_Convention).

### White Papers

Main article: [2008 French White Paper on Defence and National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_French_White_Paper_on_Defence_and_National_Security)

#### 2008

On 31 July 2007, President [Nicolas Sarkozy](557007.md) ordered M. Jean-Claude Mallet, a member of the Council of State, to head up a thirty-five member commission charged with a wide-ranging review of French defence. The commission issued its [White Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Paper) in early 2008.[^cite_note-13]

Acting upon its recommendations, President Sarkozy began making radical changes in French defense policy and structures starting in the summer of 2008. In keeping with post-[Cold War](325329.md) changes in European politics and power structures, the French military's traditional focus on territorial defence will be redirected to meet the challenges of a global threat environment. Under the reorganisation, the identification and destruction of [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) networks both in metropolitan France and in [francophone Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophone_Africa) will be the primary task of the French military. Redundant military bases will be closed and new weapons systems projects put on hold to finance the restructuring and global deployment of intervention forces. In a historic change, Sarkozy furthermore has declared that France "will now participate fully in [NATO](21133.md)," four decades after former French president General [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) withdrew from the alliance's command structure and ordered American troops off French soil.[^cite_note-14]

#### 2013

Main article: [2013 French White Paper on Defence and National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_French_White_Paper_on_Defence_and_National_Security)

In May 2014, high ranking defence chiefs of the French Armed Forces threatened to resign if the defence budget received further cuts on top of those already announced in the 2013 White Paper. They warned that further cuts would leave the armed forces unable to support operations abroad.[^cite_note-15]

### Recent operations



&#x20; France

&#x20; French military interventions since 2001: [Afghanistan](19666611.md), [Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_French%E2%80%93Ivorian_clashes), [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadian_Civil_War_\(2005%E2%80%9310\)), [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya), [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom_%E2%80%93_Horn_of_Africa), [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mali_conflict), [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic_conflict_under_the_Djotodia_administration), [Syria](30741795.md), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American-led_intervention_in_Iraq_\(2014%E2%80%93present\))

There are currently 36,000 [French troops deployed in foreign territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deployments_of_the_French_military)—such operations are known as "OPEX" for *Opérations Extérieures* ("External Operations"). Among other countries, France provides troops for the United Nations force stationed in [Haiti](13373.md) following the [2004 Haiti rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Haiti_rebellion). France has sent troops, especially [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces), into [Afghanistan](737.md) to help the United States and NATO forces fight the remains of the [Taliban](30635.md) and [Al Qaeda](1921.md). In [Opération Licorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Licorne) a force of a few thousand French soldiers is stationed in [Ivory Coast](57744.md) on a UN peacekeeping mission. These troops were initially sent under the terms of a mutual protection pact between France and the Ivory Coast, but the mission has since evolved into the current UN peacekeeping operation. The French Armed Forces have also played a leading role in the ongoing UN peacekeeping mission along the [Lebanon](17771.md)-[Israel](9282173.md) border as part of the cease-fire agreement that brought the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) to an end. Currently, France has 2,000 army personnel deployed along the border, including infantry, armour, artillery and air defence. There are also naval and air personnel deployed offshore.

The French Joint Force and Training Headquarters (*État-Major Interarmées de Force et d'Entraînement*) at Air Base 110 near [Creil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creil) maintains the ability to command a medium or large-scale international operation, and runs exercises.[^cite_note-16] In 2011, from 19 March, France participated in the enforcement of a [no-fly zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_no-fly_zone) over northern [Libya](17633.md), during the [Libyan Civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_civil_war), in order to prevent forces loyal to [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) from carrying out air attacks on [Anti-Gaddafi forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Gaddafi_forces). This operation was known as [Opération Harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Harmattan) and was part of France's involvement in the conflict in the NATO-led coalition, enforcing [UN Security Council Resolution 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_Resolution_1973). On 11 January 2013 France begun [Operation Serval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Serval) to fight Islamists in [Mali](19127.md) and the [Sahal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel_Region) with African support but without NATO involvement and launched [Operation Barkhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barkhane) to combat terror in African Sahal from 2014 to 2022.

## Personnel



[Hexagone Balard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagone_Balard), the headquarters of the French Armed Forces

The head of the French armed forces is the [President of the Republic](24899.md), in his role as *[chef des armées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chef_des_arm%C3%A9es)*. However, the Constitution puts civil and military government forces at the disposal of the *[gouvernement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_France)* (the executive cabinet of ministers chaired by the [Prime Minister](218751.md), who are not necessarily of the same political side as the president). The [Minister of the Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Armed_Forces_\(France\)) oversees the military's funding, procurement and operations.

Historically, France relied a great deal on [conscription](5735.md) to provide manpower for its military, in addition to a minority of professional career soldiers. Following the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War), the use of non-volunteer draftees in foreign operations was ended; if their unit was called up for duty in war zones, draftees were offered the choice between requesting a transfer to another unit or volunteering for the active mission.

In 1996, President [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac)'s government announced the end of conscription and in 2001, conscription formally was ended. Young people must still, however, register for possible conscription (should the situation call for it). As of 2017 the French Armed Forces have total manpower of 426,265, and has an active personnel of 368,962 (with the [Gendarmerie Nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie_Nationale_\(France\))).[^cite_note-Key_figures_in_2017-21]

It breaks down as follows (2022):[^cite_note-22]

- The [French Army](611740.md); 118,600 personnel.

- The [French Air and Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_and_Space_Force); 43,597 personnel.

- The [French Navy](383256.md); 36,044 personnel.

- Tri-service [DHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Defence_Health_service), [SEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Energy_Service_\(France\)), and [DGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9l%C3%A9gation_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale_pour_l'armement); 17,647 personnel in medical, support and administrative roles, and in the acquisition of weapon systems.

The [reserve element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_forces_of_France) of the French Armed Forces consists of two structures; the Operational Reserve and the Citizens Reserve. As of 2022 the strength of the Operational Reserve is 25,785 personnel.[^cite_note-Key_figures_in_2017-21]

Apart from the three main service branches, the French Armed Forces also includes a fourth military branch called the [National Gendarmerie](6330819.md). It had a reported strength of 103,000 active personnel and 25,000 reserve personnel in 2018.[^cite_note-gendarmerie2011-23] They are used in everyday law enforcement, and also form a coast guard formation under the command of the French Navy. There are however some elements of the Gendarmerie that participate in French external operations, providing specialised law enforcement and supporting roles.

Historically the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(France\)) functioned as the Army's reserve national defense and law enforcement militia. After 145 years since its disbandment, due to the risk of terrorist attacks in the country, the Guard was officially reactivated, this time as a service branch of the Armed Forces, on 12 October 2016.[^cite_note-24]

Since 2019, young French citizens can fulfill the [mandatory service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_conscription) *[Service national universel (SNU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_national_universel)* within the Armed Forces in the service branch of their choice.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

### National Guard (*Garde nationale*)

Main article: [National Guard (France)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(France\))

Reactivated in 2014, the National Guard serves as the official primary military and police reserve service of the Armed Forces. It is placed under the jurisdiction of [Ministry of the Armed Forces](37707412.md) and serves as a [reserve force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_force). It also doubles as a force multiplier for law enforcement personnel during contingencies and to reinforce military personnel whenever being deployed within France and abroad.
