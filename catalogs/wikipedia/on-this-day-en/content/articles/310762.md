# Marathon world record progression

Sabastian Sawe, the men's marathon world record holder.

Sabastian Sawe, the men's marathon world record holder



[Mary Keitany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Keitany) during her women-only world record run at the 2017 London Marathon with 2\:17\:01

**[World records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_record) in the [marathon](49132.md)** are ratified by [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics), the international [governing body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_governing_body) for the sport of [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_\(sport\)).[^cite_note-1]

[Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md) set the men's world record of 1\:59\:30 at the [London Marathon](83030339.md) on April 26, 2026, becoming the first person in history to run a [sub two-hour marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_two-hour_marathon) in race conditions.[^cite_note-2]

Kenyan athlete [Ruth Chepng'etich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Chepng'etich) broke the women's world record with a time of 2\:09\:56 on October 13, 2024, at the [2024 Chicago Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Chicago_Marathon), a mixed-sex race.[^cite_note-3]

In addition to the standard women's marathon world record, World Athletics recognizes a second world record for women in the "Women Only" category for races run on a course without any male athletes in the competition. The current "Women Only" record of 2\:15\:41 was set by [Tigst Assefa](51512379.md) on April 26, 2026, at the London Marathon in the elite women's race.[^cite_note-4]

## History

Marathon races were first held in 1896, but the distance was not standardized by the [International Association of Athletics Federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics) (IAAF, now World Athletics) until 1921.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

The actual distance for pre-1921 races frequently varied from the 1921 standard of 42.195 km (26 miles 385 yards). In qualifying races for the [1896 Summer Olympics](48644.md), Greek runners [Charilaos Vasilakos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charilaos_Vasilakos) (3\:18\:00) and [Ioannis Lavrentis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioannis_Lavrentis) (3\:11\:27) won the first two modern marathons.[^cite_note-7] On April 10, 1896, [Spiridon Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiridon_Louis) of Greece won [the first Olympic marathon in Athens, Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1896_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_marathon), in a time of 2\:58\:50.[^cite_note-IOC1896-8] However, the distance for the event was 40,000 meters.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-11] Three months later, British runner [Len Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Hurst) won the inaugural [Paris to Conflans Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Marathon) (also around 40 km) in a time of 2\:31\:30.[^cite_note-The_origins_of_the_marathon-12] In 1900, Hurst would better his time on the same course with a 2\:26\:28 performance.[^cite_note-16]

Later, [Shizo Kanakuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizo_Kanakuri) of Japan was reported to have set a world record of 2\:32\:45 in a November 1911 domestic qualification race for the [1912 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_Summer_Olympics), but this performance was also run over a distance of approximately 40 km.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-19]

The first marathon over the official distance was won by American [Johnny Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Hayes) at the [1908 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1908_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_marathon), with a time of 2\:55\:18.4.[^cite_note-20]

It is possible that [Stamata Revithi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamata_Revithi), who ran the 1896 Olympic course a day after Louis, is the first woman to run the modern marathon; she is said to have finished in 5+1⁄2 hours.[^cite_note-21] World Athletics credits [Violet Piercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violet_Piercy)'s 1926 performance as the first woman to race the standard marathon distance; however, other sources report that the 1918 performance of [Marie-Louise Ledru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Louise_Ledru) in the Tour de Paris set the initial mark for women.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEARRS_World_Best_Progressions_–_Road2015-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Other "unofficial" performances have also been reported to be world bests or world records over time: although her performance is not recognized by World Athletics, [Adrienne Beames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrienne_Beames) from Australia is frequently credited as the first woman to break the three-hour barrier in the marathon.[^cite_note-timeline-26][^cite_note-28]

In the 1953 [Boston Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon), the top three male finishers were thought to have broken the standing world record,[^cite_note-29] but [Keizo Yamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keizo_Yamada)'s mark of 2\:18\:51 is considered to have been set on a short course of 25.54 miles (41.1 km).[^cite_note-30] The [Boston Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Athletic_Association) also does not report Yamada's performance as a world best for this reason.[^cite_note-BAA-31]

On October 25, 1981, American [Alberto Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Salazar) and New Zealander [Allison Roe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Roe) set apparent world bests at the [1981 New York City Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_New_York_City_Marathon) (2\:08\:13 and 2\:25\:29), however, these marks were invalidated when the course was later found to have been 151 meters short.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-NYC1-33] Although World Athletics' progression notes three performances set on the same course in 1978, 1979, and 1980 by Norwegian [Grete Waitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grete_Waitz), the [Association of Road Racing Statisticians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Road_Racing_Statisticians) considers the New York City course suspect for those performances, too.[^cite_note-NYC2-34]

On April 18, 2011, the Boston Marathon produced what were at that time the two fastest marathon performances in history. Winner [Geoffrey Mutai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Mutai) of Kenya recorded a time of 2\:03\:02,[^cite_note-35] followed by countryman [Moses Mosop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Mosop) in 2\:03\:06. However, since the Boston course does not meet the criteria for record attempts (the starting line is too high relative to the finish), these times were not ratified by the IAAF.

Eight IAAF world records were set at the [Polytechnic Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Marathon) (1909, 1913, 1952–1954, 1963–1965).[^cite_note-ianridpath1-36] WA-recognized world records have been broken at all of the original five [World Marathon Majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Marathon_Majors) on numerous occasions (updated 09/2022); twelve times at the [Berlin Marathon](1663911.md), three times at the Boston Marathon, five times at the [Chicago Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Marathon), six times at the [London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Marathon), and five times at the New York City Marathon. However, the records established in the Boston event have been disputed on grounds of a downhill point-to-point course, while four of the five New York records have been disputed on grounds of a short course.

On April 26, 2026, [Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md) and [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) became the first individuals to run a record-eligible [marathon in under two hours](83044214.md), at the [2026 London Marathon](83030339.md).[^cite_note-37]

## Criteria for record eligibility

See also: [List of world records in athletics § Criteria](3443107.md#Criteria)

For a performance to be ratified as a world record by World Athletics, the marathon course on which the performance occurred must be 42.195 km (26.219 mi) long,[^cite_note-iaaf_rules-38] measured in a defined manner using the [calibrated bicycle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jones_Counter)[^cite_note-39] (the distance in kilometers being the official distance; the distance in miles is an approximation) and meet other criteria that rule out artificially fast times produced on courses aided by downhill slope or tailwind.[^cite_note-Boston_Herald-40] The criteria include:

- "The start and finish points of a course, measured along a theoretical straight line between them, shall not be further apart than 50% of the race distance."

  [^cite_note-iaaf_rules-38]

- "The decrease in elevation between the start and finish shall not exceed an average of one in a thousand (i.e., 1m per km)."

  [^cite_note-iaaf_rules-38]

In recognizing Kenyan [Geoffrey Mutai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Mutai)'s mark of 2\:03\:02 at the [2011 Boston Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Boston_Marathon) as (at the time) "the fastest Marathon ever run", the IAAF said: "Due to the elevation drop and point-to-point measurements of the Boston course, performances \[on that course] are not eligible for World record consideration."[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-iaaf_rules-38][^cite_note-42]

The [Association of Road Racing Statisticians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Road_Racing_Statisticians), an independent organization that compiles data from [road running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_running) events, also maintains an alternate marathon world best progression but with standards they consider to be more stringent.[^cite_note-43]

### Women's world record changes



[Tigst Assefa](51512379.md) during her women's world record run at the 2023 Berlin Marathon with 2\:11\:53

The IAAF Congress, at the 2011 World Championships, passed a motion changing the record eligibility criteria effective October 6, 2007, so that women's world records must be set in all-women competitions.[^cite_note-Baldwin-44] The result of the change was that Radcliffe's 2\:17\:42 performance at the 2005 London Marathon supplanted her own existing women's mark as the "world record"; the earlier performance is to be referred to as a "world best".[^cite_note-Baldwin-44]

Per the 2021 IAAF Competition Rules, "a World Record for performance achieved in mixed sex ("Mixed") races and a World Record for performance achieved in single sex ("Women only") races" are tracked separately.[^cite_note-45]

### Unofficial record attempts

In December 2016, [Nike, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.), announced that three top distance runners—[Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge), [Zersenay Tadese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zersenay_Tadese), and [Lelisa Desisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelisa_Desisa)—had agreed to forgo the spring marathon season to work with the company in an effort to run a sub-two-hour marathon.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

The [Breaking2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking2) event took place in the early morning of May 6, 2017; Kipchoge crossed the finish line with a time of 2\:00\:25.[^cite_note-50] This time was more than two minutes faster than the world record at the time.[^cite_note-51]

Kipchoge took part in a similar attempt to break the two-hour barrier in Vienna on October 12, 2019, as part of the [Ineos 1\:59 Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos_1\:59_Challenge). He successfully ran the first sub-two-hour marathon distance, with a time of 1\:59\:40.2.[^cite_note-52] The effort did not count as a new world record under IAAF rules due to the setup of the challenge. Specifically, it was not an open event, Kipchoge was handed fluids by his support team throughout, the run featured a pace car, and included rotating teams of other runners pacing Kipchoge in a formation designed to reduce wind resistance and maximize efficiency.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The achievement was recognized by [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) with the titles 'Fastest marathon distance (male)' and 'First marathon distance run under two hours' instead of an official world record.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]
