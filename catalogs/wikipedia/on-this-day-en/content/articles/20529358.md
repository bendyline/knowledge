The **Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Kingdom of Thailand** ([Abrv:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbreviation) MFA; [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กระทรวงการต่างประเทศ, [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): *_Krasuang Kan Tang Prathet_*) is the principal governmental department in charge of [foreign relations](233404.md) for [Thailand](30128.md). The ministry is headed by the Minister of Foreign Affairs, who is also a member of the [Cabinet of Thailand](7366930.md). The Minister of Foreign Affairs is appointed by the [Prime minister](273644.md). The ministry is charged with formulating and executing foreign policies for the [Kingdom of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Thailand) and also manages and maintains Thai [diplomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_missions_of_Thailand) missions around the world.[^cite_note-1]

## History

Prior to the creation of the ministry, much of the country's foreign relations were handled by the [absolute monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarch) of the day. During the [Kingdom of Ayutthaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ayutthaya) foreign relations were handled by the **"Krom Phra Khlang"** ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กรมพระคลัง) (or the Treasury Department). The head of the department was known as "Phra Khlang" ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): พระคลัง) and occasionally referred to as "Berguelang" or "Barcelon" by foreign authors. A notable *Phra Khlang Kosathibodi* during the reign of [King Narai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Narai) was [Kosa Lek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosa_Lek), who was the elder brother of the famous 17th-century Siamese ambassador to France [Kosa Pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosa_Pan). Kosa Pan also became a *Phra Khlang Kosathibodi* upon the reign of [Petracha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petracha). Soon after, a sub-department called "Kromma Tha" ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กรมท่า, "Port Department") was created to deal with foreigners.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

During the [Bangkok Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattanakosin_era) most of these features were retained. For instance, the Chau Phaya-Phraklang in his capacity as Minister of State on behalf of [Jessadabodindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessadabodindra)[^cite_note-Roberts-4] negotiated the [Siamese–American Treaty of Amity and Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siamese%E2%80%93American_Treaty_of_Amity_and_Commerce) of 1833 with [Edmund Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Roberts_\(diplomat\)) in his capacity as Minister of the United States on behalf of President Andrew Jackson.[^cite_note-5]

In 1840, [King Mongkut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Mongkut), the next king, founded the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Kingdom of Siam, which was administered directly by the king. The responsibilities and roles of the old "Krom" were shifted to this ministry. Chao Phraya Bhanuwong became the first Foreign Minister of Siam in 1871, appointed by [King Chulalongkorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Chulalongkorn). In 1881 **Prince [Devavongse Varoprakarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devavongse)** was appointed to replace him. Known today as the "father of Thai diplomacy" he reorganized and modernized the ministry to meet 19th century standards. The ministry was then permanently established at [Saranrom Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saranrom_Palace), east of the [Grand Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Palace). The ministry was divided into seven divisions:[^cite_note-6]

- Senabodi Division (Ministerial Division)

- Under-Secretary Division

- Translation Division

- Reception Division

- Accounts Division

- Filing Division

- Diplomatic Division

- Consular Division

### Foreign affairs advisers

From 1892 to 1924, the Siamese government retained the [professional legal services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_professional_privilege) of [lawyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyer) skilled in [international law](8195726.md).&#x20;

- [Gustave Rolin-Jaequemyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Rolin-Jaequemyns), an international legal scholar, served as Adviser-General from 1892 to 1902.

- [Edward Strobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Henry_Strobel), a [Harvard Law School Bemis Professor of International Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorial_positions_at_Harvard_Law_School), served as American Adviser-General from 1906 until his death in 1908. He was followed by Harvard Law Professors [Jens Westengard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Westengard), [Francis B. Sayre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Bowes_Sayre,_Sr.) and Eldon James.

### Reorganization

After the [Revolution of 1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siamese_Revolution_of_1932), the ministry came under civilian control and the minister was made a member of the constitutional government of Siam. The first minister under this new system was Phraya Srivisanvaja.[^cite_note-9]

In 1992, the ministry office was moved to [Si Ayutthaya Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Si_Ayutthaya_Road), Thung Phaya Thai Subdistrict, [Ratchathewi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchathewi_District), Bangkok in the area that used to be the headquarter of [Southeast Asia Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia_Treaty_Organization) (SEATO), the Ministry also has an office building of the Department of Consular Affairs at [Chaeng Watthana Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaeng_Watthana_Road).
