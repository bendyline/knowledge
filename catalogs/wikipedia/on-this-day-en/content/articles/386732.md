**Jarosław** (Polish: [\[jaˈrɔswaf\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/71/Pl-Jaros%C5%82aw.ogg/Pl-Jaros%C5%82aw.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/71/Pl-Jaros%C5%82aw.ogg/Pl-Jaros%C5%82aw.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-1] is a town in southeastern [Poland](22936.md),[^cite_note-TERYT-2] situated on the [San River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_\(river\)). The town had 35,475 inhabitants in 2023.[^cite_note-3] It is the capital of [Jarosław County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaros%C5%82aw_County) in the [Subcarpathian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcarpathian_Voivodeship).

Founded in the Middle Ages, Jarosław is a former residential town of various prominent Polish magnate families. It prospered as a trade center due to its location at the intersection of important trade routes, and was a notable regional center of education. Jarosław features a preserved old town with especially notable [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) landmarks.

## History

Jarosław is located in the territory of the old Polish tribe of the [Lendians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lendians), which became part of the [Duchy of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Poland) under [Mieszko I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mieszko_I). According to tradition, the town was established in 1031 by [Yaroslav the Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_the_Wise), after the area was annexed from [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_\(1025%E2%80%931385\)) by the [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), although the first confirmed mention of the town comes from 1152. The region was eventually regained by Poland, and the settlement was granted [Magdeburg town rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg_rights) by Polish Duke [Vladislaus II of Opole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladislaus_II_of_Opole) in 1375.



A historical view of Jarosław on a mural in the town center

The city quickly developed as an important trade centre and [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) on the [San River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_River), reaching the period of its greatest prosperity in the 16th and 17th centuries. It had [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route) linking [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia) with [Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenia), [Gdańsk](12099.md), and [Hungary](13275.md). Merchants from such distant countries as [Spain](26667.md), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England), [Finland](10577.md), [Armenia](10918072.md) and [Persia](14653.md) arrived for the annual three-week-long [fair](504805.md) on the [feast of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary).

Jarosław was a [private town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_town) of [Polish nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta), including the [Tarnowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnowski_family), Jarosławski, [Odrowąż](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrow%C4%85%C5%BC_family), [Kostka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostka_family), [Sieniawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sieniawski_\(Leliwa\)), [Zamoyski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamoyski_family), [Wiśniowiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi%C5%9Bniowiecki), [Koniecpolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koniecpolski), [Sobieski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Sobieski), [Sanguszko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanguszko) and [Czartoryski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czartoryski) families. The Jarosławski family of [Leliwa coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leliwa_coat_of_arms) hailed from the town.

In 1575 a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College) was established in Jarosław.[^cite_note-4] The college's prestige extended beyond the region to more distant parts of Poland, as well as to Hungary and [Wallachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallachia).[^cite_note-rp46-5] A theater was established at the college, where religious and secular plays were performed, religious music concerts were held, and poetry was written and published.[^cite_note-6] The college had one of the largest libraries in Poland.[^cite_note-7] In 1621, the first Jesuit printing house was founded.[^cite_note-rp46-5] Famous students included the founder of the [Congregation of Marian Fathers of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_of_Marian_Fathers_of_the_Immaculate_Conception) [Stanislaus Papczyński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski),[^cite_note-8] deputy of the [Sejm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) Janusz Paweł Ostrogski,[^cite_note-9] Renaissance writer and royal secretary Piotr Ciekliński, and [Rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_\(academia\)) of the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) [Sebastian Sierakowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Sierakowski).

In the 1590s [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) pillaged the surrounding countryside. (See [Moldavian Magnate Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_Magnate_Wars), *The Magnate Wars (1593–1617), Causes*.) They were unable to overcome the city's [fortifications](204118.md), but their raids started to diminish the city's economic strength and importance. Outbreaks of [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) in the 1620s, and the invasion known as the [Swedish Deluge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deluge_\(history\)) in 1655–60 further undermined the city's prominence. In March 1656, led by Polish national hero [Stefan Czarniecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Czarniecki), the Poles defeated the invading Swedes under King [Charles X Gustav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_X_Gustav_of_Sweden) in the [Battle of Jarosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jaros%C5%82aw_\(1656\)). In the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) of 1700-21, the region was repeatedly pillaged by [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Saxony), and [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) armies, causing the city to decline further.

After the fall of the [Rákóczi's War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A1k%C3%B3czi's_War_of_Independence) against [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) in 1711, Hungarian leader [Francis II Rákóczi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_II_R%C3%A1k%C3%B3czi) and his court, including essayist [Kelemen Mikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelemen_Mikes), found refuge in Jarosław.[^cite_note-10] In 1711, Rákóczi and some Hungarians left for [Gdańsk](12099.md), while some stayed, and later on, several Hungarians were buried in the local Corpus Christi Collegiate Church, before their exhumation and burial in Hungary in 1907.[^cite_note-11]

In the mid-eighteenth century, Roman Catholics constituted 53.7% of the population, members of the Greek Catholic Church 23.9%, and Jews 22.3%.[^cite_note-12]



Market square, after 1895

Jarosław was annexed by [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) in the [First Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Partition_of_Poland) in 1772. It was part of newly formed [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)) ([Austrian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Partition)) until Poland regained independence in 1918 following World War I. In 1914, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) soldiers carried out a [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogroms_in_the_Russian_Empire) in the town, killing more than 150 Jews.[^cite_note-13] In the interbellum period the city was administratively located in the Polish [Lwów Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w_Voivodeship).

Memorials to victims and veterans of World War II

During the German [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) in September 1939, which started [World War II](32927.md), this was the site of the [Battle of Jarosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jaros%C5%82aw). Germany defeated the Poles and captured the town. Shortly afterwards the German *[Einsatzgruppe I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzgruppen)* entered the town to commit various [atrocities against the population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation).[^cite_note-14] Under [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)), the town was part of the [Kraków District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w_District) of the [General Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government).[^cite_note-15] Over 60 Poles, who were either born, educated or lived and worked in Jarosław, including several policemen and the former county police chief, were murdered by the Russians in the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre) in 1940.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] In mid-1941, the Germans relocated the Stalag 327 [prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) from [Rymanów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryman%C3%B3w) to Jarosław, and then moved it to [Sanok](390560.md) and established a subcamp of the Stalag 315 camp in Jarosław.[^cite_note-22] The [Polish resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) was active in the town, and established a dedicated organization to provide aid to prisoners of war,[^cite_note-23] and distributed [Polish underground newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_underground_press) *Odwet* and *Głos Ludu*.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

In 1944, the town was captured by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) of the Soviet Union and restored to Poland, although with a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-installed [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) regime, which remained in power until the [Fall of Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Communism) in the 1980s. Some local Polish resistance officers were arrested by the Soviets and imprisoned in a Soviet camp in [Trzebuska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trzebuska).[^cite_note-26] The communists expelled most of Jarosław's Ukrainian population, at first to [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) territories and later to [territories regained from Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovered_Territories).

It was administratively located in the [Rzeszów Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w_Voivodeship) (1945–1974) and [Przemyśl Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl_Voivodeship) (1975–1998).

## Jewish Jarosław



Great Synagogue, 2022

The first Jews reportedly arrived in Jarosław in 1464. The first rabbi of Jarosław was Rabbi Nathan Neta Ashkenazi, in 1590. A year later, the new [Council of Four Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Four_Lands) (Vaad Arba Aratzot) began convening in Jarosław, rotating the meetings with the city of [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w) (Lviv).&#x20;

Until 1608 with a small Jewish community, religious facilities were not allowed. Still, Rabbi Solomon Efraim of Lontschitz (the author of "Kli Yakar"), a prominent and well known rabbi, lived here. By 1670 there was a large "government" synagogue created, although protested by the Christian community of the city. During attacks on the city by Tatars and Swedes, Jewish merchandise and sometimes homes were set on fire. In 1765, there were 1,884 Jews in the city and towns around it. A Jewish school was established sometime later. The famous rabbi Levi Isaac of [Berdyczów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdycz%C3%B3w) (Berdychiv) studied in Jarosław circa 1760 and was called "the genius of Yeruslav". A fire in 1805 burnt down the old synagogue and a new one was established more according to tradition to replace it. The new synagogue was completed in 1811. A census taken in 1901 notes that Jews were 25% of the population: 5701 Jewish families.

In a story about [Jacob Kranc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Kranc) told by Rabbi Jacob Orenstein around 1850, about the appointment of the Jarosław rabbi, Rabbi Orenstein had refused the appointment of Rabbi of Jarosław because it would be against his old uncle's appointment. The city council had already written his appointment and wished to express their sorrow for its cancellation. The [Dubner Magid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_ben_Wolf_Kranz) had just entered the city on a snowy winter day, and was taken directly to Orenstein's house, together with the city council, who happened to pass by him. But the walk up the steps was enough to create a moving speech, remembered years later, and accounted for in the book.

In 1921 the last rabbi was appointed, Rabbi Shmaiya HaLevi Steinberg. He wrote a book about the Jews of his town, and in the 1930s sent two copies to the [National Hebrew Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Israel) in [Jerusalem](16043.md). These copies are the only surviving copies of the book after the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust).

In September 1939, Jarosław was captured by Germans. Most of the Jews crossed the [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_\(river\)) river to the [Soviet-occupied part of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_Poland_annexed_by_the_Soviet_Union) and hid in the Carpathian mountains, including the elder rabbi and his family. Those that stayed were shot and killed by the German soldiers.

## Sports



Footballers of [JKS 1909 Jarosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JKS_1909_Jaros%C5%82aw) in 1909

Jarosław is a town with a long sports history. In 1889, a branch of the ["Sokół" Polish Gymnastic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Sok%C3%B3%C5%82_movement) was founded in Jarosław. Nowadays, the town's most notable sports club are:

- [JKS 1909 Jarosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JKS_1909_Jaros%C5%82aw), one of the region's oldest [football](10568.md) teams, which competes in the lower leagues

- [Znicz Jarosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Znicz_Jaros%C5%82aw), men's [basketball](3921.md) team, which competed in the [Polish Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Basketball_League) (country's top division) in the past, most recently in the 2009–10 season

- [JKS Jarosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JKS_Jaros%C5%82aw_\(handball\)), women's [handball](13730.md) team, which competes in the [Polish Women's Superliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Women's_Superliga_\(women's_handball\)) (country's top division)

- San Jarosław, women's [volleyball](32558.md) team, which competes in the I liga (second division)
