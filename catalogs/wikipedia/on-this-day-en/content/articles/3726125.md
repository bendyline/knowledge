**Liniers** is a [barrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrios_of_Buenos_Aires) (neighborhood) of [Buenos Aires](255919.md) on the edge of the city, centered on [Rivadavia Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivadavia_Avenue). It is also an important train station and bus hub, connecting western [Gran Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Buenos_Aires) with the [Buenos Aires Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Metro).

The neighborhood developed around the [Liniers railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liniers_railway_station) following its inaugural in 1872.[^cite_note-1]

The neighborhood is home to [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)) club [Club Atlético Vélez Sársfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_V%C3%A9lez_S%C3%A1rsfield), whose stadium, the [José Amalfitani Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Amalfitani_Stadium), has been established there in 1951. Liners is also the site of the Church of [San Cayetano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Cajetan), consecrated in 1900 and elevated to a [parish](23623.md) in 1913.[^cite_note-2] The Church of San Cayetano hosts thousands of faithful who gather each [feast day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_day) (August 7) to pray for employment or to give thanks for their livelihood.[^cite_note-3]

The ward is named after [Santiago de Liniers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Liniers), a colonial administrator who resisted the [British Invasions of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Invasions_in_Rio_de_la_Plata).
