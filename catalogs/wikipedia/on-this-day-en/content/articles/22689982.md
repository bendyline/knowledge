The **[2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_in_English_football)** was **[Manchester City Football Club](165813.md)'**&#x73; eighth consecutive season playing in the [Premier League](11250.md), the top division of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_England), and its thirteenth season since the Premier League was first created with Manchester City as one of its original 22 founding member clubs. Overall, it is the team's 118th season playing in a division of English football, most of which have been spent in the top flight. The club started the season under the management of [Mark Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hughes) who was sacked in mid-December after the team drew seven consecutive matches in the Premier League.[^cite_note-1] He was replaced by the Italian manager [Roberto Mancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mancini).

## Season review

New manager [Roberto Mancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mancini) began the season with only five months in the job at [Eastlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manchester_Stadium).

With a prolific 29 goals in his first season at the club, [Carlos Tevez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Tevez) was widely regarded as the club's best and most important player this season.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The previous season's fan's favourite and top scorer, [Robinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinho), was less successful, and in January he was loaned out to Brazilian club [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC) for the remainder of the season[^cite_note-4] only serving to emphasise the magnitude of his failure to deliver on the pitch anything remotely comparable to what he had already received in his bank account.

In fact, Manchester City earned itself the distinction of being the only team to do the "[league double](88184.md)" over the team that ultimately achieved the "[league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Premier_League) and [cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_FA_Cup) [double](88184.md)" this season.

### Kit information

For the 2009–10 season, the shirt sponsor for all of the club's kits was [Etihad Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad_Airways), which replaced the previous season's sponsor, [Thomas Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Cook_Group). There was also a change in the supplier of those kits for this season, with [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.)-owned [Umbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbro) replacing the previous season's supplier, [Le Coq Sportif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Coq_Sportif). As a result of the switch from its prior French kit supplier to the Greater Manchester-based Umbro, all of the club's previous season's team and goalkeeper kits were essentially replaced with new ones for this season.

The overall sky blue colour of the first team kit was retained but the style and trim of this strip was significantly changed. Completely new away and third team kits were introduced, while a new all-green goalkeeper strip replaced the previous season's gold and black strip as the primary one for use by the goalkeepers, with a newly styled and trimmed variant of the old gold and black strip which became the secondary strip for use by the goalkeepers in away fixtures.

The new all-black away team kit came with gold vertical shoulder trim on the front that enabled the kit to be colour-coordinated with the gold and black goalkeeper strip, although it was sometimes also used with the all-green goalkeeper strip. This gold and black colour scheme was, according to its designer David Blanch,[^cite_note-5] intended to be symbolic of the globe covered with bees (representing the world, to all parts of which the goods of the city were exported) that was featured on the city of [Manchester coat of arms](http://www.ngw.nl/heraldrywiki/images/2/25/Manchest.jpg). That was because the Manchester City teams in the past have established the unique tradition of always wearing this [crest on their shirts](https://web.archive.org/web/20110827053959/http://shop.mcfc.co.uk/stores/product_images/mcfc-39678.jpg?width=400\&height=400\&quality=95) when playing at Wembley (or in a major cup final elsewhere) as a symbol of their pride in representing the city of Manchester at a major sporting event. In heraldic terms, the bee was symbolic of a hive of industry, and even today the Manchester bee was often used all by itself as a shorthand emblem for the city of Manchester.

The red and black diagonal sash across the white shirts of the new third team kit was intended as a nostalgic re-mastering of the original sashed strip worn by the City team in the 1970s,[^cite_note-6] while that original design had, in its turn, been a nod back at the classic red and black striped shirts with black shorts that had originally been introduced by coach [Malcolm Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Allison) in imitation of [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.C._Milan)'s strip, and which was frequently worn in its cup ties by the successful trophy-winning [City team](https://web.archive.org/web/20140517115421/http://m.gmgrd.co.uk/res/711.\$plit/C_71_article_1419590_image_list_image_list_item_0_image.jpg?03%2F05%2F2011%2013%3A18%3A17%3A075) of the late 1960s and early 1970s.
