The **Gulf of Oman** or **Sea of Oman**,[^cite_note-1] also known as **Gulf of Makran** or **Sea of Makran**,[^cite_note-2] is a [gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) that connects the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) with the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), which then runs to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). It borders [Iran](14653.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md) on the north, [Oman](22316.md) on the south, and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) on the west.

Until the 18th century, it was known as Makran Sea and is also visible on historical maps and museums.[^cite_note-12]

## International trade

The Western side of the gulf connects to the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), a strategic route through which a third of the world's [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) and 20% of global [oil consumption](17808102.md) passes from [Middle East](19323.md) producers.[^cite_note-13]

## Ecology



A bloom of [phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton) in the Gulf of Oman, seen on 8 March 2025

Further information: [Gulf of Oman desert and semi-desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Oman_desert_and_semi-desert) and [Persian Gulf § Wildlife](24761.md#Wildlife)

In 2018, scientists confirmed the Gulf of Oman contains one of the world's largest [marine dead zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_zone_\(ecology\)), where the ocean contains little or no oxygen and marine wildlife cannot exist. The dead zone encompasses nearly the entire 165,000-square-kilometre (63,700 sq mi) Gulf of Oman and equivalent to the size of Florida, United States of America. The cause is a combination of increased [ocean warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_warming) and increased runoff of nitrogen and phosphorus from fertilizers.[^cite_note-14]

## International underwater rail tunnel

In 2018, a rail tunnel under the sea was suggested to link the UAE with the western coast of India. The [bullet train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) tunnel would be supported by pontoons and be nearly 2,000 kilometres (1,200 mi) in length.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]
