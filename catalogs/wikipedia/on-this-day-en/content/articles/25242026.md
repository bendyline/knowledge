# Coastal defence and fortification



[Castillo San Felipe del Morro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castillo_San_Felipe_del_Morro) in [Old San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_San_Juan), Puerto Rico



[Castillo San Felipe de Barajas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castillo_San_Felipe_de_Barajas) in [Cartagena de Indias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartagena_de_Indias), Colombia

This article is about military defence of coastal areas. For defence against flooding and erosion, see [Coastal management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_management).

**Coastal defence** (or **defense**) **and coastal fortification** are measures taken to provide protection against [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) attack at or near a [coastline](5236.md) (or other [shoreline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore)),[^cite_note-1] for example, [fortifications](204118.md) and [coastal artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_artillery). Because an invading enemy normally requires a [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) or [harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour) to sustain operations, such defences are usually concentrated around such facilities, or places where such facilities could be constructed.[^cite_note-gs-2] Coastal artillery fortifications generally followed the development of land fortifications, usually incorporating land defences; sometimes separate land defence forts were built to protect coastal forts. Through the middle 19th century, coastal forts could be [bastion forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion_fort), [star forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_fort), [polygonal forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonal_fort), or sea forts, the first three types often with detached gun batteries called "water batteries".[^cite_note-3] Coastal defence weapons throughout history were heavy [naval guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_gun) or weapons based on them, often supplemented by lighter weapons. In the late 19th century separate batteries of coastal artillery replaced forts in some countries; in some areas these became widely separated geographically through the mid-20th century as weapon ranges increased. The amount of landward defence provided began to vary by country from the late 19th century; by 1900 new US forts almost totally neglected these defences. [Booms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_\(navigational_barrier\)) were also usually part of a protected harbor's defences. In the middle 19th century [underwater minefields](22102.md) and later [controlled mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_mines) were often used, or stored in peacetime to be available in wartime. With the rise of the [submarine](28825.md) threat at the beginning of the 20th century, [anti-submarine nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_net) were used extensively, usually added to boom defences, with major warships often being equipped with them (to allow rapid deployment once the ship was anchored or moored) through early World War I. In World War I [railway artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_artillery) emerged and soon became part of coastal artillery in some countries; with railway artillery in coast defence some type of revolving mount had to be provided to allow tracking of fast-moving targets.[^cite_note-4]

In [littoral warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_\(military\)), coastal defence counteracts [naval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) offence, such as [naval artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_artillery), naval infantry ([marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines)), or both.

## History

Rather than the beach assault of modern [amphibious operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare), seaborne assaults of the classical and medieval age more often took the form of coastal raiders sailing up river and landing well inland of the coast. Prior to the invention of naval artillery that could sink hostile ships, the most that coastal defence could do was act as an early warning system, that could alert local naval or ground forces of the impending attack. For example, in the late Roman period the [Saxon Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Shore) was a system of forts at the mouths of navigable rivers, and watch towers along the coast of [Britannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) and [Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Gaul).

Later in Anglo-Saxon [Wessex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wessex), protection against [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) raiders took the form of coast watchers whose duty was to alert the local militia, the navy, which would attempt to intercept the raider's ships, or failing that, to destroy them after they had beached. Against smaller raiding forces, the threat of losing their ships, and their way home with their loot, was often enough to force them to curtail their attack. In addition there was a [system of fortified towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burghal_Hidage#Political_and_military_context), [burghs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgh), that were positioned at [choke points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choke_point) along navigable rivers to prevent raiders from sailing inland.

## Sea forts



[John Smith's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Smith_\(explorer\)) 1624 map of the [fortifications of the Castle Harbour Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Islands_Fortifications,_Bermuda) and [St. George's Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George's_Harbour,_Bermuda) in [Bermuda](3460.md). Construction beginning in 1612, these were the first stone fortifications, with the first coastal artillery batteries, built by [England](9316.md) in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World).

See also: [Category\:Sea forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Sea_forts)

Sea forts are completely surrounded by water – if not permanently, then at least at high tide (i.e. they are [tidal islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_island)).

Unlike most coastal fortifications, which are on the coast, sea forts are not. Instead, they are off the coast on islands, [artificial islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_islands), or are specially built structures. Some sea forts, such as [Fort Denison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Denison) or [Fort Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sumter), are within harbours in proximity to the coast, but most are at some distance off the coast. Some, such as for example [Bréhon Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%A9hon_Tower) or [Fort Drum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Drum_\(Philippines\)) completely occupy small islands; others, such as [Flakfortet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flakfortet) and [Pampus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampus), are on artificial islands built up on shoals. [Fort Louvois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Louvois) is on a built-up island, 400 meters (1,312 ft) from the shore, and connected to it by a causeway that high tide completely submerses. The most elaborate sea fort is [Murud-Janjira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murud-Janjira), which is so extensive that one might truly call it a sea fortress.

The most recent sea forts were the [Maunsell Forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maunsell_Forts), which the British built during World War II as anti-aircraft platforms. One type consisted of a concrete pontoon barge on which stood two cylindrical towers on top of which was the gun platform mounting. They were laid down in dry dock and assembled as complete units. They were then fitted out before being towed out and sunk onto their sand bank positions in 1942. The other type consisted of seven interconnected steel platforms built on stilts. Five platforms carried guns arranged in a semicircle around the sixth platform, which contained the control centre and accommodation. The seventh platform, set further out than the gun towers, was the searchlight tower.

## Coastal defence and fortification by country

### Chile



Cannons of the [Valdivian Fort System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdivian_Fort_System) in [Niebla, Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niebla,_Chile), an example of a coastal defense

Further information: [Coastal fortifications of colonial Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_fortifications_of_colonial_Chile)

In [Colonial times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Chile) the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) diverted significant resources to fortify the Chilean coast as consequence of Dutch and English raids.[^cite_note-MCLValcolonial-5]

The [Dutch occupation of Valdivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_occupation_of_Valdivia) in 1643 caused great alarm among Spanish authorities and triggered the construction of the [Valdivian Fort System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdivian_Fort_System) that begun in 1645.[^cite_note-Kock-6][^cite_note-Kris-7]

As consequence of the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War) the Valdivian Fort System was updated and reinforced from 1764 onwards. Other vulnerable localities of colonial Chile such as [Chiloé Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilo%C3%A9_Archipelago), [Concepción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concepci%C3%B3n,_Chile), [Juan Fernández Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Islands) and [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso) were also made ready for an eventual English attack.[^cite_note-MEMCh2-8][^cite_note-MCLValcolonial-5] Inspired in the recommendations of former [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_of_Chilo%C3%A9) [Santa María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Narciso_de_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa) the Spanish founded the "city-fort" of [Ancud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancud) in 1768 and separated Chiloé from the [Captaincy General of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Chile) into a direct dependency of the Viceroyalty of Peru.[^cite_note-Urbina2014-9]

### China



View looking north from the gun platform. [Taku Forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taku_Forts) Museum, China.

China first established formal coastal defences during the early [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) (14th century) to protect against attacks by [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate) ([wokou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wokou)). Coastal defences were maintained through both the Ming dynasty and the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) that followed, protecting the coast against pirates, and against the [Portuguese](23033.md) and other [European](9239.md) powers that sought to impose their will on China.[^cite_note-hkmcdmpA-10][^cite_note-hkmcdmpB-11]

Subsequently, the European powers built their own coastal defences to protect the various colonial enclaves that they established along the Chinese coast. One such, a fort built by the [British](31717.md) commanding the [Lei Yue Mun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lei_Yue_Mun) channel between [Hong Kong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Island) and the mainland, has been converted into the [Hong Kong Museum of Coastal Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Museum_of_Coastal_Defence). This tells the story of coastal defence along the South China coast from the Ming dynasty onwards.[^cite_note-hkmcdmpC-12]



Fort Zeelandia, Taiwan

Taiwan has several coastal fortifications, with some, such as [Fort Zeelandia or Anping Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Zeelandia_\(Taiwan\)) dating to the time of the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verenigde_Oostindische_Compagnie). Others, such as [Cihou Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cihou_Fort), [Eternal Golden Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_Golden_Castle), [Hobe Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobe_Fort), date more to the end of the 19th century. The [Uhrshawan Battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhrshawan_Battery) dates primarily to the first half of the 19th century. It actually underwent bombardment during the [Sino-French War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-French_War).

### Malta

Main article: [Fortifications of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Malta)



[Saint Mary's Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary's_Tower), which was built in 1618, was used by the [Armed Forces of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Malta) until 2002.

The islands of [Malta](19137.md), [Gozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gozo) and [Comino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comino) all have some form of coastal fortification. The area around the [Grand Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Harbour) was possibly first fortified during Arab rule, and by the 13th century, a castle known as the [Castrum Maris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_St_Angelo) was built in [Birgu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgu) to protect the harbour. The Maltese islands were given to [Order of Saint John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller) in 1530, who settled in [Birgu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgu) and rebuilt the Castrum Maris as [Fort Saint Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Saint_Angelo). In the 1550s, [Fort Saint Elmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Saint_Elmo) and [Fort Saint Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Saint_Michael) were built, and walls surrounded the coastal cities of Birgu and [Senglea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senglea). In 1565, the [Great Siege of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Siege_of_Malta) reduced many of these coastal fortifications to rubble, but after the siege they were rebuilt. The fortified city of [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta) was built on the Sciberras Peninsula, and further modifications were made to the fortifications over the years. The harbour area was strengthened even more by the building of the [Floriana Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floriana_Lines), [Santa Margherita Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Margherita_Lines), [Cottonera Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottonera_Lines) and [Fort Ricasoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ricasoli) in the 17th century and [Fort Manoel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Manoel) and [Fort Tigné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Tign%C3%A9) in the nearby [Marsamxett Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsamxett_Harbour) in the 18th century. The Order also built [Fort Chambray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Chambray) near [Mġarr Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%A1arr,_Gozo) in Gozo.

In the early 15th century, a number of watch posts had been established around Malta's coastline. In the early 17th century, the Order began to strengthen the coastal fortifications outside the harbour area, by building [watchtowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchtowers). The first of these was [Garzes Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garzes_Tower), which was built in 1605. The [Wignacourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wignacourt_towers), [Lascaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lascaris_towers) and [De Redin towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Redin_towers) were built over the course of the 17th century. The last coastal watchtower to be built was [Isopu Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isopu_Tower) in 1667. Between 1605 and 1667, a total of 31 towers were built, of which 22 survive today (with another 3 in ruins).[^cite_note-13]

From 1714 onwards, about 52 [batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_artillery) and [redoubts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redoubt), along with several entrenchments, were built around the coasts of Malta and Gozo. Many of these have been destroyed, but a few examples still survive.[^cite_note-14]

After the British took Malta in 1800, they modified the Order's defences in the harbour area to keep up with new technology. Malta itself, [Gibraltar](7607314.md), [Bermuda](3460.md), and [Halifax, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) were designated [Imperial fortresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress). The [Corradino Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corradino_Lines) were built in the 1870s to protect the Grand Harbour from landward attacks. Between 1872 and 1912, many forts and batteries were built around the coastline. The first of these was [Sliema Point Battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sliema_Point_Battery), built to protect the northern approach to the Grand Harbour. A chain of fortifications, including [Fort Delimara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Delimara) and [Fort Benghisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Benghisa), was also built to protect [Marsaxlokk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsaxlokk) Harbour.

From 1935 to the 1940s, the British built many [pillboxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillbox_\(military\)) in Malta for defence in case of an Italian invasion.

### New Zealand



[BL 8-inch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BL_8_inch_Mk_I_-_VII_naval_gun) Armstrong [disappearing gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearing_gun) at [North Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Head,_New_Zealand), New Zealand

Main article: [Coastal fortifications of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_fortifications_of_New_Zealand)

The coastline of [New Zealand](4913064.md) was fortified in two main waves. The first wave occurred around 1885 and was a response to fears of an attack by [Russia](25391.md). The second wave occurred during [World War II](32927.md) and was due to fears of invasion by the [Japanese](15573.md).[^cite_note-nzca-15]

The [fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications) were built from [British](31717.md) designs adapted to New Zealand conditions. These installations typically included gun emplacements, pill boxes, fire command or [observation posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_post), [camouflage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camouflage) strategies, underground [bunkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunker), sometimes with interconnected tunnels, containing [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine_\(artillery\)), supply and plotting rooms and protected engine rooms supplying power to the [gun turrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_turret) and [searchlights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Searchlight).[^cite_note-nzca-15]

### United States



[Fort McHenry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McHenry) in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland) harbor, US, typifies an early seacoast defense system prior to the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), with low earthworks. The cannons are post-[Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) era.

Main article: [Seacoast defense in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seacoast_defense_in_the_United_States)

The defence of its coasts was a major concern for the [United States](3434750.md) from its independence. Prior to the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) many coastal fortifications already dotted the Atlantic coast, as protection from pirate raids and foreign incursions. The Revolutionary War led to the construction of many additional fortifications, mostly comprising simple earthworks erected to meet specific threats.[^cite_note-BerhowHist1-16]

The prospect of war with European powers in the 1790s led to a national programme of fortification building spanning seventy years in three phases, known as the First, Second and Third Systems. By the time of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), advances in [armour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armour) and [weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon) had made [masonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonry) forts obsolete, and the combatants discovered that their [steamships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamship) and [ironclad warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_warship) could penetrate Third System defences with acceptable losses.[^cite_note-BerhowHist1-16][^cite_note-usnps-17]

In 1885 US President [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland) appointed the [Endicott Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endicott_Board), whose recommendations would lead to a large-scale modernization programme of harbour and coastal defences in the United States, especially the construction of well dispersed, open topped [reinforced concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforced_concrete) emplacements protected by sloped earthworks. Many of these featured [disappearing guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearing_guns), which sat protected behind the walls, but could be raised to fire. [Underwater mine fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_mines_in_United_States_harbor_defense) were a critical component of the defence, and smaller guns were also employed to protect the mine fields from [minesweeping vessels](217118.md). Defences of a given harbor were initially designated artillery districts, redesignated as [coast defense commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Defense_Command) in 1913 and as harbor defense commands in 1924. In 1901 the [Artillery Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Artillery_Branch_\(United_States\)) was divided into field artillery and coast artillery units, and in 1907 the [United States Army Coast Artillery Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Coast_Artillery_Corps) was created to operate these defences.[^cite_note-BerhowHist1-16]

The development of [military aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aviation) rendered these open topped emplacements vulnerable to air attack. Therefore, the next, and last, generation of coastal artillery was mounted under thick concrete shields covered with vegetation to make them virtually invisible from above. In anticipation of a conflict with [Japan](15573.md), most of the limited funds available between 1933 and 1938 were spent on the Pacific coast. In 1939–40 the threat of war in Europe prompted larger appropriations and the resumption of work along the Atlantic coast. Under a major program developed in the wake of the [Fall of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_France) in 1940, a near-total replacement of previous coast defenses was implemented, centered on [16-inch guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16%22/50_caliber_Mark_2_gun) in new [casemated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casemate) batteries. These were supplemented by [6-inch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-inch_gun_M1) and [90 mm guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90_mm_Gun_M1/M2/M3), also in new installations.[^cite_note-BerhowHist1-16]

In WW2 the [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard) would patrol the shores of the United States during the war. Some patrolled on horseback with mounted beach patrols. On 13 June 1942 Seaman 2nd Class John Cullen, patrolling the beach in [Amagansett, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagansett,_New_York), discovered the first landing of German saboteurs in [Operation Pastorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pastorius). Cullen was the first American who actually came in contact with the enemy on the shores of the United States during the war and his report led to the capture of the German sabotage team. For this, Cullen received the [Legion of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Merit).[^cite_note-18]

### United Kingdom



[Nothe Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothe_Fort) is situated beside [Weymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weymouth,_Dorset) Harbour, UK.

The walls around coastal cities, such as [Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_town_walls), had evolved from simpler Norman fortifications by the start of the 13th century. Later, [King Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England) was a prolific castle builder and sites such as [Conwy Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_Castle), built 1283 to 1289, defend river approaches as well as the surrounding land. Built 1539 to 1544, the [Device Forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Device_Forts) are a series of artillery fortifications built for [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England) to defend the southern coast of England. Between 1804 and 1812 the British authorities built a chain of towers known as [Martello Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martello_Tower) to [defend the south and east coast of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_anti-invasion_preparations_of_1803%E2%80%931805), Ireland, [Jersey](15693.md) and [Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey) against [possible invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon's_planned_invasion_of_the_United_Kingdom) from [France](5843419.md). This type of tower was also used elsewhere in the British Empire and in the United States.

In the early [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) era, [Alderney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Alderney) was strongly fortified to provide a massive anchorage for the British Navy before France became an ally of Britain in the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), even so plans changed slowly and the [Palmerston Forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston_Forts), a group of forts and associated structures were built during the Victorian period on the recommendations of the 1860 [Royal Commission on the Defence of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_the_Defence_of_the_United_Kingdom), following concerns about the strength of the French Navy.[^cite_note-Brown2006-19] In 1865 Lieutenant [Arthur Campbell Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Campbell_Walker), of the [School of Musketry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Musketry) advocated the use of [armoured trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_train) on "an iron high-road running parallel with that other 'silent highway', the source of all our greatness, the ocean, our time-honoured 'moat and circumvallation'"[^cite_note-20]

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) the British Admiralty designed eight towers code named [M-N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_M-N_Scheme) that were to be built and positioned in the [Straits of Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Dover) to protect allied merchant shipping from German [U-boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat). [Nab Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nab_Tower) is still in situ. The [Maunsell Forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maunsell_Forts) were small fortified towers, primarily for [anti-aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft) guns, built in the [Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Estuary) and [Mersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Mersey) estuaries during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).

With the advent of missile technology coastal forts became obsolete. Britain's coastal forts were therefore decommissioned in 1956 and the units manning them disbanded.[^cite_note-21]
