**Corruption in Bangladesh** has been a continuing problem. Like many other developing and [emerging countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_countries), corruption is a part of life in Bangladesh. Regular citizens routinely pay bribes for basic services and to skip queues, and officials rely on bribes to make a living.[^cite_note-1]

As of 2001, corruption in the public sector was "endemic, chronic and all pervasive".[^cite_note-Zafarullah-2001-2]

The [Anti Corruption Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti_Corruption_Commission_Bangladesh) was formed in 2004, but is considered to be largely ineffective in investigating and preventing corruption because of governmental control over it.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The Anti Corruption Commission chairman [Iqbal Mahmood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iqbal_Mahmood) admitted in November 2020 that "there are high levels of bribery and corruption in government services" in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-5]

In [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International)'s 2025 [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index), which scored 182 countries on a scale from 0 ("highly corrupt") to 100 ("very clean"),[^cite_note-6] Bangladesh scored 24. Bangladesh was ranked among the 13 most corrupt countries globally in the index.[^cite_note-DailyStar-20260211-7] For comparison with regional scores, the best score among those countries of the Asia Pacific region that appeared in the Index[^cite_note-9] was 84, the average score was 45 and the worst score was 15.[^cite_note-10] For comparison with worldwide scores, the best score was 89 (ranked 1), the average score was 42, and the worst score was 9 (ranked 181, in a two-way tie).[^cite_note-11]

Commenting on Bangladesh's score on the 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index in the wake of the August 2024 fall of the authoritarian [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md) premiership, Executive Director [Iftekharuzzaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iftekharuzzaman) of [Transparency International Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International_Bangladesh) said in early 2026, "Over the past one and a half years, the interim government has failed to take effective and exemplary action against corruption. At the same time, proposed reforms by the Anti-Corruption Commission (ACC) and other institutions have largely been ignored or left unimplemented, weakening their ability to act independently." In south Asia, only Afghanistan ranks lower than Bangladesh, he said.[^cite_note-DailyStar-20260211-7]

## Causes of corruption

After splitting from Pakistan in 1971, Bangladesh was ruled by a series of civilian and military governments. Riots surrounding the 2014 elections resulted in hundreds of deaths and [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) changed the country's "political rights" rating from 3 to 4 (with 1 being best, 7 worst). This was only one incident, however, in a modern history of highly fatal political violence.[^cite_note-trans-12]

The country's score for rule of law on the Worldwide Governance Indicators declined from 28.6 (on a scale of 0–100) in 2011 to 22.7 in 2013. Although, Bangladesh has experienced notable social and economic progress since around the turn of the century, it remained one of the world's poorest nations in 2015, with 76.5% of the population living on less than US\$2 a day.[^cite_note-13]

## Corruption by sector

As of 2001, bribery, misappropriation of funds, excessive lobbying, delays in performing services, pilferage, neglect of duties, abuse of power, and patronage were rampant throughout the public sector. This included law enforcement, customs, tax authorities, banking, education, health care, the judiciary, and utilities.[^cite_note-Zafarullah-2001-2][^cite_note-14]

A 2012 study found that 97% of MPs were involved in illegal activities, with 77% abusing their positions on local election boards, 75% abusing development projects for their own benefit, including accepting commissions in exchange for approving projects or programs, 53% being involved in outright criminal acts, 69% influencing procurement decisions, and 62% influential local elections.[^cite_note-trans-12]

Fully 45% of Bangladeshis view political parties as corrupt, while 41% regard the parliament as corrupt. Only the police and the judiciary are viewed as more corrupt. Many politicians are regarded as out-and-out criminals, while the parties also "are believed to harbour criminals and terrorists," according to *Transparency International Bangladesh*. The parties routinely exchange party funds for favours or pay them out because of extortion. They also improperly use state funds to buy jobs, licenses, and contracts for party members and other purposes. Party finances are largely non-transparent; despite regulations requiring reporting to the election commission, reports are scanty or absent and non-compliance is rarely punished. Also, both the parliament and the political parties are under the influence of business, with 56% of MPs now being business people.[^cite_note-trans-12]

### Politicians

#### Bangladesh Awami League



[Sheikh Hasina](623439.md), the former [Prime Minister of Bangladesh](461887.md), was accused in [Padma Bridge graft scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bridge_graft_scandal) in 2016-2017.

On 9 April 2012, Railway Minister [Suranjit Sengupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suranjit_Sengupta)'s assistant personal secretary, general manager of the eastern region, and commandant of security were driving to Suranjit's residence with 7.4 million [taka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_taka) of bribe money, when the driver Azam Khan turned them in.[^cite_note-weeklyblitz-15][^cite_note-16]

In 2012, [World Bank](36752.md) alleged that [Syed Abul Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Abul_Hossain) was one of the corruption conspirators in the [Padma Bridge Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bridge_Scandal).[^cite_note-17] He resigned from his office on 23 July 2012.[^cite_note-18] Former state minister for foreign affairs and MP of Awami League [Abul Hasan Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Hasan_Chowdhury) was also involved in that corruption plot. Another person involved in this case was [Mujibur Rahman Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujibur_Rahman_Chowdhury). He is the nephew of [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md), a member of parliament, and also a civil contractor. [SNC-Lavalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNC-Lavalin) admitted to bribing him in order to obtain contracts[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Soon after the case emanated, [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md) disavowed her nephew and campaigned for his opponent during the [2014 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Bangladeshi_general_election).[^cite_note-21]

On 29 August 2012, Anti Corruption Commission of Bangladesh said that they have the information that [Syed Modasser Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Modasser_Ali), adviser to the prime minister [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md), allegedly influenced [Sonali Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonali_Bank) authorities into granting scam loan to the controversial [Hallmark Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallmark-Sonali_Bank_Loan_Scam).[^cite_note-22]

On 24 April 2013, a commercial building named [Rana Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Savar_building_collapse) in Savar, Dhaka, Bangladesh collapsed due to structural failure. That building was owned by [Sohel Rana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohel_Rana_\(businessman\)), a [Jubo League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubo_League) leader.

On 27 April 2014, the incident named [Narayanganj Seven murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murders_of_the_Narayanganj_Seven) occurred. The main plotter of that incident, Nur Hossain, was the vice-president of Siddhirganj Awami League.[^cite_note-23]

On 15 October 2015, Awami League MP [Manjurul Islam Liton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjurul_Islam_Liton) was arrested for shooting a 9-year-old boy named Shahadat Hossain Shoura.[^cite_note-24]

On 10 April 2016, Bangladesh Supreme Court Appellate Division upheld 13-year jail to former minister of [Disaster Management and Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Disaster_Management_and_Relief) [Mofazzal Hossain Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mofazzal_Hossain_Chowdhury) rejecting his review plea seeking acquittal in graft case.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

In November 2016, Awami League MP [Abdur Rahman Bodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Rahman_Bodi) was sentenced to three years of prison and fined US\$1 million for illegally amassing and hiding wealth of US\$100 million.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

On 13 July 2017, Gazi Tarique Salman, a civil servant (UNO) was harassed by police, local administration,[^cite_note-33] and local Awami League leader Obaidullah Saju.[^cite_note-34]

Even though Swiss National Bank (SNB)'s data published on 29 June 2017 shows that Bangladesh has BDT 4423,00,00,00,000 in Swiss banks which is an increase by 20% since 2013–14,[^cite_note-bdn30Jun2017-35][^cite_note-fe27Jul2017-36] Bangladesh's minister of finance Abul Mal Abdul Muhit said that "Media exaggerated rise of Bangladeshi deposit in Swiss banks".[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

Bangladesh's anti-corruption commissioner filed cases against former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina and 49 ex-ministers for siphoning more than \$150 billion to various countries, including India, Hong Kong, Switzerland, Luxembourg, Singapore, Malaysia, UAE, Australia, UK, USA, and New Zealand.[^cite_note-39] Former Prime Minister [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md), her son [Sajeeb Wazed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajeeb_Wazed) and niece [Tulip Siddiq](33131881.md) accused of embezzling more than \$5 billion from the [Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooppur_Nuclear_Power_Plant) project that costs Bangladeshi tax payers \$12.65 billion.[^cite_note-40]

Al Jazeera's Investigation Exposed Bangladesh's Minister Saifuzzaman Chowdhury's exclusive property portfolio in Dubai, London, New York, San Francisco, and New Jersey. According to undercover video recordings of the Al Jazeera investigation unit, Former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina knew all along about Mr Chowdhury's corruption.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

#### Bangladesh Nationalist Party



[Tarique Rahman](5601422.md), the current [Prime Minister of Bangladesh](461887.md), was accused in digital money laundering case with [Bangladesh Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Bank) during [2001–2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Khaleda_ministry).

In 2007, Military backed caretaker government charged [Harris Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Chowdhury), former Political Secretary of Prime Minister [Khaleda Zia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaleda_Zia), of acquiring his wealth through corruption. Chowdhury was charged with involvement in the Murder of former finance minister, [Shah A M S Kibria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_A_M_S_Kibria), in 2005.[^cite_note-43] He was also charged with involvement in 15 August [2004 Dhaka grenade attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Dhaka_grenade_attack) and [Zia Charitable Trust corruption case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_Charitable_Trust_corruption_case).[^cite_note-44] On 21 December 2014, an arrest warrant was issued against him.[^cite_note-45] His properties were confiscated by the directives of Bangladesh High Court.[^cite_note-46] Harris Chowdhury is a fugitive from 2007.[^cite_note-47]

On 22 July 2007, [Wadud Bhuiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadud_Bhuiyan) received 20-years' of prison sentence for illegally amassing more than a million dollar of cash and property.[^cite_note-48]

On 16 October 2008, it has been reported that the Interpol found the trace of a deposit of BD taka 141.5 million with a bank in Hong Kong of former foreign minister [Morshed Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morshed_Khan). Both he and his son Faisal Morshed Khan have been convicted in several cases relating to corruption, hiding wealth information, [land grabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grabbing) and criminal activities after the caretaker government came to power in January 2007. ## Government



A [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti) against corruption drawn by the students after the [July Revolution](78109234.md)

Corruption abounds in Bangladeshi government offices. Many officials receive salaries without having any actual assigned work. Officials are recruited and promoted for reasons that are not objectively clear. Solicitation of bribes is common. In a 2013 survey, 76% of respondents said corruption was a problem in the public sector, and 39% said they had paid a bribe during the previous 12 months. Bribes were especially common when dealing with the police (72%), the judiciary (63%), land services (44%), and registry and permit services (33%). Bribes were paid to obtain a service (58%), to speed up service (33%), to express thanks (7%), or to obtain cheaper services (3%). These numbers, though unimpressive, mark a significant improvement over 2010.[^cite_note-trans-12]

In 2010, then president [Zillur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zillur_Rahman) pardoned twenty convicted murderers of Shabbir Ahmed Gamma. In July 2011, he again pardoned Awami League leader and convicted murderer Biplob.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

Prime examples of government corruption are [2011 Bangladesh share market scam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bangladesh_share_market_scam).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

[Padma Bridge Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bridge_Scandal), occurred in 2011, was the largest political scandal in [Bangladesh](3454.md) that involved the ruling Bangladesh Awami League's government who allegedly sought a large amount money from the Canadian construction company [SNC-Lavalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNC-Lavalin) in exchange for awarding them the construction contract.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

On 10 December 2012, some hackers hacked and posted the seventeen hours' of [Skype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype) call of chief justice [Nizamul Huq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Nizamul_Huq) on YouTube where he suggested that he was under pressure from the government to reach a quick verdict in the ongoing 1971 war crime cases against the country's senior opposition leaders.[^cite_note-92]

### Public services

The public sectors are among the country's most corrupt. In few countries is bribery more common in connection with public utilities. Nearly six out of ten firms report that they have to offer bribes, "gifts," or facilitation payments in exchange for licenses or public utility concessions or to "get things done." Unclear, contradictory regulations abound, and are often exploited by officials seeking informal payments.[^cite_note-biz-93][^cite_note-trans2-94] [Engineer Delwoar Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Engineer_Delwoar) of [Gazipur City Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazipur_City_Corporation) was killed on 21 May 2020. According to his relatives, one investigation agency, and [Transparency International Bangladesh (TIB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International_Bangladesh) Delwoar was murdered for his stance against corruption.[^cite_note-95]

### Foreign affairs

Australian Federal Police is investigating the matter related to fake passports issued to Rohingya refugees by Bangladeshi officials.[^cite_note-96] Bangladesh Police interrogated deported Rohingya refugees. During questioning, Rohingya refugees told authorities that one Bangladeshi official at the High Commission in Canberra issued the visa.[^cite_note-97]

Some 54,000 Rohingya refugees travelled to Middle Eastern countries and illegally acquired Bangladeshi passports from the Bangladeshi consulate based in Middle East.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] Some 30,000 Rohingya refugees received work permits from Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-100] According to German public broadcaster, Deutsche Welle, corrupt consulate officials issued Bangladeshi passports to 54,000 Rohingya refugees.[^cite_note-101] Saudi authorities have demanded the Bangladesh government repatriate Rohingya refugees who hold Bangladeshi passports.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

### Land administration

Corruption is widespread in the Bangladeshi land administration. Most companies expect to make informal payments in exchange for construction permits. In a country where land is scarce, title disputes are common, and title registration takes much longer than is the average in South Asia. The collapse of an eight-story factory in April 2013 was the result of substantial building code violations, and highlighted the role of corruption in the issuance of permits and licenses and in safety inspections.[^cite_note-biz-93]

### Tax administration

The [Bangladeshi tax system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Bangladesh) is highly corrupt. In no country is it more common to be charged irregular payments in connection with taxation. More than forty percent of firms surveyed expected to present tax officials with gifts. Because the National Board of Revenue is poorly administered, tax collectors are able to collude with taxpayers, accepting personal payments in exchange for lower tax rates.[^cite_note-biz-93]

### Public procurement

The awarding of government contracts in Bangladesh very frequently involves nepotism, fraud, bribery, and favouritism. Nearly half of companies surveyed expect to make irregular payments to secure government contracts. The Public Procurement Act, Public Procurement Rules, and other legislation are intended to guarantee above-board conduct in this sector, but they are at best poorly enforced.[^cite_note-biz-93]

### Judiciary

The [Bangladeshi judicial system](50847537.md) is inefficient and under the influence of the executive branch. Political appointments, promotions, and firings are routine, and it can be difficult to enforce contracts and resolve disputes.[^cite_note-trans-12] Prosecutors, who earn only \$37.50 a month, are especially susceptible to bribery.[^cite_note-biz-93]

In 2010, Judiciary of Bangladesh was ranked as the most corrupt institution of the country.[^cite_note-104] The most prominent aspect of the judiciary of Bangladesh is, it is not an independent institution of the state in that both judiciary and the executive branch of the government are overlapped.[^cite_note-105] Bangladesh's Judicial system is infested by partisanship,[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] governmental or political influence,[^cite_note-109] judicial corruption,[^cite_note-110] delays in verdicts,[^cite_note-111] and abuse of power.[^cite_note-112]

## Business

Companies have been reported to be subjected to expensive and useless license and permit requirements.[^cite_note-biz-93] In 2014–2015, business executives said corruption was the second largest obstacle to doing business in Bangladesh. Business people are often expected to bribe public officials, often in connection with annual taxes. Fully 48% of companies have been asked to pay bribes, and most firms seeking licenses are asked for bribes, with 55% of construction permits, 77% of import licenses, 56% of water connection arrangements and 48% of electricity connection arrangements involving bribes.[^cite_note-trans-12]

Politicians are involved in giving contracts to foreign companies by taking bribes or kickbacks in closed-door negotiations.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189] Major public contracts are often said to involve corruption. Fully 49% of companies said they had to present "gifts" to win state contracts, with those "gifts" averaging a value of 3% of the contract value.[^cite_note-trans-12]

Business owners do not obey proper safety codes of the buildings and workplaces. As a result, several large accidents occurred and killed hundreds.[^cite_note-190] The garment industry is also rife with corruption, with leaders in the industry obtaining tax concessions, gaining special access to infrastructure, and ignoring with impunity official safety and security regulations. Because of this brand of corruption, the Rana Plaza factory complex collapsed, killing over 1200. Owing to corruption, the builders had been able to ignore many construction regulations.[^cite_note-trans-12]

Various international sources provided \$2.9 billion to build the Padma Multilateral Purpose Bridge near Dhaka in 2011. After the World Bank learned in 2012 of "a high-level corruption conspiracy among politicians, businessmen, and private individuals" in connection with the bridge, support for the project was withdrawn by several of the sources.[^cite_note-trans-12]

Transparency International attributes the ubiquity and high level of corruption in Bangladesh to the power of business interests over the political parties and parliament.[^cite_note-biz-93]

## Anti-corruption

### Anti Corruption Commission

Main article: [Anti Corruption Commission Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti_Corruption_Commission_Bangladesh)

Anti Corruption Commission Bangladesh (ACC) was formed through an act promulgated on 23 February 2004 that came into force on 9 May 2004. The commission is often criticised for being ineffective and a wastage of resources due to the influence of the government over it.[^cite_note-212] The Anti-Corruption Commission of Bangladesh is crippled by the 2013 amendment[^cite_note-213] of the Anti Corruption Commission Act introduced by the ruling [Awami League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Awami_League) government, which makes it necessary for the commission to obtain permissions from the government to investigate or file any charge against government bureaucrats or politicians.[^cite_note-214]

In 2015, the ACC investigated the case of [Padma Bridge Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bridge_Scandal). Even though the [World Bank](36752.md) continuously pressed the government to take actions against the perpetrators, after 53 days' of investigation, ACC found nobody to be guilty. On the basis of ACC's report, Dhaka district judge court acquitted all the seven government officials who were believed to be involved in the corruption plot.[^cite_note-215] Before that, ACC even exonerated [Syed Abul Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Abul_Hossain) and ex-state minister for foreign affairs [Abul Hasan Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Hasan_Chowdhury) from the allegation of involvement in the corruption conspiracy.[^cite_note-216]

### Transparency International Bangladesh

[Transparency International Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International_Bangladesh) (TIB) is an independent, non-government, non-partisan and non-profit organisation which is a fully accredited national chapter of Berlin-based [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International) in Bangladesh. TIB undertakes research and surveys to investigate the magnitude and impact of corruption and suggest necessary moves for establishing a transparent system of governance, politics and business.[^cite_note-217] The reports and researches published by TIB regarding corruptions are routinely rejected and overlooked by the Government and other responsible wings.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221]
