The **Libyan Arab Armed Forces** (**LAAF**),[^cite_note-13] also known as the **Libyan Arab Army**[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] or **Libyan National Army** (**LNA**),[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-WashPost_LAAF-17] are the armed forces of the Libyan faction led by [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md). They were, nominally, a unified national force under the command of Haftar when he was nominated to the role on 2 March 2015[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-18] by the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)), consisting at the time of a ground force, an air force and a navy.[^cite_note-WashPost_LAAF-17][^cite_note-ThisIsAfrica_LAAF-19]\
Libyan Arab Army[^cite_note-20]

On 16 May 2014, LNA launched [Operation Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)#Operation_Dignity), a military campaign against the [General National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress) (GNC) and armed militias and Islamist militant organizations. When the internationally recognised [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) (GNA) was established in [Tripoli](31232.md), part of the Libyan military forces were named the [Libyan Army](35021571.md) to contrast with the other part that retained the LNA identity. In the [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)), the LNA was loyal to the [Libyan House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)) that met in [Tobruk](405871.md), internationally recognised until the creation of the [Government of National Accord (GNA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) in October 2015. The LNA also fights against the [Shura Council of Benghazi Revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shura_Council_of_Benghazi_Revolutionaries), as well as [Islamic State in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_in_Libya) which was a common enemy for both LNA and the Libyan Army.

About half of the LNA consists of militias[^cite_note-ISPI_Delalande_201805-2][^cite_note-JPack_Kingdom_Militias-21] including [Madkhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madkhalism) ([Salafist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement)) militias[^cite_note-Madkhali-22] and Sudanese, Chadian[^cite_note-ISPI_Delalande_201805-2][^cite_note-JPack_Kingdom_Militias-21] and Russian mercenaries, which together constitute part of the LNA's effective forces.[^cite_note-23] The LNA possesses its own air force. Most of the Libyan Navy is loyal to the GNU.[^cite_note-Anadolu_5migrants_die_Libya-24][^cite_note-JPack_Kingdom_Militias-21]

Interventions in the political system by the LNA include the late 2016 replacement of nine [elected municipal councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Libyan_local_elections) out of a total of 27, replacing elected mayors by mostly military individuals[^cite_note-TalbotDenehy-25][^cite_note-Mayors_overthrown-26][^cite_note-UNDP_CCMCE_plan-27] and, according to witnesses cited by *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)*, the 17 July 2019 abduction of House of Representatives member [Seham Sergiwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seham_Sergiwa) at her home in [Benghazi](204114.md) by the [106th Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/106th_Brigade_\(Libya\)).[^cite_note-Indep_106th_Brigade_Sergiwa-28][^cite_note-CNN_Sergewa_abducted-29] The LNA stated that it was not responsible for the Sergiwa abduction.[^cite_note-Indep_106th_Brigade_Sergiwa-28]

## Name

The **Libyan National Army** official name is the **Libyan Arab Armed Forces (LAAF)** after 2014.

Its name was due to the pre-2014 military of Libya being called the **Libyan National Army**, and parts of it became loyal to [Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_marshal_\(Libya\)). [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md), those parts became the modern LNA.

In November 2019, the United Nations Panel of Experts on Libya established under [United Nations Security Council Resolution 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1973) started using the name *Haftar Armed Forces (HAF)* to replace the name *Libyan National Army* to refer to "all armed groups associated with Haftar". It also chose to use lower case regarding "brigades" and "battalions" in order to avoid giving them "the legitimacy of being a formed military unit of a government".[^cite_note-UNPanelExperts_S_2019_914-3]<sup>: 6 </sup>

## Allies

Haftar and the LNA is de facto backed by the governments of [Egypt](8087628.md), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_proxy_war), [Russia](25391.md) and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-\:0-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] [France](5843419.md) has also provided tacit backing for Haftars forces. France carried out unprecedented air strikes by its [Airforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_and_Space_Force) on [Chadian opposition fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Northern_Chad), which are LNA's biggest opponents. This resulted in a public dispute with [Italy](14532.md), which is supporting the Government of National Accord (GNA) in Tripoli.[^cite_note-\:0-31]

[Russia](25391.md) is Haftar's most committed ally. In May 2020 the [US Africa Command (AFRICOM)](9347800.md) stated that Russia had deployed at least 14 MiG planes to the country. The plans were supported by private military contractors of [Wagner Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group) and supporting the LNA.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In addition, an unknown quantity of [Sukhoi Su-24](382335.md) aircraft were supplied by Russia to the LNA, these are operated by Russian mercenaries.[^cite_note-38]

### Special forces

[Al-Saiqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Saiqa_\(Libya\)) is an elite army unit, formed from a mixture of paratroopers and commandos. It numbers a few thousand and reports to the Ministry of Defence. It is popular in Benghazi, particularly in light of its opposition to Islamist [Ansar al-Sharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Sharia_\(Benghazi\)) group and because it is seen as a symbol of the reborn Libyan armed forces.[^cite_note-45]

### Militias

[Madkhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madkhalism) militias in the LNA include the [Tawhid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawhid) Battalion commanded by Izz al-Din al-Tarhuni; the [Tariq Ibn Ziyad Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_Ibn_Ziyad_Brigade?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Subul al-Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subul_al-Salam?action=edit\&redlink=1) group and the [al-Wadi Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Wadi_Brigade?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-MEE_LNA_composition-46] LNA groups from [Sabratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabratha), [Sorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorman), [Tiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiji,_Libya?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Badr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badr,_Libya), towns in which Madkhali preachers were active and supported Haftar, are mostly Madkhali Salafists.[^cite_note-SANA_WhoWhom_Tripoli-9]

During the [2019–20 Western Libya campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Western_Libya_campaign), the LNA was allied with the [al-Kaniyat militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kaniyat_militia?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Tarhuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarhuna).[^cite_note-setback-47][^cite_note-FP-48][^cite_note-WP-49][^cite_note-NYT-50][^cite_note-NYT2-51]

The number of auxiliary LNA forces (militias and mercenaries) was estimated in May 2019 as 18000 by Jason Pack of the [Institute for International Political Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_International_Political_Studies).[^cite_note-JPack_Kingdom_Militias-21]

### Foreign mercenaries

Foreign mercenaries operating during 2019 Western Libya offensive on behalf of the LNA include Sudanese, Chadians and Russians.[^cite_note-MEE_LNA_composition-46]

On 1 January 2022, the *5+5 Libyan Joint Military Commission* announced the deportation of 300 Sudanese mercenaries linked to the LNA from the Eastern Region.[^cite_note-56]

## History

See also: [Armed Forces of the Libyan Arab Jamahiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya)

### 2011–2013

The Libyan National Army was founded in 2011 by the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council), after forces aligned to it defeated the previous [Libyan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Army_\(1951%E2%80%932011\)) and overthrew [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md)'s government. Supply depots and bases having been damaged during the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_Civil_War), the new army is faced with the challenge of having to rebuild much of the country's military infrastructure.[^cite_note-57] [Yousef Mangoush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_Mangoush) was named as its first Chief of Staff on 2 January 2012 and the force saw its first major deployment on 23 February, when it was deployed to [Kufra](3337477.md) to intervene in a [tribal conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Kufra_conflict).[^cite_note-58]

In November 2011, the National Transitional Council began the difficult process of restructuring the army, with military personnel who defected from the Gaddafi government and former rebel fighters of the National Liberation Army forming the basis of the new Libyan Army. Major General Khalifa Belgacem Haftar was chosen as the overall commander of the new Libyan Army due to his military experience and loyalty to the revolution that overthrew Gaddafi.[^cite_note-59]

The Libyan Army only numbered "a few thousand" trained soldiers in November 2011, and was rapidly trying to train up new fighters who could keep the peace nationwide and deter rogue militias from acting without NTC orders, and was responsible for brokering a ceasefire on at least one occasion in November between warring militas from Zawiya and Al Maya.[^cite_note-60]

On 1 December 2011, it was reported that the National Liberation Army was to integrate up to 50,000 former rebel fighters into the new Libyan national army and police forces, with the aid of French training, with long-term aims to integrate as many as 200,000 fighters from the brigades that had fought against Gaddafi during the civil war.[^cite_note-61]

In December 2011, Italy agreed to provide training to the Libyan Army as it attempted to reorganize in the aftermath of the Civil War.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Also in December, large numbers of former rebels were being given jobs in the new army, whilst the government also announced that they would be free to join the special forces and the Navy too. According to [Osama al-Juwaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_al-Juwaili), the defence minister: "The idea is to inject new blood in the army which was marginalised by the tyrant (Gaddafi)"[^cite_note-64]

General [Yousef Mangoush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_Mangoush) said on 5 January 2012 that Libya's new army faces major obstacles such as rebuilding bases destroyed during the conflict, as well as disarming militias that were not part of the new army. National Army commander General [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md) said later that it could take between three and five years for Libya to field a capable enough army to protect its borders.[^cite_note-65]

On 7 May 2013, Libya's Defense Minister [Mohammed al-Barghathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Al-Barghathi) resigned due to a crisis caused by gunmen who have besieged two ministries for more than a week, a ministry official said. He later withdrew his resignation after Prime Minister Zeidan convinced him to stay.[^cite_note-66]

Under an agreement reached at the Lough Erne G8 summit in June 2013, NATO countries the United Kingdom, Italy, Turkey, and the United States undertook to help train up to 15,000 personnel from Libyan National Army units over a two-year period. They were to take units from newly formed brigades for 10-week stints of intensive infantry training. The 27th Brigade was due to start at [Bassingbourn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassingbourn_Barracks) in eastern England in January 2014.[^cite_note-The_Observer_brigades-67] As a result of disorder and sexual assaults by some Libyan army cadets, the UK cancelled the programme in November 2014. The Libyan trainees were sent back to Libya, with the exception of five who were tried for sexual offenses.[^cite_note-68]

### 2016 overthrow of mayors

In late 2016, Major-General [Abdulrazek al-Nadoori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulrazek_al-Nadoori) of the LNA replaced several of the [elected municipal mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Libyan_local_elections) in eastern Libya by unelected people, mostly military.[^cite_note-TalbotDenehy-25][^cite_note-Mayors_overthrown-26] Altogether the LNA replaced nine elected councils, out of 27 in its area of control, by military administrators.[^cite_note-UNDP_CCMCE_plan-27]

### 2025

In May 2025, days after the [2025 Tripoli clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Tripoli_clashes), the LNA held a large military parade showing off advanced equipment in Benghazi, including the [BM-30 Smerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-30_Smerch) and the [Tor missile system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_missile_system).[^cite_note-Nova-69]

What is certain is that a reasonable quantity of their equipment probably came from ransacked stocks of the original [Libyan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Army_\(1951%E2%80%932011\)) and from defectors as well. A significant amount of arms and equipment possessed by the LNA were majorly shipped by the United Arab Emirates and Russia.[^cite_note-70]

Having an airbase in Libya (Al-Khadim), the UAE constantly breached the UN arms embargo to transfer arms to the Haftar forces. An Emirati firm, Lancaster-6 was involved in transferring three Super Pumas to the LNA in June 2019. The helicopters were believed to be inoperable. The three helicopters remained inside hangars until Haftar's military parade in May 2021 and were expected to take part in his air force.[^cite_note-71]
