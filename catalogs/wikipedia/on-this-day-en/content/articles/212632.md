**Gunma Prefecture** (群馬県, *Gunma-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[ɡɯꜜm.ma, ɡɯm.maꜜ.keɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is an inland [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).[^cite_note-3] Gunma Prefecture has a population of 1,937,626 (1 October 2019) and has a geographic area of 6,362 square kilometres (2,456 sq mi). Gunma Prefecture borders [Niigata Prefecture](181660.md) and [Fukushima Prefecture](211914.md) to the north, [Nagano Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture) to the southwest, [Saitama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture) to the south, and [Tochigi Prefecture](180960.md) to the east.

[Maebashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maebashi) is the capital and [Takasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takasaki) is the largest city of Gunma Prefecture, with other major cities including [Ōta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cta,_Gunma), [Isesaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isesaki,_Gunma), and [Kiryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiry%C5%AB,_Gunma).[^cite_note-4] Gunma Prefecture is one of only eight [inland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked) prefectures, located on the northwestern corner of the [Kantō Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_Plain) with 14% of its total land being designated as [natural parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan).

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Gunma Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Gunma_Prefecture)

The ancient province of Gunma was a center of horse breeding and trading activities for the newly immigrated continental peoples (or [Toraijin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toraijin)). The arrival of horses and the remains of horse tackle coincides with the arrival of a large migration from the mainland. From this point forward, the horse became a vital part of Japanese military maneuvers, quickly displacing the older Yayoi tradition of fighting on foot.

When [Mount Haruna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Haruna) erupted in the late 6th century, Japan was still in the pre-historical phase (prior to the importation of the Chinese writing system during the Nara period). The Gunma Prefectural archaeology unit in 1994 was able to date the eruption through zoological anthropology at the corral sites that were buried in ash.

In the past, Gunma was joined with [Tochigi Prefecture](180960.md) and called *[Kenu Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenu_Province)*. This was later divided into *Kami-tsu-ke* (Upper Kenu, Gunma) and *Shimo-tsu-ke* (Lower Kenu, Tochigi). The area is sometimes referred to as Jomo (上毛, *Jōmō*). For most of Japanese history, Gunma was known as the province of [Kozuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozuke_Province).[^cite_note-5]

In the early period of contact between western nations and Japan, particularly the late Tokugawa, it was referred to by foreigners as the "Joushu States", inside (fudai, or loyalist) Tokugawa retainers and the Tokugawa family symbol is widely seen on public buildings, temples, and shrines.

The [Tenmei eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenmei_eruption) of [Mount Asama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Asama) occurred in 1783, causing enormous damage.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

The first modern silk factories were built with Italian and French assistance at [Annaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaka,_Gunma) in the 1870s. Known for their handy work in factories, the Yara Milano were renowned for their crafts.

In the early Meiji period, in what was locally called the Gunma Incident of 1884, a bloody struggle between the idealistic democratic [Westernisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westernization) and the conservative [Prussian-model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_education_system) nationalists took place in Gunma and neighboring Nagano. The modern Japanese army gunned down farmers with new repeating rifles built in Japan. The farmers in Gunma were said to be the first victims of the [Murata rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murata_rifle).

In the twentieth century, the Japanese aviation pioneer Nakajima Chikushi of Oizumi, Gunma Prefecture, founded the [Nakajima Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakajima_Aircraft_Company). At first, he produced mostly licensed models of foreign designs, but beginning with the all-Japanese [Nakajima 91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakajima_Type_91_fighter) fighter plane in 1931, his company became a world leader in aeronautical design and manufacture, with its headquarters at Ota, Gunma Ken. The factory now produces [Subaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru) motorcars and other products under the name of [Subaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru) née Fuji Heavy Industries.[^cite_note-8]

In the 1930s, German architect [Bruno Julius Florian Taut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Taut) lived and conducted research for a while in Takasaki.[^cite_note-9]

The [Girard incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girard_incident), which disturbed US-Japanese relations in the 1950s, occurred in Gunma in 1957, at [Sōmagahara Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%8Dmagahara_Base?action=edit\&redlink=1) near [Shibukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibukawa,_Gunma).

Four modern prime ministers are from Gunma, namely, [Takeo Fukuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeo_Fukuda), [Yasuhiro Nakasone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuhiro_Nakasone), [Keizo Obuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keizo_Obuchi), and [Yasuo Fukuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuo_Fukuda), the son of Takeo.

## Geography



Ono Lake, Jizō-dake



Mount Nakanodake viewed from Mount Shibutsu

One of only eight inland prefectures in Japan, Gunma is the northwesternmost prefecture of the Kantō plain. Except for the central and southeast areas, where most of the population is concentrated, it is mostly mountainous. To the north are [Niigata](181660.md) and [Fukushima](211914.md) prefectures, while to the east lies [Tochigi Prefecture](180960.md). To the west lies the [Nagano Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture), and the [Saitama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture) is to the south.

Some of the major mountains in Gunma are [Mount Akagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Akagi), [Mount Haruna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Haruna), [Mount Myōgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_My%C5%8Dgi), [Mount Nikkō-Shirane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Nikk%C5%8D-Shirane) and [Mount Asama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Asama), which is located on the Nagano border. Major rivers include the [Tone River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tone_River), the [Agatsuma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatsuma_River), and the [Karasu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karasu_River_\(Gunma\)).

As of 1 April 2012, 14% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely [Jōshin'etsu-kōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dshin'etsu-k%C5%8Dgen_National_Park), [Nikkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D_National_Park), and [Oze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oze_National_Park) National Parks and [Myōgi-Arafune-Saku Kōgen Quasi-National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My%C5%8Dgi-Arafune-Saku_K%C5%8Dgen_Quasi-National_Park).[^cite_note-10]

Because Gunma is situated in inland Japan, the difference in temperature in the summer compared to the winter is large, and there is less precipitation. This is because of the [karakkaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakkaze) ("empty wind"), a strong, dry wind that occurs in the winter when the snow falls on the coasts of Niigata. The wind carrying clouds with snow are obstructed by the [Echigo Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echigo_Mountains), and it also snows there, although the high peaks do not let the wind go past them. For this reason, the wind changes into the *kara-kaze*. On August 5, 2025, [Isesaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isesaki) a city located in Gunma Prefecture recorded a temperature of 41.8 °C, the hottest temperature ever registered in Japanese history. ## Economy

Gunma's modern industries include transport equipment and electrical equipment, concentrated around [Maebashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maebashi,_Gunma) and the eastern region nearest [Tokyo](30057.md). More traditional industries include [sericulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sericulture) and [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture). Gunma's major agricultural products include [cabbages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage) and [konnyaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konnyaku). Gunma produces over 90% of Japan's konnyaku, and two-thirds of the farms in the village of [Tsumagoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsumagoi,_Gunma) are cabbage farms.[^cite_note-13] Also, the city of [Ōta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cta,_Gunma) is famous for the car industry, notably the [Subaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru) factory.

## Culture

There is a [local dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunma_dialect), known in Japanese as '*gunma-ben*' or '*jōshū-ben*'.

Gunma has a traditional card game called *[Jomo Karuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomo_Karuta)* (上毛かるた). It features people, places, and things of regional and/or cultural importance.

### Famous foods

In 2007, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries held an event to find the top 100 best local dishes across all of Japan. Three dishes were featured from Gunma; yaki-manju, [okkirikomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkirikomi), and [konnyaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konjac).[^cite_note-14]

### Melody roads

As of 2018, Gunma is home to eleven of Japan's over thirty [Melody roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_road#Japan). 2,559 grooves cut into a 175-meter stretch of the road surface in transmit a [tactile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch) [vibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation) through the wheels into the [car body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_body).[^cite_note-GuardianJP-15][^cite_note-Noise-16][^cite_note-ITN-17] The roads can be found in Katashina, Minakami, Takayama, Kanna, Ueno, Kusatsu, Tsumagoi, Nakanojo, Takasaki, Midori, and Maebashi. Each is of a differing length and plays a different song. Naganohara also used to be home to a Melody Road playing "[Aj lučka, lučka široká](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aj_lu%C4%8Dka,_lu%C4%8Dka_%C5%A1irok%C3%A1)", though the road in question was paved over in 2013 due to noise complaints.

## Prefectural symbols

The prefectural symbol consists of the first [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji) of the word 'Gunma' surrounded by three stylized mountains symbolizing the three important mountains of Gunma Prefecture: [Mount Haruna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Haruna), [Mount Akagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Akagi), and [Mount Myōgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_My%C5%8Dgi).

For marketing, the Prefectural Government also uses [Gunma-chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunma-chan), a small [super deformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_deformed) drawing of a horse character wearing a green cap. It is used on promotional posters, banners, and other notable printed materials from the Prefectural Government. Other agencies and companies formally or informally use variations of its likeness and other horse-shaped characters when making signs or notices for work on buildings, roads, and other public notices.
