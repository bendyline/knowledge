**Republika Srpska** ([Serbian Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Cyrillic_alphabet): Република Српска [\[repǔblika sr̩̂pskaː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/28/RepublikaSrpska.ogg/RepublikaSrpska.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/28/RepublikaSrpska.ogg/RepublikaSrpska.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; lit. 'Serb Republic'), or the **Republic of Srpska**, is one of the two [political "entities"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_entities_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) of [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), the other being the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina).[^cite_note-OBrien2010-16][^cite_note-Meskic2011-17] Situated in the northern and eastern regions of the country, it recorded a population of 1,228,423 in the 2013 census. Its administrative centre and largest city is [Banja Luka](385109.md), located on the banks of the [Vrbas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrbas_\(river\)).

[Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republika_Srpska_\(1992%E2%80%931995\)) was established in 1992 at the onset of the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) with the stated purpose of safeguarding the interests of the [Serbs of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). During the conflict, a majority of [Croats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croats_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks) were [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing_in_the_Bosnian_War) from territories controlled by Republika Srpska, while the majority of Serbs were displaced or expelled from the present-day [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) to Republika Srpska. The 1995 [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement) created Republika Srpska as one of [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md)'s two constituent entities.[^cite_note-18] Today, it is inhabited by most of the [Serb](59512.md) population of the country.

Republika Srpska enjoys one of the broadest sets of prerogatives and competencies of any [subnational entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division) in Europe, with powers that in many respects resemble those of a sovereign state rather than a purely subnational entity.[^cite_note-19] It operates under a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system), with legislative power vested in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republika_Srpska\)), which comprises 83 seats.[^cite_note-24] Republika Srpska is administratively divided into 64 municipalities, known as *[opštine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Republika_Srpska)* (singular: *opština*).

## Name



Welcome sign on the [administrative line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Entity_Boundary_Line) with the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

In the name *_Republika Srpska_*, *_[Srpska](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Srpska)_* is a [noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun) [derived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphological_derivation) from the [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) of the [Serbs](59512.md) with a different [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix) from *_Srbija_* '[Serbia](29265.md)'. In [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language), many [names of countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country-name_etymologies) are formed with the *[-sk-](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction\:Proto-Slavic/-%D1%8Csk%D1%8A)* suffix (e.g. *[Bugarska](https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%83%D0%B3%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0)* 'Bulgaria', *[Danska](https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0)* 'Denmark', *[Finska](https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A4%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0)* 'Finland', *[Hrvatska](https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D1%80%D0%B2%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0)* 'Croatia', *[Irska](https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A0%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%83%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B0%20%D0%98%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0)* 'Ireland', *[Turska](https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A2%D1%83%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0)* 'Turkey'). An analogous English formation would be *Serbland* (used sporadically).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The government uses the name "Republic of Srpska" in English.[^cite_note-28]

Although *Republika Srpska* is variously [glossed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloss_\(annotation\)#In_linguistics) in English as "Serb Republic",[^cite_note-29] "Bosnian Serb Republic",[^cite_note-30] or "Republic of Srpska", the Constitution of Bosnia and Herzegovina and English-language news sources such as the [BBC](19344654.md),[^cite_note-31] *[The New York Times](30680.md)*,[^cite_note-32] and *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*[^cite_note-33] generally refer to the entity by its transliteration.

According to *[Glas Srpske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glas_Srpske)*, a Banja Luka daily, the modern entity's name was created by its first minister of culture, Ljubomir Zuković.[^cite_note-34]

## History

Main article: [History of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

### Early history

Main articles: [Early history of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_history_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Illyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrians)



Iron Age cult carriage from Banjani near Sokolac

Archaeological evidence in Republika Srpska attest to pronounced human activity in the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic). Within the wider region of Herzegovina, the discoveries tie the region's early activities to [Croatia](5573.md) and [Montenegro](20760.md).

More permanent settlement arose with the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic), which occurred along the rivers of Bosnia and Herzegovina as farming spread from the southeast. The [Butmir culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butmir_culture) developed near present-day [East Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sarajevo) on the river [Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosna_\(river\)). A variety of idols, mostly of female characters, were found in the Butmir site, along with dugouts.

The [Indo-European migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations) of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) contributed to the first use of metal tools in the region, along with the construction of burial mounds—[tumuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus), or [kurgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan). Remains of these mounds can be found in northwestern Bosnia near [Prijedor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prijedor), testament to not only denser settlement but also Bronze Age relics.[^cite_note-\:0-35]

With the influx of the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age), the [Glasinac culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasinac_culture), developing near [Sokolac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokolac,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) in eastern Republika Srpska, was one of the most important of the country's long-standing Indo-European inhabitants, the [Illyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrians). Later, these Illyrians—the [Autariatae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autariatae)—were influenced by the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) after the [Gallic invasion of the Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_settlement_of_Southeast_Europe).[^cite_note-36]

### Roman period



Territory of Republika Srpska within the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), 4th century

With the end of the [Illyrian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_Wars), most of Bosnia and Herzegovina came under [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) control within the province of [Illyricum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyricum_\(Roman_province\)). In this period, the Romans consolidated the region through the construction of a dense road network and the Romanisation of the local population. Among these roads was the *Via Argentaria*, or 'Silver Way', which transported silver from the eastern mines of Bosnia to Roman population centres. Modern placenames, such as the [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)) and [Sana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sana_\(river\)) rivers in the northwest, have Latin origins, meaning "the one" and "the healthy", respectively. This rule was not uninterrupted, however, with the suppression of the once-dominant Illyrian population came revolts such as the [Bellum Batonianum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellum_Batonianum). After 20 AD, however, the entire country was conquered by the Romans, and it was split between [Pannonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia) and [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia_\(Roman_province\)). The most prominent Roman city in Bosnia was the relatively small [Servitium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servitium), near modern-day [Gradiška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradi%C5%A1ka,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) in the northern part of the entity.

Christianity spread to the region relatively late at least partially due to the countryside's mountainous nature and its lack of large settlements. In the fourth century, however, the country began to be [Christianised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianised) en masse.[^cite_note-37] With the split of the Western and Eastern Roman Empires in 395, modern-day Republika Srpska fell under the Western Roman Empire. Testament to its and Bosnia and Herzegovina's later religious polarisation, it was conquered as a frontier of the Eastern Roman Empire, a harbinger of the religious division to come.

### Middle Ages



[Kastel Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastel_Fortress) in Banja Luka, first appearing as an early Slavic [hillfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillfort) or *gradina*

With the loosening of the Roman grip on the region came the [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period), which, given Republika Srpska's position in southeastern Europe, involved a wide variety of peoples. Among the first was the invasion of Germanic peoples from the east and north, and the territory became a part of the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom) in 476.

By 535, the territory was taken once again by the Byzantine Empire. At this time, the Empire's grip was once again relatively loose, and Slavs invaded the surrounding area. Modern-day Republika Srpska was therefore split between the mediaeval [Kingdom of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Croatia_\(925%E2%80%931102\))[^cite_note-Larousse-38] and, according to [De Administrando Imperio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Administrando_Imperio), [mediaeval Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_in_the_Middle_Ages) *[županije](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDupan)*, including, [Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_\(region\)), [Zachlumia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachlumia), [Travunija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travunija), and Serbia, then including land in eastern Bosnia.[^cite_note-39] Parts of present-day Srpska were locations of settlement of the original [White Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbs_\(tribe\)) people.[^cite_note-40]



[Stećak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste%C4%87ak), a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), on [Treskavica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treskavica) mountain

By the end of the 11th century, the entirety of Bosnia became part of the [Hungarian Crown Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Crown_Lands). Under Hungarian rule, the area was known as the [Banate of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banate_of_Bosnia). Later, however, under the rule of [Ban Kulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Kulin), regarded as the founder of Bosnia, the region became *de facto* independent. In 1377, the Banate of Bosnia became the medieval Kingdom of Bosnia, under [Tvrtko I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvrtko_I) of [House of Kotromanić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Kotromani%C4%87). The capitals of the kingdom were all located in its centre, while the northern periphery remained under nominal Hungarian rule as the region of [Usora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usora_\(zemlja\)). Architectural legacies from this period include [Kastel Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastel_Fortress) in Banja Luka, the fortress of [Doboj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doboj) as well as castles, churches, and monasteries across the country.

With the growth of the Ottoman Empire, [Stefan Tomašević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Toma%C5%A1evi%C4%87), the last [Kotromanić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotromani%C4%87_dynasty) ruler, surrendered Bosnia and Serbia to Ottoman tributary status. A Catholic, he was unpopular among the Orthodox population of Serbia, as well as the members of the [Bosnian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Church).[^cite_note-41] Refusing to pay tribute to [Mehmed the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_the_Conqueror), King Stefan was executed and much of Bosnia fell under direct [Ottoman](22278.md) rule in 1463 as the [Eyalet of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_Eyalet). The entirety of the country fell in 1482, with the founding of the [Sanjak of Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Herzegovina).

### 16th to 19th centuries



[Mehmed Paša Sokolović Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Pa%C5%A1a_Sokolovi%C4%87_Bridge), a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), in [Višegrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C5%A1egrad), founded by Ottoman Grand vizier of Serb origin [Sokollu Mehmed Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokollu_Mehmed_Pasha)

Ottoman rule in modern-day Republika Srpska saw another addition to its religious fabric—Islam. Members of the Bosnian Church, as well as many Orthodox and Catholic Bosnians, gradually converted to Islam. Ottoman rule left a profound architectural legacy in Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska. The most famous mosque from this period is the [Ferhadija mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferhat_Pasha_Mosque_\(Banja_Luka\)), located in [Banja Luka](385109.md). In addition, the subject of [Ivo Andrić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Andri%C4%87)'s book *[The Bridge on the Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bridge_on_the_Drina),* [Mehmed Paša Sokolović Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Pa%C5%A1a_Sokolovi%C4%87_Bridge) in [Višegrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C5%A1egrad), was constructed by [Mimar Sinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimar_Sinan), the most famous Ottoman architect, in 1577, for [Grand Vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_vizier) [Sokollu Mehmed Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokollu_Mehmed_Pasha). Years earlier, the same Grand Vizier was born into an [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) family in a small town in Bosnia and taken from his parents as a child for upbringing as a [janissary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janissary). His bridge is a symbol of the religious and cultural spans—and eventually conflict— that characterise Republika Srpska and Bosnia and Herzegovina.

With the [Ottoman-Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Habsburg_wars) conflicts of the late 17th and 18th centuries, parts of northern Republika Srpska became a part of the Habsburg Empire for relatively short periods. The rule was more permanent following the Austro-Hungarian invasion in 1878. Characterised by economic and social development not seen in the by-then backwards Ottoman Empire, [Austro-Hungarian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_rule_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) was welcomed by many. However, many Muslims left Bosnia, leaving Serbs as the majority in the entirety of the Condominium.[^cite_note-42]

### 20th century



The *Poplar of horror* in the [Jasenovac Memorial Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasenovac_concentration_camp), one of the key sites in the [Genocide of Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Serbs_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia), in which tens of thousands of Bosnian Serb civilians were brutally killed

With the assassination of [Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria), carried out by Bosnian Serb [Gavrilo Princip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavrilo_Princip), a member of the [Yugoslavist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavism) [Mlada Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Bosnia), World War I broke out in 1914. Following the war, the territory of modern-day Republika Srpska was incorporated into the [Vrbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrbas_Banovina), [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina_Banovina), and [Zeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_Banovina) [banovinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_the_Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats, and Slovenes, renamed Yugoslavia in 1929.

Following the outbreak of [World War II](32927.md) and the [invasion of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Yugoslavia) in 1941, modern-day Republika Srpska fell under the rule of the Nazi [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state), The [Independent State of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_State_of_Croatia). Around 300,000 Serbs are estimated to have died under the [Ustashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustashe) regime as a result of their [genocide campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Serbs_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia);[^cite_note-43] a slew of [massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mass_executions_and_massacres_in_Yugoslavia_during_World_War_II), as well as the use of a variety of concentration and extermination camps, took place in Republika Srpska during the war. The [Jasenovac concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasenovac_concentration_camp), located in modern-day Croatia, was the site of the deaths of some 100,000 people, about 47,000-52,000 of which were Serbs.[^cite_note-44] Massacres also occurred at [Garavice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garavice) and [Kruščica concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kru%C5%A1%C4%8Dica_concentration_camp) in the eastern part of Bosnia. The regime systematically and brutally massacred Serbs in villages in the countryside, using a variety of tools.[^cite_note-45] The scale of the violence meant that approximately every sixth Serb living in Bosnia-Herzegovina was the victim of a massacre and virtually every Serb had a family member that was killed in the war, mostly by the Ustaše.[^cite_note-46] An estimated 209,000 Serbs or 16.9% of its Bosnia population were killed on the territory of Bosnia–Herzegovina during the war.[^cite_note-47] Today, [monuments honouring these victims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_War_II_monuments_and_memorials_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) can be found across Republika Srpska and Bosnia and Herzegovina.

The Yugoslav royalist and [Serbian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_nationalist) movement [Chetniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetniks), a guerilla force that engaged in tactical or selective [collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wartime_collaboration) with the occupying forces for almost all of the war,[^cite_note-48] pursued [genocide against Croats and Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetnik_war_crimes_in_World_War_II),[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] which included thousands of Croat and Muslim civilians killed on the territory of modern-day Republika Srpska. The Chetniks killed an estimated 50,000 to 68,000 Muslims and Croats.[^cite_note-51] A December 1941 directive, attributed to Chetnik leader [Draža Mihailović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dra%C5%BEa_Mihailovi%C4%87), explicitly ordered the [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) of Muslims and Croats from [Sandžak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand%C5%BEak) and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md).[^cite_note-\:2-52] About 300 villages and small towns were destroyed, along with a large number of mosques and Catholic churches.[^cite_note-53] The Chetniks were almost exclusively made up of [Serbs](59512.md) except for a large number of [Montenegrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrins) who identified as Serbs.[^cite_note-\:2-52]

During the entire course of World War II in Yugoslavia, 64.1% of all Bosnian Partisans were Serbs.[^cite_note-anubih.ba-Hoare-54]

After World War II came a period of relative peace and economic development. [Ljubija mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubija_mine) and companies like [Agrokomerc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrokomerc) played a vital role in much of the economic development of the [Socialist Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). Literacy rates increased greatly, and the University of Banja Luka was founded in 1975.

### Bosnian War

#### Bosnian War and the proclamation of the Republika Srpska

Main article: [Republika Srpska (1992–1995)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republika_Srpska_\(1992%E2%80%931995\))

See also: [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) and [Bosnian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_genocide)



Territories which were controlled by [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) during the war at its greatest extent (around 1993) compared with current borders.





[Biljana Plavšić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biljana_Plav%C5%A1i%C4%87), former president of Republika Srpska, and [Ratko Mladić](22742941.md), former Commander of the [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska). Mladić was found guilty of numerous [atrocity crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrocity_crimes) (including [genocide](12441.md)) by the [ICTY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Tribunal_for_the_Former_Yugoslavia). Plavšić pleaded guilty at the ICTY to one count of persecution as a [crime against humanity](61099.md).

Representatives of main political parties and some other national organisations and institutions of [Serb people in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) met on 13 October 1990 in [Banja Luka](385109.md) and formed the 'Serbian National Council of Bosnia and Herzegovina' as a Serb political body.[^cite_note-55] In a session on 14–15 October 1991, the [People's Assembly of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Assembly_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), then part of the [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), approved the 'Memorandum on Sovereignty', as had already been done by [Slovenia](27338.md) and [Croatia](5573.md), as a way to proclaim independence from the rest of Yugoslavia. The memorandum was adopted despite opposition from 83 Serb deputies belonging to the [Serb Democratic Party](413319.md) (most of the Serb parliamentary representatives) as well as the [Serbian Renewal Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Renewal_Movement) and the [Union of Reform Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Reform_Forces), who regarded the move as illegal.[^cite_note-WP-56][^cite_note-57]

On 24 October 1991, the Serb deputies formed the [Assembly of the Serb People in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republika_Srpska\)) (*Skupština srpskog naroda u Bosni i Hercegovini*) to be the highest representative and legislative body of the [Bosnian Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Serb) population,[^cite_note-OG1.1-58][^cite_note-59] ending the tripartite coalition.

The Union of Reform Forces soon ceased to exist, but its members remained in the assembly as the [Independent Members of Parliament Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Members_of_Parliament_Caucus). The assembly undertook to address the achievement of equality between the Serbs and other peoples and the protection of the Serbs' interests, which they contended had been jeopardised by decisions of the Bosnian parliament.[^cite_note-OG1.1-58] On 9 January 1992, the assembly proclaimed the Republic of the Serb People of Bosnia and Herzegovina (*Republika srpskoga naroda Bosne i Hercegovine*), declaring it part of Yugoslavia.[^cite_note-60]

On 28 February 1992, the assembly adopted the [Constitution of the Serbian Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Republika_Srpska) (the name adopted instead of the previous *Republika srpskog naroda Bosne i Hercegovine*), which would include districts, municipalities, and regions where Serbs were the majority and also those where they had allegedly become a minority because of [persecution during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Serbs_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia). The republic was part of Yugoslavia and could enter into union with political bodies representing other peoples of Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-OG1.3-61]

The Bosnian parliament, without its Serb deputies, held a [referendum](51582.md) on the independence of Bosnia and Herzegovina on 29 February and 1 March 1992, but most Serbs boycotted it since the assembly had previously (9–10 November 1991) held a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) in the Serb regions, 96% having opted for membership of the Yugoslav federation formed by [Serbia](29265.md) and [Montenegro](20760.md).[^cite_note-Kreca-62] The referendum had a 64% turnout and 92.7% or 99% (according to different sources) voted for independence.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] On 6 March the Bosnian parliament promulgated the results of the referendum, proclaiming the republic's independence from Yugoslavia. The republic's independence was recognised by the [European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Community) on 6 April 1992 and by the United States on 7 April. On the same day the Serbs' assembly in session in Banja Luka declared a severance of governmental ties with Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-65] The name *Republika Srpska* was adopted on 12 August 1992.[^cite_note-66]

The political controversy escalated into the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War), which would last until the autumn of 1995.[^cite_note-ICTYkaradzic2009-67]

The war was ended by the [General Framework Agreement for Peace in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement), reached at [Wright-Patterson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright-Patterson_Air_Force_Base) near [Dayton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio), on 21 November and formally signed in Paris on 14 December 1995. Annex 4 of the Agreement is the current [Constitution of Bosnia and Herzegovina](12844303.md), which recognises Republika Srpska as one of its two main [political-territorial divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_divisions_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and defines the governmental functions and powers of the two entities. The [boundary lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Entity_Boundary_Line) between the entities were delineated in Annex 2 of the Agreement.[^cite_note-68]

Between 1992 and 2008, the Constitution of Republika Srpska was amended 121 times. Article 1 states that Republika Srpska is a territorially unified, indivisible, and inalienable constitutional and legal entity that shall perform its constitutional, legislative, executive, and judicial functions independently.[^cite_note-69]

### Impact of war

The [war in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) resulted in major changes in the country, some of which were quantified in a 1998 [UNESCO](21786641.md) report. Some two million people, about half the country's population, were displaced. In 1996, there were some 435,346 ethnic Serb refugees from the Federation in Republika Srpska, while another 197,925 had gone to Serbia. In 1991, 27% of the non-agricultural labour force was unemployed in Bosnia, and this number increased due to the war.[^cite_note-70] By 2009, the unemployment rate in Bosnia and Herzegovina was estimated at 29%, according to the [CIA](5183633.md)'s *[The World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook)*.[^cite_note-71] Republika Srpska's population of Serbs had increased by 547,741 due to the influx of ethnic Serb refugees from the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the former unrecognised state of the [Republic of Serbian Krajina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Serbian_Krajina) in the new [Republic of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Croatia).[^cite_note-72]



[Srebrenica Genocide Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_Genocide_Memorial)

In Eastern Bosnia, Bosnian Serbs besieged the town of Srebrenica, among others. Srebrenica was declared a UN 'Safe Area' in 1993, and it served as an enclave for Muslim refugees for the final years of the Bosnian War. In the middle of July 1995, more than 8,000 Muslim [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks), mainly men and boys, in and around the town of [Srebrenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica), were killed in what became known as the [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre), which was subsequently designated as an act of [genocide](12441.md) by the [International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Tribunal_for_the_former_Yugoslavia) and the [International Court of Justice](14918.md).

Acts of [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) against the non-Serb populations reduced the numbers of other groups. Serb police, soldiers, and irregulars attacked Muslims and Croats and burned and looted their homes. Some were killed on the spot; others were rounded up and killed elsewhere or forced to flee.[^cite_note-73] The number of Croats was reduced by 114,593 (the majority of the Croatian pre-war population), and the number of Bosniaks by some 268,907. Some 136,000 of approximately 496,000 Bosniak refugees forced to flee the territory of what is now Republika Srpska have since returned home.[^cite_note-74]



A Serbian cemetery for the victims of the war in [Bratunac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratunac)

As of 2008, 40% of Bosniaks and 8.5% of Croats had returned to Republika Srpska, while 14% of Serbs who left their homes in territories controlled by Bosniaks or Croats also returned to their pre-war communities.[^cite_note-revstrat-75]

In the early 2000s, NGOs and the [Helsinki Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Security_and_Cooperation_in_Europe) alleged discrimination against non-Serbs. The [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group) reported in 2002 that in some parts of Republika Srpska a non-Serb returnee is ten times more likely to be the victim of violent crime than a local Serb.[^cite_note-76] The Helsinki Commission, in a 2001 statement on 'Tolerance and Non-Discrimination', pointed at violence against non-Serbs, stating that in the cities of [Banja Luka](385109.md)[^cite_note-77] and [Trebinje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebinje),[^cite_note-78] mobs attacked people who sought to lay foundations for new [mosques](19894.md).

Non-Serbs have reported continuing difficulties in returning to their original homes, and the assembly has a poor record of cooperation in apprehending individuals indicted for war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocide.[^cite_note-79]

Organisations such as the [Society for Threatened Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Threatened_Peoples), reporting to the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) in 2008, have made claims of discrimination against non-Serb refugees in Republika Srpska, particularly areas with high unemployment in the Drina Valley such as [Srebrenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica), [Bratunac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratunac), [Višegrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C5%A1egrad), and [Foča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fo%C4%8Da).[^cite_note-80]

According to the Ministry for Human Rights and Refugees of Bosnia and Herzegovina, [European Union Police Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Police_Mission_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR), and other international organisations, security in both Republika Srpska and the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina in 2015 was satisfactory.[^cite_note-revstrat-75]

## Governance

Main articles: [Politics of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Republika_Srpska) and [Constitution of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Republika_Srpska)



The [National Assembly of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republika_Srpska\)) in [Banja Luka](385109.md)

According to [its constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Republika_Srpska), Republika Srpska has its president, legislature (the 83-member unicameral [National Assembly of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republika_Srpska\))), executive government, [police force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_of_Republika_Srpska), court system, customs service (under the state-level customs service), and postal service. It also has official symbols, including a coat of arms, a flag (a variant of the [Serbian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Serbia) without the coat of arms displayed) and its entity anthem. The Constitutional Law on the Coat of Arms and Anthem of Republika Srpska was ruled not in concordance with the Constitution of Bosnia and Herzegovina as it states that those symbols 'represent the statehood of Republika Srpska' and are used 'under moral norms of the Serb people'. According to the Constitutional Court's decision, the Law was to be corrected by September 2006. Republika Srpska later changed its emblem.

Although the constitution names [Sarajevo](26786.md) as the capital, the northwestern city of [Banja Luka](385109.md) is the headquarters of most of the institutions of government, including the parliament, and is, therefore, the *de facto* capital. After the war, Republika Srpska retained its army, but in August 2005, the parliament consented to transfer control of [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) to a state-level ministry and abolish the entity's defence ministry and army by 1 January 2006. These reforms were required by [NATO](21133.md) as a precondition of Bosnia and Herzegovina's admission to the [Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace) programme. Bosnia and Herzegovina joined the programme in December 2006.[^cite_note-81]

In July 2023, legislation was passed criminalising insult and defamation, with penalties of up to 10 years in prison. This is likely to jeopardise freedom of speech and silence critics. Critics have said this could turn the Republika Srpska into an authoritarian regime.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

Republika Srpska also acts as a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) with high levels of centralisation as well, contrasting the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) who uses [federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism).[^cite_note-Centralised_unitary_state-13]

### Law and judicial system

The highest court of appeal is the [Supreme Court of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Republika_Srpska). The regional courts are an instance between the Supreme Court and the basic courts. Commercial courts handle cases in their respective domains. [High Judicial and Prosecutorial Council of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Judicial_and_Prosecutorial_Council_of_Republika_Srpska) is responsible for the appointment of judges and prosecutors.

In February 2025, the [Court of Bosnia and Herzegovina](19957484.md) sentenced [Milorad Dodik](4218129.md) to one year in prison and banned him from serving as President of Republika Srpska for six years, for non-compliance with the decisions of the [High Representative for Bosnia and Herzegovina](2386573.md).[^cite_note-84] Dodik rejected the ruling, claiming he was a target of political persecution.[^cite_note-85] The sentence was confirmed on 6 August 2025.[^cite_note-86] On 18 August, his appeal was rejected, terminating Dodik's mandate as President, with the termination date marked as 12 June 2025.[^cite_note-87] The resignation of Prime Minister [Radovan Višković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radovan_Vi%C5%A1kovi%C4%87) followed on the same day.[^cite_note-88]

## Geography



[Orlovačko Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlova%C4%8Dko_Lake) located in [Sutjeska National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutjeska_National_Park)

Situated in [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe), Republika Srpska is located on the [Balkan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Peninsula), with its northern extents reaching into the [Pannonian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Basin). Republika Srpska lies between latitudes [42°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circles_of_latitude_between_the_40th_parallel_north_and_the_45th_parallel_north) and [46° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circles_of_latitude_between_the_45th_parallel_north_and_the_50th_parallel_north) and longitudes [16°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_meridian_east) and [20° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20%C2%B0_E). The entity is split into two main parts by the [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District); a hilly western part and a more varied eastern part, with high mountains in the south and flat, fertile farmland in the north. Republika Srpska, unlike its counterpart entity, is landlocked.

Like the rest of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Republika Srpska is split into a [Bosnian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_\(region\)) region in the north and a [Herzegovinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina) region in the far south. Within these two macroregions exist smaller geographical regions, from the forested hills of [Bosanska Krajina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosanska_Krajina) in the northwest to the fertile plains of [Semberija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semberija) in the northeast.

Republika Srpska covers 24,816.2 square kilometres (9,582 square miles), excluding the Brčko District, which is held in [condominium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_\(international_law\)) by both entities, but is *[de facto](43594.md)* sovereign within Bosnia and Herzegovina. Republika Srpska, if it were a country, would be the 146th largest in the world. Elevation varies greatly, with [Maglić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magli%C4%87_\(mountain\)), a peak in the Dinaric Alps near Montenegro, reaching 2,386 metres (7,828 ft), and parts nearer the [Adriatic](23275478.md) going down to sea level. The largest and most popular ski resort in Bosnia and Herzegovina is situated on the slopes of the mountain [Jahorina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahorina), in the eastern part of the entity.[^cite_note-89] Other major mountains in Republika Srpska include [Volujak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volujak_\(mountain\)), [Zelengora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelengora), [Lelija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelija), [Lebršnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebr%C5%A1nik), [Crvanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crvanj), [Orjen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orjen), [Klekovača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klekova%C4%8Da), [Vitorog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitorog), [Kozara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozara), [Romanija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanija), [Treskavica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treskavica) and [Trebević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebevi%C4%87).

### Boundary

Republika Srpska shares international borders with [Croatia](5573.md) to the north, [Serbia](29265.md) to the east, and [Montenegro](20760.md) to the southeast. Within Bosnia and Herzegovina, the [Inter-Entity Boundary Line (IEBL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Entity_Boundary_Line) marks Republika Srpska's administrative division with the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, and essentially follows the front lines at the end of the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) with some adjustments (most importantly in the western part of the country and around Sarajevo) as defined by the [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement). The total length of the IEBL is approximately 1,080 km. The IEBL is an administrative demarcation uncontrolled by military or police, and there is free movement across it.[^cite_note-90]

### Forests

Republika Srpska is one of the most forested areas in Europe, with over 50% of its land covered by forests. [Perućica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%C4%87ica) is one of the last [old-growth forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old-growth_forest) in Europe.[^cite_note-91]

Two densely wooded national parks—[Sutjeska National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutjeska_National_Park) and [Kozara National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozara_National_Park)—are located in the entity.

### Waters



[Trebinje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebinje) on the banks of the [Trebišnjica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebi%C5%A1njica)



[The protected area of Prašuma Janje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pra%C5%A1uma_Janj)



[Cvrcka Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cvrcka)

Most rivers belong to the [Black Sea](3386.md) drainage basin. The principal rivers are the [Sava](587845.md), a tributary of the [Danube](21209639.md) that forms the northern boundary with [Croatia](5573.md); the [Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosna_\(river\)), [Vrbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrbas_\(river\)), [Sana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sana_\(river\)) and [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)), which all flow north and empty into the Sava; the [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina), which flows north and forms a significant part of the eastern boundary with [Serbia](29265.md), and is also a tributary of the Sava. The [Trebišnjica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebi%C5%A1njica) is one of the longest [sinking rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_river) in the world. It belongs to the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md) drainage basin. [Skakavac Waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skakavac_Waterfall_\(Peru%C4%87ica\)) on the [Perućica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%C4%87ica) is one of the highest waterfalls in the country, at about 75 metres (246 feet) in height. The most important lakes are [Bileća Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bile%C4%87a_Lake), [Lake Bardača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Barda%C4%8Da) (which includes a protected wetland area) and [Balkana Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkana_Lake).[^cite_note-92]

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

As of the 2013 census, Republika Srpska has a total population of 1,228,423 and a population density of 49.9 inhabitants per square kilometre. Republika Srpska comprises 48% of the land area of [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), and is home to 34.79% of the country's total population. The overall life expectancy in Republika Srpska at birth was 77.15 years in 2019.[^cite_note-96]

The Republika Srpska Bureau of Statistics estimated a population of 1,114,819 in 2023.[^cite_note-rzs.rs.ba-97]

The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) in Republika Srpska is, as of 2023, 1.45 children per mother. In 2023, the total number of live births, according to the Institute of Statistics of Republika Srpska (RZS), was 16,174. That same year, the number of deaths was 20,361, resulting in a natural population decrease of 4,187 inhabitants.&#x20;

### Education



[University of East Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Sarajevo), in [Pale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale,_Republika_Srpska)

Literacy in Republika Srpska stands at 96.8 percent as of 2013. Free primary education is provided as a right to all people in Republika Srpska and Bosnia and Herzegovina. There are 187 primary schools in addition to 11 music schools and 4 centres of education for students with learning disabilities. Secondary education exists in three main channels: three-year [vocational schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_school), four-year technical schools, and four-year [grammar schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar_school) (*gimnazije*). Ten independent grammar schools exist, while an additional 30 are integrated into other schools. Music schools offer another option for students to continue their education following primary school.

The oldest and largest [public university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) in Republika Srpska is the [University of Banja Luka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Banja_Luka), which was established in 1975.[^cite_note-98] The second of two public universities in Republika Srpska is [University of East Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Sarajevo).[^cite_note-99] After the end of the [Yugoslav wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_wars) several private institutions of higher education were established, including [American University in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), [Slobomir University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobomir_University), and [University Sinergija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Sinergija). The [Academy of Sciences and Arts of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts_of_the_Republika_Srpska), founded in 1996, is the highest representative institution of science and art in Republika Srpska.[^cite_note-100]

The [National and University Library of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_University_Library_of_the_Republika_Srpska) is a [national library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_library), located in [Banja Luka](385109.md). The Museum of Contemporary Art (MSURS) houses a collection of Yugoslav and international art and is located in Banja Luka.[^cite_note-101]

### Healthcare

The health care system of Republika Srpska is intended to provide organized and planned health care to the population on its territory, both in public and private health care institutions. Health care activities are performed by health care workers and associates under the conditions and in the manner primarily prescribed by law and regulations, adopted on the basis, and under, the Law on Health Care of the Republic of Srpska.[^cite_note-\:10-103]

The public sector of Srpska includes 54 health centers, along with family medicine clinics.[^cite_note-104]

Stationary treatment is provided in hospitals located in Derventa and Prnjavor.[^cite_note-\:askva-105] Hospitals specialised for physical medicine and rehabilitation are "Mlječanica" in Kozarska Dubica and Institute "Dr Miroslav Zotović" in Banja Luka.[^cite_note-\:0-35] Patients with mental illnesses are treated in the Institute for Forensic Psychiatry in [Sokolac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokolac), which is the first and only institution of this type in BiH,[^cite_note-106] and the Hospital for Chronic Psychiatric Patients in [Modriča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modri%C4%8Da).[^cite_note-107]

University hospitals operate in [Foča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fo%C4%8Da),[^cite_note-108] and Banja Luka. The University Clinical Center of Republika Srpska (UCC RS) is the leading tertiary healthcare institution in the entity, comprising 21 clinics, 6 institutes, and 10 service units.[^cite_note-109]

### Religion

Republika Srpska has no official religion. [Freedom of religion](46006.md) is a right defined by the Constitution of Republika Srpska, which provides for legal equality of all people, irrespective of religious belief.[^cite_note-110]

&#x20;According to the 2013 census, around 85% of the residents of Republika Srpska identify as Christian. Members of the [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) form the largest religious group, accounting for a little less than 83% of the population, followed by followers of [Islam](6037917.md) and [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism). 0.59% of people describe themselves as atheist or [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism).

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

Republika Srpska uses the [Bosnia and Herzegovina convertible mark (KM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_convertible_mark). The currency is pegged to the [euro](9472.md) at a unit rate of 1.95583 convertible marks. Along with the rest of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Republika Srpska is classified as an upper-middle income economy by the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-113]

Republika Srpska's [corporate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax) rate is lower than that of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and is among the lowest in the region.[^cite_note-114]

In November 2020, the governments of Serbia and Republika Srpska announced the construction of three hydropower plants on the [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina), estimated at EUR 520 million.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

### Taxation and salaries



Average net wages (in [KM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_convertible_mark)) in Republika Srpska 1996–2015

Since 2001, Republika Srpska has initiated significant reforms in the sector of the tax system, which lowered the tax burden to 28.6%, one of the lowest in the region. The 10% rate of [capital gains tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_gains_tax) and [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) are among the lowest in Europe. [VAT](52177473.md) has been introduced in 2006. These tax advantages have led to some companies moving their business to Republika Srpska from the other entity.[^cite_note-WSJ-119]

As of 2018, there are 266,309 employed people within legal entities in Republika Srpska.[^cite_note-stats2018-120] The average net wage, according to the Institute of Statistics of Republika Srpska (RZS), is 896 km (€458) per month as of February 2019, a nominal growth of 1.0% compared to the previous month.[^cite_note-121] Unemployment contributed to [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration).[^cite_note-e314-122]

### Transport



Interchange on the Gradiška-Banja Luka highway

Transport and telecommunications infrastructure in Republika Srpska is regulated by the Ministry of Transport and Communications. Traffic infrastructure includes roads, railways, railway stations, airports, waterways, and ports in the whole territory of Republika Srpska, while telecommunication infrastructure includes telephony, telegraphy, optical cables, terrestrial communication stations, traffic telematics and others.[^cite_note-Саобраћајна_инфраструктура_у_Републици_Српској-123]

State-owned companies like [Republika Srpska Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republika_Srpska_Railways), [Pošte Srpske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po%C5%A1te_Srpske), and Republika Srpska Roads are in charge of maintaining traffic on the territory.

The basic road traffic network in Srpska includes 4,192 kilometers of public roads, of which 1,781 km of trunk roads and 2,183 km of regional roads.[^cite_note-Саобраћајна_инфраструктура_у_Републици_Српској-123] There are currently two [highways](48519.md) in Republika Srpska: Gradiška – Banja Luka highway and the "9th January highway" (Banja Luka – Doboj). ### Tourism



[Jahorina ski resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahorina_ski_resort) is the biggest in Bosnia and one of the biggest in the [Balkans](4829.md)



[Andrićgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andri%C4%87grad)

Some types of tourism in the Republic of Srpska are: mountain, spa, religious, ethno-tourism and [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism).

Republika Srpska has rich but fragmented natural resources. Popular mountains include: [Zelengora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelengora), [Treskavica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treskavica), [Jahorina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahorina), [Romanija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanija), as well as [Grmeč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grme%C4%8D), [Kozara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozara), [Ozren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozren_\(Bosnia_and_Herzegovina\)) and many others, with rich flora and hunting grounds.[^cite_note-126]

[Jahorina ski resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahorina_ski_resort) is a [mountain resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_resort) and the largest and most popular winter tourism resort in the country. The ski resort is situated on the slopes of [Jahorina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahorina) mountain in [Dinaric Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinaric_Alps). It is located 15 km (9.3 mi) from the municipality of [Pale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale,_Republika_Srpska) and 30 km (19 mi) from the [Sarajevo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo_International_Airport). The Jahorina ski resort hosted alpine skiing competitions during the [1984 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Winter_Olympics).

Royal village Kontromanićevo near Doboj and [Stanišići](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stani%C5%A1i%C4%87i) are popular destinations for ethno-tourism.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] [Andrićgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andri%C4%87grad) is a tourist complex inspired by the works of Nobel Prize winner [Ivo Andrić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Andri%C4%87), located on Drina near Višegrad.[^cite_note-Андрићград_отворен_за_посјетиоце-129] It is made of stone with around fifty object, including a local theatre, cinema, art gallery, church, Andrić's institute, hotels and various shops.[^cite_note-130]

Several rivers with clear water and potential for fishing are located in Srpska, such as [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)), [Sana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sana_\(river\)), [Tara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_\(Drina\)), [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina) and [Ukrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrina).

The best known [spas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa) in Srpska are Banja Vrućica,[^cite_note-131] Dvorovi, Guber, Laktaši, Lješljani, Mlječanica and Višegrad spa.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

An important annual event of Serbs from the region of [Bosanska Krajina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosanska_Krajina) is the Corrida of [Grmeč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grme%C4%8D). So far 248 bullfighting events have been held at Grmeč.[^cite_note-Чија_је_Грмечка_корида-134][^cite_note-135]

### External relations

In September 2006, Republika Srpska officials signed a '[special ties agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_ties_agreement)' with [Serbia](29265.md) aimed at promoting economic and institutional cooperation between Serbia and Republika Srpska. The accord was signed by Serbia's President [Boris Tadić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Tadi%C4%87) and Prime Minister [Vojislav Koštunica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojislav_Ko%C5%A1tunica), former Republika Srpska President [Dragan Čavić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_%C4%8Cavi%C4%87), and Republika Srpska Prime Minister [Milorad Dodik](4218129.md).[^cite_note-136]

### Representative offices



Representative offices of Republika Srpska worldwide

In February 2009, Republika Srpska opened a representative office in [Brussels](3708.md). While [European Union](9317.md) representatives were not present at the ceremony, top Republika Srpska officials attended the event, saying it would advance their economic, political and cultural relations with the EU. This notion has been strongly condemned by Bosniak leaders, saying that this is further proof of Republika Srpska distancing itself from [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md). The president of Republika Srpska, [Rajko Kuzmanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajko_Kuzmanovi%C4%87), told reporters that this move did not jeopardise Republika Srpska's place within Bosnia and Herzegovina. He added that Republika Srpska merely used its constitutional right 'to open up a representation office in the centre of developments of European relevance'. Republika Srpska maintains official offices in [Belgrade](55904.md), [Moscow](19004.md), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), [Jerusalem](16043.md), [Thessaloniki](40471.md), [Washington D.C.](108956.md), Brussels, and [Vienna](55866.md).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

## Culture

The [Academy of Sciences and Arts of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts_of_the_Republika_Srpska) is the most important scientific, cultural, working and representative institution of Republika Srpska. The task of this academic institution is to develop, promote and encourage [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) and artistic activity. The academy is an institution of special national interest for the Republika Srpska.[^cite_note-141]



Museum of Contemporary Art, Republika Srpska

An important carrier of the development of dramatic art in [Banja Luka](385109.md) and in the whole region is the National Theater of the Republic of Srpska.[^cite_note-142] One of the most important and the most visited cultural events in the city is the Theater Fest, which is held every year in this theater, with the participation of numerous ensembles of domestic and foreign actors.

The National and University Library of Republika Srpska is the central library of the [University of Banja Luka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Banja_Luka), the main library of all public universities in Republika Srpska and the main depository for all publications in Republika Srpska.[^cite_note-143]

The Republic Institute for the Protection of Cultural, Historical and Natural Heritage is the official administrative institution of the Republika Srpska and an organization within the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education) and Culture of the Republika Srpska.[^cite_note-144] The [Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute) registers, protects and maintains a central register of cultural, historical and natural monuments throughout the territory of the Republika Srpska.

In 1993, the Association of Serbian [Writers](45353.md) was founded in Jahorina, under the chairmanship of professor and politician Nikola Koljevic. Since 2003, the president of the association has been Zoran Kostic, who moved its headquarters from Serbian Sarajevo to Banja Luka. The president of the Sarajevo-Romanija-Drina branch is Nedeljko Zelenović.

The founding assembly of the Association of [Historians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historians) of the Republic of Srpska "[Milorad Ekmečić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milorad_Ekme%C4%8Di%C4%87)" was held in Banja Luka in December 2015. The objectives of the Association are the improvement of scientific research activities in the field of historical science in the Republic of Srpska and dissemination of these results, improvement of history teaching and professional development of teaching staff.[^cite_note-145]

### Holidays



[Cathedral of Christ the Saviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Christ_the_Saviour,_Banja_Luka) in [Banja Luka](385109.md)

According to the Law on Holidays of Republika Srpska, public holidays are divided into three categories: entity holidays, religious holidays, and holidays which are marked but do not include time off work. The entity holidays include New Year's Day (1 January), Entity Day (9 January), [International Workers' Day](39353050.md) (1 May), [Victory over Fascism Day (9 May)](337670.md), and Day of the [General Framework Agreement for Peace in Bosnia and Herzegovina (21 November)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement).[^cite_note-Zakoni-148]

Religious holidays include Christmas and Easter according to both the [Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar) and the [Gregorian calendars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar) for, respectively, [Serbian Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) and Roman Catholics, as well as [Kurban Bajram](358226.md) (Eid Al Adha) and [Bajram](154944.md) (Eid Al Fitr) for Muslims. Holidays that are marked but do not include time off work include School Day (the Feast of [Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sava), 27 January), Day of the [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) (12 May), Interior Ministry Day (4 April), and Day of the [First Serbian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Serbian_Uprising) (14 February).[^cite_note-Zakoni-148]

The most important of the entity holidays is the [Day of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Republika_Srpska), which commemorates the establishment of Republika Srpska on 9 January 1992. Constitutional Court of Bosnia and Herzegovina declared the holiday unconstitutional on 26 November 2015, stating that the main issue for it being coinciding with a religious holiday. It coincides with [Saint Stephen's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Stephen's_Day) according to the Julian calendar. The Orthodox Serbs also refer to the holiday as the *[slava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slava_\(tradition\))* of Republika Srpska, regarding [Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Stephen) as the [patron saint](68055.md) of the entity, although it is designated as a secular holiday.[^cite_note-149] Republika Srpska does not recognise the Independence Day of Bosnia and Herzegovina (1 March).[^cite_note-150]
