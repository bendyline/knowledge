**Khost** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto)[^cite_note-7], [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari)[^cite_note-8]: خوست), is one of the 34 [provinces of Afghanistan](412343.md) located in the southeastern part of the country. Khost consists of thirteen districts and the city of [Khost](400068.md) serves as the capital of the province. Historically, Khost used to be a part of [Paktia](457274.md) and a larger region surrounding Khost is still referred to as [Loya Paktia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loya_Paktia).

Throughout history, the province has been the site for numerous [rebellions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost_rebellion_\(disambiguation\)), leading to the local [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) populace to consider themselves the "traditional king-makers in Kabul".[^cite_note-9] The province was previously known as the [Southern province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Province,_Afghanistan) and was united with the neighbouring [Paktia](457274.md) province. Khost is also home to numerous universities, including [Shaikh Zayed University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaikh_Zayed_University), which is the only university in Afghanistan with a faculty in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science).[^cite_note-10]

As of 2021, it was estimated that the population of the province stood at [647,730](412343.md), which makes it the 16th most populated province in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-nsia-5]

[Khost International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost_International_Airport) began providing the province with international flights after it was inaugurated by the former president [Ashraf Ghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashraf_Ghani) on July 10, 2021.[^cite_note-11]

To the east, Khost Province is bordered by the [North Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Waziristan) and [Kurram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurram_Agency) districts of [Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province](21950.md). To the north, it is bordered by the [Nangarhar](457263.md). To the south, it is bordered by [Paktika](457294.md). To the west, it is bordered by [Paktia.](457274.md)

## History

### Second Anglo-Afghan War

See also: [Second Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Afghan_War)



Military engagement near Khost.

During the [second Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Afghan_War), British forces led by [Lord Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Roberts,_1st_Earl_Roberts) entered the province. Approximately 8,000 [raiders](6873191.md) from the [Mangal tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal_\(Pashtun_tribe\)), which had a long tradition of resisting outside control, launched several attacks on weakly protected British supply [convoys](42990.md) in Khost Province. In [reprisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reprisal), Lord Roberts ordered his forces to attack eleven Mangal villages which had launched raids that murdered several [camp followers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_follower), resulting in them being sacked and burnt. As the news of the reprisals spread over Britain, his political opponents in the [British parliament](13964.md) criticized Lord Robert's actions. At the end of the conflict, British forces withdrew from the province.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

### Khost rebellion (1924–1925)

See also: [Khost rebellion (1924-1925)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost_rebellion_\(1924-1925\))



Map of the Southern Province.

&#x20;In 1924, the province of Khost, then known as the [Southern province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Province,_Afghanistan), was the site of a rebellion against [Emir Amanullah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanullah_Khan) by the [Mangal tribe.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal_\(Pashtun_tribe\)) The rebellion began in March 1924 when Mulla Abd Allah accused a local official of violating [Sharia](28840.md) by forbidding a marriage in accordance with a new family law as the father of the bride in question had pledged her to another man whilst she was an infant. As a result, Mulla Abd Allah issued a [fatwa](11259.md) against Amanullah Khan, condemning him as a [kafir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafir)(infidel) and launching [Jihad](16203.md) after a failed attempt was made by mediators to justify the new laws. The rebels were soon joined by the son of the former king [Yaqub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Yaqub_Khan), [Abd-al Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd-al_Karim), who managed to escape British surveillance and moved to Khost, where he was crowned king by the rebels. Rebel forces then conquered the city of [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez), which is adjacent to Khost. By late July, the rebels had captured Hisarak, which was 12 kilometres far from the capital, [Kabul](16826.md). However, the rebels didn't try to seize the capital and instead returned to Khost, taking the spoils of war with them.[^cite_note-14]

The [Deobandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deobandi)-trained council of 'ulama' issued a fatwa denouncing Mulla Abd Allah as a rebel and began to provide Amanullah Khan with levies after he allowed them to alter the constitution so that it would align with their interpretation of Sharia. In August, [Shah Wali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Shah_Wali_Khan) attacked the rebels in [Logar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province) whilst the [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force) were simultaneously bombing rebel positions. By early October, government forces had regained possession of the city of Gardez. Two months later, tribal leaders from Khost travelled to Kabul with the aim of initiating a peace process, only to be thrown in jail. Mulla Abd Allah, along with his three sons, were eventually captured and executed, and the rebellion was quelled in January 1925. Shah Wali Khan burned and looted more than 300 homes in Khost and brought 600 female captives back with him to Kabul, where they were distributed amongst the [Mohammadzais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammadzai) as war booty.[^cite_note-15] In the aftermath of the government's victory, Amanullah Khan decided to construct a victory pillar in Kabul to commemorate his vanquishing of the rebels. The new pillar was meant to demonstrate the "triumph of knowledge over ignorance".[^cite_note-16]

### Soviet–Afghan War

See also: [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War)



Map of the [Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen) in 1985.

In April 1978, the [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) [People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Party_of_Afghanistan) staged a [coup d'état](25914154.md) against the then president [Mohammed Daoud Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Daoud_Khan), ending the [Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Afghanistan_\(1973%E2%80%931978\)) and establishing the [Democratic Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) under the rule of [Nur Muhammad Taraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Muhammad_Taraki), who would later be overthrown and killed by [Hafizullah Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafizullah_Amin) in 1979. The coup is also known as the [Saur Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saur_Revolution). In late 1978, a rebellion occurred in the remote region of [Nuristan](457267.md), but it didn't spread to the other parts of the country due to its isolation.[^cite_note-17] The new communist government strived to eliminate illiteracy and implement agrarian reforms by sending literacy campaigners and agrarian reformers to various provinces, including Khost.[^cite_note-18] Following a rebellion by the [Zadran tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadran_\(Pashtun_tribe\)), a Pashtun tribe native to [Loya Paktia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loya_Paktia), president Hafizullah Amin decided to launch a full scale military operation in [Paktia](457274.md). The operation was a "crushing defeat" and it, alongside the ousting of former President Taraki, was one of the reasons why the Soviets decided to intervene in December 1979, thus starting the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War).[^cite_note-19]

At the end of July 1983, the forces of [Jalaluddin Haqqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalaluddin_Haqqani) laid siege to two towns in Khost and the Tani, Mangal, Zazai and Waziri tribes began taking an active part in the fighting, despite being passive up until then. All of the aforementioned events coincided with the appeal of former King [Mohammed Zahir Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Zahir_Shah) for a united front, which caused rumours about the Royalists intending to establish a [provisional government](598010.md) in a liberated Khost. However, Khost wasn't captured and by October, the Tani tribe had withdrawn from coalition due to a tribal rivalry with the Zadran. Many rebels also returned home as winter came on. By the end of December, government forces arriving from [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez) ended the siege of the two towns and recaputered [Zazi Maidan.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazi_Maidan_District)[^cite_note-20]

Khost was considered a "bastion of the [regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan)" during the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) and its loyalty to the [Democratic Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) allowed it to be granted a [de facto](43594.md) provincial status in 1986. This provided the province with a force of paid provincial staff and an annual budget that was separate from the neighbouring provinces.[^cite_note-21] Khost was upgraded from a Loy Wuluswali (within Paktia) to a full province in 1985.[^cite_note-22]

### 1993-present

Khost Province saw the [Battles of Zhawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Zhawar) and part of [Operation Infinite Reach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Infinite_Reach). Khost Province was captured by the [Taliban](30635.md) during the [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) on August 15, 2021.[^cite_note-23]

On 16 April 2022, [Pakistani airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistani_airstrikes_in_Afghanistan) targeted several villages in [Spera District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spera_District), including Afghan-Dubai, Pasa Mela, Mir Sapar, Mandata, and Kanai, and struck refugee camps belonging to [internally displaced persons](427408.md) from [Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waziristan), killing at least 41 people, mainly women and children, and wounding 22 others, according to the Taliban interim government. Pakistan claimed it struck TTP camps.[^cite_note-aj1-24][^cite_note-pahj-25][^cite_note-ds1-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

On 22 June 2022, a [magnitude 5.9 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2022_Afghanistan_earthquake) struck the province. In Spera District, approximately 500 homes were destroyed, and 40 people were killed, with 95 others injured.[^cite_note-Padshah1-29][^cite_note-Mangal-30] Many houses constructed primarily of mud and wood were razed to the ground.[^cite_note-Padshah1-29] Heavy rain and the earthquake contributed to landslides that destroyed entire [hamlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_\(place\)).[^cite_note-TNA1-31]

## Geography

Khost Province is traversed by the [Kurram River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurram_River), which rises from the Rokian Defile, passes through the district, and then enters the "country of the [Turis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turi_\(tribe\)) or the Kurram Valley".[^cite_note-32]

### Education

Further information: [Education in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Afghanistan)

The overall literacy rate (6+ years of age) fell from 28% in 2005 to 15% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-47] The overall net enrolment rate (6–13 years of age) fell from 38% in 2005 to 37% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-47]

See also: [List of universities in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Afghanistan)



Image of [Shaikh Zayed University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaikh_Zayed_University).

There are multiple universities in Khost, the following list consists of all of the universities that can be found in the province:&#x20;

- [Shaikh Zayed University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaikh_Zayed_University) - Initially opened in [Peshawar](66230.md), Pakistan in 2000, the university was moved to Khost on orders of the former president [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai). The university bears the name of the first president of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayed_bin_Sultan_Al_Nahyan) who sponsored the construction of its campus in Khost.

- [Pamir University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamir_University) - Situated in the capital of Khost province, the university is a private higher education institute which was inaugurated on April 24, 2011.

- [Hara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hara_University) - Private university that's also situated in the city of [Khost](400068.md).

### Health

Further information: [Health in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Afghanistan)

The percentage of households with clean drinking water increased from 34% in 2005 to 35% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-47] The percentage of births attended to by a skilled birth attendant increased from 18% in 2005 to 32% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-47]
