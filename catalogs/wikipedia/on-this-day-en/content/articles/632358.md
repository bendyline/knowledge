**Lake Kariba** is the world's largest [artificial lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_lake) and [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_\(water\)) by [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reservoirs_by_volume). It lies 1,300 kilometres (810 mi) upstream from the mouth of the [Zambezi river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambezi_river) on the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), bordering [Zambia](34415.md) and [Zimbabwe](34399.md). Lake Kariba was filled between 1958 and 1963 following the completion of the [Kariba Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariba_Dam) at its northeastern end, flooding the [Kariba Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariba_Gorge) on the [Zambezi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambezi_River).

The Zimbabwean town of [Kariba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariba,_Zimbabwe) was built for construction workers on the [dam](51518.md), while some other settlements such as [Binga village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binga_village) and [Mlibizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlibizi) in Zimbabwe and [Siavonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siavonga) and [Sinazongwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinazongwe) in Zambia have expanded to house people displaced by the resulting lake.

On 11 August 2026, the ferry *Mbuya Nehanda* running from Kariba to Chalala [capsized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Lake_Kariba_ferry_accident), resulting in the death of at least 97 people, including 18 children.[^cite_note-CNN-1] It is believed to be one of the deadliest boat disasters in Zimbabwe's history.[^cite_note-Graun-2]

## Physical characteristics

Lake Kariba is over 223 km (139 mi) long and up to 40 km (25 mi) in width. It covers an area of 5,580 km<sup>2</sup> (2,150 sq mi) and its storage capacity is 185 km<sup>3</sup> (44 cu mi). The mean depth of the lake is 29 m (95 ft); the maximum depth is 97 m (318 ft). It is the world's [largest man-made reservoir by volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reservoirs_by_volume), four times as large as that created by the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam).[^cite_note-3] It was made on the orders of the government of the [Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Rhodesia_and_Nyasaland).[^cite_note-4] The enormous mass of water[^cite_note-5] is believed to have caused [induced seismicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_seismicity) in the seismically active region, including over 20 [earthquakes](10106.md) of greater than 5 magnitude on the [Richter scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richter_magnitude_scale).[^cite_note-6]

The lake has several [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island), including [Maaze Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maaze_Island?action=edit\&redlink=1), Mashape Island, [Chete Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chete_Island?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sekula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekula_Island?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sampa Karuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampa_Karuma?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Fothergill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fothergill_island?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Spurwing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spurwing?action=edit\&redlink=1), Snake Island, Antelope Island, [Bed Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bed_Island?action=edit\&redlink=1), and Chikanka.

## Ecology



[Kariba Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariba_Dam)

During the filling phase of the lake, the water was high in nutrients from decomposing inundated vegetation, creating a thick layer of fertile soil on land that became the lake bed. As a result, the [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) of Lake Kariba is vibrant. A number of [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) species have been introduced to the lake, notably the [sardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardine)-like [kapenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapenta) (transported from [Lake Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika)), which now supports a thriving commercial [fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery). Other inhabitants include the [Nile crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_crocodile) and [hippopotamuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippopotamuses).

[Gamefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamefish), particularly [tigerfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocynus), which are indigenous to the Zambezi river system, now thrive on the kapenta, which in turn encourages [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism). Both Zambia and Zimbabwe are attempting to develop tourism along their respective shores.

[Fish eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_fish_eagle), [cormorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormorant) and other water birds patrol the shorelines, as do large numbers of [elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_elephant) and other big game species including lion, cheetah, leopard, buffalo and a myriad of smaller plains game species. The southern [Matusadona National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matusadona_National_Park) was once a haven for black and white rhinoceros, but recent poaching activity has dramatically reduced their numbers.

## Protected areas

The portion of Lake Kariba which falls within Zimbabwe has been designated a [recreational park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Zimbabwe#Current_Recreational_Parks) within the [Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_Parks_and_Wildlife_Estate).
