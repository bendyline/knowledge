Parliamentary elections were held in [Cyprus](5593.md) on 24 May 2026[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] to elect 56 of the 80 members of the [House of Representatives](4497460.md).

The election marked little change for the largest parties, despite strong anti-establishment feeling among the electorate and the parties' internal struggles in their respective campaigns ahead of the vote; the centre-right [Democratic Rally (DISY)](788782.md) led by [Annita Demetriou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annita_Demetriou) (who won a [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\)) of seats) and their main rivals, the [Stefanos Stefanou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Stefanou)-led communist [Progressive Party of Working People (AKEL)](356678.md), both retained the same number of seats and the centrist [Democratic Party (DIKO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Cyprus\)) lost one seat compared to the [previous election in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Cypriot_legislative_election).&#x20;

The election was marked by the relative success of the far-right [National People's Front (ELAM)](31470503.md) who became the joint-third largest party. The biggest impacts were on the smaller parties, in particular centrist parties supporting the incumbent president [Nikos Christodoulides](58652814.md). The [EDEK Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDEK_Socialist_Party), [Democratic Alignment (DIPA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alignment_\(Cyprus\)), and the [Movement of Ecologists (KOSP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Ecologists_%E2%80%93_Citizens'_Cooperation) each lost all their seats, and so are no longer represented in the Parliament of Cyprus. They were in turn replaced by new political parties which entered the legislature for the first time; [ALMA](83028755.md), who ran on an accountability and anti-corruption platform, and [Direct Democracy (ADK)](82348843.md), a party founded by a social media personality.

## Electoral system

The 80 members of the House of Representatives are elected from six multi-member constituencies which correspond to the six [districts of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Cyprus), with the number of seats allocated according to the population of each area.[^cite_note-IPU-3][^cite_note-4] Of the 80 seats, 56 are elected by [Greek Cypriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Cypriots) and 24 by [Turkish Cypriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Cypriots).[^cite_note-IPU2-5] However, since the [Cyprus crisis of 1963–64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_crisis_of_1963%E2%80%9364) and the subsequent [Turkish invasion of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Cyprus) in 1974, no ballots have been held for the Turkish Cypriot seats, which are left unfilled.[^cite_note-6] Therefore the House of Representatives has *de facto* 56 seats.[^cite_note-7]

The election used an [open list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) form of [proportional representation](23298.md). Voters were asked to choose their preferred [party list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_list) then cast preferential votes for candidates on that list – one for every four seats available in their constituency. Party leaders (or other coalition leaders) are not required to receive preferential votes to be elected. Seats are allocated using the [Hare quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare_quota), with any remaining seats allocated to parties and lists that cleared a threshold.[^cite_note-IPU-3] The seat allocation for the 2026 election was:

### Democratic Rally

The centre-right [Democratic Rally](788782.md) (DISY) is the largest political party in [Cyprus](5593.md), holding 17 of the 56 seats in the [House of Representatives](4497460.md).[^cite_note-\:2-9] Following its defeat in the [2023 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cypriot_presidential_election)[^cite_note-\:0-10] and internal divisions resulting from the [independent candidacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_candidacy) of former DISY member [Nikos Christodoulides](58652814.md),[^cite_note-11] the party's stance as the [official opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_opposition) has been perceived as ambiguous. Several of Christodoulides' ministers are active DISY members,[^cite_note-\:10-12] and on occasions, such as the 2024 National Budget, DISY made fewer amendments to the [President's](1671074.md) proposal compared to the governing parties.[^cite_note-13] This perceived lack of opposition has contributed to public dissatisfaction with DISY's role in countering the [Christodoulides administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christodoulides_government), which has faced increasing unpopularity.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

During the candidate selection process for the [2024 European Parliament Election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Cyprus), DISY's internal elections resulted in one of the then-vice presidents, [Marios Pelekanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marios_Pelekanos?action=edit\&redlink=1), finishing second to last, excluding him from the six-candidate ballot.[^cite_note-16] Dissatisfied with the outcome, Pelekanos resigned from his leadership position, citing a series of incidents that, according to him, undermined his standing within the party.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] On 22 March 2024, it was announced following multi-day discussions with [ELAM](31470503.md) (National Popular Front) that Pelekanos would be running in the European elections under ELAM's banner. This decision was condemned by DISY leader [Annita Demetriou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annita_Demetriou), who emphasised the ideological differences between the two parties. DISY subsequently removed Pelekanos from its membership registry.[^cite_note-\:3-19][^cite_note-20] Pelekanos, who had also served as the government spokesperson during the DISY-backed [Anastasiades' administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anastasiades_government) (2021–2023), ultimately became [ELAM's](31470503.md) Press Representative.[^cite_note-\:6-21] Despite DISY securing a first-place finish in the European elections, their 24.8% share of the vote marked the worst performance in the party's history.[^cite_note-22]

[DISY](788782.md) continued to face internal challenges after the [European elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Cyprus), notably between [Averof Neofytou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Averof_Neofytou), the party's former leader and its presidential candidate in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cypriot_presidential_election), and [Nicos Anastasiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicos_Anastasiades), former party leader and [President of Cyprus](1671074.md) from 2013 to 2023. In a podcast on July 8, 2024, Anastasiades criticised Neofytou's [2023 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cypriot_presidential_election), suggesting that it had failed to resonate with DISY supporters, contributing to the party's defeat. He also questioned Neofytou's alignment with [AKEL](356678.md)-backed candidate [Andreas Mavroyiannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Mavroyiannis) during the second round of the election, to which Neofytou failed to qualify.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Anastasiades' remark that he did not want to "bother with bitter people," directed at Neofytou, prompted a response from Neofytou on social media, stating that the "bitter" ones were the thousands who had once trusted Anastasiades, along with broader criticisms of Anastasiades' handling of the [Cyprus problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_problem).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

[Neofytou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Averof_Neofytou) also clashed with current DISY leader [Annita Demetriou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annita_Demetriou) over the party's support for the [Great Sea Interconnector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Sea_Interconnector),[^cite_note-offsite.com.cy-28] a planned [HVDC interconnector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HVDC_interconnector) between the [Greek](12108.md), [Cypriot](5593.md), and [Israeli](9282173.md) [power grids](20344155.md).[^cite_note-reuters230112-29][^cite_note-EA-30] The project, primarily developed during the DISY-backed [Anastasiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicos_Anastasiades)' presidency, has strong backing from DISY. However, Neofytou expressed strong disagreement about advancing energy projects before resolving the [Cyprus problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_problem) and questioned whether the interconnection will actually reduce energy bills in [Cyprus](5593.md). This stance put him at odds with Demetriou, who strongly supports the project.[^cite_note-offsite.com.cy-28][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] In September 2024, when asked about Neofytou's potential candidacy for the [2028 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Cypriot_presidential_election)—following his own expressed interest—[Annita Demetriou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annita_Demetriou) responded that Neofytou had already been tested in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cypriot_presidential_election), and in light of the public's verdict, DISY should now move forward for the benefit of the country.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

Prominent [MPs](98066.md) [Averof Neofytou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Averof_Neofytou), [Efthymios Diplaros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efthimios_Diplaros), and [Harris Georgiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Georgiades) have announced that they will not seek re-election in the upcoming legislative election, in adherence to the party statute's 3-term-limit.[^cite_note-\:4-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-\:5-37] However, they intend to remain politically active.[^cite_note-\:4-35][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-\:5-37]

### Progressive Party of Working People

The [Progressive Party of Working People](356678.md) (AKEL), the second-largest party in [Cyprus](5593.md) with 15 of the 56 seats in the [House of Representatives](4497460.md),[^cite_note-\:2-9] experienced its worst-ever performance in the [2024 European Parliament Election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Cyprus), garnering only 21.5% of the vote and losing one of its two seats for the first time in its history.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Despite AKEL's strong showing in the [2023 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cypriot_presidential_election), with candidate [Andreas Mavroyiannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Mavroyiannis) surpassing polling expectations,[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-\:0-10] internal tensions have emerged since then.[^cite_note-43]

[Irene Charalambidou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Charalambidou), a prominent AKEL [member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament), has increasingly distanced herself from the party's positions, frequently voting independently in the [House of Representatives](4497460.md).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-\:1-45] The rift between Charalambidou and AKEL deepened during the controversy over the dismissal of [Odysseas Michaelides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseas_Michaelides), the former [Auditor General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditor_general) of [Cyprus](5593.md). Charalambidou is a longstanding supporter of Michaelides due to his efforts against perceived [corruption](3608404.md) during the [Anastasiades administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anastasiades_government). The [MP](98066.md) is ineligible to run for AKEL in the 2026 legislative elections due to term limits, and there are widespread rumours that she may join a new parliamentary movement led by Michaelides, who has garnered popularity for his [anti-corruption](58111406.md) stance.[^cite_note-\:1-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] An opinion poll conducted by RetailZoom from October 14 to 16, 2024, indicated that a hypothetical new party led by Odysseas Michaelides would secure second place in the next legislative election, receiving 21% of the vote.[^cite_note-48]

[AKEL](356678.md) rebranded itself as "AKEL – Social Alliance" for the [2024 European Parliament Election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Cyprus), following a merger with several smaller left-leaning parties.[^cite_note-49] The party intends to keep this structure for the next legislative election, with its parliamentary candidates ranging from the political centre to the traditional left.[^cite_note-50]

### National People's Front

The [far-right](221220.md) party [ELAM](31470503.md) is expected to strengthen its presence in the [House of Representatives](4497460.md), with opinion polls projecting a third-place finish, surpassing for the first time the centrist [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Cyprus\)).[^cite_note-51] Once considered a fringe movement, over the last few years ELAM has gained increasing support from mainstream [right-wing](56522.md) voters. Notably, former [DISY](788782.md) vice-president [Marios Pelekanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marios_Pelekanos?action=edit\&redlink=1) was appointed as ELAM's Press Representative, signaling broader appeal beyond its traditional base.[^cite_note-\:6-21]

In January 2025, [independent](2172378.md) [MP](98066.md) [Andreas Themistokleous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Themistokleous?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was previously elected with ELAM but later expelled for "inappropriate behavior",[^cite_note-52] announced the formation of a new political party named the *[Democratic National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Movement)*. The new party espouses a platform emphasizing [religious conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_conservatism), [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism), and strong opposition to progressive social policies ("[anti-woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-woke)"). It is anticipated to attract support from traditional ELAM voters, potentially impacting ELAM's projected growth.[^cite_note-53] As of September 2025, this has not happened.

### ALMA – Citizens for Cyprus

[ALMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALMA_\(Cyprus\)) (an acronym for Dignity, Accountability, Reform, and Development) is a new political movement founded in May 2025 by former Auditor General [Odysseas Michaelides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseas_Michaelides).[^cite_note-54] Positioned within the reformist [centre](39619035.md) and [opposition](3379016.md) space, ALMA was established with the aim of achieving systemic restructuring and political transparency in Cyprus. The party has announced its intention to contest the 2026 legislative elections as its first electoral test, with Michaelides aiming for a strong parliamentary presence as a step toward a potential presidential bid in [2028](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Cypriot_presidential_election). ALMA advocates for a federal solution to the [Cyprus problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_problem) in line with UN resolutions and EU principles, and has drawn speculation about future alliances, particularly with MP [Irene Charalambidou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Charalambidou), a known supporter of Michaelides who has distanced herself from her current party [AKEL](356678.md).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

### Volt Cyprus

[Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt_Cyprus) is a new political party that forms part of the pan-European [Volt Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt_Europa) movement and presents itself as strongly pro-European and progressive. This is the first time it is standing in the general election, and in February it unveiled its list of 56 candidates. The average age of the candidates is 48.8, and 41.1% of the list are women. Volt's MP [Alexandra Attalides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Attalides), originally elected as a member of [Movement of Ecologists – Citizens' Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Ecologists_%E2%80%93_Citizens'_Cooperation), is standing for re-election in the Nicosia district.[^cite_note-57] The party advocates for the immediate resumption and intensification of talks toward a bi-zonal, bi-communal federation with political equality, based on UN resolutions and the European acquis.[^cite_note-58]

The centre-right [Democratic Rally](788782.md) retained 17 seats, and the communist [Progressive Party of Working People](356678.md) received 15 seats, both the same as in the previous election. The far-right [ELAM](31470503.md) received eight seats, four more than before and making them the joint-third largest party.[^cite_note-63] The [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Cyprus\)) also won eight seats, down one from 2021. The [EDEK Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDEK_Socialist_Party), [Democratic Alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alignment_\(Cyprus\)), and the [Movement of Ecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Ecologists_%E2%80%93_Citizens'_Cooperation) lost all their seats (a total of 11 between them). ## Analysis

The election was mostly noted for the rise in support for the far-right strongly anti-Turkish [ELAM](31470503.md) party[^cite_note-Guardian_Cyprus_26-67] and the relative failure of the centrist and near-centrist parties such as [DIKO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Cyprus\)), [EDEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDEK_Socialist_Party),[^cite_note-Guardian_Cyprus_26-67] and [DIPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alignment_\(Cyprus\)).[^cite_note-68] Given that Cyprus has a presidential system, the balance of power is unlikely to change, however it was seen as test for the [next presidential election in 2028](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Cypriot_presidential_election), with the 3 centrist parties being in President [Nikos Christodoulides](58652814.md)' minority coalition.[^cite_note-69]

However the strong anti-establishment vote did not materialise and the election is considered a success for the establishment parties, given that they received similar vote shares as in the previous election contrary to expectations.[^cite_note-Guardian_Cyprus_26-67] The election was also a success for new parties, such [ALMA](83028755.md), who successfully ran on anti-corruption platform, and [Direct Democracy](82348843.md) which was founded by social media influencer and political outsider [Fidias Panayiotou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidias_Panayiotou).[^cite_note-70]

## Aftermath

[EDEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDEK_Socialist_Party) leader Nikos Anastasiou resigned from party leadership following the loss of the party's legislative seats.[^cite_note-EDEKResigns-71] Following the entry of [Direct Democracy Cyprus](82348843.md) into the House of Representatives, party leader [Fidias Panayiotou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidias_Panayiotou) promised to help make Cyprus better, stating that Cypriots desired new political formations in the legislature.[^cite_note-FidiasReaction-72]

Notably disappointed, [Volt's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt_Cyprus) co-leaders, Panos Loizou Parras and Andromachi Sophocleous, acknowledged the party's election defeat, but at the same time emphasised that they were proud of what they had achieved and would be reviewing the result within the party's governing bodies.[^cite_note-73]
