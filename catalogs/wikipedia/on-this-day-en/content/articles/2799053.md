The **Pomeroon River** (also *Río Pomerón*[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] *or Pomaron*[^cite_note-\:1-3]) is located in [Guyana](17238691.md), [South America](26769.md), situated between the [Orinoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco_River) and the [Essequibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essequibo_River) rivers. The area has long been inhabited by [Lokono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokono) people. The Pomeroon River is also one of the deepest rivers in Guyana.

Pomeroon is within the Guyana coastal plain and the area is populated with [mangrove swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_swamp) vegetation. Siriki Creek is an estuary of the Pomeroon River.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The mouth of the river has been deflected from pouring straight into the Atlantic ocean by a sandy spit that redirects the river to the northwest via the Cozier Canal.[^cite_note-5]

## History

According to the London Encyclopaedia of 1829,[^cite_note-\:1-3] this river was once regarded as the western boundary between the [Demerara and Essequibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demerara-Essequibo) Colony[^cite_note-\:1-3] and [Spanish Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guayana_Province).[^cite_note-\:1-3]

## Use

Coconuts are a major agricultural product in the Pomeroon area, grown for their water and [copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra).[^cite_note-6]

Tapakuma is a tributary of the Pomeroon River that was developed into a water conservancy which greatly improved cultivation of rice.[^cite_note-7]

## Settlement

Shell mounds associated with early human burials dated to the pre-ceramic Alaka phase have been unearthed in the area.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

In 1581, the [Dutch](21148.md) founded a [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeroon_\(colony\)) on the banks of the river. The settlement was destroyed by Lokono people in alliance with Spaniards in 1596. In 1658, lured by the Dutch advertisement to colonize the [Essequibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essequibo_\(colony\)), a Jewish colony settled within the Dutch lands on the Pomeroon. Their plantations were destroyed when the British of Barbados invaded in 1665 and most moved on to [Suriname](26828.md).[^cite_note-8]

An Anglican mission was established in 1840 by [William Henry Brett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Brett_\(missionary\)).[^cite_note-9] In Brett's travelogue, the eastern bank of the mouth of the Pomeroon was called Cape Nassau, and the river's source was in the [Imataka Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imataka_Mountains). The largest tributary was the Arapaiaco River, located 43 miles from the sea. Bouruma was another name for the river. He observed various Amerindian tribes settled along the river, including [Warao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warao_people) and [Lokono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokono), as well as former plantation slaves.[^cite_note-10]

Settlements on the Pomeroon include the town of [Charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity,_Guyana), and [Kabakaburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabakaburi) (an Amerindian mission settlement founded by Brett, located 16 miles up from Charity[^cite_note-11]).
