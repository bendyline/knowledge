**Paraná** ([/ˌpɑːrəˈnɑː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*PAR-ə-NAH*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key),[^cite_note-4] Brazilian Portuguese: [\[paɾɐˈna\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/98/Pt-br_Paran%C3%A1.ogg/Pt-br_Paran%C3%A1.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/98/Pt-br_Paran%C3%A1.ogg/Pt-br_Paran%C3%A1.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is one of the 26 [states of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil), in the [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Region,_Brazil) of the country. It is bordered in the north by [São Paulo](229422.md) state, in the east by the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), in the south by [Santa Catarina](256305.md) and the [Argentine](18951905.md) [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina) of [Misiones](233567.md), and in the west by [Mato Grosso do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mato_Grosso_do_Sul) and [Paraguay](23420.md), with the [Paraná River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River) as its western boundary.[^cite_note-5] It is subdivided into [399 municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Paran%C3%A1), and its capital is the city of [Curitiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curitiba). Other major cities are [Londrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Londrina), [Maringá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maring%C3%A1), [Ponta Grossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponta_Grossa), [Cascavel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascavel), [São José dos Pinhais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_dos_Pinhais) and [Foz do Iguaçu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foz_do_Igua%C3%A7u). The state is home to 5.4% of the Brazilian population and generates 6.2% of the Brazilian [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP).&#x20;

Crossed by the [Tropic of Capricorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Capricorn), Paraná has what is left of the [araucaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araucaria) forest, one of the most important subtropical forests in the world. At the border with Argentina is the [National Park of Iguaçu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Park_of_Igua%C3%A7u), considered by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md). 40 km (25 mi) from there, at the border with Paraguay, the largest dam in the world was built, the Hidroelétrica de Itaipu ([Itaipu Hydroelectric Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Dam)). The crime rate is considered low by Brazilian standards[^cite_note-6] and the state is one of the most developed ones in the nation,[^cite_note-7] ranking 4th in [gross domestic product](12594.md), only behind the states of Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo and Minas Gerais.[^cite_note-8]

## History

Main article: [History of Paraná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Paran%C3%A1)

Before the discovery of the region by European explorers, indigenous populations inhabited the region for thousands of years. This included the Carijó in the lands closer to the sea, from the [Tupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupi_people) group, and the [Caingangues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caingangues) in the interior, who belonged to the [Jê](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%AA_languages) group.[^cite_note-9]

Colonisation of the state by settlers started in the 16th century, but was mainly confined to the coasts. Being a region mostly abandoned by the Portuguese,[^cite_note-\:0-10] the region was explored by other European countries, who searched primarily for [brazilwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pau_brasil).[^cite_note-\:0-10] The most noteworthy presence was that of the Spanish, which brought with them [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit). Soon, [Jesuit reductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_reduction) were opened in the western and south-western parts of the state, whose territory largely belonged to the Spanish crown. In 1554, [Domingo Martínez de Irala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domingo_Mart%C3%ADnez_de_Irala) founded the town of Ontiveros, one [league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_\(unit\)) away from the [Guaíra Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gua%C3%ADra_Falls).[^cite_note-11]

In the 1940s, the northern part of the state was settled as a result of the expansion of the São Paulo coffee industry. The south-eastern part of the state was settled as a result of migration from [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md).[^cite_note-12]

Paraná is bounded on the north by [São Paulo state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_state), on the east by the Atlantic Ocean, on the south by [Santa Catarina state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Catarina_state) and the [Misiones Province](233567.md) of Argentina, and on the west by [Mato Grosso do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mato_Grosso_do_Sul) and the republic of Paraguay, with the Paraná River as its western boundary line.

The state can be separated into five main topographic areas, from east to west: a coastal zone, the mountains of [Serra do Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_do_Mar), and then three plateaus, each lower than the other, until the [Paraná River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River) is reached.[^cite_note-13]

### Climate



Climate types of Paraná



[Araucaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araucaria_angustifolia) is a symbol of Paraná.

According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), the north and west of the state, and the east coast, is of the *Cfa* climate type, with the remainder being *Cfb*.[^cite_note-14] An alternative analysis using the ECMWF model indicates that the *Aw* and *Cwa* Köppen types appear in the north.[^cite_note-ciencia-15] The northern part of the state is the convergence point between the Tropical Atlantic and Equatorial Continental air masses.[^cite_note-16]

#### Temperature and precipitation

The annual mean air temperature ranges between 15 and 24°, with the highest temperatures found in the northwest and the lowest around [Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmas,_Paran%C3%A1),[^cite_note-ciencia-15] which is considered the coldest city of Paraná by the meteorological body Simepar.[^cite_note-17] In the coastal plain minimum average temperatures range between 16–18 °C (61–64 °F).[^cite_note-18]

Precipitation is less than 1,200 mm (47 in) a year in the north of the state, rising to above 1,800 mm (71 in) in the southwest and southeast of the state.[^cite_note-ciencia-15]

#### Climate subtypes

The *Cfa* climate, subtropical with good distribution of annual rainfall and hot summers, occurs in the coastal plain and western parts of the state. Average temperature is 19 °C (66 °F), with rainfall of 1,500 millimetres (59.1 in) per year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarschagen1998133–134-19]

The *Cfb* climate, warm temperate with good distribution of annual rainfall and mild summers, occurs at higher elevation. The average annual temperatures are 17 °C (63 °F) and rainfall of 1,200 millimetres (47.2 in) per year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarschagen1998133–134-19]

The [2022 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_census) survey gave the following breakdown by race for the state: 7,389,932 [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Brazilians) (64.6%), 3,440,037 [Mixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardo_Brazilians) (30.1%), 485,781 [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Brazilians) (4.2%), 100,244 [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Brazilians) (0.9%), and 28,000 [Amerindian](55510.md) (0.2%).[^cite_note-22]

Parana's white population is primarily descendant of Portuguese, Italian, Polish and Ukrainian immigrants, but many German families also settled in the state.[^cite_note-23] A variation of German known as [Paraná-Wolga-Deutsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Germans#Language) originated in the area.[^cite_note-24]

Initially settled by the [Guarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaran%C3%AD_people) and [Kaingang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaingang) [Amerindians](55510.md), until the 17th century, virtually no European presence existed in Paraná. The number of settlers grew around 1750 and the population was composed of Amerindians, Portuguese, and some Spaniards. African slaves from [Angola](701.md) and [Mozambique](19301.md) were also present, but in fewer numbers than in other Brazilian areas, because Paraná was an unexplored region that did not need much slave manpower. As part of the province of São Paulo, immigration grew in the mid-19th century, mostly composed of Italian, German, Polish, Ukrainian, and Japanese peoples. While large numbers of Poles and Ukrainians are present in Paraná, their presence in the rest of Brazil is relatively small, especially Ukrainians. In the early 20th century, two waves of migration to Paraná occurred: one coming from the north, mostly of Portuguese, but also of African and Amerindian origin, and another from southern Brazil to the southwest and west, mostly of Portuguese, Italian, and German origins.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Economy



Soy plantation in [Guarapuava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarapuava)



Sugarcane in [Cianorte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cianorte)



Corn plantation in [Medianeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medianeira)

In 2013, the Paraná had the [fifth largest GDP of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Brazilian_federative_units_by_gross_domestic_product), representing 5.90% of the Brazilian GDP in 2005, against 6.4% in 2003.

About 15% of Paraná's GDP comes from [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture). Another 40% comes from [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector) and the remaining 45% comes from the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector). As for its exports, in 2012 the main products exported were soybeans (18.73%), Meat Poultry (10.50%), sugar in Natura (8.09%), Soybean Meal (8.00%) and corn (6.36%).[^cite_note-31] The state's largest company is the government-owned [Copel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copel), which supplies electricity, natural gas and other utilities to Paraná and some surrounding areas and in 1997 was among the first Brazilian companies to be listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md) (ELP).

The main economic activities are agriculture, industry ([agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness), automotive, and paper) and plant extraction (wood and [yerba mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerba_mate)).[^cite_note-32] Despite the good social indicators and high standard of living, unemployment is still a problem and the state is one of the most difficult for foreigners trying to find jobs.[^cite_note-33]

### Agriculture

In agriculture, the state stands out in the production of [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beans), [tomato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato), [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)) and [yerba mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerba_mate), in addition to also producing [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [oat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [peach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach), [tangerine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerine) and [strawberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry).

In 2020, the South Region produced 32% of the national total of cereals, vegetables and oilseeds. There were 77.2 million tons, second place in Brazil, losing only to the Midwest. Paraná (14.9%) was the 2nd largest producer in the country.[^cite_note-agenciadenoticias.ibge.gov.br-34]

In [soy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy), Paraná is the 2nd largest producer in the country, with about 16% of national production. It produced 19.8 million tons in 2020.[^cite_note-canalrural.com.br-35][^cite_note-agenciadenoticias.ibge.gov.br-34] In 2017, it was also 2nd largest producer of [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) (corn).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-36][^cite_note-Produção_de_Milho_no_Nordeste-37][^cite_note-cidadeverde.com-38] Brazil is the biggest producer of soy and the 2nd biggest producer of corn in the world.



Carrot production in Paraná

Regarding [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), Paraná was, in 2017, the fifth largest producer of cane, third of sugar and fifth of alcohol in the country. It harvested about 46 million tons of cane this year. The state's sugar and alcohol sector has 25 plants and employs around 55,000 people. The regions of Umuarama, Paranavaí, Maringá and Jacarezinho concentrate production. Brazil is the largest world producer, with 672.8 million tons harvested in 2018.[^cite_note-folhaagricola.com.br-39][^cite_note-bnb.gov.br-40]

In [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) production, Brazil produced a total of 17.6 million tons in 2018. Paraná was the 2nd largest producer in the country, with 3.2 million tons.[^cite_note-41]

Since 2006, Paraná has been leading the production of [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beans) in Brazil. Brazil is the 3rd largest producer of beans in the world, with an annual harvest of around 3 million tons, 11% of world production. In 2018, the South Region was the main bean producer with 26.4% of the total, followed by the Midwest (25.4%), Southeast Region (25.1%), Northeast (20.6%) and North (2.5%). The State of Paraná leads the ranking of the main national producers with 18.9% of the total produced.[^cite_note-agricultura.pr.gov.br-42][^cite_note-terra.com.br-43]

Paraná is the 2nd largest national producer of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), with 2.2 million tons in 2019, almost equal to Rio Grande do Sul, the biggest producer.[^cite_note-agenciadenoticias.ibge.gov.br-34][^cite_note-economia.uol.com.br-44][^cite_note-abitrigo.com.br-45] The South Region is also the largest producer of [oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oats) in Brazil. In 2019, national production was close to 800 thousand tons, being almost all carried out in the South (Paraná and Rio Grande do Sul).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

About [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)), Paraná was the 3rd largest producer in Brazil in 2018, with a total of 834 thousand tons.[^cite_note-48]

Although not a large production, Paraná is the Brazilian leader in the production of [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley). The state harvested 219.2 thousand tons in 2019, 60% of the national production. However, Brazil is far from being self-sufficient in the production of barley. The Brazilian market consumes, on average, 1.5 million tons per year. Brazil produces 335 thousand tons, close to 22%. Most, 73%, come from Argentina and Uruguay.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

In 2018, Paraná was the 4th largest produce of [tangerine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerine) in Brazil.[^cite_note-51] Paraná also has a part of the production of [peaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaches) in Brazil.[^cite_note-52] In 2019, in Brazil, there was a total production area of around 4 thousand hectares of [strawberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry). Paraná was the 4th largest producers in the country.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-53]

In [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), Paraná is the producer state located further south in the country. It was once the largest producing state in Brazil: in 1962, Paraná accounted for 58% of national production, but in 2017, it had only 2.7% of the total produced in the country. The coffee culture has been replaced by other planting crops, and the state's focus today has been to invest in special, more expensive coffee beans.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-crea-pr.org.br-55]

In 2019, Brazil produced about 900 thousand tons of [yerba mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerba_mate) annually, according to the IBGE. Paraná is the largest producer in volume and Rio Grande do Sul in plantation areas (and where the sector is more industrialized). According to 2017 data, Paraná harvested 301 thousand tons of yerba mate by extractive method, and 237 thousand tons in plantation. The productive potential of yerba mate is still little explored in Brazil, with a good part of the harvest carried out by the extractive system and with low levels of productivity. However, many new producers are adopting more professional and efficient production systems, with technical acuity of management and globalized market vision. This tends to increase Brazil's export of this product.

In 2017, the state's [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle) herd was 9.3 million head, 10th place in the country.[^cite_note-56]

In 2018, Paraná produced a total of 4.4 billion liters of [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk), making it the 2nd largest producer in the country. The city of [Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castro,_Paran%C3%A1) was the largest producer in the country in 2018, with 292 million liters of milk.[^cite_note-57]

In [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork), the 3 southern states are the largest producers in the country. Brazil had 41.1 million head in 2017. Paraná (17.2%) is the 2nd largest producer.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-58][^cite_note-valeverdefm.com.br-59]

The Brazilian [poultry](23197.md) flock, in 2018, was of the order of 1.5 billion heads. In 2017, the biggest poultry producing state in Brazil was Paraná (25.3%). In terms of [chickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chickens), in 2017 there were 242.8 million heads in the country. Among the states that were the largest producers, São Paulo led with 21.9%, followed by Paraná (10.1%).[^cite_note-60] In the production of chicken [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg), the state ranks 2nd in Brazil, with 9,6% of national production.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-58]

In [fish farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_farming), western Paraná, in municipalities close to [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Paran%C3%A1) and [Cascavel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascavel), has become the largest fish-producing region in the country, with [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia) as the main cultivated species. The west represents 69% of all production in Paraná, the largest national producer, with 112 thousand tons. Of this amount, 91% refer to tilapia breeding.[^cite_note-valeverdefm.com.br-59]

The South region was the main producer of [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey) in the country in 2017, accounting for 39.7% of the national total. Paraná was the 2nd largest producer in the country, with 14.3%.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-58]

### Manufacturing Industry



Downtown [Curitiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curitiba)



Klabin Technology complex in Telêmaco Borba



Cascavel is the fifth most populous city in the state

About [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector), Paraná had an industrial GDP of R \$92.8 billion in 2017, equivalent to 7.8% of the national industry. It employs 763,064 workers in the industry. The main industrial sectors are: Food (19.1%), Industrial Services of Public Utility, such as Electricity and Water (18.5%), Construction (17.3%), Motor Vehicles (8.1%), and Petroleum Derivatives and Biofuels (5.7%). These 5 sectors concentrate 68.7% of the state's industry.[^cite_note-61]

In Brazil, the [automotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive) sector represents close to 22% of industrial GDP. In 2019 the state came 2nd in national vehicle production, with a share of 15%. The state has [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen), [Renault](162292.md), [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi), [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo) and [DAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAF_Trucks) plants.[^cite_note-montadoras-62]



[Itaipu Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Dam) in Foz do Iguaçú



Food company BR Frango in Santo Inácio

In the [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper) and [cellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose) sector, Brazilian pulp production was 19.691 million tons in 2019. The country exported US\$7.48 billion in pulp this year, US\$3.25 billion only to China. Brazilian forest-based industry exports totaled US\$9.7 billion (US\$7.48 billion in cellulose, US\$2 billion in paper and US\$265 million in wood panels). Paper production was 10.535 million tons in 2019. The country exported 2.163 million tons. In 2016, the paper and cellulose industry in the South of the country represented 33% of the national total. This year, Paraná was the national leader in the production of roundwood (mainly [eucalyptus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus)) for the pulp and paper industry (15.9 million m<sup>3</sup>); Brazil was the second country that produced the most cellulose in the world and the eighth in the production of paper. The city that most produced these woods in Brazil was [Telêmaco Borba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel%C3%AAmaco_Borba) (PR), and the 5th largest was [Ortigueira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigueira) (PR).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-IBGE-65] In 2016, the top five states producing logs for paper and cellulose (mainly [eucalyptus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus)) were: Paraná (15.9 million m<sup>3</sup>), [São Paulo](390875.md) (14.7 million m<sup>3</sup>), Bahia (13.6 million m<sup>3</sup>), Mato Grosso do Sul (9.9 million m<sup>3</sup>) and [Minas Gerais](222651.md) (7.8 million m<sup>3</sup>). Together, they correspond to 72.7% of the national production of 85.1 million m<sup>3</sup>. The ten largest producing municipalities had 22.9% of the country's production. They were the cities of Telêmaco Borba (PR), Três Lagoas (MS), Caravelas (BA), Mucuri (BA), Ortigueira (PR), São Mateus (ES), Dom Eliseu (PR), Nova Viçosa (BA), Water Clara (MS) and Ribas do Rio Pardo (MS).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-IBGE-65]

Paraná is the largest producer of [oil shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_shale) in Brazil. In the city of [São Mateus do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Mateus_do_Sul), there is a [Petrobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras) plant specialized in the production of the material. Approximately 7,800 tons are processed daily.[^cite_note-67]

In [Food industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_industry), In 2019, Brazil was the 2nd largest exporter of processed foods in the world, with a value of U \$34.1 billion in exports. The Brazilian food and beverage industry's revenue in 2019 was R \$699.9 billion, 9.7% of the country's Gross Domestic Product. In 2015, the industrial food and beverage sector in Brazil comprised 34,800 companies (not counting bakeries), the vast majority of which were small. These companies employed more than 1,600,000 workers, making the food and beverage industry the largest employer in the manufacturing industry. There are around 570 large companies in Brazil, which concentrate a good part of the total industry revenue. Paraná created food companies of national importance such as [Frimesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frimesa?action=edit\&redlink=1), [C.Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.Vale?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Nutrimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrimental?action=edit\&redlink=1), Copacol, [Coopavel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopavel?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Matte Leão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matte_Le%C3%A3o).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

In the [electronics industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_industry), the turnover of industries in Brazil reached R \$153.0 billion in 2019, about 3% of the national GDP. The number of employees in the sector was 234.5 thousand people. Exports were US\$5.6 billion, and the country's imports were US\$32.0 billion. Brazil, despite its efforts over the decades to get rid of the dependence on technology imports, has not yet managed to reach this level. Imports are concentrated in expensive components, such as processors, microcontrollers, memories, under-mounted magnetic disks, lasers, LED and LCD. Cables for telecommunication and electricity distribution, wires, optical fibers and connectors are manufactured in the country. Brazil has two large electro-electronic production hubs, located in the Metropolitan Region of [Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campinas), in the State of São Paulo, and in [Free Economic Zone of Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Economic_Zone_of_Manaus), in the State of Amazonas. There are large internationally renowned technology companies, as well as part of the industries that participate in its supply chain. The country also has other smaller centers, and one of them is at [Curitiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curitiba), capital of Paraná.[^cite_note-dados-71][^cite_note-72] The technological center of Curitiba has companies such as [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens) and [Positivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivo_Tecnologia) Informatics. 87 companies and 16,000 employees work at Tecnoparque, an area of 127,000 m<sup>2</sup> created by state law in 2007. Tecnoparque can grow up to 400,000 m<sup>2</sup> with up to four times the number of workers it has today, reaching 68 thousand people.[^cite_note-dados-71]

In the [household appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_appliances) industry, sales of [whitegoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_appliance) (refrigerators, air conditioners, etc.) were 12.9 million units in 2017. The sector had its peak sales in 2012, with 18.9 million units. The biggest-selling brands were Brastemp, [Electrolux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolux), Consul and [Philips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips). Brastemp is originally from São Bernardo do Campo-SP. Consul is originally from Santa Catarina, having merged with Brastemp and today being part of the multinational [Whirlpool Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlpool_Corporation). Another famous Brazilian brand was Prosdócimo, founded in Curitiba, which was sold to Electrolux.[^cite_note-73]

In the [small appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_appliances) sector, Paraná has one of the famous companies: [Britânia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit%C3%A2nia), originally from Curitiba.[^cite_note-74]

## Infrastructure

### Roads



[BR-277](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-277_\(Brazil_highway\)) Highway



[BR-376](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-376_\(Brazil_highway\))

In 2021, Paraná had 120,930 km of highways, 21,173 km of which were paved, and of these, 1,475 km were [duplicated highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway).[^cite_note-75]

There is currently a project to grant the main highways in the state ([BR-369](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-369_\(Brazil_highway\)), [BR-376](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-376_\(Brazil_highway\)), [BR-277](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-277_\(Brazil_highway\)) and some others) to the private sector in a large project to double 1,782 km of highways, among other improvement works.[^cite_note-76]

The capital, Curitiba, has a ring road around the city, incomplete only because the northern part (the least populated in the region) has not yet been built.[^cite_note-77]

### International airports



Afonso Pena International Airport

The state has several airports, the main ones being:

- [Afonso Pena International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_Pena_International_Airport) is Curitiba's main airport. It is located in the nearby city of [São José dos Pinhais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_dos_Pinhais).

- [Foz do Iguaçu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foz_do_Igua%C3%A7u_International_Airport);

- [Londrina Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Londrina_Airport);

- [Cascavel Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascavel_Airport);

- [Maringá Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maring%C3%A1_Regional_Airport).

The height of air transport in the state was in 1957, when 31 cities were served by flights.[^cite_note-78]

### Ports



Port of Paranaguá

Paraná has the [Port of Paranaguá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Paranagu%C3%A1), one of the main ones in the country, which, in 2021, handled 56 million tons of cargo. There is also the Port of Antonina, which, in 2021, handled 1.5 million tons of cargo.[^cite_note-79]

Much of the production from Mato Grosso do Sul, Mato Grosso, Santa Catarina, Rio Grande do Sul, Minas Gerais, São Paulo and even [Paraguay](23420.md) is shipped to the Port of Paranaguá for export by sea. Many railroads serve Paranaguá, among the main ones are the Ferroeste, Malha Sul and the Curitiba-Paranaguá Railroad.[^cite_note-80]
