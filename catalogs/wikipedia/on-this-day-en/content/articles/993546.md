The **Japan national football team** ([Japanese](15606.md): サッカー日本代表, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): **Sakkā Nippon Daihyō*[^cite_note-11]*), also known by the nickname **Samurai Blue** ([Japanese](15606.md): サムライ・ブルー, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *_Samurai Burū_*),[^cite_note-\:4-1][^cite_note-\:5-2] represents [Japan](15573.md) in men's international [football](10568.md). It is controlled by the [Japan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association) (JFA), the governing body for [football in Japan](636792.md).

Prior to the late 1980s, Japan's national football team was largely amateur, with the sport less popular domestically than [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_in_Japan) or [sumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumo).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Since the early 1990s, following the full professionalization of the sport, Japan has emerged as one of Asia's leading teams. The national team has qualified for every [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) since [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), advancing to the knockout stage in 2002, [2010](168079.md), [2018](13327177.md), [2022](17742072.md) and [2026](36581929.md) being the most knockout appearances of any team in Asia. Since their first World Cup appearance in 1998, Japan has not received a single red card, which is a world record. Japan has also won a record four [Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Cup) titles, in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_AFC_Asian_Cup), [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AFC_Asian_Cup), [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup), and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup). In addition, the team finished as runners-up in both the [2001 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) and the [2019 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Asian_Cup). Japan is one of only three teams from the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC) to have reached the final of a senior [FIFA](11049.md) men's competition, alongside [Australia](4792249.md) and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team).

Japan's progression in a short period has served as an inspiration and example of how to develop football.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Their main rivals are [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93South_Korea_football_rivalry) and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Japan_football_rivalry); they also developed rivalries against [Iran](272865.md) and Saudi Arabia. Japan was the first team from outside the Americas to participate in the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), having been invited in the [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) editions of the tournament, although they only played in the 1999 and 2019 events.[^cite_note-goal.com-16] As of August 2026, Japan is currently at the 17th place in the FIFA world ranking, being the highest ranked Asian side since December 2022.[^cite_note-17]

## History

### 1910s–1930s: Pre-war era



[Far Eastern Championship Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Far_Eastern_Championship_Games) logo in 1917

Japan's earliest international matches were at the [1917 Far Eastern Championship Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Far_Eastern_Championship_Games) in [Tokyo](30057.md), where it was represented by a team from the [Tokyo Higher Normal School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Higher_Normal_School). Although Japan made strong showings in swimming, baseball, and track and field, its football team suffered resounding defeats to the Republic of China and the Philippines.[^cite_note-18] Nevertheless, the game was promoted in Japanese schools in the 1920s.[^cite_note-\:0-19] The Japan Football Association was formed in 1921,[^cite_note-\:1-20] and Japan joined FIFA in May 1929.[^cite_note-\:0-19] However, Japan refused to participate in the [1930 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-21]

Japan's first "true" national team (as opposed to a university team chosen to represent the country) was fielded at the [1930 Far Eastern Championship Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_Far_Eastern_Championship_Games), and drew with China for the championship title.[^cite_note-\:0-19] [Shigeyoshi Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeyoshi_Suzuki) coached the national team to its first Olympic appearance at the [1936 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) in [Berlin](3354.md).[^cite_note-\:1-20] Japan was an entrant for [1938 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), but withdrew before its scheduled qualifying match against the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-22]

After [World War II](32927.md) began, Japan did not play in international competition, except for a handful of matches against [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo) and other colonies.[^cite_note-\:0-19] Its last prewar match for purposes of [Elo ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Football_Elo_Ratings) was a friendly against the Philippines in June 1940.[^cite_note-\:2-23] While Korea was [under Japanese rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), multiple Koreans played in international competition for Japan, including [Kim Yong-sik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yong-sik) (1936–40), [Kim Sung-gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Sung-gan) (1940) and [Lee Yoo-hyung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Yoo-hyung) (1940).

### 1950s–1980s: Post-war era



Japan playing Argentine club [Racing de Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_de_C%C3%B3rdoba) at the [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_President's_Cup_Football_Tournament) [President's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Cup)

Japan's postwar debut was in the [1951 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Asian_Games) in India.[^cite_note-\:2-23] Japan re-joined FIFA in 1950 and played in [qualifiers for the 1954 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC_%E2%80%93_Group_13\)), but lost the AFC qualifying berth to [South Korea](1018627.md) after two matches.[^cite_note-\:1-20] Japan also joined the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) in 1954.[^cite_note-\:0-19]

[Dettmar Cramer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dettmar_Cramer) joined the Japan national team as coach in 1960, and helped lead the team to the round of eight at the [1964 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics) in Tokyo.[^cite_note-\:3-24] Japan's first major achievement in international football came in the [1968 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1968_Summer_Olympics) in [Mexico City](18987.md), where the team won the [bronze medal](526648.md). Although this result earned the sport increased recognition in Japan, the absence of a professional domestic league hindered its growth and Japan would not qualify for the World Cup until 30 years later.[^cite_note-25] Nonetheless, Japan were close to qualifying for the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md), but lost to South Korea in the deciding matches.

Japan made its first appearance in the AFC Asian Cup in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_AFC_Asian_Cup), where they were eliminated in the group stage following a draw with [Iran](272865.md) and losses to [South Korea](1018627.md), the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) and [Qatar](1111350.md).

The late 1980s saw concrete moves to professionalize the sport in Japan. JFA introduced a Special Licensed Player system in 1986, allowing a limited number of professional players to compete in the domestic semi-professional league. Action committees were held in 1988 and 1989 to discuss the introduction of a full professional league in Japan.[^cite_note-\:3-24]

### 1990s: Rise



The [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) match vs. [Argentina](454699.md) in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), France

In the 1990s, the Japan Football Association began the [professionalization of its national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professionalism_in_association_football). In 1991, the owners of the semi-professional [Japan Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Soccer_League) agreed to disband the league and re-form as the professional [J.League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.League), partly to raise the sport's profile and to strengthen the national team program. The following year, Japan hosted the [1992 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_AFC_Asian_Cup) and won their first title by defeating [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) 1–0 in the final.[^cite_note-26] The J.League was officially launched in 1993.[^cite_note-27]

However, in its first attempt to qualify with professional players, Japan narrowly missed a ticket to the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) after drawing with [Iraq](1039652.md) in the final match of the qualification round, remembered by fans as the "[Agony of Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agony_of_Doha)".[^cite_note-28] Japan's next tournament was a defence of their continental title at the [1996 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_AFC_Asian_Cup). The team won all their games in the group stage but were eliminated in the quarter-finals after a 2–0 loss to [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team).[^cite_note-29]

The nation's first ever FIFA World Cup appearance was in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), where Japan lost all their games. The first two fixtures went 1–0 in favour of [Argentina](454699.md) and [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), and the campaign ended with a 2–1 defeat to [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_football_team). Japan impressed, however, as all three defeats were only by a one goal margin.[^cite_note-30]

### 2000s: Two Asian Cup titles, World Cup co-hosts

In the [2000 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AFC_Asian_Cup), Japan managed to reclaim their title after defeating Saudi Arabia in the final, becoming Asian champions for the second time.[^cite_note-31]



The [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_World_Cup) match vs. [Belgium](679716.md) at [Saitama Stadium 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Stadium_2002) on 4 June

Two years later, Japan co-hosted the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) with South Korea. After a 2–2 draw with [Belgium](679716.md) in their opening match, the Japanese team advanced to the second round with a 1–0 win over [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) and a 2–0 victory against [Tunisia](1029953.md). However, they subsequently exited the tournament during the round of 16, after losing 1–0 to eventual third-place finishers [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) in extra time.[^cite_note-32]

With the [2004 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup) hosted by China, the Japanese managed to retain the title by winning their group after two victories over [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_national_football_team) and [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team), before achieving victories against [Jordan](1090065.md) and [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team). They defeated the hosts in the final 3–1.[^cite_note-33]



Japan against [Brazil](149286.md) at [Signal Iduna Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Iduna_Park) in [Dortmund](149349.md), Germany in the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)

On 8 June 2005, Japan qualified for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in Germany, its third consecutive World Cup, by beating [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team) 2–0 on neutral ground. However, Japan failed to advance to the round of 16, losing to future AFC rival [Australia](4792249.md) 3–1, drawing Croatia 0–0 and falling to [Brazil](149286.md) 4–1.[^cite_note-34]

The [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup) saw Japan fail to defend its title. Although easily winning the group over [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team), Qatar and the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), the Japanese were totally exhausted in their game against Australia, where Japan won only by a penalty shootout. Japan lost to Saudi Arabia in the semi-finals,[^cite_note-35] before failing in the third-place match against South Korea.

### 2010s



[Keisuke Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisuke_Honda) was one of Japan's most successful national team players of the 2010s, playing in three World Cups and winning the [MVP](235000.md) at the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup).

During the [2010 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), in the [fourth round of the Asian qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_AFC_fourth_round), Japan became the first team other than the host [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) to qualify after defeating [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) 1–0 away. Japan was drawn in [Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E) along with the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team).[^cite_note-36] Japan started with a 1–0 win against Cameroon,[^cite_note-37] before subsequently losing to the Netherlands 1–0.[^cite_note-38] Then, Japan resoundingly beat Denmark 3–1 to advance to the next round against [Paraguay](804025.md).[^cite_note-39] In the round of 16, Japan were eliminated from the competition following penalties after a 0–0 draw against Paraguay.[^cite_note-40]

After the 2010 FIFA World Cup, head coach [Takeshi Okada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Okada) resigned. He was replaced by former [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus) and [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) coach [Alberto Zaccheroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Zaccheroni). In his first few matches, Japan recorded victories over [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_national_football_team) (2–1) and Paraguay (1–0), as well as a 1–0 victory over Argentina.

Japan participated in the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup) in [Qatar](8238258.md). On 29 January, they beat Australia 1–0 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_Final) after extra time, their fourth Asian Cup triumph and allowing them to qualify for the [2013 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup).[^cite_note-41] The country then started their road to the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) in [Brazil](3383.md). Throughout, they suffered only two losses to Uzbekistan and [Jordan](1090065.md), and drew against Australia. After a 1–1 draw with Australia, they qualified for the 2014 World Cup, becoming the first nation aside from the hosts to qualify.[^cite_note-42]

Japan started their [2013 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) campaign with a 3–0 loss to Brazil.[^cite_note-43] They were then eliminated from the competition after losing to [Italy](362466.md) 4–3.[^cite_note-44] They lost their final match 1–2 against [Mexico](808402.md) and finished in fourth place in Group A.[^cite_note-45] One month later, in the [EAFF East Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EAFF_East_Asian_Cup), they started out with a 3–3 draw to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team). They then beat Australia 3–2 and beat South Korea 2–1 in the third and final match in the [tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_EAFF_East_Asian_Cup) to claim the title.[^cite_note-46]

Japan was placed into Group C at the 2014 FIFA World Cup alongside the [Ivory Coast](1039856.md), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_national_football_team) and [Colombia](680175.md). They fell in their first match to Ivory Coast 2–1 after initially taking the lead, allowing two goals in a two-minute span. They drew their second game to Greece 0–0. To qualify for the second round, they needed a victory against Colombia and Greece to win against Ivory Coast. Greece beat Ivory Coast 2–1, but Colombia won 4–1, eliminating Japan from the World Cup.[^cite_note-47] Alberto Zaccheroni resigned as head coach.[^cite_note-48] In July 2014, former Mexico and [Espanyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espanyol) manager [Javier Aguirre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Aguirre) took over,[^cite_note-49] and Japan lost 0–2 to [Uruguay](679782.md) in the first game he managed.

Japan won its opening match at the 2015 AFC Asian Cup in [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_D) against Asian Cup debutantes [Palestine](1302455.md) 4–0, with goals from [Yasuhito Endō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuhito_End%C5%8D), [Shinji Okazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinji_Okazaki), [Keisuke Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisuke_Honda) and [Maya Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Yoshida). Okazaki was named man of the match. They then faced Iraq and Jordan in their next group matches, which they won 1–0 and 2–0 respectively. They qualified to the knockout stage as Group D winners with nine points, seven goals scored and no goals conceded. In the quarter-finals, Japan lost to the UAE in a penalty shootout after a 1–1 draw, as Honda and [Shinji Kagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinji_Kagawa) missed their penalty kicks. Japan's elimination marked their worst performance in the tournament in 19 years.[^cite_note-50]

After the Asian Cup, Aguirre was sacked following allegations of corruption during a prior tenure.[^cite_note-51] He was replaced by [Vahid Halilhodžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahid_Halilhod%C5%BEi%C4%87) in March 2015.[^cite_note-52] Japan started on a rough note during qualification, losing to the UAE 1–2 at home.[^cite_note-53] They then picked up the pace in their other qualifier games against Iraq, Australia, and Thailand, picking up five wins and two draws. On 31 August 2017, Japan defeated Australia 2–0 at home, thus qualifying them for the 2018 World Cup in Russia, making it their sixth successive World Cup.[^cite_note-54] However, the Japan Football Association decided to sack Halilhodžić on 9 April 2018, only ten weeks before the World Cup, citing reasons of a breakdown in relationship between the coach and players, and poor recent friendly results, and appoint the Technical Director, Japanese coach [Akira Nishino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Nishino_\(footballer\)) as the new manager.[^cite_note-55]



Japan before their match against [Iran](272865.md) at the [2019 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Asian_Cup)

Japan made history in the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) by defeating [Colombia](680175.md) 2–1, their first ever victory by any AFC team against a [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) team in an official tournament,[^cite_note-56] as well as Japan's first ever victory at the FIFA World Cup finals in UEFA nations. Their second match against [Senegal](1017436.md) ended in a draw with goals from [Takashi Inui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takashi_Inui) and Keisuke Honda.[^cite_note-57] Japan were defeated in their last group game in the Group H against [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) 1–0,[^cite_note-58] leaving Japan and Senegal tied for second with an identical record; however, as Japan had received two fewer yellow cards, Japan advanced to the knockout stage on the Fair Play Points tiebreaker, the first team to do so.[^cite_note-59] The match with Poland caused [controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2018_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Group_H_fair_play_controversy), as Japan were made aware of their advantage over Senegal with ten minutes left and decided to play an extremely conservative game with no attempts to take a shot on goal, despite losing 1–0, with some fans booing the players.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] The match received comparison to the [1982 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) [Disgrace of Gijón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disgrace_of_Gij%C3%B3n), in which a similar game was played.[^cite_note-63] Japan were the only AFC team to have qualified to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-64]

In the round of 16 against [Belgium](679716.md), Japan took a surprising 2–0 lead with a goal in the 48th minute by [Genki Haraguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genki_Haraguchi) and another in the 52nd by [Takashi Inui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takashi_Inui), but yielded three goals afterwards, including the winner by [Nacer Chadli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacer_Chadli) on the counterattack in the 94th minute. This was Japan's third time having reached the [last 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Round_of_16).[^cite_note-65] Japan's defeat to eventual third-place finishers Belgium was the first time a nation had lost a [knockout match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage) at the [World Cup](11370.md) after taking a two-goal advantage since [England](9904.md) lost to [West Germany](250204.md) 3–2 in [extra-time](633242.md) in the [quarter-final of the 1970 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#West_Germany_vs_England).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] This scenario was due to the naivety of the Japanese,[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] who were very offensive and did not fall back enough in defense once the two-goal lead was acquired, leaving a lot of space to the Belgians, who also took advantage of their well-calculated tactics and superior strategies to turn the game around. However, Japan's impressive performance was praised by fans, pundits and the media.[^cite_note-71]

Japan participated in the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) and had an almost successful tournament. The team easily topped group F after defeating [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_national_football_team) 3–2,[^cite_note-72] [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) 1–0[^cite_note-73] and Uzbekistan 2–1.[^cite_note-74] Japan, which opted for a cautious defensive approach (as the offensive approach lead to a regretful scenario against Belgium during the World Cup 2018), also beat fellow powerhouse Saudi Arabia in the round of sixteen and dark horse Vietnam in the quarter-finals by the narrowest of margins like during the group stage, both 2 knockout phases' games ended with 1–0 margin; *Blue Samurai* resist intense Saudi domination.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] After defeating Iran 3–0 to reach the final,[^cite_note-77] Japan's hope to win their fifth Asian Cup in two decades shattered with the team suffering a 1–3 loss to Qatar, who won the Asian Cup for the first time.[^cite_note-78]

Japan were invited to the [2019 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), their second appearance at the tournament, and brought a young squad to the competition. They were placed in Group C with Uruguay, Chile and Ecuador. The nation lost their opening match 4–0 to Chile,[^cite_note-79] before bouncing back and drawing against Uruguay 2–2.[^cite_note-80] Japan needed a win against Ecuador to qualify for the knockouts, however they drew 1–1 and missed out due to inferior goal difference to Paraguay.[^cite_note-81] Aftermath saw Japan played a friendly game against the Paraguayans, and won 2–0 at home.

### 2020s



Japan starting line up where they went on to win against Germany 2–1

After China was removed as host of the [2022 EAFF E-1 Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_EAFF_E-1_Football_Championship), it was announced that Japan was the new host. After topping the table with two wins and one draw, Japan won the competition for the second time in their history.[^cite_note-82]

Japan qualified for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar, and were drawn into [Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E) with Germany, [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) and [Spain](609825.md). On 23 November, Japan produced an upset, beating Germany 2–1, with two goals in an eight-minute span during the second half.[^cite_note-\:45-83] After losing to Costa Rica 1–0,[^cite_note-84] going into the final matchday, every team in Group E could qualify or be eliminated, with no team assured of any placement. In the end, Japan managed to qualify for the round of 16 by defeating Spain 2–1 in their final group stage match, while also contributing to Germany's elimination from the tournament.[^cite_note-85] By topping their group, Japan went on to face Croatia[^cite_note-86] in the round of 16 where they would lose 3–1 on penalties after a 1–1 draw.[^cite_note-87] It was the third team in 52 years to have come from behind twice in one tournament, following Brazil and (West) Germany.[^cite_note-88] They beat Spain with the lowest possession (18%) of the ball for a winning side since the [1966 FIFA World Cup](61629.md).[^cite_note-OptaJoe-89][^cite_note-90] It also was the first time that an Asian team topped their FIFA World Cup group held outside their home country, and also the first Asian team to reach the knockouts twice in a row.[^cite_note-91]

With a successful [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) behind them and a run of 10 consecutive victories in all competitions since June 2023, many of them in style, Japan were considered the favourites for the [2023 Asian Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup) in Qatar, especially as the Samurai Blue had a strong squad.[^cite_note-92] Japan, however, were the biggest disappointment of the tournament, putting in a catastrophic performance. [Hajime Moriyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajime_Moriyasu)'s men began the tournament with an unconvincing victory over a [Vietnamese side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) deprived of a number of key players (4–2, having broken the deadlock in the 85th minute and trailed 1–2 shortly after the half-hour mark),[^cite_note-93] before going on to play a nightmarish game against [Iraq](1039652.md) (1–2). This defeat, Japan's first in the group phase since their first appearance at the continental showpiece in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_AFC_Asian_Cup), condemned them to finish at best 2nd in the group due to their unfavourable head-to-head record. The Land of the Rising Sun made sure of the points by overcoming Indonesia (3–1)[^cite_note-94] and then eliminated Bahrain by the same scoreline in the Round of 16.[^cite_note-95] Japan met [Iran](272865.md) in the quarter-finals for a rematch of the previous edition's semi-final, and got the game off to a perfect start with [Hidemasa Morita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidemasa_Morita)'s 28th-minute opener, before falling completely flat in the second half, succumbing to Iran's fiery attacks and lacking the ideas to trouble Team Melli on the counter-attack, who exacted revenge and turned the match on its head thanks to some blatant defensive errors by [Kō Itakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8D_Itakura) (1–2).[^cite_note-96] This elimination was a real fiasco for a Japanese team that had suffered 2 defeats in the same competition, including one in the 1st round, something that hadn't happened since the [1988 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_AFC_Asian_Cup) (a draw and 3 defeats), but it was the first time since Japanese football turned professional. In particular, Japan showed worrying signs at the back, conceding at least one goal in every match, many of them from avoidable errors by inexperienced goalkeeper [Zion Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zion_Suzuki) (due to the withdrawal of the usual starting goalkeepers) or by their defensive hinge, which, unlike the previous edition, was often overwhelmed; all this while often playing on alternating current and reacting too late or intermittently showing an apathetic face (a non-match in the first half against [Iraq](1039652.md), the botched 2nd half against [Iran](272865.md)).[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] The squad also had to contend with an extra-sporting controversy, with the sudden departure of [Junya Itō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junya_It%C5%8D) shortly before the crucial match against the Persians, the player from Reims having been accused of sexual assault.[^cite_note-100]

On 20 March 2025, a 2–0 win against [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team) saw Japan become the first non-host nation to qualify for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-101] Japan was drawn into [Group F](81660196.md) with [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), [Tunisia](1029953.md), and the [UEFA playoff path B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round#Path_B) winner, [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team).[^cite_note-102]

On 31 March 2026, Japan secured a 1–0 win against [England](9904.md) at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md), with England beaten by an Asian side for the first time in history.[^cite_note-103]

Japan qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) and advanced to the round of 32 with one win and two draw in the first stage.[^cite_note-104] On 29 June 2026, Japan was eliminated in the first knockout round in a 2–1 loss against [Brazil](149286.md) at [Houston Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRG_Stadium).[^cite_note-105]

### Nicknames

Japan's national football team is nicknamed the *[Samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai) Blue* (サムライ・ブルー, *Samurai Burū*) by the [JFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association).[^cite_note-\:4-1][^cite_note-\:5-2] The team also is often known by the [last name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_name) of the manager. For example, under [Takeshi Okada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Okada), the team was known as Okada Japan (岡田ジャパン, *Okada Japan*),[^cite_note-106] or during the 2022 World Cup, the team is referred by the current manager's ([Hajime Moriyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajime_Moriyasu)) name, as "Moriyasu Japan" (森保ジャパン, *Moriyasu Japan*).[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

### Kit



Japan at the [1936 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics)

The national team [kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(association_football\)) design has gone through several alterations in the past. In the early 1980s, the kit was white with blue trim. The kits worn for the 1992 Asian Cup consisted of white stripes (stylized to form a wing) with red diamonds. During the 1996 Asian Cup and the 1998 World Cup, the national team kits were blue jerseys with red and white flame designs on the sleeves, and were designed by JFA (with the sponsor alternating each year between Asics, Puma, and Adidas). The 1996 design was reproduced in a special kit used against [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team) on 7 June 2017.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-Museum-110]

JFA has historically been identified with the colors blue and white, as opposed to the white and red of the [Flag of Japan](144954.md). Prior to 1930, the best ranked [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_team) team of the year would represent Japan, and wear their school's own uniform. JFA first formed an independent national squad for the [1930 Far Eastern Championship Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_Far_Eastern_Championship_Games), selecting the best players from across the country, and the players chose [Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo)'s color of light blue for their first uniform. They did attempt to switch to a red and white colorway in 1989, failed to qualify for the [1990 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) and the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_Asian_Qualifiers), and switched back to blue in 1992.[^cite_note-Museum-110][^cite_note-Why_Blue-111][^cite_note-112] In 2021, Japan released a special 100th anniversary kit for a friendly match against [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_football_team). The kit was also used by the U-24 team against [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_national_under-23_football_team) on 5 June 2021.[^cite_note-113]

Japan's kit is provided by German company [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas), the team's exclusive kit supplier since April 1999.[^cite_note-114] Before that, [Asics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asics) and [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_AG) had been the team's official apparel sponsor.

### Crest



[Yatagarasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yatagarasu)

The crest or emblem of the national team was adopted in late 2017 as part of a larger rebranding by the Japan Football Association.[^cite_note-117] The crest features the [Yatagarasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yatagarasu), a three-legged crow from [Japanese mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_mythology) that is a symbol for the sun, holding a solid red ball that is like the sun from [national flag](144954.md). The text "JFA" (for the Japan Football Association) is inscribed at the bottom of the crow. A red stripe is also present at the center of the shield behind the crow. The shield has a metallic gold trim and has a thicker black outline. The name of the country represented by the national team "Japan" is also inscribed within the black border.[^cite_note-soccer365-118][^cite_note-119]

The previous crest used from 1996 had a shield with a more complex shape. The ball held by the Yatagarasu had white details. The text "Japan" is absent and "JFA" is written in a different typeface.[^cite_note-soccer365-118]

The Yatagarasu was first seen on the Japan shirts in 1988, where it was on a yellow circle with a blue outline with "JAPAN FOOTBALL ASSOCIATION" written around it. In 1991, the emblem changed to a white shield with a red vertical stripe on the center with the crow on it and "JFA" written in a green [Gothic typeface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackletter). This crest was used until 1996. Before 1988, Japan used the [national flag](144954.md) outlined in red (and with JFA written in black on the lower left corner of the flag) on the shirts.[^cite_note-Museum-110]

### Rivalries

#### South Korea

Main article: [Japan–South Korea football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93South_Korea_football_rivalry)

Japan maintains a strong football rivalry with [South Korea](1018627.md). Japan have met South Korea 80 times, trailing the statistic at 17 wins, 23 draws, and 42 losses, while scoring 74 goals and conceding 124. Since November 1991, when the Japan Professional Football League was launched, the record is even with 10 wins, 12 draws and 10 losses.[^cite_note-KFA_alltimerecord-120] Both countries have made themselves unrivalled in both Asian Cup and World Cup records, being two of the most successful Asian football teams, and they hosted the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_World_Cup) in a joint bid.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

#### North Korea

Japan maintains a rivalry with [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team), although the rivalry is infrequent due to political circumstances.[^cite_note-123] Japan have met North Korea 22 times, with a record of 10 wins, 4 draws and 8 losses.[^cite_note-124]

#### China

Main article: [China–Japan football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Japan_football_rivalry)

Previously, when Japan had not established professional football, Japan had only 4 wins, 3 draws and 13 losses against China. However, Japan's establishment of professional football helped turn the tide, and the Japanese have been able to exert domination with 16 wins, 6 draws and just 2 losses. China has not beaten Japan since March 1998, and lost to them 3–1 in the [2004 AFC Asian Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup_final).[^cite_note-125]

#### Australia

Main article: [Australia–Japan football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Japan_football_rivalry)

Japan began to develop a fierce rivalry with fellow Asian powerhouse [Australia](4792249.md), shortly after the latter joined the Asian Football Confederation.[^cite_note-126] The rivalry is regarded as one of Asia's biggest football rivalries.[^cite_note-127] The rivalry is a relatively recent one, born from a number of highly competitive matches between the two teams since Australia joined the AFC in 2006.[^cite_note-128] The rivalry began at the 2006 World Cup where the two countries were grouped together, and continued with the two countries meeting regularly in various AFC competitions, such as the [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup), the [2011 AFC Asian Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_Final) and the [2013 EAFF East Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_EAFF_East_Asian_Cup).

Japan have met Australia 28 times, with a record of 11 wins, 10 draws and 7 losses.[^cite_note-129]

#### Saudi Arabia

Japan and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) are two of the most historically successful national teams in Asia and have frequently met each other in many major tournaments, including in the Asian Cup and World Cup qualification.[^cite_note-130] The frequency is also rivalled by high-level performances of Japan and Saudi Arabia in the Asian and global football stage, as they are two of the three AFC members (alongside Australia) to reach the final of any senior FIFA competition. Both countries also gained headlines for producing "unexpected" wins in the [2022 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Cup), with Japan earning wins against [Germany](250204.md) and [Spain](609825.md) and Saudi Arabia against eventual champions [Argentina](454699.md) in the group stage. These wins over World Cup-winning oppositions have allowed comparisons of developments between Japan and Saudi Arabia to emerge.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

In the head-to-head record, Japan have an advantage over the Saudis with 12 wins, 2 draws and 6 losses.[^cite_note-133]

#### Iran

Japan and Iran have a combined seven Asian Cup titles, with a rivalry developing in the 1990s due to the famous "[Joy of Johor Bahru](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B8%E3%83%A7%E3%83%9B%E3%83%BC%E3%83%AB%E3%83%90%E3%83%AB%E3%81%AE%E6%AD%93%E5%96%9C)" (ジョホール・バルの歓喜, *Johōru Baru no kanki*) that gave Japan a ticket to their first World Cup defeating the Persians by 3–2 in a neutral Asian play-off hosted in [Johor Bahru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Bahru), Malaysia.

More recently, the two countries met in the 2019 and 2023 Asian Cups. The Samurai Blue defeated Team Melli 3–0 in the semifinals of the 2019 edition. The Iranians got revenge four years later eliminating Japan 2–1 in the quarter-finals.

Japan have met Iran 19 times, with a record of 6 wins, 6 draws and 7 losses.[^cite_note-134]

### Mascot

The mascots are "Karappe" (カラッペ) and "Karara" (カララ), two [Yatagarasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-legged_crow) wearing the Japan national football team kit. The mascots were designed by Japanese manga artist [Susumu Matsushita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susumu_Matsushita). Each year when a new kit is launched, the mascots' uniforms are updated in order to match the kit being used by the team.

For the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), the [Pokémon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon) character [Pikachu](269816.md) served as the mascot.[^cite_note-138]

### Copa América

Main article: [Japan at the Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_at_the_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica)

Japan was the first team from outside the [Americas](29833.md) to participate in the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), having been invited to the [1999 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica).[^cite_note-goal.com-16] Japan was also invited in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and initially accepted the invitation. However, following the [Tōhoku earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake), the JFA later withdrew on 16 May 2011, citing the difficulty of releasing some Japanese players from European teams to play as replacements.[^cite_note-ESPNFC.com-155] On the next day, [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) invited [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) to replace Japan in the competition.

On 16 August 2013, CONMEBOL president [Eugenio Figueredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Figueredo) announced that Japan was invited to the [2015 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica).[^cite_note-156] However, Japan later declined the invitation due to scheduling problems.[^cite_note-157]

On 14 May 2018, CONMEBOL announced that Japan, alongside [Qatar](1111350.md), would be the two invited teams for the [2019 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica).[^cite_note-158]
