The **Basque Country** or **Basque Autonomous Community** ([/bæsk, bɑːsk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)),[^cite_note-6] also officially called **Euskadi** (Basque pronunciation: [\[eus̺kadi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Basque)), is an [autonomous community](55002.md) in northern [Spain](26667.md). It includes the Basque provinces of [Álava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lava) (Araba), [Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscay) (Bizkaia), and [Gipuzkoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gipuzkoa) (Guipúzcoa). It surrounds two enclaves, [Treviño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevi%C3%B1o_enclave) and [Valle de Villaverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_de_Villaverde).&#x20;

The Basque Country was granted the status of *[nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalities_and_regions_of_Spain)*, attributed by the [Spanish Constitution of 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Constitution_of_1978). The autonomous community is based on the [Statute of Autonomy of the Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy_of_the_Basque_Country), a foundational legal document providing the framework for the development of the [Basque people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_people) in the [Southern Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Basque_Country). Parallel to this, [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre), which narrowly rejected a joint statute of autonomy in 1932,[^cite_note-7] was granted a separate chartered statute in 1982.

Currently there is no official capital in the autonomous community, but the city of [Vitoria-Gasteiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitoria-Gasteiz), in the province of Álava, is the de facto capital as the location of the [Basque Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Parliament), the headquarters of the [Basque Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Government), and the residence of the President of the Basque Autonomous Community (the [Palace of Ajuria Enea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuria_Enea)). The [High Court of Justice of the Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_Justice_of_Spain) has its headquarters in the city of [Bilbao](68029.md). Whilst Vitoria-Gasteiz is the largest municipality in area, with 277 km<sup>2</sup> (107 sq mi), Bilbao is the largest in population, with a population of 347,342, located in the province of Biscay within a conurbation of 875,552 people.

The term *Basque Country* may also refer to the [larger cultural region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(greater_region\)) (Basque: *Euskal Herria*), the home of the Basque people, which includes the autonomous community.

### Plaiaundi Ecology Park

The [Plaiaundi Ecology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaiaundi) is a 24-hectare coastal wetland lying where the [Bidasoa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidasoa) meets the sea in the [Bay of Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Biscay). The nature of [Plaiaundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaiaundi) consists of a wide variety of flora (visitors view them mainly in the spring) and fauna (visitors with binoculars arrive all during the year, because of the birds migratory habits).[^cite_note-8]

Almost half of the autonomous community's 2,227,684 inhabitants[^cite_note-population-3] live in the [Bilbao metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilbao_metropolitan_area), almost the entirety of the [province of Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscay). Six of the ten most populous cities in the region form part of Bilbao's [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) (Bilbao, [Barakaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barakaldo), [Getxo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getxo), [Portugalete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugalete), [Santurtzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santurtzi) and [Basauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basauri)), which is widely known as [Greater Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Bilbao).

With 28.2% of the Basque Country population born outside this region,[^cite_note-elmundo.es-10] immigration is crucial to Basque demographics. Over the 20th century most of this immigration came from other parts of Spain, typically from [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)) or [Castile and León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n). Over recent years, sizeable numbers of this population have returned to their birthplaces and most immigration to the Basque country now comes from abroad, chiefly from South America.[^cite_note-elmundo.es-10]

As of 2024, there are 295,902 foreigners in the Basque Country, constituting 13.3% of the population.[^cite_note-population-3]

### Languages

See also: [Basque language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) and [Spanish language](26825.md)



"Spanish Kingdoms in 1030". The first written record in [Spanish](26825.md) and [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) are in the [Glosas Emilianenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glosas_Emilianenses). The map shows the [Kingdom of Pamplona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pamplona) between 1029 and 1035

Spanish and Basque are co-official in all territories of the autonomous community. The Basque-speaking areas in the modern-day autonomous community are set against the wider context of the Basque language, spoken to the east in Navarre and the French Basque Country. The whole Basque-speaking territory has experienced both decline and expansion in its history. The Basque language experienced a gradual territorial contraction throughout the last nine centuries,[^cite_note-13] and very severe deterioration of its sociolinguistic status for much of the 20th century due to heavy immigration from other parts of Spain, the virtual nonexistence of Basque-language schooling, and national policies implemented by the different Spanish régimes (see [Language policies of Francoist Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_policies_of_Francoist_Spain)). After the advent of the [Statute of Autonomy of the Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy_of_the_Basque_Country) in 1982 following Franco's death, this reductive trend was gradually reversed thanks to the Basque-language schools and the new education system. Basque has always had a strong presence in most of [Gipuzkoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gipuzkoa), central and eastern [Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscay) and the northern edge of [Álava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lava), while most Basque speakers in western Biscay and the rest of Álava are second-language speakers.

The 2006 sociolinguistic survey[^cite_note-14] of all Basque provinces showed that in 2006 of all people aged 16 and above in the Basque Autonomous Community, 30.1% were fluent Basque speakers, 18.3% passive speakers and 51.5% did not speak Basque. The percentage of Basque speakers was highest in Gipuzkoa (49.1% speakers) and lowest in Álava (14.2%). These results represent an increase on previous years (29.5% in 2001, 27.7% in 1996 and 24.1% in 1991). The highest percentage of speakers was now found in the 16–24 age range (57.5%), while only 25.0% of those 65 and older reported speaking Basque.

Ten years later, the sociolinguistic survey showed that in 2016 of all people aged 16 and above in the Basque Autonomous Community, 33.9% were fluent Basque speakers, 19.1% passive speakers and 47% did not speak Basque. The proportion of Basque speakers was again highest in Gipuzkoa (50.6%) and lowest in Álava (19.2%).

The 2021 sociolinguistic survey found that 36.2% of the population above age 16 spoke Basque, 18.6% were passive speakers and 45.3% did not speak Basque. In Gipuzkoa 51.8% spoke the language, in Biscay 30.6%, and in Álava 22.4% did.[^cite_note-15]

## History

This section is about the history of the Autonomous Community since 1978. For wider coverage, see [History of the Basques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Basques).



Monument to the [Battle of Vitoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vitoria), part of [Spanish Independence War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Independence_War) against the French rule.

The forerunner of the Gernika Statute was the short-lived Statute of Autonomy for Álava, Gipuzkoa and Biscay, which came to be enforced in October 1936 just in Biscay, with the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) already raging, and which was automatically abolished when the Spanish Nationalist troops occupied the territory.

Before the Spanish Constitution of 1978 and its system of autonomous communities, these three provinces were known in Spanish as the *Provincias Vascongadas* [since 1833](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1833_territorial_division_of_Spain).[^cite_note-16] The political structure of the new autonomous community is defined in the [Gernika Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy_of_the_Basque_Country), which was approved by a majority in a [referendum](51582.md) held on 25 October 1979. Nowadays it is one of the most decentralised regions in the world; in this regard it has been described as having "*more autonomy than just about any other in Europe*"[^cite_note-17] by *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*.



*Churruca's death at the Battle of Trafalgar*. Basque navigators were key for the navy of [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) and later the [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy).

As regards the bounds to the Spanish Constitution, [Basque nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_nationalist) cite the fact that in the [1978 Spanish Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Constitution_of_1978) referendum, which was passed with a majority of votes and a poor turnout in this area, the Basque Country had the highest [abstention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstention)[^cite_note-18] (the Basque Nationalist Party had endorsed abstention on the grounds that the Constitution was being forced upon them without any Basque input). To this, the "NO" vote in this referendum was also higher in the Basque Country than in the rest of the state. All in all, many Basques believe that they are not bound to a constitution that they never endorsed.

The Statute of Autonomy of the Basque Country is an organic law but powers have been devolved gradually during decades according to re-negotiations between the Spanish and the consecutive Basque regional governments to reach an effective implementation, while the transfer of many powers are still due and has always been a matter of heated political discussion. Basque nationalists often put down this limitation in the devolution of powers to concessions made to appease the military involved in the [23-F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23-F) coup d'état attempt (1981).

In 2003, the governing [Basque Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Nationalist_Party) (PNV) proposed to alter this statute through the [Ibarretxe Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibarretxe_Plan). The Ibarretxe bill was approved by absolute majority in the Basque Parliament after much discussion, as it was subject to lengthy legal objections—on the grounds that it contradicts the Spanish Constitution—that were ultimately overcome. Despite its mandate of the majority of the autonomous Parliament, the main two parties in Spain (PSOE, PP) imposed a blockade on a discussion of the Plan in the Spanish Parliament ([Madrid](41188263.md) [Cortes Generales](519703.md)), resulting in its rejection for debate by a large majority of that Parliament in January 2005.

Since the first autonomic cabinet, the Basque Nationalist Party has held office in the Basque Autonomous Community except for a 2009–2012 term, led by Patxi López (PSE-PSOE). The current Basque prime minister is [Iñigo Urkullu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C3%B1igo_Urkullu), also a member of the [Basque Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Nationalist_Party). Despite [ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_\(separatist_group\))'s ceasefire in 2011, this autonomous community shows the highest rate of police per 100 inhabitants in Western Europe by 2018. As agreed with the Spanish premier [Zapatero](19614411.md) in 2004, Urkullu intends to increase the figure of [ertzainas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ertzaintza), while the Spanish [PP](404727.md)'s Ministry of Interior rejects a pullback of Spanish police bodies, as demanded by the large majority of the political forces in the autonomous parliament, even pointing to an increase of the Guardia Civil in the future.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Governmental institutions



Basque parliament building in Vitoria-Gasteiz



Lehendakaritza, president's office in [Vitoria-Gasteiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitoria-Gasteiz).

The current laws configure the autonomous community as a federation of its present-day three constituent provinces. These western Basque districts kept governing themselves by their own laws and institutions even after the Castilian invasion in 1199–1200. The new king upheld their institutional system issued from the [consuetudinary law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custom_\(law\)) prevalent in Basque and Pyrenean territories. This limited self-government, similar to the one for [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre), was partially suppressed in 1839 and totally in 1876 in exchange for an agreement on tax-collection and a number of administrative prerogatives. These in turn were [suspended by Franco](https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Decreto-Ley_de_23_de_junio_de_1937) for Gipuzkoa and Biscay, but restored by the [Spanish Constitution of 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Constitution_of_1978).

The post-Franco Spanish Constitution of 1978 acknowledges historical rights and attempts a compromise in the old conflict between [centralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralism) and the different national identities (Basque, Catalan and Galician). A negotiation between [UCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_the_Democratic_Centre_\(Spain\))'s Suárez in office and PNV led to the establishment of the Basque statute, with its first article stating that the [Basque people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_people) (*Euskal Herria*) takes on an institutional personality in the form of the Basque Autonomous Community; the 2nd article goes on to establish that it may be constituted by Álava, Biscay, Gipuzkoa, as well as [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre).[^cite_note-21] Provincial councils provided with actual relevant attributions (taxation, etc.) were restored to these provinces (called therefore *diputación foral*), but Navarre [detached from the Basque political process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Basque_people#The_present). A specific approach to the national realities in Spain was eventually diffused by a legal provision allowing for the establishment of [autonomous administrations and parliaments](55002.md) to any region in Spain (e.g. [Castile and León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n), [Catalonia](6822.md), the [Valencian Community](20514168.md), etc.), while the Basques, Catalans, and Galicians were acknowledged historic specificity.&#x20;



[Ajuria Enea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuria_Enea) Palace, official residence of the [Lehendakari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehendakari) in Vitoria-Gasteiz

The provinces in the Basque Country still perform tax collection in their respective territories, but with limited margin in decision making under the Spanish and European governments. Under this intricate system, the *Diputaciones Forales* (Basque: *Foru Aldundiak*) administer most of each of the provinces but are coordinated by the autonomous [Basque Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Government) (Spanish *Gobierno Vasco*, Basque: *Eusko Jaurlaritza*). The autonomous community has its own police force (the [Ertzaintza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ertzaintza)), controls [Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_education_system) and Health Systems, and has a [Basque radio/TV station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskal_Irrati_Telebista). These and only some of the powers acknowledge in the Gernika Statute have since 1980 been transferred to the Autonomous Community by the [Cortes Generales](519703.md) under the [Gernika Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_gernika).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] The seats of the [Basque Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Parliament) and Government are in [Vitoria-Gasteiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitoria-Gasteiz), so it is the capital city *[de facto](43594.md)*, but the Basque Autonomous Community has no capital *[de jure](43594.md)*.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The Parliament is composed of 25 representatives from each of the three provinces. The Basque Parliament elects the [Lehendakari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehendakari) (President of the Autonomous Community of the Basque Country) who forms a government following regular parliamentary procedures. Until 2020, except for the 2009–2012 period, all *Lehendakaris* (even those in 1937 and during the exile) have been members of the [Basque Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Nationalist_Party) (Euzko Alderdi Jeltzalea) (moderate and [Christian-Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian-Democrat)) since 1978. Since 1982 until the late 1990s, [Basque nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasuna) ideologically closer to [ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_\(separatist_group\)) refused to turn out in the Basque parliament, a significant wedge of the parliament. Currently, the Basque Government is headed by [Iñigo Urkullu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C3%B1igo_Urkullu) ([PNV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Nationalist_Party)).

### Present-day political dynamics

See also: [Basque Country independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_independence)

ETA's permanent ceasefire (2010–2011) opened the possibility of new governmental alliances and has enabled EH Bildu's electoral success and rise to governmental institutions ([Gipuzkoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gipuzkoa), and capital city [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n), 2011–2015). In the [2012 Basque parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_parliamentary_election,_2012), the PNV obtained a plurality of the votes, followed by the left-wing nationalist coalition [EH Bildu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EH_Bildu) ([Eusko Alkartasuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusko_Alkartasuna), [Sortu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortu), [Alternatiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternatiba,_Basque_political_party)).

In 2016 the Basque regional election was held on 25 September to elect the 11th Parliament of the Basque Autonomous Community, which left a [hung parliament](652214.md), with the combination of Basque nationalist parties (PNV and [EH Bildu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EH_Bildu)) representing the largest wedge in the parliament of the Basque Autonomous Community, the main Spanish parties PP and PSOE's branches occupying a 24% of the seats, and [Podemos – Ahal Dugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_\(Spanish_political_party\)) accounting for 11 seats (14.66% of the total). However, the leading party PNV renewed its traditional alliance with the [PSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_the_Basque_Country%E2%80%93Basque_Country_Left) to form government.

During the [2017 Catalan referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_independence_referendum,_2017) crisis, the parliament showed its sympathy and support to the Catalan independence referendum and lashed out at the Spanish government's stance on the issue, denouncing any measures it may take against the vote or 'democracy' altogether.[^cite_note-26] Besides supporting the vote, Basques of this community showed a preference for further self-government (43.5%), with independence and present-day *status quo* ranking as second and third options (22.6% and 18.9%).[^cite_note-27] In 2016, the parliament of the autonomous community passed a Police Abuses Act spanning the period between 1978 and 1999; it was shortly repealed after challenged by the public prosecutor and appealed also by virtually all police and Civil Guard unions. Incoming Spanish premier [Pedro Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_S%C3%A1nchez_\(politician\)) agreed to lift the public prosecutor's block on the law in exchange for altering its content.[^cite_note-28] Right-wing parties in Spain, [Vox](41928488.md), PP, and [Ciudadanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_\(Spanish_political_party\)), sent one MP out of 18 to the Spanish Congress from this autonomous community in the 2019 November general election in contrast with their rise and important presence in the overall Spanish results.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### Territorial issues

The statute, insofar as it is addressed and provides an administrative framework for the Basque people, provides the mechanisms for neighbouring Navarre to join the three western provinces if it wishes to do so, since at least part of it is ethnically Basque. The Basque Government used the "[Laurak Bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurak_Bat)", which included the arms of Navarre, as its symbol for many years. The Navarrese Government protested, and tribunals ruled in their favour. The Basque Government replaced it with an empty red field.

Navarre is one of the historical Basque territories and even claimed by the [Basque nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_nationalism) as the core of the Basque nation. There are also two [enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave) surrounded by Basque territory—[Treviño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave_of_Trevi%C3%B1o) (Basque: *Trebiñu*) and [Valle de Villaverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_de_Villaverde) (Basque: *Villaverde-Turtzioz*)—which belong to the fellow neighbouring communities of [Castile and León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n) and [Cantabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantabria) respectively, for which a legal connection to the Basque Country has become an on-off matter of political discussion.

## Economy



[Iberdrola Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberdrola_Tower) in Bilbao



[BBVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA) head-office building in Bilbao



Garaia technology center in [Mondragón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondrag%C3%B3n), one of several [science parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_parks) located in the Basque Country

The Basque Autonomous Community [ranks first in Spain in terms of per capita product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_lists_of_Spanish_autonomous_communities#GRP_\(PPP\)), it's the most [economically productive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Productivity) region of the country with a [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) per capita (adjusted to purchasing power parity, PPP) being 22% higher than that of the [European Union](9317.md) and 30% higher than Spain's average in 2016[^cite_note-Eustat-31] at €34,400.[^cite_note-Regional_GDP_-_Eurostat-32] In 2019, the community showed a surplus in public finances, at 0.3% of the GDP.[^cite_note-Euskal-33]

Industrial activities were traditionally centred on steel and shipbuilding, mainly due to the rich [iron ore](145352.md) resources found during the 19th century around Bilbao. The Estuary of Bilbao was the centre of Euskadi's [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) during the 19th and the first half of the 20th century. These activities decayed during the [economic crisis](2878852.md) of the 1970s and 1980s, giving ground for the development of the [services sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector) and new technologies.



[Edinburgh](9602.md) tram car assembled in [CAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construcciones_y_Auxiliar_de_Ferrocarriles) [Beasain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beasain) (Gipuzkoa)

Today, the strongest industrial sectors of the Basque Country's economy are the [manufacturing sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing), present in the valleys of Biscay and Gipuzkoa; [aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics) and [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) in Vitoria-Gasteiz; and [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance) and [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy), in Bilbao.

The biggest companies in the Basque Country are: [BBVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA), one of the largest [financial institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank) in the world and Spain's second largest bank; the multinational energy company [Iberdrola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberdrola) (both of them have their headquarters in Bilbao); [Mondragón Cooperative Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondrag%C3%B3n_Cooperative_Corporation), the largest [cooperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative) in the world; [railway vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) manufacturer [CAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construcciones_y_Auxiliar_de_Ferrocarriles) and [Gamesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Gamesa), the world's second largest [wind turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) manufacturer.[^cite_note-reuters120418-34]

Eight out of ten Spanish municipalities with the lowest unemployment rates were found across this autonomous community in 2015, highlighting such towns as [Arrasate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrasate), [Portugalete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugalete) and [Barakaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barakaldo) with a strong manufacturing industrial make-up.[^cite_note-35] The Basque Autonomous Community ranked above other communities in Spain in terms of resilience in the face of the economic crisis, going on to become a beacon and a subject of study in Europe.[^cite_note-36]

In 2013 the Basque Country outperformed Spain in several parameters, but economic overall figures were highly variable. Spanish figures are subject to conspicuous seasonal fluctuation, relying on its tourist and services sectors, while Basque performance is rather based on mid- and long-term results, according with its more industrial focus. In the last quarter of 2017, unemployment in this autonomous community rose to 11.1%[^cite_note-37] (8.43% in [Gipuzkoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gipuzkoa)), second lowest in Spain after [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre), at a percentage slightly higher than the EU average (10.8%),[^cite_note-38] but still ahead of the Spanish overall unemployment rate of around 16.55%,[^cite_note-39] the second highest in the EU.[^cite_note-40]

In regards to GDP performance, 2017 was a remarkably positive year for the Basque Autonomous Community. It underwent an increase in GDP of 3.0%, close to the Spanish increase, 3.1%. In the last term of 2013, the public debt of the Basque Autonomous Community stood at 13.00% of its GDP, totalling €3,753 per capita,[^cite_note-41] as compared to Spain's overall 93.90%, totalling €20,383 per capita.[^cite_note-42]

The Basque Government's high-ranking officials, as well as Basque-based party leaders and personalities, have protested and voiced their concern over the detrimental effects of austerity measures passed by the Spanish Government as of 2011, overruling Basque taxation powers, may be having on industry and trade, especially export. Basque officials have strongly advocated for participation, along with Navarre, in the [Ecofin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecofin), with a full membership, in order to defend Basque interests in line with Basque reality and fiscal status, and not as a Spanish subsidiary.

### Transport



[AP-8 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopista_AP-8) in [Eibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eibar)

The strategic geographical location of the Basque Country as a link between the northwest and centre of Spain and the rest of Europe makes this territory heavily transited.

#### Road

The main backbones of road transport are the [AP-8 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopista_AP-8) which links Bilbao, San Sebastián and the French border and the [A-1 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_A-1) which links San Sebastián and Vitoria-Gasteiz with central Spain. Other important routes include the [AP-68 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopista_AP-68) which links Bilbao with the Mediterranean.

#### Rail

[Euskal Trenbide Sarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskal_Trenbide_Sarea) (*Basque Railway Network*) is the Basque Government-owned company that maintains and creates the railway infrastructure in the autonomous region. [Euskotren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskotren) is the Basque Government-owned [narrow gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_gauge) rail company that operates commuter services in Bilbao and San Sebastián, intercity Bilbao-San Sebastián service, and [Euskotren Tranbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskotren_Tranbia) tram services in Bilbao and Vitoria-Gasteiz.

The [Bilbao Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilbao_Metro) operates two [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) lines that serve the Greater Bilbao area while Euskotren operates a third which opened in 2017. Euskotren operates a [metro-like service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Metro) in the San Sebastián area.

The Spanish government owns two main [Renfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe) [broad gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian-gauge_railways) lines that link Vitoria-Gasteiz with San Sebastián and Bilbao with central Spain. It also operates [Cercanías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas) commuter lines in both Bilbao and San Sebastián.



The Basque Y

The [FEVE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarriles_Espa%C3%B1oles_de_V%C3%ADa_Estrecha) narrow gauge rail company operates a commuter line between Bilbao and [Balmaseda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmaseda) and links Bilbao with the rest of northern Spain.

A new [high-speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) network (called [Basque Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Y)) currently under construction will link the three capitals in 'Y' formation. Because of the rough geography of the territory, most of the network will run through tunnels, with a total estimated cost of up to €10 billion.

The estimated ecological impact of the project has encouraged the formation of a group campaigning against it called *AHTrik Ez Elkarlana*. The group uses social disobedience to oppose the project and promotes referendums against it in the towns it most affects. In spite of the vocal opposition to the project by this and other community groups (as well as [EH Bildu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EH_Bildu)), work continues, not without uncertainty. In early 2015, an estimate suggested that the average Basque intercity fare would rise to a non-competitive €25, while the Spanish central government's funding has been subject to continuous delays, spurring the irritation of the Basque government in Vitoria-Gasteiz.

#### Airports



Bilbao Airport

The three capitals have airports:

- [Bilbao Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilbao_Airport) (BIO) International

- [Vitoria Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitoria_Airport) (VIT)

- [San Sebastián Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Airport) (EAS)

Of the three, the most important hub and entry point to the Basque Country is Bilbao Airport, offering many international connections. Nearly 4,600,000 passengers passed through it in 2016.[^cite_note-43]

#### Seaports

The two most important ports are the [Port of Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Bilbao) and the [Port of Pasaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasaia). There are also minor fishing ports, such as [Bermeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermeo) and [Ondarroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondarroa).

The Port of Bilbao is by far the most important in the Basque Country and the north of Spain, being the fourth most important in Spain with over 38 million tons of traffic.

All cruising routes arrive in Bilbao and there is a [ferry](50771.md) service linking Bilbao with [Portsmouth](24207.md) (United Kingdom).

## Cuisine

Main article: [Basque cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_cuisine)



Two sample pintxos

[Basque cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_cuisine) is an important part of [Basque culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_culture). According to the chef [Ferran Adrià](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferran_Adri%C3%A0), [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n) "in terms of the average quality of the food, in terms of what you can get at any place you happen to walk into, maybe it is—probably it is, yes—the best in the world."[^cite_note-44] The most popular dishes are seafood, fish (for example [Marmitako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmitako)) and "[Pintxos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pintxo)", bar finger food.

During the 1970s, several chefs from the Basque Country, particularly [Juan Mari Arzak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mari_Arzak) and [Pedro Subijana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Subijana), led a gastronomic revolution, translating to Spain the principles of French nouvelle cuisine. The first Spanish restaurant to be awarded 3 stars in the Michelin Guide was, in fact, *Zalacaín*, a Basque restaurant, although located in Madrid. Today, the Basque Country, alongside Catalonia, is the Spanish region with a higher density of stars in the Michelin Guide, and it has become a preferred destination of many gastronomic tourists, both domestic and international. Four restaurants boast 3 stars, the highest possible award: [Juan María Arzak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mari_Arzak) (*Arzak* restaurant), [Martín Berasategui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_Berasategui) (*Berasategui* restaurant), [Pedro Subijana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Subijana) (chef of *Akelarre*) and [Eneko Atxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eneko_Atxa) (*Azurmendi* restaurant).

The coastal city of [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n) is home to the [Basque Culinary Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Culinary_Center), an academic research institution focused on higher education and research in the areas of gastronomy and nutrition.

Basque food is one of the reasons for [tourism to the Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_Basque_Country), especially the *pintxos*. A popular way to socialise is "*ir de pintxos*" or *txikiteo*, a Basque version of a [pub crawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pub_crawl), albeit generally more civilised.

## Sports



[Pelota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_pelota) ([jai alai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_alai)) court in Sara, Lapurdi.

[Basque rural sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_rural_sports), known as *Herri Kirolak* in [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language), are a number of sports competitions rooted in the traditional lifestyles of the [Basque people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_people), for example [Basque pelota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_pelota), the Basque version of the European game family that includes [real tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_tennis) and [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)). Basque players, playing for either the Spanish or the French teams, dominate international competitions.

The Basque country is also home to former national football [champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish_football_champions) [Athletic Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao) from Bilbao. It has a strict *[Cantera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantera)* policy of [employing only players born or trained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao_signing_policy) in the [Basque Country (greater region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(greater_region\)). Athletic's policy does not apply to head coaches, with famous names as [Howard Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Kendall) and [Jupp Heynckes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupp_Heynckes) coaching the team at various points. In spite of this, the club shares with worldwide heavyweights [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_C.F.) and [FC Barcelona](68187.md) the distinction of never being relegated from the top flight.[^cite_note-45]

Another major Basque Country club is [Real Sociedad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Sociedad) from San Sebastián, who contest the [Basque derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_derby) with Athletic. Real Sociedad used to practise the same policy, until they signed Irish [striker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striker_\(football\)) [John Aldridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Aldridge) in the late 1980s. Since then, Real Sociedad have had many foreign players. [Xabi Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xabi_Alonso) became the only Basque player to win both the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifa_World_Cup) and the [Champions League](44220.md) and he played in [Real Sociedad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Sociedad). The region is also home to another [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) club [Deportivo Alavés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_Alav%C3%A9s).

The most renowned Basque footballer of all time is possibly [Andoni Zubizarreta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andoni_Zubizarreta) who holds the record for appearances in [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) with 622 games and has won six league titles and the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Cup). Nowadays, the best known Basque football player is Xabi Alonso, winner of two UEFA European Championships and one World Cup, who played for Real Sociedad, Liverpool, Real Madrid and [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayern_Munich), but retired in 2017. Other notable Basque players include [Mikel Arteta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Arteta), [Asier Illarramendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asier_Illarramendi), [Andoni Iraola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andoni_Iraola), [Aritz Aduriz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aritz_Aduriz), [Mikel Oyarzabal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Oyarzabal), [Xabi Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xabi_Prieto) and [Ander Herrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ander_Herrera). Both Athletic and Real Sociedad have won the Spanish league, including dominating the competition in the early 1980s, with the last title won by a Basque club being Athletic's [1984 title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_La_Liga).

At international level, Basque players were especially prominent in [Spanish selections](609825.md) prior to the Civil War, with all of those at the [1928 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_squads#Spain), and the majority of the [1920 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_squads#Spain) and [1934 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#Spain) squads, born in the region. There is an [unofficial 'national' team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_national_football_team) which plays occasional friendlies, however its squads pick players from the wider territory including Navarre and the French Basque Country.

Cycling as a sport is popular in the Basque Country. [Abraham Olano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Olano) has won the [Vuelta a España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuelta_a_Espa%C3%B1a) and the [World Championship](7171411.md). The [UCI World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_World_Tour) [Movistar Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movistar_Team_\(men's_team\)) hails from the Basque Country.[^cite_note-46] Also previously known as Caisse d'Épargne, the Movistar team traces its history back to the **Banesto** team that included [Miguel Induráin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Indur%C3%A1in). The region is home to the [Tour of the Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_of_the_Basque_Country) stage race and the [Clásica de San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A1sica_de_San_Sebasti%C3%A1n) one day race. The [Euskaltel–Euskadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskaltel%E2%80%93Euskadi_\(1994%E2%80%932013\)) team was also part of the World Tour until its disbandment in 2014. It was an unofficial Basque national team and was partly funded by the Basque Government. Its riders were either Basque, or at least grown up in the Basque cycling culture, and former members of the team have been strong contenders in the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France) held annually in July and [Vuelta a España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuelta_a_Espa%C3%B1a) held in September. Team leaders have included riders such as [Iban Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iban_Mayo), [Haimar Zubeldia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haimar_Zubeldia), [Samuel Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_S%C3%A1nchez), [David Etxebarria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Etxebarria), [Igor Antón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Ant%C3%B3n), [Mikel Landa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Landa) and [Mikel Nieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Nieve). The team was revived in 2020 at [UCI ProTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_ProTeam) level when Euskaltel reinstated its sponsorship.
