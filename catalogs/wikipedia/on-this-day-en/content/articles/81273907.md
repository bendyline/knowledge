**Mary Elizabeth Brunkow[^cite_note-\:1-1]** (born April 13, 1961) is an American [molecular biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biologist), [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist) and Nobel Prize laureate. She is known for co-identifying the gene later named [FOXP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOXP3) as the cause of the [scurfy mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scurfy_mouse) phenotype, a finding that became foundational for modern [regulatory T cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cell) biology.&#x20;

In 2025, she was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](52502.md) with [Fred Ramsdell](60424610.md) and [Shimon Sakaguchi](48010182.md) for their work in [peripheral immune tolerance](13780711.md).&#x20;

## Early life and education

Brunkow was born on April 13, 1961 in [Portland, Oregon](23503.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-portland-3] She graduated from [St. Mary's Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Academy_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) in Portland in 1979.[^cite_note-portland-3]&#x20;

Brunkow received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) with a major in molecular and cellular biology from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1983[^cite_note-\:0-4] and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1991.[^cite_note-\:2-5] Her doctoral advisor was [Shirley M. Tilghman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_M._Tilghman).[^cite_note-\:2-5] Her doctoral dissertation was titled *Expression and function of the H19 gene in transgenic mice* (1991).[^cite_note-\:1-1]&#x20;

## Career

Brunkow worked in industry research in the Seattle area, at [Celltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celltech) R\&D in Bothell, Washington, which is where she and [Fred Ramsdell](60424610.md) performed their Nobel Prize-winning work on FOXP3,[^cite_note-6] and later she became senior program manager at the [Institute for Systems Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Systems_Biology) in Seattle.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

Brunkow is a co-author of the 2001 *[Nature Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Genetics)* paper that identified the scurfy gene product, initially termed scurfin and later known as FOXP3, linking its disruption to a fatal [lymphoproliferative disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoproliferative_disorder) in mice.[^cite_note-disruption-8][^cite_note-began-9]&#x20;

Brunkow's most cited work mapped the scurfy defect to FOXP3 and demonstrated that loss of this transcription factor drives uncontrolled T cell activation and lethal lymphoproliferation, positioning FOXP3 at the center of peripheral immune tolerance mediated by regulatory T cells.[^cite_note-disruption-8][^cite_note-10] The genetic identification of FOXP3 provided a molecular basis for understanding how the immune system restrains self-reactivity outside the [thymus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymus) and catalyzed extensive work on regulatory T cell development and function.[^cite_note-began-9][^cite_note-11]&#x20;

## Honors and awards

On October 6, 2025, the [Nobel Assembly at the Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Assembly_at_the_Karolinska_Institute) in [Stockholm, Sweden](26741.md), announced that Brunkow, [Fred Ramsdell](60424610.md), and [Shimon Sakaguchi](48010182.md) would share the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](52502.md) for discoveries concerning peripheral immune tolerance.[^cite_note-AP-12][^cite_note-REU-13] On June 24, 2026, Brunkow received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Achievement) presented by Awards Council members [Frances Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Arnold) and [Lotte Bjerre Knudsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Bjerre_Knudsen) at a ceremony in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]&#x20;
