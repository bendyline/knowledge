Since the [partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947 and subsequent creation of the [dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India) and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan), the two countries have been involved in a number of wars, conflicts, and military standoffs. A long-running [dispute over Kashmir](3030955.md) and [cross-border terrorism](317645.md) have been the predominant cause of conflict between the two states, with the exception of the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971), which occurred as a direct result of hostilities stemming from the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) in erstwhile [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) (now [Bangladesh](3454.md)).

## Background

Further information: [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) and [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement)



Four nations ([India](14533.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Ceylon (Sri Lanka)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Ceylon) and [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Burma)) that gained independence in 1947 and 1948

The [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) came in 1947 with the sudden grant of independence.[^cite_note-Khan,_2007-6] It was the intention of those who wished for a Muslim state to have a clean partition between independent and equal "Pakistan" and "Hindustan" once independence came.[^cite_note-Ambedkar,_1946-7]

Nearly one third of the Muslim population of India remained in the new India.[^cite_note-Dixit,_2002-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Inter-communal violence between Hindus, Sikhs and Muslims resulted in between 200,000 and 2 million casualties leaving 14 million people displaced.[^cite_note-Khan,_2007-6][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETalbotSingh20092-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Springer_Science_&_Business_Media-13]

[Princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) in India were provided with an [Instrument of Accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Accession) to accede to either India or Pakistan.[^cite_note-14]

## Wars

### Indo-Pakistani war of 1947–1948

Main article: [Indo-Pakistani war of 1947–1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_war_of_1947%E2%80%931948)

The war, also called the **first Indo-Pakistani war**, started in October 1947 when Pakistan feared that the [Maharaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja) of the [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) of [Kashmir and Jammu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)) would accede to India. Following partition, [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states) were left to choose whether to join India or Pakistan or to remain independent. Jammu and Kashmir, the largest of the princely states, had a majority Muslim population and significant fraction of Hindu population, all ruled by the Hindu [Maharaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja) [Hari Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Singh). Tribal Islamic forces with support from the army of Pakistan attacked and occupied parts of the princely state forcing the Maharaja to sign the [Instrument of Accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Accession_\(Jammu_and_Kashmir\)) of the princely state to the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India) to receive Indian military aid. The UN Security Council passed [Resolution 47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_47) on 22 April 1948. The fronts solidified gradually along what came to be known as the [Line of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control). A formal cease-fire was declared at 23\:59 on the night of 1 January 1949.[^cite_note-Offl_Hist_1947-15]<sup>: 379 </sup> India gained control of about two-thirds of the state ([Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley), [Jammu](43066157.md) and [Ladakh](303611.md)) whereas Pakistan gained roughly a third of Kashmir ([Azad Kashmir](2745.md), and [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md)). The Pakistan controlled areas are collectively referred to as Pakistan administered Kashmir.[^cite_note-South_Asia_in_World_Politics-16][^cite_note-The_Kingfisher_History_Encyclopedia-17][^cite_note-New_Zealand_Defence_Quarterly-18][^cite_note-Perspectives_on_Kashmir:_the_roots_of_conflict_in_South_Asia-19]

### Indo-Pakistani war of 1965

Main article: [Indo-Pakistani war of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_war_of_1965)



Pakistani Army Position, MG1A3 AA, 1965 War

This war started following Pakistan's [Operation Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gibraltar), which was designed to infiltrate forces into [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)) to precipitate an insurgency against rule by India. India retaliated by launching a full-scale military attack on [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan). The seventeen-day war caused thousands of casualties on both sides and witnessed the largest engagement of armored vehicles and the largest tank battle since World War II.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavid_R._Higgins2016-20][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERachna_Bisht2015-21] The hostilities between the two countries ended after a ceasefire was declared following diplomatic intervention by the Soviet Union and USA and the subsequent issuance of the [Tashkent Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent_Declaration).[^cite_note-Lyon2008-22] India had the upper hand over Pakistan when the ceasefire was declared.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-USLib-24][^cite_note-Hagerty-25][^cite_note-26]

### Indo-Pakistani war of 1971

Main articles: [Indo-Pakistani war of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_war_of_1971) and [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War)

This war was unique in the way that it did not involve the issue of Kashmir, but was rather precipitated by the crisis created by the political battle brewing in erstwhile [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) (now [Bangladesh](3454.md)) between [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman), Leader of [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan), and [Yahya Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan) and [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto), leaders of West Pakistan. This would culminate in the declaration of Independence of [Bangladesh](3454.md) from the state system of Pakistan. Following [Operation Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Searchlight) and the [1971 Bangladesh atrocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Bangladesh_atrocities), about 10 million Bengalis in East Pakistan took refuge in neighbouring India.[^cite_note-28] India intervened in the ongoing [Bangladesh liberation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War).[^cite_note-LATimes,_2002-29][^cite_note-The_Daily_Star,_2011-30] After a large scale [pre-emptive strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Chengiz_Khan) by Pakistan, full-scale hostilities between the two countries commenced.

Pakistan attacked at several places along India's western border with Pakistan, but the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) successfully held their positions. The Indian Army quickly responded to the Pakistan Army's movements in the west and made some initial gains, including capturing around 15,010 square kilometres (5,795 square miles)[^cite_note-Shuja_Nawaz_2008-31][^cite_note-books.google.com-32][^cite_note-https-33] of Pakistani territory (land gained by India in Pakistani Kashmir, [Pakistani Punjab](24751.md) and [Sindh](28972.md) sectors but gifted it back to Pakistan in the [Simla Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simla_Agreement) of 1972, as a gesture of goodwill). Within two weeks of intense fighting, Pakistani forces in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) [surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Surrender_\(1971\)) to the joint command of Indian and Bangladeshi forces following which the [People's Republic of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bangladesh) was created.[^cite_note-Leonard,_2006-34] The war resulted in the surrender of more than 90,000 Pakistani Army troops.[^cite_note-35] In the words of one Pakistani author, "Pakistan lost half its navy, a quarter of its air force and a third of its army".[^cite_note-36]

### Kargil War (1999)

Main article: [Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War)

This conflict between the two countries was mostly limited. During early 1999, Pakistani troops infiltrated across the [Line of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control) (LoC) and occupied Indian territory mostly in the [Kargil district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_district). India responded by launching a major military and diplomatic offensive to drive out the Pakistani infiltrators.[^cite_note-Stanley-37] Two months into the conflict, Indian troops had slowly retaken most of the ridges that were encroached by the infiltrators.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] According to official count, an estimated 75%–80% of the intruded area and nearly all high ground was back under Indian control.[^cite_note-VP_Malik-40] Fearing large-scale escalation in military conflict, the international community, led by the [United States](3434750.md), increased diplomatic pressure on Pakistan to withdraw forces from remaining Indian territory.[^cite_note-Stanley-37][^cite_note-Dettman-41] Faced with the possibility of international isolation, the already fragile [Pakistani economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Pakistan) was weakened further.[^cite_note-Samina_Ahmed-42][^cite_note-43] The morale of Pakistani forces after the withdrawal declined as many units of the [Northern Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Light_Infantry) suffered heavy casualties.[^cite_note-Summary-44][^cite_note-45] The government refused to accept the dead bodies of many officers,[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] an issue that provoked outrage and protests in the Northern Areas.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Pakistan initially did not acknowledge many of its casualties, but [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif) later said that over 4,000 Pakistani troops were killed in the operation and that Pakistan had lost the conflict.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-Kapur-51] By the end of July 1999, organized hostilities in the Kargil district had ceased.[^cite_note-Dettman-41] The war was a major military defeat for the Pakistani Army.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

## Other conflicts

Apart from the aforementioned wars, there have been other regional conflicts between the two nations from time to time, which have not bordered on all-out war.[^cite_note-Lyon2008-22]

### Siachen conflict (1984–2003)

Main article: [Siachen conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siachen_conflict)

The **Siachen conflict**, sometimes referred to as the **Siachen Glacier conflict** or the **Siachen War**, was a military conflict between India and Pakistan over the disputed 1,000-square-mile (2,600 km<sup>2</sup>)[^cite_note-Time-54] [Siachen Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siachen_Glacier) region in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir).[^cite_note-Lyon2008-22][^cite_note-Wirsing1998-55] The conflict was started in 1984 by India's successful capture of the Siachen Glacier as part of [Operation Meghdoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Meghdoot), and continued with [Operation Rajiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rajiv) in 1987. India took control of the 70-kilometre-long (43 mi) Siachen Glacier and its tributary glaciers, as well as all the main passes and heights of the [Saltoro Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltoro_Mountains) immediately west of the glacier, including [Sia La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sia_La), [Bilafond La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilafond_La), and [Gyong La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyong_La). Pakistan controls the glacial valleys immediately west of the Saltoro Ridge.[^cite_note-Wirsing-56] A cease-fire went into effect in 2003,[^cite_note-Watson-57] but both sides maintain a heavy military presence in the area.

### India-Pakistan conflict (2025)

Main article: [2025 India–Pakistan conflict](79896622.md)

The **2025** **India–Pakistan conflict** was a brief armed conflict between [India](14533.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md) that began on 7 May 2025, after India launched missile strikes on Pakistan, codenamed **Operation Sindoor**.[^cite_note-62] India stated that the operation was a response to the [Pahalgam attack](79778862.md) on 22 April by militants in the Indian administered Kashmir killing 26 civilians, mostly tourists. The attack intensified [tensions between India and Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_standoff) as India accused Pakistan of supporting [cross-border terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_state-sponsored_terrorism), which Pakistan denied.

According to India, the missile strikes of Operation Sindoor targeted the camps and infrastructure of militant groups [Jaish-e-Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Mohammed) and [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md), and no Pakistani military facilities were targeted. According to Pakistan, the Indian strikes targeted civilian areas, including mosques, killing 31 Pakistani civilians. Several Indian fighter jets were shot down during the operation, though the exact number is disputed.[^cite_note-63] Following these strikes, border skirmishes and drone strikes occurred between the two countries. On 10 May, Pakistan launched an operation codenamed **Operation Bunyan al-Marsus**,[^cite_note-64] targeting several Indian military bases.[^cite_note-65] In retaliation, India also continued Operation Sindoor, expanding its scope to target Pakistani military installations.[^cite_note-66] This conflict marked the first drone battle between the two nuclear-armed nations.

After three days of the conflict, both India and Pakistan announced that a ceasefire was agreed, effective from 5\:00 pm [IST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Standard_Time)/04\:30 pm [PKT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Standard_Time) (11\:30 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time)) on 10 May, 2025. The ceasefire led to the end of this brief conflict.

## Risk of nuclear conflict

The [nuclear conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_conflict) between both countries is of passive strategic nature with [nuclear doctrine of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_doctrine_of_Pakistan) stating a [first strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-emptive_nuclear_strike) policy, although the strike would only be initiated if and only if, the Pakistan Armed Forces are unable to halt an invasion (as for example in [1971 war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971)) or a nuclear strike is launched against Pakistan, whereas India has a declared [policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction#India's_no-first-use_policy) of [no first use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_first_use#India). According to a peer-reviewed study published in the journal *[Nature Food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Food)* in August 2022, a nuclear war between India and Pakistan could kill more than 2 billion indirectly by [starvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starvation) during a [nuclear winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_winter).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

- **[Pokhran-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-I) ([Smiling Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiling_Buddha)):** On 18 May 1974 India detonated an 8-[kiloton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiloton) nuclear device at [Pokhran Test Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran#Pokhran_Test_Range),

  [^cite_note-\:0-137]

  &#x20;becoming the first nation to become nuclear capable outside the five permanent members of [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) as well as dragging Pakistan along with it into a [nuclear arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_arms_race#India_and_Pakistan). Pakistani prime minister [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto) had promised in 1965 that "if India builds the bomb, we will eat grass or leaves, even go hungry, but we will get one of our own", and India's Pokhran-I test spurred the Pakistani nuclear weapons program to greater efforts.

  [^cite_note-138]

  &#x20;The [Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Atomic_Energy_Commission) (PAEC) Chairman [Munir Ahmed Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munir_Ahmed_Khan) said that the test would force Pakistan to test its own nuclear bomb.

  [^cite_note-139]

- **[Kirana-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirana-I):** In the 1980s a series of 24 different [cold tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_cold_test) were conducted by PAEC, led by chairman Munir Ahmad Khan under extreme secrecy.

  [^cite_note-pakistanpaedia.com-140]

  &#x20;The tunnels at [Kirana Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirana_Hills), Sargodha, are reported to have been bored after the Chagai nuclear test sites, it is widely believed that the tunnels were constructed sometime between 1979 and 1983. As in Chagai, the tunnels at Kirana Hills had been bored and then sealed and this task was also undertaken by PAEC's DTD.

  [^cite_note-pakistanpaedia.com-140]

  &#x20;Later due to excessive US intelligence and satellite focus on the Kirana Hills site, it was abandoned and nuclear weapons testing was shifted to the [Kala Chitta Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_Chitta_Range).

  [^cite_note-141]

- **[Pokhran-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-II) ([Operation Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Shakti)):** On 11 May 1998 India detonated another five nuclear devices at [Pokhran Test Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran#Pokhran_Test_Range). With jubilation and large scale approval from the Indian society came International sanctions as a reaction to this test, the most vehement reaction of all coming from Pakistan. Great ire was raised in Pakistan, which issued a stern statement claiming that India was instigating a nuclear arms race in the region. Pakistan vowed to match India's nuclear capability with statements like: "We are in a headlong arms race on the subcontinent".

  [^cite_note-142]

  [^cite_note-143]

- **[Chagai-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagai-I): ([Youm-e-Takbir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youm-e-Takbir))** Within half a month of [Pokhran-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-II), on 28 May 1998 Pakistan detonated five nuclear devices to reciprocate India in the [nuclear arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_arms_race#India_and_Pakistan). The Pakistani public, like the Indian, reacted with a celebration and a heightened sense of [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_nationalism) for responding to India in kind and becoming the only Muslim nuclear power. The day was later given the title [Youm-e-Takbir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youm-e-Takbir) to further proclaim such.

  [^cite_note-144]

  [^cite_note-nuclearweaponarchive1998-145]

  [^cite_note-146]

  [^cite_note-147]

- **[Chagai-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagai-II):** Two days later, on 30 May 1998, Pakistan detonated a sixth nuclear device completing its own series of underground tests with this being the last the two nations have carried out to date.

  [^cite_note-nuclearweaponarchive1998-145]

  [^cite_note-148]
