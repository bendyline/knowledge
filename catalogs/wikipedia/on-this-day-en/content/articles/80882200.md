The **2026 Major League Baseball season** began on March 25 with a single night game featuring the [San Francisco Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) hosting the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) and ended on September 27. The postseason began on September 29. The World Series will begin on October 23 and will end with [Game 7](15664872.md) (if necessary) on October 31. The [96th All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Major_League_Baseball_All-Star_Game) was played on July 14 at [Citizens Bank Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Bank_Park) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), Pennsylvania, the home of the [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies).[^cite_note-MLBschedule-1]

Only two teams failed to complete the entire 162-game regular season schedule, as the game between the [Baltimore Orioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Orioles) and the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) at [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium) on September 27 was canceled due to rain and never made up, owing to scheduling constraints and the game being inconsequential to the playoffs. It would be the first time since [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Major_League_Baseball_season) that some teams played more regular season games than the others.[^cite_note-2]

## Schedule

Major League Baseball released its 2026 schedule on August 26, 2025. Each team will play 162 regular-season games. As has been the case since 2025, each team was scheduled to play 13 games against their division rivals, totaling 52 games. Each team would play six games against eight opponents and seven games against two opponents in the same league for a total of 62 games. Each team would also play 48 interleague games, including a six-game home-and-home series against their designated interleague rival.&#x20;

The regular season started on March 25, with a standalone night game featuring the [San Francisco Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) hosting the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees). The slate of [Opening Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_Day) games then took place on March 26, the earliest that it has been scheduled in MLB history. This season's "Rivalry Weekend" is planned on May 15–17, again 11 series featuring prime "interleague" rivals and the matches reversed from the previous year.[^cite_note-MLBschedule-1]

The [MLB Mexico City Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Mexico_City_Series) returned when the [Arizona Diamondbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Diamondbacks) and the [San Diego Padres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Padres) played a two-game series at [Alfredo Harp Helú Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Alfredo_Harp_Hel%C3%BA) in [Mexico City](18987.md) on April 25–26.[^cite_note-3] The scheduled return of the [London Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Series) was cancelled due to scheduling conflicts at [London Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stadium) and Fox's availability due to the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-4] The World Cup also affected schedules of the [Kansas City Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Royals), [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies), [Seattle Mariners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Mariners), and [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Rangers_\(baseball\)), whose home stadiums are located near World Cup venues.[^cite_note-MLBschedule-1]

The [96th All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Major_League_Baseball_All-Star_Game) was played on July 14 at [Citizens Bank Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Bank_Park) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md), the home of the [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies). The game was part of the celebration of the [250th anniversary](57473130.md) of the [signing of the United States Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signing_of_the_United_States_Declaration_of_Independence).[^cite_note-MLBschedule-1]

The [MLB at Field of Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_at_Field_of_Dreams) game returned on August 13 after a three-season absence, with the [Minnesota Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Twins) hosting the [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies).[^cite_note-FieldOfDreams-5] The [MLB Little League Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Little_League_Classic) at [Bowman Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muncy_Bank_Ballpark_at_Historic_Bowman_Field) in [Williamsport, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsport,_Pennsylvania), was played on August 23 between the [Atlanta Braves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Braves) and the [Milwaukee Brewers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Brewers).

A [Subway Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subway_Series) [matchup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mets%E2%80%93Yankees_rivalry) between the [New York Mets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets) and the New York Yankees was played at [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium) on September 11–13 to mark the 25th anniversary of the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md).[^cite_note-MLBschedule-1][^cite_note-91125thAnniversary-6] The two teams squared off at [Citi Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citi_Field) for the 20th anniversary.[^cite_note-91125thAnniversary-6]

## Rule changes

On September 23, 2025, MLB's competition committee approved use of the [Automated Ball-Strike System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_Ball-Strike_System) (ABS) beginning with the 2026 season. Batters, pitchers, and catchers are allowed to request challenges by tapping on their helmet or cap. Each team starts with two challenges, retains a challenge if it is successful, and receives additional challenges in extra innings, if the team is out of challenges during regulation.[^cite_note-abs-approved-7]

- [Chase DeLauter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_DeLauter) ([CLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Guardians)):&#x20;

  - Became the seventh player to hit two home runs in his Major League debut on March 26, hitting home runs in the first and ninth innings.

    [^cite_note-2HRinFirstGame-37]

  - Homered in his first three career regular-season games against the [Seattle Mariners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Mariners), becoming the third player in Major League history to achieve this mark. He joins [Trevor Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Story) (2016) and [Kyle Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Lewis) in 2019. Story homered in his first four career games.

    [^cite_note-first3gamesHR-38]

* [José Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Fern%C3%A1ndez_\(infielder\)) ([ARI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Diamondbacks)):&#x20;

  - Became the eighth player to hit two home runs in his Major League debut on March 31, hitting home runs in the fourth and eighth innings.

    [^cite_note-2HRinFirstGame-37]

- [Munetaka Murakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munetaka_Murakami) ([CWS](5945.md)):&#x20;

  - Homered in his first three career regular-season games against the [Milwaukee Brewers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Brewers), becoming the fourth player in Major League history to achieve this mark. He joins Trevor Story, Kyle Lewis, and Chase DeLauter.

    [^cite_note-first3gamesHR-38]

  - Hit his fourth home run of the season on April 4 against the [Toronto Blue Jays](30847.md). The four home runs are the most by a Japanese-born player in his first eight career games.

    [^cite_note-39]

  - Tied a Major League rookie record by hitting a home run in his fifth consecutive game on April 22 against the [Arizona Diamondbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Diamondbacks). He became the 13th rookie to achieve this mark.

    [^cite_note-40]

  - With his 10th home run on April 22 against the [Arizona Diamondbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Diamondbacks), Murakami's ten home runs are the most in Major League history by a Japanese-born player in his first 24 games.

    [^cite_note-Consecutive-41]

  - With his 15th home run on May 8 against the [Seattle Mariners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Mariners), Murakami has become the first player in Major League history to homer in the first game of eight straight series.

    [^cite_note-42]

* [Joey Wiemer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Wiemer) ([WAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Nationals)):&#x20;

  - Tied the Major League record for most consecutive plate appearances to reach base to start a season with ten on March 30 against the [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies). He is the second player in the live ball era (since 1920) to reach base ten consecutive times to start the season, tying [Carlos Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Delgado) of the [Toronto Blue Jays](30847.md) who accomplished this feat in 2002.

    [^cite_note-43]

- [Munetaka Murakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munetaka_Murakami) / [Colson Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colson_Montgomery) ([CWS](5945.md)):&#x20;

  - With their home runs on April 22 against the [Arizona Diamondbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Diamondbacks), Murakami (five games) and Montgomery (four games) became the first set of teammates to each homer in four consecutive games in Major League History.

    [^cite_note-Consecutive-41]

  - With home runs in the same game on May 1, it was the seventh time this season that Murakami and Montgomery homered in the same game. That's the most by any teammate duo in their team's first 35 games of a season in Major League history, and it was only game 32. The duo broke a tie at six with 1994 [Colorado Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rockies)' [Andres Galarraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andres_Galarraga) and [Ellis Burks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_Burks), 1980 [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies)' [Mike Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Schmidt) and [Greg Luzinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Luzinski), and 1956 [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees)' [Mickey Mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mantle) and [Yogi Berra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogi_Berra).

    [^cite_note-44]

* [Kyle Schwarber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Schwarber) ([PHI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies)):&#x20;

  - Recorded his 350th career home run on April 30 against the [San Francisco Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) in the first inning of game one. He became the 105th player to reach this mark. Schwarber now ranks seventh in Major League history in the fewest career games to 350 home runs, with 1,321 games played. The six that were faster are [Aaron Judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Judge), [Mark McGwire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_McGwire), [Juan González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Gonz%C3%A1lez_\(baseball\)), [Alex Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Rodriguez), [Harmon Killebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmon_Killebrew), and [Albert Pujols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Pujols).

    [^cite_note-45]

  - With his 30th home run on June 28 against the [New York Mets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets), Schwarber became the ninth player in Major League history to hit 30-plus home runs in each of their first five seasons with a franchise. He joins [Babe Ruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babe_Ruth) (NYY), [Jimmie Foxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Foxx) (BOS), [Barry Bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Bonds) (SF), [Manny Ramirez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Ramirez) (BOS), [Albert Pujols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Pujols) (STL), [David Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ortiz) (BOS), [Alex Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Rodriguez) (NYY), and [Miguel Cabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Cabrera) (DET).

The sleeves feature an "ATL" insignia modeled after the former logo of [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_\(American_TV_channel\)), in homage to the team's [former broadcasts on the station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braves_TBS_Baseball).

The jersey is a cream color base, which features "BMORE" across its chest in a dark green that matches the color of their outfield wall and orange outlines. It also features an [Oriole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriole_blackbird) bird perched on top of the "R", which marks a return of the Oriole logo to their uniform set. It has a homerun patch on the left sleeve which pays tribute to [Eutaw Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutaw_Street) that runs parallel to the iconic warehouse behind the ball park and that has the saying "From The Stoop to The Yard" along with the distance to home plate "410", as well as an area code for [Maryland](18858.md). Any player who hits a homerun to Eutaw Street, they receive a brass plaques that includes the player name, date of the homerun and distance. The right sleeve features a patch in dark green and orange which has the Baltimore-based company and team sponsor [T. Rowe Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Rowe_Price). The trim of the uniform is in Orioles orange is designed with brick paying tribute to the warehouse on Eutaw Street and has a jocktag on the bottom of the jersey which features a 19th century clock that reads "CHARM CITY" and "BAL", which has been a part of the ballpark above the centerfield scoreboard since the park opened April 6, 1992. The hats and batting helmets use the same colors as the uniform that has an orange "B" which is inspired by the Baltimore Baseball Club of the 1890's.

For the first time since 2006, the entire Reds uniform will feature pinstripes in a dark red, the sleeves will be a third shade of Red but does not feature the pinstripes to pay tribute to the vest style uniforms worn from 1993–2006. The hat has the fourth shade in which the bill of the hat is a darker shade of Red different from the body of the uniform but has the updated "C" logo. The right sleeve has a patch in black and a fifth shade of Red that says "CINCY", which also has a graphic that resembles the [Tyler Davidson Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Davidson_Fountain) on [Fountain Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_Square,_Cincinnati) which is located in [Downtown Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Cincinnati). The Reds will continue to use both versions of their City Connect uniforms and will alternate wearing the different versions on Friday and Saturday home games throughout the season.

The team has nicknamed them the "Forever Fountain" uniform that celebrates the city nickname "City of Fountains". The jersey features a "R" logo on the left chest that starts in Royal Blue at the bottom and blends into fuchsia at the top, which is similar to the logo from the original 1969 team logo with a crown on top of the "R". The hat also features the same crown logo as the jersey logo and uses the same color blend from Royal Blue on the brim to fuchsia on the top of the hat. The jersey numbers are the same font as the official logo of Kansas City, along with a white line around two vertical stripes on the armbands of the jersey, symbolizing teams unique position along the state lines between [Missouri](19571.md) and [Kansas](16716.md). At the bottom of the right sleeve is a heart which reflects the team being in the middle of the nations' heartland. Inside the collar of the jersey is the phrase "HEY HEY HEY HEY", which was a song famously covered by [The Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles) called "[Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_\(Leiber_and_Stoller_song\))' in 1969 at [Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Stadium_\(Kansas_City,_Missouri\)) and now plays after everyone Royals win at [Kauffman Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kauffman_Stadium).

The script lettering is a blend of script that has influence from the early 20th century American Association Milwaukee Brewers, classic supper clubs and vintage Wisconsin brewery labels. The jersey has trim in the design of Wheat and Barley braids that pays tribute to Wisconsin's agricultural roots and brewing industries across that State that is also the inspiration for the team name. The left sleeve features a patch of the old Barrelman logo in front of an outline of the State of Wisconsin using the same color outlines throughout the uniform. Inside the collar of the jersey is the inscription "Forward" which is the [State motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._state_and_territory_mottos) for Wisconsin, which means to honor the past while continuing to progress forward. The bottom of the jersey features a jocktag in the shape of a fishing bobber with a baseball design resting on top of a Wisconsin Lake celebrating one of the favorite state pastimes. The hat uses the same coloring and script with a "W" in front of the Wisconsin State border.

The jocktag features a [papel picado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papel_picado) resembles the team being located close to [Mexico](3966054.md) and has all the previous Padre logos and names. The hat uses the same cochineal red and has a block "T" logo in white using a raised weave that is similar to the design used in Mexican artistry.

### Radio

#### National

While ESPN's aforementioned opt-out also included [ESPN Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_Radio)'s national radio rights to Major League Baseball, ESPN renegotiated with the league to maintain its existing rights, including *Sunday Night Baseball*, the All-Star Game, and postseason games.[^cite_note-ESPN_rights-161][^cite_note-ESPN_opt-out-154]

This will be the third season of the league's five-year deal with [SiriusXM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiriusXM) and [SiriusXM Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiriusXM_Canada) to simulcast all 30 teams' local regular season and postseason broadcasts.[^cite_note-231]

Rizzs has been the Mariners' radio announcer starting in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Seattle_Mariners_season), and besides a stint with the Tigers from 1992 to 1994, remained with the Mariners during this period.

  [^cite_note-232]

Aside from the Mets, Rose also called [National Hockey League](21809.md) games with both the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) and [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders) between 1989 and 2016.
