# Ferris wheel

This article is about a type of amusement ride. For the original example, first erected in Chicago in 1893, see [Ferris Wheel (1893)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferris_Wheel_\(1893\)). For other uses, see [Ferris wheel (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferris_wheel_\(disambiguation\)).

"Giant wheel" redirects here. For other uses, see [Giant Wheel (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Wheel_\(disambiguation\)).



[Ain Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_Dubai), the world's largest Ferris wheel since 2021, in [Dubai](211583.md).

A **Ferris wheel** (also called a **big wheel**, **giant wheel** or an **observation wheel**) is an [amusement ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amusement_ride) consisting of a rotating upright wheel with multiple passenger-carrying components (commonly referred to as passenger cars, cabins, tubs, gondolas, capsules, or pods) attached to the [rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rim_\(wheel\)) in such a way that as the wheel turns, they are kept upright, usually by [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity). Some of the largest modern Ferris wheels have cars mounted on the outside of the rim, with [electric motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motor) to independently rotate each car to keep it upright.&#x20;

The [original Ferris Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferris_Wheel_\(1893\)) was designed and constructed by [George Washington Gale Ferris Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_Gale_Ferris_Jr.) as a landmark for the 1893 [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition) in [Chicago](6886.md), although much smaller wooden wheels of a similar idea predate Ferris's wheel, dating perhaps to the 1500s. The generic term "Ferris wheel" is now used in American English for all such structures, which have become a very common type of [amusement ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amusement_ride) at amusement parks, [state fairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_fair), and other fairs or carnivals in the United States.[^cite_note-stillturning-1]&#x20;

The tallest Ferris wheel is the 250-metre (820 ft) [Ain Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_Dubai) in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), which opened in October 2021.&#x20;

## Terminology and design

The term *Ferris wheel* comes from the designer of the best known early example, [George Washington Gale Ferris Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_Gale_Ferris_Jr.), whose Ferris Wheel Company built a large amusement for the Chicago [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition) in 1893.&#x20;

The Ferris Wheel Company was formed in 1892 for the \[construction and operation of] "wheels of the Ferris or other types for the purpose of observation or amusement",[^cite_note-Anderson-2] the source of the modern usage *observation wheel* for the type.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]&#x20;

Design variations include single- (cantilevered) or twin-sided support for the wheel, and whether the cars or capsules are oriented upright by gravity or by electric motors. The most prevalent design uses twin-sided support and gravity-oriented capsules.&#x20;

## Early history

"Pleasure wheels", whose passengers rode in chairs suspended from large wooden rings turned by strong men, may have originated in 17th-century Bulgaria.[^cite_note-stillturning-1][^cite_note-eyes_in_the_sky-5]&#x20;

*The Travels of [Peter Mundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Mundy) in Europe and Asia, 1608–1667*[^cite_note-6] describes and illustrates "*severall Sorts of Swinginge used in their Publique rejoyceings att their Feast of Biram*" on 17 May 1620 at [Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippopolis_\(Thrace\)) (now [Plovdiv](182553.md)) in the [Ottoman](22278.md) [Balkans](4829.md).[^cite_note-eyes_in_the_sky-5] Among means "*lesse dangerous and troublesome*" was one:&#x20;

> *like a Craine wheele att Customhowse Key and turned in that Manner, whereon Children sitt on little seats hunge round about in severall parts thereof, And though it turne right upp and downe, and that the Children are sometymes on the upper part of the wheele, and sometymes on the lower, yett they alwaies sitt upright.*

Five years earlier, in 1615, [Pietro Della Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Della_Valle), a Roman traveller who sent letters from [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), Persia, and India, attended a Ramadan festival in Constantinople. He describes the fireworks, floats, and great swings, then comments on riding the Great Wheel:[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

> I was delighted to find myself swept upwards and downwards at such speed. But the wheel turned round so rapidly that a Greek who was sitting near me couldn't bear it any longer, and shouted out "soni! soni!" (enough! enough!)

Similar wheels also appeared in England in the 17th century, and subsequently elsewhere around the world, including India, Romania, and Siberia.[^cite_note-eyes_in_the_sky-5]&#x20;

A Frenchman, Antonio Manguino, introduced the idea to the United States in 1848, when he constructed a wooden pleasure wheel to attract visitors to his start-up fair in Walton Spring, [Georgia](48830.md).&#x20;

### Somers' Roundabout



A 50 foot wooden wheel built by William Somers in [Atlantic City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey), in 1891 was an immediate precursor to the original [Ferris Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferris_Wheel_\(1893\))

In 1891, William Somers installed a fifty-foot wooden wheel at [Atlantic City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey), and later others at [Asbury Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asbury_Park,_New_Jersey), and [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island), New York.[^cite_note-8] In 1893 he was granted the first U.S. patent for a "Roundabout".[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-patents-10] [George Washington Gale Ferris Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_Gale_Ferris_Jr.) rode on Somers' wheel in Atlantic City before designing his wheel for the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition). In 1893, Somers filed a lawsuit against Ferris for patent infringement; however, Ferris and his lawyers successfully argued that the Ferris Wheel and its technology differed greatly from Somers' wheel, and the case was dismissed.[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

### The original Ferris Wheel

Main article: [Ferris Wheel (1893)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferris_Wheel_\(1893\))



The original Chicago [Ferris Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferris_Wheel_\(1893\)), built for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition

The original Ferris wheel, sometimes referred to as the Chicago Wheel, was designed and constructed by Ferris Jr. and opened in 1893; however, an earlier wheel was created for the New York State fair in 1854, created by two Erie Canal workers.[^cite_note-WDL-12][^cite_note-Anderson-2][^cite_note-Meehan-13][^cite_note-14]&#x20;

With a height of 80.4 metres (264 ft), it was the tallest attraction at the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition) in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois), where it opened to the public on June 21, 1893.[^cite_note-WDL-12] It was intended to rival the 324-metre (1,063 ft) [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower), the centerpiece of the [1889 Paris Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_\(1889\)).&#x20;

Ferris was a graduate of [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute) and a bridge-builder in [Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania). He began his career in the railroad industry and then pursued an interest in bridge building. Ferris understood the growing need for structural steel and founded G.W\.G. Ferris & Co. in Pittsburgh, a firm that tested and inspected metals for railroads and bridge builders.&#x20;

The wheel rotated on a 71-[ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_ton), 45.5-foot (13.9 m) axle comprising what was at that time the world's largest hollow forging, manufactured in Pittsburgh by the [Bethlehem Iron Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Steel) and weighing 89,320 pounds (40,510 kg), together with two 16-foot-diameter (4.9 m) cast-iron spiders weighing 53,031 pounds (24,054 kg).[^cite_note-Meehan-13]&#x20;

There were 36 cars, each fitted with 40 revolving chairs and able to accommodate up to 60 people, giving a total capacity of 2,160.[^cite_note-Anderson-2] The cars were 24 feet long, 13 feet wide, and 10 feet high.[^cite_note-15] The wheel carried some 38,000 passengers daily.[^cite_note-stillturning-1] It took 20 minutes to complete two revolutions: the first involved six stops for passenger exchange, and the second was a nine-minute non-stop rotation, for which the ticket holder paid 50 cents.&#x20;

The Exposition ended in October 1893, and the wheel, which had closed in April 1894, was dismantled and stored until the following year. It was then rebuilt on Chicago's North Side, near the high-income enclave of [Lincoln Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Park_\(Chicago\)). [William D. Boyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_D._Boyce), then a resident, filed a Circuit Court action against the owners of the wheel to have it removed, but without success. It operated there from October 1895 until 1903, when it was again dismantled, then transported by rail to [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri) for the [1904 World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase_Exposition) and finally destroyed by controlled demolition using dynamite on May 11, 1906.[^cite_note-16]&#x20;

### Antique Ferris wheels

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| [Wiener Riesenrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Riesenrad), Vienna, built in 1897, originally had 30 passenger cabins but was rebuilt with 15 cabins following a fire in 1944 |  |

The [Wiener Riesenrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Riesenrad) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) for "Viennese Giant Wheel") is a surviving example of 19th-century Ferris wheels. Erected in 1897 in the [Wurstelprater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurstelprater) section of [Prater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prater) public park in the [Leopoldstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldstadt) district of [Vienna](55866.md), [Austria](26964606.md), to celebrate Emperor [Franz Josef I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria)'s [Golden Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee), it has a height of 64.75 metres (212 ft)[^cite_note-17] and originally had 30 passenger cars. A demolition permit for the Riesenrad was issued in 1916, but due to a lack of funds with which to carry out the destruction, it survived.[^cite_note-wienerriesenradhistory-18]&#x20;

Following the demolition of the 96-metre (315 ft) [Grande Roue de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Roue_de_Paris) in 1920,[^cite_note-Anderson-2][^cite_note-worldfairs-19] the Riesenrad became the world's tallest extant Ferris wheel. In 1944 it burnt down, but was rebuilt the following year[^cite_note-wienerriesenradhistory-18] with 15 passenger cars, and remained the world's tallest extant wheel until the 85-metre (279 ft) [Technocosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocosmos) was constructed in 1984 for [Expo '85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_%2785), at [Tsukuba, Ibaraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba,_Ibaraki), [Japan](15573.md).&#x20;

Still in operation today, it is one of Vienna's most popular [tourist attractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_attraction), and over the years has featured in numerous films (including *Madame Solange d\`Atalide* (1914),[^cite_note-wienerriesenradhistory-18] *[Letter from an Unknown Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_from_an_Unknown_Woman_\(1948_film\))* (1948), *[The Third Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Third_Man)* (1949), *[The Living Daylights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Living_Daylights)* (1987), *[Before Sunrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Sunrise)* (1995) and novels.&#x20;

## World's tallest Ferris wheels

The 94 m [Great Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wheel) at Earls Court, London, world's tallest Ferris wheel 1895–1900

**Chronology of world's tallest wheels**&#x20;

- **1893**: the original Ferris Wheel was 70.4 metres (231 ft) tall. Built for the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition) in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois), it was moved to [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri), in 1904 for the [Louisiana Purchase Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase_Exposition), and demolished there in 1906.

- **1895**: the [Great Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wheel) was built for the Empire of India Exhibition at [Earls Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earls_Court), [London](17867.md), [UK](31717.md), and was 310 feet (94.5 m) tall.

  [^cite_note-ehp-20]

  &#x20;Construction began in March 1894

  [^cite_note-21]

  &#x20;and it opened to the public on July 17, 1895.

  [^cite_note-22]

  &#x20;It stayed in service until 1906 and was demolished in 1907, having carried over 2.5 million passengers.

  [^cite_note-23]

- **1900**: the [Grande Roue de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Roue_de_Paris) was built for the [Exposition Universelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_\(1900\)), a world's fair held in [Paris](22989.md), [France](5843419.md). It was demolished in 1920,

  [^cite_note-Anderson-2]

  &#x20;but its 96-metre (315 ft) height was not surpassed until almost 90 years after its construction.

  [^cite_note-worldfairs-19]

- **1920**: the [Wiener Riesenrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Riesenrad) was built to celebrate the [Golden Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee) of Emperor [Franz Josef I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria), at the entrance of the [Wurstelprater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurstelprater) amusement park in [Austria](26964606.md)'s capital [Vienna](55866.md). Constructed in 1897, when the Grande Roue de Paris was demolished in 1920, the Riesenrad became the world's tallest extant Ferris wheel with 64.75-metre (212 ft), and it remained so for the next 65 years until 1985, its 97th year.

- **1985**: [Technocosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocosmos), later renamed Technostar, was an 85-metre (279 ft) tall giant Ferris wheel, originally built for the [Expo 85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_85_\(Tsukuba,_Japan\)) World Fair in [Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba), [Ibaraki](181029.md), [Japan](15573.md). Work began on dismantling Technostar in November 2009.

- **1989**: the [Cosmo Clock 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmo_Clock_21) was built for the YES '89 Yokohama Exposition at [Minato Mirai 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato_Mirai_21), [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama), [Japan](15573.md). Originally constructed with a height of 107.5 metres (353 ft),

  [^cite_note-senyo89-24]

  &#x20;it was dismantled in 1997 and then in 1999 relocated onto a taller base which increased its overall height to 112.5 metres (369 ft).

  [^cite_note-senyo99-25]

- **1992**: Igosu 108 at [Biwako Tower](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%B3%E3%82%8F%E6%B9%96%E3%82%BF%E3%83%AF%E3%83%BC), [Ōtsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ctsu), [Shiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiga_Prefecture), [Japan](15573.md), opened April 26 at 108 metres (354 ft) tall, hence its name. It has since been moved to Vietnam, where it opened as the [Sun Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Wheel_\(Da_Nang\)) on a new base, now totaling 115 metres (377 ft) tall.

  [^cite_note-auto-26]

- **1997**: the [Tempozan Ferris Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempozan_Ferris_Wheel), in [Osaka](52230.md), [Japan](15573.md), opened to the public on July 13, and is 112.5 metres (369 ft) tall.

  [^cite_note-27]

- **1999**: the [Daikanransha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daikanransha) at Palette Town in [Odaiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odaiba), [Tokyo](30057.md), Japan, is 115 metres (377 ft) tall.

  [^cite_note-daikanransha-28]

- **2000**: the [London Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Eye), in [London](17867.md), [United Kingdom](31717.md), is 135 metres (443 ft) tall. Although officially opened on December 31, 1999, it did not open to the public until March 2000 due to technical problems.

- **2006**: the [Star of Nanchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_Nanchang), in [Nanchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang), [Jiangxi](173817.md) Province, [China](5405.md), opened for business in May and is 160 metres (525 ft) tall.

- **2008**: the [Singapore Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Flyer), in [Singapore](27318.md), is 165 metres (541 ft) tall. It started rotating on February 11, and officially opened to the public on March 1, 2008.

- **2014**: the [High Roller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Roller_\(Ferris_wheel\)), in [Las Vegas, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas,_Nevada), United States, is 167.6 metres (550 ft) tall. It opened to the public on March 31, 2014.

  [^cite_note-Trejos-29]

- **2021**: the [Ain Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_Dubai) in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) is 250-metre (820 ft). It opened to the public on October 21, 2021.

### Future wheels

Following the success of the 135-metre (443 ft) [London Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Eye), which opened in 2000, giant Ferris wheels have been proposed for many other cities; however, many of these projects have stalled or failed.[^cite_note-52]&#x20;

#### Construction in progress

- Isfahan Eye, a 222 m (728 ft) Ferris wheel in Mount Soffeh, [Iran](14653.md), under development by the city's municipality. It will be built with a financing of 1000 billion toman.

  [^cite_note-53]

- The 139 m (456 ft) [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) [OCT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese_Town_Limited) Funland Ferris Wheel has passed national inspections in early 2023 and is about to open to the public.

  [^cite_note-欢乐滨江-54]

- Dhaka Eye, a 90 m (300 ft) Ferris wheel, as a part of Bashundhara Central Park, an amusement park under construction in [Dhaka](56656.md), Bangladesh.

  [^cite_note-55]

- The [Rueda de Buenos Aires](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rueda_de_Buenos_Aires) will be a 81.5 m (267 ft) Ferris wheel located at [Dike 1](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dique_1_\(Puerto_Madero\)) of [Puerto Madero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Madero) in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), [Argentina](18951905.md).

  [^cite_note-56]

  &#x20;It will have 36 cabins with capacity for 8 people and the tour will last approximately 30 minutes.

### Centreless wheels



[Big O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_O_\(Ferris_wheel\)), a 60-metre (197 ft) tall centreless wheel at [Tokyo Dome City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Dome_City) in Japan

In the centreless (sometimes called hubless or spokeless) wheel design, there is no central hub, and the rim of the wheel stays fixed in place. Instead, each car travels around the rim. The first centreless wheel built was the [Big O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_O_\(Ferris_wheel\)) at [Tokyo Dome City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Dome_City) in Japan.[^cite_note-142] Its 60-metre (197 ft) height has since been surpassed by the 145-metre (475.7 ft) high [Bailang River Bridge Ferris Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailang_River_Bridge_Ferris_Wheel) on the upper deck of the Bailang River Bridge in [Shandong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Province), China, which opened in 2017.[^cite_note-143]&#x20;

The first centreless wheel in North America opened in January 2019 at the indoor Méga Parc in [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City), Canada.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] The 23.5 m (77 ft) wheel at Méga Parc was designed and manufactured by Larson International.[^cite_note-146]&#x20;

### Transportable wheels

Transportable Ferris wheels are designed to be operated at multiple locations, whereas fixed wheels are usually intended for permanent installation. Small transportable designs may be permanently mounted on [trailers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailer_\(vehicle\)), and can be moved intact. Larger transportable wheels are designed to be repeatedly dismantled and rebuilt, some using water ballast instead of the permanent foundations of their fixed counterparts. Larger transportable wheels were designed with a self-erecting mechanism in the absence of mobile cranes reaching high enough since 1958 in Europe. Spokes must be stiff enough to carry their own weight and assemble the wheel without auxiliary scaffolding.&#x20;

Fixed wheels are also sometimes dismantled and relocated. Larger examples include the original [Ferris Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferris_Wheel_\(1893\)), which operated at two sites in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois), and a third in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri); [Technocosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocosmos)/Technostar, which moved to [Expoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expoland), [Osaka](52230.md), after [Expo '85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_%2785), [Tsukuba, Ibaraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba,_Ibaraki), for which it was built, ended; and [Cosmo Clock 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmo_Clock_21), which added 5 metres (16 ft) onto its original 107.5-metre (353 ft) height when erected for the second time at [Minato Mirai 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato_Mirai_21), [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama), in 1999.&#x20;

The world's tallest transportable wheel today is the 78-metre (256 ft) [Bussink Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bussink_Design) [R80XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R80XL).[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]&#x20;



[Roue de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roue_de_Paris), a [Ronald Bussink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Bussink) R60 transportable wheel, at [Geleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geleen) in the Netherlands in 2005

One of the most famous transportable wheels is the 60-metre (197 ft) [Roue de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roue_de_Paris), originally installed on the [Place de la Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_de_la_Concorde) in [Paris](22989.md) for the 2000 [millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium) celebrations. Roue de Paris left [France](5843419.md) in 2002 and in 2003–04 operated in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Birmingham) and [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Manchester), [England](9316.md). In 2005 it visited first [Geleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geleen) then [Amsterdam](844.md), [Netherlands](21148.md), before returning to England to operate at [Gateshead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateshead). In 2006 it was erected at the [Suan Lum Night Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suan_Lum_Night_Bazaar) in [Bangkok](56615.md), [Thailand](30128.md), and by 2008 had made its way to [Antwerp](32149462.md), [Belgium](3343.md).[^cite_note-151]&#x20;

Roue de Paris is a [Ronald Bussink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Bussink) series R60 design using 40,000 litres (8,800 imperial gallons; 11,000 US gallons) of water ballast to provide a stable base. The R60 weighs 365 tonnes (402 short tons), and can be erected in 72 hours and dismantled in 60 hours by a specialist team. Transport requires seven 20-foot container lorries, ten open trailer lorries, and one closed trailer lorry. Its 42-passenger cars can be loaded either 3 or 6 at a time; each car can carry 8 people.[^cite_note-RDPtechnical-152] Bussink R60 wheels have operated in Australia ([Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Brisbane)), Canada ([Niagara Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_SkyWheel)), France ([Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roue_de_Paris)), Malaysia ([Kuala Lumpur & Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_on_Malaysia)), México ([Puebla](413436.md)), UK ([Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast_Wheel), [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Birmingham), [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Manchester), [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Sheffield)), US ([Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyView_Atlanta), [Myrtle Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtle_Beach_SkyWheel)), and elsewhere.&#x20;

Other notable transportable wheels include the 60-metre (197 ft) [Steiger Ferris Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiger_Ferris_Wheel), which was the world's tallest transportable wheel when it began operating in 1980.[^cite_note-153] It has 42 passenger cars,[^cite_note-techdata-154] and weighs 450 tons.[^cite_note-city-155] On October 11, 2010, it collapsed at the Kramermarkt in [Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldenburg_\(city\)), [Germany](11867.md), during deconstruction.[^cite_note-156]&#x20;

### Double and triple wheels

A double Ferris wheel with a horizontal turntable was patented in 1939 by John F. Courtney, working for Velare & Courtney. In Courtney's design, there were two independent Ferris wheels, each rotating at either end of a cantilever arm. The cantilever arm was supported at its midpoint by a tall vertical support, and the arm itself rotated about its middle pivot point.[^cite_note-157] The design was similar to the earlier Aeriocycle, but the double wheel patented by Courtney allowed the cantilever arm to make a complete rotation, while the Aeriocycle was limited to a seesaw motion.[^cite_note-158] Courtney continued to file additional patents on improved designs through the 1950s to make them more portable,[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] and at about the same time, the Velare brothers patented the "Space Wheel", a side-by-side double with four total Ferris wheels.[^cite_note-161]&#x20;

The design was later sold to the [Allan Herschell Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Herschell_Company) in 1959 and marketed as the "Sky Wheel"; the first sale as the Sky Wheel was to 20th Century Rides in October 1960.[^cite_note-162] The Sky Wheel seated up to 32 riders in 16 two-person cars, with 8 cars per wheel, and riders reached a peak of approximately 80 feet (24 m). The height and popularity of the Sky Wheel was eclipsed by larger single wheels in the late 1980s and early 1990s; it has since largely disappeared from common use.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] As of 2018, there are four known Sky Wheels that remain in operation.[^cite_note-165]&#x20;

In March 1966, Thomas Glen Robinson and Ralph G. Robinson received a patent for a Planetary Amusement Ride, a distinct double-wheel design. In the Robinsons' patent, the cantilever arm was bent at a slightly obtuse angle, and the cars were carried on a spoked "spider" rotating structure at each end of the cantilever. With the obtuse-angle cantilever, one spider could be lowered to the ground in a horizontal plane so that all the cars on that spider could be unloaded and loaded simultaneously, while the spider on the other end of the cantilever would continue to rotate in a near-vertical plane.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]&#x20;

Robinson sold two of these rides – Astrowheel, which operated at the former [Six Flags AstroWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_AstroWorld) in [Houston](13774.md), Texas,[^cite_note-168] and [Galaxy](1017230.md#Galaxy), which operated at [Six Flags Magic Mountain](1017230.md) in [Valencia, California](27991949.md). Both were manufactured by Astron International Corporation.[^cite_note-Parkives-160414-169] Astrowheel was part of the original lineup of rides when Astroworld opened in 1968;[^cite_note-170] it was removed in 1981 to make way for the Warp 10 ride.[^cite_note-171] Astrowheel had an eight-spoked spider at the end of each arm, and each tip had a separate car for eight cars in total on each end.[^cite_note-172] In contrast, Galaxy had double the capacity with a four-spoked spider at the end of each arm; each tip bore an independent four-spoked sub-spider for sixteen cars in total on each end. Like Astrowheel, Galaxy was part of the lineup at Magic Mountain when the park opened in 1971, and was removed in 1980 when Six Flags took over ownership of both parks.[^cite_note-173]&#x20;

Swiss broker [Intamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intamin) marketed a similar series of double wheels manufactured by [Waagner-Biro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waagner-Biro), comprising a vertical column supporting a straight cantilever arm, with each end of the cantilever arm ending in a spoked Ferris wheel. The first Intamin product was [Giant Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Wheel_\(Hersheypark\)) at [Hersheypark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hersheypark) in [Hershey, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey,_Pennsylvania), which operated from 1973 to 2004.[^cite_note-Parkives-160414-169] Other double wheels made by Waagner-Biro/Intamin include Zodiac ([Kings Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Island), [Mason, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason,_Ohio); 1975–86;[^cite_note-174] moved to [Wonderland Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonderland_Sydney) and operated 1989–2004), Scorpion ([Parque de la Ciudad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_de_la_Ciudad), [Buenos Aires](255919.md), Argentina; 1982–2003), and Double Wheel ([Kuwait Entertainment City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_Entertainment_City), [Kuwait City](57021.md), Kuwait; 1984–91).[^cite_note-AP-doubletriple-175]&#x20;

A triple variant was custom designed for the [Marriott Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriott_Corporation) and debuted at both Marriott's Great America parks (now [Six Flags Great America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_America), [Gurnee, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurnee,_Illinois), and [California's Great America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California%27s_Great_America), [Santa Clara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara,_California)) in 1976 as [Sky Whirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Whirl). Each ride had three main components: the three spiders/wheels with their passenger cars; the triple-spoked supporting arm; and the single central supporting column. Each wheel rotated about one of the three ends of the supporting arm. The supporting arm would, in turn, rotate about its central hub as a single unit at the top of the supporting column. The axis about which the supporting arm turned was offset from vertical (i.e., the plane of rotation was not horizontal), so that as the supporting arm rotated, each wheel was raised and lowered. When lowered, one wheel was horizontal at ground level. At the same time, the other wheels remained raised and continued to rotate in a near-vertical plane at considerable height. The lowered horizontal wheel was brought to a standstill for simultaneous loading and unloading of all its passenger cars.[^cite_note-GAP-sky-whirl-176]&#x20;

The Sky Whirl was also known as a triple Ferris wheel,[^cite_note-177] Triple Giant Wheel,[^cite_note-178] or Triple Tree Wheel; it was 33 metres (108 ft) in height.[^cite_note-179] The Sky Whirl in Santa Clara was filmed for a memorable rescue scene in *[Beverly Hills Cop III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills_Cop_III)* (renamed to "The Spider" for the film).[^cite_note-180] The Santa Clara ride, renamed Triple Wheel in post-Marriott years, closed on September 1, 1997. The Gurnee ride closed in 2000.[^cite_note-GAP-sky-whirl-176] Two triple wheels were built for Asian clients: Tree Triple Wheel at [Seibu-en](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seibu-en) ([Tokorozawa, Saitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokorozawa,_Saitama), Japan; 1985–2004) and Hydra at [Lotte World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_World) ([Seoul](19159283.md), South Korea; 1989–97).[^cite_note-AP-doubletriple-175]&#x20;

### Eccentric wheels

An **eccentric wheel** (sometimes called a **sliding wheel**[^cite_note-originald-181] or **coaster wheel**[^cite_note-182]) differs from a conventional Ferris wheel in that some or all of its passenger cars are not fixed directly to the rim of the wheel, but instead slide on rails between the rim and the hub as the wheel rotates.&#x20;

The two most famous eccentric wheels are [Wonder Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Wheel), at [Deno's Wonder Wheel Amusement Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deno%27s_Wonder_Wheel_Amusement_Park), [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island), US, and [Pixar Pal-A-Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar_Pal-A-Round) (previously Sun Wheel and Mickey's Fun Wheel), at [Disney California Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_California_Adventure), US. The latter is a replica of the former. There is a second replica in [Yokohama Dreamland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama_Dreamland), [Japan](15573.md).[^cite_note-wwhistory-183]&#x20;

[Pixar Pal-A-Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar_Pal-A-Round) is 48.8 metres (160 ft) tall[^cite_note-originald-181] and has 24 fully enclosed passenger cars, each able to carry six passengers. Each passenger car is decorated with the face of a [Pixar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar) character. Sixteen slide inward and outward as the wheel rotates; the remainder are fixed to the rim. There are separate boarding queues for sliding and fixed cars, so that passengers may choose between the two.[^cite_note-allears-184] Inspired by [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island)'s 1920 Wonder Wheel, it was designed by [Walt Disney Imagineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Imagineering) and [Waagner Biro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waagner_Biro), completed in 2001 as the Sun Wheel, later refurbished and reopened in 2009 as Mickey's Fun Wheel, and again rethemed as Pixar Pal-A-Round in 2018.[^cite_note-originald-181]&#x20;

[Wonder Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Wheel) was built in 1920, is 45.7 metres (150 ft) tall, and can carry 144 people.[^cite_note-185]&#x20;
