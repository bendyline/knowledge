**British Columbia**[^cite_note-9] is the westernmost [province of Canada](75763.md). Situated in the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) between the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) and the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains), the province has a diverse geography, with rugged landscapes that include rocky coastlines, sandy beaches, forests, lakes, mountains, inland deserts, and grassy plains.[^cite_note-10] British Columbia borders the province of [Alberta](717.md) to the east; the territories of [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon) and [Northwest Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territories) to the north; the [US states](18618239.md) of [Washington](13015878.md), [Idaho](14607.md), and [Montana](19978.md) to the south; and [Alaska](624.md) to the northwest. With an estimated population of 5.68 million in 2025, it is Canada's [third-most populous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Canada_by_province_and_territory). The capital of British Columbia is [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia), while the province's largest city is [Vancouver](32706.md). Vancouver and its suburbs together make up [the third-largest metropolitan area in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_census_metropolitan_areas_and_agglomerations_in_Canada), with the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census) recording 2.6 million people in [Metro Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver_Regional_District).[^cite_note-11] British Columbia is Canada's third-largest province by total area, after [Quebec](7954867.md) and [Ontario](22218.md).[^cite_note-12]

The [first known](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) human inhabitants of the area settled in British Columbia at least 10,000 years ago. They include the [Coast Salish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Salish), [Tsilhqotʼin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsilhqot%CA%BCin), and [Haida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haida_people) peoples, among many others. One of the earliest British settlements in the area was [Fort Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Victoria_\(British_Columbia\)), established in 1843, which gave rise to the city of Victoria, the capital of the [Colony of Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Vancouver_Island). The [Colony of British Columbia (1858–1866)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1858%E2%80%931866\)) was subsequently founded by [Richard Clement Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Clement_Moody),[^cite_note-Richard_Clement_Moody_Obituary,_ICE-13] and by the [Royal Engineers, Columbia Detachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Engineers,_Columbia_Detachment), in response to the [Fraser Canyon Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Canyon_Gold_Rush). Moody selected the site for and founded the mainland colony's capital [New Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Westminster).[^cite_note-Edward_1887_215_in_New_Westminster_District_Directory-14][^cite_note-DFB_Richard_Clement_Moody-15][^cite_note-DNB-16] The colonies of Vancouver Island and British Columbia were incorporated in 1866, after which Victoria became the [united colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1866%E2%80%931871\))'s capital. In 1871, British Columbia entered [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) as the sixth province of Canada, in enactment of the *[British Columbia Terms of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Terms_of_Union)*.

British Columbia is a [diverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_\(politics\)) and [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism) province, drawing on a plethora of cultural influences from its [diasporas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) of [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Canadians), [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Canadians), and [Asian Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Canadians), as well as the [Indigenous population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Pacific_Northwest_Coast). Though the province's ethnic majority originates from the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles), many British Columbians also trace their ancestors to [continental Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe), [East Asia](19605700.md), and [South Asia](21566765.md).[^cite_note-2016CensusBCEthnicOrigin-17] [Indigenous Canadians](196075.md) constitute about 6 percent of the province's total population.[^cite_note-indigenouspopulation2021-18] Christianity is the largest religion in the region, though the majority of the population is non-religious.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] English is the common language of the province, although [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language), [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), and [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) also have a large presence in the Metro Vancouver region. The [Franco-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Columbian) community is an officially recognized linguistic minority, and around one percent of British Columbians claim [French as their mother tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadians).[^cite_note-2016CensusBCLanguage-21] British Columbia is home to at least 34 distinct [Indigenous languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-22]

Major sectors of [British Columbia's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_British_Columbia) include forestry, mining, filmmaking and [video production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_production), tourism, real estate, construction, wholesale, and retail. Its main exports include lumber and timber, [pulp and paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_and_paper) products, copper, coal, and natural gas.[^cite_note-23] British Columbia exhibits [high property values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) and is a significant centre for [maritime trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_trade):[^cite_note-24] the [Port of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Vancouver) is the largest port in Canada and the most diversified port in North America.[^cite_note-25] Although less than 5 percent of British Columbia is [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land), significant agriculture exists in the [Fraser Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Valley) and [Okanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanagan) due to the [warmer climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_British_Columbia).[^cite_note-26] British Columbia is home to 45% of all publicly listed companies in Canada.[^cite_note-27]

## Etymology

The name of the province was chosen by [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria), when the [Colony of British Columbia (1858–1866)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1858%E2%80%931866\)), i.e., "the Mainland", became a British colony in 1858.[^cite_note-28] It refers to the [Columbia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_District), the British name for the territory drained by the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River), in southeastern British Columbia, which was the namesake of the [Columbia Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Department) of the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company). Queen Victoria chose *British* Columbia to distinguish what was the British sector of the Columbia District from the United States' ("American Columbia" or "Southern Columbia"), which became the [Oregon Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Territory) on August 8, 1848, as a result of the treaty.[^cite_note-29]

Ultimately, the *Columbia* in the name *British Columbia* is derived from the name of the *[Columbia Rediviva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Rediviva)*, an American ship which lent its name to the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) and later the wider region;[^cite_note-30] the *Columbia* in the name *Columbia Rediviva* came from [the name *Columbia* for the New World or parts thereof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_\(personification\)), a reference to [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus).

The governments of Canada and British Columbia recognize *Colombie-Britannique* as the French name for the province.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_British_Columbia)



British Columbia's [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography) is epitomized by the variety and intensity of its physical relief, which has defined patterns of [settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement) and industry since colonization.

British Columbia is bordered to the west by the Pacific Ocean and the American state of [Alaska](624.md), to the north by [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon) and the [Northwest Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territories), to the east by the province of [Alberta](717.md), and to the south by the American states of [Washington](13015878.md), [Idaho](14607.md), and [Montana](19978.md). The [southern border of British Columbia](630260.md) was established by the 1846 Oregon Treaty, although its history is tied with lands as far south as California. British Columbia's land area is 944,735 square kilometres (364,764 sq mi). [British Columbia's rugged coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Coast) stretches for more than 27,000 kilometres (17,000 mi), and includes deep, mountainous fjords and about 6,000 islands, most of which are uninhabited. It is the only province in Canada that borders the Pacific Ocean. British Columbia's highest mountain is [Mount Fairweather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fairweather); the highest mountain entirely within the province is [Mount Waddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Waddington). British Columbia is considered to be part of the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) and the [Cascadia bioregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascadia_\(bioregion\)), along with the [American states](18618239.md) of Alaska, Idaho, (western) Montana, [Oregon](26811621.md), Washington, and [(northern) California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_California).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

British Columbia's capital city, [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia), is located on the southeastern tip of [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island). Only a narrow strip of Vancouver Island, from [Campbell River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_River,_British_Columbia) to Victoria, is significantly populated. Much of the western part of Vancouver Island and the rest of the coast is covered by [temperate rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_rainforest). The province's most populous city, [Vancouver](32706.md), sits at the confluence of the [Fraser River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_River) and [Georgia Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Strait) in the southwest corner of the mainland, an area commonly known as the [Lower Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Mainland). By land area, [Abbotsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbotsford,_British_Columbia) is the province's largest city. [Vanderhoof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderhoof,_British_Columbia) is near the geographic centre of the province.[^cite_note-35] The northern, mostly mountainous, two-thirds of the province is largely unpopulated and undeveloped, except for the area east of the [Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Rockies), where the [Peace River Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_River_Country) contains BC's portion of the [Canadian Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies), centred at the city of [Dawson Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson_Creek).

The [Coast Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Mountains) and the [Inside Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_Passage)'s many [inlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord) provide some of British Columbia's scenery, which forms the backdrop and context for a growing outdoor adventure and [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) industry. 75 percent of the province is mountainous (more than 1,000 m \[3,300 ft] [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_mean_sea_level)); 60 percent is forested; and only about 5 percent is arable.

The province's mainland away from the coastal regions is somewhat moderated by the Pacific Ocean. Terrain ranges from dry inland forests and [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) valleys, to the range and canyon districts of the [Central and Southern Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Interior), to boreal forest and subarctic prairie in the Northern Interior. High mountain regions both north and south have [subalpine flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane_ecosystem)[^cite_note-Subalpine-36] and [subalpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subalpine_climate).

The [Okanagan wine area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanagan_Valley_\(wine_region\)), extending from [Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon,_British_Columbia) to [Osoyoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osoyoos) at the [Oroville–Osoyoos Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oroville%E2%80%93Osoyoos_Border_Crossing), is one of several wine and [cider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider)-producing regions in Canada. Other wine regions in British Columbia include the [Cowichan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowichan_Valley) on Vancouver Island and the [Fraser Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Valley).

The Southern Interior cities of [Kamloops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamloops) and [Penticton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penticton) have some of the warmest and longest summer climates in Canada (while higher elevations are cold and snowy), although their temperatures are often exceeded north of the [Fraser Canyon](1897554.md), close to the confluence of the Fraser and [Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_River) rivers, where the terrain is rugged and covered with desert-type flora. Semi-desert grassland is found in large areas of the [Interior Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Plateau), with land uses ranging from ranching at lower altitudes to forestry at higher ones.

### Climate



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) in British Columbia



The [Strait of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Georgia), near [Vancouver](32706.md)

Because of the many mountain ranges and rugged coastline, British Columbia's climate varies dramatically across the province.

Coastal southern British Columbia has a mild and rainy climate influenced by the [North Pacific Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pacific_Current). Most of the region is classified as [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), though pockets of [warm-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-summer_Mediterranean) also exist in the far-southern parts of the coast. Precipitation averages above 1,000 millimetres (39 in) in almost all of the coastal region, and [Hucuktlis Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hucuktlis_Lake) on [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island) receives an average of 6,903 mm (271.8 in) of rain annually.

Due to the blocking presence of successive mountain ranges, the climate of some of the interior valleys of the province (such as the Thompson, parts of the Fraser Canyon, the southern Cariboo and parts of the Okanagan) is [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) with certain locations receiving less than 250 millimetres (10 in) in annual precipitation. The annual mean temperature in the most populated areas of the province is up to 12 °C (54 °F), the mildest anywhere in Canada.

The valleys of the Southern Interior have short winters with only brief bouts of cold or infrequent heavy snow, while those in the [Cariboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariboo), in the [Central Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Interior#Central_Interior), are colder because of increased altitude and latitude, but without the intensity or duration experienced at similar latitudes elsewhere in Canada. Outside of the driest valleys, the Southern and Central Interior generally have a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) with widely variable precipitation. For example, the average daily low in [Prince George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George,_British_Columbia) (roughly in the middle of the province) in January is −12 °C (10 °F).[^cite_note-37] Small towns in the southern interior with high elevation such as [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_British_Columbia) are typically colder and snowier than cities in the valleys.[^cite_note-Princeton-38]

Heavy snowfall occurs in all elevated mountainous terrain providing bases for skiers in both south and central British Columbia. Annual snowfall on highway mountain passes in the southern interior rivals some of the snowiest cities in Canada,[^cite_note-39] and freezing rain and fog are sometimes present on such roads as well.[^cite_note-40] This can result in hazardous driving conditions, as people are usually travelling between warmer areas such as Vancouver or Kamloops, and may be unaware that the conditions may be slippery and cold.[^cite_note-Coq-41]



[Shuswap Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuswap_Lake) as seen from [Sorrento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento,_British_Columbia)

Winters are generally severe in the Northern Interior which is generally in the [subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate) zone, but even there, milder air can penetrate far inland. The coldest temperature in British Columbia was recorded in [Smith River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_River,_British_Columbia), where it dropped to −58.9 °C (−74.0 °F) on January 31, 1947,[^cite_note-KCH-WD2-2002-42] one of the coldest readings recorded anywhere in North America. [Atlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlin,_British_Columbia) in the province's far northwest, along with the adjoining Southern Lakes region of [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon), get midwinter thaws caused by the [Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_wind) effect, which is also common (and much warmer) in more southerly parts of the Interior.

During winter on the coast, rainfall, sometimes relentless heavy rain, dominates because of consistent barrages of cyclonic low-pressure systems from the North Pacific. Average snowfall on the coast during a normal winter is between 25 and 50 centimetres (10 and 20 in), but on occasion (and not every winter) heavy snowfalls with more than 20 centimetres (8 in) and well below freezing temperatures arrive when modified arctic air reaches coastal areas, typically for short periods, and can take temperatures below −10 °C (14 °F), even at sea level. Arctic outflow winds can occasionally result in wind chill temperatures at or even below −17.8 °C (0.0 °F).[^cite_note-43] While winters are very wet, coastal areas are generally milder and dry during summer under the influence of stable anti-cyclonic high pressure.

Southern Interior valleys are hot in summer; for example, in [Osoyoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osoyoos), the July maximum temperature averages 31.7 °C (89.1 °F), making it the hottest month of any location in Canada; this hot weather sometimes spreads towards the coast or to the far north of the province. Temperatures often exceed 40 °C (104 °F) in the lower elevations of valleys in the Interior during mid-summer, with the record high of 49.6 °C (121.3 °F) being held in [Lytton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lytton,_British_Columbia) on June 29, 2021, during [a record-breaking heat wave that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_North_America_heat_wave).[^cite_note-44]



The [Okanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanagan) region has a climate suitable for vineyards.

The extended summer dryness often creates conditions that spark forest fires, from dry-lightning or man-made causes. Many areas of the province are often covered by a blanket of heavy cloud and low fog during the winter months, in contrast to abundant summer sunshine. Annual sunshine hours vary from 2200 near Cranbrook and Victoria to less than 1300 in [Prince Rupert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Rupert,_British_Columbia), on the [North Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Coast#North_Coast) just south of [Southeast Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Alaska).

The exception to British Columbia's wet and cloudy winters is during the [El Niño](317311.md) phase. During El Niño events, the jet stream is much farther south across North America, making the province's winters milder and drier than normal. Winters are much wetter and cooler during the opposite phase, [La Niña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ni%C3%B1a).

### Parks and protected areas

Main articles: [List of protected areas of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protected_areas_of_British_Columbia) and [List of provincial parks in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_parks_in_British_Columbia)



[Mount Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Robson), [Canadian Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Rockies)



[Odaray Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odaray_Mountain) and [Lake O'Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_O'Hara)

There are 14 designations of parks and protected areas in the province that reflect the different administration and creation of these areas in a modern context. There are 141 ecological reserves, 35 provincial marine parks, 7 provincial heritage sites, 6 [National Historic Sites of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Sites_of_Canada), 4 [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park) and 3 national park reserves. 12.5 percent of the province's area (114,000 km<sup>2</sup> or 44,000 sq mi) is considered protected under one of the 14 different designations that includes over 800 distinct areas.

British Columbia contains seven of [Canada's national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_Canada) and National Park Reserves:

- [Glacier National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier_National_Park_\(Canada\))

- [Gulf Islands National Park Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Islands_National_Park_Reserve)

- [Gwaii Haanas National Park Reserve and Haida Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwaii_Haanas_National_Park_Reserve_and_Haida_Heritage_Site)

- [Kootenay National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kootenay_National_Park)

- [Mount Revelstoke National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Revelstoke_National_Park)

- [Pacific Rim National Park Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim_National_Park_Reserve)

- [Yoho National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoho_National_Park)

British Columbia contains a large number of [provincial parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_park), run by BC Parks under the aegis of the Ministry of Environment. British Columbia's provincial parks system is the second largest parks system in Canada, the largest being Canada's National Parks system.

Another tier of parks in British Columbia are [regional parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_park), which are maintained and run by [the province's regional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regional_districts_of_British_Columbia). The Ministry of Forests operates forest recreation sites.

In addition to these areas, over 47,000 square kilometres (18,000 sq mi) of [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land) are protected by the [Agricultural Land Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_Land_Reserve).

### Fauna



[Yoho National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoho_National_Park)

Much of the province is undeveloped, so populations of many mammalian species that have become rare in much of the United States still flourish in British Columbia.[^cite_note-46] Watching animals of various sorts, including a very wide range of birds, has long been popular. Bears ([grizzly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grizzly_bear), [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_black_bear)—including the [Kermode bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermode_bear) or spirit bear) live here, as do [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer), [elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk), [moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose), [caribou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer), [big-horn sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bighorn_sheep), [mountain goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_goat), [marmots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmot), [beavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver), [muskrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskrat), [coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyote), [wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf), [mustelids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustelidae) (such as [wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverine), [badgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badger) and [fishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_\(animal\))), [cougars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar), [eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle), [ospreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osprey), [herons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heron), [Canada geese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_goose), [swans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan), [loons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loon), [hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawk), owls, [ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_raven), [harlequin ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlequin_duck), and many other sorts of ducks. Smaller birds ([robins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_robin), [jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay), [grosbeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosbeak), [chickadees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tit_\(bird\)), and so on) also abound.[^cite_note-47] Murrelets are known from Frederick Island, a small island off the coast of [Haida Gwaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haida_Gwaii).[^cite_note-48]

Many healthy populations of fish are present, including [salmonids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmonidae) such as several species of [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon), [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout), [steelhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_trout), and [char](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvelinus). Besides salmon and trout, sport-fishers in BC also catch [halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halibut), [bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_\(fish\)), and [sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon). On the coast, [harbour seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_seal) and [river otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_river_otter) are common.[^cite_note-49] [Cetacean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacea) species native to the coast include the [orca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orca), [humpback whale](231728.md), [grey whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_whale), [harbour porpoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_porpoise), [Dall's porpoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dall's_porpoise), [Pacific white-sided dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_white-sided_dolphin) and [minke whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minke_whale).

## History

Main article: [History of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_British_Columbia)

### Indigenous societies

Main articles: [Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Pacific_Northwest_Coast), [Indigenous peoples of the Northwest Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Northwest_Plateau), and [The Canadian Crown and Aboriginal peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadian_Crown_and_Aboriginal_peoples)



[ʼNamgis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BCNamgis) *Thunderbird Transformation Mask*, 19th century

The area now known as British Columbia is home to First Nations groups with several indigenous languages. There are more than 200 First Nations in BC. Prior to contact (with non-Aboriginal people), human history is known from oral histories, archaeological investigations, and from early records from explorers encountering societies early in the period.

The arrival of [Paleoindians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoindians) from [Beringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beringia) took place between 20,000 and 12,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Muckle2007-53] [Hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer) families were the main social structure from 10,000 to 5,000 years ago.[^cite_note-BastianMitchell2004-54] The nomadic population lived in non-permanent structures foraging for nuts, berries and edible roots while hunting and trapping larger and small game for food and furs.[^cite_note-BastianMitchell2004-54] Around 5,000 years ago individual groups started to focus on resources available to them locally. Coast Salish peoples had complex land management practices linked to ecosystem health and resilience. Forest gardens on Canada's northwest coast included crabapple, hazelnut, cranberry, wild plum, and wild cherry species.[^cite_note-55] Thus with the passage of time there is a pattern of increasing regional generalization with a more [sedentary lifestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedentary_lifestyle).[^cite_note-BastianMitchell2004-54] These indigenous populations evolved over the next 5,000 years across a large area into many groups with shared traditions and customs.

To the northwest of the province are the peoples of the [Na-Dene languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na-Dene_languages), which include the [Athapaskan-speaking peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabaskan_languages) and the [Tlingit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlingit), who live on the islands of southern Alaska and northern British Columbia. The Na-Dene language group is believed to be linked to the [Yeniseian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeniseian_languages) of Siberia:[^cite_note-BENGTSON-56] the [Dene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dene) of the western Arctic may represent a distinct wave of migration from Asia to North America.[^cite_note-BENGTSON-56] The [Interior of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Interior) is home to the [Salishan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salishan_languages) groups such as the [Shuswap (Secwepemc)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secw%C3%A9pemc), [Okanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syilx) and Athabaskan language groups, primarily the [Dakelh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakelh) (Carrier) and the [Tsilhqotʼin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsilhqot%CA%BCin).[^cite_note-Archives-57]



[Kwakwaka'wakw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwakwaka'wakw) house pole depicting a woman holding an infant, second half of the 19th century

The inlets and valleys of the British Columbia coast shelter large, distinctive populations, such as the [Haida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haida_people), [Kwakwakaʼwakw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwakwaka%CA%BCwakw) and [Nuu-chah-nulth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuu-chah-nulth), sustained by the region's abundant salmon and shellfish.[^cite_note-Archives-57] These peoples developed [complex cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_society) dependent on the [western red cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_red_cedar) that included wooden houses, seagoing whaling and war canoes and elaborately carved [potlatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potlatch) items and [totem poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totem_poles).[^cite_note-Archives-57]

Contact with Europeans brought a series of devastating epidemics of diseases the people had no immunity to.[^cite_note-58] The population dramatically collapsed, culminating in the 1862 smallpox outbreak in Victoria that spread throughout the coast. European settlement did not bode well for the remaining native population of British Columbia. Colonial officials deemed colonists could make better use of the land than the First Nations people, and thus the land should be owned by the colonists.[^cite_note-gilmartin2009-59]<sup>: 120 </sup> To ensure colonists would be able to settle properly and make use of the land, First Nations were forcibly relocated onto [reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reserve), which were often too small to support their way of life.[^cite_note-gilmartin2009-59]<sup>: 120–121 </sup> By the 1930s, British Columbia had over 1500 reserves.[^cite_note-gilmartin2009-59]<sup>: 121 </sup>

### Fur trade and colonial era

Main articles: [Columbia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_District) and [New Caledonia (Canada)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonia_\(Canada\))

See also: [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country) and [Oregon boundary dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_boundary_dispute)

Lands now known as British Columbia were added to the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) during the 19th century. Colonies originally begun with the support of the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) (Vancouver Island, the mainland) were amalgamated, then entered Confederation as British Columbia in 1871 as part of the Dominion of Canada.

During the 1770s, [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) killed at least 30 percent of the Pacific Northwest [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada).[^cite_note-60] This devastating epidemic was the first in a series; the [1862 Pacific Northwest smallpox epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1862_Pacific_Northwest_smallpox_epidemic) killed about half to two-thirds of the native population of what became British Columbia.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-Boyd-62][^cite_note-lange-63]



Fort Victoria in 1845

The arrival of Europeans began around the mid-18th century, as [fur traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) entered the area to harvest [sea otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_otter). While it is thought [Francis Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Drake) may have explored the British Columbian coast in 1579, it was [Juan Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_P%C3%A9rez_Hern%C3%A1ndez) who completed the first documented voyage, which took place in 1774. [Juan Francisco de la Bodega y Quadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Francisco_de_la_Bodega_y_Quadra) explored the coast in 1775. In doing so, Pérez and Quadra reasserted the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) claim for the [Pacific coast](751843.md), first made by [Vasco Núñez de Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_de_Balboa) in 1513.

The explorations of [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook) in 1778 and [George Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Vancouver) in 1792 and 1793 established British jurisdiction over the coastal area north and west of the Columbia River. In 1793, [Alexander Mackenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Mackenzie_\(explorer\)) was the first European to journey across North America overland to the Pacific Ocean, inscribing a stone marking his accomplishment on the shoreline of [Dean Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Channel) near [Bella Coola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Coola,_British_Columbia). His expedition theoretically established British sovereignty inland, and a succession of other fur company explorers charted the maze of rivers and mountain ranges between the Canadian Prairies and the Pacific. Mackenzie and other explorers—notably [John Finlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Finlay_\(fur_trader\)), [Simon Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_\(explorer\)), [Samuel Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Black), and [David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thompson_\(explorer\))—were primarily concerned with extending the [fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade), rather than political considerations. In 1794, by the third of a series of agreements known as the [Nootka Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nootka_Convention), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) conceded its claims of exclusivity in the Pacific. This opened the way for formal claims and colonization by other powers, including Britain, but because of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), there was little British action on its claims in the region until later.

The establishment of [trading posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_post) by the [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company) and the Hudson's Bay Company (HBC), effectively established a permanent British presence in the region. The Columbia District was broadly defined as being south of 54°40′ north latitude, (the southern limit of [Russian America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_colonization_of_the_Americas)), north of Mexican-controlled California, and west of the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains). It was, by the [Anglo-American Convention of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1818), under the "joint occupancy and use" of citizens of the United States and subjects of Britain.[^cite_note-64] This co-occupancy was ended with the [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty) of 1846.

The major supply route was the [York Factory Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Factory_Express) between [Hudson Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Bay) and [Fort Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Vancouver). Some of the early outposts grew into settlements, communities and cities. Among the places in British Columbia that began as fur trading posts are [Fort St. John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_St._John,_British_Columbia) (established 1794); [Hudson's Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Hope) (1805); [Fort Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Nelson,_British_Columbia) (1805); [Fort St. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_St._James) (1806); [Prince George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George,_British_Columbia) (1807); [Kamloops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamloops) (1812); [Fort Langley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Langley) (1827); [Fort Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Victoria_\(British_Columbia\)) (1843); [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale,_British_Columbia) (1848); and [Nanaimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaimo) (1853). Fur company posts that became cities in what is now the United States include [Vancouver, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver,_Washington) (Fort Vancouver), formerly the "capital" of [Hudson's Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) operations in the Columbia District, [Colville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colville,_Washington) and [Walla Walla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walla_Walla,_Washington) (old [Fort Nez Percés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Nez_Perc%C3%A9s)).



[Fort Rupert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Rupert), Vancouver Island, 1851

With the amalgamation of the two fur trading companies in 1821, modern-day British Columbia existed in three fur-trading departments. The bulk of the central and northern interior was organized into the [New Caledonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonia_\(Canada\)) district, administered from [Fort St. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_St._James). The interior south of the [Thompson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_River) watershed and north of the Columbia was organized into the Columbia District, administered from Fort Vancouver on the lower Columbia River. The northeast corner of the province east of the Rockies, known as the Peace River Block, was attached to the much larger [Athabasca District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Athabasca), headquartered in [Fort Chipewyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Chipewyan), in present-day Alberta.

Until 1849, these Pacific Slope districts were unorganized territory within [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America). While operating under the de facto jurisdiction of HBC administrators, the region—unlike [Rupert's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert's_Land) to the north and east—was never a corporate concession. Instead, the company merely held a monopoly to trade with the First Nations inhabitants. This status quo was disrupted by the westward expansion of American exploration and overlapping claims of territorial sovereignty, particularly in the southern [Columbia Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River_drainage_basin) across present-day Washington and [Oregon](26811621.md). In 1846, the [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty) divided the territory along the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north) to the [Strait of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Georgia), with the area south of this boundary (excluding Vancouver Island and the [Gulf Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Islands)) transferred to sole American sovereignty. The [Colony of Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Vancouver_Island) was created in 1849, with Victoria designated as the capital. New Caledonia, as the whole of the mainland rather than just its north-central Interior came to be called, continued to be an unorganized territory of British North America, "administered" by individual HBC trading post managers.

### Colony of British Columbia (1858–1866)

Main articles: [Colony of British Columbia (1858–66)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1858%E2%80%9366\)), [Richard Clement Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Clement_Moody), and [James Douglas (governor)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Douglas_\(governor\))

With the [Fraser Canyon Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Canyon_Gold_Rush) in 1858, an influx of Americans into New Caledonia prompted the [colonial office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Colonies) to designate the mainland as the Colony of British Columbia. When news of the Fraser Canyon Gold Rush reached London, Richard Clement Moody was hand-picked by the [Colonial Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Office), under [Edward Bulwer-Lytton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bulwer-Lytton), to establish British order and to transform the newly established Colony of British Columbia into the British Empire's "bulwark in the farthest west"[^cite_note-Donald_J._Hauka_2003,_p.146-65] and "found a second England on the shores of the Pacific".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarman200771-66] Lytton desired to send to the colony "representatives of the best of British culture, not just a police force": he sought men who possessed "courtesy, high breeding and urbane knowledge of the world"[^cite_note-Scott1983-67]<sup>: 13 </sup> and he decided to send Moody, whom the government considered to be the archetypal "[English gentleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landed_gentry) and British Officer"[^cite_note-Scott1983-67]<sup>: 19 </sup> to lead the [Royal Engineers, Columbia Detachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Engineers,_Columbia_Detachment).

Moody and his family arrived in British Columbia in December 1858.[^cite_note-Richard_Clement_Moody_Obituary,_ICE-13] He was sworn in as the first [lieutenant governor of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_of_British_Columbia) and appointed Chief Commissioner of Lands and Works for British Columbia.[^cite_note-DFB_Richard_Clement_Moody-15] On the advice of Lytton, Moody hired [Robert Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Burnaby) as his personal secretary.[^cite_note-DFB_Richard_Clement_Moody-15]



*Cattle near the Maas* by Dutch painter [Aelbert Cuyp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelbert_Cuyp). Moody likened his vision of the nascent Colony of British Columbia to the pastoral scenes painted by Cuyp.

In British Columbia, Moody "wanted to build a city of beauty in the wilderness" and planned his city as an iconic visual metaphor for British dominance, "styled and located with the objective of reinforcing the authority of the crown and of the robe".[^cite_note-Scott1983-67]<sup>: 26 </sup> Subsequent to the enactment of the *Pre-emption Act* of 1860, Moody settled the [Lower Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Mainland). He selected the site and founded the new capital, [New Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Westminster). He selected the site due to the strategic excellence of its position and the quality of its port.[^cite_note-Scott1983-67]<sup>: 26 </sup> He was also struck by the majestic beauty of the site, writing in his letter to Blackwood,

> The entrance to the Frazer is very striking—Extending miles to the right & left are low marsh lands (apparently of very rich qualities) & yet [fr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic) the Background of Superb Mountains-- Swiss in outline, dark in woods, grandly towering into the clouds there is a sublimity that deeply impresses you. Everything is large and magnificent, worthy of the entrance to the Queen of England's dominions on the Pacific mainland. ... My imagination converted the silent marshes into [Cuyp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyp)-like pictures of horses and cattle lazily fattening in rich meadows in a glowing sunset. ... The water of the deep clear Frazer was of a glassy stillness, not a ripple before us, except when a fish rose to the surface or broods of wild ducks fluttered away.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarman20077-69]



Victoria, 1864

Lord Lytton "forgot the practicalities of paying for clearing and developing the site and the town" and the efforts of Moody's engineers were continuously hampered by insufficient funds, which, together with the continuous opposition of [Governor James Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Douglas_\(governor\)),[^cite_note-Scott1983-67] whom [Thomas Frederick Elliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Frederick_Elliot?action=edit\&redlink=1) described as "like any other fraud",[^cite_note-70] "made it impossible for \[Moody's] design to be fulfilled".[^cite_note-Ormsby-71][^cite_note-heraldicscienceheraldique.com-72][^cite_note-Scott1983-67]<sup>: 27 </sup>

Moody and the Royal Engineers also built an extensive road network, including what would become [Kingsway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsway_\(Vancouver\)), connecting New Westminster to [False Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Creek), the North Road between [Port Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Moody) and New Westminster, and the [Cariboo Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariboo_Road) and [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park).[^cite_note-royalengineers.ca-73] He named [Burnaby Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby_Lake) after his private secretary [Robert Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Burnaby) and named Port Coquitlam's 400-foot "Mary Hill" after his wife. As part of the surveying effort, several tracts were designated "government reserves", which included Stanley Park as a military reserve (a strategic location in case of an American invasion). The *Pre-emption Act* did not specify conditions for distributing the land, so large parcels were snapped up by speculators, including 1,518 hectares (3,750 acres) by Moody himself. For this he was criticized by local newspapermen for [land grabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grabbing). Moody designed the first [coat of arms of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_British_Columbia). Port Moody is named after him. It was established at the end of a trail that connected New Westminster with Burrard Inlet to defend New Westminster from potential attack from the US.

By 1862, the [Cariboo Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariboo_Gold_Rush), attracting an additional 5000 miners, was underway, and Douglas hastened construction of the Great North Road (commonly known now as the [Cariboo Wagon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariboo_Wagon_Road)) up the [Fraser Canyon](1897554.md) to the prospecting region around [Barkerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barkerville). By the time of this gold rush, the character of the colony was changing, as a more stable population of British colonists settled in the region, establishing businesses, opening [sawmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawmill), and engaging in [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) and agriculture. With this increased stability, objections to the colony's absentee governor and the lack of [responsible government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsible_government) began to be vocalized, led by the influential editor of the [New Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Westminster) *British Columbian* and future [premier of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_British_Columbia) [John Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Robson_\(politician\)), who had wanted Richard Clement Moody's office to include that of Governor of British Columbia, to make Douglas obsolete.[^cite_note-Ormsby-71] A series of petitions requesting an assembly were ignored by Douglas and the colonial office until Douglas was eased out of office in 1864. Finally, the colony would have both an assembly and a resident governor.

### Later gold rushes

A series of gold rushes in various parts of the province followed, the largest being the [Cariboo Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariboo_Gold_Rush) in 1862, forcing the colonial administration into deeper debt as it struggled to meet the extensive infrastructure needs of far-flung boom communities like [Barkerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barkerville,_British_Columbia) and [Lillooet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillooet), which sprang up overnight. The Vancouver Island colony was facing financial crises of its own, and pressure to merge the two eventually [succeeded in 1866](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1866%E2%80%9371\)), when the colony of British Columbia was amalgamated with the [Colony of Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Vancouver_Island) to form the [Colony of British Columbia (1866–1871)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1866%E2%80%931871\)), which was, in turn, succeeded by the present day province of British Columbia following the [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) of 1871.

### Rapid growth and development (1860s to 1910s)

Main articles: [Amor De Cosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amor_De_Cosmos), [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation), and [Canada in World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_in_World_War_I)



[Lord Strathcona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Smith,_1st_Baron_Strathcona_and_Mount_Royal) drives the [Last Spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Spike_\(Canadian_Pacific_Railway\)) of the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway), at [Craigellachie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigellachie,_British_Columbia), November 7, 1885. Completion of the [transcontinental railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_railroad) was a condition of British Columbia's entry into [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation).



Memorial to the "last spike" in Craigellachie

The Confederation League led the chorus pressing for the colony to join Canada, which in 1867 had been created out of three British North American colonies (the [Province of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada), Nova Scotia and [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick)), making up four provinces. With the agreement by the Canadian government to extend the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway) to British Columbia and assume the colony's debt, British Columbia became the sixth province to join [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) on July 20, 1871.&#x20;

The Treaty of Washington sent the "[Pig War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_War_\(1859\))", a San Juan Islands border dispute, to arbitration in 1871. In 1903, the province's territory shrank again after the [Alaska boundary dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_boundary_dispute) settled the vague boundary of the [Alaska Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Panhandle).

Population in British Columbia expanded as mining, [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry), agriculture, and [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) developed. Mining activity was notable throughout the Mainland, so a common epithet for it, even after provincehood, was "the Gold Colony".[^cite_note-74] Agriculture attracted settlers to the fertile Fraser Valley. Cattle ranchers and later fruit growers came to the drier grasslands of the Thompson Rivers, the Cariboo, the [Chilcotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilcotin_Country), and the Okanagan. Forestry drew workers to the temperate rainforests of the coast, which was also the locus of a growing fishery.

The completion of the railway in 1885 contributed to the economy, facilitating the transportation of the region's considerable resources to the east. The milltown of Granville, also known as [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown), was selected as the terminus. This prompted the incorporation of the city of Vancouver in 1886. The completion of the [Port of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Vancouver) spurred rapid growth, and in less than fifty years the city surpassed [Winnipeg](100730.md), Manitoba, as the largest in Western Canada. The early decades of the province were ones in which issues of land use—specifically, its settlement and development—were paramount. This included expropriation from First Nations people of their land, control over its resources, as well as the ability to trade in some resources, such as fishing.

Establishing a [labour force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_force) to develop the province was problematic, and British Columbia was a destination of immigration from Europe, China, Japan and India. The influx of a non-[European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) population stimulated resentment from the dominant ethnic groups, resulting in agitation and an attempt to restrict the ability of [Asian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people) to immigrate to British Columbia through the imposition of the [Chinese head tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_head_tax).[^cite_note-75] This resentment culminated in mob attacks against Chinese and Japanese immigrants in Vancouver in 1887 and 1907.

### 20th century

Further information: [Canada in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_in_World_War_II)



*[Wait for Me, Daddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wait_for_Me,_Daddy)*, a photo taken by [Claude P. Dettloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_P._Dettloff) of the [British Columbia Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_British_Columbia_Regiment_\(Duke_of_Connaught's_Own\)_\(RCAC\)) marching in [New Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Westminster), October 1940

In [World War I](4764461.md), the province responded strongly to the call to assist the British Empire against its German foes in French and Belgian battlefields. About 55,570 of the province's 400,000 residents, the highest per-capita rate in Canada, responded to the military's need. About 6,225 men from the province died in combat.[^cite_note-76]

In 1914, a second transcontinental rail line, the [Grand Trunk Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Pacific_Railway), was completed. This opened up the North Coast and [Bulkley Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulkley_Valley) region to new economic opportunities. What had previously been an almost exclusively fur-trading and subsistence economy soon became an area for forestry, farming, and mining. This sector attracted workers from Asia and Europe, leading to a diverse but conflict-ridden society. The early 20th century saw significant interaction between immigrants, [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada), and economic forces. There was a rise in the labour movement, marked by strikes and conflicts such as the [1935 docker's strike at Ballantyne Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ballantyne_Pier) and the [On-to-Ottawa Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-to-Ottawa_Trek). These events underscored tensions between workers and big business, often mediated by the Communist Party. Racial and ethnic relations were strained, with legislation reflecting the era's racial prejudices, notably against Asian immigrants and First Nations. The early and mid-20th century was marred by incidents like the [Komagata Maru incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komagata_Maru_incident), highlighting anti-Asian sentiment.

The interwar period and World War II introduced significant changes, including [prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition) and its eventual repeal, and the [internment of Japanese Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Canadians). The post-war era saw coalition governments and a booming economy, spearheaded by infrastructure projects and industrial expansion. The [Social Credit Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Social_Credit_Party), under [W.A.C. Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W.A.C._Bennett), dominated BC politics, initiating major projects and laying the groundwork for future economic growth. The 1970s and 1980s brought economic challenges and political shifts, culminating in the [Expo 86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_86) world's fair and the end of Social Credit dominance. This period also saw significant social movements, such as [Operation Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Solidarity). There was a transition to [New Democratic Party](19283982.md) governance in the 1990s, focusing on environmental conservation and economic struggles. In its second term especially, the NDP government faced political scandals, such as the [fast ferry scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_ferry_scandal), that ultimately contributed to its downfall.

### 21st century



The cauldron of the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) in Vancouver

In the [2001 provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_British_Columbia_general_election), [Gordon Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Campbell_\(Canadian_politician\))'s Liberals defeated the NDP, gaining 77 out of 79 total seats in the provincial legislature. Campbell instituted reforms and removed some of the NDP's policies, along with selling off the previous government's "fast ferries", lowering income taxes, and instituting the controversial long-term lease of [BC Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Rail) to [Canadian National Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_National_Railway). Campbell led his party to victory in the [2005 provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_British_Columbia_general_election) against a substantially strengthened NDP opposition and won a third term in the [2009 provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_British_Columbia_general_election).

The province won a bid to host the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) in [Vancouver](32706.md) and [Whistler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistler,_British_Columbia). In 2003, Vancouver's residents had voted in a referendum accepting the responsibilities of the host city should it win its bid. 64 percent of residents voted in favour of hosting.[^cite_note-77] After the Olympic joy faded, Campbell's popularity fell. His management style, implementation of the [Harmonized Sales Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonized_Sales_Tax) (HST) despite election promises not to introduce it, and cancellation of the [BC Rail corruption trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Legislature_raids) led to low approval ratings and loss of caucus support: he resigned in November 2010.[^cite_note-78] In early 2011, former deputy premier [Christy Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christy_Clark) became leader of the Liberals. Early Clark government actions included raising the minimum wage, creating a new statutory holiday in February called "[Family Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Day_\(Canada\))", and pushing the development of BC's [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) industry. In the lead-up to the [2013 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_British_Columbia_general_election), the Liberals lagged behind the NDP by a double-digit gap in the polls but were able to achieve a surprise victory, winning a majority and making Clark the first woman to lead a party to victory in BC.[^cite_note-79] Her government went on to balance the budget, implement changes to liquor laws and continue with the question of the proposed [Enbridge Northern Gateway Pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enbridge_Northern_Gateway_Pipelines). In [the 2017 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_British_Columbia_general_election), the NDP formed a minority government with the support of the Green Party through a [confidence and supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) agreement. In July 2017, NDP leader John Horgan was sworn in as a premier. Clark resigned and [Andrew Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wilkinson) became leader of the BC Liberals. In the [2020 British Columbia general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_British_Columbia_general_election), the NDP won 57 seats and formed a majority government. Wilkinson resigned as the leader of the BC Liberals.

British Columbia has been significantly affected by demographic changes within Canada and around the world. Vancouver was a major destination for many immigrants from [Hong Kong](13404.md) who left the former UK colony prior to [its handover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Hong_Kong) to China. Trends of urbanization mean the [Greater Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver) area now includes 51 percent of the province's population, followed by [Greater Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Victoria,_British_Columbia) with 8 percent. These two metropolitan regions have dominated the demographics of BC.

By 2018, housing prices in Vancouver were the second-least affordable in the world.[^cite_note-80] Many experts point to evidence of money-laundering from China as a contributing factor. The high price of residential real estate has led to the implementation of an empty homes tax, a housing speculation and vacancy tax, and a foreign buyers' tax on housing.[^cite_note-81] The net number of people coming to BC from other provinces in 2016 was almost four times larger than in 2012 and BC was the largest net recipient of [interprovincial migrants in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interprovincial_migration_in_Canada).[^cite_note-82] In 2023, British Columbia experienced a net population loss of 8,624; a substantial percentage of which were people who moved to [Alberta](717.md).[^cite_note-83]

By 2021, the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) had had [a major effect on the province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_British_Columbia),[^cite_note-84] with over 2,000 deaths and 250,000 confirmed cases. However, the [COVID-19 vaccine](63319438.md) reduced the spread, with 78 percent of people in BC over the age of five having been fully vaccinated. Also in 2021 but unrelated to COVID-19, the [unmarked gravesites of hundreds of Indigenous children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Indian_residential_school_gravesites) were discovered at three former [Indian residential schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Indian_residential_school_system) ([Kamloops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamloops_Indian_Residential_School), [St. Eugene's Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kootenay_Indian_Residential_School), [Kuper Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuper_Island_Indian_Residential_School)).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-\:1-86]

Wildfires burned more than 1,000,000 hectares (2,500,000 acres) of British Columbia in 2017, 2018, 2023, and 2024. Wildfires in 2023 burned nearly 3,000,000 hectares (7,400,000 acres) and incurred a cost of more than a billion dollars.[^cite_note-87]

Population density map of British Columbia

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_British_Columbia)



[Canada Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Place) in Downtown Vancouver



The entrance to [Telus Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telus_Garden)

BC's economy is diverse, with service-producing industries accounting for the largest portion of the province's GDP.[^cite_note-101] It is the terminus of two transcontinental railways, and the site of 27 major marine cargo and passenger terminals. Though less than 5 percent of its vast 944,735 square kilometres (364,764 sq mi) land is [arable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land), the province is agriculturally rich (particularly in the [Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Valley) and [Okanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanagan) valleys), because of milder weather near the coast and in certain sheltered southern valleys.&#x20;

Its climate encourages [outdoor recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outdoor_recreation) and tourism, though its economic mainstay has long been [resource extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_extraction), principally logging, farming, and mining. Vancouver, the province's largest city, serves as the headquarters of many western-based natural resource companies. It also benefits from a strong housing market and a per capita income well above the national average. While the coast of British Columbia and some valleys in the south-central part of the province have mild weather, the majority of its land mass experiences a cold-winter-temperate climate similar to the rest of Canada. The [Northern Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_of_British_Columbia) region has a [subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate) with very cold winters. The climate of Vancouver is by far the mildest winter climate of the major Canadian cities, with nighttime January temperatures averaging above the freezing point.[^cite_note-102]

British Columbia has a history of being a resource dominated economy, centred on the forestry industry but also with fluctuating importance in mining. Employment in the resource sector has fallen steadily as a percentage of employment, and new jobs are mostly in the construction and retail/service sectors. It now has the highest percentage of service industry jobs in the west, constituting 72 percent of industry, compared to the 60 percent Western Canadian average.[^cite_note-103] The largest section of this employment is in finance, insurance, real estate and corporate management; however, many areas outside of metropolitan areas are still heavily reliant on resource extraction. With its film industry known as [Hollywood North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_North), the Vancouver region is the third-largest feature [film production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmmaking) location in North America, after [Los Angeles](18110.md) and [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-104]

The economic history of British Columbia is replete with tales of [dramatic upswings and downswings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cycle). This [boom and bust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_and_bust) pattern has influenced the politics, culture and business climate of the province. Economic activity related to mining in particular has widely fluctuated with changes in commodity prices over time, with documented costs to community health.[^cite_note-105]

In 2020, British Columbia had the third-largest GDP in Canada, with a GDP of \$309 billion and a GDP per capita of \$60,090.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] British Columbia's [debt-to-GDP ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt-to-GDP_ratio) is edging up to 15.0 percent in [fiscal year](5752099.md) 2019–20, and it is expected to reach 16.1 percent by 2021–22.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] British Columbia's economy experienced strong growth in recent years with a total growth rate of 9.6% from 2017 to 2021, a growth rate that was second in the country.[^cite_note-110]

## Government and politics

Main articles: [Politics of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_British_Columbia), [Government of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_British_Columbia), [List of regional districts of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regional_districts_of_British_Columbia), and [Monarchy in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_in_British_Columbia)



The [British Columbia Parliament Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Parliament_Buildings) in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia)



Coat of arms' [escutcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escutcheon_\(heraldry\)) of the current [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_British_Columbia)

The [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_British_Columbia), [Wendy Lisogar-Cocchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy_Lisogar-Cocchia), is the Crown's representative in the province. During the absence of the lieutenant governor, the Governor in Council ([federal Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Canada)) may appoint an administrator to execute the duties of the office. This is usually the [chief justice of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Court_of_Appeal).[^cite_note-111] British Columbia is divided into *[regional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_district)* to better enable municipalities and rural areas to work together at a regional level.

British Columbia has a [93-member elected legislative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_British_Columbia), elected by the [plurality voting system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting_system), though from 2003 to 2009 there was significant debate about switching to a [single transferable vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_transferable_vote) system called [BC-STV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC-STV). The government of the day appoints ministers for various portfolios, what are officially part of the [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_British_Columbia), of whom the [province's premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_British_Columbia) is chair.



[David Eby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Eby) is [premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_British_Columbia), BC's [head of government](37570.md).

The province is currently governed by the [British Columbia New Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_New_Democratic_Party) (BC NDP) under [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_\(Canada\)) [David Eby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Eby). The 2017 provincial election saw the Liberal Party take 43 seats, the NDP take 41, and the [British Columbia Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Green_Party) take 3. No party met the minimum of 44 seats for a majority, therefore leading to the first minority government since 1953. Following the election, the Greens entered into negotiations with both the Liberals and NDP, eventually announcing they would support an NDP minority government.&#x20;

Previously, the right-of-centre [British Columbia Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Liberal_Party) governed the province for 16 years between 2001 and 2017, and won the largest landslide election in British Columbia history in 2001, with 77 of 79 seats. The legislature became more evenly divided between the Liberals and NDP following the 2005 (46 Liberal seats of 79) and 2009 (49 Liberal seats of 85) provincial elections. The NDP and its predecessor the [Co-operative Commonwealth Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-operative_Commonwealth_Federation) (CCF) have been the main opposition force to right-wing parties since the 1930s and have governed with majority governments in 1972–1975, 1991–2001 and since 2020, with a minority government from 2017 to 2020. The [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Canada) plays a larger role in the politics of British Columbia than Green parties do in most other jurisdictions in Canada. After a breakthrough election in 2001 (12.39 percent), the party's vote share declined (2005 – 9.17 percent, 2009 – 8.09 percent, 2013 – 8.13 percent) before increasing again to a record high of 16.84 percent at the 2017 election.

The British Columbia Liberal Party is not related to the federal Liberal Party and does not share the same ideology. Instead, the BC Liberal party is a rather diverse coalition, made up of the remnants of the Social Credit Party, many [federal Liberals](149536.md), [federal Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada), and those who would otherwise support right-of-centre or free enterprise parties. In 2022, [Kevin Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Falcon) was elected leader of the BC Liberals, promising to rename the party in an effort to distance themselves from their federal counterparts. In 2023, the party rebranded as [BC United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_United). Historically, there have commonly been [third parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_party_\(politics\)) present in the legislature, including the Liberals themselves from 1952 to 1975. The [BC Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_British_Columbia) is the current third party in British Columbia, with three seats in the legislature.

Prior to the rise of the Liberal Party, British Columbia's main political party was the BC Social Credit Party, which governed the province for 20 years. While sharing some ideology with the subsequent Liberal government, they were more right-wing, although they undertook nationalization of various important monopolies, notably BC Hydro and BC Ferries.



The meeting chamber of the Legislative Assembly

British Columbia is known for having politically active labour [unions](17626.md) who have traditionally supported the NDP or its predecessor, the CCF.

British Columbia's political history is typified by scandal and a cast of colourful characters, beginning with colonial-era land scandals and abuses of power by early officials, such as those that led to [McGowan's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGowan's_War) in 1858–59. Notable scandals in Social Credit years included the [Robert Bonner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bonner_\(politician\)) Affair and the [Fantasy Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_Gardens) scandal which forced Premier Bill Vander Zalm to resign and ended the Social Credit era. NDP scandals included Bingogate, which brought down NDP Premier Mike Harcourt, and the alleged scandal named Casinogate which drove NDP Premier Glen Clark to resign.&#x20;

A variety of scandals plagued the 2001–2017 Liberal government, including Premier Gordon Campbell's arrest for drunk driving in Maui and the resignation of various cabinet ministers because of conflict-of-interest allegations. A [raid on the Parliament Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Legislature_Raids) on December 28, 2003,[^cite_note-112] in Victoria, including the Premier's Office, resulted in charges only for ministerial aides, although key cabinet members from the time resigned. Campbell eventually resigned in late 2010 due to opposition to his government's plan to introduce a [Harmonized Sales Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonized_Sales_Tax) (HST) and was replaced by [Christy Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christy_Clark) as premier in the 2011 BC Liberal leadership election.

British Columbia is underrepresented in the [Senate of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada), leading Premier Christy Clark to refuse to cooperate with the federal government's reforms for senate appointments to be made based on the recommendations of an advisory board that would use non-partisan criteria. Hours after that plan was unveiled in Ottawa on December 3, 2015, Clark issued a statement that it did "not address what's been wrong with the Senate since the beginning".[^cite_note-113]

The imbalance in representation in that House is apparent when considering population size. The six senators from BC constitute only one for every 775,000 people vs. one for every 75,000 in Prince Edward Island, which has four senators. Nova Scotia and New Brunswick have much smaller populations than BC, yet each has ten senators according to a Global News summary.[^cite_note-Baldrey-114] Correcting this imbalance would require a constitutional amendment, but that is unlikely to be supported by the Atlantic provinces.[^cite_note-Baldrey-114]

## Transportation

Transportation played a huge role in British Columbia's history. The Rocky Mountains and the ranges west of them constituted a significant obstacle to overland travel until the completion of the transcontinental railway in 1885. The Peace River Canyon through the Rocky Mountains was the route the earliest explorers and fur traders used. Fur trade routes were only marginally used for access to British Columbia through the mountains. Travel from the rest of Canada before 1885 meant the difficulty of overland travel via the United States, around [Cape Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Horn) or overseas from Asia. Nearly all travel and freight to and from the region occurred via the Pacific Ocean, primarily through the ports of Victoria and New Westminster.

Until the 1930s, rail was the only means of overland travel to and from the rest of Canada; travellers using motor vehicles needed to journey through the United States. With the construction of the Inter-Provincial Highway in 1932, now known as the [Crowsnest Pass Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowsnest_Highway), and later the [Trans-Canada Highway](70775.md), road transportation evolved into the preferred mode of overland travel to and from the rest of Canada.

As of 2021, the number of [electric vehicles sold in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_electric_vehicles_in_British_Columbia), as a percentage of total vehicle sales, was the highest of any Canadian province or US state.[^cite_note-116]

### Roads and highways

Main article: [List of British Columbia provincial highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_Columbia_provincial_highways)



The [Alex Fraser Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Fraser_Bridge) on [Highway 91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Highway_91) between Richmond and Delta

Because of its size and rugged, varying topography, British Columbia requires thousands of kilometres of provincial highways to connect its communities. British Columbia's roads systems were notoriously poorly maintained and dangerous until a concentrated program of improvement was initiated in the 1950s and 1960s. There are now [freeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway) in Greater Victoria, the Lower Mainland, and Central Interior of the province. Much of the rest of the province, where traffic volumes are generally low, is accessible by well-maintained generally high-mobility two-lane [arterial highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arterial_road) with additional passing lanes in mountainous areas and usually only a few stop-controlled intersections outside the main urban areas.



British Columbia Highway 1 near Brentwood, Burnaby

A couple of busy intercity corridors outside Greater Vancouver feature more heavily signalized limited-mobility arterial highways that are mostly four-lane and often divided by portable median [traffic barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_barrier). [Highway 1](633679.md) on [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island) and Highway 97 through the [Okanagan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanagan_Valley) are medium- to high-volume roadways with variable posted speeds that range from 50 km/h (31 mph) to maximums just slightly lower than the principal [grade-separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade-separated) highways.[^cite_note-BC_highways-117]

Numerous [traffic lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_light) operate in place of [interchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_\(road\)) on both arterials as long-term cost-cutting measures. Signalization along both these highways is heaviest through urban areas and along inter-urban sections where traffic volumes are similar to and sometimes higher than the freeways, but where funding is not available for upgrades to interchanges or construction of high-mobility alternative routes or bypasses. The building and maintenance of provincial highways is the responsibility of the [British Columbia Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transportation_and_Infrastructure_\(British_Columbia\)).[^cite_note-BC_highways-117]

There are only five major routes to the rest of Canada. From south to north they are: [BC Highway 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_provincial_highway_3) through the [Crowsnest Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowsnest_Pass), the [Vermilion Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermilion_Pass) (Highway 93 in both [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Highway_93) and [Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Highway_93)), the [Kicking Horse Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kicking_Horse_Pass), the latter being used by the Trans-Canada Highway entering Alberta through [Banff National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banff_National_Park), the [Yellowhead Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowhead_Highway) (16) through [Jasper National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasper_National_Park), and [Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Highway_2) through [Dawson Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson_Creek). There are also several highway crossings to the adjoining American states of Washington, Idaho, and Montana. The longest highway is Highway 97, running 2,081 kilometres (1,293 mi) from the British Columbia-Washington border at [Osoyoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osoyoos,_British_Columbia) north to [Watson Lake, Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_Lake,_Yukon) and which includes the British Columbia portion of the [Alaska Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Highway).

### Public transit



[SkyTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTrain_\(Vancouver\)) is the rail rapid transit system that serves Metro Vancouver.



[Trolley buses in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolley_buses_in_Vancouver)

Prior to 1979, surface [public transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) in the Vancouver and Victoria metropolitan areas was administered by BC Hydro, the provincially owned electricity utility. Subsequently, the province established [BC Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Transit) to oversee and operate all municipal transportation systems. In 1998, the Greater Vancouver Transportation Authority, now [TransLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransLink_\(British_Columbia\)), a separate authority for routes within the [Metro Vancouver Regional District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver_Regional_District), was established. Some smaller island communities, such as [Gabriola Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriola_Island)[^cite_note-118] and, formerly, [Pender Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pender_Island)[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] operate routes independent of BC Transit or TransLink.&#x20;

In 2018, BC Transit expanded to provide intercity routes,[^cite_note-121] particularly in the Northern region of British Columbia. Other intercity routes were introduced connecting southern communities in preparation of the cancellation of [Greyhound Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Canada)'s pullout from Western Canada,[^cite_note-122] though options for intercity bus travel are still extremely limited.

Public transit in British Columbia consists mainly of diesel buses, although Vancouver is also serviced by a fleet of [trolley buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolley_bus). Several experimental buses are being tested such as [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle) buses that have both gasoline and electric engines. Additionally, there are CNG-fuelled buses being tested and used in Nanaimo and Kamloops systems.[^cite_note-123] British Columbia also tested a fleet of Hydrogen-fuelled buses for the Vancouver-Whistler Winter Olympics in 2010.[^cite_note-124]

TransLink operates [SkyTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTrain_\(Vancouver\)), an automated [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system serving the cities of Vancouver, Burnaby, New Westminster, Surrey, Richmond, Coquitlam, and Port Moody. In 2009, the Canada Line SkyTrain was completed, linking Vancouver International Airport and the city of Richmond to downtown Vancouver bringing the total to three operating metro lines.

A new extension to Coquitlam and [Port Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Moody) (the [Evergreen Extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen_Extension) of the [Millennium Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Line)) was completed in December 2016. Construction of an extension of the Millennium Line westwards through Vancouver to Arbutus Street began in February 2021,[^cite_note-125] with future plans to extend the line farther west from [Arbutus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbutus_station) to the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia).[^cite_note-126] Fare gates have been added to all existing stations, though in the past, SkyTrain used a proof of payment honour system. In the capital city of Victoria, BC Transit and the provincial government's infrastructure ministry are working together to create a bus rapid transit from the Westshore communities to downtown Victoria.[^cite_note-127] In [Kamloops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamloops), there is a bus rapid transit GPS trial underway to see how bus rapid transit affects smaller cities, rather than larger ones, like Victoria and Vancouver.[^cite_note-128]

A CPR train traversing the [Stoney Creek Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoney_Creek_Bridge)

Rail development expanded greatly in the decades after the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway) was completed, in 1885, and was the chief mode of long-distance surface transportation until the expansion and improvement of the provincial highways system began in the 1950s. Two major routes through the [Yellowhead Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowhead_Pass) competed with the Canadian Pacific Railway – the [Grand Trunk Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Pacific_Railway), terminating at Prince Rupert, and the [Canadian Northern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Northern_Railway), terminating at Vancouver.

The [British Columbia Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Electric_Railway) provided rail services in Victoria and Vancouver between the nineteenth century and mid twentieth century.

The [Pacific Great Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Rail) line supplemented this service, providing a north–south route between interior resource communities and the coast. The Pacific Great Eastern (later known as British Columbia Railway and now owned by Canadian National Railway) connects Fort St James, Fort Nelson, and [Tumbler Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbler_Ridge,_British_Columbia) with [North Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vancouver_\(city\)). The E\&N Railway, rebranded as the [Island Rail Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Rail_Corridor), formerly served the commercial and passenger train markets of Vancouver Island. Service along the route is now minimal. Vancouver Island was host to the [last logging railway in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englewood_Railway) until its closure in 2017.

Current passenger services in British Columbia are limited. [Via Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Rail) operates 10 long-distance trains per week on two lines.[^cite_note-129] Local services are limited to two regions, with [TransLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransLink_\(British_Columbia\)) providing rapid transit and commuter services in the [Lower Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Mainland) and by the [Seton Lake Indian Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seton_Lake_Indian_Band) South of [Lillooet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillooet) with the [Kaoham Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaoham_Shuttle). [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) runs international passenger service between Vancouver, [Seattle](11388236.md), and intermediate points.[^cite_note-130]

Several [heritage railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_railway) operate within the province, including the [White Pass and Yukon Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Pass_and_Yukon_Route) that runs between [Alaska](624.md) and the [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon) via British Columbia.

### Water



*Spirit of Vancouver Island* S-class ferry

[BC Ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Ferries) was established as a provincial crown corporation in 1960 to provide passenger and vehicle ferry service between Vancouver Island and the Lower Mainland as a cheaper and more reliable alternative to the service operated by the Canadian Pacific Railway and other private operators. It now operates 25 routes among the [islands of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_British_Columbia), and between the islands and the mainland.&#x20;

Ferry service to Washington is offered by the [Washington State Ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Ferries) (between [Sidney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney,_British_Columbia) and [Anacortes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anacortes,_Washington)) and [Black Ball Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Coho) (between Victoria and [Port Angeles, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Angeles,_Washington)). Ferry service over inland lakes and rivers is provided by the [Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Ministry_of_Transportation_and_Infrastructure). Other coastal ferries are operated privately.

The [SeaBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaBus) ferry connects [Lonsdale Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonsdale_Quay) in the [City of North Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_North_Vancouver) and [Waterfront station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfront_station_\(Vancouver\)) in [Vancouver](32706.md).

Commercial ocean transport is of vital importance. Major ports are at Vancouver, [Roberts Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberts_Bank) (near [Tsawwassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsawwassen,_British_Columbia)), Prince Rupert, and Victoria.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

Vancouver, Victoria, and Prince Rupert are also major ports of call for [cruise ships](314855.md). In 2007, a large maritime [container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization) was opened in Prince Rupert with an inland sorting port in Prince George.

### Air

Main article: [List of airports in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_British_Columbia)

There are over 200 airports throughout British Columbia, the major ones being the [Vancouver International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Airport), the [Victoria International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_International_Airport), the [Kelowna International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelowna_International_Airport), and the [Abbotsford International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbotsford_International_Airport), the first three of which each served over 1,000,000 passengers in 2005. As of 2017, Vancouver International Airport is the [2nd busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Canada) in the country and the second biggest International Gateway on the west coast (after Los Angeles) with an estimated 26.4 million travellers passing through in 2019.[^cite_note-133]

## Arts and culture

See also: [List of museums in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_British_Columbia)

### Visual arts

See also: [Northwest Coast art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Coast_art)



[*Church at Yuquot Village*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Indian_Church_\(painting\)) by Emily Carr (1929)

The earliest known visual art produced in the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest), and what would become British Columbia, was by First Nations such as the Coast Salish, Haida, [Heiltsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiltsuk), and Tsimshian, among others. Such Indigenous work comes particularly in the form of [woodcarving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodcarving), as seen in [totem poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totem_poles), [transformation masks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_mask), and [canoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoes), as well as [textile arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_arts) like [Chilkat weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilkat_weaving) and [button blankets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Button_blankets). Traditional Indigenous art of the Pacific Northwest is typically distinguished by the [formline style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formline_art), which is defined as "continuous, flowing, curvilinear lines that turn, swell and diminish in a prescribed manner. They are used for figure outlines, internal design elements and in abstract compositions."[^cite_note-134]

[Western styles and forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_art) were introduced to the region through the establishment of British North American settlements in the late 18th century. Notable English-Canadian artists of 19th and early 20th century British Columbia include architect [Francis Rattenbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Rattenbury), designer [James Blomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blomfield), and painter [Emily Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Carr).

Vancouver's [art scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_scene) was dominated by [lyrical abstraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyrical_abstraction) and [surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealist_art) landscape painting in the mid-20th century through such artists as [B. C. Binning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._C._Binning), [Jack Shadbolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Shadbolt), [Gordon A. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_A._Smith), [Takao Tanabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takao_Tanabe), [Don Jarvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Jarvis), and [Toni Onley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Onley). In the following decades, the city would undergo more artistic diversification with the emergence of [conceptual art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_art), [communication art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_design), [video art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_art), and performance art.[^cite_note-135]

The [Vancouver School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_School) of [conceptual photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_photography) encompasses a cohort of Vancouver-based artists who gained notoriety in the 1980s. This school is generally considered to include artists [Jeff Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Wall), [Ian Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Wallace_\(artist\)), [Ken Lum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Lum), [Roy Arden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Arden), [Stan Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Douglas), and [Rodney Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_Graham).[^cite_note-136]

Vancouver maintains roughly 350 works of outdoor public art.[^cite_note-137] Some notable works include *[A-maze-ing Laughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-maze-ing_Laughter)*, *[Digital Orca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Orca)*, *[Girl in a Wetsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_in_a_Wetsuit)*, *[Angel of Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_of_Victory)*, *[The Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birds_\(sculpture\))*, and the [Brockton Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brockton_Point) totem poles.

### Performing arts



The Vancouver Symphony Orchestra at the Orpheum concert hall, 2019

British Columbia is home to the [Vancouver Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Opera), the [City Opera of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Opera_of_Vancouver), [Ballet BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_BC), [contemporary dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_dance) companies [Holy Body Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Body_Tattoo), [Kidd Pivot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidd_Pivot), [Mascall Dance Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Mascall), and [butoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butoh) dance troupe [Kokoro Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokoro_Dance). It is also the home province for a plethora of independent [theatre companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_companies), including the [Arts Club Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_Club_Theatre_Company), the Shakespearean [Bard on the Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_on_the_Beach), and [Theatre Under the Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Under_the_Stars_\(Vancouver\)). Performing arts venues include the [Queen Elizabeth Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Theatre), the [Orpheum Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheum_\(Vancouver\)), and the [Royal Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Theatre_\(Victoria,_British_Columbia\)), among others.

#### Music

See also: [Music of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Vancouver) and [List of colleges in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_in_British_Columbia)

British Columbia is the third largest music-producing province in Canada and the local music industry generates an estimated yearly revenue of \$265 million.[^cite_note-138] The province is home to the [Vancouver Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Symphony_Orchestra), the [Okanagan Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanagan_Symphony_Orchestra), the [Vancouver Metropolitan Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Metropolitan_Orchestra), the [Vancouver Youth Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Youth_Symphony_Orchestra), the [Richmond Delta Youth Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Delta_Youth_Orchestra), and the [Victoria Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Symphony).&#x20;

Some important [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music) acts include bands such as [Spirit of the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_of_the_West), [Theory of a Deadman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_a_Deadman), [Trooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trooper_\(band\)), [Gob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gob_\(band\)), and [The New Pornographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Pornographers), and solo artists such as [Bryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Adams), [Carly Rae Jepsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carly_Rae_Jepsen), [Mac DeMarco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_DeMarco), [Michael Bublé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bubl%C3%A9), [Nelly Furtado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelly_Furtado), and [Diana Krall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Krall). [Music festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_festivals) in BC have included the [Squamish Valley Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squamish_Valley_Music_Festival), [Shambhala Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shambhala_Music_Festival), and [Pemberton Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemberton_Music_Festival).[^cite_note-139]

### Cuisine

See also: [Canadian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_cuisine), [Cuisine of the Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_the_Pacific_Northwest), and [Rocky Mountain cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_cuisine)



The BC roll is a kind of sushi containing barbecued salmon and cucumber

British Columbian cuisine is commonly associated with healthy living, [fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_cuisine), fresh local ingredients, and innovation.[^cite_note-Iconic_Foods_of_British_Columbia-140] It can be divided into two broadly-defined traditions: [cuisine associated with the west coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest_cuisine), which incorporates a variety of seafood elements, and [cuisine associated with the interior of the province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_cuisine), which embraces local [game meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_meat), [farm-to-table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm-to-table) produce, and methods of [curing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curing_\(food_preservation\)) and [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_\(cooking\)). Seafood is an important staple of the province's local food culture due to its proximity to the Pacific Ocean, as well as the region's numerous rivers and lakes. BC is known for several unique dishes and is a producer of fruit, wine,[^cite_note-Frommers_r561-141] and cheese.[^cite_note-2022_o454-142]

Seafoods of British Columbia include [sushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushi) ([BC roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_roll), [dynamite roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamite_roll), [California roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_roll)), [dungeness crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeness_crab) (boiled, [tacos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacos)), [spot prawns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandalus_platyceros), wild [pacific salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_salmon) (smoked, candied, [teriyaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teriyaki), [chowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowder), [sandwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwich)), and [halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halibut) ([baked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baked), lemon ginger), as well as [delicacies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delicacy) like [white sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_sturgeon) caviar and [geoduck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoduck).[^cite_note-Must_Try_British_Columbia_Recipes-143][^cite_note-Iconic_Foods_of_British_Columbia-140][^cite_note-Taste_Atlas-144]



Nanaimo bars originate from the city of [Nanaimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaimo) and consist of a crumb and nut base, [custard](62149.md) middle, and [ganache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganache) top layer

British Columbia is also home to numerous unique non-seafood culinary staples. Some dishes include [borscht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borscht), [Salt Spring Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Spring_Island) lamb, [Japadog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japadog) street food, and [Butter chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_chicken) pizza. Some unique pastries include apple cranberry [cinnamon buns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon_buns), [Nanaimo bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaimo_bars), and Victoria creams.[^cite_note-Iconic_Foods_of_British_Columbia-140][^cite_note-Must_Try_British_Columbia_Recipes-143][^cite_note-British_Columbia_Food_and_Drink-145] British Columbia also produces several distinct local cheeses, such as kabritt, Castle Blue, and Comox Brie.[^cite_note-Taste_Atlas-144] The [London Fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_fog_\(beverage\)) tea [latte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latte) was invented in Vancouver and remains a popular beverage among coffee shops in the Pacific Northwest and Western Canada; it is referred to as a "Vancouver Fog" in Scotland.[^cite_note-146]

The [Okanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanagan) produces many unique fruits originating from the region, including [Ambrosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrosia_apple) and [Spartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartan_apple) apples, [Stella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_cherry) and [Skeena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeena_cherry) cherries, and [Corontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_\(grape\)) grapes. Other fruits grown in the province include [peaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaches), [pears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pears), [plums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plums), [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricots), [strawberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberries), [blackberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackberries), [cranberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranberries), and [loganberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loganberries).[^cite_note-British_Columbia_Food_and_Drink-145]

British Columbia is renowned for its wine production. The primary wine-producing regions include the Okanagan, the [Similkameen Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similkameen_Valley), Vancouver Island, the [Gulf Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Islands), and the [Fraser Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Valley). As of November 2014, there are 280 licensed grape wineries and 929 vineyards.[^cite_note-147]

## Outdoor life and athletics



Ice sailing in [Whistler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistler,_British_Columbia)



Shoreline Trail in Victoria

Given its varied mountainous terrain and its coasts, lakes, rivers, and forests, British Columbia has long been enjoyed for pursuits like hiking and camping, [rock climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_climbing) and mountaineering, hunting and fishing.

Water sports, both motorized and non-motorized, are enjoyed in many places. [Sea kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_kayak) opportunities abound on the British Columbia coast with its [fjords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord). [Whitewater rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewater_rafting) and [kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayak) are popular on many inland rivers. Sailing and [sailboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsurfing) are widely enjoyed.

In winter, [cross-country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing) and [telemark skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemark_skiing) are much enjoyed, and in recent decades high-quality [downhill skiing](39576.md) has been developed in the Coast Mountain range and the Rockies, as well as in the southern areas of the Shuswap Highlands and the [Columbia Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Mountains). [Snowboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowboarding) has mushroomed in popularity since the early 1990s. The [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) downhill events were held in the [Whistler Blackcomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistler_Blackcomb) area of the province, while the indoor events were conducted in the Vancouver area.

In Vancouver, Victoria and some other cities, opportunities for [joggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogging) and bicyclists have been developed. Cross-country bike touring has been popular since the [ten-speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_gearing) bike became available many years ago. Since the advent of the more robust [mountain bike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_bike), trails in more rugged and wild places have been developed for them. A 2016 poll on global biking website Pinkbike rated BC as the top destination mountain bikers would like to ride.[^cite_note-148] Some of the province's retired rail beds have been converted and maintained for hiking, biking, and cross-country skiing. [Longboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longboarding) is also a popular activity because of the hilly geography of the region.

In certain areas, there are businesses, non-profit societies, or municipal governments dedicated to promoting [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) in their region. A number of British Columbia farmers offer visitors to combine tourism with farm work, for example, through the [WWOOF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWOOF) Canada program.[^cite_note-149]

## Education



[Hatley Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatley_Castle) on the campus of [Royal Roads University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Roads_University)

### K-12 education

See also: [Education in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_British_Columbia)

British Columbia is home to a comprehensive education system consisting of public schools and independent schools, overseen by the provincial Ministry of Education. The public school system has 59 anglophone school districts and one francophone school district, the *[Conseil scolaire francophone de la Colombie-Britannique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_scolaire_francophone_de_la_Colombie-Britannique),* which operates French-language public schools throughout the province.[^cite_note-150] The anglophone school districts are governed by school board trustees who are directly elected by the school district's residents.&#x20;

86 percent of students are enrolled in the public school system. British Columbia has one of the highest shares of independent school enrolment among Canadian provinces, at 14 percent of the student population, due to its relatively generous funding model; most independent schools receive 50 percent of the operating funding that their public counterparts receive from the government. Less than 1 percent of students are home schooled.

Like most other provinces in Canada, education is compulsory from ages 6 to 16 (grades 1–10). The vast majority of students remain in school until they graduate from high school ([grade 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_grade)) at the age of 18. In order to graduate with a graduation certificate, known as a Dogwood Diploma in BC, students must take a minimum of 80 course credits during grades 10 to 12. These credits include a variety of required courses (e.g. in language arts, social studies, mathematics, and science), as well as elective courses.[^cite_note-151]

Academic achievement in British Columbia is relatively good, although it has been slipping in recent years by some measures. In 2020, 86 percent of students in British Columbia graduated from high school within six years of entering grade 8.[^cite_note-152] According to the 2018 Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) scores, students in British Columbia scored the second highest in reading ability, fourth highest in mathematic prowess, and fourth highest in science knowledge of the 10 Canadian provinces, although these scores have declined significantly since the 2000 and 2015 assessments.[^cite_note-153]
