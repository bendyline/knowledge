The **[2010–11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_NHL_season) [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md) season** was the [41st season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Buffalo_Sabres_seasons) for the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) franchise that was established on May 22, 1970.[^cite_note-1] Due to the [2004–05 NHL lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout), this was the 40th season of play for the franchise and was celebrated as such by the team.

This was the last time that the Sabres qualified for postseason play until [2026](80157146.md).

## Off-season

To commemorate the team's 40th anniversary, the Sabres went retro, reverting to their pre-1996 logo and to the design of the first season uniforms (blue with gold and white trim) with a silver trim the team has been using as a third uniform since the 2008–09 season. The new road uniforms would be white with blue and gold trim, similar to the original 1970–71 uniforms; a new third jersey was used paying homage to the [AHL Bisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bisons_\(American_Hockey_League\)) that played in the city prior to 1970. The jersey would come complete with the team's 40th anniversary logo (the current logo with "1970", the team's first season, inside.)

## Regular season

### Franchise sale

On November 30, 2010, Ken Campbell of *[The Hockey News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hockey_News)* reported a story that billionaire [Terrence Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrence_Pegula) had signed a letter of intent to purchase the Sabres from owners [Tom Golisano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Golisano), [Larry Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Quinn_\(ice_hockey\)) and Daniel DiPofi for US\$150 million. Pegula was the founder, president and CEO of [East Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Resources), one of the largest privately held companies in the United States before selling the company.[^cite_note-2] After the report was released, Quinn claimed that the report was "untrue" but had refused further comment.[^cite_note-3] The \$150 million was later determined to be an undervalued amount, as *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine had valued the team at just under \$170 million in 2010.

In December 2010, Pegula officially expressed interest in buying the Sabres for \$170 million and submitted a letter of intent to the NHL.

In January 2021, Golisano reportedly issued a counteroffer with an asking price of US\$175 million.[^cite_note-4] An agreement between Pegula and Golisano to sell the team was reached on January 29, 2011, with Pegula buying the team for \$189 million (\$175 million with \$14 million in debt included)[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] with the Sabres and Golisano officially making an announcement in a press conference on February 3, 2011.[^cite_note-7] League owners approved the sale on February 18.[^cite_note-8] In the conference, it was revealed that an unnamed bidder submitted a much higher bid than Pegula's, but made the bid contingent upon moving the team.[^cite_note-9] The description is consistent with that of [Jim Balsillie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Balsillie), who has made public his efforts to move a team to [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), a move that the Sabres have actively opposed. Terry Pegula named former [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) executive [Ted Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Black) to be the team president.[^cite_note-10] Pegula was introduced as the Sabres' owner in a public ceremony at HSBC Arena on February 23, accompanied by what would be the final appearance of all three members of [The French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_French_Connection_\(ice_hockey\)) line before [Rick Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Martin)'s death three weeks later.

## Playoffs

The Sabres qualified for the playoffs and played the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) in the Eastern Conference Quarterfinals. The Sabres–Flyers series marked the ninth time, and first since [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the teams have met in the playoffs, most of any Sabres opponent. The Flyers lead the match-up four series to three. This series was the first time a Sabres–Flyers series has gone seven games.
