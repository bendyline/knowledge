**Slovakia–Ukraine relations** are the foreign relations between [Slovakia](26830.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md). Both countries established diplomatic relations on 1 January 1993. Slovakia has an embassy in [Kyiv](585629.md), a general consulate in [Uzhhorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod), and two honorary consulates (in [Donetsk](562951.md) and [Uzhhorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod)). Ukraine has an embassy in [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava) and a general consulate in [Prešov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre%C5%A1ov).

The countries share 97 km of [common border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia%E2%80%93Ukraine_border). There are between 40,000 and 100,000 people of [Ukrainian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_diaspora) living in Slovakia. Slovakia is a [European Union member](10890716.md) and Ukraine is a [European Union candidate](36830143.md). Both countries are full members of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe).

## History

During the [Interwar era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_era) the Ukrainian [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) [Zakarpattia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarpattia_Oblast) was part of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Czechoslovak_Republic), before being [ceded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Vienna_Award) to [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-Serhy-1] In 1993, during an official visit to Ukraine, the first [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Slovakia) of independent Slovakia [Michal Kováč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michal_Kov%C3%A1%C4%8D) signed an agreement on establishing constructive relations between the two countries. Multiple visits have subsequently been made from Slovakia to Ukraine from 2000 to present. In March 2022, the Slovak embassy in Kyiv temporarily suspended its work due to [the Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). The embassy resumed its work on 12 April 2022.[^cite_note-2]

### 2020s

See also: [Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)](70149799.md) and [Accession of Ukraine to the European Union](36830143.md)

In April 2023, [Slovakia](26830.md) banned the import of grain from [Ukraine](31750.md), following similar decisions made by [Poland](22936.md) and [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-3]

Ukraine's [Uzhhorod Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod_Airport) is located near the [Slovak-Ukraine border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia%E2%80%93Ukraine_border), requiring the potential use of Slovak airspace by planes landing or taking off at the airport.[^cite_note-4]

On 25 October 2023, [Robert Fico](4127226.md) became the Prime Minister of Slovakia.&#x20;

Fico is widely seen as pro-Russian,[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] and his government has stopped militarily supporting Ukraine, saying he "will not send one bullet" to Ukraine.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-\:4-9][^cite_note-10] Fico has been opposing sanctions against Russia, with his standpoint on Ukraine being compared to that of [Viktor Orbán](750582.md).[^cite_note-\:4-9]

In an RTVS interview, Fico questioned Ukraine's sovereignty and independence, claiming that Ukraine is just a US puppet, sparking outrage in both Slovakia and Ukraine. He has also stated that Slovakia will veto Ukraine's NATO membership, and has pushed for a peace deal, even if Ukraine suffers territorial losses. His words regarding Ukraine have been described as "heartless", "vulgar" and "disgraceful".[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Upon taking office, Fico officially ended Slovakia's arms supplies to Ukraine. However, he has since taken a somewhat different line on Ukraine in office than during his election campaign. During a meeting with Ukrainian [Prime Minister](1256678.md) [Denys Shmyhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denys_Shmyhal) in January 2024, Fico promised not to block private Slovak arms companies from selling to Ukraine, not to block EU financial support for Ukraine, and to support the [accession of Ukraine to the European Union](36830143.md). He described Slovakia's political differences with Ukraine as "minor" and claimed to support Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

On 29 July 2024, Prime Minister [Robert Fico](4127226.md) threatened to suspend Slovakia's [diesel](81761.md) exports to [Ukraine](31750.md) if the Ukrainian government continues to suspend [pipeline](51111.md) oil transport from [Russian oil](10450931.md) company [Lukoil](1027881.md), which Slovakia claims is causing a national [energy crisis](261316.md).[^cite_note-15]



Fico meeting Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in Moscow, 22 December 2024

Fico met with Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in December 2024 in Moscow to discuss primarily the transfer of Russian gas to Slovakia.[^cite_note-NYTPutin-16] He become the third Western leader to do so since the war started.[^cite_note-Visit-17]

In 2025 February, The [Slovak National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_National_Party) criticised Fico for his support for [United Nations General Assembly Resolution ES-11/7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/7), suggesting that Slovakia should have opposed the resolution in line with Hungary's position.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

On 5 September 2025, Fico met with Ukrainian President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) in [Uzhhorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod), Ukraine. During the meeting, Fico voiced support for Ukraine's accession to the EU.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

On 18 February 2026, Slovakia suspended exports of [diesel fuel](81761.md) to Ukraine during the [2026 Slovak–Ukraine oil dispute](82494874.md).[^cite_note-22]

On 23 February 2026, Slovakia halts exports of emergency electricity supplies to Ukraine during the [2026 Slovak–Ukraine oil dispute](82494874.md).[^cite_note-23]

During the [2026 Slovak–Ukraine oil dispute](82494874.md), Fico explicitly threatened that Slovakia would withdraw its support for [Ukraine's accession to the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine's_accession_to_the_European_Union) if the oil flow through the Druzhba pipeline was not immediately restored.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]
