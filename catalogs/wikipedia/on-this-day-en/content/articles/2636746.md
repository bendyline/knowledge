**Mrauk U**[^cite_note-2] ([/məˈraʊuː, ˈmraʊuː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/78/Mrauk_U_pronunciation.mp3?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/78/Mrauk_U_pronunciation.mp3?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*mə-ROW-oo, MROW-oo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town) in northern [Rakhine State](493373.md), [Myanmar](19457.md). It is the capital of [Mrauk-U Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrauk-U_Township), a subregion of the [Mrauk-U District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrauk-U_District).

Mrauk U is culturally significant for the local [Rakhine (Arakanese) people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) and is the location of many important archeological sites. From 1430 until 1785, it was the capital of the [Mrauk U Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrauk_U_Kingdom), the largest and most powerful Rakhine kingdom in history.[^cite_note-3]

## Etymology

Some Rakhine scholars state that the name Mrauk U means 'The First Accomplishment' in archaic [Arakanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_language). This is based on the story of the Arakanese being able to crush an invasion by the [Pyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyu) in the mid 10th Century by a [Mro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mro_people_\(Awa_Khami\)) prince, Pai Phru. The town is said to be located roughly near the region where the Pyu invaders were decimated. The prince went on to claim the throne of [Waithali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waithali) from his uncle who had murdered the prince's father due to a love scandal. The battle was seen as the first accomplishment of the prince, and thus, the name 'First Accomplishment' came to be associated with the place.[^cite_note-auto-4]

### Mythology

A dismissed myth was that in the region where Mrauk U was to be constructed, lived a lonely female monkey. She met a peacock and the two later cohabited. The female monkey conceived with the peacock, and it laid an egg. A human son was born from the egg and he grew up to become a mighty prince. The prince later built a city near the jungle, and in respect of his birth story, the city was called Myauk-U meaning "Monkey's Egg".[^cite_note-auto-4]

## History

See also: [Kingdom of Mrauk U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U)



Mrauk-U, or Arrakan (city of Arrakan), in the first plan of the Portuguese settlement of Daingri-pet, from *Wouter Schouten: Oost-Indische Voyagie, t.o. p. 148. 1676*.

In 1430, King [Min Saw Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Saw_Mon) established Mrauk U as the capital of the last unified [Arakanese Kingdom](493373.md). The city eventually reached a size of 160,000 in the early seventeenth century.[^cite_note-5] Mrauk U served as the capital of the Mrauk U kingdom and its 49 kings till the conquest of the kingdom by the Burmese [Konbaung Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konbaung_Dynasty) in 1784.

Mrauk-U was divided into three periods: the earliest period (1430–1530), the middle period (1531–1638), and the last period (1638–1784). In Arakan antiquities at the Mrauk-U seem to provide rational evidence of where Buddhism settled. The golden days of Mrauk U city, those of the 16th and 17th centuries were contemporary to the days of Tudor kings, the Moghuls, the Ayutthaya kings and Ava (Inwa), Taungoo and Hanthawaddy kings of Myanmar. Mrauk U was cosmopolitan city, fortified by a 30-kilometer long fortification and an intricate net of moats and canals. At the centre of the city was the Royal Place, looming high over the surrounding area like an Asian Acropolis. Waterways formed by canals and creeks earned the fame of distinct resemblance to Venice.



An illustration of Mrauk U under British occupation in 1834. We are looking from the east, where the Pioneers (an engineer group) were camped. The walled palace at this time was still occupied by buildings.

Mrauk U contains numerous archaeological remains, including stone inscriptions, Buddha images and representations of the Buddha's footprints. Many temples and pagodas survive in and around the town, and a number remain in active use as places of worship.[^cite_note-6]&#x20;



A Topographical Plan of the City and Fortifications of Arracan(Arakan) Mrauk-U by Brigade General Joseph Wanton Morrison, British Army in 1825

The area was a site of fighting during the [Arakan Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_campaign_\(1942%E2%80%931943\)), in the latter part of the Second World War.

On 8 February 2024 the [Arakan Army](38106209.md) captured the city from the [Tatmadaw](20394.md) after overrunning Police Battalion 31 in the northeast of the city.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

### Incidents

On 16 January 2018, [Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) protesters [rioted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrauk_U_riot) near a government building in Mrauk U after a ban was issued by local authorities on an event that commemorated the anniversary of the [Kingdom of Mrauk U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U)'s dissolution.[^cite_note-AungKhine2-9][^cite_note-Betty2-10] In response to protesters attempting to seize the government building, police fired [live ammunition](18940621.md) into the crowd, killing seven and wounding twelve.[^cite_note-Thar2-11][^cite_note-StraitAFP2-12]

On 10 December 2025, a hospital in Mrauk-U [was bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mrauk-U_hospital_airstrike) by the Myanmar military junta, killing 33 people and injuring around 80 others.[^cite_note-13]

## Geography

Mrauk U lies roughly 11 kilometres (6.8 mi) east of the [Kaladan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaladan_River) on the banks of its minor tributaries. The town is located on a small outcrop of the [Rakhine Yoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Mountains) on the eastern side of the Kaladan's alluvial plain. Thus, the surrounding countryside is hilly yet also contains a great deal of marshes, mangroves and lakes.

### Climate

Mrauk U, like all of Rakhine State, has an extreme [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Am*). The town receives over 3,600 millimetres or 140 inches of rain a year from [the southwest monsoon](57630.md#Southwest_monsoon), making it one of the wettest parts of monsoon Asia.[^cite_note-14] Despite this, Mrauk U is actually less wet than most of Rakhine State The wet season usually begins in late May and ends by mid-October.[^cite_note-15]

Although located in a tropical region, Mrauk U enjoys lower temperatures during the arid northeastern monsoon. From mid-October to mid-March during the cool season, temperatures can drop to 13 °C or 55.4 °F.[^cite_note-16] This season coincides with the tourist season for Myanmar.

The rainfall on 19 July 2011 was nearly 24 cm (9.4 in), the highest for 33 years. There was flooding because of continuous heavy rains in that month.[^cite_note-17]

## Trading

Due to its proximity to the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md), Mrauk U developed into an important regional trade hub, acting as both a back door to the Burmese hinterland and also as an important port along the eastern shore of the Bay of Bengal. It became a transit point for goods such as rice, ivory, elephants, tree sap and deer hide from Ava in Myanmar, and of cotton, slaves, horses, cowrie, spices and textiles from Bengal, India, Persia, Arabia, Dutches and Portuguese. Alongside [Pegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegu) and later [Syriam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriam), it was one of the most important ports in Myanmar up until the eighteenth century.

The city also traded with non-Asian powers such as [Portugal](23033.md) and then the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) of the [Netherlands](21148.md). The Dutch East India Company (VOC) established trading relations with the Arakanese in 1608 after the Portuguese fell in favour due to the lack of loyalty of Portuguese mercenaries, such as [Filipe de Brito e Nicote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipe_de_Brito_e_Nicote) in the service of the Arakanese king. The VOC established a permanent factory in Mrauk U in 1635, and operated in Arakan till 1665.[^cite_note-19]

At its [zenith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith), Mrauk U was the centre of a kingdom which stretched from the shores of the Ganges river to the western reaches of the [Ayeyarwady River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayeyarwady_River).[^cite_note-20] According to popular Arakanese legend, there were 12 'cities of the Ganges' which constitute roughly half of modern-day Bangladesh which were governed by Mrauk U, including [Dhaka](56656.md) and [Chittagong](380451.md). During that period, its kings minted coins inscribed in [Arakanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakanese_language), [Kufic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kufic) and [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language).

Much of Mrauk U's historical description is drawn from the writings of Friar [Sebastian Manrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Manrique), a Portuguese [Augustinian](144980.md) friar who resided in Mrauk U from 1630 to 1635.

## Rice production

The area around Mrauk U ranks second in the production of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) in Myanmar, after the [Irrawaddy Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_Delta).

## Religion

As Mrauk U and her kingdom prospered, the kings, ministers and peasants built many [pagodas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_pagoda) and [temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaung) around the town to reflect their devotion. Thus, Mrauk U houses a rich collection of temples and pagodas second only to the Central Burmese town of [Bagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagan), in Myanmar. Most of Mrauk U's temples were constructed of hewn stone bricks, unlike the mud and clay bricks of Bagan.

The most notable temples in Mrauk U are the [Shite-thaung Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shite-thaung_Temple) (*Temple of 80,000 Images* or *Temple of Victory*), [Htukkanthein Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Htukkanthein_Temple) *(tukkan Ordination Hall)*, the [Koe-thaung Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koe-thaung_Temple) *(Temple of 90,000 Images)* and the [Five Man pagodas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Man_pagodas?action=edit\&redlink=1).

In 2017, an international commission urged Myanmar to nominate Mrauk U for [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) status. The proposal is backed by [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan) and archaeologists are now cataloguing and protecting the city's many sites in preparation for nomination.[^cite_note-Guardian-21]

Although Mrauk U is a primarily Buddhist site, there are several religious buildings of other faiths. The most notable would be the old Shindhikhan Mosque, built during Min Saw Mon's reign, in the southeast of the town. The mosque was destroyed during the 1980s and 1990s.[^cite_note-22] [Friar Manrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastien_Manrique) also mentions the presence of a Roman Catholic church and a small number of converts and foreign born Catholics (including ronin who were forced out of [Japan](15573.md) due to persecution by the shogunate of [Tokugawa Ieyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Ieyasu)). During this time, the Portuguese missionaries built churches and introduced [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) religious practices.[^cite_note-23]

The Japanese ronins are also known to ventured in Southeast Asia, including the Mrauk U Kingdom, where they offered their military expertise. They served as elite guards and these Japanese warriors played active roles in the Arakanese royal court notably around the time of King [Min Phalaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Phalaung) and [Min Razagyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Razagyi).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Tourism

Prior to the [current civil war](69807451.md) in Myanmar, Mrauk U was a major [archaeological](18951655.md) and [tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist) destination.[^cite_note-26] The main attractions are the temples and ruins around the town. The remains of the main palace roughly form the centre of the town. Tourists could travel to Mrauk U by a domestic flight from [Yangon](57404.md) to [Sittwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittwe) and then aboard a boat from Sittwe via the Kaladan River. Currently, because Mrauk-U is controlled by the [Arakan Army](38106209.md), there is no tourism in the town.

## Infrastructure

Although a tourist destination, most of Mrauk U has very basic infrastructure. It has a Township hospital, a Basic Education High School and a market. Like much of Rakhine state and the whole of Myanmar, Mrauk U has 24-hour electricity.
