The **Somalia national football team** ([Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language): *Kooxda Qaranka Soomaaliya*) represents [Somalia](27358.md) in international [football](10568.md) and is controlled by the [Somali Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Football_Federation) (SFF), a member of the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF). Somalia's first national team captain was [Mohamed Shangole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Shangole), while its longest serving captain has been [Hasan Babay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Babay). Somalia has never qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) nor the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md).

## History

The first Somali football teams were established in the 1940s. The competitions were basic in structure and were associated with the anti-colonial movement. The [Somali Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Youth_League) (SYL), the nation's first political party, had put together a team of local youth to play against the Italian expatriate teams. The soccer team the FYL had assembled, which would later change its name to Bondhere, won the first several competitions. In 1951, the [Somali Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Football_Federation) (SFF) was founded. The first Somali commissioner for sport was later established in 1958.

Although the Somali national football team took part in preliminary matches, it has never qualified for the [World Cup](11370.md). For many years after the outbreak of the [civil war](3070332.md) in the early 1990s, FIFA-sanctioned games could not be played within the country. Qualifying matches for the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) and the World Cup were instead contested away from home. However, following the pacification of the capital [Mogadishu](60154.md) in 2011, the SFF began preparations for the first major sporting event to be held in years at the [Mogadishu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu_Stadium), in December 2012.

Prior to 2019, the Somali FA decided they would admit to all their young and upcoming youth talents and put out local trials to fortify both Olympic and national football teams.

In addition, many from the Somali diaspora in the past have produced quality football players such as [Islam Feruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Feruz) and [Mukhtar Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhtar_Ali).

On 5 September 2019, Somalia won their first qualifying match since 1984 (against [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_national_football_team) - AFCON Qualifying) and their first-ever FIFA World Cup qualifying match, beating [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team) by 1–0.[^cite_note-3] They nearly advanced to the second round but lost 3–1 in Zimbabwe after two late goals for the warriors, consigning the Ocean Stars to an early exit.

From the 1970s up until the 1990s, the Somali national football team used to wear the Somali Flag and socks with the Somali Flag for home kits and the inverse as away kits,[^cite_note-4] the two primary colours of the Somali national flags, in the 2010s up until the present, the national team have now replaced the solid sky blue shirts for vertically blue and white striped shirts.

The crest of the Somali national team used to be the [coat of arms of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Somalia) but it is now replaced with the [Somali Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Football_Federation) crest. The Ocean Stars plays their home games in the [Mogadishu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu_Stadium), (As the stadium was being rebuilt, the national team played matches at Engineer Yaarisow Stadium, which is Mogadishu's secondary stadium for sporting events).
