**Iga Natalia Świątek**[^cite_note-5] (born 31 May 2001) is a Polish professional [tennis](29773.md) player. Currently ranked world No. 9 in women's singles by the [WTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_rankings), she has held the world [No. 1 ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players) for a total of 125 weeks, [seventh-most of all-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players#Weeks_at_No._1). Świątek has won 26 [WTA Tour](1398173.md)–level singles titles, including six [major](197638.md#Tournaments) titles: four at the [French Open](147724.md), one at [Wimbledon](91195.md), and one at the [US Open](82729.md). She has also won the [2023 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) and twelve [WTA 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_1000) events. Świątek is the first Pole to win a major singles title.

As a junior, Świątek was the [2018 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls'_doubles) girls' doubles champion alongside [Caty McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caty_McNally) and the [2018 Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Girls'_singles) girls' singles champion. She began playing regularly on the WTA Tour in 2019, and entered the top 50 at 18 years old after her first Tour final and a fourth-round appearance at the [2019 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). In [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Świątek won her first major at the French Open in dominant fashion, losing no more than five games in any singles match. She entered the top ten of the WTA rankings for the first time in May 2021.

In early 2022, Świątek surged into dominance with a 37-match winning streak, the longest on the WTA Tour in the 21st century, becoming world No. 1 in the process. With major titles at the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [US Opens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she finished 2022 as the world's best player. She repeated the year-end No. 1 feat in 2023 by defending her [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) title and claiming the WTA Finals, and won the French Open for a third straight edition in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). Świątek won her first [grass court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_court) title at the [2025 Wimbledon Championships](80007712.md). She has claimed the French Open title at four of her eight appearances at the tournament, having never lost a match before the fourth round. In 2025 Świątek was suspended for one month for a drug found in contaminated sleep medication.

Świątek has an all-court playing style. She won the [WTA Fan Favorite Shot of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Fan_Favorite_Awards#WTA_Shot_of_the_Year) in 2019 with a [drop shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_shot) from the baseline, and was voted [WTA Fan Favorite Singles Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Fan_Favorite_Awards#Fan_Favorite_Singles_Player_of_the_Year) in 2020. In 2023, she was named [*L'Équipe* Champion of Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'%C3%89quipe_Champion_of_Champions) and [Polish Sports Personality of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Sports_Personality_of_the_Year) and included on *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))*'s annual list of the [100 most influential people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100).

## Early life and background

Świątek was born on 31 May 2001 in Warsaw to Dorota and [Tomasz Świątek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomasz_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek). Her father is a former [rower](25736.md) who competed in the [men's quadruple sculls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowing_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_quadruple_sculls) event at the [1988 Seoul Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Seoul_Olympics),[^cite_note-footsteps-6] while her mother is an [orthodontist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodontist). She has a sister, Agata, who is about three years older and is a dentist.[^cite_note-parents-7]

Their father wanted his daughters to become competitive athletes and preferred they take up an individual sport rather than a team sport to have better control of their chances of success. Agata started out as a swimmer but switched to tennis. She briefly competed on the [ITF Junior Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit) in 2013 at about 15 years old, but stopped playing due to injuries.[^cite_note-insider-8][^cite_note-father-interview-9][^cite_note-agata-10] Świątek followed her sister into tennis because she wanted to beat her and also because she wanted to be more like her. Świątek trained at Mera Warsaw as a 14-year-old before later moving to [Legia Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legia_Warsaw_\(sports_club\)).[^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-passa-12]

## Junior years



Świątek with the [2018 Wimbledon junior champion's trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Girls'_singles)

Świątek reached a career-high ranking of No. 5 as a junior player. She began competing in [ITF Junior Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit) events in 2015 and won back-to-back low-level Grade 4 titles in April and May at age 13. Before the end of the year, she moved up to Grade 2 events and finished runner-up in both singles and doubles at the Czech Junior Open. Świątek made her junior Grand Slam tournament debut in 2016 at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_French_Open), reaching the quarterfinals in both singles and doubles. She followed this up with her best junior title to date at the Grade 1 Canadian Open Junior Championships, defeating [Olga Danilović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Danilovi%C4%87) in the final.[^cite_note-itf-junior-singles-13][^cite_note-itf-junior-doubles-14]

Świątek had a strong first half of 2017. She won both the singles and doubles titles at the Grade 1 [Traralgon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traralgon) Junior International. Although she lost her opening round match at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Australian_Open), she partnered with compatriot [Maja Chwalińska](53209114.md) to make her first final in a major tournament, finishing runner-up in doubles to the North American team of [Bianca Andreescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianca_Andreescu) and [Carson Branstine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_Branstine).[^cite_note-itf-junior-singles-13][^cite_note-itf-junior-doubles-14] She then reached her first Grade A singles final at the [Trofeo Bonfiglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofeo_Bonfiglio), losing to [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md).[^cite_note-\:2-15] Her season came to an end after another quarterfinal at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_Open),[^cite_note-itf-junior-singles-13][^cite_note-itf-junior-doubles-14] after which she had right ankle surgery that kept her out for seven months.[^cite_note-injury-2017-16][^cite_note-WimbledonGirlsSingles-17][^cite_note-18]

Despite competing in only two Grand Slam tournaments in 2018 and three singles events in total, Świątek finished her junior career with her best season.[^cite_note-itf-junior-singles-13][^cite_note-itf-junior-doubles-14] She returned to the junior tour at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open) after a one-year absence and reached the semi-finals in singles, losing to [Caty McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caty_McNally).[^cite_note-19] She fared better in doubles, partnering with McNally to win her first junior major title. They defeated the Japanese team of [Yuki Naito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Naito) and Naho Sato in the final.[^cite_note-french-open-doubles-20]

Świątek played only singles at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships). As an unseeded player due to her absence, she was drawn against top seed [Whitney Osuigwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Osuigwe) in the first round. After winning that match in three sets,[^cite_note-21] she did not drop another set during the rest of the tournament and won the championship for her only junior major singles title. She defeated [Leonie Küng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonie_K%C3%BCng) in the final.[^cite_note-WimbledonGirlsSingles-17][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] Świątek then teamed up with the Slovenian [Kaja Juvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Juvan) to compete in the [2018 Summer Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Summer_Youth_Olympics). They reached the final and defeated the Japanese team of [Yuki Naito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Naito) and Naho Sato. She then finished her junior career.[^cite_note-24]

## Professional

### 2016–2018: Seven ITF Circuit championships



Świątek at the [2018 Summer Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Summer_Youth_Olympics)

Świątek began competing on the [ITF Women's Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Women's_Circuit) in 2016 and played on the circuit through the end of 2018. She won all seven ITF singles finals she reached, ranging from the first four at the \$10k to \$15k level to one at the \$25k level followed up by two at the \$60k level. Her first three titles came at 15 years old.[^cite_note-itf-singles-25] The fourth was in February 2018 in her first tournament back from a seven-month layoff due to injury.[^cite_note-injury-2017-16] After a \$25k title in April, Świątek moved up to higher-level ITF events later that month. Following her junior Wimbledon title in July, she skipped the junior US Open to stay in Europe. During the two weeks of the US Open, she won back-to-back \$60k titles at the [NEK Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEK_Ladies_Open) in Hungary and the [Montreux Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux_Ladies_Open) in Switzerland.[^cite_note-itf-singles-25] During the second event, she defeated top seed and world No. 120, [Mariana Duque-Mariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Duque-Mari%C3%B1o).[^cite_note-wta-matches-26][^cite_note-27] These were Świątek's last two ITF tournaments of the year.[^cite_note-itf-singles-25] With these two titles, she entered the top 200 for the first time at the age of 17, rising from No. 298 to No. 180 in those two weeks.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-28]

### 2019: First WTA Tour final, top 50



Świątek hitting a forehand at 2018 Wimbledon

Despite never playing on the WTA Tour before 2019, Świątek was able to compete in only tour-level events throughout the year. After failing to qualify at the [Auckland Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_ASB_Classic), she qualified for her first major main draw at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_Open). She then defeated No. 82 [Ana Bogdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Bogdan), in three sets, in her debut match to reach the second round. At her next three tournaments, she also qualified at the [Hungarian Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Hungarian_Ladies_Open), but not at either of the Premier Mandatory events in March.[^cite_note-wta-matches-26]

Świątek made her first WTA breakthrough at the [Ladies Open Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ladies_Open_Lugano) in April. With her first direct acceptance into a main draw, she made her first WTA final. During the event, she [upset](1169845.md) No. 3 seed [Viktória Kužmová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikt%C3%B3ria_Ku%C5%BEmov%C3%A1) in the second round for her first top-50 victory.[^cite_note-29] She ultimately finished runner-up to [Polona Hercog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polona_Hercog) in three sets.[^cite_note-30] Moreover, a precise cross-court [forehand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forehand) drop shot she hit against [Kristýna Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krist%C3%BDna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1) in the semi-final was voted the 2019 [WTA Shot of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Awards#Fan_Favorite_Shot_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-shot-of-the-year-31] With the runner-up, she also made her debut in the top 100 while still 17 years old.[^cite_note-32] Świątek closed out her clay court season with a fourth-round appearance at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open) in her second major.[^cite_note-wta-matches-26] She upset No. 16 [Wang Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Qiang_\(tennis\)) in the second round for her first top-20 victory before losing to defending champion [Simona Halep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simona_Halep).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

Świątek could not repeat her French Open success at the remaining major tournaments of the year, losing her opening match at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships) and in the second round at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-wta-matches-26] Her best result in the second half of the season was a third-round appearance at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rogers_Cup). During the event, she upset No. 18, [Caroline Wozniacki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Wozniacki), before losing to No. 2, [Naomi Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Osaka).[^cite_note-wta-matches-26][^cite_note-wozniacki-35] With this result, she entered the top 50 for the first time a week later.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-28] She missed the rest of the season after the US Open to undergo foot surgery and finished the year at No. 61 in the world.[^cite_note-footsteps-6][^cite_note-wta-rankings-28]

Świątek finished 2019 as runner-up to Simona Halep in the voting for the [WTA Fan Favorite Singles Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Fan_Favorite_Awards) award.[^cite_note-36]

### 2020: French Open champion, top 20

See also: [2020 Iga Świątek tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season)

Świątek made her return to the WTA Tour at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open). She matched her best result at a major with another fourth-round appearance, this time highlighted by a victory over No. 20, [Donna Vekić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87).[^cite_note-footsteps-6][^cite_note-37] She defeated Vekić again at the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Qatar_Total_Open), her last match win before the WTA Tour was shut down for six months due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-wta-matches-26] Świątek continued her major tournament success once the tour resumed. She reached the third round at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_\(tennis\)), losing to eventual runner-up [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka).[^cite_note-38]

Entering the rescheduled [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open) in September, Świątek was ranked No. 54 in the world. Nonetheless, she won the singles event for her maiden WTA title. During the tournament, she defeated 2019 runner-up and world No. 19, [Markéta Vondroušová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1), in the opening round.[^cite_note-wta-matches-26][^cite_note-39] Her biggest upset was a victory in the fourth round over top seed and world No. 2, Simona Halep, who was on a 17-match win streak and was also the heavy favourite to win the title.[^cite_note-halep-upset-40][^cite_note-41] She only lost three games against Halep after winning just one game against her in the same round of the French Open the previous year.[^cite_note-halep-upset-40] Świątek defeated world No. 6, [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin), in the final to become the first Polish[^cite_note-42] player to win a major singles title and the lowest-ranked French Open champion in the history of the WTA rankings. She also became the youngest singles champion at the tournament since [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) in 2005 and the youngest women's singles winner since [Monica Seles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Seles) in 1992. She won the title without dropping a set or more than five games to any opponent, and the 28 games she lost in total were tied for the second-fewest among French Open singles champions in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era) behind only the 20 games [Steffi Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steffi_Graf) lost in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_French_Open). With the title, Świątek rose to No. 17 in the world. She also played the doubles event, partnering with [Nicole Melichar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Melichar) for the first time. The pair reached the semi-finals, and also did not drop a set until their last match.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

In 2020, Świątek was voted the WTA Fan Favorite Singles Player of the Year.[^cite_note-47]

### 2021: Italian Open champion, top 10

See also: [2021 Iga Świątek tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season)



Świątek at the [2021 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open)

At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Świątek was seeded 15th and recorded wins over [Arantxa Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arantxa_Rus), [Camila Giorgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Giorgi) and [Fiona Ferro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Ferro). She reached the fourth round where she lost to Simona Halep, in three sets.[^cite_note-48] At [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Adelaide_International), she won her first WTA Tour title without dropping a set in the whole tournament. She defeated [Belinda Bencic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Bencic) in the final, in straight sets.[^cite_note-49] As a result, she entered the top 15 for the first time in her career, in March 2021.[^cite_note-50]

Seeded 15th, Świątek won her first career WTA 1000-title at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), defeating former champion [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1) in just 46 minutes without the loss of a single game. She advanced to the final after defeating two-time champion and world No. 5, [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md), and the second best-ranked teenager [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff) on the same day, as well as saving match points in her third-round match against [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1).[^cite_note-51] Świątek became the third player to win a title after saving match point en route in 2021, alongside Naomi Osaka at the Australian Open and [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty) in Miami. She also became the fourth teenager to win a WTA 1000 event.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] This successful run to her third career title moved her into the top 10 in the singles rankings on 17 May 2021, as world No. 9.[^cite_note-54]

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open), Świątek was seeded at No. 8. She opened her title defense winning against [Kaja Juvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Juvan) in the first round, and then defeated [Rebecca Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Peterson), [Anett Kontaveit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anett_Kontaveit) and [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk). She won 22 straight sets at the French Open but then lost in the quarterfinals to [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari). In doubles, seeded 14th with [Bethanie Mattek-Sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethanie_Mattek-Sands) as a pair, playing just their third event together, they reached the final[^cite_note-55] where they were defeated by Barbora Krejčíková and [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1) in straight sets. As a result, Świątek reached the top 50 at a career-high ranking of No. 42 in doubles for the first time in her career.

She started her grass-court season at the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Eastbourne_International) where she was seeded at No. 4. After defeating [Heather Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_Watson), she lost to [Daria Kasatkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina) in the second round. As the seventh seed at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships), Świątek defeated [Hsieh Su-wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsieh_Su-wei), [Vera Zvonareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Zvonareva), and [Irina-Camelia Begu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina-Camelia_Begu), all in straight sets. In the fourth round, she lost to [Ons Jabeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ons_Jabeur), in three sets.[^cite_note-56] With her win over Anett Kontaveit in the third round of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_\(tennis\)), she became the only player to have reached the second week of all four major championships in the 2021 season.[^cite_note-57] She qualified for the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_WTA_Finals) for the first time in her career.[^cite_note-58]

### 2022: Two Major titles, 37-match win streak, world No. 1

See also: [2022 Iga Świątek tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season)

Świątek started her season by reaching the semifinals at the [Adelaide International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Adelaide_International_1_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-59] She then reached another semifinal at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-60] Following a second-round match loss against [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko), Świątek won the next six tournaments she entered — [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles),[^cite_note-61] [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles),[^cite_note-62] [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles),[^cite_note-63] [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles),[^cite_note-64] [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)[^cite_note-65] and, for her second major title, [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)[^cite_note-66] — before losing to [Alizé Cornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliz%C3%A9_Cornet) at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) in the third round.[^cite_note-wtatennis.com-67] Świątek reached the [world No. 1 singles ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players), and became the fourth as well as the youngest woman (11th player overall) to complete the [Sunshine Double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Double) in the process.[^cite_note-68] She also accumulated a 37-match win streak, the longest by a woman in the 21st century.[^cite_note-wtatennis.com-67] Świątek had a lacklustre performance throughout the summer. She lost to [Caroline Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Garcia) in the quarterfinals in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_Poland_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles),[^cite_note-69] [Beatriz Haddad Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_Haddad_Maia) in the third round in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys) in the third round in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). However, she returned on form at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), winning her third major by defeating Ons Jabeur in the final.[^cite_note-70] She became the first woman to win both the French Open and US Open in the same season since [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams) in 2013.[^cite_note-71]

Świątek reached the final at the [Ostrava Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ostrava_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), but she lost to Barbora Krejčíková in a three-set match that lasted 3 hours and 16 minutes, the longest match of her career so far.[^cite_note-72] She subsequently played at the [San Diego Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_San_Diego_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), winning her eighth title by defeating Donna Vekić, in three sets.[^cite_note-73] At the [2022 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) held in [Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth), Texas, Świątek won the group stage without dropping a set, defeating Daria Kasatkina, Caroline Garcia and Coco Gauff, respectively. However, she was upset by [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md) in the semi-finals in three sets. Nevertheless, she finished the season as year-end No. 1 and posted a record win-loss 67–9 in 2022, the most wins in a single season since Serena Williams in 2013.[^cite_note-74] She also became the first player since Serena Williams in 2013 to collect over 11,000 ranking points in a single season.[^cite_note-75] She accumulated the second highest ranking points total in WTA history, second only to Serena Williams in 2013.[^cite_note-76]

### 2023: French Open and WTA Finals champion

See also: [2023 Iga Świątek tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season)

Świątek's 2023 campaign was statistically less dominant than her [2022 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season), but she still managed to win six titles in the year. She started 2023 as only the fourth woman in WTA history to be ranked world No. 1 for 40 or more consecutive weeks in their first stint as the top-ranked player.[^cite_note-77] In her first tournament of the year representing Poland at the [United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Cup), she partnered with her compatriots including [Hubert Hurkacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Hurkacz) and reached the semi-finals.[^cite_note-78] At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open), she lost in the fourth round in straight sets against [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md),[^cite_note-79] who would upset Świątek two more times later in the year, respectively in the semi-finals at the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open) and in the quarterfinals at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]



Świątek practicing her forehand before 2023 US Open

She did not win any tournament until the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Qatar_Total_Open), where she did not drop a set throughout the tournament and lost only five games.[^cite_note-82] The clay season saw her successfully defend her [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix) title,[^cite_note-83] as well as win her fourth major at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open) by defeating [Karolína Muchová](55266410.md) in the final.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] After improvement on the grass court with a quarterfinal performance at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships),[^cite_note-86] which ended her 14-match win streak,[^cite_note-87] she won her home tournament at the [Poland Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Poland_Open).[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_\(tennis\)), Świątek lost in the fourth round to Jelena Ostapenko, which ended her reign at world No. 1.[^cite_note-91] Her 75-week reign at No. 1 is the third-longest streak in the Open Era among players in their first stint as the top player, behind only Steffi Graf and [Martina Hingis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Hingis).[^cite_note-92] Nevertheless, she went on to win the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-93] At the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals), she won the title without dropping a set, becoming the first player to do so since Serena Williams in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_WTA_Tour_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles). She also dropped just 20 games throughout the entire tournament, the fewest since the reintroduction of the round-robin format in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_WTA_Tour_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles) and beat Serena Williams's record of 32 overall games dropped. By conceding only one game to [Jessica Pegula](32944701.md), Świątek also broke the record for fewest games lost in a final, previously held by [Martina Navratilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Navratilova) in 1983 and [Kim Clijsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Clijsters) in 2003 (two each).[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-WTA_Tour-95] Winning the title saw her reclaiming the top ranking and clinching year-end No. 1 for the second season in a row.[^cite_note-96]

### 2024: French Open three-peat, Olympic bronze, suspension

See also: [2024 Iga Świątek tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season)

Świątek entered the [United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Cup) in an international team competition representing Team Poland. She reached the final winning all of her singles matches and received the MVP award of the event.[^cite_note-97] In the third round of the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she was defeated by Linda Nosková in three sets and as a result of this loss her 18-match winning streak, which started in September 2023, came to an end.[^cite_note-98]

In February, Świątek entered the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) as a two-time defending champion. She reached the final, having defeated [Sorana Cîrstea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea),[^cite_note-99] [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova) and Viktoria Azarenka.[^cite_note-100] In the final, she faced Elena Rybakina, winning in straight sets[^cite_note-101] and claiming her seventh WTA 1000 title. Świątek did not drop a set en route to the title for the second consecutive year. She also became the first person to win three consecutive titles in Doha and the first player to win three consecutive titles at the same hardcourt tournament since Serena Williams in 2015.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] At the next WTA 1000 tournament in [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Dubai_Tennis_Championships), she reached back-to-back quarterfinals defeating Elina Svitolina in the round of 16. She was the fourth player since the WTA-1000 format's introduction in 2009 the make the quarterfinals in 9+ consecutive appearances (between Dubai 2023 – Dubai 2024) after Serena Williams, [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova) and [Agnieszka Radwańska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnieszka_Radwa%C5%84ska).[^cite_note-104] She then advanced to the semifinals where she lost to [Anna Kalinskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya) in straight sets.[^cite_note-105]

In March, Świątek won the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) for a second time, defeating Maria Sakkari in the final.[^cite_note-106] This was her second title of the season and 19th overall.[^cite_note-107] In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open), she recorded her 100th WTA 1000 career match win defeating Camila Giorgi in straight sets.[^cite_note-108]

In April, Świątek entered the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles) as a two-time defending champion and reached the semifinal where she lost to Elena Rybakina in three sets,[^cite_note-109] which ended her 10-match winning streak in the tournament.[^cite_note-110] In the same month, she entered her 100th week as the reigning world No. 1 surpassing [Lindsay Davenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Davenport) and moving to the ninth place in the all-time table.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] At the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she reached the final where she faced the defending champion and second seed, Aryna Sabalenka. Świątek played the longest match against Sabalenka in her career thus far, which lasted over three hours. Świątek saved three championship points in the third set and won her 20th career title.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] The match was called the best of their rivalry,[^cite_note-\:3-116][^cite_note-\:4-117][^cite_note-\:5-118] with Świątek stating that it was "the most intense and crazy final" she has played.[^cite_note-119]

In May, at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_\(tennis\)), she reached a second consecutive final with a win over world No. 3, Coco Gauff, in straight sets.[^cite_note-120] In the final, she beat Aryna Sabalenka, this time in straight sets, becoming the most successful Polish tennis player in terms of the number of titles won surpassing [Agnieszka Radwańska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnieszka_Radwa%C5%84ska) with 21. It was also her tenth WTA 1000 title and fourth on clay, and the third in Rome. She became the third female player after Serena Williams and [Dinara Safina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinara_Safina) to complete the Madrid-Rome double,[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] and the first player, female or male, to achieve it since 2013 when both Williams and [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) won Madrid and Rome.[^cite_note-123]

Świątek won the [2024 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles),[^cite_note-124] which was her third French Open title in a row and fourth overall. Świątek lost one set during the tournament, which was to Naomi Osaka in the second round, in which Osaka held match point in the third set.[^cite_note-125] After defeating Osaka, Świątek lost only 17 games, defeating Marie Bouzková in the third round, Anastasia Potapova in the fourth round, Markéta Vondroušová in the quarterfinal, Coco Gauff in the semi-final, and [Jasmine Paolini](46663470.md) in the final. Świątek became the third player in history to win three consecutive French Open titles, after [Monica Seles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Seles) and [Justine Henin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justine_Henin), and the second woman to win Madrid, Rome, and the French Open in the same season, after Serena Williams in 2013.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

Having not played since her French Open triumph, Świątek lost in round three at the [2024 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships) to Yulia Putintseva, thus ending her 21-match winning streak.[^cite_note-128]

At the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Świątek defeated [Irina-Camelia Begu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina-Camelia_Begu), [Diane Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Parry), [Wang Xiyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xiyu) and [Danielle Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Collins). In the semifinal, she lost to the eventual gold medalist Zheng Qinwen in straight sets.[^cite_note-\:6-129][^cite_note-130] This marked Świątek's first loss in Paris after 1,149 days of staying undefeated.[^cite_note-131] In the bronze-medal match, she beat [Anna Karolína Schmiedlová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Karol%C3%ADna_Schmiedlov%C3%A1) in straight sets, becoming the first player from Poland to win an Olympic medal in tennis.[^cite_note-132]

At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Świątek reached the semifinals where she lost to Aryna Sabalenka.[^cite_note-133] At the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_\(tennis\)), Świątek lost in the quarterfinals to Jessica Pegula.[^cite_note-134] She played at the WTA Finals as the No. 2 seed and defending champion, but lost to Coco Gauff in two sets. Her defeat meant Sabalenka would finish the year as WTA No. 1.[^cite_note-Carayol-135] The ITF proclaimed Świątek its year-end world champion for 2024.[^cite_note-136]

Świątek received a one-month suspension in November 2024 following a positive test for a banned substance [trimetazidine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimetazidine). The drug was found to be a contaminant in a sleep medication [melatonin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melatonin) she was taking, so she was handed a penalty that was at the lowest end of the range for "No Significant Fault or Negligence".[^cite_note-137] Nevertheless, she had to miss three tournaments while her case was being considered and forfeit her prize money from the Cincinnati Open.[^cite_note-138]

### 2025: Wimbledon champion, Career Surface Slam

See also: [2025 Iga Świątek tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season)

Świątek entered the [United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_Cup) representing Team Poland for the third time in her career. With her team, Świątek advanced to the final that was won by Team United States.[^cite_note-139] In this edition, Świątek played five singles matches and lost only one of them, against Coco Gauff in the final tie.[^cite_note-140] She also played two [mixed doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_doubles) matches: one alongside [Hubert Hurkacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Hurkacz) and the other alongside [Jan Zieliński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Zieli%C5%84ski). She and her partners won both those matches.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Świątek made it to her first semifinal since [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), but lost to eventual champion Madison Keys in three sets despite winning the first set and holding a match point in the deciding set.[^cite_note-143] At the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open), Świątek reached the semifinals, before losing to [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko) extending their head-to-head to 0–5.[^cite_note-144] She was then defeated in the quarter-finals of the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Dubai_Tennis_Championships) by the eventual champion, [Mirra Andreeva](71915009.md).[^cite_note-145] In the semifinals of the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open), Świątek again lost to Andreeva, this time in three sets.[^cite_note-146]

During the clay-court season, Świątek failed to defend both her [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) titles, winning only one game in both sets against Coco Gauff in the Madrid semifinal (her lowest number of game wins on clay since the 2019 French Open),[^cite_note-147] and suffered a third-round loss to Danielle Collins in Rome.[^cite_note-148] As a result, Świątek fell to world No. 5, her lowest ranking since March 2022.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Świątek was seeded fifth, beating former major champions [Emma Raducanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Raducanu) and Elena Rybakina, the latter of which lasted three sets despite Świątek's being a set and a break behind. After beating Elina Svitolina in the quarterfinals, Świątek lost her semifinal match to Sabalenka in three sets. This marked the first time Świątek did not reach the final since 2021, and snapped her 26-match-winning streak at the French Open. As a result, she dropped further in the rankings to No. 7.[^cite_note-\:0-149]

During the grass-court season, Świątek reached the final of the [Bad Homburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Homburg_Open) by defeating Jasmine Paolini in the semifinals. This marked her first tour-level final on grass, with her previous best result being reaching the semifinal at the same tournament. In the final, she lost to Jessica Pegula.[^cite_note-150]

At [Wimbledon](80007712.md), Świątek continued her career-best grass-court form to reach the semifinal for the first time, in which she defeated Belinda Bencic to the loss of only two games to make her first Wimbledon final. She defeated [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova) 6–0, 6–0 in their [first professional matchup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles_final) to win her first Wimbledon title.[^cite_note-151] It was the first double bagel women's singles Wimbledon final since 1911.[^cite_note-152]

Świątek claimed a maiden victory at the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) in August. After defeating Elena Rybakina in the semi-finals,[^cite_note-153] she then triumphed over Jasmine Paolini to seal the title. Świątek did not drop a set during the tournament.[^cite_note-154] She then entered the [mixed doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Mixed_doubles) at the [2025 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_\(tennis\)), which were held the week prior to the singles draw. She and doubles partner [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud) were defeated in the final by Sara Errani and [Andrea Vavassori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Vavassori).[^cite_note-155] In the [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) draw, she exited the tournament in the quarter-finals after being overcome by Amanda Anisimova in straight sets.[^cite_note-156]

At the [WTA Finals](78728546.md), Świątek won her opening round-robin group game against Madison Keys, but then suffered successive defeats to Elena Rybakina and Amanda Anisimova to exit the tournament.[^cite_note-157]

### 2026: Canadian Open and United Cup champion

See also: [2026 Iga Świątek tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek_tennis_season)

In January, Świątek was part of the Polish team that defeated Switzerland in the final of the [United Cup](81647045.md). She lost her opening singles match of the final to Belinda Bencic, but Poland went on to win the next two matches to win the tournament for the first time.[^cite_note-158] Her attempt to complete a [Career Grand Slam](197638.md#Career_Grand_Slam) at the [2026 Australian Open](81715708.md) ended at the quarterfinal stage. She was defeated in straight sets by eventual champion [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md).[^cite_note-159] Świątek reached the quarterfinals of the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open), losing to Maria Sakkari,[^cite_note-160] and then withdrew from the [Dubai Tennis Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-161] She then progressed to the quarterfinals of the [BNP Paribas Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) where she was defeated by Elina Svitolina.[^cite_note-162] Świątek also dropped to No. 3 again after Rybakina reached the final.

Świątek then lost her opening match in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) to compatriot [Magda Linette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magda_Linette), ending a streak of 73 opening match wins.[^cite_note-163] On March 23, 2026, shortly after the Miami exit, Świątek announced a coaching split with Wim Fissette. ## Playing style



Świątek hitting a backhand at the 2023 US Open

Świątek has an aggressive, all-court style of play and incorporates a lot of variety into her game. Because her game style is focused on offence, she typically generates high amounts of both winners and unforced errors.[^cite_note-wozniacki-35][^cite_note-all-court-199][^cite_note-200] She has described her style of play on clay as "a big serve, topspin, and backhand down the line".[^cite_note-insider-8] Despite her aggressive style of play, she plays with margin, and constructs points carefully until she creates an opportunity to hit a winner, and in all seven of her matches played at the 2020 French Open, she hit more winners than unforced errors. In total at the French Open that year, she hit 175 winners to 127 unforced errors. The basis of Świątek's game was described by tennis journalist [Christopher Clarey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Clarey) for *[The New York Times](30680.md)* as: her sliding ability, allowing her to defend from the corners *à la* [Kim Clijsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Clijsters) and [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md), a "sprinter's speed" when moving forward to the net, power and topspin akin to her idol [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), and mental strength forged through sports psychology.[^cite_note-201]

Her forehand and backhand are fast and powerful, with her forehand being hit with significant topspin due to her employing an extreme western [grip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grip_\(tennis\)); on her run to the title at the French Open in 2020, Świątek's average forehand speed was 73 mph (117 km/h), only 4 km/h below that of the average male forehand speed. She even hit some forehands up to 79 mph (127 km/h), the fastest of any female player in the draw and exceeded only by [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) on the men's side. Her forehand topspin reached 3,453 rpm at the French Open, comparable with her idol Rafael Nadal.[^cite_note-202] Świątek's backhand speed peaked at 76 mph (122 km/h) at the French Open, the fastest of any female player in the draw, and equal to [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem)'s backhand speed, the fastest of any male player at the French Open.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-WTA_May_19,_2022-204] Considering her dominance on clay, Świątek is often called the "Queen of Clay".[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207]

Świątek aims to come to the net, and has good volleying skills due to her doubles experience.[^cite_note-bounce-208] Świątek possesses an accurate first serve, peaking at 123 mph (198 km/h),[^cite_note-209] and averaging at 108 mph (174 km/h), allowing her to serve aces, dictate play from the first stroke, and win a majority of first-serve points. She possesses an effective kick serve, and an effective slice serve, which are deployed as second serves, preventing opponents from scoring free points. In earlier years of her career, she regularly used the drop shot, and won the 2019 WTA Shot of the Year with a cross-court drop shot from the baseline that landed on the sideline well inside the service box.[^cite_note-shot-of-the-year-31][^cite_note-210] Since the beginning of her collaboration with Tomasz Wiktorowski, she simplified her game and currently very rarely employs this shot.[^cite_note-211] Świątek aims to gain the advantage in a point by hitting the ball early on the rise.[^cite_note-bounce-208] Further strengths include her exceptional speed, movement, and court coverage, detailed and intricate footwork, and intelligent point construction. Her favourite surface is clay, having grown up playing on that surface, although she has had success on all surfaces.[^cite_note-212] Her clay court success is enhanced by her ability to slide on the surface; as her career has progressed, she has also developed the ability to slide on hard and grass courts, too.

### Bagels and Breadsticks

Over Świątek's WTA career up until May 2024 an average of 40.6 percent of her matches have included either a 6–0 (bagel) set or a 6–1 (breadstick) set. As a result, many have started to use the term "Iga's Bakery" to describe this.[^cite_note-Eccleshare-213] During the 2023 season she won a bagel set in 29 percent of her matches compared with an average of 11.4 per cent of matches for matches in which she wasn't playing.[^cite_note-Eccleshare-213] Her closest bagel rivals are Coco Gauff and Jessica Pegula, both of whom scored eight in 2023.[^cite_note-Eccleshare-213]

As of July 2025, she has dispensed the rarer double bagel nine times in her WTA career, three of which have come during major tournaments (which were against Xinyu Wang in the 2023 French Open, against [Anastasia Potapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) in the 2024 French Open, and against Amanda Anisimova in the 2025 Wimbledon final).[^cite_note-214]

## Coaching team

Świątek's primary coach in her early junior years was Michał Kaznowski, who she worked with up to the 2016 French Open.[^cite_note-passa-12][^cite_note-215] Jolanta Rusin-Krzepota was her physical preparation trainer for almost four years through the end of the 2019 US Open.[^cite_note-216] Świątek was coached by [Piotr Sierzputowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Sierzputowski) beginning in 2016.[^cite_note-217] While Sierzputowski was her coach, British former professional tennis player and former Polish Davis Cup coach [Nick Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Brown_\(tennis\)) also served as a main consultant on occasion.[^cite_note-218] Świątek also works with sports psychologist [Daria Abramowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Abramowicz) and fitness trainer/physiologist Maciej Ryszczuk.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-WTA201005-220][^cite_note-NYT210207-221][^cite_note-222]

In December 2021, Świątek announced that she was splitting from Sierzputowski after nearly six years working together. She wrote, "This change is really challenging for me, and this decision wasn't easy, either". Świątek had hired Sierzputowski when she was 15 and still playing in junior tournaments.[^cite_note-223] From the end of 2021 until October 2024, she was coached by [Tomasz Wiktorowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomasz_Wiktorowski),[^cite_note-coach-224] who also previously worked with [Agnieszka Radwańska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnieszka_Radwa%C5%84ska).[^cite_note-225] In October 2024 it was announced that she had parted ways with Wiktorowski,[^cite_note-coach-224][^cite_note-226] appointing [Wim Fissette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wim_Fissette) as his replacement later that month.[^cite_note-227] In an Instagram post on 23 March 2026, days after her streak of 73 consecutive opening round wins ended at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Open_\(tennis\)), Swiatek announced she and Fissette have parted ways.[^cite_note-228]

On April 2, 2026, Świątek announced that [Francisco Roig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Roig), a longtime [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) coach, joined her team.[^cite_note-229]

### Advocating mental health awareness

On 10 October 2021, Świątek donated \$50,000 of her prize money in support of [World Mental Health Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Mental_Health_Day), after progressing to the last 16 of the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Wells_Open). "I would say in sports, for me, it was always important to use that kind of help because I always thought that in my mental toughness there is some strength that I can use on court and I can also develop in that manner," she said. After winning in at the French Open in 2020, Świątek revealed that using her sports psychologist, Daria Abramowicz, had helped her get over the line. "It's just good to stay open-minded. If you need that kind of help, then go for it. If you're up to it and if you're open-minded, I think it helps a lot," Świątek added.[^cite_note-253]

In her runner-up speech after losing the final to [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) on the [Ostrava Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrava_Open) on 9 October 2022, Świątek announced she will donate all of her prize money (€58,032) won in the tournament to Polish non-profit organizations and foundations to celebrate [World Mental Health Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Mental_Health_Day). "This is the most difficult moment of this tournament for me. I do not know what to say. Thank you so much. Whether I win or not, I am fulfilling my dreams, and you with me. I wish I won cause I would be able to donate more but I would like to announce that I will donate my prize money to non-profit organisations in Poland on Monday, which is World Mental Health Day. I hope this money can help a lot of people and make some change".[^cite_note-254]

On 12 October 2023, Świątek announced that she would donate 300,000 PLN (\$70,000) to [UNICEF](19698110.md) Poland to "help in the field of mental health in Poland and all over the world", as part of celebration of [World Mental Health Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Mental_Health_Day).[^cite_note-255] Along with the major announcement, she shared her thoughts and motivated other people as well. "I would like to encourage you to have a look around and see if there's someone there who could use your help. You can make a donation of your choice to organizations like UNICEF that professionally help people (if you're able to)."

### Support of Ukraine

Following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022](79400268.md), Świątek has often worn ribbons or other accessories in Ukrainian colors, and on several occasions has publicly declared her support for Ukraine in her winner speeches.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259]

On 23 July 2022, Świątek hosted a charity tennis event in [Kraków](16815.md), Poland to raise funds for children and teenagers impacted by the war in [Ukraine](31750.md). The one-day event featured a mixed doubles exhibition match and a set of singles between Świątek and [Agnieszka Radwańska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnieszka_Radwa%C5%84ska). Ukraine football great [Andriy Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriy_Shevchenko) was a special guest. [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md) of Ukraine served as umpire for the event. Świątek stated, "I hope that we can see each other in large numbers in [TAURON Arena Krakow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauron_Arena) and in front of the television to show the strength of sport when it unites us in helping and gives us at least a little joy."[^cite_note-260] Świątek later announced on Twitter that the event has raised over 2,5 mln złoty (over €500,000), which would be spread between [United 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United24), Elina Svitolina Foundation and [UNICEF Poland](19698110.md).[^cite_note-261]

On 10 August, Świątek has been announced as the part of line up for "Tennis Plays for Peace" exhibition, which took place on 24 August with other numerous current and former tennis stars like [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), [Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md), [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff), [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe), [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas), [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini), [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari) and many others, 100% of the proceeds going to GlobalGiving, the international non-profit identified by Tennis Plays for Peace.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263]

On 9 January 2023, Świątek took to Twitter and [Instagram](31591547.md) to announce that her 'Iga & Friends' and '1ga' T-shirts will be available for sale at [Allegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegro_\(website\)) Charity, where she and [UNICEF](19698110.md) Poland have once again joined forces to raise funds for children in war-torn Ukraine.[^cite_note-264] On 11 January, Świątek announced on her social media platforms that she will be auctioning off her [US Open](82729.md) winning gear and French Open shoes, among others all proceeds would also be going straight to UNICEF.[^cite_note-265]

## Personal life

Świątek lives in [Raszyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raszyn), Poland.[^cite_note-266] She is a keen reader and in an interview has explained it helps her relax between tennis matches.[^cite_note-267] One of her favourite dishes is a Polish speciality, *[Makaron z truskawkami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasta_with_strawberries)* (pasta with strawberries).[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] Świątek has expressed her love for [rock music](25423.md), citing bands such as [Guns N' Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guns_N'_Roses), [AC/DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC/DC), [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin), [Pearl Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Jam) and [Red Hot Chili Peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Chili_Peppers) as some of her favourites.[^cite_note-270] She is a fan of [LEGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego) and has stated that building LEGO while on tour aids her mentally and calms her down.[^cite_note-271]

Świątek is an avid fan of the American musician [Taylor Swift](5422144.md) and has been to some of her concerts.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273]
