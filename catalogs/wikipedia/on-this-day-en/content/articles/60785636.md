# International students in Australia

&#x20; Australia ranked third in top study abroad destinations with a total market value of over 5 billion [AUD](81215.md) (3.47 billion [USD](18717338.md)) generated by international students in 2018.[^cite_note-1] In 2023, 786,891 international students were enrolled in educational programs in [Australia](4689264.md), which was 27% higher than the previous year. In 2024 this is forecasted to increase again by 18%.[^cite_note-\:32-2] They come to gain a high-quality education, possibly immigrate, or to experience a different life. Each year, many new international students will enroll in Australian courses and add to the already significant international student body around the country.

Australia has the highest ratio of international students per capita in the world by a large margin.[^cite_note-\:21-3] In 2019, international students represented an average 26.7% of the student body population in Australian universities. International education therefore represents one of the country's largest exports and has a pronounced influence on the country's demographics, with a significant proportion of international students remaining in Australia after graduation on various skill and employment visas.[^cite_note-4]

Australia has by far the highest percentage of international students in the world, relative to total population,[^cite_note-\:21-3] with 1 international student per 33.6 people in 2023 (786,891 students,[^cite_note-\:32-2] 26.45 million residents). If the 18% growth seen in 2024 continues,[^cite_note-\:32-2] this number will increase to 1 in 28.8 (3.48% of the population).

According to data released by the [Departure of Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Education_and_Training_\(Australia\)) of the Australian Government in 2023, [China](5405.md) is the most significant source of international students to Australia. [India](14533.md) had the second largest proportion of the enrollments, followed by [Nepal](171166.md), [Colombia](5222.md) and [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-\:16-5]

All international students are required to obtain a valid [student visa](330265.md) before they arrive in Australia.[^cite_note-\:17-6] When students apply for their student visa through the [Department of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Home_Affairs_\(Australia\)) of the [Australian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Australia), they need to submit the electronic Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) and a compulsory English language proficiency test score to lodge their visa application. Also, each student visa applicant must prove that they have enough financial ability to pay for their tuition fees, books and daily living expenses while they study in Australia.[^cite_note-\:7-7]

After the Australian government published the new immigration policy, international students are encouraged to study, work and stay in regional areas in Australia, such as [Adelaide](1148.md), [Tasmania](29944.md) and the [Northern Territory](21638.md).[^cite_note-\:18-8] In order to protect the local workforce, the Australian government intends to reduce the number of accepted migrants, and in the long-term, the government aims to set the cap of skilled immigration to about 160,000.[^cite_note-\:0-9]

In 2023, according to the Department of Education of the Australian Government, New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland are the states attracting the largest portion of international students.[^cite_note-\:32-2]

## Definitions

In Australia, a student is considered an international student if they are not an [Australian citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_citizen), [Australian permanent resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_permanent_resident), [New Zealand citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealander), or a holder of an [Australian Permanent resident](http://www.auzworldmigration.com.au/) [humanitarian visa](50128538.md).[^cite_note-10]

## Requirements

### Student visa

Generally, international students as citizens of other countries are required to obtain a valid [student visa](330265.md), which ascertains their legal status for staying in Australia. Students who have obtained the [Student Visa](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500) (subclass 500) can stay in Australia for up to 5 years, depending on their enrolment.[^cite_note-\:17-6] Approximately, 400,000 overseas students are granted this visa each year. In Australia, when students apply for their visa, they need to get the [Confirmation of Enrolment](https://ask.unimelb.edu.au/app/answers/detail/a_id/233/~/confirmation-of-enrolment-%28coe%29/session/L2F2LzEvdGltZS8xNTU4NjY3MDQwL3NpZC9mVUdTZDlQT2hhVHRzV0xuNnRuZkFMMFVJOVJGZURad3dMS2pweE1mZE9KNWV4bHdqc25lcnNPTnY0TDQ5Y3V2R1FSTkhCUWJhMnp3NnFJbWJmWkFQT1pDZ0tvZDgxVkp0NlFneUw4Z3pGYVVOVHRGNjc2VWgwWXclMjElMjE=) (CoE) for each course and submit it to the [Department of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Home_Affairs_\(Australia\)) of the [Australian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Australia).[^cite_note-\:1-13] [Overseas Student Health Cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Student_Health_Cover) (OSHC) is compulsory for all overseas students.[^cite_note-14] Each student visa applicant must prove that they have enough financial ability to pay for their tuition, books and daily living expenses while they study in Australia.[^cite_note-\:1-13] Overseas students enrolled in an English Language Intensive Courses for Overseas Students (ELICOS, offered by secondary schools)[^cite_note-15] with a student visa are required to study full time (tourist and visitors to Australia can also participate in ELICOS study during their stay).[^cite_note-\:6-16]

On 1 July 2016, after a new [Simplified Student Visa Framework](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/what-we-do/education-program/what-we-do/simplified-student-visa-framework) (SSVF) was implemented in Australia,[^cite_note-\:15-20] the Department of Home Affairs of Australian Government introduced a new immigration risk framework[^cite_note-21] to determine the amount of documents a student needs to submit with their application form, based on their country of citizenship and intended education provider.[^cite_note-22]

According to the new released [Simplified Student Visa Framework](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/what-we-do/education-program/what-we-do/simplified-student-visa-framework) (SSVF), foreign students are able to provide a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)[^cite_note-23] to meet the English language requirements for their student visa application.[^cite_note-\:15-20] The Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) is a piece of evidence to prove that the English language requirements for their course are equal to or higher than the requirements for a student visa. However, those foreign students who assessed at a higher immigration risk level will still need to attend an English language test and to provide evidence of their English language proficiency through one of the English tests accepted by Australia with an approved test score.[^cite_note-\:8-24]

### English language proficiency requirements

International students who apply to study in Australia[^cite_note-25] will need to meet English language proficiency requirements to obtain a valid [student visa](330265.md).[^cite_note-\:8-24] Students must have taken the English language test in the last two years before they apply for a students visa, or the validity will expire.[^cite_note-\:7-7]

#### English language proficiency evidence exemptions

All international students will need to meet the English language proficiency requirements to gain entry into their course and receive a student visa.[^cite_note-26]

Some international students are exempt from providing evidence of English language proficiency with their visa application if they satisfy one or more of the following requirements:[^cite_note-\:8-24][^cite_note-\:7-7]

- Students who are a citizen and [passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport) holder of [UK](31717.md), [USA](3434750.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md) or [Ireland](14560.md).

- An applicant who is a Foreign Affairs or, Defence sponsored student or a Secondary Exchange Student (AASES).

- Students who have completed at least five years of study in one or more of the following countries: [Australia](4689264.md), [UK](31717.md), [USA](3434750.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [South Africa](17416221.md), or [Ireland](14560.md).

- In last two years of applying for a student visa, students who have completed either the [Senior Secondary Certificate of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Secondary_Certificate_of_Education) or a course leading to a qualification from the [Australian Qualifications Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Qualifications_Framework)

  [^cite_note-27]

  &#x20;at the Certificate IV or higher level in Australia.

- Students enrolled full time in school studies as a principal course (including secondary exchange programs), a registered [postgraduate research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_research) or a standalone [English Language Intensive Courses for Overseas Students](https://www.asqa.gov.au/cricos-registration/english-language-intensive-courses-overseas-students-elicos) (ELICOS) program

All other students are required to provide evidence of English language proficiency (with a Confirmation of Enrolment(CoE) or a [minimum ielts score for australia student visa](https://www.atlantisvisas.com.au/blog/student-visa-australia-english-language-requirements)).

## Impact on Australia's economy

According to the survey prepared by Deloitte Access Economics and the Australian government, international education was predicted to contribute about seventeen billion to Australia's [GDP](12594.md) in a 2014-15 financial year.[^cite_note-\:14-34] This value is the sum of:

- Twelve billion of direct value which is the paid tuition fee to the education providers and the learning materials and services which sold to the students

- Five billion of indirect value, which is the payment of the education providers to their suppliers, for example, printing and information technology support services.

Apart from that, international students make a substantial contribution to the development of many regional areas as there is at least five per cent of students living and studying in those communities. In 2014–15, the international student spent about 3 billion of expenditure (for purchasing goods and services) to [Melbourne](17306237.md) while 900 million indirect value flowed to regional communities and massively supported the agricultural products and other linkages. Finally, the consequence of student consumption supported approximately 12,746 jobs in regional [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)).[^cite_note-\:14-34]

Furthermore, in 2014–2015, The stocks of international students were estimated to deliver about 130,000 skilled migrants after the graduation year. This number of skilled migrants had increased 3% of Australia's current workforce with [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education).[^cite_note-\:14-34] In the financial year of 2018, international students inject \$31.9 billion into the [Australian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Australia), directly boosting Australian jobs and wages, generating jobs, supporting wages, and lifting the living standards of Australia.[^cite_note-35]

International students inject almost \$32 billion into Australia's economy each year and support 130,000 Australian jobs. They also bring Australia and the world closer together, and built important diplomatic and personal ties.[^cite_note-36]

According to the warning by NSW auditors, Australian universities is now suffering from a "market concentration risk" since their finance and budget rely too much on foreign student fees. As the report shows, in the [New South Wales](21654.md), the international students tuition fees injects \$2.8 billion AUD into universities, which accounts for 28% of the total revenue.[^cite_note-\:20-37] The [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney) and the [University of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales), two universities in NSW, accounted for more than 50% of their total revenue from international students' tuition fees.[^cite_note-\:20-37]

## Controversies

In recent years, the increasing number of international students in Australia has raised several concerns. One major issue pertains to changes in Australia's international student intake policy and the [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination) based on the economic status of their home countries. Critics argue that Australia discriminates against citizens from certain countries, alleging that these citizens come from nations with lower economic opportunities compared to Australia.[^cite_note-38]

Another significant concern is the rising safety issues faced by international students. Despite Australia's status as a developed country, reports indicate that many international students experience mental health challenges, and the country lacks adequate preventive and support systems to address these issues. A 2019 report revealed that rising educational and financial pressures, isolation, homesickness, and difficulties in accessing mental health support have led to an increase in suicides among international students.[^cite_note-39] Scholars also express apprehension about the ability of international students to actively engage in English-based systems within Australia. For many international students, [English is not their first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_as_a_second_or_foreign_language), and they may struggle with it. Factors such as [social isolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_isolation) due to language barriers, racial and cultural differences, and other challenges increasingly hinder their ability to improve their English language skills.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Recent research highlights that international students who struggle to navigate the available supports in Australia, are increasingly turning to generative artificial intelligence technologies for support, which may compound their vulnerabilities. [^cite_note-42] As reliance on digital technologies for managing wellbeing is unevenly shaped by privilege, financial resources, and temporal flexibility, this raises new questions about the implications of international students' digital dependence for data justice. [^cite_note-43]

### Extension of 2019–20 Hong Kong protests into universities campus

Since March 2019, there has been a number of [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests) owing to the [2019 Hong Kong extradition bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Hong_Kong_extradition_bill). Some students from Hong Kong would like to support the protest in Hong Kong, but some of them said that they felt being monitored by the mainland students, and were provoked by the students from mainland China even if the Hong Kong students were solely expressing their opinion peacefully.[^cite_note-44] On 24 July 2019, there was a supporting protest at the Great Court of [Queensland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_University) (UQ) initiated by [Drew Pavlou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Pavlou), a student representative to UQ's senate. The protest was echoed by some students from Hong Kong. They urged for the acceptance of the five requests in Hong Kong (Complete withdrawal of the bill, Withdrawal determining the protests as "riots", cancellation of Political prosecution on the protesters, independent investigation on the violence of the police and the implementation of real universal suffrage), urging for the closure of the [Confucius Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius_Institute) at the Queensland University, shutting down the [Xinjiang internment camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_internment_camps). Nevertheless, the protest was developed into a conflict. Some students from mainland China (The Consulate-General of PRC in Brisbane claimed that also included students from Hong Kong,[^cite_note-45] but it was not proven by other media) initiated anti-separation and anti Hong Kong independence protests. Some mainland destroyed the Lennon Wall and poster established by the Hong Kong students, and openly sang the Chinese National Song. Pavlou was attacked by the Chinese student and was injured.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

PRC Consul-General in Brisbane responded by confirming the patriotic act of the mainland students and condemning the "anti-Chinese" activities initiated by the Hong Kong students.[^cite_note-51] In October 2019, Pavlou has sought a court order similar to a restraining order against the Chinese Consul-General in Brisbane, Xu Jie.[^cite_note-52]

## Immigration policy for international students

Most international students who come to study in Australia wish to reside here permanently.[^cite_note-\:0-9] The student-migration nexus in Australia is complex and is constantly evolving.[^cite_note-\:19-18]

The current immigration policy published by Australian government encourages foreign students to work and stay in regional areas, such as [Adelaide](1148.md) and [Tasmania](29944.md), by reducing the quota of [skilled independent visa](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/skilled-independent-189)[^cite_note-\:4-53] entrants (change from subclass 175[^cite_note-54] and subclass 885[^cite_note-55] to subclass 189[^cite_note-\:4-53]), and in the meantime increasing the number of [skilled regional (provisional) visa](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/skilled-regional-provisional-489)[^cite_note-\:5-56] (change from subclass 457 to 489[^cite_note-\:5-56]).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-\:0-9]

In 2017, the government accepted about 162,000 migrants, which was about 10% lower than the accepted amount in 2016. The long-term forecast of the Australian Government aims to set the cap of skilled immigrants to about 160,000.[^cite_note-\:0-9]

Some educational institutions in Australia offering courses primarily to international students have been accused of being [ghost colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_colleges_in_Australia). These institutions primarily serve to facilitate migration, and provide little or no legitimate educational offerings.[^cite_note-62]
