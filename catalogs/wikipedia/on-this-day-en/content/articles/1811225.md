**Makerfield** ([/ˈmeɪkərfiːld/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies_of_the_Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom)[^cite_note-n-3] in [Greater Manchester](79819.md) represented in the [House of Commons](13828.md) of the [UK Parliament](13964.md). It is currently represented by [Andy Burnham](414818.md), the current [Prime Minister](24150.md), who was elected after [Josh Simons](77295092.md)' resignation to allow Burnham to run in a [by-election](83181171.md) in June 2026. The seat has had only [Labour](19279158.md) MPs since its creation in 1983.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## History

This seat was formed in 1983 from parts of the [Ince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ince_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Wigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigan_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leigh_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and [Westhoughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westhoughton_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) constituencies.

## Constituency profile

Makerfield is considered one of the [safest Labour seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_seat) in the country, in terms of length of tenure by the party. The Labour Party held the predecessor seat of Ince from 1906 until 1983 when the current constituency was created.[^cite_note-6] In 2010 the constituency, of the 650 nationally, polled the 105th-highest share of the vote for the [Labour Party](19279158.md).&#x20;

Labour's majority fell significantly in 2019 as with many "[Red Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wall_\(British_politics\))" seats, however it did not fall to the Conservatives, unlike neighbouring [Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leigh_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)). In 2024, there was little increase in Labour's share of the vote or majority, instead there was a significant increase in Reform UK's vote share, pushing the Conservatives into third place. In 2015, Reform's predecessor, [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP), also came second, however Labour's majority was over 13,000, compared to over 5,000 over Reform in 2024. The [2026 by-election](83181171.md), however, saw a 3.5% swing back to Labour, in marked contrast to Reform comfortably leading the national polls at the time of the election.

There is no town called Makerfield itself; instead, the name refers to the suffix of 'in-Makerfield' of the towns [Ashton-in-Makerfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashton-in-Makerfield) and [Ince-in-Makerfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ince-in-Makerfield), though since 2010 the latter has been part of the Wigan constituency. The seat comprises mostly working-class residential suburbs south of Wigan and to the west of [Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leigh,_Greater_Manchester). Deprivation is lower than that of neighbouring towns, and home ownership is higher, with a mostly skilled working-class population and a lower-than-average proportion of ethnic minorities.[^cite_note-7] There is some semi-rural land towards the west of the constituency where it borders [St Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Helens,_Merseyside), and green buffers separating the constituent towns and villages. The area was formerly noted for coal-mining. There remains a small amount of light industry – though not as much as in Wigan – but residential land-use is increasing as the towns continue to grow. The area is also home to [Winstanley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winstanley_College), one of the highest-performing sixth-form colleges in the country, which has around 1800 students enrolled.

## Boundaries

### Historic

**1983–1997**: The Metropolitan Borough of Wigan wards of Abram, Ashton-Golborne, Bryn, Lightshaw, Orrell, Winstanley, and Worsley Mesnes.

**1997–2010**: The Metropolitan Borough of Wigan wards of Abram, Ashton-Golborne, Bryn, Ince, Orrell, Winstanley, and Worsley Mesnes.

**2010–2024**: The Metropolitan Borough of Wigan wards of Abram, Ashton, Bryn, Hindley, Hindley Green, Orrell, Winstanley, and Worsley Mesnes.

### Current

Further to the [2023 review of Westminster constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_review_of_Westminster_constituencies) which came into effect for 2024 general election, the boundaries of the constituency were expanded slightly by adding small areas (as they existed on 1 December 2020) of the Atherleigh ward (part of polling district LCA) and the Leigh West ward (polling district LDA).[^cite_note-8]

Following a local government boundary review which came into effect in May 2023,[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] the constituency now comprises the following wards of the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan from the 2024 general election:

- Abram; Ashton-in-Makerfield South; Bryn with Ashton-in-Makerfield North; Hindley; Hindley Green (*nearly all*); Leigh West (*small part*); Orrell; Winstanley; Worsley Mesnes; and very small parts of Golborne & Lowton West, and Ince.

  [^cite_note-11]

Makerfield consists of the western and central section of the [Metropolitan Borough of Wigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Wigan) in [Greater Manchester](79819.md). It comprises the wards to the south and to the west of [Wigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigan) and to the west of [Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leigh,_Greater_Manchester).
