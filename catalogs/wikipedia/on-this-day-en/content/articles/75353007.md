# Sudan Doctors Union



Sudan Doctors Union logo

[^cite_note-1]

[^cite_note-2]

&#x20;The **Sudan Doctors Union** (SDU) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): نقابة أطباء السودان),[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:2-4] also sometimes referred to as **Sudan Doctors Syndicate,** **Sudan Doctors' Trade Union**[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] or **Sudanese Doctors Association**, is a professional association of Sudanese doctors. It has been actively involved in addressing the challenges faced by the healthcare sector in Sudan.[^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-9] The SDU documents the fatalities and injuries during the Sudanese conflicts,[^cite_note-\:2-4][^cite_note-10] and their members have been subjected to shootings,[^cite_note-11] torture,[^cite_note-12] and abductions[^cite_note-13] by the government and armed forces in Sudan.[^cite_note-14]

## History

It is probable that the SDU became a recognised union following the [revolution in October 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_1964_Revolution) and the reestablishment of the Sudan Trade Union Law in 1966.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:3-16]

Through its history, the SDU has called for international support due to the collapse of the public health sector and the lack of public services provision in public facilities.[^cite_note-\:0-7] The SDU has highlighted that doctors have borne the brunt of a collapsed health system and have gone into several general strikes demanding improvement of the health system and doctors’ working conditions in Sudan.[^cite_note-\:0-7] The SDU has also been involved in providing medical and financial aid to wounded protesters during periods of civil unrest.[^cite_note-\:0-7] However, this has led to some members of the SDU being detained.[^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-\:1-8]

During the [Sudanese revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_revolution) of 2019, the **Central Committee of Sudan Doctors** (CCSD) was one of the trade unions that formed the [Sudanese Professionals Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Professionals_Association) (SPA) which had a prominent role in the protests.[^cite_note-17]

In the midst of the [2023 conflict in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Sudan_\(2023\)), influential social media users who were in favor of the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) launched an assault on the Sudanese Doctors Union. They wrongfully alleged that the SDU was biased towards the [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) and the [Janjaweed](683270.md). These unfounded claims posed a threat to both the standing and security of the healthcare workers.[^cite_note-18] Despite these challenges, the SDS continues to advocate for improvements in the healthcare sector and the working conditions of doctors in Sudan.[^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-\:1-8]

The SDU established global branches to support Sudanese doctors living abroad. These include a branch in the United Kingdom set up in the mid-1970s,[^cite_note-19] one in the USA,[^cite_note-20] another in Ireland founded in 2010,[^cite_note-21] and a Canadian branch that was established on 24 February 2019.[^cite_note-22] The SDU in Qatar, which opened a helpline within two days of the start of the war in Sudan (2023), is staffed by 136 doctors from more than 36 specialties.[^cite_note-23]
