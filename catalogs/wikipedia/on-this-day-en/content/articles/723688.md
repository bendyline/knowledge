The **Canada men's national soccer team** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Équipe du Canada de soccer masculin*)[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] represents [Canada](5042916.md) in men's international [soccer](10568.md), which is governed by the [Canadian Soccer Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Soccer_Association) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Association canadienne de soccer*), the governing body for [soccer in Canada](579183.md) founded in 1912. It has been an affiliate member of [FIFA](11049.md) since 1912 and a founding affiliate member of [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) since 1961.[^cite_note-6] Regionally, it is an affiliate member of [NAFU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Football_Union) in the North American Zone.

Canada has played in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) three times: [1986](157231.md),[^cite_note-7] [2022](17742072.md), and [2026](36581929.md). Their best result came in 2026 where they co-hosted the tournament and reached the round of 16. It has qualified for the [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) once ([2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_Confederations_Cup)).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Canada has participated twenty times in CONCACAF's premier continental competition, winning the [CONCACAF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Championship) in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_CONCACAF_Championship) and the successor [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup). It also finished as runners-up in the [CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Nations_League) in the [2023 finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Finals). It participated once in the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), finishing in fourth place in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica).

Regionally, the team won the [North American Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Nations_Cup) as hosts in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_North_American_Nations_Cup).

## History

### Early years

Football was being played in Canada with the Dominion Football Association (1877) and [Western Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Football_Association) (1880) acting as precursors to the modern-day Canadian Soccer Association.[^cite_note-10] In 1885, the WFA sent a team to [New Jersey](21648.md) to take on a side put forth by the [American Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_Association_\(1884%E2%80%931924\)), the then-unofficial governing body of the sport in the United States. On November 28, 1885, in an unofficial friendly, Canada defeated the [United States](582488.md) 1–0 at Clark Field in the [East Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Newark,_New_Jersey) neighbourhood of [Kearny, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kearny,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The American team won 3–2 in a return match one year later.[^cite_note-14] In 1888, a team represented the WFA in a [tour of the British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1888_Great_Britain_Tour), earning a record of nine wins, five draws, and nine losses.[^cite_note-15] The squad comprised 16 Canadian-born players with the only exception being tour organizer [David Forsyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Forsyth_\(soccer\)), who had immigrated to Canada one year after his birth.[^cite_note-history-16]



The [Galt F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galt_F.C.) team that represented Canada and won gold at the [1904 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1904_Summer_Olympics)

In 1904 [Galt F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galt_F.C.) represented the WFA at the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1904_Summer_Olympics) in [St. Louis](27687.md), [Missouri](19571.md).[^cite_note-17] As just one of three teams competing, Galt defeated two American clubs, [Christian Brothers College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Brothers_College_High_School) (7–0) and [St. Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Rose_Parish) (4–0) to win the tournament.[^cite_note-\:0-18] The *[Toronto Mail and Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Mail_and_Empire)* of November 18, 1904, reports that "Immediately after the game, the Galt aggregation, numbering about 50 persons, retired to the office of James W. Sullivan, chief of the Department of Physical Culture, where they received their prize. After a short talk by Mr. James E. Conlon of the Physical Culture Department, Mayor Mundy, of the City of Galt, presented each player on the winning team with a beautiful gold medal."[^cite_note-\:0-18] The medals are clearly engraved with the name of the company in St. Louis that made them.

In 1905, a British team of touring amateurs nicknamed the "Pilgrims" toured Canada,[^cite_note-\:1-19] with their match against Galt billed as the "championship of the world".[^cite_note-\:2-20] The match was played in front of 3500 fans in [Galt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galt,_Ontario), now part of [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Ontario), [Ontario](22218.md), and ended in a 3–3 draw.[^cite_note-\:2-20] Earlier the Pilgrims had been beaten 2–1 by Berlin Rangers, in the city now known as [Kitchener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchener,_Ontario).[^cite_note-\:1-19]



The team that toured Australia in 1924

The Canadian national team toured Australia in 1924, playing a series of "test" friendlies against their hosts, including their first official match, a 3–2 friendly defeat to the [Australian national team](4792249.md) in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane), [Queensland](59051.md) on June 7, 1924.[^cite_note-21] Canada also played Australia at the Jubilee Oval, Adelaide on Saturday July 12, 1924, and defeated them by 4 goals to 1.[^cite_note-22] In 1925, Canada played their old rivals, the [United States](582488.md), in [Montreal](7954681.md), winning 1–0 on [Ed McLaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_McLaine)'s goal.[^cite_note-23] In a return match in November 1925 in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](8210131.md), Canada was defeated 1–6.[^cite_note-24] One year later, Canada lost 2–6 to the Americans in the same city[^cite_note-25] before playing four internationals in a 1927 tour of New Zealand.[^cite_note-history-16]

The New Zealand tour included a total of 22 games, of which Canada won 19 with only 2 defeats. Most of the games were against local combined teams although Canada also played [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_association_football_team) in four occasions (scores: 2–2, 2–1, 0–1, 4–1).[^cite_note-26]

### 1928 to 1987



Canadian football team in Mexico City on July 5, 1957

Following the lead of British football associations, Canada withdrew from [FIFA](11049.md) in 1928 over a dispute regarding broken time payments to amateur players.[^cite_note-27] They rejoined the confederation in 1946 and took part in World Cup qualifying in the [North American Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Football_Confederation) (NAFC) (a precursor to CONCACAF) for the first time in 1957,[^cite_note-28] the first time they had played as a national team in 30 years. Under the guidance of head coach [Don Petrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Petrie), Canada defeated the United States in [Toronto](64646.md) 5–1 in their opening game, but lost two games in Mexico (failing to play a home game due to financial reasons) 0–2 and 0–3 before defeating the U.S. 3–2 in St. Louis. [Mexico](808402.md) advanced as group winners, meaning that Canada missed out on the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) in Sweden.[^cite_note-history-16]

Canada withdrew from World Cup qualifying for [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup) due to scheduling constraints[^cite_note-29] and did not enter a team for [1966](61629.md).[^cite_note-auto-30] They did compete in football however at the [1967 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1967_Pan_American_Games),[^cite_note-auto-30] their first time to do so in the sixth edition of the games, which they hosted in [Winnipeg](100730.md). Canada finished fourth place,[^cite_note-auto-30] helped somewhat by defending champion [Brazil](149286.md)'s absence.

A 0–0 draw away to [Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_national_football_team) meant the Canadians, under coach [Peter Dinsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Dinsdale), could not advance out of the first round of [qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the [1970 World Cup](157227.md).[^cite_note-31] Dinsdale was replaced by [Frank Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Pike_\(soccer\)).[^cite_note-32] In their [second participation in soccer at the Pan Am games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1971_Pan_American_Games), held in [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali), Canada finished second in their opening round group (to hosts [Colombia](680175.md)). In the final group round however, they managed only one win (over Colombia) and finished next to last.

Canada again failed at the first hurdle in qualifying for the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-auto-30] Under German head coach [Eckhard Krautzun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckhard_Krautzun), they finished second in a home and away qualifying group for the [1973 CONCACAF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_CONCACAF_Championship) (to Mexico). For the [1975 Pan Am Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1975_Pan_American_Games), Canada, along with most of the larger Pan Am countries, sent their Olympic team, which was amateur (and senior aged), to compete. After narrowly qualifying out of the first round, the Canucks were soundly defeated by [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team), [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_national_football_team), and Mexico, conceding a total of 14 goals while scoring none.[^cite_note-33] At the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics) at home the following year, under head coach [Colin Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Morris_\(Canadian_soccer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), the amateur Canadian side failed to get out of the first round, losing both of their games.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] This despite the brilliant play of [Jimmy Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Douglas_\(Canadian_soccer\)), who scored a goal against a [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv)-dominated [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) side and another goal against [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team), Canada's only two goals in the tournament.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-KatzerBudy2010-37]

In their North American qualifying group for the [1977 CONCACAF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_CONCACAF_Championship), with both group winners and runners-up now advancing, Canada, again under head coach Krautzun, qualified as runners-up after defeating the Americans 3–0 in a neutral site one-match play-off, played in [Port-au-Prince](57391.md), [Haiti](13373.md).[^cite_note-38] In the championship, played in [Monterrey](40455.md) and [Mexico City](18987.md), Mexico won all five of their matches with a +15 goal difference to win the tournament handily.[^cite_note-\:3-39] Canada finished fourth.[^cite_note-\:3-39]

Matters were different however at the next [CONCACAF championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_CONCACAF_Championship), in 1981, played in [Tegucigalpa](25994100.md), Honduras. Canada entered the tournament raising eyebrows by winning their qualifying group over Mexico and the United States, even achieving a 1–1 draw against Mexico at the [Azteca Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azteca_Stadium) with [Gerry Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Gray_\(soccer\)) scoring from a direct free kick in the 88th-minute.[^cite_note-40] In the final round, the Canadians opened strongly with a 1–0 win over [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team),[^cite_note-41] with [Mike Stojanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Stojanovic) the goal-scorer, and a 1–1 draw with [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti_national_football_team), with Stojanovic scoring again. They next lost to the hosts [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) 1–2 and then drew Mexico 1–1 with [Ian Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Bridge) scoring the equalizer via a corner kick.[^cite_note-42] A win in their final game against Cuba would have put them through to [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), but they were held to a 2–2 draw, allowing El Salvador to qualify as tournament runners-up.

Between 1981 and 1985, Canada continued to develop under the guidance of English coach [Tony Waiters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Waiters). After a strong performance at the [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics), Waiters would see the Maple Leafs through to their first World Cup finals appearance in 1985. A 1–1 away draw to [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_national_football_team) was key in allowing them to eliminate *Los Chapines* in the first round group.[^cite_note-43] The second round was also closely contested, in part as this Canadian squad was strong defensively but had limited ability to score goals. The Canucks managed to eke out a 1–0 away win over Honduras, thanks to a [George Pakos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pakos) winner,[^cite_note-44] hold Costa Rica scoreless in [San José](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9,_Costa_Rica),[^cite_note-45] and then in their final game, one they needed to draw to qualify, beat *Los Catrachos* a second time, 2–1 in [St. John's](70581.md), [Newfoundland](21980.md), with Pakos and [Igor Vrablic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Vrablic) the goal scorers.[^cite_note-46] The victory not only secured their first World Cup finals berth,[^cite_note-47] but also the crown of CONCACAF champions for the first time, although Mexico did not compete, having already qualified automatically for the World Cup as hosts.[^cite_note-48]

At the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md), Canada impressed defensively in their first game against [France](250197.md), only conceding a late [Jean-Pierre Papin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Papin) goal after Papin had missed several earlier chances.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] However, Canada could not build on their stubborn performance against France, losing their next two matches to both [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) and the Soviet Union 0–2,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] finishing the group stage in last place with zero points.[^cite_note-53]

Four Canadian players ([Chris Chueden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Chueden), [Hector Marinaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hector_Marinaro_\(soccer,_born_1964\)), [David Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Norman_\(soccer\)) and Vrablic) were involved in a match fixing betting scandal at the [Merlion Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlion_Cup) tournament in Singapore two months after the World Cup. The four players were suspended by the Canadian Soccer Association for "bringing the game into disrepute". Norman was reinstated in 1992 after admitting his involvement in the scandal. Vrablic never played for Canada again.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

### 1990s

Qualification for the [1990 World Cup](157232.md) lasted all of two matches for Canada, a home-and-away series with Guatemala, played in October 1988. The Central Americans won the first game 1–0 in [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City)[^cite_note-56] while Canada prevailed in [Vancouver](32706.md) 3–2.[^cite_note-57] Tied on goal difference, *Los Chapines* advanced on the [away goals rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_goals_rule).[^cite_note-58]

1990 saw Canada take part in the first [North American Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Nations_Cup), hosting the three-team tournament. Mexico and Canada sent their full squads, but the [United States](582488.md) sent a 'B' team.[^cite_note-\:4-59] Canada won the [tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_North_American_Nations_Cup) after a 1–0 win over the United States on May 6[^cite_note-\:4-59] and a 2–1 win over Mexico on May 13.[^cite_note-\:4-59] All three Canadian goals were scored by [John Catliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Catliff),[^cite_note-60] the tournament's top scorer.

Canada came close to qualifying for the World Cup again in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)) under the guidance of a defender on the 1986 team, [Bob Lenarduzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Lenarduzzi).[^cite_note-61] They entered the tournament at the second round stage and advanced as group runners-up.[^cite_note-62] Canada competed strongly in the final qualifying round, drawing their first match in Tegucigalpa after a controversial penalty allowed the Hondurans to draw even, winning their next two, over El Salvador and Honduras in [Vancouver](32706.md), losing convincingly at Azteca Stadium, and winning 2–1 in [San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador). They went into their final group match against Mexico, in Toronto, needing a win to win the group and thus qualify directly for the World Cup.[^cite_note-\:5-63] Canada went up 1–0 on a goal credited to [Alex Bunbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Bunbury) off a free kick, but Mexico scored twice to win, 2–1.[^cite_note-64] The loss meant Canada finished second and advanced to an intercontinental play-off series where they needed to win two rounds to qualify for the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-\:5-63] The Reds went up against [Oceania Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation)'s champions Australia. Canada won the first leg 2–1 in Edmonton.[^cite_note-65] Australia led the second leg 2–1 at the end of 90 minutes, sending the tie to extra time. There was no score in the extra 30 minutes, meaning the series was decided by a [penalty shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout_\(association_football\)) which Australia won 4–1 to eliminate Canada from contention.[^cite_note-66] Australia went on to lose 2–1 on aggregate to [Argentina](454699.md), who advanced to the World Cup.[^cite_note-67]

With the World Cup to be played in the United States, Canada had the opportunity to play a number of high-profile squads in tune-up matches.[^cite_note-\:6-68] They played against [Morocco](1024865.md), Brazil, [Germany](250204.md), [Spain](609825.md), and the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) all within 13 days.[^cite_note-\:6-68] Canada held eventual World Cup champions Brazil to a 1–1 draw at [Commonwealth Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Stadium_\(Edmonton\)), on a 69th-minute equalizer by [Eddy Berdusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddy_Berdusco), on Canada's only real scoring chance in the game.[^cite_note-69]

With three countries set to qualify out of CONCACAF for the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), and with Canada handily winning their second round group over El Salvador, [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team), and Cuba,[^cite_note-70] expectations were high for a second qualification in 12 years in the spring of 1997. The aging Canadians, however, fared miserably, losing their opening game to Mexico 0–4 and the following match to the U.S. 0–3. In their next two matches, against El Salvador and [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_football_team), they could only manage two 0–0 draws in Vancouver. A 1–0 win over Costa Rica in Edmonton in their next match thanks to a goal by Berdusco gave Canada some hope at the halfway point but losses to both Jamaica and El Salvador away ended any aspirations as they finished bottom of the group with 6 points from 10 games and a −15 goal difference. Having overseen two consecutive World Cup campaigns ending in the side failing to qualify, Lenarduzzi stepped down in 1997 and was replaced by interim head coach [Bruce Twamley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Twamley).

### 2000s

A headshot of Carlo Corazzin, winner of the 2000 Gold Cup's Golden Boot.

Carlo Corazzin, who won the Golden Boot award during the 2000 CONCACAF Gold Cup

The Canadian Soccer Association turned to another German to lead the senior national team in 1998 with the signing of [Holger Osieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Osieck).[^cite_note-71] Success came quickly with Canada winning the [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) in February 2000.[^cite_note-72] After emerging from the first round on a coin-toss tiebreaker with invited side [Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_national_football_team), the Canucks scored a quarter-final upset win over Mexico.[^cite_note-73] The win set the stage for an unprecedented run to the final, where Canada defeated [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_national_football_team) 2–0 at [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum).[^cite_note-74] Canada swept the awards ceremony, with goalkeeper [Craig Forrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Forrest) winning MVP honours, [Carlo Corazzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Corazzin) securing the Golden Boot, and [Richard Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hastings) named Rookie of the Tournament.[^cite_note-75]

Expectations were again high following the winter's result, but the campaign sputtered. A positive 1–0 away result in [Havana](49719.md) in June was followed by a listless 0–0 home draw against Cuba.[^cite_note-76] For the semi-final round two out of four teams advanced. Canada was eliminated from World Cup contention after finishing third in the semi-final round. Canada managed just one goal in 6 games while conceding 8 to finish third in the standings, well adrift of advancing sides [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_national_football_team) and Mexico.

Winning the Gold Cup earned Canada a place in the [2001 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), where the highlight was holding Brazil to a 0–0 draw.[^cite_note-77] The Gold Cup victory also won them an invitation to compete in the [2001 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). When security concerns prompted the cancellation of the tournament, Canada disbanded their training camp.[^cite_note-78] The tournament was then reinstated and held on schedule.[^cite_note-79] The Canadian Soccer Association announced they would not be able to participate in the reinstated tournament.[^cite_note-80]

Canada had another strong showing in the [2002 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), losing to the United States in the semi-finals in penalties, and then defeating South Korea in the third-place game, 2–1. The Gold Cup was held [the following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), and Canada was eliminated in the first round on goal difference, leading to head coach Osieck resigning in September 2003 and former player [Colin Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Miller_\(soccer,_born_1964\)) put in charge as an interim.

2004 marked the beginning of [2006 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)) and a new era under the guidance of former Canadian skipper [Frank Yallop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Yallop). Things began brightly, with the Canadians dispatching of [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_national_football_team) handily in the preliminary round, 8–0 on aggregate, in a home-and-home series. Matters turned, however, with Canada finishing bottom in a group featuring Costa Rica, Guatemala, and Honduras. They got only 5 points from 6 matches and a −4 goal difference. Hard times continued under Yallop as the Canucks again went out at the first barrier in the [Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), losing to both the U.S. and Costa Rica, while defeating Cuba. The coach stayed on through 2005 into the following summer, overseeing a series a friendlies against European sides. He resigned on June 7, 2006, finishing with a win-loss record of 8–9–3.

Things turned around under interim coach [Stephen Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hart_\(footballer\))'s guidance. Canada opened their [2007 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) campaign with a 2–1 win over Costa Rica. A 1–2 upset loss to upstarts [Guadeloupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadeloupe_national_football_team) was followed by a 2–0 victory over Haiti, securing Canada first-place in their group. They next beat Guatemala 3–0 in their quarter-final match setting up a semi-final showdown with the host Americans. Substitute [Iain Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iain_Hume) scored for Canada in the 76th minute to cut the United States' lead to 2–1. After the United States were reduced to ten men, Canada pressed for the equalizer but were denied when [Atiba Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiba_Hutchinson)'s stoppage-time goal was incorrectly flagged offside by linesman Ricardo Louisville and Canada was eliminated.

The team faced criticism for its poor handling of goalkeeper [Greg Sutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Sutton_\(soccer\)), who suffered a concussion during a practice prior to the start of the Gold Cup. Without a doctor accompanying the team, Sutton instead saw a local physician who cleared him to practice, resulting in Sutton suffering post-concussion syndrome. Sutton was lost to his professional club [Toronto FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_FC) for nearly a year.[^cite_note-81]

Prior to the Gold Cup on May 18, 2007, the Canadian Soccer Association announced that former national team player [Dale Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Mitchell_\(soccer\)) would take over as head coach of the senior team after the [2007 FIFA U-20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_U-20_World_Cup). Mitchell had previously served as an assistant coach under Coach Frank Yallop. Under Mitchell, Canada drew friendlies with [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_national_football_team) and against Costa Rica, lost 0–2 to [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team), had a 1–0 win over [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique_national_football_team), and a 0–2 defeat to [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia_national_football_team). Optimism grew, however, as Canada played well in a 2–3 loss to Brazil.

Despite defeating [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines_national_football_team) 7–1 on aggregate in a second-round series—they had had a [bye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bye_\(sport\)) in the first—Canada did not play at the level they had at the Gold Cup and were eliminated from [qualifying for the 2010 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification). They conceded an equalizer shortly after scoring the opening goal in a 1–1 draw to Jamaica at [BMO Field](3163911.md), conceded two second-half goals in quick succession in a 1–2 home loss to Honduras at [Saputo Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saputo_Stadium), and then lost away to Mexico and Honduras. They finished last in the four-team group with just 2 points from 6 matches. On March 27, 2009, head coach Dale Mitchell was fired. The president of the Canadian Soccer Association, Dominic Maestracci, said that "the Canadian Soccer Association is committed to the future of our men's national team program. We have made this decision to move the program in a new direction."[^cite_note-82] Technical director Stephen Hart was renamed as interim head coach. On December 9, 2009, Hart was named as head coach.

### 2010s



The national soccer team of Canada in 2010

Stephen Hart's first competitive action as the full-time head coach was a poor showing at the [2011 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), not managing to get out of the group stage. However, during the early stages [qualifying for the 2014 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)), Canada topped their group in the second round but were eliminated in the [third round of CONCACAF qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round#Group_C), finishing one point behind Honduras and Panama after losing 8–1 in Honduras on the final match day.



Canada during the national anthem prior to the qualifying match against [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_men's_national_football_team) on June 12, 2012, at [BMO Field](3163911.md)

After a series of interim coaching changes following Stephen Hart's dismissal on October 12, 2012 [Benito Floro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Floro) replaced Colin Miller as Canada's coach on August 1, 2013.[^cite_note-83] Being a coach with top-flight management experience in [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga), he was expected to help Canada raise its competitiveness prior to [2018 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification). In the midst of Floro's player identification and restructuring phase, the team experienced many difficulties including a 958-minute goal-scoring drought, which was finally broken by Atiba Hutchinson in a 1–1 draw with [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) on May 23, 2014. Despite showing improvement with two draws in Europe, Canada continued to shed FIFA points having gone winless for nearly two years, and sank to their lowest ever FIFA ranking of 122 in August 2014. Canada ended a 16-match winless streak on September 10, 2014, defeating Jamaica 3–1 in Toronto.[^cite_note-84]

Canada was drawn into the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) second round of qualifying against [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica_national_football_team) in June 2015.[^cite_note-85] Canada entered the [second round of 2018 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_second_round) against Dominica with a game at Windsor Park in Dominica which they won 2–0 with goals from [Cyle Larin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyle_Larin) and a penalty converted by [Russell Teibert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Teibert). In the return leg at BMO Field in front of 9,749 fans they defeated Dominica 4–0 with two goals from [Tosaint Ricketts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosaint_Ricketts) and one each from [Tesho Akindele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesho_Akindele) and Cyle Larin.

The team did not score a single goal and finished last in their group in the [2015 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) after two 0–0 draws to El Salvador and Costa Rica, while also suffering a 1–0 loss against Jamaica.[^cite_note-86] Canada then advanced to the [third round of 2018 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round) against Belize, winning 4–1 on aggregate and advancing to the [fourth round of 2018 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_fourth_round). Canada was drawn into a group against Honduras, El Salvador and Mexico. They played their first pair of matches in the fourth round on November 13 and 17, 2015. The first match was played in Vancouver at [BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place) against Honduras, resulting in a 1–0 win for Canada thanks to a deflected goal by Cyle Larin. The crowd of 20,108 set a new record for the Canadian men's team in the province of [British Columbia](3392.md).[^cite_note-87] In their next game on November 17, away at El Salvador, Canada drew with El Salvador 0-0 as [Julian De Guzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_De_Guzman) broke Canada's record for most caps for the national team with his 85th cap, passing [Paul Stalteri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Stalteri)'s record of 84 caps.[^cite_note-88] With this result in Canada's last game of 2015, they ended off the year conceding just three goals in their final 12 games and in 14 games overall, they ended off with a record of 6 wins, 6 draws, and 2 losses.

On March 25, 2016, in a World Cup qualifier against Mexico at BC Place, 54,798 people were recorded in the stadium which set a new attendance record for a Canadian national team of any sport.[^cite_note-89] Ultimately, however, Canada lost the game 3–0, but remained in second place in the group, keeping them in contention for World Cup qualification. On September 6, 2016, after failing to qualify for the fifth round of 2018 World Cup qualifying despite a 3–1 win over El Salvador, head coach Benito Floro was sacked on September 14, ending his reign as coach of the national team.[^cite_note-90]

Canada recorded a historic 2–0 win against the [United States](582488.md) at [BMO Field](3163911.md) on October 15, 2019.

Canada announced [Octavio Zambrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavio_Zambrano) as the new coach of the national team on May 16, 2017,[^cite_note-91] replacing [Michael Findlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Findlay_\(soccer\)) who was the interim coach after Floro's departure.[^cite_note-92] He guided Canada to a quarterfinal finish at the [2017 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), with the team getting out of the group stage for the first time since [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup).[^cite_note-93] However, on January 8, 2018, Zambrano was let go and was replaced with [John Herdman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herdman),[^cite_note-94] who previously was the head coach of the [Canadian women's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_women's_national_soccer_team).

Under Herdman, Canada qualified for the top division in the [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_CONCACAF_Nations_League) of the [CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Nations_League) following an undefeated [qualifying campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_CONCACAF_Nations_League_qualifying).[^cite_note-95] Competing in [CONCACAF Nations League A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_CONCACAF_Nations_League_A), Canada earned a 2–0 victory over the United States at BMO Field, Canada's first win against their American rivals since 1985.[^cite_note-96] However, Canada would fall to a 4–1 defeat against the United States in the away leg and failed to qualify for the [Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Finals).[^cite_note-97]

### 2020s

Canada's national team was marked by the arrival of a [golden generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_generation) of new young players, led by the first Canadian [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) winner [Alphonso Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonso_Davies) of [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich), the most expensive Canadian soccer player in history, [Jonathan David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_David) who joined [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille_OSC) for a €30-million fee in 2020 and the establishment of the [Canadian Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Premier_League), the first fully professional soccer league in the country.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] In the [first round of World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_first_round), Canada finished with a 4–0–0 record to win Group B and progress to the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_second_round).[^cite_note-101] The second round was two leg home-and-away tie against [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti_national_football_team) which Canada won 4–0 on aggregate with a 1–0 win in the away leg and a 3–0 win in the home leg, the latter being played at [SeatGeek Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeatGeek_Stadium) in [Chicago](6886.md) due to [COVID-19 restrictions in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada). The victory over Haiti led to Canada qualifying for the [third and final round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round) of World Cup qualifying for the first time since 1997.[^cite_note-\:7-102]

#### 2022 FIFA World Cup

Canada began the [third round of World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round) unbeaten in its first eleven matches, finishing 2021 with its [first win over Mexico in over 20 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_v_Mexico_\(2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification\)) to finish the year at the top of the table.[^cite_note-103] It also ended the year 40th in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings), its highest-ever position to date, earning the team the honour of "Most Improved Side" after having started the year ranked 72nd.[^cite_note-104]

On March 27, 2022, Canada defeated Jamaica [4–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round#Matches#Matchday_13) on Matchday 13 to qualify for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in [Qatar](8238258.md). This ended a 36-year drought since the first and only time Canada played in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), in [1986](157231.md).[^cite_note-105] The speed of the team's ascent was such that it was subsequently revealed that neither the federation nor [kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(association_football\)) supplier [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) had anticipated them qualifying, and as a result they would be the only team in Qatar to not receive a special kit for the occasion. Defender [Sam Adekugbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Adekugbe) remarked "I think that just shows that no one really believed in us. I don't think Canada believed."[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

Main article: [2022 FIFA World Cup Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F)



Canada at the 2022 World Cup

In Canada's first match of the tournament against [Belgium](679716.md) on November 23, Davies failed to score an early penalty in an eventual 1–0 loss, despite Canada dictating most of the play, and failing to convert any of their 22 shots.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] Four days later, Davies scored Canada's first ever goal at the World Cup, in a game against [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team). Croatia came back to win 4–1, eliminating Canada from the tournament after two matches.[^cite_note-110] Canada were defeated 2–1 by [Morocco](1024865.md) in their final group match on December 1, finishing in last place in their group with zero points.[^cite_note-111] Post-event assessments of Canada's performance were mixed, with many saying that opportunities had been missed, while also creating possibilities for the future [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) that Canada would co-host.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

#### Road to 2026 World Cup

Canada automatically qualified for the 2026 World Cup as co-host in February 2023. They also qualified to the [2023 CONCACAF Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Finals) by winning [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_CONCACAF_Nations_League#Group_C). A 2–0 victory over [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team) on June 15, 2023, saw the national team reach its first final in 23 years, where they lost to the United States 2–0.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] Following this, many of the team's established players opted not to attend the [2023 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) held later that same summer. After some difficulties in the group stage, Canada reached the quarter-final of the tournament, losing to the United States in a penalty shootout.[^cite_note-119] The summer championships marked the end of Herdman's tenure with Canada Soccer, with his departure coming at the end of August to manage [Toronto FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_FC) of [Major League Soccer](71802.md), amidst media discussion of conflicts with the federation over funding and playing opportunities.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

Interim coach [Mauro Biello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauro_Biello) was tasked with guiding the team through the quarter-finals of the [2023–24 CONCACAF Nations League A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_CONCACAF_Nations_League_A) in November 2023, where victory in a two-legged aggregate match or [away goals rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_goals_rule) against Jamaica would both send them to the following spring's semi-finals and qualify them to participate in [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL)'s [2024 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). After defeating Jamaica 2–1 in the away leg, Canada needed only a draw in the home match at BMO Field to advance. Leading halfway through, the team collapsed to lose 3–2 in the second half and 3–2 on away goals, as a result missing both the Nations League semi-final and immediate qualification to the Copa América.[^cite_note-CBC231121-123][^cite_note-124] The loss to Jamaica prompted considerable criticism of the team, Biello, and the federation,[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-Athletic231122-126][^cite_note-127] with *[The Athletic](54936152.md)* opining that Canada's "humiliating loss" should be considered its worst defeat since falling 8–1 to Honduras in World Cup qualification in 2012.[^cite_note-Athletic231122-126] As a result, the [qualifying play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_qualifying_play-offs) for a final place in the Copa América was scheduled on March 23, 2024, against [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_national_football_team),[^cite_note-CBC231121-123][^cite_note-128] which they later won 2–0.[^cite_note-129]

In May 2024, Canada Soccer hired [Jesse Marsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Marsch) to serve as Herdman's permanent replacement as coach.[^cite_note-130] Due to ongoing financial difficulties, the money to pay Marsch was found via donations from MLS clubs [CF Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF_Montreal), Toronto FC, and [Vancouver Whitecaps FC](22060729.md) and other private parties. This drew media attention due to the unusual arrangement of the [naming rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_rights) to the coaching position being given as part of the arrangement, called the "MLS Canada Men's National Team Head Coach".[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] In advance of the Copa América, Marsch's first matches with the team were two friendlies against high-profile opponents, first a lopsided 4–0 loss to the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_men's_national_football_team),[^cite_note-133] and then a goalless draw with second-ranked [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_men's_national_football_team) that was generally considered a major success.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] Canada began [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Group_A) play at the Copa against world No. 1-ranked [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_men's_national_football_team), a 2–0 loss that nevertheless was generally considered a credible showing.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-Sportsnet240709-137] They defeated [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_men's_national_football_team) 1–0, with David scoring the team's first goal of the Marsch era to earn the country's first victory over a [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) team since the [2000 Gold Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup_final).[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] A draw with [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_men's_national_football_team) saw Canada finish second in the group and advance to the knockout stage,[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] where they defeated [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_national_football_team) 4–3 on penalties after a 1–1 draw in regulation time to reach a semi-final rematch with Argentina.[^cite_note-Sportsnet240709-137] After another 2–0 loss to the Argentines, Canada played [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay_men's_national_football_team) in the third-place match. They led 2–1 for the final ten minutes of regulation, but allowed a tying goal in stoppage time, and lost 4–3 on penalties to finish fourth.[^cite_note-142]

On September 7, 2024, Canada defeated the United States 2–1 in a friendly match in [Kansas City, Kansas](115009.md), marking their first victory against the Americans on American soil since 1957.[^cite_note-143] Following two victories over [Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_national_football_team) in the November 2024 [CONCACAF Nations League quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_CONCACAF_Nations_League_A), the team rose to 31st in the FIFA rankings, a new high.[^cite_note-144] Entering the [2024–25 Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Finals) with the hopes of claiming the team's first trophy in a generation, Canada instead lost 2–0 to Mexico in the semi-final match.[^cite_note-145] They rallied to defeat the United States 2–1 in the third-place match.[^cite_note-146] This was the first time since 1985 that Canada had defeated the United States in consecutive meetings.[^cite_note-147]

On June 7, 2025, Canada defeated [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team) in a match at BMO Field, the team's first victory against a [UEFA](32332.md) side since March 2011.[^cite_note-148] Later in the month, the team competed at the [2025 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), its second chance that year to win a trophy. After defeating Honduras 6–0 in their tournament opener,[^cite_note-149] they drew [Curaçao](24862626.md) and beat El Salvador 2–0, finishing first in their group. Heavy favourites in the quarter-final against [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_national_football_team), they held a 1–0 lead before [Jacob Shaffelburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Shaffelburg) was sent off with a second yellow card. The Guatemalans subsequently tied the game, before prevailing 6–5 on penalties. Canada's performance in the tournament was widely criticized.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]

#### 2026 FIFA World Cup



BC Place in Vancouver before Canada's Group B match vs Qatar at the 2026 FIFA World Cup.

Canada's preparations for its home World Cup were dogged by injuries to several key players, most notably Alphonso Davies, who was announced to be unavailable for the team's opening match.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] Scoring potential was also questioned, having only tallied seven goals in its final eight pre-tournament contests,[^cite_note-155] and with star striker Jonathan David having had a poor club season.[^cite_note-156]

BMO Field hosted Canada's opening match of the World Cup on June 12, 2026, facing [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md). Despite conceding the first ever World Cup goal scored in the country in the first half, substitute Cyle Larin equalized for Canada in the second half, and they secured their first World Cup point via a 1–1 draw.[^cite_note-157] Appearing in their second group stage match at Vancouver's [BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place) on June 18, Canada beat [Qatar](1111350.md) 6–0 to earn their first win in the World Cup. Their six-goal margin of victory was the widest for a CONCACAF team in World Cup history,[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] with David scoring the first World Cup hat trick for a host nation since [1966](61629.md).[^cite_note-160] Midfielder [Ismaël Koné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isma%C3%ABl_Kon%C3%A9) suffered a fractured left leg as a result of a tackle by Qatar's [Assim Madibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assim_Madibo),[^cite_note-161] which the team vowed to use as motivation in the remainder of the tournament.[^cite_note-162] After a 2–1 loss to [Switzerland](681862.md), Canada finished second in [Group B](81660182.md), qualifying to the [round of 32](81667041.md).[^cite_note-163] In the country's first-ever appearance in a World Cup knockout match, [Stephen Eustáquio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Eust%C3%A1quio) scored the lone goal in second half stoppage time to give Canada a 1–0 victory over [South Africa](1018598.md) at [Inglewood, California](107640.md)'s [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md).[^cite_note-164] In [Houston's NRG Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRG_Stadium), Canada faced Morocco in the round of 16 and were defeated 3-0 despite taking the 2022 semifinalists to the halftime break scoreless.[^cite_note-165]

## Rivalries

### United States

See also: [Canada–United States sports rivalries § Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_sports_rivalries#Soccer)

Canada has a rivalry with the [United States](582488.md), stemming from a generally [friendly rivalry](5199.md) between the two countries. The two teams frequently face each other in the Gold Cup. With 42 matches played, the United States currently leads the series with 19 wins, 12 draws, and 11 losses, outscoring the Canadians 66–47.

Canada has [qualified for two FIFA World Cups](33529697.md) while the U.S. has [qualified for 10](40877989.md) (not counting 1994 or 2026, where the USA qualified automatically as hosts for both tournaments, while Canada also qualified for the latter as co-hosts). Until recently, Canada was not seen as a competitive rival by a number of American fans as it had not beaten the United States in a 34-year stretch.[^cite_note-166] That streak was snapped on October 15, 2019, when Canada defeated the United States 2–0 at [BMO Field](3163911.md). One month later, on November 15, the United States beat Canada 4–1 in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida), and defeated the same opponent 1–0 in the [2021 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) in Kansas City, Kansas. In [2022 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)), Canada earned a 1–1 draw in [Nashville, Tennessee](22018.md) and defeated the United States 2–0 in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario).

On June 18, 2023, the United States defeated Canada 2–0 in the [2022–23 CONCACAF Nations League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_final) in [Las Vegas](47737.md), Nevada, marking the first time the two nations faced each other in the final of a major CONCACAF tournament.[^cite_note-167] Three weeks later, the U.S. eliminated Canada 3–2 on penalties in the [2023 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup_knockout_stage) quarter-final.

In their first Copa América appearance in 2024, Canada advanced from the group stage, while the United States was eliminated in the group stage as tournament host.[^cite_note-168]

In an international friendly on September 7, 2024, Canada defeated the United States 2–1 in [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), marking their first win over the United States on U.S. soil in 67 years.[^cite_note-169]

On March 23, 2025, Canada defeated the United States 2–1 in the [2024–25 CONCACAF Nations League third-place match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_CONCACAF_Nations_League#Third_place_play-off), marking their second consecutive win over the United States and their first back-to-back victories against them since 1985.[^cite_note-170] The match was played amid [heightened political tensions between Canada and the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_trade_war_with_Canada); the previous month, Canada head coach Jesse Marsch had criticized U.S. President [Donald Trump's](4848272.md) comments about Canada becoming the "51st state" during the tournament's media day.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172] In stoppage time, a scuffle involving players from both teams broke out after [Gio Reyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Reyna) brought down [Jacob Shaffelburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Shaffelburg) near the touchline.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174]

## Head-to-head record

Main article: [Canada men's national football team head-to-head record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_men's_national_football_team_head-to-head_record)

As of September 26, 2026, the complete official match record of the Canada men's national team comprises 483 matches: 182 wins, 116 draws and 185 losses.[^cite_note-188] During these matches, the team scored 619 times and conceded 604 goals. Canada's highest winning margin is 11 goals, which was achieved against the [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands_national_football_team) in 2021 (11–0).[^cite_note-189] Their longest winning streak is eight wins,[^cite_note-190] and their unbeaten record is 15 consecutive official matches.
