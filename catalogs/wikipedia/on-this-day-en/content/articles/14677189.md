The **Torre dei Conti** is a medieval fortified [tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower) in Rome, Italy, located near the [Colosseum](49603.md) and the [Roman Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum).

## Description

Now standing at 29 metres (95 ft),[^cite_note-RTE3-2025-1] it was once 50–60 metres (160–200 ft) tall,[^cite_note-mann-2] and gained the nickname of *Torre Maggiore* (Major Tower) for its size.[^cite_note-RTE3-2025-1] Originally covered in [travertine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travertine) salvaged from the ruins of the Imperial Fora,[^cite_note-rai1-3] this covering was in turn stripped for use in the construction of the [Porta Pia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Pia) in the 16th century, designed by [Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo).

## History

The initial construction took place under the pontificate of [Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I), in the mid-ninth century.[^cite_note-Roma_SPQR-4] The tower's main phase of construction was by the architect Marchione d'Arezzo in 1238.[^cite_note-Roma_SPQR-4][^cite_note-xtian-5] It was commissioned by Ricardo Conti, brother of [Pope Innocent III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III) as a fortified residence for his family, the [Conti di Segni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conti_di_Segni), over one of the [exedrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exedra) of the [portico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico) of the four apses of the [Imperial fora (The Temple of Peace)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fora#Temple_of_Peace) near the [Forum of Nerva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_of_Nerva).[^cite_note-RTE3-2025-1] It stood on the border with the territory of the rival family of the [Frangipani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frangipani_family).

The upper floors were destroyed by a series of earthquakes culminating in [that of 1348](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_of_1348), after which it was abandoned until 1620, when it was rebuilt by the [Apostolic Camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Camera). Other earthquakes in 1630 and 1644 caused damage which was repaired at the end of the 17th century by [Pope Alexander VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII), who added two buttresses.[^cite_note-mann-2]

With the opening of the [Via Cavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Cavour,_Rome) in the 19th century and the [Via dei Fori Imperiali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_dei_Fori_Imperiali) in the early 20th century, the tower was left isolated from other buildings. In 1937, the tower was donated by [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) to the [Arditi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arditi) (Italian stormtroopers), which retained ownership until 1943. The tower contains the mausoleum of General [Alessandro Parisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Parisi_\(politician\)), a leader of the Arditi who died in an automobile accident in 1938 and whose remains are preserved there in an ancient Roman [sarcophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus).

The tower was used until 2006 to host city hall offices[^cite_note-ind031125-6] and was left derelict until 2022, when renovations to make the building a museum covering the recent phases of the [Imperial Fora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fora) began.[^cite_note-7]

## Collapse





Before and after the tower's partial collapse in November 2025

On 3 November 2025, the tower partially collapsed during renovation works.[^cite_note-Giuffrida-2025-8] Two separate collapses occurred within 90 minutes of each other, leaving the tower with significant internal damage.[^cite_note-ind031125-6] One of the Romanian workers was killed and another was injured.[^cite_note-9]
