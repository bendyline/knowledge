The **Red-Greens** ([Swedish](26689.md): *De rödgröna*)[^cite_note-aftonbladet-1] is an umbrella term which refers to the three [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics) to [left-wing](18499.md) political parties of Sweden: the [Social Democrats](202978.md) (S), the [Left Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Party_\(Sweden\)) (V) and the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Sweden\)) (MP). The term originates from the launch of a [left-wing](18499.md) [political and electoral alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_alliance) between the parties on 7 December 2008.[^cite_note-thelocal-2]

This alliance, which was largely based on the [Norwegian](21241.md) [Red-Green Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-Green_Coalition_\(Norway\)),[^cite_note-dagsavisen-3] consisted of the Social Democrats, the Green Party and the Left Party which were in [opposition](3379016.md) to the [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right) [Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_\(Sweden\)) coalition government. The three component parties of the Red-Greens, which faced the voters as three separate parties in the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Swedish_general_election), aimed to reach agreements on significant areas of policy before the election. The parties aimed to achieve a majority in the following Swedish general election on 19 September 2010, in an unsuccessful bid to form a coalition government. The Red-Green pact was put to a pause on 26 October 2010,[^cite_note-thelocal.se-4] and was completely dissolved (according to a spokesperson for the Green Party) on 26 November.[^cite_note-Dagens_Nyheter-5]

The Red-Greens as a political alliance was revived following the [2014 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Swedish_general_election), in the form of a coalition government - the [Löfven Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6fven_I_Cabinet). The government consisted of the Social Democrats and Greens and was supported in the Riksdag by the Left Party. The three parties won 144 out of 349 Riksdag seats in the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Swedish_general_election); 100 Social Democrat and 16 Green with the support of 28 Left.[^cite_note-6]

## Background

The Red-Greens took their cue from the [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) [Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_\(Sweden\)), the co-operation between four centre-right parties which is considered to have contributed to these parties' success in the [2006 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Swedish_general_election). The cooperation represented a significant development since the Social Democrats, especially the party leadership of [Mona Sahlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mona_Sahlin), previously have been sceptical about too close a co-operation with the Left Party, which was officially a [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party) until 1990. The Social Democratic minority government led by [Göran Persson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6ran_Persson) before the 2006 election had much closer cooperation with the Green Party than with the Left Party.

In October 2008 a deeper co-operation between the Social Democrats and the Green Party was announced, and a common shadow budget for 2009 was presented. In December 2008, the Left Party was included in the co-operation and the Red-Greens was launched. In the [2010 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Swedish_general_election), the Red-Greens lost 22 seats in comparison with 2006 elections. The Social Democrats lost 5%, thus scoring their worst result since 1914. The [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Sweden\)) made a significant transformation from the smallest elected party to the third largest party during the term, overtaking the [Left Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Party_\(Sweden\)), the [Christian Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democrats_\(Sweden\)), the [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberals_\(Sweden\)) and the [Centre Party](217370.md).

The Red-Green pact lost in 2010 elections and was put on pause on 26 October 2010,[^cite_note-thelocal.se-4] and completely dissolved (according to a spokesperson for the Green Party) on 26 November.[^cite_note-Dagens_Nyheter-5] In the [2014 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Swedish_general_election), The Social Democrats were the largest party, but they did not have enough seats to form a majority, prompting them to make a deal with the Green Party in order to form a coalition. They sought support from the Left Party, reviving the alliance between the Social Democrats and The Greens. A minority government, the coalition which only held 138 out of 349 seats depended on the support from the Left Party and the opposing Alliance parties. The Red-Greens participated together in [2018 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Swedish_general_election), receiving 144 seats.[^cite_note-9] This is the worst result of the left-wing parties since 1914.
