The **Parliament of Ghana** is the [unicameral](204304.md) legislature of [Ghana](12067.md). It consists of 276 members, who are elected for four-year terms in single-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) using a [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md) system.

## History

[Legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) representation in [Ghana](12067.md) dates back to 1850, when the country was a British [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) known as [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)).[^cite_note-5] The body called the [Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Council), was purely advisory as the governor exercised all legislative and executive powers. Reforms were introduced in 1916 and 1925, although the governor's power remained extensive. In 1946, a new [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) was introduced that allowed for an unofficial member of the Legislative Council to become its president while the governor ceased to be the *[ex officio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio)* president of the body. This system continued until 1951 when the Legislature elected its first [speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_\(politics\)), Sir [Emmanuel Charles Quist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Charles_Quist).

1951 was also the first year that elections based on [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) was held. The [Convention People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_People's_Party) (CPP), formed in 1949 and led by [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah), won [the election that was held](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_legislative_election,_1951). Another party called the [United Gold Coast Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Gold_Coast_Convention) (UGCC) led by [J.B. Danquah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.B._Danquah) performed poorly and was disbanded soon after. Nkrumah, who had been jailed in early 1950 for [subversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subversion_\(politics\)), was released and appointed Leader of Government Business, becoming the country's first [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Ghana) in the following year.

[Legislative assembly elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_legislative_election,_1954) held in 1954 resulted in another CPP victory, with the party winning 71 out of a total of 104 seats. It also won 71 out of 104 seats in the [1956 legislative assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_legislative_election,_1956). The Gold Coast was renamed to Ghana and granted independence on Wednesday, 6 March 1957, while retaining the British monarch as head of state. The Legislative Assembly was renamed the National Assembly.

After the approval of a new Republican constitution, Ghana officially became a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) on 1 July 1960 with Kwame Nkrumah as its [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana). [The plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_constitutional_referendum,_1960) was taken as a fresh mandate from the people and the terms of National Assembly members were extended for another five years. A [one-party state](102459.md) was introduced following [a referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_constitutional_referendum,_1964) in 1964. As a result, only CPP candidates stood in the [National Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_parliamentary_election,_1965) held in 1965. Nkrumah was overthrown in 1966 by the military, which banned political parties and dissolved the National Assembly.

The country returned to civilian rule in 1969. [Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_parliamentary_election,_1969) held on 29 August the same year resulted in victory for the [Progress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Party_\(Ghana\)) (PP) of [Kofi Abrefa Busia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Abrefa_Busia), which won 105 of the National Assembly's 140 seats. He took office as [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heads_of_Government_of_Ghana) on 3 September 1969. His government was toppled in a 1972 military [coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup).

During the Third Republic, which lasted from 1979 to 1981, the dominant party in the National Assembly was the [People's National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_National_Party_\(Ghana\)) (PNP), led by Hilla Limann, which won 71 out of 104 seats in [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_parliamentary_election,_1979) held on 18 June 1979. After the military intervened in 1981, all elected institutions were dissolved and political party activity was prohibited.

**Parliament of the Fourth Republic**

After 11 years of military rule, a new constitution was approved in a [1992 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_constitutional_referendum,_1992). [Presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_presidential_election,_1992) were held in November and were won by [Jerry Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Rawlings), leader of the 1981 coup and subsequent military ruler. The opposition contested the results and [boycotted](18591996.md) the [December parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_parliamentary_election,_1992). As a result, Rawlings' [National Democratic Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Congress_\(Ghana\)) (NDC) won 189 out of 200 seats in Parliament.

All parties participated in the [1996 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_parliamentary_election,_1996). The NDC won 133 out of a total of 200 seats, while the main opposition [New Patriotic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Patriotic_Party) (NPP) won 60. Two smaller political party groups won the remaining seats.

The [2000 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_parliamentary_election,_2000) were significant in the sense that, President Rawlings was constitutionally barred from seeking another term. In the [presidential poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_presidential_election,_2000), [John Kufuor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kufuor) of the NPP defeated the NDC candidate [John Atta Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Atta_Mills) in a [run-off](25932.md) election. In the 200-seat Parliament, the NPP won 100, followed by the NDC's 92. Smaller political party groups and independent candidates won the remaining seats.

Kufuor was [re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_presidential_election,_2004) in 2004 and the New Patriotic Party (NPP) won 128 out of 230 seats in the concurrent [parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_parliamentary_election,_2004). The main opposition National Democratic Congress (NDC) won 94, while two other parties - The [People's National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_National_Convention_\(Ghana\)) (PNC) and Convention People's Party (CPP) - won 4 and 3 seats respectively. [Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_\(politician\)) candidates captured the remaining seat.

The simple majority (or [first past the post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_past_the_post)) [voting system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_system) is used in Ghana's parliamentary elections. Since 2012, the country is divided into 275 single-member [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies). Members serve four-year terms.

### Leadership structure



U.S. President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) shakes hands after delivering a speech to the Ghanaian Parliament at the [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House_of_Ghana) in July 2009.



US Speaker [Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi) delivering a speech to the Ghanaian Parliament at the [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House_of_Ghana) in July 2019

- **Speaker** - The speaker presides over the Parliament and enforces observance of all rules that govern its conduct. After a general election, the majority party in Parliament in consultation with other parties nominates a speaker.

  [^cite_note-6]

  &#x20;The speaker cannot be a member of Parliament, though they must possess the qualifications to stand for elections as a member of Parliament. A person, on appointment as speaker, must resign and declare the seat occupied in Parliament as vacant. The speaker is assisted by two deputy speakers (first and second deputy speakers), who are elected at the commencement of every Parliament. They must come from different political parties. The current speaker is [Alban Kingsford Sumani Bagbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alban_Sumana_Bagbin).

  [^cite_note-\:0-7]

* **First deputy speaker** - The first deputy speaker presides over the sittings of Parliament whenever the speaker is absent. The current first deputy speaker is Hon. [Bernard Ahiafor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Ahiafor) of the [National Democratic Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Congress_\(Ghana\)) (NDC).

* **Second deputy speaker** - The second deputy speaker presides over the sittings of Parliament in the absence of the speaker and the first deputy speaker. The current second deputy speaker is [Andrew Amoako Asiamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomena_\(Ghana_parliament_constituency\)) an independent candidate.

* **Majority leader** - The majority leader is elected from the party with a majority of parliamentary seats. A deputy majority leader and a majority chief whip assist him, constituting the majority leadership of Parliament. The current majority leader is [Mahama Ayariga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahama_Ayariga) of the NDC.

* **Deputy majority leader**- A deputy majority leader and a chief whip assist him, constituting the majority leadership of Parliament. The current deputy majority leader is [Kweku George Ricketts Hagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kweku_George_Ricketts-Hagan) of the NDC.

* **Minority leader** - The minority leader is elected from the second largest party in Parliament. A deputy minority leader and a chief whip assist him, constituting the minority leadership of Parliament. The current minority leader is [Alexander Afenyo-Markin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Afenyo-Markin) of the NPP.

* **Deputy minority leader** - A deputy minority leader and a chief whip assist him, constituting the minority leadership of Parliament. The current deputy minority leader is [Patricia Appiagyei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Appiagyei) of the NPP.
