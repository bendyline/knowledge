**St. Petersburg** (also spelled as **Saint Petersburg**)[^cite_note-gnis-2] is a city in [Pinellas County, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinellas_County,_Florida), United States. As of the 2020 census, the population was 258,308, making it the [fifth-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Florida) in Florida and the most populous city in the state that is not a county seat (the city of [Clearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater,_Florida) is the seat of Pinellas County).[^cite_note-4] It is the second-most populous city in the [Tampa Bay area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_area), which is the second-largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in Florida with an estimated population of about 3.29 million in 2022.[^cite_note-5]

St. Petersburg is located on the [Pinellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinellas) [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) between [Tampa Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay) and the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), and is connected to mainland Florida to the north. Locals often refer to the city as "**St. Pete**" or, more colloquially, "**the Burg**".[^cite_note-6] Neighboring [St. Pete Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Pete_Beach,_Florida) formally shortened its name in 1994 after a vote by its residents. St. Petersburg is governed by a mayor and city council.[^cite_note-7]

With an average of 361 days of sunshine annually, and a [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) for the most consecutive days of sunshine (768 days between 1967 and 1969),[^cite_note-stpetedotorg-8][^cite_note-9] it is nicknamed "The Sunshine City."[^cite_note-stpetedotorg-8] Located on the Gulf of Mexico, the average water temperature is typically around 76 °F (24 °C).[^cite_note-10] Due to its good weather, the city has long been a popular retirement destination, although in recent years the population has moved in a much more youthful direction.[^cite_note-\:4-11]

## History

### Early Spanish exploration

When the Spanish first arrived in the area of Tampa Bay, they encountered people of the [Safety Harbor culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_Harbor_culture). About 20 sites with [temple mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_Harbor_culture#Temple_mounds) have been found around Tampa Bay, with several in Pinellas County. Best known of the Safety Harbor people was the chiefdom of [Tocobaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocobaga), which was likely located at the [Safety Harbor site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_Harbor_site) in [Philippe Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Park) in northern Pinellas County. The [Pánfilo de Narváez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1nfilo_de_Narv%C3%A1ez) expedition landed on the shores of [Boca Ciega Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Ciega_Bay) at the [Jungle Prada Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Prada_Site) on April 14, 1528. It was the first inland exploration of North America. Of 300 men on the expedition only four survived. One of the survivors of the expedition, [Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvar_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_Cabeza_de_Vaca), wrote the first book describing the peoples, wildlife, flora and fauna of inland North America in his *Relacion*, published in Spain in 1542.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### 19th century

The city was co-founded by [John C. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Constantine_Williams_Sr.), formerly of [Detroit](8687.md), who purchased the land in 1875, and by [Peter Demens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Demens), who was instrumental in bringing the terminus of the [Orange Belt Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Belt_Railway) there in 1888.[^cite_note-\:2-15] St. Petersburg was incorporated as a town on February 29, 1892, when it had a population of 300 people.[^cite_note-\:7-16][^cite_note-17]

Local lore claims John C. Williams and Peter Demens flipped a coin to see who would have the honor of naming the city.[^cite_note-\:6-18][^cite_note-19] When Demens won the coin toss, the city was named after [Saint Petersburg, Russia](24320051.md), where Peter Demens had spent half of his youth,[^cite_note-20] while John C. Williams named the first hotel after his birthplace, Detroit (a hotel built by Demens).[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-21] The Detroit Hotel still exists downtown on Central Ave, but has been turned into a [condominium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_\(living_space\)).[^cite_note-22]

The oldest operating hotels are the Pier Hotel (formally Hotel Cordova), built in 1921, and The Exchange Hotel (formally The Heritage Hotel), built in 1926.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

The first major newspaper to debut in Tampa Bay was the *[St. Petersburg Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Times)* which established in 1884.[^cite_note-25] [Philadelphia](50585.md) publisher [F. A. Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._A._Davis) turned on St. Petersburg's first electrical service in 1897.[^cite_note-Davis-26] The city's first major industry was born in 1899 when Henry W. Hibbs (1862–1942), a native of [Newport, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_North_Carolina), established his wholesale fish business at the end of the railroad pier, which extended out to the shipping channel. Within a year, Hibbs Fish Company was shipping more than 1,000 pounds (450 kg) of fish each day.[^cite_note-Deese-27]

A historic marker on the 2nd Avenue side of St. Peter's Episcopal Cathedral, facing [Williams Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Park), notes that the cathedral and several adjacent churches played a central role in St. Petersburg being known as the "City of Churches." Among the oldest remaining structures downtown, St. Peter's was built in the Gothic Revival style in 1899, funded by philanthropist [Edwin H. Tomlinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_H._Tomlinson) in honor of his father. The area surrounding Williams Park also features other historic congregations—including First Baptist, First Congregational, and First United Methodist—all established within a few decades of the city's founding in 1892. Architectural records indicate that of fewer than ten Gothic Revival buildings remaining citywide, seven churches were built between 1887 and 1925, most clustered around [Williams Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Park) and Central Avenue. Earlier still, the first Episcopal church in the area was established at Pinellas Point in 1887, prior to the city's own founding. This pattern of early church building by multiple denominations reflects how religious institutions served as foundational anchors in St. Petersburg's civic and cultural development—even before the city's official incorporation.[^cite_note-Grismer-28]

The [Episcopalians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopalians) had the opportunity of establishing the first church in the southernmost part of St. Petersburg, Florida at Pinellas Point. There was a meeting held at the home of Robert Staunton on April 20, 1887, and it was planned to construct a church named St. Bartholomew's, on Lakeview Avenue. This church was completed late in 1887 and services were held there until 1895 when the property was given to the owners of the St. Peter's Church in St. Petersburg and were used for burial purposes. Hence another first Episcopalian church in St. Petersburg was created at the lot at Eleventh street and Second avenue north and was completed late in 1889. Edwin H. Tomlinson was one of the earliest supporters of St. Petersburg, Florida, benefiting and blessing the city in many ways. In one such case, Edwin paid the price in full for the St. Peter's Church and construction of the church was completed early in 1899. In the context of St. Petersburg being nicknamed "The City of Churches," it is imperative to note how the first church and in fact many more churches were founded before even the city itself was (founded in 1888 and became a city in 1892). The various denominations of Christianity at the time practiced and planted their own churches in St. Petersburg. The order being the Episcopalian Church in 1887 then again in 1889, the [Congregational Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational_Church) in 1890 between Ninth and Tenth streets near the present Central avenue (the [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterians) of the town joined forces with the Congregationalists to work together to accomplish it), the First Avenue [Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Episcopal_Church) 1892 at Central avenue and First street, the First [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist) Church which began congregation in 1893 and built their church in 1896 at the southeast corner of Sixth street and Central avenue (the congregation worshipped in the First Methodist Church while the new church was being built), the [Catholic Church](606848.md) in 1908 at Sixth street south between Fifth and Sixth avenues (the mass attended Point Pinellas beginning in 1892 and raised money to build this church in the meantime), the First [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist) Church built in 1892 at Central avenue and Seventh street, the First [Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church) built in 1909 at the corner of Second avenue and Fourth street south, the [Grace Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Baptist) Church built in 1909 at the railroad tracks on Fourth street south, the Trinity Evangelical Lutheran Church built in 1911 at Fifth street and Second avenue south, and First Church of Christ – Scientist (Christian Science Society) met in 1900 with fifteen people in the old Strowger building on the southeast corner of Central avenue and Fourth street – once the organization gathered more than sixteen members they had enough to be known as a church and an advertised practitioner – in 1913 the church bought a building at Second avenue north near Second street. In total 11 different denominations in these early founding days of the city planted their roots which were vital in helping guide these pioneers, were often a light for them, these churches were a beating heart for them – hence why these locations were built in such central locations in the city and cases of denominations crossing the aisle while their churches were being constructed to give them a place to worship and congregate in the meantime such as with the First Baptist Church which shared congregation space with the First Methodist in 1893 as their church was being built. Ultimately it shows that even if that had subtle differences in their interpretations or perspectives on their faith, they all had the same goals in who they were serving – their community, the Lord, and continuing to build and maintain their relationship with Christ and the Lord at the end of the day.[^cite_note-Grismer-28]

The decision by these early settlers to establish houses of worship near the civic center underscored the role of faith communities as central pillars in the formation of the city. Each denomination "planted" its roots—both symbolically and physically—around Central Avenue, creating a religious and architectural landscape that led directly to the city's lasting nickname: the "City of Churches." Today, that tradition continues, with dozens of active congregations across the city and new faith communities still being established, reflecting the ongoing importance of religious life in St. Petersburg's civic identity. Perhaps the largest growing church in the local Tampa Bay area which includes St. Petersburg, is Radiant Church, which was founded in 2013, it is a fast-growing, non-denominational church currently with 11 locations all across the bay area with a 12th location that was just announced. Radiant Church drew fewer than 200 guests in the early days, and it averaged nearly 8,000 members from their nine church locations at the time in 2024.[^cite_note-29] In terms of this tradition continuing of churches being planted in this city, as recently as this year, NewSpring plans to plant Artisan City Church in St. Petersburg in 2025.[^cite_note-30]

### 20th century

St. Petersburg was incorporated as a city in June 1903.[^cite_note-\:2-15] With this transition, the development of the downtown waterfront had dredging of a deeper shipping channel from 1906 to 1908 which opened St. Petersburg to larger shipping. Further dredging improved the port facilities through the 1910s.[^cite_note-31] By then the city's population had quadrupled to a population of 4,127 citizens.[^cite_note-32] F. A. Davis was instrumental to bringing the first [trolley](30733.md) service in 1904.[^cite_note-Davis-26]

&#x20;In 1914, [Al Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Lang) invited the [St. Louis Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Browns) to move their spring training into the city, then worked tirelessly to make Grapefruit League training in and around St. Petersburg the destination for baseball teams and their fans by the 1920s. Lang eventually became mayor and ambassador for the city, and helped its permanent population grow tenfold in just a decade.[^cite_note-33]



The [Benoist XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benoist_XIV) pictured taking off for the first time on January 1, 1914

In 1914 an airplane service across Tampa Bay from St. Petersburg to Tampa and back was initiated, generally considered the first scheduled commercial airline flight. The flight took former mayor Abe Pheil to Tampa.[^cite_note-10_Fun_Facts_about_St._Petersburg-34] The company name was the [St. Petersburg-Tampa Airboat Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg-Tampa_Airboat_Line), and the pilot was [Tony Jannus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Jannus) flying a [Benoist XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benoist_XIV) [flying boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_boat).[^cite_note-35] The Tony Jannus Award is presented annually for outstanding achievement in the airline industry.[^cite_note-36]

St. Petersburg's first library opened on December 1, 1915, which still operates to this day as the [Mirror Lake Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_Lake_Library).[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-37]

The city and its tourism industry burgeoned in the 1920s, with up to a quarter million visitors annually coming from Canada, the North and the Midwest by automobile, yacht, and railroad. The city was the principal [Gulf Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Coast) destination for long-distance trains of the [Atlantic Coast Line Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Line_Railroad)'s *[Southland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southland_\(train\))* (from Chicago and Cincinnati) and *Gulf Coast Limited*[^cite_note-38] (from New York, succeeded by the *[West Coast Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champion_\(train\)#Atlantic_Coast_Line)*), and [Seaboard Air Line Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaboard_Air_Line_Railroad) trains such as the *Southern States Special*[^cite_note-39] (from New York, succeeded by the *[Silver Meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Meteor)*). Travel time from across the bay was cut due to the [Gandy Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandy_Bridge)'s opening in 1924, allowing direct access to Tampa and the rest of central Florida.

The city took on a Mediterranean flair, with [Old Spanish Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Spanish_Trail_\(auto_trail\)) style architecture promoted by [Snell Isle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snell_Isle) founder Perry Snell, whose new country club island homes adopted many elements of Moorish design. Those same elements[^cite_note-\:2-15] were echoed in the city's new Vinoy, Jungle Country Club, Don Cesar and other fine hotels, as well as in Snell's new skyscraper office building downtown.[^cite_note-40] The 1926 opening of the Million Dollar Pier marked the peak of the boom, adding an attraction that brought both tourists and townspeople together to enjoy fishing, amusements, trolley access and even a local radio station.[^cite_note-\:5-41]

The St. Petersburg flag was created in 1927 and was designed by Mayor C.J. Maurer along with a committee of other public officials. It featured an array of colors symbolic of St. Pete's culture including the sunshine, water and land. The idea came after officials called for a new logo which later became the design for the flag. The pelican featured in the center became a symbol for the "Feed the Pelican Fund" which has supported the birds during the winter months.[^cite_note-42]



[Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) campaign parade in St. Petersburg, October 18, 1960

Tourism declined by the late 1920s and early 1930s due to the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression). The city recovered later in the 1930s with the help of the [Public Works Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Works_Administration), including a \$10 million investment plan in 1939 which helped build the St. Petersburg City Hall.[^cite_note-\:2-15]

St. Petersburg was a bit challenged as a city or rather had to make adaptations because it was mainly a tourist economy, and with the coming of [World War II](32927.md), that economy collapsed at first. It was only a few months after [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor) when the city's hotels and boardinghouses were barren. To quote historian [Raymond Arsenault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Arsenault) who experienced it firsthand, "Few cities felt the winds of war as early as St. Petersburg, and even fewer underwent such a thoroughgoing militarization." The local leaders paused and thought about what they could do to adapt to the situation at hand, for what could be done with the city's many hotels and the perks that came with? Then there was a lightbulb moment by someone to use St. Petersburg's extensive supply of hotels, cafeterias, recreational facilities and other amenities to generously give it to our armed services as a training site. Then an aggressive campaign led by the city's chamber of commerce, city officials and Congressman J. Harden Peterson convinced Washington that the ball was in their court. The War Department responded and selected St. Petersburg as a major technical services training center for the [U.S. Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Air_Corps). The city's Bayboro Harbor became a training base, The Vinoy Park Hotel was used as the headquarters, and every major hotel in the city was leased to the U.S. Air Corps except the Suwannee - for what little business remained for them was kept, and dozens of smaller hotels were leased as well. In addition to the army air corps, the [Coast Guard](32223.md), and the [United States Maritime Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Maritime_Service) were trained in St. Petersburg also. By the time the training center was discontinued in July 1943, an estimated 120,000 troops had seen St. Petersburg as trainees and instructors.[^cite_note-Michaels-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] After the war, many of those troops who were stationed in St. Petersburg returned as residents or tourists.[^cite_note-\:2-15]

In the 1950s, St. Petersburg experienced another population boom, with the return of retiree resettlement to the city. In 1954 the original [Sunshine Skyway Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Skyway_Bridge) opened its first span to link St. Petersburg with [Manatee County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manatee_County,_Florida), connecting the next year to [U.S. Route 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_19) in the city. With a large influx of car traffic, it was decided to remove the city's streetcar lines.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-\:3-47]

The development of major transportation continued into the 1960s with the completion of the [Howard Frankland Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Frankland_Bridge) in 1960, creating another connection between St. Petersburg and Tampa.[^cite_note-\:3-47] St. Petersburg also received its first stadium named the [Bayfront Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayfront_Center) which hosted the first professional hockey league in Tampa Bay.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] A new municipal marina and the [Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)) were also built downtown.[^cite_note-\:2-15] St. Petersburg is home to one of the world's largest reclaimed water systems that was built in the 1970s which flows 37 million gallons of water per day to provide for customers located throughout the city.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

In [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_in_aviation), a full-scale flying replica of the [Benoist XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benoist_XIV) [flying boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_boat) was constructed by [Florida Aviation Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Aviation_Historical_Society?action=edit\&redlink=1) for the 70th anniversary of the flight. This aircraft is now on loan to the St. Petersburg Museum of History in St. Petersburg, Florida.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

In 1887, only a few dirt paths led to St. Petersburg, on which African Americans made their way to St. Petersburg. Many gained employment building railways, and settled in an area known as Pepper Town, where residents grew pepper. The neighborhood existed into the 1960s, and became the first of several black neighborhoods to maintain [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws) and government regulations that drew boundaries around said African American neighborhoods.[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57] The first African American church was built in 1894. More African American neighborhoods, including Little Egypt, Sugar Hill, and Lincoln Court, were established around the Gas Plant, which developed its own business district. A hospital for African Americans was established in 1923.[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57] Segregation continued in these areas until the 1950s, when black leaders such as Robert Swain advocated for civil rights in St. Petersburg, and obtained building permits after threatening to sue the city for violating his civil rights.[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57]

In 1880, the city was 53% white people to 47% black people.[^cite_note-58] The city publicly boasted the [Ku Klux Klan](16779.md) klaverns' role in violence, and that they were "always on hand to help intimidate."[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57] Law enforcement would enforce racially based curfews.[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57] By the 1920s, St. Petersburg neighborhoods were segregated. In 1914, white residents lynched a black man suspected of murder and rape, and in 1905, after a black man knifed to death the city's police chief, a mob stormed the jail and "shot him to pieces."[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57] In 1914, [John Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynching_of_John_Evans) was accused of beating and raping a white woman, and a crowd of 1,500 white residents dragged him from his jail cell and lynched him.[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57]

In 1944, the Army Service Forces feared race riots, and identified the location of black-owned newspapers and radio stations. Emergency orders were broadcast saying: "All liquor stores, bars, dance halls, moving picture theaters and public places, where people may congregate have been ordered closed. The streets have been ordered cleared...The congregation of more than three persons at any place is prohibited."[^cite_note-Mormino-59]

From 1955 to 1959, black residents organized swim-ins of the segregated beaches and pools. The St. Petersburg government responded by closing these venues and arresting participants. This continued up to and after the 1957 *Alsup v. St. Petersburg* ruling that St. Petersburg could not segregate beaches or pools. In 1959, after continued pressure from residents through swim-in's, the government reopened them as an integrated venues.[^cite_note-\:15-55] In 1968, the city's mostly-black [sanitation workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_sanitation_strike_of_1968) went on strike for higher wages.[^cite_note-60] Integration began in the 1960s, which included restaurants serving black people, integrated washrooms, and blacks hired as bus drivers (1964) and police officers.[^cite_note-St._Petersburg's_Historic_African_A-57]

### Contemporary history



1969 postcard of the [Gandy Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandy_Bridge) by the Hartman Litho Sales Company

Development of the first [Major League Baseball](38776.md) team to be located in the Tampa Bay area began in St. Petersburg throughout the 1970s. The city tried to encourage numerous teams through the United States to make St. Petersburg their new home.[^cite_note-\:2-15] Designs for a ballpark were first presented in 1983 and construction for a permanent dome stadium began in 1986.[^cite_note-61] This process controversially required the demolition of the mostly-Black Gas Plant neighborhood. The stadium opened in 1990 as the Florida Suncoast Dome, renamed the Thunderdome in 1993. After many attempts to attract tenants to the new stadium, Major League Baseball gave St. Petersburg a franchise in 1995. In 1996, the dome was renamed a third time to [Tropicana Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicana_Field) after naming rights were established with [Tropicana Dole Beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicana_Products).[^cite_note-62] The [Tampa Bay Devil Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Devil_Rays) was then established in 1998 after the stadium's renovation and the new team played their first game on March 31, 1998, giving the Tampa Bay area their first professional baseball team.[^cite_note-63]

[I-275](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_275_\(Florida\)) was expanded across [the bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay) from [Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida) through St. Petersburg in the 1970s. Additional spurs [I-175](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_175) and [I-375](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_375_\(Florida\)) were built afterward, extending from the main highway to the northern and southern edges of the downtown area. The city population continued to multiply during the 20th century, booming through the 1970s as a retirement destination for Americans from [midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) cities, reaching 238,647 in the 1980 census. Racial tensions persisted, and [1996 riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg,_Florida_riots_of_1996) were sparked by the shooting of a black teenager by a white St. Pete police officer. Growth stagnated in the subsequent decade and a half, but since the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), renewed interest in urban living by family-aged residents and the expansion of the downtown university and related services has led to growth.

### Topography

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 137.6 square miles (356.4 km<sup>2</sup>). 61.7 square miles (159.9 km<sup>2</sup>) of it is land, and 75.9 square miles (196.5 km<sup>2</sup>) of it (55.13%) is water.[^cite_note-64] St. Petersburg is bordered by Tampa Bay's three sections, Old, Middle and Lower Tampa Bay.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

### Neighborhoods

Main article: [Neighborhoods in St. Petersburg, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_St._Petersburg,_Florida)

St. Petersburg is home to more than 100 neighborhoods,[^cite_note-67] with most of the historic districts located near the bay.[^cite_note-68] On the central Eastern edge of the city is [Downtown St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_St._Petersburg,_Florida), which includes the city's residential and commercial skyscrapers, art galleries, museums, and parks.[^cite_note-\:10-69]

The downtown area is home to the [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) and to many [start-up companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Start-up_company), corporate branches, banks, law firms, and restaurants.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Apart from downtown's business and cultural offerings, the area also includes a branch of St. Petersburg College and the campus of the University of South Florida-St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-\:10-69] The downtown district is home to two professional sports teams, the Tampa Bay Rays, which play in the western part of downtown at Tropicana Field, and the Tampa Bay Rowdies, which play along the downtown waterfront at Al Lang Stadium.[^cite_note-\:10-69] The emerging Edge district on the western edge of downtown is rapidly growing as development spreads down Central Avenue.

North of downtown lie the [Historic Old Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Shore_Historic_District_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)) and [Snell Isle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snell_Isle), which both have [Mediterranean style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Revival_architecture) historic and waterfront homes, parks, and recreational areas.[^cite_note-\:11-72][^cite_note-73] Old Northeast is home to a [shopping district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_district), city landmarks, beaches, and small shops as well as small residential high rises.[^cite_note-\:11-72] Snell Isle was founded by [C. Perry Snell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snell_Isle) who bought up the land to develop upscale properties in the 1900s, and helped create some of St. Petersburg's resorts such as the Vinoy Park Hotel and the [St. Petersburg Woman's Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Woman's_Club),[^cite_note-74] both of which are listed on the [U.S.](3434750.md) National Register of Historic Places.[^cite_note-75] The far north consists of the [Gateway area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway,_Pinellas_County,_Florida) which overlaps part of Pinellas Park, home to major employers such as [Home Shopping Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Shopping_Network) and currently the site of much construction of residential and business buildings and of new toll roads.

The central portion of St. Petersburg includes the [Grand Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central,_St._Petersburg,_Florida) and [Historic Kenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenwood_Historic_District_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)). The Grand Central District houses many of the city's cafes, art galleries, restaurants, and bars all owing to the [Craftsman style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craftsman_style) architecture.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] Historic Kenwood is filled with art studios and galleries similarly to the Grand Central District.[^cite_note-78]

South of downtown is [Historic Roser Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roser_Park_Historic_District), which houses historic Mediterranean and Eclectic style housing, parks, and museums.[^cite_note-79] The neighborhood is divided by Booker Creek which flows into Bayboro Harbor.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

In far western St. Petersburg, north of the separate city of [South Pasadena, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pasadena,_FL), is the neighborhood of Pasadena, which includes the intersection of [State Road 693](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Road_693_\(Florida\)) (Pasadena Avenue) and [County Road 150](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Road_150_\(Pinellas_County,_Florida\)) (Central Avenue).

#### Downtown

Downtown St. Petersburg is the Central Business District, containing high rises for office and residential use. The *[Tampa Bay Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Times)* newspaper is headquartered in the downtown area.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] The [Poynter Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poynter_Institute), which owns the paper, is located on 3rd Street South.[^cite_note-84]

The [Mahaffey Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahaffey_Theater) complex, the [Morean Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morean_Arts_Center), dozens of other art galleries, [The Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Coliseum), [Palladium Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium_at_St._Petersburg_College), and [Jannus Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannus_Live) are among the galleries and cultural venues featured downtown.[^cite_note-\:8-85] Several prominent museums are located in the perimeter. Many of them have received notable accolades, including the [Chihuly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuly) Collection presented by the Morean Arts Center, the [Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)), the [Salvador Dalí Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD_Museum), the now-closed [Florida International Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_International_Museum), the [St. Petersburg Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Museum_of_History), [Florida Holocaust Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Holocaust_Museum), and the [James Museum of Western and Wildlife Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Museum_of_Western_and_Wildlife_Art).[^cite_note-\:8-85] The city hosts many outdoor festivals throughout the year.[^cite_note-86]



*Bending Arc* Sculpture in 2024 by [Janet Echelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Echelman)

Jutting a half mile into the bay is the [St. Petersburg Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Pier), a major tourist attraction that offers various activities. "The Lens" design which was chosen by the International Design Competition Jury and accepted by City Council later had its contract terminated by a citywide election during the summer of 2013.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Following this, the "Pier Park" was chosen out of the 16 new design teams that submitted work in late 2014 and in 2015 the Pier Park was set for construction in early 2017.[^cite_note-\:5-41] The new Pier District opened on July 6, 2020, and contains green space, the Marketplace, playground, splash pad, and several public art installations, including [Janet Echelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Echelman)'s aerial net sculpture, *Bending Arc*.[^cite_note-89]

Downtown also contains the [University of South Florida St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Florida_St._Petersburg) and a downtown branch of [St. Petersburg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_College).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] The downtown perimeter includes several parks, most of which are waterfront or lakefront. Straub Park is nearly a half mile long, boasts a waterfront location, and is home of the St. Petersburg Museum of Fine Arts.[^cite_note-92] Because of the number of parks in the downtown area, [The Trust for Public Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trust_for_Public_Land) ranks St. Petersburg 1st in Florida and 15th out of 100 of the largest cities in the U.S.[^cite_note-93] The [Vinoy Park Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinoy_Park_Hotel) has a bayfront location, a spot on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places),[^cite_note-94] and an [AAA](298817.md) Four-Diamond rating.[^cite_note-95] It fronts [Vinoy Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinoy_Park), which holds music festivals, including the [Warped Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warped_Tour). Nearby is the historic Tramor Cafeteria building, now part of the *Tampa Bay Times*. The city is connected via the Looper Trolley.[^cite_note-96]

Many dining and nightlife locations can be found downtown on or near Central Avenue extending to 34th Street in the west or Beach Drive along the waterfront. Venues include Jannus Live and the [State Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Theatre_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)). The active nightlife scene is credited to recent demographic and regulatory changes.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] In 2010, the city council voted to extend bar hours until 3 A.M., identical to cross-bay "rival" Tampa.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

[Tropicana Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicana_Field), home of [Major League Baseball](38776.md)'s [Tampa Bay Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rays), is located in the western part of downtown. Until 2008, the team played its spring training games at nearby [Progress Energy Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Energy_Park). This setup was unique, making St. Petersburg the first city that played host to its baseball team during spring training as well as the regular season since the [1919 Philadelphia Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_Philadelphia_Athletics_season).[^cite_note-101] At the end of 2007, there was a debate over [a new stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rays_Ballpark) to be built on the downtown waterfront at the current Progress Energy Park site. Tropicana Field would be demolished and replaced with prime residential and retail space. Completion of the stadium was planned for 2012; however, the proposal was tabled indefinitely while a community-based organization investigates all alternatives for new stadium construction.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] In 2022, the Rays organization and the city came to an agreement over redevelopment; the Trop will be demolished by 2027, to be replaced by a new stadium and a restored Gas Plant neighborhood surrounding it.[^cite_note-105] Although originally set to expire in 2027, the [Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rays) use agreement for [Tropicana Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicana_Field) was extended until 2028 due to the damage caused by [Hurricane Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Milton) in 2024 and will continue to be extended every year the field remains unplayable.[^cite_note-\:13-106] Previous plans to move forward with the construction of a new stadium in St. Petersburg's Historic Gas Plant District have since been scrapped, with the [Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rays) stepping away from the deal.[^cite_note-\:13-106] The repairs to [Tropicana field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicana_Field) have been estimated to cost upwards of \$55 million and the city aims to have them completed in time for the 2026 season.[^cite_note-107]



&#x20;The [S. H. Kress and Co. Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._H._Kress_and_Co._Building_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)) is on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).



The waterfront and Electric Pier - Saint Petersburg, Florida, circa 1910.

St. Petersburg has the third-largest dedicated public waterfront park system in North America,[^cite_note-108] with a waterfront park system that stretches 7 miles (11 km) and is used year-round for public events, festivals and other activities.[^cite_note-109] In the early 20th century, citizens and city leaders engaged in a long and boisterous debate over the future of the young city's waterfront space, with one side advocating for commercial, port and industrial development and the other side advocating for a long-term commitment to parks and public access to the waterfront. It was during the earliest days of the St. Petersburg Chamber of Commerce in 1908, that their committee at the time, investigated and reported that "The piers, all sorts of riff-raff, and especially where the outgoing tide leaves large stretches of sand covered with a variety of animal and vegetable matter in all stages of decay - does not well comport with a live, progressive city such as St. Petersburg aspires and claims to be." One of the chamber of commerce's many purposes is to serve as a collective voice for its local business members - with the overarching goal of helping them thrive, and one of the biggest roles the chamber has is promoting tourism too, which ultimately brings their city's business more visitors and money to the economy. For those reasons, when the St. Petersburg Chamber of Commerce heard that the waterfront could have a stigma attached to it - that was bad news to be hearing, for it meant it could be negatively impacting the city surrounding it. The committee urged the board of the chamber to take action by convincing the city officials to follow their plan that would lead them to plead into striking a deal with those who owned the waterfront lots at the time, and to acquire whatever remained of other necessary, relevant properties. The public access and park contingent won the debate when, on Christmas Eve 1909, the city announced the acquisition of the waterfront land that is encompassed by the waterfront park system.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-Grismer-28] From the beginning of the St. Petersburg Chamber of Commerce's inception until even today, the chamber has made it clear the importance of the public access, upkeep, maintenance, expansion plans, and passion for their city's waterfront. Today the chamber has a Downtown Waterfront Committee, which has quarterly meetings involving public and private entities. Anyone can RSVP to be a part of this committee, and recommendations made during these meetings often go to the chambers board of directors meetings.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

The city is also becoming one of the largest destinations in Florida for [kiteboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitesurfing) with locations such as [Fort De Soto Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Desoto), [Pass-a-Grille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pass-a-Grille_Historic_District), and Ten-Cent.[^cite_note-113]

The St. Petersburg [Shuffleboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuffleboard) Club was established in 1924 and gained attention as the "World's Largest Shuffleboard Club" with 110 courts and over 5,000 members in the 1950s and 1960s.[^cite_note-114]

Northshore Aquatic Complex is a public pool and small water park located downtown on the St. Petersburg waterfront. Northshore contains a 50-meter pool with diving board, 25 meter training pool with zero depth entry, a play pool, and is home to both [Saint Petersburg Aquatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Aquatics) swim club and Saint Petersburg Masters swim club.[^cite_note-115]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of the Tampa Bay area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_Tampa_Bay_area)



Coconut palms at the Gizella Kopsick Palm Arboretum in the northeastern part of the city

St. Petersburg has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*) with some characteristics of a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) ([Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), with a defined rainy season from June through September. Many portions of St. Petersburg, especially along the bay and in south St. Petersburg, have tropical microclimates due to the maritime influence of the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) and [Tampa Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay). As a result, tropical flora like [coconut palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_palm) and [royal palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_palm) can be found throughout the city, and the city is home to the Gizella Kopsick Palm Arboretum, a 2-acre (0.81 ha) park which houses over 500 palms and cycads,[^cite_note-116] including a pair of large Jamaican Tall coconut palms which predate the freeze of 1989. St. Petersburg, like the rest of the Tampa Bay area, is occasionally affected by [tropical storms](8282374.md) and [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane). There were 2 hurricanes in 2024 that impacted St. Petersburg within 2 weeks of each other: Hurricane Helene followed by Hurricane Milton. However, both made landfall in other regions of the state (the [Big Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bend_\(Florida\)) and [Southwest Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Florida), respectively). The last time a hurricane directly struck the city was in [1921](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_Tampa_Bay_hurricane).

### 2010 census

As of the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), there were 244,769 people, 106,755 households, and 58,353 families residing in the city.[^cite_note-127]

In 2010, of all [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)) residents, 0.8% were [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_American), 0.5% were [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_American), 0.5% were [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_American), 0.3% were [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American), 0.1% were [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_American), 0.1% were [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_American), and 1.0% were other Asians.[^cite_note-\:12-128] Also in 2010, of all [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) people within the city, 5,272 (2.2%) were [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_people), 2,855 (1.2%) were [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American), 2,835 (1.2%) were [Cuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_American), and other Hispanic or Latino residents made up 5,252 (2.1%) of the population.[^cite_note-\:12-128]

According to the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Census), the city population density was 3,964.4 inhabitants per square mile (1,530.7/km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-\:12-128] 84.1% of households were occupied while 15.9% were not occupied. 3,888 (1.6%) lived in non-institutionalized group-quarters and 2,719 (1.1%) were institutionalized. As of 2010, 23,304 (21.4%) had children under the age of 18 living in them, 37,847 (34.8%) were [opposite-sex married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) living together, 16,425 (15.1%) had a female householder with no husband present, 4,849 (4.5%) had a male householder with no wife present. There were 9,453 (3.9%) unmarried partnerships. 39,397 households (36.2%) were made up of individuals, and 28,267 (26.0%) had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.19. In 2010, [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_\(U.S._Census\)) made up 54.3% while non-families made up 45.7%; the average family size was 2.88. The median age of the city was 41.6 years.[^cite_note-\:12-128]

### 2000 census

As of 2000, 23.85% of households had children under the age of 18 living with them, 37.295% were married couples living together, 13.8% had a female householder with no adult living partner present, and 43.8% were non-families. 35.6% of all households were made up of individuals, and 13.3% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.10 and the average family size was 2.865.

In 2000, the city's population was spread out, with 21.5% under the age of 18, 7.7% from 18 to 24, 30.2% from 25 to 44, 23.1% from 45 to 64, and 17.4% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 39.24 years. For every 100 females, there were 91.2 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 87.7 males.

As of 2000, the median income for a household in the city was \$34,597, and the median income for a family was \$43,198. Males had a median income of \$30,794 versus \$27,860 for females. The per capita income for the city was \$21,107. About 9.2% of families and 13.3% of the population were below the poverty line, including 19.1% of those under age 18 and 10.8% of those age 65 or over. In 2010, 17.8% of the population was under the poverty line, including 32.2% of those under age 18 and 14.1% of those age 65 or over.

### Languages

As of 2000, those who spoke only [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) as a [first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) at home accounted were 88.53% of residents, while [Spanish](26825.md) was spoken by 4.43%, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) by 0.78%, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) by 0.72% of speakers, [Vietnamese](32511.md) by 0.67%, [Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian_language) by 0.52%, and [Laotian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_language) by 0.51% of the population.[^cite_note-129]

### Crime

As of 2012, it ranked 58th highest in the United States when it comes to violent crime.[^cite_note-130] In 2013, St. Petersburg ranks in the bottom tenth for safety among cities in Florida.[^cite_note-131] Evidence of the social unrest and the schism within the city, particularly between South St. Petersburg and the rest of the city came with the [St. Petersburg, Florida riots of 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg,_Florida_riot_of_1996). Police Officer David Crawford was murdered in February 2011 by then-teenager [Nicholas Lindsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Lindsey).[^cite_note-132]

### Religion

As of 2020, 35.9% of St. Petersburg residents consider themselves religious. Catholics make up the largest group at 14 percent followed by Methodists and Baptists, each of which compose of about four percent of the religious community.[^cite_note-133] [The Diocese of St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Saint_Petersburg) governs 74 Catholic parishes as well as 46 schools and 480,000 Catholics in the Tampa Bay area. [Bishop Gregory Parkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Parkes) currently leads the Diocese of St. Petersburg which covers five counties in the state of Florida.[^cite_note-134]

## Art and culture

Museums and cultural centers include the [Salvador Dalí Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD_Museum), the [Chihuly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuly) [Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morean_Arts_Center), [Museum of Fine Arts, St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts,_St._Petersburg), and the [Museum of the American Arts and Crafts Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_American_Arts_and_Crafts_Movement).[^cite_note-St._Petersburg_Arts_Alliance-140][^cite_note-St._Petersburg_arts_venues-141]

St. Petersburg includes more than 75 art galleries[^cite_note-EDC_Report-142] and seven distinct art districts.[^cite_note-143] The Warehouse Arts District emerged circa 2009 alongside a significant [glass art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_art) market that has become known as "the Glass Coast".[^cite_note-The_Artisan-144][^cite_note-Visit_St._Pete-Clearwater-145] The *Deuces Live District* is home to the city of St. Petersburg's African American cultural heritage, and includes locally owned art galleries and other specialty businesses,[^cite_note-\:14-146] as well as historic buildings such as the [Royal Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Theater_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)).[^cite_note-147] The *M.L. King North District* includes restaurants and cafes.[^cite_note-148]

St. Petersburg features more than 600 public art murals,[^cite_note-Smithsonian_Magazine-149][^cite_note-St._Pete_Murals-150] which are the focus of the city's annual SHINE Mural Festival. Since its inception in 2015, the SHINE Mural Festival has been responsible for creating more than 150 murals in the city.[^cite_note-St._Petersburg_SHINE_Festival-151] Other notable art fairs in the city include the long running St. Petersburg Fine Art Festival and the Mainsail Art Festival.

### Events

One of the first of many major events of the year that takes place is the Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Parade, in January.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] The parade hosts a Battle of the Bands, and drum line extravaganzas that have been duplicated in other cities.[^cite_note-154]

In March the city hosts the annual Firestone [Grand Prix of St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_of_St._Petersburg). This is located in downtown St. Petersburg and is the first round of the [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series).[^cite_note-155] It usually lasts three days with practice rounds, qualifications, and two main races.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] The 100 lap Grand Prix's circuit has included a section of the [Albert Whitted Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Whitted_Airport).[^cite_note-158]

Bluesfest, hosted in the Vinoy Park,[^cite_note-159] hosts multiple live blues artists, offers views of the Tampa Bay waters from the park, and provides drinks and free food.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

One of the many art festivals, called the Mainsail Art Festival, is a free entry art exhibition at the Vinoy Park, which offers art sold by local artists. It also provides live music, awards, and food courts.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

The Saint Petersburg Art Festival takes place every February.[^cite_note-164]

The downtown triathlon event is hosted by [St. Anthony's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony's_Hospital_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)) and involves a 1.5k swim through Tampa Bay, a 40k bike along the waterways, and a 10k run through the neighborhoods.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

The Green Thumb Festival, which originated in 1986 to promote tree beautification and planting in the city of St. Petersburg, is currently held in Walter Fuller Park.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

A major event that takes place in June is the St. Pete Pride weekend, when the [LGBT community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_community) and supporters celebrate in the streets with festivals, the 27/82 concert, and an LGBT pride parade.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] The weekend also hosts a variety of block parties, food stands, DJ stands, art festivals, local hosted parties, and the LGBT welcoming center.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

In July, the 4th of July firework celebration invites the citizens to downtown St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-173]

Greenhouse and USF St. Pete's College of Business host an annual event known as "St. Pete Pitch Night" in October that hosts judges and business pitches.[^cite_note-174]

In November, the annual [Ribfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribfest) is held at Vinoy Park.[^cite_note-175] It includes family fun zones, drinking vendors, and two stages hosting many country music artists.[^cite_note-176]

SHINE St. Pete Mural Festival is an annual event hosted by the St. Petersburg Arts Alliance. The event began in 2015 and since has contributed to nearly 93 murals designed by artists from across the globe. 2020 marked the first event entirely composed of Florida-based artists, more specifically from the Tampa Bay area.[^cite_note-177]

From the end of November through December are holiday events. A tree lighting ceremony starts the celebrations. The [Santa Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Parade) is followed by Snowfest with "[glice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_ice)" skating, toboggan slides, and Kiddyland.[^cite_note-\:9-178] Kids meet Santa and ice skate in the North Straub Park. North and South Straub Park are decorated with holiday lights and decorations while the Vinoy Park is decorated with large greeting cards created by the recreational centers in St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-\:9-178]

In mid-December, the city hosts an annual [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA) football game in Tropicana Field entitled the [St. Petersburg Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Bowl).[^cite_note-179]

On December 31, St. Petersburg has the year's last event, First Night St. Petersburg, where people celebrate the arts from venues across the city.[^cite_note-180]

The American Stage in the Park hosts many different shows at the Demens Landing Park throughout the year.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

St. Petersburg hosted the [Miss Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Florida) Pageant eleven consecutive years from 2004 to 2015, until the pageant was moved to [Lakeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeland,_Florida).[^cite_note-183]

The city hosts a year-round event known as the "Second Saturday ArtWalk".[^cite_note-184]

The city hosted the [2024 Royal Rumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Rumble_\(2024\)) on January 27, 2024, on the Tropicana Field[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186]

### Tourism



The [Salvador Dalí Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD_Museum)

The arts are a major contributor to the region's tourism. The [Salvador Dalí Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD_Museum) houses the largest collection of [Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD)'s works outside of Europe, including a number of famous and large-scale paintings such as *[The Discovery of America by Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Discovery_of_America_by_Christopher_Columbus)*.[^cite_note-187] The Salvador Dalí Museum received more than 400,000 visitors annually prior to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), 75% of which were not local to the [Tampa Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_area).[^cite_note-Dali_Museum_expansion_report-188] Other art museums in the city include a [Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)) and The [Chihuly Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuly_Collection), located on Central Avenue, which houses glass sculptures of [Dale Chihuly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Chihuly).[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] The [Museum of the American Arts and Crafts Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_American_Arts_and_Crafts_Movement) was expected to open in 2017, and after delays opened to the public in September 2021.[^cite_note-191]

Beyond the arts, St. Petersburg is home to a children's museum ([Great Explorations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Explorations)), Holocaust Museum, as well as the Dr. Carter G. Woodson African American Museum which highlights the life of Carter G. Woodson who founded the Associated Publishers and is the author of nearly 30 books still prevalent today.[^cite_note-192] Past exhibits and events featured in the museum include the Ray McLendon Exhibit, a discussion of race and politics series, and a seminar on the conviction of Michael Morgan.[^cite_note-193] The St. Petersburg Museum of History has a full-size replica of the Benoist XIV seaplane and is located near the approximate spot by the St. Petersburg Pier where the first scheduled commercial flight departed.[^cite_note-194]



The [Mahaffey Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahaffey_Theater) is one of St. Petersburg's popular tourism spots.

There are various other smaller art galleries and entertainment venues, especially in the downtown area, which has seen a boom in development since the mid-1990s; these include the [Mahaffey Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahaffey_Theater) complex, American Stage (an equity regional theater), [The Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Coliseum), Palladium Theatre, the Arts Center, and the Florida Craftsmen Gallery.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

The old St. Petersburg Pier was a popular tourist attraction which closed in May 2013, and has been replaced with a new pier that opened in late 2020.[^cite_note-197] The *[Bounty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_\(1960_ship\))*, a replica of [HMS *Bounty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Bounty) that was used in the [1962 Technicolor remake of *Mutiny on the Bounty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutiny_on_the_Bounty_\(1962_film\)), starring [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando), was permanently docked near the pier for many years until the ship was sold to [Ted Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Turner) in 1986.[^cite_note-198] The *Bounty*, however, sometimes visited St. Petersburg for the winter in the following years before its sinking in 2012.[^cite_note-199] In 2010, the St. Petersburg City Council voted to demolish and rebuild the pier.[^cite_note-200] The new pier will be opening "in phases" in 2020. A ceremony celebrating the opening is scheduled for 4 July.[^cite_note-201]

The city had a [Madame Tussaud Wax Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Tussaud_Wax_Museum) between 1963 and 1989.[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]

The downtown [Sundial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundial_St._Pete) shopping complex opened in May 2014, sitting on the renovated site of a shopping and entertainment complex formerly called BayWalk that originally opened in 2000.[^cite_note-204] It contains a 12-screen movie theater originally owned by [Muvico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muvico) and now owned by [AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres), as well as many chain restaurants and retail shops. The Sundial St. Pete has nightlife destinations, as does the block surrounding Jannus Live. Restaurants serving ethnic and domestic culinary specialties can be found throughout the downtown area.

Every Saturday morning from October to May, the downtown area hosts a [farmers' market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmers'_market) – The Saturday Morning Market – in the parking area of [Al Lang Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Lang_Stadium) (formerly Progress Energy Park). In the summer months, the market moves to [Williams Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Park) to take advantage of the shade and create a more comfortable experience.[^cite_note-205] Local vendors sell the fruits of their labors (whether edible or decorative) alongside artists of all kinds including live music.[^cite_note-206]



Dan Wheldon Way during the [2012 Honda Grand Prix of St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Honda_Grand_Prix_of_St._Petersburg)

West of downtown on Central Avenue is the 600 Block Arts District, which contains Bohemian art and clothing stores.[^cite_note-207] The eve-N-odd gallery is located in the historic Crislip Arcade built in 1925.[^cite_note-208] The refurbished shopping arcade is one of 13 original city arcades built in the city. Only three are left, and only the Crislip arcade is still being used as a place for small businesses to set up shop.[^cite_note-209] Further west is the Grand Central District located within Historic Kenwood District. It is known for its artistic community, [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) presence, and the annual St. Pete Pride parade.[^cite_note-210] Haslam's Bookstore, closed since 2020, can also be found in the Grand Central District. It was the largest independent bookstore in Florida, with over 30,000 square feet.[^cite_note-211] As its name implies, Old Northeast is adjacent to downtown from the northeast. It is known for its historic status and eclectic architecture.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213]

St. Petersburg boasts two historic neighborhoods: Roser Park, located just south of the downtown area, and Grenada Terrace, in the Old Northeast Neighborhood. Both are known for stately architecture, and together comprise the urban core of St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215]

A bronze statue in honor of St. Pete resident [Elder Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_Jordan) stands on 22nd Street and Seventh Avenue as of October 2020. Jordan was a slave from birth up until the age of 15 when he bought his freedom and moved to St. Petersburg where he created a successful business.[^cite_note-216]



[Williams Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Park) with bandshell, one of the many public green spaces in the area

North of downtown is the [Great Explorations Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Explorations_Children's_Museum), an interactive museum featuring a Children's Village with giant pretend stores, fire house and pet vet clinic, and preschool, science, music, art, and water exhibits. It is located next to [Sunken Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunken_Gardens_\(Florida\)).[^cite_note-217]

4th Street as a whole, from Downtown up to Gandy Boulevard, is home to many restaurants and bars running the gamut from fast food to [haute cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute_cuisine). This area is called the "Garden District", although as of 2010 this name is not widely in use.[^cite_note-218]

Boyd Hill Nature Park, located on Lake Maggiore, is a 245-acre (0.99 km<sup>2</sup>) preserve where one can see many of the endangered plants and rare wildlife of Tampa Bay.[^cite_note-219] A bird exhibit houses [bald eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle), [owls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owl), [hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawk), and other species.[^cite_note-220]

The area's main shopping mall is [Tyrone Square Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrone_Square_Mall), constructed in 1972 and is located in the northwestern part of the city.[^cite_note-221]

### Demonstrations and protests

Pride month takes place annually throughout the month of June to celebrate and recognize the identities of LGBTQ+ persons.[^cite_note-225] The city of St. Petersburg hosts a variety of events to celebrate Pride Month including the annual Pride Parade.[^cite_note-226]

The annual Women's March in the month of January typically takes place in Williams Park where thousands of individuals gather to march for female rights and equality. The last documented Women's March in St. Pete dates back to 2018 following the resurgence of the #MeToo Movement.[^cite_note-227]

[Demonstrations and protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests) began following the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd).[^cite_note-228]

Two teams, the [Tampa Bay Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rays) of [Major League Baseball](38776.md) and [Tampa Bay Rowdies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rowdies) of the [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(2011%E2%80%932017\)), play in St. Petersburg proper, while the other two teams play across the bay in [Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida). As their names suggest, all of the teams represent the entire [Tampa Bay area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_area) and seek to draw fans from both sides of [Tampa Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay).

The Tampa-St. Petersburg area hosted the [Super Bowl LV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LV), where the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers) took on the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) at [Raymond James Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_James_Stadium) on February 7, 2021.[^cite_note-229]

The Rays began play in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Tampa_Bay_Devil_Rays_season), finishing last in the [American League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League)'s [East Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League_East_Division) in nine of the first ten seasons they played,[^cite_note-230] including their last year known as the "Devil Rays": [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Tampa_Bay_Devil_Rays_season).[^cite_note-231] In 2008, their 11th season, they held off the [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox) and won the AL East Division Championship for the first time.[^cite_note-232] In the playoffs, they again faced the Red Sox in the ALCS.[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] They defeated Boston and won the [American League Pennant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_American_League_Championship_Series).[^cite_note-235] However, they lost to the [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies) in the [2008 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_World_Series).[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237] The Rays also made an appearance in the [2020 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_World_Series) where they faced the [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md).[^cite_note-238]



[Tropicana Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicana_Field) shown from the upper deck during the first game of the [2010 Tampa Bay Rays season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Tampa_Bay_Rays_season)

From their inception until [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Tampa_Bay_Rays_season), the Rays played their regular season games at Tropicana Field and their [spring training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_training) games at historic Al Lang Stadium, formerly Progress Energy Park, giving them the unique distinction of being the only team in Major League Baseball that played its spring training games in their home city in more than 70 years. Beginning in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Tampa_Bay_Rays_season), the Rays have held spring training at [Charlotte Sports Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Sports_Park) in [Port Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Charlotte,_Florida), ending a 94-year streak of springtime baseball in the city.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240] Tropicana Field, the home venue of the Rays, played host to the 1999 [Final Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Championship#Final_Four).[^cite_note-241] Despite not having a team in the city since 2000 (with the [St. Petersburg Devil Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Devil_Rays)), St. Petersburg is home to [Minor League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball)'s main headquarters.[^cite_note-242] Due to the damage on Tropicana Field caused by [Hurricane Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Milton), the Rays spent the 2025 season at [George M. Steinbrenner Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Steinbrenner_Field) in Tampa.

St. Petersburg is home to the [Grand Prix of St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_of_St._Petersburg), the inaugural race was held in April 2005.[^cite_note-243] The circuit itself is made of downtown streets passing [Al Lang Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Lang_Stadium), the marina, and a runway in Albert Whitted Airport,[^cite_note-244] and streets are temporarily blocked off for the annual [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series) race.[^cite_note-245] The race was postponed in 2020 due to the spread of the COVID-19 coronavirus, and was rescheduled as the final race of the season, rather than the first race.[^cite_note-246] In 2012, the road intersecting Turn 10 was renamed Dan Wheldon Way in memory of [Dan Wheldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Wheldon), who won the 2005 race thanks to a move made on that turn.[^cite_note-247] Wheldon was killed in an accident at the [Las Vegas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Motor_Speedway) in the [2011 season finale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_IZOD_IndyCar_World_Championship).[^cite_note-248] In 2026 the circuit began hosting the [NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series) alongside the IndyCar Series on the same weekend.[^cite_note-249]



The newly renovated [Al Lang Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Lang_Stadium) for the [Tampa Bay Rowdies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rowdies)

The Tampa Bay Rowdies of the United Soccer League began play in Tampa in 2010 and moved to Al Lang Stadium in 2011. The long-time baseball venue is named after [Al Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Lang), a former mayor of St. Petersburg who was instrumental in bringing [spring training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_training) to the city in 1914. The Rowdies initially shared Al Lang Stadium with various amateur baseball events, but eventually took over operation of the facility and has converted it into a soccer-only facility[^cite_note-\:0-250] The Rowdies' ownership has expressed interest in moving up to join [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS) and a 2016 referendum gave the club permission to build a larger privately funded stadium at the site of Al Lang Stadium if the move takes place.[^cite_note-251]

The [Bay Area Pelicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Area_Pelicans) Rugby Football Club has made their home in St. Petersburg since 1977.

## Government

See also: [List of mayors of St. Petersburg, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_St._Petersburg,_Florida)



St. Petersburg's city hall

The city of St. Petersburg has been governed under a [strong mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_mayor) form of government since 1993. The Mayor of St. Petersburg and the St. Petersburg City Council members are elected to a four-year term, limited to two consecutive terms. Currently the mayor of St. Petersburg is [Ken Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Welch) who took office on January 6, 2022. The legislative body consists of eight City Council members representing each of their designated city districts.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253]

St. Petersburg is split between [Florida's 13th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida's_13th_congressional_district), represented in Congress by Republican [Anna Paulina Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Paulina_Luna), and [Florida's 14th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida's_14th_congressional_district), represented by Democrat [Kathy Castor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Castor). The city is more left-leaning than the surrounding county. In 2020, 62.3% percent of voters in St. Petersburg's urban core cast their ballots for [Democratic](5043544.md) candidate [Joe Biden](145422.md).[^cite_note-254]

### Higher education

St. Petersburg is home to several institutions of higher education. The University of South Florida St. Petersburg is an autonomous campus in the [University of South Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Florida) system.[^cite_note-256] The University of South Florida St. Petersburg serves 6,500 students. [Eckerd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckerd_College), founded in 1958, is a private four-year [liberal arts college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts_college).[^cite_note-\:1-257] Also in St. Petersburg is the [Poynter Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poynter_Institute), a journalism institute which owns the *Tampa Bay Times* in a unique arrangement.[^cite_note-258] [Stetson Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stetson_Law_School) is located in [Gulfport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfport,_Florida), which is adjacent to St. Petersburg between the south beaches. St. Petersburg College, founded in 1927, is a state college within the Florida College System. It has an average of 65,000 students spread across 11 campuses and centers in the Bay area, four of which are in St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-259]

Other colleges and universities in the wider Tampa Bay Area include the University of South Florida and the [University of Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tampa) located in Tampa and [Hillsborough Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_Community_College), with campuses across [Hillsborough County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_County,_Florida).[^cite_note-\:1-257]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation



The [Sunshine Skyway Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Skyway_Bridge) viewed from [Tampa Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay)

#### Roads

The city is connected to Tampa by the east by [causeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causeway) and bridges across Tampa Bay,[^cite_note-270] and to [Bradenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradenton,_Florida) in the south by the Sunshine Skyway Bridge ([Interstate 275](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_275_\(Florida\))),[^cite_note-271] which traverses the mouth of the bay. It is also served by Interstates [175](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_175) and [375](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_375_\(Florida\)), which branch off I-275 into the southern and northern areas of downtown respectively.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273] The Gandy Bridge, conceived by [George Gandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gandy) and opened in 1924, was the first causeway to be built across Tampa Bay, connecting St. Petersburg and Tampa cities without a circuitous 43-mile (69 km) trip around the bay through [Oldsmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldsmar,_Florida).[^cite_note-274]

As for the history of the roads of St. Petersburg, the total and complete failure of Hillsborough County to build roads on the peninsula was one of the main reasons why Pinellas County was formed. Through Pinellas County, a system of marl roads was built throughout the county, giving good service for a while until the rain gave trouble with the marl surface. A demand was then made for brick roads, which was met with opposition for the price of bricks was much more expensive, the election was eventually passed a new network of nine-foot brick roads was completed by November 15, 1916. It had only been a few years after that, and it was apparent that there was a mistake with the fact that 9 foot roads were not wide enough to handle the heavy traffic, which was at the time two cars to pass each other - which was essential. There was another issue that the shoulders could not be maintained in good condition either, probably due to the fact that drivers had to constantly use them due to the road not being wide enough to begin with. It took years later for the election to finally be called and passed to create wider and better roads on a bond issue of \$2,597,000.[^cite_note-Grismer-28]

#### Airports

Main article: [List of airports in the Tampa Bay area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_Tampa_Bay_area)

Nearby [Tampa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_International_Airport) (TPA) provides air transportation for many passengers.[^cite_note-275] Historically, smaller airlines with service to smaller cities and towns have operated at [St. Petersburg-Clearwater International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg-Clearwater_International_Airport), with a number of air carriers only providing only seasonal services.[^cite_note-276] The exception at the St. Petersburg-Clearwater International Airport (PIE) is [Allegiant Air](1372018.md) which currently operates [Airbus A319](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A319) and [A320](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A320) mainline jetliners into the airfield with year-round nonstop service from many cities in the eastern U.S.[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] [Albert Whitted Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Whitted_Airport) provides general aviation services near the heart of downtown St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280]

#### Mass transit



Trolley serving Downtown

Mass transit in St. Petersburg is provided by the [Pinellas Suncoast Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinellas_Suncoast_Transit_Authority) (PSTA). A sightseeing trolley, called The Looper, also travels to key downtown destinations daily such as [USFSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Florida_St._Petersburg), Sundial, Vinoy Hotel, and the multiple museums around the city.[^cite_note-281][^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283] Short-term bike sharing is also offered via [Coast Bike Share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Bike_Share?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285]

In 2022, PSTA launched the [SunRunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunRunner?action=edit\&redlink=1), a bus rapid transit service connecting downtown St. Petersburg to [St. Pete Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Pete_Beach). The SunRunner is the first bus rapid transit line in the [Tampa Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Area) and is expected to spur economic development along the Central Avenue corridor in [Pinellas County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinellas_County). In its first weekend alone, the SunRunner had over 10,000 passengers.[^cite_note-286] Ridership continues to be steady; however, PSTA hiked fares, citing a homelessness issue.[^cite_note-287]

#### Railroads

[CSX Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSX_Transportation) operates a former [Atlantic Coast Line Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Line_Railroad) branch line which sees daily rail traffic from north Tampa though [Safety Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_Harbor,_Florida), [Clearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater,_Florida), and [Largo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largo,_Florida). As of March 2008, the portion that ran into downtown St. Petersburg and the adjacent western industrial areas was abandoned. There is a small rail yard to the northwest of downtown St. Petersburg at the new end of the rail line with several spur lines serving industries in the area.[^cite_note-tampabay.com-288]

The former [Seaboard Air Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaboard_Air_Line) branch from the western coastal portion of the county was abandoned in the 1980s and converted to a popular recreational trail called the [Pinellas Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinellas_Trail).[^cite_note-289]

Notable former stations include the [St. Petersburg ACL station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_\(Amtrak_station\)), which became an Amtrak station from 1971 to 1983, St. Petersburg Seaboard Air Line Passenger Station, and the [St. Petersburg Seaboard Coast Line station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaboard_Coast_Line_Railroad_station_\(St._Petersburg,_Florida\)).[^cite_note-tampabay.com-288]

#### Port and marinas



Boats located at South Basin

One of the main sea transportation areas in St. Petersburg is the [Port of St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_St._Petersburg), which is located in downtown St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-290] Boat marinas in downtown St. Petersburg are also available such as the Municipal Marina which located in the Southern and Central Yacht Basins,[^cite_note-291] and Harborage Marina located in the Bayboro Harbor.[^cite_note-292] The Cross-Bay ferry runs, at a fee, from St. Pete to downtown Tampa.[^cite_note-293]

### Utilities

The city of St. Petersburg's major electricity system is provided by [Duke Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Energy), the city's major gas system is provided by [TECO Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TECO_Energy) in the industrial and commercial parts of the city, and the city's water services are provided by the city of St. Petersburg.[^cite_note-294]
