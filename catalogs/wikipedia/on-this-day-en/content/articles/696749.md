**Dera Ismail Khan** ([/deɪrʌ-ɪsmaɪ.iːl-xɑːn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language): ڈیرہ اسماعیل خان, [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): ډېره اسماعيل خان), abbreviated as **DI Khan**,[^cite_note-3] is a city and headquarters of [Dera Ismail Khan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Ismail_Khan_District), located in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), Pakistan. It is the [10th largest city of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_in_Pakistan) and [sixth largest in the province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa_by_population) of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa by population. Dera Ismail Khan is situated on the west bank of the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River), at its junction with the [Gomal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomal_River).

It is 300 kilometres (190 mi) south of the provincial capital [Peshawar](66230.md), and 230 kilometres (140 mi) northwest of [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan), [Punjab](24751.md).[^cite_note-IGI269-4]

## Etymology

In the [Saraiki language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language), the word *ḍerā* means "tent, encampment", and is commonly found in the name of towns in the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley) such as [Dera Ghazi Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Ghazi_Khan) and [Dera Bugti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Bugti). It is named after the [Dodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodai_tribe) mercenary Ismail Khan, son of [Malik Sohrab Dodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Sohrab_Dodai), who founded the town.[^cite_note-5] As such the name of the city means "Camp of Ismail Khan."

## History

See also: [Derajat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derajat), [History of Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Multan), [History of Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Punjab), and [History of Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sindh)

### Foundation

#### Early history



Map of D. I. Khan

The region around Dera Ismail Khan has been inhabited for millennia, as evidenced by the nearby site of [Rehman Dheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehman_Dheri) — a [pre-Harappan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilisation) archaeological site dating back from 3300 BCE.[^cite_note-6] In the seventh century, the city had a large population of [Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) and [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist).[^cite_note-7]

#### Medieval history

D. I. Khan is located in the historical [Derajat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derajat) region, which was established in the 15th century, when [Baloch people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) were invited to settle the region by Shah Husayn, of the [Langah Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langah_Sultanate) of [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan). The Baloch settlers were assimilated through the later waves of [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) settlement, although villagers along the alluvial plains are typically Baloch or [Jat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jat).[^cite_note-tolbort-8]

Dera Ismail Khan region was part of [Multan Subah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan_Subah) of [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire), though D. I Khan never attained a station of great power or importance before the British period. D. I. Khan grew prosperous as a trading centre for [Powindah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi_people) nomads, during Nader Shah's invasion of the Mughal Empire.

D. I. Khan was ruled by nine generations of Baloch leaders descended directly from Ismail Khan. The last, Nusrat Khan, was removed from power after the city was captured by [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) in 1750. In 1794, the city was granted to Nawab Muhammad Khan Sadozai by [Shahzada Kamran Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahzada_Kamran_Durrani).[^cite_note-tolbort-8]

#### Re establishment of the city

The original town was swept away by flooding on the Indus River in 1823. The present city was founded by Nawab Sher Muhammad Khan of the [Saddozai clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadduzai) in 1825, and now stands four miles (6  km) away from the permanent channel of the river, atop a small plateau.[^cite_note-EB1911-9] Nawab Sadozai took into consideration the opinions of Diwan Lakhi Mal and Tej Bhan Nandwani for the city's reconstruction.[^cite_note-c2d-10] Architects were brought in from Punjab, who designed a city where Hindus would live south of the city center and Muslims north of it.[^cite_note-c2d-10]

Four bazaars were laid in each of the cardinal directions, with all four converging in the town's central *[Chowgalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowgalla)*.[^cite_note-c2d-10] The rebuilt city contained a large bazaar for Afghan traders, and the city prospered from trade via the [Gomal Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomal_Pass).[^cite_note-DIKC-11] An eight foot mud wall with nine gates was built around the city during this time as well,[^cite_note-c2d-10] some of which such as the *Kaneran Wala* and *Sakki* survive until today. All existing buildings date from no earlier than the 19th century.[^cite_note-IGI269-4]

### Sikh rule (1821–1849)

Dera Ismail Khan remained under Sadozai rule from the nearby town of [Mankera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankera) until it was annexed by [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) ruler Maharaja [Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh) of the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire) in 1836.[^cite_note-c2d-10] Diwan Lakhi Mal appointed city's *Kardar* ruler, though the Nawabs of the city from the Durrani order were allowed to maintain their title and some income.[^cite_note-c2d-10] The city suffered under punitive taxes that lead to frequent complaints in the Sikh *Darbar* at Lahore, resulting in several changes of *Kardar*.[^cite_note-c2d-10]

### Colonial British India era

D. I. Khan's first deputy commissioner under [British Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire) was General Henry Charles Van Cortland, who arrived in February 1848, before departing later that year to quell a revolt in nearby Multan. Following the defeat of rebels at Multan, Lieutenant Butler was made the next deputy commissioner of D. I. Khan and [Bannu](3769844.md).[^cite_note-c2d-10] Under his rule, city's infrastructure and colonial administration system were established in which top posts were exclusively occupied by the British.[^cite_note-c2d-10] The city was on the edge of the Tribal Areas — lands that were frequently in rebellion against British Indian Empire. The town did not rise up in revolt against British Indian Empire during the 1857 [Sepoy Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy_Mutiny).[^cite_note-c2d-10] In 1861, D. I. Khan was made into the Divisional Capital of the new [Dera Ismail Khan Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Ismail_Khan_Division): analogous to a British county.[^cite_note-c2d-10]

The municipality was constituted in 1867, while the [Dera Ismail Khan Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Ismail_Khan_Cantonment) was established in 1894.[^cite_note-DIKC-11] By 1881 the city's population was 22,164. The military [cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonment) area, which lies southeast of the town, had an area of 44 square miles (110 km<sup>2</sup>), excluding the portion known as Fort Akalgarh on the northwest side.&#x20;

The Dera Ismail Brigade had its winter headquarters at Dera Ismail Khan, and the garrison consisted of a mountain battery, a regiment of Native cavalry, and three regiments of Native infantry. Detachments from these regiments helped to garrison the outposts of Drazinda, Jandola, and Jatta. The "Civil Lines" neighborhood was built to the south.[^cite_note-IGI269-4]

The town possessed a civil hospital; its chief educational institutions were two aided Anglo-vernacular high schools, one maintained by the Church Missionary Society and the other by the Bharatri Sabha, and an Anglo-vernacular middle school maintained by the municipality.[^cite_note-IGI269-4]

According to the 1901 census, the population of Dera Ismail Khan was 31,737, of whom 18,662 were Muslims, 11,486 Hindus, and 1,420 Sikhs. Of the total, 3,450 lived in the cantonment.[^cite_note-12]

The predominantly Muslim population (which accounted for 60% of the local population of Dera Ismail Khan) may have supported the [Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League) and the [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement).

### After Independence of 1947

After the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) and independence of Pakistan in 1947, the minority [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) and [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) migrated to India, while the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) migrants from India settled in Dera Ismail Khan. In India, Model Town, Vijay Nagar and [Derawal Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derawal_Nagar) colony in [Delhi](37756.md) absorbed many Hindu former residents of D. I. Khan.[^cite_note-The_Tribune_-_Derawal_Nagar-13]

Following an influx of refugees following the 1979 [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), D. I. Khan's population tripled.[^cite_note-DIKC-11]

During the [November 2008 Dera Ismail Khan bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2008_Dera_Ismail_Khan_bombing), Shia religious leader Allama Nazir Hussain Shah was shot dead in a sectarian killing with Shah Iqbal Hussain. During his funeral prayers, a suicide bomber blew himself up, killing nine people and injuring 39. In the [January 2009 bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2009_Dera_Ismail_Khan_bombings), 52 people were injured while 16 were killed as three explosions took place in the city.

[In May 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2009_Dera_Ismail_Khan_bombings), three explosions caused the deaths of five people while nine were injured. On 14 June 2009, eight people were killed while 27 injured in an explosion in Pir Market near a bus stand.[^cite_note-14] On 29 July 2009, two people were killed and four injured when a bomb planted in car detonated near the district court.[^cite_note-15] On [May 18, 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Dera_Ismail_Khan_bombing), a bomb planted on a motorcycle exploded near a police van killing 13 people while injuring 14.[^cite_note-16] On [25 June 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Kulachi_police_station_attack), between 10 and 12 militants of [Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehreek-i-Taliban_Pakistan) attacked a police station in the nearby town of [Kulachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulachi), killing 10 police officers.[^cite_note-17] The [Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-e-Taliban_Pakistan) claimed the responsibility of these attacks.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

In 2014, the wide-scale [Operation Zarb-e-Azb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zarb-e-Azb) was launched against militants throughout Pakistan, which resulted in a greatly improved security environment by 2016, although on January 4, 2017 [15 people including five policemen were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Dera_Ismail_Khan_bombing) when a remote control planted bomb exploded on Bannu Road.[^cite_note-20] On 17 February 2017, another five people, including four police officers, were killed after unidentified suspects opened fire on a police van near Mission Mor.[^cite_note-21]

As part of the 2015 [China-Pakistan Economic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China-Pakistan_Economic_Corridor), it was announced that D. I. Khan would be the terminus of the [Hakla–Dera Ismail Khan Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakla%E2%80%93Dera_Ismail_Khan_Motorway) — a four-lane [controlled access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_access) motorway, 280 km in length, that is to begin at the [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) near [Islamabad](51153.md) and serve as part of the [Western Alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Alignment) of the economic corridor.[^cite_note-thenews.com.pk-22]

On 3 November 2023, a [bomb blast occurred in Dera Ismail Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dera_Ismail_Khan_bombing), in which a number of people were killed and injured.[^cite_note-23]

On 25 January 2026, a suicide bombing took place at the home of Noor Alam Mehsud, a pro-government community leader, killing at least seven people and injuring 25, no groups claimed reasonability for the attack.[^cite_note-24]

## Geography

### Climate

Dera Ismail Khan has a [hot arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWh*), bordering on a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) (*BSh*), with sweltering summers and warm winters. Rain mainly falls in two distinct periods: in the late winter and early spring from February to April, and in the monsoon from July to September.

D. I Khan is situated at an altitude of 177 metres above sea level.

## Transportation

### Road



Yarik Toll Plaza of [Islamabad-Dera Ismail Khan Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-14_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) that serves as south terminal of the motorway

The city is connected to Bannu via the highway, which further connects it to the provincial capital of Peshawar via [Kohat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohat) and [Darra Adam Khel](3724191.md). Another road connects D. I. Khan to Mianwali through [Chashma Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chashma_Barrage), and another connects the city to [Zhob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhob). The third major road connects it to [Bhakkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakkar) in Punjab, situated on the eastern bank of the Indus River. A bridge on the Indus River was constructed in the early 1980s, before which the approach to Bhakkar was made through a [boat bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_of_boats). Another road connect it to district [Tank](4592102.md) which further leads towards [Jandola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jandola)-AngoorAdda areas of South [South Waziristan](4175082.md) district at North west and [Pezu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darra_Pezu) on eastern side.

D. I. Khan is at the terminus of the [Hakla–Dera Ismail Khan Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakla%E2%80%93Dera_Ismail_Khan_Motorway) — a 280-km, four-lane [controlled access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_access) motorway that is to extend from the Hakla Interchange on the [M1 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway_\(Pakistan\)), near [Islamabad](51153.md) to D. I. Khan.[^cite_note-thenews.com.pk-22]

### Air

The city is served by [Dera Ismail Khan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Ismail_Khan_Airport), though no commercial flights operate to the airport.
