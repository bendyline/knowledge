The **Queen Elizabeth II Law Courts**, in [Derby Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Square), [Liverpool](18081.md), are operated by [His Majesty's Courts and Tribunals Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Courts_and_Tribunals_Service). The building is used by the [Crown Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Court),[^cite_note-hmcs20110401-1-1] the Magistrates' Court, Liverpool District [Probate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probate) Registry[^cite_note-2] and the Liverpool [Youth Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_justice_in_England_and_Wales#Trial_in_the_Youth_Court).[^cite_note-3]

## History

Until the mid-1980s, all Crown Court cases were heard in [St George's Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George's_Hall,_Liverpool).[^cite_note-4] However, as the number of court cases in Liverpool grew, it became necessary to commission a more modern courthouse for both criminal and civil matters: the site selected by the [Lord Chancellor's Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Chancellor's_Department) had been occupied by [Liverpool Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Castle) between the 13th and 18th century.[^cite_note-mooch-5]

The new building was commissioned by the now-defunct [Property Services Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_Services_Agency), who were seeking a design which expressed authority and power.[^cite_note-Wallsgrove21-6] Construction of the new building started in 1973.[^cite_note-pevsner-7] It was designed by [Farmer and Dark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer_and_Dark)[^cite_note-8] in the [brutalist style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture), built with vertically ribbed [pre-cast concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-cast_concrete) panels in dark, reddish tones[^cite_note-Pevsner2-9] at a cost of £43.4 million,[^cite_note-10] and was officially opened in 1984.[^cite_note-mooch-5]

Hearings of the Liverpool Magistrates Court, which had been held at the [courthouse in Dale Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Magistrates'_Court), transferred to the Queen Elizabeth II Law Courts in 2015.[^cite_note-11]

## Architecture

The design by Farmer and Dark features towers on the north and south sides which were intended to echo Liverpool Castle.[^cite_note-mooch-5] Pollard and [Pevsner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaus_Pevsner) describe the architecture as "hulking" and "dispiriting", but "richly sculptural".[^cite_note-pevsner-7][^cite_note-Pevsner2-9] The front facade of the building features a four metres wide version of the [Royal coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_coat_of_arms_of_the_United_Kingdom). Cast in [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete), it was designed by British sculptor [Richard Kindersley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kindersley).[^cite_note-12] The interior, which accommodates 28 court rooms on 10 storeys, contains strips of brown ceramic tiles in strips between the staircase tower windows.[^cite_note-Pevsner2-9]

## Notable trials

Notable trials have included:

- The trial and acquittal of [Ken Dodd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Dodd#Tax_evasion_court_case) for tax evasion in 1990

  [^cite_note-13]

- The trial and conviction of Michael Barton for the [murder of Anthony Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Anthony_Walker) in 2005

  [^cite_note-aw20110401-3-14]

- The trial and conviction of Sean Mercer for the [murder of Rhys Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Rhys_Jones) in 2008

  [^cite_note-mrj20110401-3-15]
