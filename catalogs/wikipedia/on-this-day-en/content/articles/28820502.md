**Argentines**, also known as **Argentinians**[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] or **Argentineans**,[^cite_note-23] are people associated with [Argentina](18951905.md). These ties may be legal, historical, ancestral, or cultural. For most Argentines, several of these connections exist and are collectively the source of their Argentine identity.

Argentina is a [multiethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiethnic_society) society, home to people of various [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity), [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\)), [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), [denomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_denomination), and [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality) origins, with the majority of the population made up of [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) immigrants and their descendants.[^cite_note-encuesta-24][^cite_note-Coke-25][^cite_note-26] As a result, Argentines do not equate their nationality with ethnicity, but with citizenship and allegiance to Argentina. Aside from the indigenous population, nearly all Argentines or their ancestors [immigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_European_immigration_wave_to_Argentina) within the past five centuries. Among countries in the world that have received the most immigrants in modern history, Argentina, with 6.6 million, ranks second to the [United States](3434750.md) (27 million), and ahead of other immigrant destinations such as [Canada](5042916.md), [Brazil](3383.md), and [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-Capitulo7-27][^cite_note-Labor+and+Immigration-28]

It is estimated that between 79.4% to 85% of Argentina's population are [whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Argentines) (predominantly [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_of_European_descent) or [Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_of_Levantine_descent) ancestry), while [mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo_Argentines) (mixed [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_of_European_descent) and [Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindians) ancestry without predominance) constitute between 10.7% to 16.3%; according to the 2022 census, [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_in_Argentina) (predominantly [Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindians) ancestry) make up 2.8%, while [blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people_in_Argentina) account 0.7% (predominantly [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_African_people) ancestry).[^cite_note-Argentina_Nationality-29][^cite_note-Argentina:_Noun_&_Adjective-30][^cite_note-31] Regardless of predominant ancestry, it is estimated that between 30% and 56% of Argentines have at least one [Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_America) ancestor, and between 4% and 6% have at least one [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_African_people) ancestor.[^cite_note-LaNación-32][^cite_note-estructura-33][^cite_note-INADI-34]

### Overview



Argentines during the opening parade of the XXXIV [Immigrant's Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant's_Festival) in [Oberá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ober%C3%A1), [Misiones](233567.md).

Argentina is a [multiethnic society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiethnic_society), which means that it is home to people of many different ethnic, racial, religious, and denominational backgrounds. Argentina is a [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot) of different peoples.[^cite_note-35]

In the mid-19th century a large wave of immigration started to arrive in Argentina due to new Constitutional policies that encouraged immigration, and issues in the countries the immigrants came from, such as wars, poverty, hunger, and famines. The main immigration sources were from Europe, the countries from the [Near](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East) and the Middle East, Russia, and Japan. Eventually, Argentina became the country with the second-largest number of immigrants in the period, with 6.6 million, second only to the United States with 27 million.[^cite_note-Capitulo7-27][^cite_note-Labor+and+Immigration-28]

Therefore, most Argentines have some European ancestry (with a significant [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America) component, and a less prominent [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Argentines) component), and are either descendants of colonial-era settlers and/or of the 19th and 20th century [immigrants from Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_European_immigration_wave_to_Argentina).[^cite_note-caputo-36]

The most common ethnic groups are [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) (mostly [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_people) and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_people) descent) and/or [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizos) (mixed [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) and [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) ancestry).[^cite_note-37] According to Argentine government websites, it is estimated that more than 20 million Argentines, more than 46% of the total population, have Italian ancestry, wholly or in part.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Argentines descend mostly from Spaniards, Italians, Native Americans and to a lesser extent from French, Germans, East Asians, and others.

Immigration of recent decades includes mainly [Paraguayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_Argentines), [Bolivians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_Argentines), [Peruvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Argentines), and [Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians), among other [Latin Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Americans), Eastern Europeans, Africans, Arabs, and Asians.[^cite_note-lastdecade-40][^cite_note-41]

- Homburguer et al., 2015, *[PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) Genetics*: 67% European, 28% Amerindian, 4% African and 1.4% Asian.

A team led by Daniel Corach conducted a study in 2010, analyzing 246 samples from eight provinces and three different regions of the country. The results were as follows: [Mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA) analysis again showed a great Amerindian contribution by maternal lineage, at 53.7%, with 44.3% of European contribution, and a 2% African contribution. The analysis of [Y-Chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-Chromosome) DNA revealed a 94.1% of European contribution, and only 4.9% and 0.9% of Native American and Black African contribution, respectively. The study of 24 [autosomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosomal_DNA) markers also proved a large European contribution of 78.5%, against 17.3% of Amerindian and 4.2% Black African contributions.[^cite_note-\:0-52]

Several studies found out that the European ancestry in Argentina comes mainly from the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) and [Italy](14532.md) with a much lower contribution from [Central](5188.md) and [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The Italian component appears strongest in the East and Center-West, while the Spanish influence dominates in the North East and North West.[^cite_note-57]

### European Argentines

Main article: [Argentines of European descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_of_European_descent)

Further information: [White Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Argentines)



[Belarusian Argentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Argentines) woman during Immigrant Day festivities in Buenos Aires, 2010.



Descendants of [Welsh settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Wladfa) in [Rawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawson,_Chubut), [Chubut Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubut_Province)

Argentines of partial European include the Argentine descendants of colonists from Spain during the colonial period prior to 1810,[^cite_note-scarecrow-58] and mainly of immigrants from Europe in the [great immigratory wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_European_immigration_wave_to_Argentina) from the mid 19th century to the mid 20th century.[^cite_note-AcercaImmi-59] No recent Argentine census has included comprehensive questions on ethnicity, although numerous studies have determined that European Argentines have been a majority in the country since 1914.[^cite_note-uaemex-60]

The most numerous immigrant European communities are: [Spaniards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Argentine) (including [Basques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Argentine), [Asturians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturians) and [Galicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Argentine)), [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Argentine) (62.5% of the population have some degree of Italian descent),[^cite_note-61] [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Argentine), Scandinavians (mainly [Danes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Argentine) and [Swedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Argentine)), Slavs (including [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Argentina), [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Argentine), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Argentine), [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs_in_Argentina), [Bulgarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians_in_South_America), [Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_Argentine), [Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_of_Serb_descent) and [Croats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croats_in_Argentina)), [Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Argentine), the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Argentine) (including francophone [Basques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Argentine)), the [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Argentine), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Argentine), the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Argentine), among others in smaller number.

There are approximately 300,000 [Romani descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Argentina) in Argentina. They belong to the Romani subgroups Greek, [Moldavian](19260.md) and Russian [Kalderash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalderash), some [Lovari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovari) and some Chilean Xoraxane. There are also Spanish Kalé and [Boyash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyash) living in Argentina.[^cite_note-62]

### Mixed Argentines

Main article: [Mestizo Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo_Argentines)



A gaucho man in [Junín de los Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun%C3%ADn_de_los_Andes), [Neuquén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuqu%C3%A9n_province).

Within the population totals, there may be an imprecise amount of mixed population. In one of the most comprehensive genetic studies involving the population of Argentina, 441 Argentines from across the North East, Salta, Chubut and Buenos Aires (especially the urban conglomeration of Buenos Aires) of the country, it was observed that the sample population comprised on average of 65% European, followed by 31% Amerindian, and finally 4% of African ancestry; however, this study was unweighted and meant to be a representation of the diversity of Argentine DNA rather than a demonstration of the average ethnic composition of the country. It was also found there were great differences in the ancestry amongst Argentines as one traveled across the country. A study by Daniel Corach that attempted to find the average Argentine ancestry by weighing the population of various regions gave a significantly higher estimate of European ancestry at 78.5% of the average Argentine's autosomal DNA.[^cite_note-63]

### Indigenous Argentines

Main article: [Indigenous peoples in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_in_Argentina)



Family in [Cachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachi,_Argentina), [Salta Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salta_Province)

Argentina has 35 officially recognized indigenous people groups.[^cite_note-indi_indec-64] As of the 2010 [census](6889.md) \[[INDEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Statistics_and_Census_of_Argentina)], some 955,032 Argentines (2.38% of the country's population) self-identify as [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) or first-generation descendants of indigenous peoples.[^cite_note-encuesta2010NOA-65]

The most populous indigenous groups were the [Aonikenk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehuelche_people), [Kolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolla_people), [Qom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qom_people), [Wichí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wich%C3%AD_people), [Diaguita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaguita), [Mocoví](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mocov%C3%AD_people), [Huarpe peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huarpe_people), [Mapuche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche) and [Guarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarani_people)[^cite_note-indi_indec-64] Many Argentines also identify as having at least one indigenous ancestor; a genetic study conducted by the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires) in 2011 showed that 56% of the 320 Argentines sampled were shown to have at least one indigenous ancestor in one parental lineage and around 11% had indigenous ancestors in both parental lineages.[^cite_note-estructura-33] Other studies suggest that figure could be 30%.[^cite_note-LaNación-32][^cite_note-DW-66]

[Jujuy Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jujuy_Province), in the [Argentine Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Northwest), is home to the highest percentage of households (15%) with at least one indigenous resident or a direct descendant of an indigenous person; [Chubut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubut_Province) and [Neuquén Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuqu%C3%A9n_Province), in [Patagonia](65433.md), have upwards of 12%.[^cite_note-67]

### Asian Argentines

Main article: [Asian Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Argentines)

Argentines of Asian ancestry are defined as either born within [Argentina](18951905.md), or born elsewhere and later to become a citizen or [resident](1284324.md) of Argentina. Asian Argentines settled in Argentina in large numbers during several waves of immigration in the 19th and 20th centuries. In the 19th century, West Asian immigrants, primarily from Lebanon and Syria came as a result of the [1860 Mount Lebanon civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_Mount_Lebanon_civil_war).[^cite_note-oni.escuelas.edu.artoo-68] In the early 20th century, a small wave of East Asian immigrants, particularly from Japan came to the country.

#### East Asians Argentines

Main article: [East Asian Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Argentines)



East Asian Argentines at the Korean stand of the 2012 Gastronomic Patio held in [Buenos Aires](255919.md).

The first Argentines of East Asian descent were a small group of Japanese immigrants, mainly from the [Okinawa prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_prefecture), which came in the period between the early and mid 20th century. In the 1960s, [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_people) began to arrive, and in the 1980s, [Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_people) immigrants. The 1990s brought the largest wave of Asian immigration so far to Argentina, from mainland Chinese immigrants, eventually becoming the fourth largest immigrant community in 2013, after Paraguayans, Bolivians, and Peruvians.[^cite_note-lastdecade-40] The small East Asian Argentine population has generally kept a low profile, and is accepted by greater Argentine society. Primarily living in their own [neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrios_of_Buenos_Aires) in Buenos Aires, many currently own their own businesses of varying sizes – largely textiles, grocery stores, and buffet-style restaurants.

#### West Asian Argentines

Main articles: [Arab Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Argentines), [Armenian Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Argentines), and [Turkish Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Argentines)

Further information: [White Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Argentines)



Arab-Argentines during the Day of the immigrants in [Buenos Aires](255919.md).

Arabs and Argentines with partial Arab ancestry represent about 3.2 million people, whose ancestry traces back to any of various waves of immigrants, largely from the [Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) region of Western Asia, from what is now [Syria](7515849.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md); and from [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia) and [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) in a lesser extent.[^cite_note-69] Due to the fact that many Arab countries were under control of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) by the time the large immigration wave took place, most Arabs entered the country with [Turkish](11125639.md) passports, and so they are colloquially referred to as *los turcos*.

There are also 80,000 to 135,000 descendants of Armenians in Argentina, most of them in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), representing one of the largest [Armenian diasporas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Diaspora) in the world.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-Rumbosur.org-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-est1-73]

### Black Argentines

Main article: [Afro-Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Argentines)



Afro-Argentine man in [Merlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlo,_Buenos_Aires) with a [masacalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masacalla), used for Argentine [candombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candombe).

According to the Argentine national census of 2010, 149,493[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] (0.37% of the country's overall population) identified as [Afro-Argentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Argentine), although according to [gene pools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_pools) studies, the Argentine population with some degree of Sub-Saharan African descent would be around 7.5%.[^cite_note-caputo-36] World Bank and Argentine government estimates have suggested the Argentine population with at least some African ancestry could number over 2 million, or between 4% and 6% of the population.[^cite_note-INADI-34][^cite_note-76]

Despite the fact that in the 1960s it was calculated that Argentina owed two thirds of the volume of its population to European immigration,[^cite_note-77] over 5% of Argentines state they have at least one black ancestor, and a further 20% state they do not know whether or not they have any black ancestors.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Genetic studies carried out in 2005 showed that the average level of African genetic contribution in the population of Buenos Aires is 2.2%, but that this component is concentrated in 10% of the population who display notably higher levels of African ancestry.[^cite_note-FejermanCarnese2005-80] Today there is still a notable Afro-Argentine community in the [Buenos Aires](255919.md) districts of [San Telmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Telmo) and [La Boca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Boca). There are also quite a few African-descended Argentines in [Merlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlo,_Buenos_Aires) and [Ciudad Evita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Evita), in the Buenos Aires metropolitan area.

[Immigration from Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_Argentines) was one of the earliest African migratory flows in the post-colonial era, beginning as early as the late 19th century and well into the 20th century.[^cite_note-81] Today, Cape Verdeans constitute one of the largest African immigrant communities, numbering over 15,000; they mainly live in port cities in [Buenos Aires Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Province), such as [Ensenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensenada,_Buenos_Aires) and [Dock Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dock_Sud).[^cite_note-82] Immigration from Senegal, Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Angola and other African countries in recent decades has caused a surge in the country's black population as well.[^cite_note-PAGINA12-83]

## Languages

Main article: [Languages of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Argentina)

See also: [List of indigenous languages of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_indigenous_languages_of_Argentina)



Sign in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco,_Argentina), [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_Province,_Argentina), in [Spanish](26825.md), [Italian](14708.md) and [Piedmontese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmontese_language), reading: "Welcome to the city of San Francisco, the capital of the Argentine Piedmont".



Sign in [Buenos Aires](255919.md) in [Spanish](26825.md) and [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language), reading the street name and the Korean phonetic transliteration.

Although Spanish is dominant, being the national language spoken by virtually all Argentines,[^cite_note-84] at least 40 languages are spoken in Argentina. Languages spoken by at least 100,000 Argentines include [Amerindian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas) such as [Southern Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Quechua), [Guaraní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaran%C3%AD_language) and [Mapudungun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapudungun), and immigrant languages such as [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German), Italian, English, French or [Levantine Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantine_Arabic).[^cite_note-85]

Two native languages are [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct_languages) ([Abipón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abip%C3%B3n_language) and [Chané](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan%C3%A9_language)), while some others are [endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_languages), spoken by elderly people whose descendants do not speak the languages[^cite_note-LAR-86] (such as [Vilela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilela_language), [Puelche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puelche_language), [Tehuelche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehuelche_language) and [Selkʼnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selk%CA%BCnam_language)).

There are also other communities of immigrants that speak their native languages, such as the Chinese language spoken by at least half of the over 60,000 Chinese immigrants (mostly in [Buenos Aires](255919.md)) and an [Occitan-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_Language) community in [Pigüé, Buenos Aires Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig%C3%BC%C3%A9). [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) is also spoken by over 35,000 people in the [Chubut Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubut_Province). This includes a dialect called [Patagonian Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagonian_Welsh), which has developed since the start of the [Welsh settlement in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_settlement_in_Argentina) in 1865.[^cite_note-Ariel_Brooks-87]

A high percentage of Argentines are proficient in the English language since its teaching is included in educational establishments as early as kindergarten. According to the 2023 edition of the English Proficiency Index, Argentines have the highest level of English proficiency in [Latin America](18524.md), ranking 28th worldwide.[^cite_note-English_Proficiency_Index-88]

Argentines during a [Catholic Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Mass). [Christianity](5211.md) is the majority, historical, and cultural religion in Argentina.

A majority of the population of Argentina is [Christian](18337522.md). According to CONICET survey on creeds, about 76.5% of Argentines are [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism_in_Argentina), 11.3% religiously indifferent, 9% [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) (with 7.9% in [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism) denominations), 1.2% [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), and 0.9% [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints).[^cite_note-90]

Argentina is home to the largest [Jewish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora) in [Latin America](18524.md) and 6th in the world, numbering 182,300 individuals or 0.45% of the population.[^cite_note-91] On a city level, [Buenos Aires](255919.md) has the [second largest population of Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Argentina) in the Americas, second only to New York City.

Argentina is also home to the largest [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) minority in Latin America (see [Islam in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Argentina)). Although accurate statistics on religion are not available (because the national census does not solicit religious data), the actual size of [Argentina](18951905.md)'s Muslim community is estimated to be around 1% of the total population (400,000 to 500,000 members), according to the [International Religious Freedom Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Religious_Freedom_Report) in 2015.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] [Buenos Aires](255919.md) hosts the largest mosque in [Latin America](18524.md).[^cite_note-clarin-94]

[Buenos Aires](255919.md) also features notable [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church), [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Orthodox_Church) and [Russian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) churches, [Presbyterian Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism_in_South_Korea) and [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) churches, as well as [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) temples, among other minority religions.

## Emigration



Supporters of [Club Atlético San Lorenzo de Almagro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_San_Lorenzo_de_Almagro) of [Barcelona](4443.md).

According to official estimates there are 600,000 Argentines worldwide, and according to estimates by the [International Organization for Migration](36893.md) there have been about 806,369 since 2001. The first wave of emigration occurred during the military dictatorship between 1976 and 1983, principally to Spain, the United States, Mexico and Venezuela. During the 1990s, due to the abolition of visas between Argentina and the United States, thousands of Argentines emigrated to North America. The last major wave of emigration occurred during the 2001 crisis, mainly to Europe, especially Spain, although there was also an increase in emigration to neighboring countries, particularly Brazil, Chile and Paraguay.

### Europe

See also: [Argentines in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_in_France) and [Argentines in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_in_Spain)

The rate of Argentine emigration to Europe (especially to [Spain](26667.md) and [Italy](14532.md)[^cite_note-95]) peaked in the late 1970s and early 1980s and is noteworthy.[^cite_note-96] Spain and Italy have the largest Argentine communities in Europe, however, there are also important communities in [France](5843419.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Germany](11867.md).

### Americas

See also: [Argentine Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Americans), [Argentines in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_in_Chile), [Latin American Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Canadians), [Argentine Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Mexicans), [Argentine Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Brazilians), [Argentines in Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentines_in_Uruguay), and [Argentine Venezuelans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Venezuelans)

The most popular immigration destinations in the Americas are: the [United States](3434750.md) and [Brazil](3383.md), and to a lesser degree, mostly to ([Uruguay](31843.md) and [Canada](5042916.md)): [Chile](5489.md), [Paraguay](23420.md) and [Bolivia](3462.md), while other communities settled in [Venezuela](32374.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Colombia](5222.md), [Ecuador](9334.md) and [Costa Rica](5551.md).

### Middle East

See also: [Argentine Jews in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Jews_in_Israel)

[Israel](9282173.md) is home to the largest Argentine diaspora in the Middle East.[^cite_note-97]

### Oceania

See also: [Argentine Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Australians)

In Oceania, [Australia](4689264.md) has the largest Argentine community, followed by [New Zealand](4913064.md).
