**Hakainde Hichilema** (born 4 June 1962), often known by his initials **HH** and the nickname "**Bally**", is a Zambian businessman, farmer, and politician who is the seventh and current [president of Zambia](450150.md) since 24 August 2021.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Since the death of [Guy Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Scott) in 2026, he is the only living person who has been president of Zambia.

Hichilema has led the [United Party for National Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Party_for_National_Development) since 2006, following the death of party founder [Anderson Mazoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Mazoka). Prior to his election, he was a major opponent of [Edgar Lungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Lungu), who was president from 2015 to 2021. On 11 April 2017, Hichilema was arrested and charged with [treason](31292.md), a move that was seen as an illegitimate act by Lungu to silence a political rival.[^cite_note-\:3-2] The arrest and charge were widely condemned, with protests held in Zambia and abroad, demanding Hichilema's release and condemning the increasing authoritarianism of Lungu's regime. Hichilema was released from prison on 16 August 2017, and the charge of treason was dropped.[^cite_note-\:4-3][^cite_note-4] After having contested five previous presidential elections, he won the [2021 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Zambian_general_election) and was re-elected to a second consecutive term in [2026](78707374.md).

## Early life and career

Hichilema was born on 4 June, 1962 in a village in [Monze District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monze_District) in present-day Zambia. He received a [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) to study at the [University of Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zambia) and graduated in 1986 with a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in [Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_administration). He thereafter pursued an [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBA) in [Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance) and [Business Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_management) at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham) in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-5]

He served as the [chief executive officer](52234.md) of [Coopers and Lybrand Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopers_and_Lybrand) from 1994 to 1998, and of [Grant Thornton Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Thornton_International) from 1998 to 2006.[^cite_note-cv-6]

## Political career

Hichilema is a member of the ruling [United Party for National Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Party_for_National_Development), a liberal [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Zambia). Following the death of [Anderson Mazoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Mazoka) in 2006, he was elected as the party's new President. He also served as the leader of the [United Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Democratic_Alliance_\(Zambia\)) (UDA), an alliance of three opposition political parties.

In the [2006 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Zambian_presidential_election), Hichilema was the candidate of the UDA and ran against [incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incumbent) president [Levy Mwanawasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levy_Mwanawasa) of the [Movement for Multiparty Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Multiparty_Democracy) and [Patriotic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Front_\(Zambia\)) candidate [Michael Sata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Sata). He received the endorsement of former President [Kenneth Kaunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Kaunda). The election was held on 28 September 2006 and Hichilema took third place with about 25% of the vote.

Hichilema ran as the UPND candidate in the [2008 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Zambian_presidential_election), which was called following the death of President Levy Mwanawasa.[^cite_note-7] He came 3rd with 19.7% of the vote. In June 2009, Hichilema's party, the UPND, formed a pact with [Michael Sata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Sata)'s Patriotic Front (PF) to contest the [2011 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Zambian_general_election) together. However, indecision on the pact candidate, deep mistrust, and accusations of [tribalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribalism) from both sides resulted in the collapse of the pact in March 2011.[^cite_note-8] Hichilema ultimately came in third place in the 2011 election.

He was one of the two main candidates in the [January 2015 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Zambian_presidential_election), which he lost by a narrow margin of 27,757 votes (1.66%) against the ruling party's candidate, [Edgar Lungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Lungu). Hichilema denounced the election as a [sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_election) and urged his supporters to remain calm.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] He again faced Lungu as the main opposition candidate in the [August 2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Zambian_general_election), and was again narrowly defeated.[^cite_note-11] In April 2017, he was arrested on suspicion of [treason](31292.md) and charged with attempting to overthrow the government.[^cite_note-\:3-2] He was in prison for four months before being given a [nolle prosequi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolle_prosequi).[^cite_note-\:4-3][^cite_note-\:5-12]

On 17 December 2017, Hichilema featured on [ZNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZNBC)'s Sunday Interview, making him the first opposition political leader to be hosted on that program.[^cite_note-13] This was after management at ZNBC had granted a request by the UPND through its spokesman, Charles Kakoma, who had written to ZNBC Director General Richard Mwanza requesting that Hichilema is featured on the Sunday Interview on 17 December.[^cite_note-Mast-14][^cite_note-15]

Hichilema was invited to speak at [The Royal Institute of International Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Institute_of_International_Affairs), commonly known as Chatham House, on 31 October 2017.[^cite_note-16] He returned to Chatham House following his election as President, on 5 November 2021.[^cite_note-17]

### 2017 arrest and treason charge

Hichilema was arrested on 11 April 2017. On the night of 11 April 2017 the [Zambian Police](56690853.md) broke into Hichilema's compound to arrest the country's main opposition leader, ordered by President [Edgar Lungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Lungu)'s government[^cite_note-\:7-18][^cite_note-news24.com-19][^cite_note-\:8-20] and charged with [treason](31292.md) after he was accused of [endangering](3722991.md) the president's life after his [motorcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcade) allegedly refused to give way to the one transporting Lungu,[^cite_note-\:8-20] a case which many viewed as a minor [traffic offence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_violation)[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] and not one that could amount to treason. Hichilema strongly denied the charge, which carries a maximum sentence of [death penalty](5902.md).[^cite_note-\:8-20]

The police used excessive force[^cite_note-23] to enter Hichilema's residence damaging his home and property, beat up all his workers, stole money, jewellery, as well as underwear, shoes, speakers, blankets, carpets and food from the kitchen and defecated on Hichilema's bed.[^cite_note-auto1-24] [Teargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teargas) canisters were thrown inside Hichilema's home[^cite_note-25] gassing Hichilema, his asthmatic wife, and his children, who collapsed several times due to inhaling the gas.[^cite_note-auto1-24]

#### Time in jail

Hichilema said in an interview on [HARDtalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HARDTalk) that during his time in prison he was held in [solitary confinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitary_confinement) for eight days without food, water, light, or visitation, was tortured by having his penis [pepper sprayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepper_sprayed), and accused President Lungu of having tried to kill him.[^cite_note-26] He was the first Zambian opposition political leader to be hosted on HARDtalk and the third Zambian politician featured after former President [Levy Mwanawasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levy_Mwanawasa) and former Vice-President [Guy Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Scott). The arrest was the subject of the episode of [Al Jazeera](30204052.md)'s *The Stream* TV program titled *Is Zambia's Democracy in Danger?,* which aired on 30 May 2017.[^cite_note-27]

Zambia's first president [Kenneth Kaunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Kaunda) was turned away by prison officials when he visited Hichilema in Prison.[^cite_note-auto-28] Former President of [Nigeria](21383.md) [Olusegun Obasanjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olusegun_Obasanjo) visited Hichilema in prison.[^cite_note-29] Secretary General of the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), Baroness [Patricia Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Scotland) visited Hichilema in prison twice.[^cite_note-30] [Mmusi Maimane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mmusi_Maimane), leader of the [Democratic Alliance (South Africa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) was also turned away by [Zambia Police](56690853.md) when he came to Zambia to attend Hichilema's court appearance. He was not allowed to disembark from the plane, had his phone confiscated, and was roughed up.[^cite_note-enca.com-31] This prompted South Africa's Foreign Ministry to summon Zambia's Ambassador to South Africa [Emmanuel Mwamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Mwamba) to explain the actions of the Zambian regime.

#### Condemnation and protests

While Hichilema was in jail, President Lungu imposed a [state of emergency](298608.md),[^cite_note-32] a move critics saw as an effort to tighten his grip on power.[^cite_note-news24.com-19]

Hichilema's arrest was widely condemned. The [United States](3434750.md),[^cite_note-33] the [European Union](9317.md)[^cite_note-34] and the [European Parliament](9581.md)[^cite_note-35] denounced the arrest. The [Africa Liberal Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Liberal_Network) condemned the arrest as an attempt by President Lungu to silence dissent and opposition.[^cite_note-36] The Catholic bishops strongly condemned the arrest and said Zambia had become a [dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship) under President Edgar Lungu.[^cite_note-\:7-18] [Julius Malema](18397339.md), the leader of South Africa's EFF party, accused Zambia's president Edgar Lungu of [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) style repression for detaining Hichilema on treason charges[^cite_note-news24.com-19] and called president Lungu a "coward".[^cite_note-news24.com-19] [Mmusi Maimane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mmusi_Maimane), the leader of South Africa's DA party, who was denied entry into Zambia to visit Hichilema in Jail[^cite_note-enca.com-31] (stopped from exiting the plane at [Lusaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusaka)'s [Kenneth Kaunda International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Kaunda_International_Airport)) strongly denounced the trumped up charges against Hichilema.[^cite_note-37]

Protests broke out in Zambia, South Africa[^cite_note-38] and the [United Kingdom](31717.md)[^cite_note-39] demanding the release of Hichilema and condemning Edgar Lungu's [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) rule and deteriorating human rights in Zambia.

#### Release from prison

Celebrations took place throughout Zambia when Hichilema was released from prison on 16 August 2017, scores of people lined up the roads of Lusaka to have a glimpse of Hichilema as his motorcade left prison.[^cite_note-40] Former UN Secretary General [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan), congratulated the Zambian authorities for dropping the treason charges against Hichilema and releasing him from prison.[^cite_note-41] Thanksgiving prayers were held to celebrate Hichilema's release from jail at the [Cathedral of Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Holy_Cross,_Lusaka) in Lusaka on 29 August 2017 and Hichilema was present at the event which drew large crowds and was aired live on television. The event was originally scheduled to take place the previous week on 24 August but was blocked by heavily armed state police who sealed off the venue. Hichilema became more popular after his release and was awarded the Africa Freedom Award in [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa. He was invited to speak at Chatham House in London and was also invited to speak in South Africa by [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) Members of Parliament.[^cite_note-42] A book about Hichilema's time in a prison called *Hakainde Hichilema's Prison Diary*[^cite_note-43] was released on 29 September 2017 by journalist Fredrick Misebezi.[^cite_note-auto-28] Hichilema endorsed the book and urged the public to read it.[^cite_note-44]

### Police warning and caution in 2020

On 23 December 2020, Hichilema received a warning and caution at the [Zambian Police](56690853.md) Headquarters in [Lusaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusaka) for an alleged offence of "*conspiracy to defraud contrary to section 313 of the Penal Code, Cap 87 of the Laws of Zambia"*, relating to the purchase of a property in 2004.[^cite_note-45] As Hichilema arrived for questioning, police clashed with [UPND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPND) supporters. In an attempt to disperse the crowd, police reportedly shot dead a State Prosecutor and a UPND supporter.[^cite_note-46]

## Presidency (2021–present)



Hichilema with the President of the European Parliament [Roberta Metsola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberta_Metsola) on 23 June 2022

Main article: [Presidency of Hakainde Hichilema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Hakainde_Hichilema)

See also: [2021 Zambian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Zambian_general_election) and [Inauguration of Hakainde Hichilema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inauguration_of_Hakainde_Hichilema)

Hichilema ran for President for the sixth time in the election held on 12 August 2021. The electoral commission chairman [Esau Chulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esau_Chulu) declared that he had won the election in the early hours of 16 August, defeating the incumbent, Edgar Lungu.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:1-47] Following his victory, Hichilema declared his wish to appoint qualified Zambians into his administration. Among his appointments were those of [Sylvia Masebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Masebo), [Situmbeko Musokotwane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situmbeko_Musokotwane), and [Jito Kayumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jito_Kayumba), among others. On 24 August Hichilema was inaugurated as President of Zambia in the [National Heroes Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Heroes_Stadium) in Lusaka.[^cite_note-48]

On 20 December 2024, two people were arrested on suspicion of plotting to [hex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incantation) Hichilema after being hired by a brother of politician [Jay Emmanuel Banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Emmanuel_Banda).[^cite_note-49] They were sentenced to two years' imprisonment on 15 September 2025.[^cite_note-50]

Hichilema was reelected as president in the [2026 Zambian general election](78707374.md) with at least 60% of the vote.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

### Foreign policy

See also: [List of international presidential trips made by Hakainde Hichilema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_presidential_trips_made_by_Hakainde_Hichilema)



Map highlighting countries where Hichilema made [official visits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_presidential_trips_made_by_Hakainde_Hichilema) while president



&#x20;Hichilema meets with [First Minister of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland), [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney), October 2025

In 2021 for the [76th United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-sixth_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly) he made his first international trip to the United States, meeting [World Bank](36752.md) and [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) heads and American Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris). In 2022 Zambia voted to condemn Russia's invasion of Ukraine in the UN. In April that year a US [Office of Security Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Security_Assistance_Organizations) was established in the country. The same month a new Chinese Chamber of Commerce was founded in Lusaka, and on 31 May Hichilema and President [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) had a phone call.[^cite_note-\:6-53] On 21 September 2022, Hichilema addressed the general debate of the [77th session of the United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-seventh_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly).[^cite_note-54]

In May 2022, at the annual [African Mining Indaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Mining_Indaba?action=edit\&redlink=1) held in Cape Town, Hichilema delivered a keynote speech where he "welcomed two landmark investments", by [First Quantum Minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Quantum_Minerals) (FQM) worth \$1.25 billion in expanding the production of [Kansanshi mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansanshi_mine) and extending its life, and \$100 million in investment to operationalize the \$250 million [Enterprise Nickel Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_Nickel_Mine?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-55] That month Zambia and the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) also signed an agreement for developing an electric battery [value chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_chain).[^cite_note-\:6-53]

In 2022 the President Hichilema and Tanzanian President [Samia Suluhu Hassan](38577619.md) agreed to revitalize the [TAZARA Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAZARA_Railway) and upgrade it to [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) through [public-private partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership).[^cite_note-56]

Hichilema refers to himself as the country's '[chief marketing officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_marketing_officer)', and has travelled to the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, and Africa to promote the Zambian economy. In addition to the UAE ([Expo2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2020)) and Belgium ([EU-Africa summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_European_Union%E2%80%93African_Union_Summit)). Hichilema has conducted regional trips to Angola, Botswana, the DRC, Egypt, Eswatini, Ethiopia, Kenya, Lesotho, Malawi, Senegal, South Africa, Tanzania, Namibia and Rwanda. He also participated in the 2022 Commonwealth Summit ([CHOGM 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting)**)** held in Kigali. In October 2021, he led a delegation to [COP26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) and in 2022, Zambia chaired the African Group of Negotiators at [COP27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference).[^cite_note-57]

In October 2024, he fired three constitutional court judges.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

On 4 September 2025, Hichilema joined the Advisory Board of the [Global Center on Adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Center_on_Adaptation).[^cite_note-60]

On 4 June 2026 Hichilema signed the Education (Amendment) Act, 2026, making free education a permanent legal right.[^cite_note-61]

## Personal life

Hichilema is married to [Mutinta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutinta_Hichilema) and has three children.[^cite_note-cv-6] He is a baptized member of the [Seventh-day Adventist Church](28632.md)[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-\:2-68] and on 12 December 2020, he and his wife were invested as [Master Guides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathfinders_\(Seventh-day_Adventist\)) in Lusaka.[^cite_note-\:2-68] Hichilema is a [millionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millionaire) and the second-largest cattle [rancher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancher) in Zambia.[^cite_note-69]

In December 2014, he denied being a [Freemason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemason) and labeled people accusing him as malicious.[^cite_note-70] He also sued Bishop [Edward Chomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Chomba?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md) for [defamation](28661.md) after the former called him a [Satanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satanist) and a Freemason.[^cite_note-71]

Hichilema was named in the [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers), with the leak stating that he was the director of [Bermuda](3460.md)-based company AfNat Resources Ltd from March to August 2006. The company engaged in [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) exploration in Zambia and other African countries. According to the [International Consortium of Investigative Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Consortium_of_Investigative_Journalists), "the company was listed on London's alternative investment market until 2010 when it was purchased by Canadian mining company Axmin for about \$14 million."[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]
