**Jalalabad**,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] previously known as **Tarunshahr**,[^cite_note-\:2-6] and historically known as **Nagarahara** or **Adinapur**,[^cite_note-\:3-7] is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Afghanistan) in eastern [Afghanistan](737.md), serving as the capital of [Nangarhar Province](457263.md). It has an estimated population of 318,733 people.[^cite_note-population-3] [Qari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qari) Bismellah Bilal is the current [mayor](101146.md) of the city.[^cite_note-Municipality_of_Jalalabad-1] Mohammad Ishaq Saeed serves as the current deputy mayor.[^cite_note-8]

Jalalabad is home to [Nangarhar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangarhar_University), which is in the northwestern area of the city. The [Jalalabad Airport](8652455.md) is in the southeastern part of the city. Jalalabad has a number of [industrial parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_parks), [bazaars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazaars), business centers, public parks, [banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_banks_in_Afghanistan), hotels, restaurants, [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mosques_in_Afghanistan), [hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Afghanistan), [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Afghanistan), and places to play [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Afghanistan) or just relax. The city is connected by a road network with [Asadabad](815053.md) to the northeast, [Torkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torkham) to the southeast, and [Kabul](16826.md) to the west. It is about 80 mi (130 km) from the city of Kabul.

Jalalabad is located at the junction of the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) and the [Kunar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River) in a [plateau](538005.md) to the south of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) mountains.[^cite_note-dailystar.com.lb-9] It is a leading center of social and trade activity because of its proximity with the Torkham [border checkpoint](288329.md) and [border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_crossing), 65 km (40 mi) away.[^cite_note-10] Major industries include [poultry farming](12116844.md), processing agricultural products such as dried fruits, [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olives), [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm), [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranges) and [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), and producing household products and various types of snacks. For centuries the city was favored by Afghan kings[^cite_note-11] and it has a cultural significance in [Afghan poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_poetry).[^cite_note-dailystar.com.lb-9] During [Timur Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur_Shah_Durrani)'s reign of the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire), Jalalabad served as the Afghan [winter capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_capital).[^cite_note-WrightC-12]

### Ancient and medieval

Known at the time by names such as [Nagarahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagara_\(ancient_city\)) and Adinapur, Jalalabad was a major center of [Greco-Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Buddhist_art) culture during the late [1st millennium BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_millennium_BCE), focused on sites such as [Ahin Posh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahin_Posh).

The first surviving references to the city are in early 1st millennium CE accounts by visiting [Chinese](372983.md) Buddhist monks. In or about 400 CE, [Faxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faxian) visited "Nagarahara" and worshiped at sacred Buddhist sites, such as the "Cave of the Buddha's Shadow” (佛影窟).[^cite_note-13] In 630 CE, [Xuan Zang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang), visited "Adinapur" and other locations nearby.

The Buddhist era began to end after the region was [conquered by Muslim forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquests_of_Afghanistan) during the late 1st millennium. However, complete conversions to Islam evidently did not occur quickly. In *[Hudud-al-Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudud_ul-'alam)*, written in 982 CE, there is reference to a village near Jalalabad where a local ruler had Muslim, Buddhist and [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) wives.[^cite_note-14]

The region became part of the Muslim [Ghaznavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids) in the 10th century. [Sabuktigin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabuktigin) annexed the land all the way west of the [Neelum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelum_River) in Kashmir. "The Afghans and Khiljies who resided among the mountains having taken the oath of allegiance to Sabuktigin, many of them were enlisted in his army, after which he returned in triumph to [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni)."[^cite_note-Farishta-2-15] The [Ghurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurids) succeeded the Ghaznavids and expanded the Islamic culture further into [Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan). The region around Jalalabad later became part of the [Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalji_dynasty) territory, followed by that of the [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurids).[^cite_note-16]

### Modern



The Bagh-e Shahi palace and gardens, c. 1920.



The Emir's gardens, pictured in the 19th century.

It is said that the original name of Jalalabad was **Adinapur or Nagar(a)hara**.[^cite_note-\:3-7] Jalalabad was named in the last decade of the sixteenth century in honor of [Jalal-uddin Mohammad Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal-uddin_Mohammad_Akbar), ruler of the Mughal Empire at the time. His grandfather [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) had chosen the site for this city which was built by his grandson.[^cite_note-17]

It remained part of the Mughal Empire until around 1738 when [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) and his [Afsharid forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_dynasty) defeated the Mughals. Nader Shah's forces were accompanied by the young [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) and his 4,000-strong Afghan army from southern Afghanistan. In 1747, he founded the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) (Afghan Empire) after re-conquering the area. The Afghan army has long used the city while going back and forth during their military campaigns into Hindustan.

In 1834, [Dost Mohammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dost_Mohammad_Khan) subjugated Jalalabad in his [campaign to Jalalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dost_Mohammad's_Campaign_to_Jalalabad_\(1834\)).[^cite_note-18] The British-Indian forces invaded Jalalabad in 1838, during the [First Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Afghan_War). In the 1842 [Battle of Jellalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jellalabad), [Akbar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Akbar_Khan) besieged the [British troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_troops) on their way to Jalalabad. In 1878, during the [Second Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Afghan_War), the British again invaded and set up camps in Jalalabad but withdrew two years later.

Jalalabad was [besieged by rebel tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jalalabad_\(1928%E2%80%931929\)) in November 1928, marking the start of the [Afghan Civil War of 1928-1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1928%E2%80%931929\)).[^cite_note-\:0-19][^cite_note-\:1-20]

Jalalabad is considered one of the most important cities of the [Pashtun culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_culture). Seraj-ul-Emarat, the residence of Amir [Habibullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habibullah_Khan) and King [Amanullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanullah_Khan) was destroyed in 1929 when [Habibullah Kalakani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habibullah_Kalakani) rose to power; the other sanctuaries however, retain vestiges of the past. The [mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum) of both rulers is enclosed by a garden facing Seraj-ul-Emart. The [Sulaimankhels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaimankhel), a Pashtun family famous for their [scientific research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_research), is from Jalalabad. Other celebrated Pashtun families originate from the villages near Jalalabad too.

By the early 1970s, construction had started on a new [planned community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community) within Jalalabad called Reg-e Shamshad Khan.[^cite_note-21] From 1978 to early 1990s, the city served as a strategic location for the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-backed [Democratic Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) and was temporarily renamed to Tarunshahr, in honour of Daoud Tarun.[^cite_note-\:2-6] In March 1989, two [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen) rebel factions backed by Pakistan and the United States assaulted the city during the [Battle of Jalalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1989%E2%80%931992\)). However, [government forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) managed to drive them out within two months, which was a major setback to the rebel fighters and Pakistan's [ISI](2159021.md).[^cite_note-22] The city was heavily bombarded and hundreds of [civilians](145804.md) were killed. Many buildings, such as schools, hospitals and public buildings were destroyed during the 2-month battle.[^cite_note-23]

On April 19, 1992, after then-[President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Afghanistan) [Najibullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Najibullah) resigned, Jalalabad fell to mujahideen rebel forces under [Yunus Khalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunus_Khalis).[^cite_note-24] On September 12, 1996, the [Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)) took control of the city until they were toppled by the US-backed Afghan forces in late 2001, which resulted in the [Afghan Interim Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Interim_Administration) under [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai) taking control. According to the United States, [al-Qaeda](1921.md) had been building military [training camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darunta_training_camp) in the area.&#x20;

In March 2007, US marines murdered 19 unarmed civilians and wounded 50 near Jalalabad, in an incident compared by [The New York Times](30680.md) to the [Haditha massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haditha_massacre). None of those responsible received any serious punishment.[^cite_note-25] The [Jalalabad Airport](8652455.md) has long served as a [military base](843566.md) for the [NATO forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission). Many [suicide attacks](18835454.md) by [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) [insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgent) have taken place, including in [August 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Indian_consulate_in_Jalalabad), [April 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Jalalabad_suicide_bombing), [January 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Children_Jalalabad_attack), [July 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2018_Jalalabad_suicide_bombing), [September 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2018_Jalalabad_suicide_bombing), [October 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Jalalabad_suicide_bombing) and [August 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalalabad_prison_attack). The groups responsible for the attacks include the Taliban, [Haqqani Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haqqani_Network), al-Qaeda, and [ISIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_of_Iraq_and_the_Levant_%E2%80%93_Khorasan_Province) (Daesh). On August 15, 2021, the Taliban took control of [Kabul](68481047.md) and Jalalabad.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Geography

Further information: [Geography of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Afghanistan)

Jalalabad is a strategic city in eastern Afghanistan. It connects by a road network with [Asadabad](815053.md) to the northeast, [Torkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torkham) to the southeast, and [Kabul](16826.md) to the west. It is at an elevation of 575 m (1,886 ft) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level), next to where the [Kunar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River) merges into the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River). The city is administratively divided into 6 city districts (*[nahias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiyah)*), covering a land area of 122 km<sup>2</sup> (47 sq mi) or 12,796 ha (31,620 acres).[^cite_note-Volume-I-2][^cite_note-Volume-II-28]

View towards the city from the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) banks to the north

Agriculture is the predominant land use at 44%, higher density of dwellings is found in Districts 1–5 and vacant plots are largely clustered in District 6. Districts 1–6 all have a grid network of roads.[^cite_note-Volume-II-28]

### Flora and fauna

Further information: [Fauna of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Afghanistan)

Jalalabad is home to a large number of fruit. Various types of citrus fruits like [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)), [tangerine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerine), [grapefruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapefruit), [lemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon), [lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_\(fruit\)) grow in gardens as well as in orchards.[^cite_note-Michel-29][^cite_note-Sweet_orange_yield_to_exceed_2200MT_this_year:_MoAIL-30] The *narindj* variety of orange is the most common one which has yellow skin and its taste is a combination of orange and grapefruit. The grapefruits grown here have a diameter of eight or nine inches. Per year 1800 tonnes of pomegranates, 334 tonnes of grapes, and 7750 tonnes of mulberries are produced in Jalalabad.[^cite_note-31] The fruits are sold in local markets and transported to [Kabul](16826.md) markets from where they are exported. The second most common crop is local *vatani* variety of sugarcane. It contains 15% sugar by weight.[^cite_note-32] Jalalabad also has one of the largest [date farms in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm_farming_in_Afghanistan).[^cite_note-Nangarhar_Orchard_Expects_Over_20_Tons_of_Dates_as_Local_Production_Rises-33]

### Climate

Jalalabad's climate is [hot desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): BWh), and it is one of the hottest localities in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-34] The city's climate has close resemblance to that of [Arizona](21883824.md) in the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-35] It receives six to eight inches (152 to 203 mm) of rainfall per annum which are limited to winter and the months of spring. Frosts are not common, and during the summer, the temperature can reach a maximum of 120 °F (49 °C).[^cite_note-Michel-29]

The north and southwestern parts of the city which has lower elevation are welcoming places to winds from the north and west cooling the parts in summer months. Jalalabad has the highest relative humidity in summer compared to other Afghan cities. However, the moderate temperatures of winter has led to various people down the history establishing their settlements in the city.[^cite_note-Michel-29] Because of its warm temperature relative to most of Afghanistan, Jalalabad (alongside [Peshawar](66230.md)) was often the "[winter capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_capital)" of various Afghan rulers of the past centuries,[^cite_note-WrightC-12][^cite_note-36] while rich people would relocate to villas in Jalalabad to avoid the freezing temperatures in [Kabul](16826.md).

## Demographics

Further information: [Demographics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Afghanistan)

Jalalabad has an estimated population of 318,733 people.[^cite_note-population-3] In 2015 there were 39,586 [dwelling units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwelling_unit) in the city.[^cite_note-Volume-II-28]

Nearly all residents of Jalalabad are [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), followers of [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam). The city also has some [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Afghanistan) and [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Afghanistan), although the community has dwindled since the 1980s.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

## Economy

Further information: [Economy of Afghanistan](9895.md)

Jalalabad has a number of [industrial parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_parks). There are at least two [steel mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_steel_manufacturers_in_Afghanistan) in the city. Major industries in the city include [poultry farming](12116844.md),[^cite_note-43] processing agricultural products such as [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(fruit\)) and [dried fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dried_fruits), [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) products, [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm), [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranges), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane),[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-Nangarhar_Orchard_Expects_Over_20_Tons_of_Dates_as_Local_Production_Rises-33][^cite_note-Sweet_orange_yield_to_exceed_2200MT_this_year:_MoAIL-30][^cite_note-45] and producing household products and various types of snacks.

### Transportation

Further information: [Transport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan)



[Auto rickshaws](593765.md) in Jalalabad

Jalalabad is connected by a road network with [Asadabad](815053.md) to the northeast, Torkham to the southeast, and Kabul to west. The [Kabul–Jalalabad Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul%E2%80%93Jalalabad_Road) is heavily used day and night, and still remains dangerous due to many accidents and the occasional rock falling from the high mountains. In January 2026 the city banned all [auto rickshaw](593765.md) movements on the main roads in the city's center.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

The [Jalalabad Airport](8652455.md) (also known as Nangarhar Airport) is located roughly 3 mi (4.8 km) southeast from the city's center. It is a [domestic airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_airport) for civilian use.[^cite_note-48] It serves the population of the provinces of Nangarhar, Kunar and Nuristan.

There is a proposal for the establishment of a [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Afghanistan) from [Mazar-i-Sharif](20517.md) in [Balkh Province](457218.md) to Jalalabad and then to Torkham in [Momand Dara District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momand_Dara_District).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

## Sports

Further information: [Sport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Afghanistan)



Nangarhar Football Stadium (during construction in 2011)

[Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_Afghanistan), [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Afghanistan), [futsal](188197.md) and [volleyball](32558.md) are the most popular sports in Afghanistan. Jalalabad is represented in domestic cricket competitions by the Nangarhar province cricket team. National team member Hamid Hasan was born in the province and he currently represents Afghanistan in international cricket. The [Ghazi Amanullah International Cricket Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Amanullah_International_Cricket_Stadium) is the first international standard cricket stadium in Afghanistan. It is located in the [Ghazi Amanullah Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Amanullah_Town), a modern suburb on the southeastern fringe of Jalalabad in Nangarhar Province. Construction on the stadium began in March 2010 when the foundation stone was laid by [Omar Zakhilwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Zakhilwal), the then-[Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Afghanistan\)) and President of the [Afghanistan Cricket Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_Cricket_Board). The project, which was developed on 30 acres of land donated by the developer constructing the Ghazi Amanullah Town, cost up the first phase of construction \$1.8 million. The first phase, which took one year to complete, included the completion of the stadium itself. The remainder of the phases will see the construction of a pavilion, accommodation for players and administrative buildings. The stadium, which has a capacity of 14,000, was completed before the national team and under-19 team left for Canada and the Under-19 Cricket World Cup Qualifier in Ireland respectively. The two sides inaugurated the stadium in a [Twenty20](770690.md) match. It is hoped that the stadium will be able to attract international teams to play Afghanistan, who currently have One Day International status until at least 2013.
