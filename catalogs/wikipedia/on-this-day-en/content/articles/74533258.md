The **ambassador of Mexico to Peru** is the highest ranking diplomatic representative of the [United Mexican States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mexican_States) to the [Republic of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Peru). The ambassador services the Mexican embassy, located at the district of [San Isidro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_District,_Lima), in [Lima](85423.md). In 2025, the embassy was reduced to an interests sections due to Peru breaking diplomatic relations with Mexico.[^cite_note-2]

## Background

Historically, both nations were host to great [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) cultures; the [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs) and [Mayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayas) in Mexico and the [Incas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incas) in Peru.[^cite_note-3] During colonization, both nations were part of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) until the early 19th century. Mexico was part of [Viceroyalty of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain) while Peru was part of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru).

[Diplomatic relations](39326409.md) between Mexico and Peru were established on March 3, 1823 (but informally date back to October 6, 1821).[^cite_note-Secretaria-4] On the same date, the first Peruvian ambassador to Mexico presented his credentials to Emperor [Agustín de Itúrbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agust%C3%ADn_de_It%C3%BArbide).[^cite_note-Historia-5] This was two years after Peru gained its independence from Spain.[^cite_note-History-6] In October 1892, Mexico opened its first consulate in [Lima](85423.md) followed by the opening of an embassy on 14 June 1937.[^cite_note-Embassy-7] Peru inaugurated an embassy on January 17, 1966.[^cite_note-8]

Relations between both countries have historically been continuous and amicable, with few exceptions. In 1932, a letter written by [Víctor Raúl Haya de la Torre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Ra%C3%BAl_Haya_de_la_Torre) (at the time exiled in Mexico) was published in the Peruvian press, having been delivered to Lima via a Mexican diplomatic pouch. The event caused Peru to freeze its relations with Mexico, only reestablishing them on May 23 of the following year with the mediation of [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic).[^cite_note-Historia-5][^cite_note-Letter-9] Following the souring of relations due to Mexico's role in the [2022 political crisis in Peru](72438093.md), relations were not severed despite the designation of Mexican ambassador to Peru, Pablo Monroy Conesa, as a *[persona non grata](521241.md)* as well as the declaration of an ultimatum for him to leave the country.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] During this period, First Lady [Lilia Paredes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilia_Paredes) and her two children were granted diplomatic asylum, staying at the embassy's residence in Coronel Portillo Avenue prior to their departure.[^cite_note-12]

In 2025, relations were severed by Peru after Mexico granted the asylum request of former Prime Minister [Betssy Chávez](72343741.md) on November 3, giving *chargé d'affaires* Karla Tatiana Ornelas Loera an ultimatum to leave the country.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In the meantime, security was tightened at the chancery in Jorge Basadre Avenue, as well as the residence, where Chávez would be staying.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]
