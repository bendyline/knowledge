The **Gaza–Israel barrier** (sometimes called the **Iron Wall**[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]) is an Israeli [border barrier](40601687.md) located on the Israeli side of the **Gaza–Israel border**.[^cite_note-Anne_Barnard-4] Before the [Gaza war](74998519.md), the [Erez Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erez_Crossing), in the north of the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), was the only crossing point for people and goods coming from [Israel](9282173.md) into the Gaza Strip. A second crossing point, the [Kerem Shalom border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerem_Shalom_border_crossing), is used exclusively for goods coming from [Egypt](8087628.md) as Israel did not allow goods to go directly from Egypt into Gaza through the [Egypt–Gaza border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Gaza_border),[^cite_note-agreed_principles_for-5][^cite_note-Greg_Myre-6] except for the [Salah Al Din Gate](10475618.md#Rafah_Land_Port), which opened in 2018.

A fence along the border was first constructed by Israel in 1971[^cite_note-Sara_M._Roy-7] as a security barrier, and has been rebuilt and upgraded since. It was constructed by Israel to control the movement of people as well as goods between the Gaza Strip and Israel, which it could not achieve by normal border crossings.

There is also one crossing along the Egypt–Gaza border, the [Rafah Crossing](10475618.md), which is limited to the crossing of people; as per Israel's demand, any cargo or goods that are to enter Gaza must go through Israel, usually through the Kerem Shalom border crossing.[^cite_note-agreed_principles_for-5][^cite_note-Greg_Myre-6]

## Political background

In 1993, Israel and the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) signed [the first of the Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_I_Accord) establishing the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) with limited administrative control of the [West Bank](33209.md) and [Gaza Strip](12047.md). Pursuant to the Accords, Israel continues to maintain control of the Gaza Strip's [airspace](353557.md), land borders (with the exception of Gaza's border with Egypt, abandoned by Israel in 2005), and [territorial waters](496983.md).

In 2005, [Israel unilaterally withdrew its troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel's_unilateral_disengagement_plan) from the Gaza Strip, along with thousands of Israeli settlers. Israel thus claims to have ended the occupation. However, this claim has been challenged on the basis that Israel continues to exercise control over Gaza's territorial waters and airspace, despite Gaza not being part of Israel and Gazans not having [Israeli passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_passport).[^cite_note-8]

The Gaza-Israel border straddles one of the starkest economic contrasts in the world (the [Korean Demilitarized Zone](328874.md) is the other): on the Israeli side GDP per capita is \$55,000 and population density is low, while on the Palestinian side, the GDP per capita is \$1,250 and population density is among the highest in the world.[^cite_note-9]

Commentators point out that fortifications like the *Iron Wall* will result in a more diabolical state of affairs not for the Gazans but for the Israeli society itself as it may never look past such metaphorical [stonewalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewalling) to reach a socio-political compromise with the Palestinians.[^cite_note-10]

## Barrier structure



Section of the border barrier showing restrictions, September 2023.

Israel started construction of the first 60 kilometers (37 mi) long barrier along its border with the Gaza Strip in 1994. In the 1994 [Interim Agreement on the West Bank and the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Agreement_on_the_West_Bank_and_the_Gaza_Strip), it was agreed that "the security fence erected by Israel around the Gaza Strip shall remain in place and that the line demarcated by the fence, as shown on the map, shall be authoritative only for the purpose of the Agreement"[^cite_note-11] (i.e. the barrier does not necessarily constitute the border). The initial barrier was completed in 1996.

Before the 2005 disengagement Israeli military maintained a one-kilometer buffer zone within Gaza along the border wall which prevented the militants to approach the border, sometimes with gunfire. After the IDF withdrawal the border became easily reachable by the Palestinians.[^cite_note-12] Therefore, Israel launched the construction of the enhanced security system along the Gaza border, estimated to cost \$220 million and to be completed in mid-2006.[^cite_note-wotri-13]

It includes a 7-meter wall with sensors, remote-control machine guns and barbed wire in the three areas where the border runs adjacent to Israeli settlements.[^cite_note-wotri-13] The land taken from the corresponding kibbutzim was compensated for, with some controversies.[^cite_note-14]

Overall, the first barrier is a barbed-wire fence without sensors. The second barrier codenamed Hoovers A is 20 meters off and consist of a road and a fence with sensors. These existed before 2005. A new element is a 70-150 meter wide buffer zone codenamed Hoovers B with [motion sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_sensor) in the ground and surrounded by a new sensor-equipped fence with watchtowers every 2 kilometers, equipped with remote-control machine guns instead of soldiers, which could be targets of Palestinian snipers.[^cite_note-wotri-13]

The barrier is patrolled both from the air and on the ground.[^cite_note-wotri-13]

## Response from Gaza

The barrier has met with opposition and protests from some Palestinians in Gaza.[^cite_note-15]

The barrier was largely torn down by Palestinians at the beginning of the [Al-Aqsa Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Intifada) in September 2000, followed by many terror attacks.[^cite_note-Doron_Almog-16] The barrier was rebuilt between December 2000 and June 2001. A one-kilometer buffer zone was added, in addition to new high technology observation posts. Soldiers were also given new rules of engagement,[^cite_note-Doron_Almog-16] which, according to *[Ha'aretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ha'aretz)*, allow soldiers to fire at anyone seen crawling there at night illegally into Israeli territory.[^cite_note-Amos_Harel-17] Palestinians attempting to cross the barrier into Israel by stealth have been shot and killed.[^cite_note-CTV1-18]

The barrier has been effective in preventing [terrorists](30636.md) and [suicide bombers](18835454.md) from entering Israel from Gaza. Since 1996, virtually all suicide bombers trying to leave Gaza have detonated their charges at the barrier's crossing points and were stopped while trying to cross the barrier elsewhere.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] On 14 March 2004, a suicide bomber originating from within the Gaza Strip successfully carried out an attack in [Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdod), Israel.[^cite_note-21]

The barrier's effectiveness prompted a shift in the tactics of Palestinian militants who commenced [firing Qassam rockets and mortars over the barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel).[^cite_note-Doron_Almog-16][^cite_note-Bradley_Burston-22]



A [Merkava Mark IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkava) tank patrols the Gaza border (February 2012)



Israeli soldiers crossing the fence surrounding the Gaza Strip, [Gaza War](74998519.md), May 2024

On 27 December 2008, Israel launched the [2008 Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%9309\)), consisting of airstrikes and ground incursions against targets in the Gaza Strip, with the stated aim of stopping the rocket fire[^cite_note-23] from and arms smuggling into the territory.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-guardian.co.uk-25] The war ended on 18 January 2009, when both sides ceased military action.[^cite_note-xinhua_one_week-26][^cite_note-cbc.ca_one_week-27] Israel completed its withdrawal on 21 January,[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] and thousands of rockets and mortars have been fired from the Gaza Strip since.

### Support for a similar Egypt–Gaza barrier

Main article: [Egypt–Gaza barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Gaza_barrier)

Palestinian Authority President [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md) declared support for the [Egypt–Gaza barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Gaza_barrier), adding: "It is the Egyptians’ sovereign right in their own country. Legitimate supplies should be brought through the legal crossings",[^cite_note-30] although he made no such comment towards Israel's sovereign rights. The United States announced its support for the Egypt-Gaza barrier saying it would prevent weapons smuggling.[^cite_note-31] Cairo's main [Al-Azhar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Azhar_University) officially backed the government's decision for an Egypt-Gaza barrier saying that it was the "state's right to build along its walls facilities and obstacles that will enhance its security."[^cite_note-32]

### Tunnels under the barrier

Main article: [Palestinian tunnel warfare in the Gaza Strip](43413500.md)



[IDF Caterpillar D9R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDF_Caterpillar_D9R) armored bulldozer working in the Palestinian side of the Israel-Gaza barrier in order to expose explosive devices

Because of the effectiveness of the barrier in stopping infiltration of Israel by militants, they adopted a strategy of digging tunnels under the barrier. On 25 June 2006, Palestinians used an 800-metre tunnel dug over a period of months to infiltrate Israel. They attacked a patrolling Israeli armored unit, killed two Israeli soldiers, and captured another one, [Gilad Shalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilad_Shalit).[^cite_note-CBSNews-33]

Between January and October 2013, three other tunnels were identified – two of which were packed with explosives.[^cite_note-34] The discovery of similarly constructed tunnels in other parts of the world have led to updated [threat assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threat_assessment) estimates.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

During the [2014 Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Gaza_war), Israel encountered Hamas militants who popped out of tunnels into Israel and attacked soldiers along the border. After the war, Israel located and destroyed 32 tunnels. In 2018, Israel destroyed three new tunnels.[^cite_note-under-37]

### Underground anti-tunnel barrier

In response to the large number of tunnels being dug, which could be used for infiltration by militants, in mid-2017, Israel began construction of an underground border wall several meters in depth along the border.[^cite_note-under-37] This **anti-tunnel barrier along the Gaza–Israel border** (sometimes referred to as the **smart wall on the Israel–Gaza border**)[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] is an underground [slurry wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry_wall) constructed by [Israel](9282173.md) along the entire 40-kilometer (25 mi) length of the Gaza–Israel border to prevent infiltration into Israel by digging [tunnels](43413500.md) under the Gaza–Israel barrier. The project includes [excavation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digging) to classified depths, and the construction of thick [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete) [walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall) combined with sensors and [alarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alarm) devices.[^cite_note-41]

In mid-2017, Israel began construction of the underground wall several metres in depth.[^cite_note-42] The barrier is equipped with sensors that can detect tunnel construction.[^cite_note-43]

The underground anti-tunnel barrier, and 81% of the barrier above the ground, was completed in March 2021.[^cite_note-44] The whole project was completed in December 2021.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The project had been estimated to cost 3 billion [shekels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_new_shekel) (\$833 million)[^cite_note-47] to 3.5 billion shekels (\$1.11 billion).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

The wall is located entirely on Israeli land.[^cite_note-50] The anti-tunnel barrier was completed in March 2021.[^cite_note-51]

#### Detections

In October 2020, sensors in the underground structure identified a Hamas tunnel. An Israeli military official called the tunnel "The most significant tunnel we have seen to date, both in terms of depth and infrastructure".[^cite_note-52]

## Crossing points

From the Palestinian perspective, the crossings are crucial to the economy of the Gaza Strip and to the daily needs of the population.[^cite_note-Greg_Myre-6] Chief [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) negotiator [Saeb Erekat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeb_Erekat) analyzed the closures of the crossings and said they have "proven to be counter-productive".[^cite_note-53]

### Israel–Gaza

As of 2023, there were two open border crossings with Israel, with an additional option via an Israel–Egypt crossing. During the war ensuing from the [October 7 Hamas attack](74998317.md), two more emergency gates were eventually opened for aid convoys.

#### Kerem Shalom Crossing

The [Kerem Shalom Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerem_Shalom_Crossing) is the main cargo crossing, used by trucks carrying goods from Israel or Egypt to the Gaza Strip. Managed by the [Israel Airports Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Airports_Authority).[^cite_note-54]

#### Erez or Beit Hanoun Crossing

Main article: [Erez Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erez_Crossing)



Erez Crossing

The Erez or Beit Hanoun Crossing is the only one open for people. A pedestrian and cargo crossing into Israel, it is located in northern Gaza. The crossing is currently restricted to Arab residents under the jurisdiction of the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) and to [Egyptian](8087628.md) nationals or international aid officials only, and is closed to tourists. Palestinians who have a permit to work in Israel or those with permits allowing them to receive free medical treatment or to visit immediate family who are in prisons may use this crossing when it is open for pedestrian travel.[^cite_note-PCHR-55]

Though 5,000 Palestinians are permitted to use the Erez Crossing to go to their places of work inside Israel, the crossing was frequently closed by the Israeli authorities, impeding their ability to get to work.[^cite_note-Greg_Myre-6] Additionally, the permits issued have not always been honoured by soldiers, who in some cases confiscated them at the crossing.[^cite_note-PCHR-55]

On 7 October 2023, Erez Crossing was attacked and destroyed by Hamas. It remained closed until 1 May 2024, when it was reopened for aid transports heading into Gaza.[^cite_note-56]

#### Gate 96 emergency crossing

On 12 March 2024, as a "pilot project", the 96th gate in the security fence (near Kibbutz [Be'eri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'eri)) was used by a convoy of six trucks taking humanitarian aid to northern Gaza under IDF protection.[^cite_note-57] Gate 96 is slowly becoming a fixture during the war allowing aid to reach Gaza City directly, be it on a minor scale.[^cite_note-58]

#### "Northern Crossing" emergency gate

On 12 April 2024, a newly built wartime emergency crossing for humanitarian aid was opened near Kibbutz [Zikim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zikim).[^cite_note-ZKM-59] The content of the trucks is checked by the IDF at the Kerem Shalom border crossing facility.[^cite_note-ZKM-59]
