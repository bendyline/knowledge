The **Minister of Foreign Affairs of the People's Republic of China** is the head of the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](7453497.md) of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) and one of the country's top and most important [State Council](44498.md) posts. Officially, the minister is nominated by the [premier of the State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_China), who is then approved by the [National People's Congress](38878740.md) or its [Standing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress) and appointed by the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_China).[^cite_note-\:0222-1]

The minister usually is also a member of the [Central Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) of the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) and a [state councillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_councillor). The Minister is the second-highest ranking diplomat in China after the director of the [Office of the Central Foreign Affairs Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Central_Foreign_Affairs_Commission).[^cite_note-2] The current minister is [Wang Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Yi_\(politician\)), who concurrently serves as the director of the Office of the Central Foreign Affairs Commission.

## History

The post was initially established after the [proclamation of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) on 1 October 1949 as the minister of foreign affairs of the Central People's Government, with [Zhou Enlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Enlai) being appointed as both the minister and the [premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_China).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartin202157-4]
