**US-Bangla Airlines** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): ইউএস বাংলা এয়ারলাইন্স) is the second [largest airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_airlines_in_the_world) in [Bangladesh](3454.md) by fleet size.[^cite_note-USB_330-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-10fleet-4] It is headquartered in [Dhaka](56656.md) and based at [Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahjalal_International_Airport) under the umbrella companies of **US-Bangla Group**.[^cite_note-About_us-5][^cite_note-Daily_Star-6]

## History

### Foundation and early years

US-Bangla Airlines commenced operations with domestic flights on 17 July 2014.[^cite_note-Daily_Star-6][^cite_note-About_us-5] Initially, the airline launched two domestic destinations, [Chittagong](380451.md) and [Jessore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessore_\(city\)), from its hub in Dhaka.[^cite_note-Daily_Star-6] Flights from Dhaka to [Cox's Bazar](2271204.md) were launched in August and to [Saidpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saidpur_City) in October.[^cite_note-Independent-7]

In July 2016, the airline announced plans to phase in its first three [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-800) aircraft in September of the same year, and to subsequently launch international routes, for example to [Singapore](27318.md) and [Dubai](211583.md).[^cite_note-monitor-8] On 29 April 2019, the airline started flights to [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou_Airport), becoming the first Bangladeshi airline to operate flights to China.[^cite_note-9] The airline started flights from Dhaka to [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_International_Airport) (via Chittagong) on 31 March 2019 also becoming first Bangladeshi airline carrier to operate flights to Southern India.[^cite_note-10]

In February 2019, US-Bangla Airlines announced an order for four [ATR 72-600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72-600) aircraft, to be used on domestic flights. On 22 March 2019, the first aircraft was delivered from [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse%E2%80%93Blagnac_Airport) to [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Airport) via [El Dabaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Dabaa) and [Muscat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_International_Airport).

By September 2019, US-Bangla Airlines had become the second largest airline in terms of fleet size in Bangladesh, after the national flag carrier [Biman Bangladesh Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biman_Bangladesh_Airlines).[^cite_note-10fleet-4]

### Development since 2020

The airline was supposed to start direct flights from [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet) to other Bangladeshi cities such as [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Airport), [Cox's Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Airport) and [Jessore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessore_Airport), as well as a flight from Chittagong to Jessore, in order to make the city of [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet) more accessible, from mid-2020.[^cite_note-11] It also announced plans to add four more international routes to [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md), [Colombo](56636.md) and [Malé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%A9) in the beginning of 2021.[^cite_note-12] However, these plans were postponed as a result of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).

In November 2021, a senior authority expressed plans for expansion of both destinations and fleet, with the carrier willing to add seven new international routes, including [Jeddah](277718.md), [Dammam](479214.md), [Medina](36636.md), [Riyadh](51554.md), [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah), [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Colombo](56636.md), [Sydney](27862.md), [New Delhi](51585.md) and [Hyderabad](37534.md) from Dhaka. It was also considering introducing a Dhaka–[New York](645042.md) service in the future, if Bangladesh earned Civil Aviation Category–I status. Subsequently, several European destinations — including [London](17867.md), [Paris](22989.md), [Amsterdam](844.md) and [Rome](25458.md) — were planned to be introduced by 2024 with the newly acquired [Airbus A330-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-\:1-14]

On 9 February 2024, the company received its first wide-body aircraft, an [Airbus A330-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330) leased from [Hi Fly's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi_Fly_\(airline\)) Maltese division. The aircraft has 436 seats in an [all-Economy class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_class) configuration, and another is planned to be acquired. These aircraft are intended to be used on routes to Middle Eastern cities, including those in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md) and [Bahrain](18933277.md), as well as for [Hajj](23487999.md) flights.[^cite_note-USB_330-2][^cite_note-15]

## Corporate affairs



The branch office in [Uttara, Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttara,_Dhaka)

US-Bangla Airlines' headquarters are in the [Baridhara Diplomatic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baridhara_Diplomatic_Zone) in [Dhaka](56656.md). Though the airline is the flagship company of the parent US-Bangla Group, the group owns numerous businesses in other industries such as real estate, education, media, electronics, and consumer foods.

Though US-Bangla Airlines is the flagship company of the conglomerate, it is arguably one of the fastest rising conglomerates in Bangladesh. It rebranded its current name from US-Bangla Assets in 2009. Its other notable business is real estate company US-Bangla asset which is developing Purbachal American City, one of the largest commercial and residential real estate projects in Bangladesh. The group now owns educational businesses such as [Green University of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_University_of_Bangladesh), one of the earliest private universities of Bangladesh,[^cite_note-16] and US-Bangla Medical College and Hospital. The group also owns a leather company in Bangladesh, US-Bangla Leather, which has not yet started its production. It also owns US-Bangla Hi-tech Industries which is also yet to set up. It has its footing in media businesses through US-Bangla Media and Communications.[^cite_note-daily-sun.com-17] It also owns food and grocery delivery system [Foodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foodi) and e-commerce platform Cartup.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Accidents and incidents

- On 12 March 2018, [US-Bangla Airlines Flight 211](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US-Bangla_Airlines_Flight_211), a Bombardier Dash 8 Q400-402 with registration S2-AGU, crashed while landing at [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport), killing 51 of the 71 people on board.

  [^cite_note-33]

  [^cite_note-34]

  &#x20;The final accident report, published on 27 January 2019, concluded "the probable cause of the accident is due to disorientation and a complete loss of situational awareness in the part of a crewmember".

  [^cite_note-35]

  [^cite_note-36]

* On 26 September 2018, US-Bangla Airlines Flight 141, a [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-800) with registration S2-AJA, took off from Dhaka airport at 11\:30 am with 164 passengers and seven crew members on board. It was scheduled to land at the Cox's Bazar Airport at 12\:30 pm. On approach to Cox's Bazar, the nose-wheel mechanism jammed and prevented the wheel hatch from opening. The pilot put the Boeing 737-800 into a holding pattern over Chittagong to burn off fuel so it would be lighter and the risk of fire on impact would be reduced. The plane eventually touched down at 1\:18 pm. Some passengers received minor injuries as they attempted to disembark the aircraft hurriedly. This incident was similar to [JetBlue Flight 292](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue_Flight_292), which experienced a similar incident in the US in 2005.
