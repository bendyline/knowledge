The **Airbus A321neo** is a [narrow-body single-aisle airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-body_aircraft) created by [Airbus](26220236.md). The A321neo (*neo* being an acronym for "new engine option") was developed from the [Airbus A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321) and is part of the [Airbus A320neo family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo_family), alongside the [A319neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A319neo) and [A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo_family#A320neo), being the longest member of the family.[^cite_note-3] It typically seats 180 to 220 passengers in a two-class configuration, with up to 244 passengers in a high-density arrangement.[^cite_note-4]

The A321neo was announced by Airbus in December 2010, as an improvement and replacement to the A321ceo.[^cite_note-5] Fitted with new engines and [sharklets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingtip_device) as standard, the A321neo has the longest fuselage of any Airbus [narrow-body airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-body_airliner) in commercial use. Available with either [CFM International LEAP-1A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International_LEAP-1A) or [Pratt & Whitney PW1100G-JM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_PW1000G) engines, Airbus advertises a 20% increase in [fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency) per passenger, with 500 nautical miles (930 km; 580 mi) more range, or 2 tonnes (4,400 lb) more of payload. Boeing introduced a new generation of their competing narrowbody family, the [737 MAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/737_MAX), nine days before the introduction of the A321neo.[^cite_note-6]

The A321neo has the same overall length as the A321ceo, with an increased fuel efficiency and performance rating. The A321neo has a range of 3,995 nautical miles (7,399 km), with an [MTOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTOW) (maximum take-off weight) of 97 tonnes (214,000 lb), and its engine has 109 to 156 kN (24,500 to 35,000 lb) of [thrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust).

The A321neo began production in 2016, with final assembly taking place in [Hamburg, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Hamburg-Finkenwerder), as well as in [Toulouse, France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Toulouse); [Mobile, United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Mobile), and [Tianjin, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_China).[^cite_note-7] It entered service with [Virgin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_America) on 31 May 2017, taking its first commercial flight.[^cite_note-8] As of June 2025, a total of 7,064 A321neo aircraft had been ordered by 88 disclosed customers, of which 1,752 aircraft had been delivered.[^cite_note-Airbus_O_D-2]

## Development

The A321neo's development was announced by Airbus in 2010, 16 years after the introduction of the original [A321ceo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A321ceo).[^cite_note-9]



Airbus A321neo prototype

The first flight of the Airbus A321neo took place in Hamburg. The prototype, registered D-AVXB and equipped with CFM International LEAP-1A engines, was flown by test pilots Martin Scheuermann and Bernardo Saez Benito Hernandez. The flight lasted 29 minutes, and performed various tests during that time. [AerCap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AerCap) was the first customer to order the aircraft on 27 April 2011,[^cite_note-10] with [IndiGo](2085799.md) being the first [airline](1942.md) to order the aircraft, on 22 June 2011, ordering 304 of the type. The first A321neo entered commercial service with [Virgin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_America) in May 2017, who merged with [Alaska Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Airlines) in 2018; the latter also acquired all of Virgin's aircraft.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

It received its type certification with Pratt & Whitney engines on 15 December 2016[^cite_note-\:1-13], and simultaneous certification from the [European Union Aviation Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Aviation_Safety_Agency) (EASA) and [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) for the CFM LEAP-powered variant on 1 March 2017. The first A321neo, leased by [GECAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GECAS), was delivered in Hamburg to Virgin America, configured with 185 seats and LEAP engines, and entered service on 31 May 2017.[^cite_note-14]

### Delivery delays



[Hawaiian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Airlines)' A321neos were delivered late.

As Pratt & Whitney encountered early reliability issues with the PW1100G, retrofitting fixes affected the deliveries.[^cite_note-AvWeek9Aug2017-15] [Cebu Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_Pacific) was due to add its first three A321neos to its 40 A320ceos by the end of 2017 but agreed to postpone them; it was to receive seven A321ceos in 2018, starting in March, to upgauge A320 routes from the slot-constrained [Manila Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Airport) and redeploy some of its international [A330s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A330) to shorter-haul routes.[^cite_note-AvWeek9Aug2017-15] [Air New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_New_Zealand) has at least seven A321neos in its 13 A320-family orders, increasing seating capacity by 27% over A320ceos currently used on short-haul international routes, mainly to [Australia](4689264.md); the neos were delayed until July 2018 for the A320neos and September 2018 for the A321neos with a new, higher density and some A320ceo leases to be extended for the interim.[^cite_note-AvWeek9Aug2017-15]

Hawaiian's first two A321neos were planned to have been delivered in 2017 before its upcoming winter peak season but were postponed to early 2018, a "frustrating" and "irritating" delay, with another nine in 2018, mostly in the first half.[^cite_note-AvWeek9Aug2017-15] They are intended to open up thinner routes to the U.S. mainland not viable with its [widebodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-body_aircraft), such as [Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_IAP) to [Maui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahului_Airport), or better matched and allowing two routes to be expanded to daily service instead of seasonal, bypassing its [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_K._Inouye_International_Airport) hub for half of the A321neo fleet.[^cite_note-AvWeek9Aug2017-15]

Well suited for 2,100–2,300 nmi (3,900–4,300 km; 2,400–2,600 mi) routes to the [US West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_West_Coast), Hawaiian's 189-seat A321neos are more efficient than the competing narrow-body aircraft and even have slightly lower per-seat costs than its 294-seat [A330-200s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A330-200).[^cite_note-16]

## Design



Sharklets on a [Turkish Airlines](167925.md) A321neo

The A321neo is a narrow-body aircraft with a retractable tricycle [landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear), powered by two wing pylon-mounted [turbofan engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan_engines). It is a [low-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-wing) [cantilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilever) [monoplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoplane) with a conventional tail unit having a single vertical stabiliser and rudder. Changes from the A321ceo includes a new engine[^cite_note-17] and extended fuselage fuel tanks.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-18].

### Aircraft Cabin Flex (ACF)

Early A321neo aircraft retained the exit configuration of A321ceo with four pairs of doors, two forward of the wing and two aft.

In 2015, the Aircraft Cabin Flex (ACF)[^cite_note-19] layout became available as an option.[^cite_note-20] The ACF layout replaces the second pair of doors ahead of the wing (L2/R2) with two pairs of Type III+ [overwing exits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwing_exit) with dual lane slides designed to accommodate higher passenger flow and moves the third pair of doors (L3/R3), located aft of the wing, further aft.[^cite_note-RGN-21]

The ACF layout gives operators flexibility to activate or deactivate exits based on their selected seating density. For lower-density layouts, the L3/R3 doors or one pair of overwing exits can be replaced with plugs,[^cite_note-RGN-21] which are lighter and do not require door mechanisms or associated maintenance.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

In higher-density configurations, maximum seating capacity increases from 220 to a theoretical exit limit of 250 passengers, although such a configuration has not been certified. Certification limits vary by regulator. The [European Union Aviation Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Aviation_Safety_Agency) (EASA) permits up to 244 passengers when using larger Type C\* doors at both ends with dual lane slides, removing bulkhead walls, and relocating flight attendant jump seats to be deployed in front of the lavatory doors.[^cite_note-AvWeek31jan2018-24] The [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA), by contrast, does not recognize these enhanced Type C\* and Type III+ exit types and classifies them as standard Type C and Type III exits, limiting capacity to 200 passengers under existing rules. Airbus has sought similar recognition from the FAA to allow higher seating capacities.[^cite_note-25]

When equipped to carry 240 passengers, fuel efficiency per seat is improved by approximately 6%. Combined with new engines and sharklets, Airbus projected a fuel burn reduction exceeding 20%.[^cite_note-26] The modifications add about 100 kg (220 lb) to the aircraft's weight.[^cite_note-a321LR-27]

The first A321neo ACF was in final assembly in Hamburg in October 2017 and made its first flight on 31 January 2018.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The first aircraft with this configuration was delivered to [Turkish Airlines](167925.md) in July 2018[^cite_note-30], and ACF became the standard A321neo layout from 2020.[^cite_note-31]

## Variants

The A321neo is available in multiple variants differentiated by fuel capacity. The A321neo can be configured with up to three optional auxiliary centre tanks (ACTs), including one in the forward cargo hold and up to two in the aft cargo hold, occupying space otherwise available for cargo. Aircraft equipped with three ACTs are designated A321LR (Long Range)[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-32].

The A321XLR includes a rear centre tank (RCT) as standard and can be fitted with an additional ACT in the forward cargo hold, also reducing available cargo space.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-33]

### A321neo



[Virgin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_America) received the first A321neo and put it into service in May 2017.

The base A321neo has the same length as the original A321ceo but includes [structural strengthening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_engineering#Aerospace_structures) in the [landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear) and [wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing), increased [wing loading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_loading) and other minor modifications in order to increase Maximum Takeoff Weight ([MTOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTOW)). Its first customer was [ILFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILFC).[^cite_note-35]

The Airbus A321neo prototype, D-AVXB, first flew on 9 February 2016.[^cite_note-36] It suffered a [tailstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailstrike) three days later and was flown to Toulouse for repairs, delaying the certification programme for several weeks.[^cite_note-37]



Economy class cabin on an [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines) Airbus A321neo featuring full-LED [ambient lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_lighting)

The A321neo received its type certification with Pratt & Whitney engines on 15 December 2016,[^cite_note-38] and simultaneous EASA and FAA certification for the CFM LEAP-powered variant on 1 March 2017.[^cite_note-39] The first A321neo, leased by [GECAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GECAS), was delivered in Hamburg to Virgin America, configured with 184 seats and LEAP engines, and entered service in May 2017.[^cite_note-40]

The neo empty weight is 1.8 t (4,000 lb) greater than the ceo, due to its new engines and associated airframe modifications: engine pylons, wing structure and bleed and oil systems were adapted.[^cite_note-AvWeek26May2017-41] At the same maximum weight, it reaches FL310 30 nmi (35 mi; 56 km) and 4 minutes earlier than the ceo.[^cite_note-AvWeek26May2017-41]

At FL330 (10,000 m or 33,000 ft), [ISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Standard_Atmosphere) −2 °C (28 °F) and 67 t (148,000 lb), it burns 2,200 kilograms per hour (4,850 lb/h) at Mach 0.76 (819 km/h; 509 mph; 442 kn) long-range cruise or 2,440 kg/h (5,400 lb/h) at Mach 0.80 (862 km/h; 536 mph; 465 kn) high-speed cruise.[^cite_note-Flight26May2017-42] To offer similar takeoff performance, pitch response to stick input is a rate-command to hit the 3° per second rotation rate to capture the right pitch attitude and there is an "electronic tail bumper" preventing a tailstrike if the stick is less than three-quarters of the way aft; additional thrust, slower rotation and lift-off speeds require more rudder authority and its maximum deflection went from 25° to 30°.[^cite_note-Flight26May2017-42]

By January 2018, the A321neo had received 1,920 orders, exceeding orders for the A321ceo.[^cite_note-Flight1feb2018-43] By this time, the A321neo accounted for 32% of all A320neo family orders, whereas the original A321 represented just 22% of A320ceo family orders.[^cite_note-Flight1feb2018-43] By July 2022, the A321neo represented over 53% of all A320neo family orders.[^cite_note-44]

In 2018, the A321neo list price was US\$129.5 million.[^cite_note-45]

### A321LR



The A321LR was introduced by [Arkia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkia) on 13 November 2018.

In October 2014, Airbus started marketing a 164-seat, 97 t (214,000 lb) MTOW variant with up to three optional removable auxiliary [fuel tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_fuel_tanks) (ACT) called the A321neoLR (Long Range) with 100 nmi (190 km; 120 mi) more operational range than a [Boeing 757](151995.md)-200 configured with 169 seats, 27% lower trip costs and 24% lower per seat costs; it was scheduled for introduction in the second half of 2018, two years after the A321neo.[^cite_note-46]

Airbus launched the A321LR on 13 January 2015 with [Air Lease Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Lease_Corporation) as the launch customer, hoping to sell 1,000 examples of the variant.[^cite_note-47] The initial layout of 164 seats (20 in business, 30 in [premium economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_economy) and 114 in economy) was replaced by a two-class 206-seat configuration (16 in business and 190 in economy).[^cite_note-a321LR-27] Range is 4,000 nmi (7,400 km; 4,600 mi), 500 nmi (930 km; 580 mi) greater than the regular 93.5t MTOW A321neo,[^cite_note-a321LR-27] making it the first version of the A320 family to have true [transatlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_flight) capability, thus replacing the Boeing 757 in the middle of the market.[^cite_note-48] The A321LR has the Cabin Flex layout and was to be first delivered in Q4 2018.[^cite_note-49]



The A321LR prototype.

Certification was aimed for the second quarter of 2018, with a programme including tests with one, two, three, or no additional centre tanks and a transatlantic flight on 13 February.[^cite_note-50] Test flights included a LEAP-powered, long range 4,100 nmi (7,600 km; 4,700 mi) flight by [great circle distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_circle_distance), flown in near 11 hours and the equivalent of 162 passengers over 4,700 nmi (8,700 km; 5,400 mi) including headwinds, with 5 crew and 11 technicians.[^cite_note-51] Airbus announced its joint FAA/EASA certification on 2 October 2018, including [ETOPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETOPS) up to 180 minutes, allowing any transatlantic route.[^cite_note-52]

As original launch operator [Primera Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Air) ceased operations, the first was delivered to Israeli carrier [Arkia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkia)\[^cite_note-[www.airbus.com-2021-53](http://www.airbus.com-2021-53/)] in November 2018, while 120 orders have been secured from about 12 operators: Norwegian, [TAP Air Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAP_Air_Portugal), [Air Transat](348076.md), [Aer Lingus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aer_Lingus), [Air Astana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Astana), [Air Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Arabia) and [Azores Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores_Airlines) will receive theirs from 2019, and Jetstar and Peach in 2020.[^cite_note-54]

On 13 November 2018, Arkia received the first A321LR,\[^cite_note-[www.airbus.com-2021-53](http://www.airbus.com-2021-53/)] featuring 220 seats in a single-class and to be deployed to [London](17867.md), Paris, Barcelona for up to 5-hour sectors, or to Zanzibar and the Seychelles, saying it is the first narrow-body more efficient than the [757-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/757-300) it operates.[^cite_note-55]

In April 2019, JetBlue announced its intention to use the A321LR on routes to London from [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_International_Airport) and [New York-JFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York-JFK); the airline has converted 13 of its orders for the A321neo to the A321LR to serve these routes. The airline started its service from JFK to [London Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow) on 11 August 2021, and to [Gatwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick) on 29 September.

On 13 April 2019, the UAE branch of [Air Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Arabia) received its first of the six A321LRs (WV072) with 97 t (214,000 lb) MTOW, these aircraft are expected to be used on long-haul routes departing from [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_International_Airport) to [Nairobi](142547.md), [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvarnabhumi_Airport), [Phuket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuket_International_Airport), [Milan Bergamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Bergamo) and [Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur_International_Airport), the longest being SHJ-KUL with over 7 hours of air time when returning to the UAE.[^cite_note-56]



VH-OFE, first A321LR of [Jetstar Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetstar_Airways)

On 24 July 2022, [Jetstar Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetstar_Airways) received its first A321LR,[^cite_note-57] the aircraft is fitted with one Auxiliary Fuel Tank (ACT).[^cite_note-58]



B-324K, China’s first A321LR

On 24 October 2022, [Sichuan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Airlines) received its first A321LR, a total of six have been ordered, they are mainly designated to operate the nonstop [Chengdu Tianfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Tianfu) — [Malé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velana_International_Airport) and Chengdu Tianfu — [Tokyo Narita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Narita) route, which can take over 6 hours on some segments during the winter season, it is the first Chinese airline to receive this type.[^cite_note-59]

On 24 July 2025, [Etihad Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad_Airways) received its first of the 30 ordered A321LR,[^cite_note-60] the aircraft is fitted with 2 Auxiliary Fuel Tanks (ACT).[^cite_note-61]

### A321XLR



An A321XLR prototype taxiing at [Airbus Hamburg-Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Finkenwerder_Airport) in 2022

In January 2018, Airbus stated that it was studying an A321LR variant with a further increased MTOW, requiring strengthened landing gear.[^cite_note-AvWeek31jan2018-24] With a lower-density cabin, it was expected to fly almost 5,000 nmi (9,300 km; 5,800 mi)[^cite_note-AvWeek31jan2018-24][^cite_note-AvWeek20jul2018-62] The proposed A321XLR (Xtra Long Range) with a range extended to 4,500 nmi (8,300 km; 5,200 mi) was to be launched in 2019 to enter service in 2021 or 2022.[^cite_note-AvWeek20jul2018-62] Integrated in the fuselage to save weight, the centre fuel tank was to be enlarged.[^cite_note-AvWeek20jul2018-62] As of July 2018, about 200–300 nmi (370–560 km; 230–350 mi) of the targeted range increase had already been secured; additional work was needed to achieve the remaining 200 nmi (370 km; 230 mi).[^cite_note-AvWeek20jul2018-62]

In October 2018, the A321XLR was proposed to Air Transat and [AerCap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AerCap): Air Transat could reach Southern European destinations such as [Split, Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia) from [Montreal](7954681.md) and [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-63] In November, Airbus indicated that, compared to the A321LR, the A321XLR would have an increase to 100 t (220,000 lb) in MTOW and an increase of 700 nmi (1,300 km; 810 mi) in range, with the same wing and engines, increased fuel capacity and strengthened landing gear.[^cite_note-64] In January 2019, [Air Canada](145623.md) expressed interest in using narrow-body aircraft for transatlantic routes and said it was considering options including the A321XLR and the [Boeing 737 MAX](32924090.md).[^cite_note-65]

#### Potential stretch

In 2018, analysts reported that competing with the [Boeing NMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_NMA) concept airliner could require stretching the A321neo by one or two rows: its take-off weight could be increased to 100 t (220,000 lb) by tweaking its wing and strengthening its landing gear, requiring more engine thrust; or it could receive a lighter and larger new wing, more costly to develop but with the same thrust.[^cite_note-66] A stretch would probably involve fore and aft plugs to keep its [centre of gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_and_balance), but tailstrike clearance could constrain [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanced_field) speed and performance, while further [cabin crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_crew) would be needed over 250 seats.[^cite_note-Flight1feb2018-43] The NMA project has been suspended since 2022, leaving no forthcoming competitor for the potential stretch variant of the A321neo.[^cite_note-67]

#### Commercial launch



[Iberia](98613.md) was the launch customer of the A321XLR.

The A321XLR was officially launched at the [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show) on 17 June 2019, with deliveries at that time expected from 2023.[^cite_note-68] Its design offers 4,700 nmi (8,700 km; 5,400 mi) of range and features a new permanent rear centre tank (RCT) for more fuel, strengthened landing gear for a 101 t (223,000 lb) MTOW, and an optimised wing trailing-edge flap to preserve take-off performance.[^cite_note-69] The RCT will hold 12,900 L (3,400 US gal) of fuel, the equivalent of four 3,121 L (824 US gal) current Additional Centre Tanks (ACTs), yet has a weight equivalent to a single ACT and takes up the cargo hold space of two; a forward ACT can also be fitted if necessary.[^cite_note-70] As the [sharklets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingtip_device#Blended_winglets) lowered take-off and landing speeds, the switch from a double-[slotted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flap_\(aeronautics\)#Slotted_flap) to single-slotted inboard flap will reduce complexity, weight and drag. The [FMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_management_system) can set intermediate flap positions. The revised design could be applied to other A321neo variants.[^cite_note-71]

Orders from several lessors and airlines were announced at the show, starting with [Middle East Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Airlines), which ordered four A321XLRs, making it the launch airline customer.[^cite_note-72] Air Lease Corporation ordered 27 A321XLRs alongside 23 other A321neos and 50 A220-300s.[^cite_note-73] [IAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Airlines_Group) quoted a \$142 million list price as it committed to 28 aircraft, including 8 for [Iberia](98613.md), 6 for Aer Lingus, plus 14 options.[^cite_note-74] [Qantas Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas_Group) placed an order for 36 XLRs, to be operated on routes between Australia and [Asia](689.md), and is also set to be one of the launch customers.[^cite_note-75] [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines) converted 30 A321neo orders to XLRs and ordered an additional 20 XLRs.[^cite_note-76] [Indigo Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigo_Partners) also placed an order for 50 XLRs for its airline divisions and Frontier Airlines ordered 18, bringing the total number of commitments announced at the show to 243.[^cite_note-77]

Some are cautious about the potential market: [Lufthansa](97733.md) sees the variant as a "niche aircraft" less comfortable than widebodies,[^cite_note-78] and a large lessor is hesitant as it expects a 400–500 aircraft market.[^cite_note-79] Airbus argues that the minimal changes mean it can be used as a regular A321neo[^cite_note-80] and ALC forecast potential for 50 operators in the next five years. The market could prefer shorter [turnaround](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_ground_handling) times to more range.[^cite_note-AvWeek27jun2019-81]

On 29 October 2019, IndiGo placed a firm order for 300 A320neo family aircraft, including 69 A321XLR.[^cite_note-82] On 3 December 2019, [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) announced an order to purchase 50 new Airbus A321XLR aircraft, with deliveries beginning in 2024, to replace their Boeing 757–200 fleet.[^cite_note-83] Valued at \$7.1 billion before discounts (\$142M each), United plans to use these aircraft for additional destinations in Europe from its East Coast hubs in Washington, DC and Newark.[^cite_note-84] In April 2020, 450 orders for the XLR had been received from 24 customers.[^cite_note-Flight21apr2020-85] In March 2023, 465 orders for the A321XLR were received.[^cite_note-86] In July 2023, Icelandair ordered 13 XLR.[^cite_note-87]

[Iberia](98613.md) announced on 19 May 2024 that the company will be "the first airline in the world to add the new Airbus A321XLR to its fleet".[^cite_note-88]

In July 2024, the Airbus A321XLR powered by CFMI LEAP engines received EASA certification and conducted a demonstration flight at the [Farnborough Airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Airshow).[^cite_note-89] The Pratt & Whitney PW1100G-powered variant subsequently received type certification from both the FAA and EASA in February 2025.[^cite_note-90]

#### Manufacturing



Airbus A321neos awaiting completion at the [Hamburg Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Finkenwerder_Airport) final assembly line.

The XLR needs 10–15% more work than an ACF, itself requiring 30% more work than a standard A321neo.[^cite_note-AvWeek27jun2019-81] Engines used for now generate 150 kN (33,000 lb) of thrust and could be sufficient, and no more than the 160 kN (35,000 lb) already certified are needed for the XLR.[^cite_note-AvWeek27jun2019-81] The XLR is a low-risk, high-commonality variant but more substantial upgrades could include a composite wing or a stretch.[^cite_note-AvWeek27jun2019-81]

By April 2020, Airbus had cut the first metal for the centre wingbox, while Safran had begun undercarriage [forging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forging) production.[^cite_note-Flight21apr2020-85] Premium Aerotec will manufacture the specific aft centre fuel tank primary structure, Spirit AeroSystems will build the inboard single-slotted flap, FACC AG will produce a modified belly [fairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_fairing), while [Collins Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_Aerospace) and [Parker Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Aerospace) are developing the fuel systems.[^cite_note-Flight21apr2020-85] By August 2020, Premium Aerotec had started producing the rear centre tank for the first A321XLR in [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg), to be transferred to Airbus's Hamburg plant in early 2021.[^cite_note-91]

By April 2021, a standard A321LR fuselage section had been withdrawn from the Hamburg production line for use as a "pre-industrial system accelerator" to test the integration of XLR-specific systems; at Saint-Nazaire, a nose section was serving as an integration test bed for a new instrument panel assembly, before being used to analyse structural reinforcements needed for the XLR.[^cite_note-92] The fuselage sections, wings, landing gear and tailplanes of the first test aircraft were delivered to the Hamburg Final Assembly Line in November, and its structure was completed by early December, among three planned development aircraft, and before entry into service in 2023.[^cite_note-93]

The first A321XLR prototype was rolled out in May 2022, equipped with [CFM LEAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_LEAP) engines.[^cite_note-94] The first flight took place on 15 June 2022 from Hamburg.[^cite_note-95] However, entry into service was pushed back to 2024 from the initial 2023 date to address fuel tank issues raised by regulators.[^cite_note-96] A revised design with special conditions was approved by the United States [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) in December 2022.[^cite_note-97]

#### Entry into service

The A321XLR received its type certificate from EASA on 19 July 2024 and from the FAA on 2 October 2024.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] The first A321XLR was delivered to [Iberia](98613.md) on 30 October 2024[^cite_note-101] and conducted its first revenue flight on 6 November 2024.[^cite_note-102] The first long-haul flight with passengers was on 14 November 2024, from [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Barajas_Airport) to [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_International_Airport).[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

### A321MPA

The **A321MPA** (Maritime Patrol Aircraft) is a military maritime patrol and anti-submarine warfare derivative under development, based on the long-range A321XLR platform.[^cite_note-TWZ-Feb2025-105] First introduced as a concept at the Euronaval exhibition in November 2024,[^cite_note-106] the variant was chosen by the French Ministry of Armed Forces over a competing Dassault proposal based on the Falcon 10X business jet. In February 2025, the French Defence Procurement Agency ([DGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale_de_l'armement)) awarded a 24-month definition and risk-reduction contract to Airbus Defence and Space as the prime contractor, alongside [Thales Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group), to mature the platform ahead of a full development and production contract expected at the end of 2026.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-EuroSD-Feb2025-108]

The aircraft is scheduled to enter service with the [French Navy](383256.md) between 2030 and 2040, replacing its aging [Bréguet 1150 Atlantic II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%A9guet_1150_Atlantic) fleet. Airbus also intends to market the platform internationally to compete with the [Boeing P-8 Poseidon](731169.md), drawing significant export interest following its presentation at the Singapore Airshow in February 2026.[^cite_note-109]

Billed as a "flying frigate," the A321MPA leverages the expanded volume and payload capacity of the A321XLR airframe. Key modifications include observation windows beneath the cockpit and a dedicated internal weapons bay housed in an underfloor ventral gondola behind the wing to minimize aerodynamic drag. This bay allows for the internal carriage of anti-submarine torpedoes and future anti-ship and cruise missiles (FMAN/FMC).[^cite_note-EuroSD-Feb2025-108][^cite_note-TWZ-Feb2025-105] The mission architecture relies heavily on a Thales-developed electronics suite, incorporating a next-generation active electronically scanned array (AESA) search radar, acoustic processing systems for active and passive sonobuoys, electronic support measures (ESM), and a tail-mounted [magnetic anomaly detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_anomaly_detector) (MAD).[^cite_note-TWZ-Feb2025-105]

### Engines

Suffix "N" (e.g., A321-271**N**) denotes A321 airframes with the standard (STD) cabin door layout. "NX" (e.g., A321-272**NX**) denotes airframes built with the Cabin Flex (ACF) configuration, including all A321LR aircraft. "NY" (e.g., A321-271**NY**) denotes A321XLR models. No separate suffix exists for A321LR aircraft, which are included within the "NX" (ACF) designation.

Within the model number, the digit "5" (e.g., A321-2**5**1N) denotes aircraft powered by [CFM International LEAP-1A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International_LEAP-1A) engines, while "7" (e.g., A321-2**7**1N) denotes [Pratt & Whitney PW1100G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_PW1000G) engines. The final digit indicates thrust rating: "1" (e.g., A321-25**1**NX) denotes the baseline (standard-thrust) configuration; "2" (e.g., A321-25**2**NX) denotes a derated configuration, in which the engines operate at reduced thrust; and "3" (e.g., A321-25**3**NX) denotes an uprated configuration, in which the engines operate at higher thrust than the baseline.
