Demonstration against the Article 23 law in Macau

The **Macau National Security Law** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 維護國家安全法, lit. 'Safeguarding National Security Law'; [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Lei relativa à defesa da segurança do Estado*, lit. 'Law related to the defense of the security of the State') is a law in [Macau](19068.md) which prohibits and punishes acts of treason, secession, and subversion against the [Central government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), as well as preparative acts leading to any of the three acts. Taken into effect on 3 March 2009, the purpose of the law is to fulfil Article 23 of the [Macau Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_Basic_Law), the *de facto* [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) of the [Macau Special Administration Region](19068.md).[^cite_note-PeoplesDaily-1][^cite_note-BBC-2][^cite_note-Basic_Law-3]

## History

According to the [Secretary for Administration and Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_for_Administration_and_Justice_\(Macau\)) [Florinda da Rosa Silva Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florinda_da_Rosa_Silva_Chan), drafting began in 2004, taking into account examples from [Portugal](23033.md) and [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-scmp1-4] Until 1999, Macau was [a colony of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_sovereignty_over_Macau). The draft was released on 22 October 2008. It proposed to ban treason, attempts to overthrow the [Chinese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) and theft of national secrets. Some of the proposed offenses carry a maximum penalty of 25 years in jail.[^cite_note-hks1-5]

The law came into force in 2009. This caused some disquiet and concern among [pro-democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-democracy_camp_\(Macau\)), who fear that this new law could limit Macao residents' [freedom of expression](21401843.md) and protest, and that it is intended to serve as an example for its rebellious counterpart, Hong Kong.[^cite_note-6]

### Response to drafting of the law

[Edmund Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Ho), [Chief Executive of Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Executive_of_Macau), said in a press conference that the bill targets "serious criminal behavior" and will not limit protests or criticism of [Beijing](18603746.md). He further said "Chanting a few slogans, writing a few articles criticizing the central government or the Macau government, these activities won't be regulated by this proposed law."[^cite_note-hks1-5] Macau Legislator Au Kam-san said "We don't want to see any [mainland](55079.md) style national security law. It would be acceptable to enact a law based on the [Johannesburg Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg_Principles?action=edit\&redlink=1)."[^cite_note-scmp2-7]

Political commentator Larry So Man-yum said the legislation would do well in Macau given residents' patriotism and their lack of awareness about [civil rights](37071.md). "There will be absolutely no problem. Compared to [Hongkongers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongkonger), Macau people have high levels of acceptance for the central government. No "Broomhead" will emerge in Macau."[^cite_note-scmp2-7] In 2003, Secretary for Security [Regina Ip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Ip) was nicknamed "Broomhead" for attempting to sell [Article 23 in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Basic_Law_Article_23).[^cite_note-scmp2-7] The [Hong Kong government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_government) on 22 October responded with having no plan to embark on the legislation, adding its most pressing commitments are economic and livelihood issues.[^cite_note-8]

### Hong Kong protests

During the [2019–2020 Hong Kong protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests), Macau stayed largely silent,[^cite_note-9] as was predicted.[^cite_note-scmp2-7] This meant that no modification to Macau's NSL was made at the moment, making it, in the view of the Central Government, "a posterchild of the [one country, two systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_country,_two_systems)" policy,[^cite_note-10] and "an example for Taiwan".[^cite_note-ABC_News-11] This has been attributed to Macau's strong economic performance and the comparatively greater willingness of the Portuguese colonial administration to allow Chinese integration earlier on, including during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution).[^cite_note-ABC_News-11]

### 2023 update

National Security legislation was updated on 18 May 2023, when the Legislature unanimously[^cite_note-12] passed amendments aimed at "further protecting National Security in the complicated environment faced by China",[^cite_note-13] including expanding the definition of secession to include acts of non-violence, and redefining the crime of "theft of official secrets" as "violation of official secrets".[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]
