**Receptor tyrosine-protein kinase erbB-2** is a [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein) that normally resides in the membranes of cells and is encoded by the ***ERBB2*** [gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene). ERBB is abbreviated from erythroblastic oncogene B, a gene originally isolated from the avian genome. The human protein is also frequently referred to as **HER2** (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2) or **CD340** ([cluster of differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_of_differentiation) 340).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

HER2 is a member of the human [epidermal growth factor receptor (HER/EGFR/ERBB) family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ErbB). But contrary to other members of the ERBB family, HER2 does not directly bind ligand. HER2 activation results from heterodimerization with another ERBB member or by homodimerization when HER2 concentration are high, for instance in cancer.[^cite_note-8] [Amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_duplication) or over-expression of this [oncogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogene) has been shown to play an important role in the development and progression of certain aggressive types of [breast cancer](70547.md). In recent years the protein has become an important [biomarker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomarker_\(medicine\)) and target of therapy for approximately 30% of breast cancer patients.[^cite_note-pmid23320171-9]

## Name

*HER2* is named as such due to structural similarities with human epidermal growth factor receptor 1, or [HER1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermal_growth_factor_receptor)*.* The *Neu* alias of HER2 derives its name from the parent [glioblastoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glioblastoma) cell line - a type of *neural* tumor found in rodents. *ErbB-2* was named for its similarity to avian erythroblastosis oncogene &#x42;*, ErbB*; the [oncogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogene) later shown to code for epidermal growth factor receptor, [EGFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermal_growth_factor_receptor). Molecular cloning of EGFR discovered that HER2, Neu, and ErbB-2 are all encoded by the same [orthologs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthologous_gene).[^cite_note-Coussens_1985-10]

## Gene

*ERBB2*, a known [proto-oncogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-oncogene),[^cite_note-11] is located at the long arm of human [chromosome 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome_17) (17q12).[^cite_note-12]

## Function

The [ErbB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ErbB) family consists of four individual [plasma membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane)-bound [receptor tyrosine kinases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_tyrosine_kinase). One of these is erbB-2; the other members are [erbB-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermal_growth_factor_receptor), [erbB-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ERBB3) (neuregulin-binding; lacks kinase domain), and [erbB-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ERBB4). All four contain an extracellular ligand binding domain, a transmembrane domain, and an intracellular domain that can interact with a multitude of signaling molecules and exhibit both ligand-dependent and ligand-independent activity. Notably, no ligands for HER2 have yet been identified.[^cite_note-13] HER2 can heterodimerise with any of the other three receptors and is considered to be the preferred dimerisation partner of the other ErbB receptors.[^cite_note-Olayioye_2001-14] Dimerisation results in the clustering of receptors within [lipid rafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_raft) and [autophosphorylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autophosphorylation) of tyrosine residues within the cytoplasmic domain of the receptors to activate a variety of signaling pathways.[^cite_note-15]

### Signal transduction

Signaling pathways activated by HER2 include:[^cite_note-pmid19887469-16]

- [mitogen-activated protein kinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitogen-activated_protein_kinase) (MAPK)

- [phosphoinositide 3-kinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphoinositide_3-kinase) (PI3K/Akt)

- [phospholipase C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phospholipase_C) γ

- [protein kinase C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_kinase_C) (PKC)

- [signal transducer and activator of transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STAT_protein) (STAT)

In summary, signaling through the ErbB family of receptors promotes cell proliferation and opposes [apoptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis), and therefore must be tightly regulated to prevent uncontrolled cell growth from occurring.

## Clinical significance

### Cancer

Amplification, also known as over-expression of the *ERBB2* gene, occurs in approximately 10-30% of [breast cancers](70547.md).[^cite_note-pmid23320171-9][^cite_note-pmid16236735-17][^cite_note-18] HER2-positive breast cancers are associated with increased disease recurrence and a poor prognosis compared with other identifiably genetically distinct breast cancers; however, drug agents targeting HER2 have significantly and positively altered the otherwise poor prognosis of HER2-positive breast cancer.[^cite_note-pmid17993237-19] Over-expression of HER2 is also known to occur in [adenocarcinomas of the lung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenocarcinoma_of_the_lung),[^cite_note-20] ovary,[^cite_note-21] and stomach, and in aggressive forms of uterine cancer, such as [uterine serous carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_serous_carcinoma).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Buza2014-23] E.g., HER2 is over-expressed in approximately 7-34% of patients with gastric cancer[^cite_note-Rüschoff2012-24][^cite_note-pmid21556317-25] and in 30% of salivary duct carcinomas.[^cite_note-26] The *ERBB2* gene is physically near the gene encoding [GRB7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRB7) on chromosome 17. GRB7 is a proto-oncogene associated with breast, testicular germ cell, gastric, and esophageal tumours and is frequently co-amplified in cancer cells with HER2 amplification.[^cite_note-27]

The high expression of HER2 correlates with better survival in esophageal adenocarcinoma.[^cite_note-28]

The high amplification of HER2 copy number positively contributes to the survival time of gastric cardia adenocarcinoma patients.[^cite_note-29]

### Mutations

Furthermore, diverse structural alterations have been identified that cause ligand-independent firing of this receptor, doing so in the absence of receptor over-expression. HER2 is found in a variety of tumours and some of these tumours carry point mutations in the sequence specifying the transmembrane domain of HER2. Substitution of a valine for a glutamic acid or a glutamine in the transmembrane domain can result in the constitutive dimerisation of this protein in the absence of a ligand.[^cite_note-30]

HER2 mutations have been found in [non-small-cell lung cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-small-cell_lung_cancer) (NSCLC) and can direct treatment.[^cite_note-Mazières2013-31]

## As a drug target

HER2 is the target of the [monoclonal antibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoclonal_antibody) [trastuzumab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trastuzumab) (marketed as Herceptin). Trastuzumab is effective only in cancers where HER2 is over-expressed. One year of trastuzumab therapy is recommended for all patients with HER2-positive breast cancer who are also receiving chemotherapy.[^cite_note-pmid25848335-32] Randomized trials have demonstrated no additional benefit beyond 12 months, whereas 6 months has been shown to be inferior to 12. Trastuzumab is administered intravenously weekly or every 3 weeks.[^cite_note-33]

An important downstream effect of trastuzumab binding to HER2 is an increase in [p27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDKN1B), a protein that halts cell proliferation.[^cite_note-Le_2005-34] Another monoclonal antibody, [Pertuzumab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertuzumab), which inhibits dimerisation of HER2 and HER3 receptors, was approved by the FDA for use in combination with trastuzumab in June 2012.

As of November 2015, there are a number of ongoing and recently completed clinical trials of novel targeted agents for HER2+ metastatic breast cancer, e.g. [margetuximab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margetuximab).[^cite_note-35]

Additionally, [NeuVax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeuVax) ([Galena Biopharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galena_Biopharma)) is a peptide-based immunotherapy that directs "killer" [T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_cell) to target and destroy cancer cells that express HER2. It has entered phase 3 clinical trials.

It has been found that patients with ER+ ([estrogen receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen_receptor) positive)/HER2+ compared with ER-/HER2+ breast cancers may actually benefit more from drugs that inhibit the [PI3K/AKT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PI3K/AKT/mTOR_pathway) molecular pathway.[^cite_note-pmid19552798-36]

Over-expression of HER2 can also be suppressed by the amplification of other genes. Research is currently being conducted to discover which genes may have this desired effect.

The expression of HER2 is regulated by signaling through estrogen receptors. Normally, [estradiol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estradiol) and [tamoxifen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamoxifen) acting through the estrogen receptor down-regulate the expression of HER2. However, when the ratio of the [coactivator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coactivator_\(genetics\)) [AIB-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_receptor_coactivator_3) exceeds that of the [corepressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corepressor_\(genetics\)) [PAX2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAX2), the expression of HER2 is upregulated in the presence of tamoxifen, leading to tamoxifen-resistant [breast cancer](70547.md).[^cite_note-urlCORDIS_:_News-37][^cite_note-Hurtado1_2008-38]

Among approved anti-HER2 therapeutics are also [tyrosine kinase inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrosine_kinase_inhibitor) (such as [lapatinib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapatinib), [neratinib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neratinib), and [tucatinib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucatinib)) and antibody-drug conjugates ([ado-trastuzumab emtansine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ado-trastuzumab_emtansine) and [trastuzumab deruxtecan](62630349.md)).[^cite_note-39]

## Diagnostics

HER2 testing is performed on [breast biopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_biopsy) of breast cancer patients to assess prognosis and to determine suitability for trastuzumab therapy. It is important that trastuzumab is restricted to HER2-positive individuals as it is expensive and has been associated with cardiac toxicity.[^cite_note-pmid17687157-40] For HER2-positive tumors, the benefits of trastuzumab clearly outweigh the risks.

Tests are usually performed on [breast biopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_biopsy) samples obtained by either [fine-needle aspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-needle_aspiration), core needle biopsy, [vacuum-assisted breast biopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum-assisted_breast_biopsy), or surgical excision.&#x20;

[Immunohistochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunohistochemistry) (IHC) is generally used to measure the amount of HER2 protein present in the sample, with [fluorescence in situ hybridisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence_in_situ_hybridisation) (FISH) being used on samples that are equivocal in IHC. However, in several locations, FISH is used initially, followed by IHC in equivocal cases.[^cite_note-pmid27899970-41]

### Serum

The extracellular domain of HER2 can be shed from the surface of tumour cells and enter the circulation. Measurement of serum HER2 by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay ([ELISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELISA)) offers a far less invasive method of determining HER2 status than a biopsy and consequently has been extensively investigated. Results so far have suggested that changes in serum HER2 concentrations may be useful in predicting response to trastuzumab therapy.[^cite_note-pmid18661530-48] However, its ability to determine eligibility for trastuzumab therapy is less clear.[^cite_note-pmid19255335-49]
