**Austin** ([/ˈɔːstɪn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/03/En-us-Austin.oga/En-us-Austin.oga.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/03/En-us-Austin.oga/En-us-Austin.oga.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*AW-stin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-9] is the [capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) of the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Texas](29810.md). With a population of 961,855 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census),[^cite_note-10] it is the [12th-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the U.S., [fifth-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Texas_by_population) in Texas, and second-most populous U.S. state capital (after [Phoenix, Arizona](49121.md)), while the [Austin metro area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Austin) with an estimated 2.55 million residents is the [25th-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the nation. Austin is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) and most populous city of [Travis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_County,_Texas), with portions extending into [Hays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hays_County,_Texas) and [Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamson_County,_Texas) counties. Incorporated on December 27, 1839,[^cite_note-11] it has been one of the fastest-growing large cities in the United States since 2010.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Located in [Central Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Texas) within the greater [Texas Hill Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Hill_Country), it is home to numerous lakes, rivers, and waterways, including [Lady Bird Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Lake) and [Lake Travis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Travis) on the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(Texas\)), [Barton Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Springs_Pool), [McKinney Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinney_Falls_State_Park), and [Lake Walter E. Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Walter_E._Long). Austin and [San Antonio](53848.md) are approximately 80 miles (129 km) apart, and both fall along the [I-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35_in_Texas) corridor. This combined metropolitan region of [San Antonio–Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio%E2%80%93Austin_metroplex) has approximately 5 million people.[^cite_note-sa-atxmetro-15] Austin is the southernmost state capital in the [contiguous United States](83759.md) and is considered a [Gamma + level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network#Gamma_+) [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) as categorized by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-16]

[Residents of Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Austin,_Texas) are sometimes known as Austinites.[^cite_note-Long_2010-17] They include a diverse mix of government employees, college students, musicians, high-tech workers, and [blue-collar workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-collar_worker). The city's official slogan promotes Austin as "The Live Music Capital of the World", a reference to the city's many musicians and live music venues, as well as the long-running [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) TV concert series *[Austin City Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits)*.[^cite_note-motto-18][^cite_note-atx_motto-19] Austin is the site of [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest) (SXSW), an annual conglomeration of parallel [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_festival), [interactive media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_media), and [music festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_festival). The city also adopted "[Silicon Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Hills)" as a nickname in the 1990s due to a rapid influx of technology and development companies. In recent years, some Austinites have adopted the unofficial slogan "[Keep Austin Weird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Austin_Weird)",[^cite_note-20] which refers to the desire to protect small, unique, and local businesses from being overrun by large corporations.[^cite_note-weird-21] Ongoing rapid development and [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) challenge its [bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemianism) roots and fuel nostalgia for “Old Austin".  Since the late 19th century, Austin has also been known as the "[City of the Violet Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_the_Violet_Crown)", because of the colorful glow of light across the hills just after sunset.[^cite_note-22]

Emerging from a strong economic focus on government and education, Austin has become a center for technology and business since the 1990s.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-austin_history-24] The technology roots in Austin can be traced back to the 1960s, when defense electronics contractor [Tracor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracor) (now [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems)) began operations in the city in 1962. IBM followed in 1967, opening a facility to produce its Selectric typewriters. Texas Instruments was set up in Austin two years later, and Motorola (now NXP Semiconductors) started semiconductor chip manufacturing in 1974. A number of [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies have headquarters or regional offices in Austin, including [3M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M), [Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Micro_Devices), [Agilent Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agilent_Technologies), [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com), [Apple](856.md), [CrowdStrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CrowdStrike), [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell), [Expedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedia_Group), [Facebook (Meta)](62420226.md), [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), [Google](1092923.md), [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM), [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel), [NXP Semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NXP_Semiconductors), [Oracle](22591.md), [Tesla](5533631.md), and [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments). With regard to education, Austin is the home of the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin), one of the largest universities in the U.S., with over 50,000 students.[^cite_note-25] In 2021, Austin became home to [Austin FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_FC), the first (and currently only) [major professional sports team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) in the city.

## History

Main article: [History of Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Austin,_Texas)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Austin,_Texas).

Austin, Travis County and Williamson County have been the site of human habitation since at least 9200 BC. The area's earliest known inhabitants lived during the late [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) (Ice Age) and are linked to the [Clovis culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_culture) around 9200 BC (over 11,200 years ago), based on evidence found throughout the area and documented at the much-studied [Gault Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gault_\(archaeological_site\)), midway between [Georgetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown,_Texas) and [Fort Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Hood).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Excavations at the [French Legation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Legation) in downtown Austin revealed a site utilized during the Archaic period of [North America](21139.md), about 5,000 years ago. Artifacts dating to the Paleo-Indians period show possible use as early as 9,000 years ago.[^cite_note-28]

Spanish colonists traveled through the area, though few permanent settlements were created for some time. The [Espinosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidro_de_Espinosa)-[Olivares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_de_Olivares)-[Aguirre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Aguirre) expedition of 1709 reached the Colorado River near today's Austin hoping to meet with the Tejas Indians ([Hasinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasinai)). It was met by members of a number of Indigenous Peoples: Yojuan (AKA [Yojuane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yojuane)), Simomo, and Tusonibi. Guides for the expedition included Indigenous Peoples they had encountered just previously living near [San Pedro Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Springs): Chaularame, [Payaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payaya_people), Sana, [Sijame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sijame), and Siupan. With orders not to cross the Colorado the expedition returned to Mexico.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-HOT:_Hays_Co-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In 1730, three [Catholic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_missions) from [East Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Texas) were combined and reestablished as one mission on the south side of the Colorado River, in what is now [Zilker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilker_Park), in Austin. The mission was in this area for only about seven months, then was moved to [San Antonio de Béxar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_de_B%C3%A9xar) and split into three missions.[^cite_note-33]

Many of the Indigenous Peoples the Spanish met on early expeditions, for example the [Payaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payaya_people), are classified as [Coahuiltecan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coahuiltecan).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Today Coahuiltecan descendants include Austin's Barton Springs among the four springs part of their creation story dating to prehistoric times; these springs are Comal Springs, Barton Springs, San Marcos Springs and San Antonio Springs. While there are many other tribes associated with Austin, Central Texas, of which Austin is a part, was not the long-term ancestral homeland of the ethnographically well-known [Comanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comanche), [Kiowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiowa), [Lipan Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipan_Apache_people), Waco (a branch of the [Wichita)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_people) or even the [Tonkawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonkawa), all having arrived in Central Texas just before or during the early European contact period.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

During the 1830s, pioneers began to settle the area in central Austin along the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(Texas\)). Spanish forts were established in what are now [Bastrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastrop,_Texas) and [San Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marcos,_Texas).[^cite_note-HOT:_Hays_Co-30][^cite_note-HOT:_Bastrop-42] Following [Mexico's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mexico#Independence_era_\(1808%E2%80%931829\)), new settlements were established in [Central Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Texas).[^cite_note-HOT:_Bastrop-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-HOT\:Travis_Co-44]



Statue of the Goddess of Liberty on the [Texas State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Capitol) grounds, prior to installation atop the rotunda

In 1835–1836, Texans fought and won [independence from Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Revolution). Texas thus became an independent country with its own president, congress, and monetary system. In 1839, the Texas Congress formed a commission to seek a site for the new capital of the [Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas) to replace [Houston](13774.md).[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] When he was Vice President of Texas, [Mirabeau B. Lamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirabeau_B._Lamar) had visited the area during a [buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_bison)-hunting expedition between 1837 and 1838. He advised the commissioners to consider the area on the north bank of the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(Texas\)) (near the present-day [Congress Avenue Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Avenue_Bridge)), noting the area's hills, waterways, and pleasant surroundings.[^cite_note-Patoski_2010-46] It was seen as a convenient crossroads for trade routes between [Santa Fe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe,_New_Mexico) and [Galveston Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galveston_Bay), as well as routes between Northern Mexico and the [Red River](194242.md).[^cite_note-Erlichman_2006-47] In 1839, the site was chosen, and briefly incorporated under the name "Waterloo".[^cite_note-48] Shortly afterward, the name was changed to Austin in honor of [Stephen F. Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_F._Austin), the "Father of Texas" and the republic's first secretary of state.

The city grew throughout the 19th century and became a center for government and education with the construction of the [Texas State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Capitol) and the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin).[^cite_note-49]



Second capitol building in Austin

[Edwin Waller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Waller) was picked by Lamar to survey the village and draft a plan laying out the new capital.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] The original site was narrowed to 640 acres (260 ha) that fronted the Colorado River between two creeks, [Shoal Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal_Creek,_Austin,_Texas) and [Waller Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waller_Creek), which was later named in his honor. Waller and a team of surveyors developed Austin's first [city plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_plan), commonly known as the [Waller Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waller_Plan), dividing the site into a 14-block grid plan bisected by a broad north–south thoroughfare, Congress Avenue, running up from the river to Capital Square, where the new Texas State Capitol was to be constructed. A temporary one-story capitol was erected on the corner of Colorado and 8th Streets. On August 1, 1839, the first auction of 217 out of 306 lots total was held.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45][^cite_note-Erlichman_2006-47] The Waller Plan designed and surveyed now forms the basis of downtown Austin.

Initially, the new capital thrived but Lamar's political enemy, [Sam Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Houston), used two Mexican army incursions to [San Antonio](53848.md) as an excuse to move the government. Sam Houston fought bitterly against Lamar's decision to establish the capital in such a remote wilderness. The men and women who traveled mainly from Houston to conduct government business were intensely disappointed as well. By 1840, the population had risen to 856, nearly half of whom fled Austin when Congress recessed.[^cite_note-50] The resident [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) population listed in January of this same year was 176.[^cite_note-51] The fear of Austin's proximity to the Indians and Mexico, which still considered Texas a part of their land, created an immense motive for Sam Houston, the first and third President of the Republic of Texas, to relocate the capital once again in 1841. Upon threats of Mexican troops in Texas, Houston raided the Land Office to transfer all official documents to Houston for safe keeping in what was later known as the [Archive War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archive_War), but the people of Austin would not allow this unaccompanied decision to be executed. The documents stayed, but the capital would temporarily move from Austin to Houston to [Washington-on-the-Brazos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington-on-the-Brazos,_Texas). Without the governmental body, Austin's population declined to a low of only a few hundred people throughout the early 1840s. The voting by the fourth President of the Republic, [Anson Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anson_Jones), and Congress, who reconvened in Austin in 1845, settled the issue to keep Austin the seat of government, as well as annex the Republic of Texas into the United States.

In 1860, 38% of Travis County residents were [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-52] In 1861, with the outbreak of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), voters in Austin and other Central Texas communities voted against secession.[^cite_note-HOT:_Bastrop-42][^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] However, as the war progressed and fears of attack by [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) forces increased, Austin contributed hundreds of men to the [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) forces, albeit often via Confederate conscription laws, not popular with many Texans.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-\:1-55] Some Austinites, like prominent lawyer and businessman, Josiah Fisk, namesake of Fiskville, left Texas when threatened with conscription.[^cite_note-56] Some opted trying to avoid conscription by hiding in remote locations such as [Hamilton Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Pool_Preserve).[^cite_note-57] Others mounted armed resistance to the Confederacy.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-\:1-55][^cite_note-60]

The African American population of Austin swelled dramatically after the enforcement of the [Emancipation Proclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_Proclamation) in Texas by [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army) General [Gordon Granger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Granger) at [Galveston](53840.md), in an event commemorated as [Juneteenth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juneteenth). Black communities such as [Wheatville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheatville,_Austin), Pleasant Hill, and Clarksville were established, with Clarksville being the oldest surviving freedomtown ‒ the original post-Civil War settlements founded by former African-American slaves ‒ west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River).[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] In 1870, blacks made up 36.5% of Austin's population.[^cite_note-census-61]



An 1873 illustration of [Edwin Waller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Waller)'s layout for Austin

The postwar period saw dramatic population and economic growth. The opening of the [Houston and Texas Central Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_and_Texas_Central_Railway) (H\&TC) in 1871[^cite_note-62] turned Austin into the major trading center for the region, with the ability to transport both cotton and cattle. The [Missouri, Kansas & Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri%E2%80%93Kansas%E2%80%93Texas_Railroad) (MKT) line followed close behind.[^cite_note-63] Austin was also the terminus of the southernmost leg of the [Chisholm Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisholm_Trail), and "drovers" pushed cattle north to the railroad.[^cite_note-64] Cotton was one of the few crops produced locally for export, and a [cotton gin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_gin) engine was located downtown near the trains for "ginning" cotton of its seeds and turning the product into bales for shipment.[^cite_note-65] However, as other new railroads were built through the region in the 1870s, Austin began to lose its primacy in trade to the surrounding communities.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] In addition, the areas east of Austin took over cattle and cotton production from Austin, especially in towns like [Hutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutto,_Texas) and [Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor,_Texas) that sit over the [blackland prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_blackland_prairies), with its deep, rich soils for producing cotton and hay.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

In September 1881, Austin public schools held their first classes. The same year, Tillotson Collegiate and Normal Institute (now part of [Huston–Tillotson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huston%E2%80%93Tillotson_University)) opened its doors. The [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) held its first classes in 1883, although classes had been held in the original wooden state capitol for four years before.[^cite_note-68]

During the 1880s, Austin gained new prominence as the [state capitol building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Capitol) was completed in 1888 and claimed as the seventh largest building in the world.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] In the late 19th century, Austin expanded its city limits to more than three times its former area, and the first granite dam was built on the Colorado River to power a new street car line and the new "[moon towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonlight_tower)".[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] The first dam washed away in a flood on April 7, 1900.[^cite_note-69]

In the late 1920s and 1930s, Austin implemented the [1928 Austin city plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Austin_city_plan) through a series of civic development and beautification projects that created much of the city's infrastructure and many of its parks. In addition, the state legislature established the [Lower Colorado River Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Colorado_River_Authority) (LCRA) that, along with the city of Austin, created the system of dams along the Colorado River to form the [Highland Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Highland_Lakes). These projects were enabled in large part because the [Public Works Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Works_Administration) provided Austin with greater funding for municipal construction projects than other Texas cities.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45]

During the early 20th century, a three-way system of social segregation emerged in Austin, with Anglos, African Americans and Mexicans being separated by custom or law in most aspects of life, including housing, health care, and education. Deed restrictions also played an important role in [residential segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residential_segregation_in_the_United_States). After 1935 most housing deeds prohibited African Americans (and sometimes other nonwhite groups) from using land.[^cite_note-70] Combined with the system of segregated public services, racial segregation increased in Austin during the first half of the twentieth century, with African Americans and Mexicans experiencing high levels of discrimination and social marginalization.[^cite_note-71]

In 1940, the destroyed granite dam on the [Colorado River](180968.md) was finally replaced by a hollow concrete dam[^cite_note-72] that formed Lake McDonald (now called [Lake Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Austin)) and which has withstood all floods since. In addition, the much larger Mansfield Dam was built by the LCRA upstream of Austin to form Lake Travis, a flood-control reservoir.[^cite_note-73]

In the early 20th century, the [Texas Oil Boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Oil_Boom) took hold, creating tremendous economic opportunities in Southeast Texas and North Texas. The growth generated by this boom largely passed by Austin at first, with the city slipping from fourth largest to tenth largest in Texas between 1880 and 1920.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45]

After a severe lull in economic growth from the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States), Austin resumed its steady development. Following the mid-20th century, Austin became established as one of Texas' major metropolitan centers. In 1970, the U.S. Census Bureau reported Austin's population as 14.5% Hispanic, 11.9% black, and 73.4% non-Hispanic white.[^cite_note-census-61] In the late 20th century, Austin emerged as an important high tech center for [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) and software. The [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) emerged as a major university.[^cite_note-74]

The 1970s saw Austin's emergence in the national music scene, with local artists such as [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson), [Asleep at the Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asleep_at_the_Wheel), [Eric Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Johnson_\(guitarist\)) and [Stevie Ray Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Ray_Vaughan) and iconic music venues such as the [Armadillo World Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadillo_World_Headquarters). Over time, the long-running television program *Austin City Limits*, its namesake Austin City Limits Festival, and the [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest) music festival solidified the city's place in the music industry.[^cite_note-austin_history-24]

## Geography



Austin as seen from space, 2020

Austin, the southernmost state capital of the contiguous 48 states, is located in [Central Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Texas) on the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(Texas\)). Austin is 146 miles (230 km) northwest of [Houston](13774.md),[^cite_note-75] 182 miles (290 km) south of [Dallas](53838.md),[^cite_note-76] and 74 miles (120 km) northeast of [San Antonio](53848.md).[^cite_note-77]

Austin occupies a total area of 305.1 square miles (790.1 km<sup>2</sup>). Approximately 7.2 square miles (18.6 km<sup>2</sup>) of this area is water.[^cite_note-Census_2010-8] Austin is situated at the foot of the [Balcones Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balcones_Escarpment), on the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(Texas\)), with three artificial lakes within the city limits: [Lady Bird Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Lake) (formerly known as Town Lake), Lake Austin (both created by dams along the Colorado River), and Lake Walter E. Long that is partly used for cooling water for the Decker Power Plant. [Mansfield Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_Dam) and the foot of [Lake Travis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Travis) are located within the city's limits.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45] Lady Bird Lake, Lake Austin, and Lake Travis are each on the Colorado River.[^cite_note-HOT:_Austin-45]

The elevation of Austin varies from 425 feet (130 m) to approximately 1,000 feet (305 m) above sea level.[^cite_note-Abbott_2003-78] Due to the fact it straddles the [Balcones Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balcones_Fault), much of the eastern part of the city is flat, with heavy clay and loam soils, whereas the western part and western suburbs consist of rolling hills on the edge of the [Texas Hill Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Hill_Country).[^cite_note-Baird_2009-79] Because the hills to the west are primarily [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) rock with a thin covering of topsoil, portions of the city are frequently subjected to [flash floods](244391.md) from the runoff caused by thunderstorms.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] To help control this runoff and to generate hydroelectric power, the [Lower Colorado River Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Colorado_River_Authority) operates a series of dams that form the [Texas Highland Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Highland_Lakes). The lakes also provide venues for boating, swimming, and other forms of recreation within several parks on the lake shores.[^cite_note-82]

Austin is located at the intersection of four major ecological regions, and is consequently a temperate-to-hot green oasis with a highly variable climate having some characteristics of the desert, the tropics, and a wetter climate.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] The area is very diverse ecologically and biologically, and is home to a variety of animals and plants.[^cite_note-Vines_1984-85] Notably, the area is home to many types of wildflowers that blossom throughout the year but especially in the spring. This includes the popular [bluebonnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluebonnet_\(plant\)), some planted by ["Lady Bird" Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Johnson), wife of former President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson).[^cite_note-86]

The soils of Austin range from shallow, gravelly clay loams over limestone in the western outskirts to deep, fine sandy loams, silty clay loams, silty clays or clays in the city's eastern part. Some of the clays have pronounced shrink-swell properties and are difficult to work under most moisture conditions. Many of Austin's soils, especially the clay-rich types, are slightly to moderately alkaline and have free [calcium carbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_carbonate).[^cite_note-87]

### Cityscape

See also: [List of Austin neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Austin_neighborhoods) and [List of tallest buildings in Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Austin,_Texas)

Downtown Austin, Texas from across the Colorado River, October 2022

Downtown Austin, Texas from across the Colorado River, October 2022

Austin's skyline historically was modest, dominated by the Texas State Capitol and the University of Texas [Main Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Building_\(University_of_Texas_at_Austin\)). However, since the 2000s, many new high-rise towers have been constructed.[^cite_note-88] Austin is currently undergoing a skyscraper boom, which includes recent construction on new office, hotel and residential buildings. Downtown's buildings are somewhat spread out, partly due to a set of [zoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoning) restrictions that preserve the view of the [Texas State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Capitol) from various locations around Austin, known as the [Capitol View Corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Capitol_View_Corridors).[^cite_note-89]



One of the 15 remaining [moonlight towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonlight_towers_\(Austin,_Texas\)) in Austin

At night, parts of Austin are lit by "artificial moonlight" from [moonlight towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonlight_tower) built to illuminate the central part of the city.[^cite_note-90] The 165-foot (50 m) moonlight towers were built in the late 19th century and are now recognized as historic landmarks. Only 15 of the 31 original innovative towers remain standing in Austin, but none remain in any of the other cities where they were installed. The towers are featured in the 1993 film *[Dazed and Confused](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazed_and_Confused_\(film\))*.

In December 2023, amid rising home prices, the Austin City Council loosened the city's zoning rules to permit by-right development of triplexes on lots zoned for single family housing and loosened restrictions on tiny homes.[^cite_note-91]

#### Downtown

Main article: [Downtown Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Austin)

The central business district of Austin is home to the tallest condo towers in the state, with [The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent_\(Austin,_Texas\)) (58 stories and 690 ft (210 m) tall) and [The Austonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Austonian) (topping out at 56 floors and 685 ft (209 m) tall). The Independent became the tallest all-residential building in the U.S. west of [Chicago](6886.md) when topped out in 2018. In 2005, then-Mayor Will Wynn set out a goal of having 25,000 people living downtown by 2015.[^cite_note-92] Although downtown's growth did not meet this goal, downtown's residential population did surge from an estimated 5,000 in 2005 to 12,000 in 2015.[^cite_note-93] The skyline has drastically changed in recent years, and the residential real estate market has remained relatively strong. As of December 2016, there were 31 high rise projects either under construction, approved or planned to be completed in Austin's downtown core between 2017 and 2020. Sixteen of those were set to rise above 400 ft (120 m) tall, including four above 600', and eight above 500'. An additional 15 towers were slated to stand between 300' and 399' tall.

Austin is located within the middle of a unique, narrow transitional zone between the dry deserts of the American Southwest and the lush, green, more humid regions of the American Southeast. Its climate, topography, and vegetation share characteristics of both. Officially, Austin has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa* under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), *Cfhl* under the [Trewartha climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification)). This climate is typified by long, very hot summers, short, mild winters, and warm to hot spring and fall seasons in-between. Austin averages 34.32 inches (872 mm) of annual rainfall distributed mostly evenly throughout the year, though spring and fall are the wettest seasons. Sunshine is common during all seasons, with 2,650 hours, or 60.3% of the possible total, of bright sunshine per year.[^cite_note-94]

Summers in Austin are very hot, with average July and August highs frequently reaching 35 °C (95 °F) or above. Highs reach 90 °F (32 °C) on 123 days per year, of which 29 days reach 100 °F (38 °C); all years in the 1991-2020 period recorded at least 1 day of the latter.[^cite_note-NWSC-NOAA-95] The average daytime high is 70 °F (21 °C) or warmer between March 1 and November 21, rising to 80 °F (27 °C) or warmer between April 14 and October 24, and reaching 90 °F (32 °C) or warmer between May 30 and September 18.[^cite_note-NWSC-NOAA-95] The highest ever recorded temperature was 112 °F (44 °C) occurring on September 5, 2000, and August 28, 2011.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] An uncommon characteristic of Austin's climate is its highly variable humidity, which fluctuates frequently depending on the shifting patterns of air flow and wind direction. It is common for a lengthy series of warm, dry, low-humidity days to be occasionally interrupted by very warm and humid days, and vice versa. Humidity rises with winds from the east or southeast, when the air drifts inland from the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), but decreases significantly with winds from the west or southwest, bringing air flowing from [Chihuahuan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuahuan_Desert) areas of [West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas) or Northern Mexico.[^cite_note-NWSC-NOAA-95]

Winters in Austin are mild, although occasional short-lived bursts of cold weather known as "[Blue Northers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Norther)" can occur. January is the coolest month with an average daytime high of 62.5 °F (17 °C). The overnight low drops to or below freezing 12 times per year,[^cite_note-NWSC-NOAA-95] and sinks below 45 °F (7 °C) during 76 evenings per year, mostly between mid-December and mid-February. The average first and last dates for a freeze are December 1 and February 15, giving Austin an average growing season of 288 days, and the coldest temperature of the year is normally about 24.2 °F (−4 °C) under the 1991-2020 climate normals, putting Austin in USDA zone 9a.[^cite_note-NWSC-NOAA-95]

Conversely, winter months also produce warm days on a regular basis. On average, 10 days in January reach or exceed 70 °F (21 °C) and 1 day reaches 80 °F (27 °C); during the 1991-2020 period, all Januarys had at least 1 day with a high of 70 °F (21 °C) or more, and most (60%) had at least 1 day with a high of 80 °F (27 °C) or more.[^cite_note-NWSC-NOAA-95] The lowest ever recorded temperature in the city was −2 °F (−19 °C) on January 31, 1949. Roughly every two years Austin experiences an [ice storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_storm) that freezes roads over and cripples travel in the city for 24 to 48 hours.[^cite_note-98] When Austin received 0.04 inches (1 mm) of ice on January 24, 2014, there were 278 vehicular collisions.[^cite_note-99] Similarly, snowfall is rare in Austin.[^cite_note-100] A snow event of 0.9 inches (2 cm) on February 4, 2011, caused more than 300 car crashes.[^cite_note-101] The most recent major snow event occurred February 14–15, 2021, when 6.4 inches (16 cm) of snow fell at Austin's [Camp Mabry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Mabry), the largest two-day snowfall since records began being kept in 1948.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

Typical of [Central Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Texas), severe weather in Austin is a threat that can strike during any season. However, it is most common during the spring. According to most classifications, Austin lies within the extreme southern periphery of [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley), although many sources place Austin outside of Tornado Alley altogether.[^cite_note-Data_Foundry:_Austin_Risk_Assessment-104] Consequently, tornadoes strike Austin less frequently than areas farther to the north.[^cite_note-Data_Foundry:_Austin_Risk_Assessment-104] However, severe weather and/or [supercell thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercell) can occur multiple times per year, bringing damaging winds, lightning, heavy rain, and occasional flash flooding to the city.[^cite_note-105] The deadliest storm to ever strike city limits was the [twin tornadoes storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_Austin_twin_tornadoes) of May 4, 1922, while the deadliest [tornado outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_outbreak) to ever strike the metro area was the [Central Texas tornado outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Central_Texas_tornado_outbreak) of May 27, 1997.

### Natural disasters

#### 2011 drought

Main article: [2011 Southern US drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Southern_US_drought)



The 2011 Texas drought dried up many of central Texas' waterways. This boat was left to sit in the middle of what is normally a branch of [Lake Travis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Travis), part of the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(Texas\)).

From October 2010 through September 2011, both major reporting stations in Austin, Camp Mabry and Bergstrom Int'l, had the least rainfall of a [water year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_year) on record, receiving less than a third of normal precipitation.[^cite_note-NWSC-NOAA-95] This was a result of [La Niña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ni%C3%B1a) conditions in the eastern Pacific Ocean where water was significantly cooler than normal. David Brown, a regional official with the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, explained that "these kinds of droughts will have effects that are even more extreme in the future, given a warming and drying regional climate."[^cite_note-112] The drought, coupled with exceedingly high temperatures throughout the summer of 2011, caused many wildfires throughout [Texas](29810.md), including notably the [Bastrop County Complex Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastrop_County_Complex_Fire) in neighboring Bastrop, Texas.

#### 2018 flooding and water crisis

Main article: [Llano River § 2018 Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llano_River#2018_Flood)

In the fall of 2018, Austin and surrounding areas received heavy rainfall and [flash flooding](244391.md) following [Hurricane Sergio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sergio_\(2018\)).[^cite_note-rainfall-113] The [Lower Colorado River Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Colorado_River_Authority) opened four [floodgates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodgates) of the [Mansfield Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_Dam) after Lake Travis was recorded at 146% full at 704.3 feet (214.7 m).[^cite_note-114] From October 22 to October 29, 2018, the City of Austin issued a mandatory citywide [boil-water advisory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boil-water_advisory) after the [Highland Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Highland_Lakes), home to the city's main water supply, became overwhelmed by unprecedented amounts of silt, dirt, and debris that had washed in from the [Llano River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llano_River).[^cite_note-115] Austin Water, the city's water utility, has the capacity to process up to 300 million gallons of water per day; however, the elevated level of [turbidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbidity) reduced output to only 105 million gallons per day. Since Austin residents consumed an average of 120 million gallons of water per day, the infrastructure was not able to keep up with demand.[^cite_note-rainfall-113]

#### 2021 winter storm

Main article: [February 13–17, 2021 North American winter storm § Central and Southern Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_13%E2%80%9317,_2021_North_American_winter_storm#Central_and_Southern_Plains)

See also: [2021 Texas power crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Texas_power_crisis)



Austin covered in snow on February 15, 2021. Photo from [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESA).

In February 2021, [Winter Storm Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Storm_Uri) dropped prolific amounts of snow across Texas and Oklahoma, including Austin. The Austin area received a total of 6.4 inches (160 mm) of snowfall between February 14 and 15, with snow cover persisting until February 20.[^cite_note-116] This marked the longest time the area had had more than 1 inch (25 mm) of snow, with the previous longest time being three days in [January 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_North_American_cold_wave).[^cite_note-117]

Lack of [winterization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterization) in [natural gas power plants](12438990.md), which supply a large amount of power to the [Texas grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Interconnection), and increased energy demand caused ERCOT and [Austin Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Energy) to enact [rolling blackouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_blackouts) in order to avoid total grid collapse between February 15 and February 18.[^cite_note-118] Initial rolling blackouts were to last for a maximum of 40 minutes, however lack of energy production caused many blackouts to last for much longer, at the peak of the blackouts an estimated 40% of Austin Energy homes were without power.[^cite_note-119]

Starting on February 15, Austin Water received reports of pipe breaks, hourly water demand increased from 150 million gallons per day on February 15 to a peak hourly demand of 260 million gallons per day on February 16. On the morning of February 17 demand increased to 330 million gallons per day, the resulting drop of water pressure caused the Austin area to enter into a [boil-water advisory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boil-water_advisory) which would last until water pressure was restored on February 23.[^cite_note-120]

#### 2023 winter storm

Main article: [January – February, 2023 ice storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_%E2%80%93_February,_2023_ice_storm)

Beginning January 30, 2023 the City of Austin experienced a winter freeze which left 170,000 Austin Energy customers without electricity or heat for several days. The slow pace of repairs and lack of public information from City officials frustrated many residents.[^cite_note-121] A week after the freeze and when Austin City Council members were proposing to evaluate his employment, City Manager Spencer Cronk finally apologized.[^cite_note-122] On Thursday February 16, 2023, Cronk was fired by the Austin City Council for the city's response to the winter storm.[^cite_note-123] Former City Manager Jesus Garcia was named Interim City Manager.[^cite_note-124]

### Parks

The Austin Parks and Recreation Department received the Excellence in Aquatics award in 1999 and the Gold Medal Awards in 2004 from the [National Recreation and Park Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Recreation_and_Park_Association).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

To strengthen the region's parks system, which spans more than 29,000 acres (11,736 ha), The Austin Parks Foundation was established in 1992 to develop and improve parks in and around Austin. APF works to fill the city's park funding gap by leveraging volunteers, philanthropists, park advocates, and strategic collaborations to develop, maintain and enhance Austin's parks, trails and green spaces.

[Lady Bird Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Lake) (formerly Town Lake)[^cite_note-127] is a river-like reservoir on the Colorado River. The lake is a popular recreational area for [paddleboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddleboarding), [kayaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayak), [canoes](99960.md), [dragon boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_boat), and [rowing shells](25736.md). Austin's warm climate and the river's calm waters, nearly 6 miles (9.7 km) length and straight courses are especially popular with [crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_rowing) teams and clubs. Other recreational attractions along the shores of the lake include swimming in [Deep Eddy Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Eddy_Pool), the oldest swimming pool in Texas, and Red Bud Isle, a small island formed by the 1900 [collapse of the McDonald Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Dam_failure_\(Texas\)) that serves as a recreation area with a dog park and access to the lake for canoeing and fishing.[^cite_note-128] The 10.1 miles (16.3 km) Ann and Roy Butler Hike and Bike Trail forms a complete circuit around the lake. A local nonprofit, The Trail Foundation, is the Trail's private steward and has built amenities and infrastructure including trailheads, lakefront gathering areas, restrooms, exercise equipment, as well as doing Trailwide [ecological restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_restoration) work on an ongoing basis. The Butler Trail loop was completed in 2014 with the public-private partnership 1-mile Boardwalk project.[^cite_note-129]

Along the shores of Lady Bird Lake is the 350 acres (140 hectares) [Zilker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilker_Park), which contains large open lawns, sports fields, cross country courses, historical markers, concession stands, and picnic areas.[^cite_note-130] Zilker Park is also home to numerous attractions, including the [Zilker Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilker_Botanical_Garden), the [Umlauf Sculpture Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umlauf_Sculpture_Garden), Zilker Hillside Theater, the Austin Nature & Science Center, and the Zilker Zephyr, a 12 in (305 mm) gauge [miniature railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridable_miniature_railway) carries passengers on a tour around the park.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] [Auditorium Shores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditorium_Shores), an [urban park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_park) along the lake, is home to the [Palmer Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmer_Auditorium), the [Long Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts), and an [off-leash dog park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_park) on the water.[^cite_note-133] Both Zilker Park and Auditorium Shores have a direct view of the Downtown skyline.[^cite_note-134]

### Barton Creek Greenbelt

The [Barton Creek Greenbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Creek_Greenbelt) is a 7.25-mile (11.67 km) public [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt) managed by the City of Austin's Park and Recreation Department. The Greenbelt, which begins at [Zilker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilker_Park) and stretches South/Southwest to the Woods of Westlake [subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivision_\(land\)), is characterized by large [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) cliffs, dense foliage, and shallow bodies of water. Popular activities include [rock climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_climbing), mountain biking, and hiking. Some well known naturally forming swimming holes along Austin's greenbelt include Twin Falls, Sculpture Falls, Gus Fruh Pool, and Campbell's Hole. During years of heavy rainfall, the water level of the creek rises high enough to allow [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_swimming), [cliff diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_diving), [kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaking), [paddle boarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddleboarding), and [tubing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubing_\(recreation\)).

### Swimming holes

Austin is home to more than 50 public pools and [swimming holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_hole). These include [Deep Eddy Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Eddy_Pool), Texas' oldest human-made swimming pool, and [Barton Springs Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Springs_Pool), the nation's largest natural swimming pool in an urban area.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] Barton Springs Pool is spring-fed while Deep Eddy is well-fed. Both range in temperature from about 68.0 °F (20.0 °C) during the winter to about 71.6 °F (22.0 °C) during the summer.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] [Hippie Hollow Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie_Hollow_Park), a county park situated along Lake Travis, is the only officially sanctioned [clothing-optional public park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nude_beach) in Texas. [Hamilton Pool Preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Pool_Preserve) is a natural pool that was created when the dome of an underground river collapsed due to massive erosion thousands of years ago. The pool, located about 23 miles (37 km) west of Austin, is a popular summer swimming spot for visitors and residents. Hamilton Pool Preserve consists of 232 acres (0.94 km<sup>2</sup>) of protected natural habitat featuring a jade green pool into which a 50-foot (15 m) waterfall flows.[^cite_note-140]

#### Other parks

In May 2021, voters in the City of Austin reinstated a public camping ban.[^cite_note-141] That includes downtown green spaces as well as trails and greenbelts such as along Barton Creek.[^cite_note-143]

[McKinney Falls State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinney_Falls_State_Park) is a [state park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_park) administered by the [Texas Parks and Wildlife Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Parks_and_Wildlife_Department), located at the confluence of [Onion Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion_Creek_\(Texas\)) and Williamson Creek. The park includes several designated hiking trails and [campsites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campground) with water and electric. The namesake features of the park are the scenic upper and lower falls along Onion Creek. The [Emma Long Metropolitan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Long_Metropolitan_Park) is a municipal park along the shores of [Lake Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Austin), originally constructed by the [Civilian Conservation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Conservation_Corps). The 284 acres (115 ha) [Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Johnson_Wildflower_Center) is a [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) and [arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum) that features more than 800 species of native Texas plants in both garden and natural settings; the Wildflower Center is located 10 miles (16 km) southwest of Downtown in [Circle C Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_C_Ranch).[^cite_note-144] Roy G. Guerrero Park is located along the Colorado River in [East Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Riverside-Oltorf,_Austin,_Texas) and contains miles of wooded trails, a sandy beach along the river, and a [disc golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_golf) course.

Covert Park, located on the top of [Mount Bonnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Bonnell), is a popular tourist destination overlooking Lake Austin and the Colorado River. The mount provides a vista for viewing the city of Austin, Lake Austin, and the surrounding hills.[^cite_note-145] It was designated a [Recorded Texas Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_Texas_Historic_Landmark) in 1969, bearing Marker number 6473,[^cite_note-146] and was listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in 2015.

The [Louis René Barrera Indiangrass Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Ren%C3%A9_Barrera_Indiangrass_Wildlife_Sanctuary), located on the north shore of Lake Walter E. Long, is a park managed by the Austin Parks and Recreation Department with the goal of restoring the [Blackland Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackland_Prairie). While not open to the public, it is accessible through guided tours.

In the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Austin had a population of 961,855. The median age was 33.0 years. 19.4% of residents were under the age of 18 and 9.5% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 102.0 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 101.4 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-148][^cite_note-Census2020PL-149]

99.5% of residents lived in urban areas, while 0.5% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-150]

There were 410,868 households in Austin, of which 24.9% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 35.0% were married-couple households, 26.5% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 29.0% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 35.3% of all households were made up of individuals and 6.1% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-148]

There were 444,426 housing units, of which 7.6% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 41.2% were owner-occupied and 58.8% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 1.2% and the rental vacancy rate was 7.8%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-148]

The 2000 census found that the median household income was [US\$](18717338.md)42,689. The median family income was \$54,091. Males had a median income of \$35,545 compared to \$30,046 for females. The per capita income for the city was \$24,163. About 9.1% of families and 14.4% of the population were below the poverty line, including 16.5% of those under age 18 and 8.7% of those age 65 or over. The median house price was \$185,906 in 2009, and it has increased every year since 2004.[^cite_note-151] The median value of a house which the owner occupies was \$318,400 in 2019—higher than the average American home value of \$240,500.[^cite_note-152]

### Religion

In 2018, 52.4% of Austin's population identified as religious.[^cite_note-TXReligion-163] The majority of Austinites identified themselves as [Christians](5211.md); about 25.2% of the population claimed affiliation with the [Catholic Church](606848.md).[^cite_note-TXReligion-163] The city's Catholic population is served by the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Austin), headquartered at the [Cathedral of Saint Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_\(Austin,_Texas\)). Nationwide, 23% of Americans identified as Catholic in 2016.[^cite_note-164]

Other significant Christian groups in Austin include [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists) (8.7%), followed by [Methodists](20119.md) (4.3%), [Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (1.5%), [Episcopalians or Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) (1.0%), [Lutherans](23371382.md) (0.8%), [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism) (0.6%), [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism) (0.3%), and other Christians such as the [Disciples of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church_\(Disciples_of_Christ\)) and [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md) (7.1%).[^cite_note-TXReligion-163]

In 2018, 1.7% of Austin's population were Muslims, when 0.8% of Americans nationwide claimed affiliation with the Islamic faith.[^cite_note-TXReligion-163] The dominant branch of Islam is [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam). Established in 1977, the largest [mosque](19894.md) in Austin is the [Islamic Center of Greater Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Center_of_Greater_Austin). The community is affiliated with the [Islamic Society of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Society_of_North_America). In 2018, [Judaism](15624.md) formed less than 0.1% of the religious demographic in Austin. [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism), [Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Judaism), and [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Judaism) congregations are present in the community.[^cite_note-TXReligion-163]

[Eastern faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_religions) including [Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) made up 0.9% of Austin's religious population.[^cite_note-TXReligion-163] Several Hindu temples exist in the Austin Metropolitan area with the most notable one being [Radha Madhav Dham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radha_Madhav_Dham). Austin is also home to an active secular humanist community, hosting nationwide television shows and charity work.[^cite_note-165]

### Homelessness

In 2019, there were 2,255 individuals experiencing homelessness in Travis County. Of those, 1,169 were sheltered and 1,086 were unsheltered.[^cite_note-166] In September 2019, the Austin City Council approved \$62.7 million for programs aimed at homelessness, which includes housing displacement prevention, crisis mitigation, and [affordable housing](3531662.md). The city council also earmarked \$500,000 for crisis services and encampment cleanups.[^cite_note-167]

In June 2019, following *[Martin v. Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_v._Boise)*, a federal court ruling on homelessness sleeping in public,[^cite_note-168] the Austin City Council lifted a 25-year-old ban on camping, sitting, or lying down in public unless doing so causes an obstruction. The resolution also included the approval of a new housing-focused shelter in South Austin.[^cite_note-169] In early October 2019, Texas Governor [Greg Abbott](1492735.md) sent a letter to Mayor Steve Adler threatening to deploy state resources to combat the camping ban repeal.[^cite_note-Bova-170] On October 17, 2019, the City Council revised the camping ordinance, which imposed increased restrictions on sidewalk camping.[^cite_note-Bova-170] In November 2019, the State of Texas opened a temporary homeless encampment on a former vehicle storage yard owned by the [Texas Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Department_of_Transportation).[^cite_note-171]

In May 2021, the camping ban was reinstated after a [ballot proposition](51582.md) was approved by 57% of voters. The ban introduces penalties for camping, sitting, or lying down on a public sidewalk or sleeping outdoors in or near Downtown Austin or the area around the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) campus. The ordinance also prohibits solicitation at certain locations.[^cite_note-172]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

In 2009, 72.7% of Austin (city) commuters drove alone, with other [mode shares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_share) being: 10.4% carpool, 6% were [remote workers](158555.md), 5% use transit, 2.3% walk, and 1% bicycle.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] In 2016, the [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey) estimated modal shares for Austin (city) commuters of 73.5% for driving alone, 9.6% for carpooling, 3.6% for riding transit, 2% for walking, and 1.5% for cycling.[^cite_note-183] The city of Austin has a lower than average percentage of households without a car. In 2015, 6.9 percent of Austin households lacked a car, and decreased slightly to 6 percent in 2016. The national average was 8.7 percent in 2016. Austin averaged 1.65 cars per household in 2016, compared to a national average of 1.8.[^cite_note-184]

#### Highways



The [Pennybacker Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennybacker_Bridge) is the signature element of [Loop 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Loop_360) in the [Texas Hill Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Hill_Country).

Central Austin lies between two major north–south freeways: [I-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35_in_Texas) to the east and the [Mopac Expressway (Loop 1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_Loop_1) to the west. [US 183](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_183_in_Texas) runs from northwest to southeast, and [SH 71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_71) crosses the southern part of the city from east to west, completing a rough "box" around central and north-central Austin. Austin is the largest city in the United States to be served by only one Interstate Highway.

[US 290](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_290) enters Austin from the east and merges into I-35. Its highway designation continues south on I-35 and then becomes part of SH 71, continuing to the west. US 290 splits from Highway 71 in southwest Austin, in an interchange known as "The Y". SH 71 continues to [Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady,_Texas), and Highway 290 continues west to interchange for [I-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_10_in_Texas) near [Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction,_Texas). I-35 continues south through [San Antonio](53848.md) to [Laredo](136773.md) on the Mexican border. I-35 is the highway link to the Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex in Northern Texas. There are two links to Houston (US 290 and SH 71/I-10). US 183 leads northwest of Austin toward [Lampasas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampasas,_Texas).

In the mid-1980s, construction was completed on [Loop 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_Loop_360), a scenic highway that curves through the hill country from near the 71/Mopac interchange in the south to near the US 183/Mopac interchange in the north. The iconic [Pennybacker Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennybacker_Bridge), also known as the "360 Bridge," crosses Lake Austin to connect the northern and southern portions of Loop 360.

#### Tollways



Interchange of [Interstate 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35) and [State Highway 45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Highway_45_\(Texas\))

[SH 130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_130) is a bypass route designed to relieve [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion), starting from I-35 just north of Georgetown and running along a parallel route to the east, where it bypasses [Round Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Rock,_Texas), Austin, [San Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marcos,_Texas) and [New Braunfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Braunfels,_Texas) before ending at [I-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_10_in_Texas) east of [Seguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seguin,_Texas), where drivers could drive 30 miles (48 km) west to return to I-35 in [San Antonio](53848.md). The first segment was opened in November 2006, which was located east of [Austin–Bergstrom International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin%E2%80%93Bergstrom_International_Airport) at Austin's southeast corner on [SH 71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_71). Highway 130 runs concurrently with SH 45 from [Pflugerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pflugerville,_Texas) on the north until it reaches [US 183](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_183_in_Texas) well south of Austin, at which point [SR 45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_45) continues west. The entire route of SH 130 is now complete. The final leg opened on November 1, 2012. The highway is noted for having a maximum speed limit of 85 mph (137 km/h) for the entire route. The 41-mile (66 km) section of the toll road between Mustang Ridge and Seguin has a posted speed limit of 85 mph (137 km/h), the highest posted [speed limit in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_limits_in_the_United_States).

[SH 45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_45) runs east–west from just south of US 183 in [Cedar Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Park,_Texas) to 130 inside [Pflugerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pflugerville,_Texas) (just east of Round Rock). A tolled extension of State Highway Loop 1 was also created. A new southeast leg of SH 45 has recently been completed, running from US 183 and the south end of Segment 5 of TX-130 south of Austin due west to I-35 at the [FM 1327](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Farm_to_Market_Roads_in_Texas#FM_1327)/Creedmoor Road exit between the south end of Austin and [Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda,_Texas). The 183A Toll Road opened in March 2007, providing a tolled alternative to US 183 through the cities of [Leander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leander,_Texas) and Cedar Park. Currently under construction is a change to East US 290 from US 183 to the town of Manor. Officially, the tollway will be dubbed Tollway 290 with "Manor Expressway" as nickname.

Despite the overwhelming initial opposition to the toll road concept when it was first announced, all three toll roads have exceeded revenue projections.[^cite_note-Wear_2009-185]

#### Airports



The Barbara Jordan Terminal at [Austin–Bergstrom International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin%E2%80%93Bergstrom_International_Airport)

Austin's primary airport is [Austin–Bergstrom International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin%E2%80%93Bergstrom_International_Airport) (ABIA) ([IATA code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_by_IATA_code:_A#AU) **AUS**), located 5 miles (8 km) southeast of the city. The airport is on the site of the former [Bergstrom Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergstrom_Air_Force_Base), which was closed in 1993 as part of the [Base Realignment and Closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_Realignment_and_Closure) process. Until 1999, [Robert Mueller Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mueller_Municipal_Airport) was Austin's main airport until ABIA took that role and the old airport was shut down.[^cite_note-LA_Times_Closed-186] [Austin Executive Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Executive_Airport), along with several smaller airports outside the city center, serves general aviation traffic.

#### Public transportation

See also: [CapMetro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro)



[CapMetro Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro_Rail) train at [Downtown station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_station_\(CapMetro_Rail\))

The Capital Metropolitan Transportation Authority ([CapMetro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro)) provides public transportation to the city, primarily with its [CapMetro Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro_Bus) local bus service, the [CapMetro Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro_Bus#CapMetro_Express) express bus system, as well as a [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) service, [CapMetro Rapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro_Rapid). CapMetro opened a 32-mile (51 km) [hybrid rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail_in_the_United_States#%22Hybrid_rail_systems%22) system, [CapMetro Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro_Rail), in 2010.[^cite_note-Cap_Metro_All_Systems_Go_2009-192] The system consists of a single line serving downtown Austin, the neighborhoods of East Austin, [North Central Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_Austin), and Northwest Austin plus the suburb of [Leander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leander,_Texas).

Since it began operations in 1985, CapMetro has proposed adding [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) services to its network. Despite support from the City Council, voters rejected light rail proposals in 2000[^cite_note-Clark-Madison_2000-193] and 2014.[^cite_note-Curington_2014-194] However, in 2020, voters approved CapMetro's US\$10 billion transit expansion plan, [Project Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Connect), by a comfortable margin. The plan proposes 2 new light rail lines, an additional bus rapid transit line (which could be converted to light rail in the future), a second commuter rail line, several new MetroRapid lines, more MetroExpress routes, and a number of other infrastructure, technology and service expansion projects.[^cite_note-Cap_Metro_Project_Connect-195]

[Capital Area Rural Transportation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Area_Rural_Transportation_System) connects Austin with outlying suburbs and surrounding rural areas.

#### Ride sharing

Austin is served by several [ride-sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridesharing_company) companies including [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) and [Lyft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyft).[^cite_note-Uber_Austin-196][^cite_note-Lyft_Austin-197][^cite_note-RideAustin-198] On May 9, 2016, Uber and Lyft voluntarily ceased operations in Austin in response to a city ordinance that required ride sharing company drivers to get fingerprint checks, have their vehicles labeled, and not pick up or drop off in certain city lanes.[^cite_note-MacMillan_2016-199][^cite_note-Woodyard_2016-200] Uber and Lyft resumed service in the summer of 2017.[^cite_note-Samuels_2017-201] The city was previously served by [Fasten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasten_\(company\)) until they ceased all operations in the city in March 2018.[^cite_note-Jechow_2018-202]

Austin is also served by Electric Cab of North America's six-passenger [electric cabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_car) that operate on a flexible route from the [Kramer MetroRail Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramer_station) to [Domain Northside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Domain_\(Austin,_Texas\)) and from the [Downtown CapMetro Rail station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_station_\(CapMetro_Rail\)) and MetroRapid stops to locations between the [Austin Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Convention_Center) and near Sixth and Bowie streets by [Whole Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Foods_Market).[^cite_note-Denney_2018-203]

[Carsharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsharing) service [Zipcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipcar) operates in Austin and, until 2019, the city was also served by [Car2Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car2Go) which kept its North American headquarters in the city even after pulling out.[^cite_note-Turner_2019-204]

#### Cycling and walking



The Pfluger Pedestrian Bridge over the Colorado River

The city's bike advocacy organization is Bike Austin.[^cite_note-205] BikeTexas, a state-level advocacy organization, also has its main office in Austin.[^cite_note-206]

[Bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle) are a popular transportation choice among students, faculty, and staff at the University of Texas. According to a survey done at the University of Texas, 57% of commuters bike to campus.[^cite_note-207]

The City of Austin and CapMetro jointly operate [CapMetro Bikeshare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapMetro#Other_services), a public [bike-sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike-sharing) system of electric-assist bikes in central Austin that connects riders to transit stations. CapMetro Bikeshare was formerly known as MetroBike and, before that, Austin BCycle.[^cite_note-208] In 2018, [Lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LimeBike) began offering [dockless bikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dockless_bikes), which do not need to be docked at a designated station.[^cite_note-209]

#### Parking lots

In November 2023, Austin became the largest city in the US that has abolished [parking mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_mandates). It did so to encourage walking, biking, and public transit use, as well as to lower the cost of housing and increase the amount of housing units that can be built in the city. [Portland](23503.md) and [Minneapolis](6097240.md) also took this action.[^cite_note-213]

## Culture



The Museum of the Weird on Sixth Street



The Emma S. Barrientos Mexican American Cultural Center, located on Lady Bird Lake at 600 River Street

"Keep Austin Weird" has been a local [motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motto) for years, featured on bumper stickers and T-shirts. This motto has not only been used in promoting Austin's eccentricity and diversity, but is also meant to bolster support of local independent businesses.[^cite_note-weird-21] According to the 2010 book *[Weird City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weird_City)* the phrase was begun by a local Austin Community College librarian, Red Wassenich, and his wife, Karen Pavelka, who were concerned about Austin's "rapid descent into commercialism and overdevelopment".[^cite_note-Long_2010-17] The slogan has been interpreted many ways since its inception, but remains an important symbol for many Austinites who wish to voice concerns over rapid growth and development. Austin has a long history of vocal citizen resistance to development projects perceived to degrade the environment, or to threaten the natural and cultural landscapes.[^cite_note-Swearingen_2010-214]

According to the [Nielsen Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Company), adults in Austin read and contribute to blogs more than those in any other U.S. metropolitan area and have the highest Internet usage in all of Texas.[^cite_note-Nielsen_Company-215] In 2013, Austin was the most active city on [Reddit](3829005.md), having the largest number of views per capita.[^cite_note-Dunbar_2013-216]

[South Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Congress) is a shopping district stretching down South Congress Avenue from Downtown. This area is home to coffee shops, eccentric stores, restaurants, food trucks, trailers, and festivals. It prides itself on "Keeping Austin Weird," especially with development in the surrounding area(s). Many Austinites attribute its enduring popularity to the unobstructed [view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Capitol_View_Corridors) of the Texas State Capitol.[^cite_note-Patoski_2010-46]

The [Rainey Street Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainey_Street_Historic_District) is a neighborhood in Downtown Austin formerly consisting of [bungalow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungalow) style homes built in the early 20th century. Since the early 2010s, the former [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) residential street has turned into a popular nightlife district. Much of the historic homes have been renovated into hotels, condominiums, bars and restaurants, many of which feature large porches and outdoor yards for patrons.[^cite_note-217] The Rainey Street district is also home to the Emma S. Barrientos Mexican American Cultural Center.

Austin has been part of the [UNESCO Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) under the category [Media Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_arts).[^cite_note-218]

### Old Austin



Austin, Texas average monthly rent, 2010 to 2022

"Old Austin" is an [adage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adage) often used by [nostalgic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostalgic) natives.[^cite_note-219] The term "Old Austin" refers to a time when the city was smaller and more [bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemianism) with a considerably lower cost of living and better known for its lack of traffic, [hipsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipster_\(contemporary_subculture\)), and [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl).[^cite_note-220] It is often employed by longtime residents expressing displeasure at the rapidly changing culture,[^cite_note-221] or when referencing nostalgia of Austin culture.[^cite_note-222]

The growth and popularity of Austin[^cite_note-223] can be seen by the expansive development taking place in its downtown landscape.[^cite_note-224] This growth can have a negative impact on longtime small businesses that cannot keep up with the expenses associated with [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) and the rising cost of real estate.[^cite_note-225] A former Austin musician, [Dale Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Watson_\(singer\)), described his move away from Austin, "I just really feel the city has sold itself. Just because you're going to get \$45 million for a company to come to town – if it's not in the best interest of the town, I don't think they should do it. This city was never about money. It was about quality of life."[^cite_note-226]

Though much is changing rapidly in Austin, businesses such as Thundercloud Subs are thought by many to maintain classic Austin business cultural sentiments unique to the history of the city; as Diana Burgess stated, "I definitely appreciate that they haven't raised their prices a ton or made things super fancy. I think it speaks to that original Old Austin vibe. A lot of us that grew up here really appreciate that."[^cite_note-227] Aaron Franklin, owner of [Franklin Barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Barbecue), credited the Old Austin cultural mindset and community support with the success of his barbecue restaurant and the long lines that have supported his business since starting it out of a food trailer in 2009.[^cite_note-228]

### Annual cultural events

[Sixth Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Street_\(Austin,_Texas\)) on a weekend night

The [O. Henry House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sidney_Porter_House) Museum hosts the annual [O. Henry Pun-Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Henry_Pun-Off), a pun contest where the successful contestants exhibit wit akin to that of the author [William Sydney Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sydney_Porter).

Other annual events include [Eeyore's Birthday Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eeyore's_Birthday_Party), [Spamarama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spamarama), Austin [Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_pride) Festival & Parade in August, the Austin Reggae Festival in April,[^cite_note-229] Kite Festival, Texas Craft Brewers Festival in September,[^cite_note-230] Art City Austin in April,[^cite_note-231] East Austin Studio Tour in November,[^cite_note-232] and [Carnaval Brasileiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnaval_Brasileiro_in_Austin,_Texas) in February. Sixth Street features annual festivals such as the [Pecan Street Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecan_Street_Festival) and Halloween night. The three-day [Austin City Limits Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits_Music_Festival) has been held in [Zilker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilker_Park) every year since 2002. Every year around the end of March and the beginning of April, Austin is home to "Texas Relay Weekend".

Austin's Zilker Park Tree is a Christmas display made of lights strung from the top of a [Moonlight tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonlight_tower) in Zilker Park. The Zilker Tree is lit in December along with the "Trail of Lights," an Austin Christmas tradition.[^cite_note-233] The Trail of Lights was canceled four times, first starting in 2001 and 2002 due to the September 11 Attacks, and again in 2010 and 2011 due to budget shortfalls, but the trail was turned back on for the 2012 holiday season.[^cite_note-234]

From 1962 to 1998, the [Austin Aqua Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Aqua_Festival), or "Aqua Fest", took place on the shores of Town Lake (now known as [Lady Bird Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Lake)). Originally conceived as a summer tourism draw, the multi-day event evolved from water-themed activities to a broader civic festival due to growth and community interest. Eventually attendance and financial solvency began to dwindle as larger music and summer festivals grew in prominence.

### Cuisine and breweries



A food truck trailer park in South Austin

Notable Austin cuisine includes [Texas barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_barbecue) and [Tex-Mex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tex-Mex); [Franklin Barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Barbecue) in Austin's has sold out of [brisket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisket) every day since its establishment.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] [Breakfast tacos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_taco) and [queso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_con_queso) are popular food items in the city; Austin is sometimes called the "home of the breakfast taco".[^cite_note-237] [Kolaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolach_\(cake\)) are a common pastry in Austin bakeries due to the large [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Americans) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) immigrant population in Texas.[^cite_note-238] The [Oasis Restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis_Restaurant) is the largest outdoor restaurant in Texas, which promotes itself as the "Sunset Capital of Texas" with its terraced views looking West over [Lake Travis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Travis).[^cite_note-239] [Birdie's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdie's), a [counter-service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_service) restaurant and wine bar that opened in 2021, was *Food & Wine*'s 2023 Restaurant of the Year.[^cite_note-\:0-240][^cite_note-\:6-241][^cite_note-\:3-242]

[P. Terry's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._Terry's), an Austin-based fast food burger chain, has a loyal following among Austinites.[^cite_note-243] Some other Austin-based chain restaurants include [Amy's Ice Creams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy's_Ice_Creams), [Chuy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuy's), [DoubleDave's Pizzaworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoubleDave's_Pizzaworks), and [Schlotzky's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlotzky's). The [Chili's at 45th and Lamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili's_at_45th_and_Lamar) has been the subject of internet memes since 2011.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-statesman-245][^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]

Austin is also home to a large number of [food trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_trucks), with 1,256 food trucks operating in 2016.[^cite_note-FoodTrucks-248] The city of Austin has the second-largest number of food trucks per capita in the United States.[^cite_note-FoodTrucks-248] Austin's first [food hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_hall), "Fareground," features a number of Austin-based food vendors and a bar in the ground level and courtyard of [One Congress Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Congress_Plaza).[^cite_note-249]

Austin has a large [craft beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_beer) scene, with over 50 [microbreweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbreweries) in the metro area.[^cite_note-250] Notable Austin-area breweries include [Jester King Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jester_King_Brewery), [Live Oak Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Oak_Brewing_Company), and [Real Ale Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Ale_Brewing_Company).

### Music

Main article: [Music of Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Austin)



The 2009 [Austin City Limits Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits_Music_Festival) with view of stages and Downtown Austin

The official slogan of Austin is "*The Live Music Capital of the World"*, and the city has a vibrant live [music scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Austin) with more music venues per capita than any other U.S. city.[^cite_note-motto-18][^cite_note-atx_motto-19] Austin's music revolves around the many [nightclubs](19321330.md) on 6th Street and an annual music/film/[interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive) festival known as [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest) (SXSW). The concentration of restaurants, bars, and music venues in the city's downtown core is a major contributor to Austin's live music scene, as the ZIP Code encompassing the downtown entertainment district hosts the most bar or alcohol-serving establishments in the U.S.[^cite_note-251]

The longest-running concert music program on American television, *[Austin City Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits)*, is recorded at [ACL Live at The Moody Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACL_Live_at_The_Moody_Theater), located in the bottom floor of the 478 feet (146 m) [W Hotels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_Hotels) in Austin.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] *Austin City Limits* and [C3 Presents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C3_Presents) produce the [Austin City Limits Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits_Music_Festival), an annual music and art festival held at [Zilker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilker_Park) in Austin. Other music events include the Urban Music Festival, [Fun Fun Fun Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fun_Fun_Fun_Fest), [Wobeon Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wobeon_Music_Festival), Chaos In Tejas, Seismic Music Festival at the Concourse Project, and [Old Settler's Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Settler's_Music_Festival). [Austin Lyric Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Lyric_Opera) performs multiple operas each year, including the 2007 opening of [Philip Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Glass)'s *[Waiting for the Barbarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waiting_for_the_Barbarians_\(opera\))*, written by University of Texas at Austin alumnus [J. M. Coetzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._M._Coetzee).[^cite_note-Rossie_2009-254]

The [Austin Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Symphony_Orchestra) performs a range of classical, pop and family performances and is led by music director and conductor [Peter Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bay). The Austin Baroque Orchestra and La Follia Austin Baroque ensembles both give historically informed performances of [Baroque music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music). The [Texas Early Music Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Early_Music_Project) regularly performs music from the [Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_music) and [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_music) eras, as well as the Baroque.

### Theater



The [State Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Theatre_\(Austin,_Texas\)) and [Paramount Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Theatre_\(Austin,_Texas\)) on Congress Avenue in Downtown Austin

Austin has a strong theater culture, with dozens of itinerant and resident companies producing a variety of work. A volunteer-run arts organization supporting creative expression and counter-culture community - Church of the Friendly Ghost (COTFG) helped many experimental programs get their start in Austin, TX. The city also has live performance theater venues such as the [Zachary Scott Theatre Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachary_Scott_Theatre_Center), Vortex Repertory Company, Salvage Vanguard Theater, Rude Mechanicals' the Off Center, Austin Playhouse, Scottish Rite Children's Theater, [Hyde Park Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park_Theatre), the Blue Theater, [The Hideout Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hideout_Theatre), and [Esther's Follies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther's_Follies).[^cite_note-258] The [Victory Grill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Grill) was a renowned venue on the [Chitlin' Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitlin'_Circuit).[^cite_note-259] Public art and performances in the parks and on bridges are popular. Austin hosts the Fuse Box Festival each April featuring theater artists.[^cite_note-260]

The [Paramount Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Theatre_\(Austin,_Texas\)), opened in downtown Austin in 1915, contributes to Austin's theater and film culture, showing classic films throughout the summer and hosting regional premieres for films such as *[Miss Congeniality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Congeniality_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-261] The neighboring [State Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Theatre_\(Austin,_Texas\)), which opened in 1935, merged with the Paramount in 2000 to form the Austin Theatre Alliance, the nonprofit organization that operates both venues.[^cite_note-AustinTheatreHistory-262] The [Zilker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilker_Park) Summer Musical is a long-running outdoor musical.[^cite_note-263]

The [Long Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts) is a 2,300-seat theater built partly with materials reused from the old Lester E. Palmer Auditorium.

[Ballet Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_Austin) is among the fifteen largest ballet academies in the country.[^cite_note-264] Each year Ballet Austin's 20-member professional company performs ballets from a wide variety of choreographers, including their artistic director, [Stephen Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Mills). The city is also home to the [Ballet East Dance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_East_Dance_Company), a modern dance ensemble, and the [Tapestry Dance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapestry_Dance_Company) which performs a variety of dance genres.

The Austin [Improvisational theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisational_theatre) scene has several theaters: ColdTowne Theater, The Hideout Theater, and The Fallout Theater. Austin also hosts the [Out of Bounds Comedy Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_Bounds_Comedy_Festival), which draws comedic artists in all disciplines to Austin. Earlier Austin improv festivals included the [Big Stinkin' International Improv & Sketch Comedy Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Stinkin'_International_Improv_&_Sketch_Comedy_Festival), held from 1996 to 1999.[^cite_note-265]

### Libraries



A view of Austin Central Library from César Chávez Street

The [Austin Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Public_Library) is operated by the City of Austin and consists of the Central Library on [César Chávez Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Chavez_Street_\(Austin\)), the [Austin History Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_History_Center), 20 branches and the Recycled Reads bookstore and [upcycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcycling) facility.[^cite_note-266] The APL library system also has mobile libraries – bookmobile buses and a human-powered trike and trailer called "unbound: sin fronteras".[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268]

The Central Library, which is an anchor to the redevelopment of the former [Seaholm Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaholm_Power_Plant) site and the [Shoal Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal_Creek,_Austin,_Texas) Walk, opened on October 28, 2017.[^cite_note-269] The six-story Central Library contains a living [rooftop garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooftop_garden), reading porches, an indoor reading room, [bicycle parking station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_parking_station), large indoor and outdoor event spaces, a gift shop, an art gallery, café, and a "technology petting zoo" where visitors can play with next-generation gadgets like [3D printers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printing).[^cite_note-270] In 2018, [Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\)) magazine named the Austin Central Library on its list of "World's Greatest Places".[^cite_note-271]

### Museums and other points of interest



The [Lyndon Baines Johnson Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Baines_Johnson_Presidential_Library) on the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) campus in Austin

Museums in Austin include the [Texas Science and Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Science_and_Natural_History_Museum), the [George Washington Carver Museum and Cultural Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_Carver_Museum_and_Cultural_Center), [Thinkery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinkery), the [Blanton Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanton_Museum_of_Art) (reopened in 2006), the [Bob Bullock Texas State History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Bullock_Texas_State_History_Museum) across the street (which opened in 2000), [The Contemporary Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Contemporary_Austin), the [Elisabet Ney Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabet_Ney_Museum), the [Women and Their Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_and_Their_Work) gallery, and the galleries at the [Harry Ransom Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Ransom_Center). The [Texas State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Capitol) itself is also a major tourist attraction.

The [Driskill Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driskill_Hotel), built in 1886, once owned by [George W. Littlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Littlefield), and located at 6th and Brazos streets, was finished just before the construction of the Capitol building. [Sixth Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Street_\(Austin\)) is a musical hub for the city. The Enchanted Forest, a multi-acre outdoor music, art, and performance art space in South Austin hosts events such as fire-dancing and circus-like-acts.[^cite_note-272] Austin is also home to the [Lyndon Baines Johnson Library and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Baines_Johnson_Library_and_Museum), which houses documents and artifacts related to the Johnson administration, including LBJ's limousine and a re-creation of the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office).



The [HOPE Outdoor Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOPE_Outdoor_Gallery), overlooked by the historic Texas Military Academy building, the oldest standing educational building in Texas. The gallery has since been demolished.

Locally produced art is featured at the [South Austin Museum of Popular Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Austin_Museum_of_Popular_Culture). The [Mexic-Arte Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexic-Arte_Museum) is a Mexican and Mexican-American art museum founded in 1983. Austin is also home to the [O. Henry Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sydney_Porter_House), which served as the residence of [O. Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Henry) from 1893 to 1895. Farmers' markets are popular attractions, providing a variety of locally grown and often organic foods.[^cite_note-273]

Austin also has many odd statues and landmarks, such as the *[Stevie Ray Vaughan Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Ray_Vaughan_Memorial)*, the [*Willie Nelson* statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson_statue), the Mangia dinosaur, the Loca Maria lady at Taco Xpress, the Hyde Park Gym's giant flexed arm, and [Daniel Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Johnston)'s *[Hi, How are You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi,_How_are_You)?* Jeremiah the Innocent frog mural.[^cite_note-274]

The [Ann W. Richards Congress Avenue Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_W._Richards_Congress_Avenue_Bridge) houses the world's largest urban population of [Mexican free-tailed bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_free-tailed_bat). Starting in March,[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276] up to 1.5 million bats take up residence inside the bridge's expansion and contraction zones as well as in long horizontal grooves running the length of the bridge's underside, an environment ideally suited for raising their young. Every evening around sunset, the bats emerge in search of insects, an exit visible on [weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar). Watching the bat emergence is an event that is popular with locals and tourists, with more than 100,000 viewers per year. The bats migrate to Mexico each winter.[^cite_note-277]

The [Austin Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Zoo), located in [unincorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area) western [Travis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_County,_Texas), is a [rescue zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_coordination_centre) that provides sanctuary to displaced animals from a variety of situations, including those involving neglect.

The HOPE Outdoor Gallery was a public, three-story outdoor [street art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_art) project located on Baylor Street in the [Clarksville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksville,_Austin,_Texas) neighborhood.[^cite_note-278] The gallery, which consisted of the foundations of a failed multifamily development,[^cite_note-279] was a constantly-evolving canvas of [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti) and [murals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murals). Also known as "Castle Hill" or simply "Graffiti Park", the site on Baylor Street was closed to the public in early January 2019 but remained intact, behind a fence and with an armed guard, in mid-March 2019.[^cite_note-280] The gallery will build a new art park at Carson Creek Ranch in Southeast Austin.[^cite_note-281]

During the 2005–2006 academic term, the [Longhorns football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Longhorns_football) was named the [NCAA Division I FBS National Football Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_FBS_National_Football_Championship), and the [Longhorns baseball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Longhorns_baseball) won the [College World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_College_World_Series).[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283] The football team plays its home games in the state's second-largest sports stadium, [Darrell K Royal–Texas Memorial Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrell_K_Royal%E2%80%93Texas_Memorial_Stadium), seating over 101,000 fans.[^cite_note-284] Baseball games are played at [UFCU Disch–Falk Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFCU_Disch%E2%80%93Falk_Field).

Austin was the most populous city in the United States without a [major-league professional sports team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_of_the_United_States_and_Canada), which changed in 2021 with [Austin FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_FC)'s entry to MLS.[^cite_note-285] Minor-league professional sports came to Austin in 1996, when the [Austin Ice Bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Ice_Bats) began playing at the Travis County Expo Center;[^cite_note-286] they were later replaced by the [AHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) [Texas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Stars).[^cite_note-287] Austin has hosted a number of other professional teams, including the [Austin Spurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Spurs) of the [NBA G League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_G_League),[^cite_note-288] the [Austin Aztex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Aztex) of the [United Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_League), the [Austin Outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Outlaws) in [WFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Football_Alliance) football, and the [Austin Aces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Aces) in [WTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_TeamTennis) tennis.

Natural features like the bicycle-friendly [Texas Hill Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Hill_Country) and generally mild [climate](#Climate) make Austin the home of several endurance and multi-sport races and communities. The Capitol 10,000 is the largest 10 k race in Texas, and approximately fifth largest in the United States.[^cite_note-289] The [Austin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Marathon) has been run in the city every year since 1992. Additionally, the city is home to the largest 5 mile race in Texas,[^cite_note-290] named the Turkey Trot as it is run annually on Thanksgiving. Started in 1991 by Thundercloud Subs, a local sandwich chain (who still sponsors the event), the event has grown to host over 20,000 runners. All proceeds are donated to Caritas of Austin, a local charity.

The Austin-founded American Swimming Association hosts several swim races around town. Austin is also the hometown of several cycling groups and the disgraced cyclist [Lance Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Armstrong).[^cite_note-291] Combining these three disciplines is a growing crop of triathlons, including the Capital of Texas Triathlon held every [Memorial Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day) on and around Lady Bird Lake, [Auditorium Shores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditorium_Shores), and [Downtown Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Austin).[^cite_note-292]

Austin is home to the [Circuit of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_of_the_Americas) (COTA), a grade 1 [Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile](248019.md) specification 3.427-mile (5.515 km) [motor racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_racing) facility which hosts the [Formula One](10854.md) [United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-293] The State of Texas has pledged \$25 million in public funds annually for 10 years to pay the sanctioning fees for the race.[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295] Built at an estimated cost of \$250 to \$300 million, the circuit opened in 2012 and is located just east of the [Austin Bergstrom International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Bergstrom_International_Airport).[^cite_note-296][^cite_note-297] The circuit also hosts the [EchoPark Automotive Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Grand_Prix) NASCAR race in late March each year.

The summer of 2014 marked the inaugural season for [World TeamTennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_TeamTennis) team [Austin Aces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Aces), formerly [Orange County Breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Breakers) of the southern California region. The Austin Aces played their matches at the [Cedar Park Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Park_Center) northwest of Austin, and featured former professionals [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick) and [Marion Bartoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Bartoli), as well as current WTA tour player [Vera Zvonareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Zvonareva).[^cite_note-298] The team left after the 2015 season.

In 2017, [Precourt Sports Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Precourt) announced a plan to move the [Columbus Crew SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus_Crew_SC) soccer franchise from [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio) to Austin.[^cite_note-299] Precourt negotiated an agreement with the City of Austin to build a \$200 million privately funded stadium on public land at 10414 McKalla Place,[^cite_note-300] following initial interest in Butler Shores Metropolitan Park and Roy G. Guerrero Colorado River Park.[^cite_note-301] As part of an arrangement with the league, operational rights of Columbus Crew SC were sold in late 2018,[^cite_note-302] and Austin FC was announced as Major League Soccer's 27th franchise on January 15, 2019, with the expansion team starting play in 2021.[^cite_note-303]

The [Austin Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Country_Club) is a private golf club located along the shores of the Colorado River, right next to the [Pennybacker Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennybacker_Bridge). Founded in 1899, the club moved to its third and present site in 1984, which features a challenging layout designed by noted course architect [Pete Dye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Dye).[^cite_note-304][^cite_note-305]

Austin hosted the [BLAST.TV Austin Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_Austin_Major_2025), the 22nd [*Counter-Strike* Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Strike_Major_Championships) [esports](564204.md) tournament, from June 9 to 22, 2025.[^cite_note-306]

## Government

### City government

See also: [List of mayors of Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Austin,_Texas) and [Austin City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Council)



Austin City Hall

Austin is administered by an 11-member [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council) (10 council members elected by geographic district plus a mayor elected at large). The council is accompanied by a hired [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager) under the [manager-council system of municipal governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council-manager_government). Council and mayoral elections are non-partisan, with a runoff in case there is no majority winner. A referendum approved by voters on November 6, 2012, changed the council composition from six council members plus a mayor elected at large to the current "10+1" district system. Supporters maintained that the at-large system would increase participation for all areas of the city, especially for those which had lacked representation from City Council.[^cite_note-307]

November 2014 marked the first election under the new system. The [Federal government](195149.md) had forced San Antonio and Dallas to abandon at-large systems before 1987; however, the court could not show a racist pattern in Austin and upheld the city's at-large system during a 1984 lawsuit. In five elections between 1973 and 1994 Austin voters rejected single-member districts.[^cite_note-308]

Austin formerly operated its city hall at 128 West 8th Street.[^cite_note-309] Antoine Predock and Cotera Kolar Negrete & Reed Architects designed a new city hall building, which was intended to reflect what *[The Dallas Morning News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dallas_Morning_News)* referred to as a "crazy-quilt vitality, that embraces everything from country music to environmental protests and high-tech swagger".[^cite_note-310] The new city hall, built from recycled materials, has solar panels in its garage.[^cite_note-311] The city hall, at 301 West Second Street, opened in November 2004.[^cite_note-312] [Kirk Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_Watson) is the current mayor of Austin, assuming the office for a second non-consecutive term on January 6, 2023.

In the 2012 elections, City Council elections were moved from May to November and City council members were given staggered term limits[^cite_note-313] In 2022 Proposition D moved the term of the Austin Mayor to coincide with Presidential election years, so [Kirk Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_Watson) would only serve two years unlike his predecessor [Steve Adler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Adler_\(politician\))[^cite_note-314]

Law enforcement in Austin is provided by the [Austin Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Police_Department), except for state government buildings, which are patrolled by the [Texas Department of Public Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Department_of_Public_Safety). The University of Texas Police operate from the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin).

Fire protection within the city limits is provided by the [Austin Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Fire_Department), while the surrounding county is divided into twelve geographical areas known as emergency services districts, which are covered by separate regional fire departments.[^cite_note-315] Emergency medical services are provided for the whole county by Austin-Travis County Emergency Medical Services.[^cite_note-316][^cite_note-317]

In 2003, the city adopted a resolution against the [USA PATRIOT Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_PATRIOT_Act) that reaffirmed constitutionally guaranteed rights.

The current mayor of Austin is [Kirk Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_Watson).[^cite_note-318] As of 2025, though positions are officiallly nonpartisan, all elected member of the city council are [Democrats](5043544.md).[^cite_note-319]

### Crime

As of 2019, Austin is one of the safest large cities in the United States.[^cite_note-326] In 2019, the FBI named Austin the 11th safest city on a list of 22 American cities with a population above 400,000.[^cite_note-327]

FBI statistics show that overall violent and property crimes dropped in Austin in 2015, but increased in suburban areas of the city.[^cite_note-328] One such southeastern suburb, [Del Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Valle,_Texas), reported eight homicides within two months in 2016.[^cite_note-329] According to 2016 [APD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Police_Department) crime statistics, the 78723 census tract had the most violent crime, with 6 murders, 25 rapes, and 81 robberies.[^cite_note-330] The city had 39 homicides in 2016, the most since 1997.[^cite_note-331][^cite_note-332]

#### Notable incidents

In 1884 and 1885, one of the earliest recorded [serial killings](42626.md) in the United States occurred in Austin, in which 8 people were murdered by a suspect known as the "[Servant Girl Annihilator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servant_Girl_Annihilator)".[^cite_note-TexasMonthly-333][^cite_note-334][^cite_note-335]

One of the first American [mass](50822291.md) [school shooting](59628.md) incidents took place in Austin on August 1, 1966, when [Charles Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Whitman) shot 43 people, killing 13 from the top of the University of Texas tower.[^cite_note-texasmonthly.com-336] The [University of Texas tower shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_tower_shooting) led to the formation of the [SWAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAT) team of the [Austin Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Police_Department).[^cite_note-texasmonthly.com-336][^cite_note-337][^cite_note-338]

In 1991, [four teenage girls were murdered in a yogurt shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Austin_yogurt_shop_murders) by serial killer [Robert Eugene Brashers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Eugene_Brashers).[^cite_note-cjn3o35-339] A police officer responded to reports of a fire at the [I Can't Believe It's Yogurt!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Can't_Believe_It's_Yogurt!) store on Anderson Lane and discovered the girls' bodies in a back room. The murders were unsolved until 2025.[^cite_note-340]

In 2010, Andrew Joseph Stack III deliberately crashed his [Piper PA-28 Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_PA-28_Cherokee) into Echelon 1, a building in which the [Internal Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service) was a lessee of, housing 190 employees.[^cite_note-341] The resulting explosion killed one and injured 13 IRS employees, partially damaged the building and cost the IRS a total of \$38.6 million.[^cite_note-342] *(see [2010 Austin suicide attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Austin_suicide_attack))*

A [series of bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_serial_bombings) occurred in Austin in March 2018. Over the course of 20 days, five [package bombs](49760.md) exploded, killing two people and injuring another five. The suspect, 23-year-old Mark Anthony Conditt of [Pflugerville, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pflugerville,_Texas), [blew himself up](18835454.md) inside his vehicle after he was pulled over by police on March 21, also injuring a police officer.[^cite_note-time_Aust-343][^cite_note-npr._Mark-344]

In 2020, Austin was the victim of a [cyberattack](37925700.md) by the Russian group [Berserk Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berserk_Bear), possibly related to the [U.S. federal government data breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_federal_government_data_breach) earlier that year.[^cite_note-intercept-2020-12-17-345][^cite_note-346]

On April 18, 2021, a [shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting) occurred at the Arboretum Oaks Apartments near [The Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arboretum_\(Austin,_Texas\)) [shopping center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_center), in which a former [Travis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_County) Sheriff's Office [detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detective) killed his ex-wife, his adoptive daughter, and his daughter's boyfriend.[^cite_note-347][^cite_note-Statesman.shooting-348][^cite_note-349] The suspect, who was previously charged with [child sexual assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_sexual_assault), was arrested in [Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor,_Texas) after a 20-hour manhunt.[^cite_note-CNN.Shooting-350][^cite_note-USATODAY.Shooting-351]

A mass shooting took place in the early morning of June 12, 2021, on [Sixth Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Street_\(Austin,_Texas\)), which resulted in 14 people injured and one dead. The man killed was believed to be an innocent bystander who was struck as he was standing outside a bar. A 19-year-old suspect, De'ondre "Dre" White, was formally charged and arrested in [Killeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killeen,_Texas) nearly two weeks after the shooting.[^cite_note-352][^cite_note-353][^cite_note-354]

In 2024, Zacharia Doar, a 23-year old Palestinian-American man, was attacked and stabbed in the chest on West 26th Street, [West Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Campus,_Austin,_Texas), after returning from a rally in support of Palestinian human rights. The assailant, 36-year old Bert James Baker was arrested at the scene and charged with aggravated assault with a deadly weapon.[^cite_note-355][^cite_note-356]

On August 11, 2025, a [mass shooting](31883778.md) occurred outside a [Target](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Corporation) store in North Austin, killing three people. The suspect fled the Target by [hijacking](362652.md) a [car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car) belonging to one of the victims. After leaving the Target area, the suspect crashed the stolen [vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle) and proceeded to steal another vehicle from a [car dealership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_dealership). #### Issues

A controversial turning point in the political history of the Austin area was the [2003 Texas redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Texas_redistricting). Before then, Austin had been entirely or almost entirely within the borders of a single congressional district–what was then the 10th District–for over a century. Opponents characterized the resulting district layout as excessively partisan [gerrymandering](12987.md), and the plan was challenged in court by Democratic and minority activists. The [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) has never struck down a redistricting plan for being excessively partisan.[^cite_note-366]

In late 2003, the plan was upheld by a three-judge federal panel. In June 2006, the matter was largely settled when the Supreme Court, in a 7–2 decision, upheld the entire congressional redistricting plan with the exception of a Hispanic-majority district in southwest Texas. This affected Austin's districting, as U.S. Rep. [Lloyd Doggett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Doggett)'s district (U.S. Congressional District 25) was found to be insufficiently compact to compensate for the reduced [minority influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_influence) in the southwest district; it was redrawn so that it took in most of southeastern Travis County and several counties to its south and east.[^cite_note-367]

#### Environmental movement

The distinguishing political movement of Austin politics has been that of the [environmental movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_movement), which spawned the parallel neighborhood movement, then the more recent conservationist movement (as typified by the Hill Country Conservancy),[^cite_note-368] and eventually the current ongoing debate about "sense of place" and preserving the Austin quality of life. Much of the environmental movement has matured into a debate on issues related to saving and creating an Austin "sense of place".[^cite_note-Swearingen_2010-214]

In 2012, Austin became just one of a few cities in [Texas](29810.md) to ban the sale and use of plastic bags. The ban ended in 2018 due to a court ruling that regarded all bag bans in the state to contravene the Texas Solid Waste Disposal Act.[^cite_note-Hoffberger-369] In 2016, Austin became the first Gold designee of the [SolSmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SolSmart) program,[^cite_note-370] a national program from the [U.S. Department of Energy](62866.md) that recognizes local governments for enacting solar-friendly measures at the local level.[^cite_note-371][^cite_note-372]

## Education

According to the 2015–2019 Census estimates, 51.7% of Austin residents aged 25 and over have earned at least a [bachelor's degree](188874.md), compared with the national figure of 32.1%. 19.4% hold a [graduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_degree) or [professional degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_degree), compared with the national figure of 12.4%.[^cite_note-373]

### Public primary and secondary education

Approximately half of the city by area is served by the [Austin Independent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Independent_School_District). This district includes notable schools such as the magnet [Liberal Arts and Science Academy High School of Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Arts_and_Science_Academy_High_School_of_Austin,_Texas) (LASA), which, by test scores, has consistently been within the top thirty high schools in the nation, as well as [The Ann Richards School for Young Women Leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ann_Richards_School_for_Young_Women_Leaders). The remaining portion of Austin is served by adjoining school districts, including [Round Rock ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Rock_Independent_School_District), [Pflugerville ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pflugerville_Independent_School_District), [Leander ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leander_Independent_School_District), [Manor ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_Independent_School_District), [Del Valle ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Valle_Independent_School_District), [Lake Travis ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Travis_Independent_School_District), [Hays ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hays_Consolidated_Independent_School_District), and [Eanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eanes_Independent_School_District) [ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_school_district).[^cite_note-375]

The Austin metropolitan area includes 27 [charter school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_school) systems.[^cite_note-austin-chamber.org-376] [University of Texas Elementary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Elementary_School) is in the city.

There are two state-operated schools for children with disabilities: [Texas School for the Blind and Visually Impaired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_School_for_the_Blind_and_Visually_Impaired) and [Texas School for the Deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_School_for_the_Deaf). [Texas Blind, Deaf, and Orphan School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Blind,_Deaf,_and_Orphan_School), in the segregation era, had black deaf and blind students.

## Mass media

See also: [List of newspapers in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Texas), [List of radio stations in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_radio_stations_in_Texas), and [List of television stations in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_stations_in_Texas)

Austin's main daily newspaper is the *[Austin American-Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_American-Statesman)*. *[The Austin Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Austin_Chronicle)* is Austin's [alternative weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_weekly), while *[The Daily Texan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Texan)* is the student newspaper of the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin). Austin's business newspaper is the weekly *[Austin Business Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Business_Journal)*. *The Austin Monitor* is an online outlet that specializes in insider reporting on City Hall, Travis County Commissioners Court, [AISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Independent_School_District), and other related local civics beats. The *Monitor* is backed by the nonprofit Capital of Texas Media Foundation. Austin also has numerous smaller special interest or sub-regional newspapers such as the *[Oak Hill Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Hill_Gazette)*, *Westlake Picayune*, *Hill Country News*, *Round Rock Leader*, *NOKOA*, and *The Villager* among others. *[Texas Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Monthly)*, a major regional magazine, is also headquartered in Austin. [Austin Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Way) is a luxury lifestyle magazine serving Austin's affluent community, published seven times a year by Modern Luxury Media. The *[Texas Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Observer)*, a muckraking biweekly political magazine, has been based in Austin for over five decades. The weekly *[Community Impact Newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Impact_Newspaper)* published by John Garrett, former publisher of the *Austin Business Journal* has five regional editions and is delivered to every house and business within certain ZIP codes and all of the news is specific to those ZIP codes.[^cite_note-377]

Another statewide publication based in Austin is *[The Texas Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Texas_Tribune)*, an on-line publication focused on Texas politics.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-378] The *Tribune* is "user-supported" through donations, a business model similar to public radio.[^cite_note-379] The editor is [Evan Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Smith_\(journalist\)), former editor of *Texas Monthly*. Smith co-founded the *Texas Tribune*, a nonprofit, non-partisan public media organization, with Austin venture capitalist John Thornton and veteran journalist Ross Ramsey.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-378][^cite_note-380]

Commercial radio stations include [KASE-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KASE-FM) ([country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music)), [KVET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVET_\(AM\)) (sports), [KVET-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVET-FM) (country), [KKMJ-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KKMJ-FM) ([adult contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_contemporary)), [KLBJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLBJ_\(AM\)) (talk), [KLBJ-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLBJ-FM) ([classic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_rock)), [KJFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KJFK_\(AM\)) (variety hits), [KFMK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFMK) (contemporary Christian), [KOKE-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOKE-FM) ([progressive country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_country)) and [KPEZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPEZ) ([rhythmic contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythmic_contemporary)). [KUT-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KUT-FM) is the leading [public radio](178808.md) station in Texas and produces the majority of its content locally.[^cite_note-381] [KOOP (FM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOOP_\(FM\)) is a volunteer-run radio station with more than 60 locally produced programs.[^cite_note-382] [KVRX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVRX) is the student-run college radio station of the University of Texas at Austin with a focus on local and non-mainstream music and community programming.[^cite_note-383] Other listener-supported stations include [KAZI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAZI) ([urban contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_contemporary)), and [KMFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMFA) ([classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music)).

Network television stations (affiliations in parentheses) include [KTBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTBC_\(TV\)) (Fox [O\&O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owned-and-operated_station)), [KVUE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVUE) (ABC), [KXAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KXAN) (NBC), [KEYE-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEYE-TV) (CBS), [KLRU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLRU) (PBS), [KNVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KNVA) (The CW), [KBVO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KBVO_\(TV\)) (MyNetworkTV), and [KAKW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAKW) (Univision O\&O). KLRU produces several award-winning locally produced programs such as *[Austin City Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits)*.[^cite_note-384] Despite Austin's explosive growth, it is only a medium-sized market (currently 38th) because the suburban and rural areas are not much larger than the city proper. Additionally, the proximity of San Antonio truncates the potential market area.

Alex Jones, journalist, radio show host and filmmaker, produces his talk show *[The Alex Jones Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Alex_Jones_Show)* in Austin which broadcasts nationally on more than 60 AM and FM radio stations in the United States, [WWCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWCR) Radio shortwave and [XM Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM_Radio): Channel 166.[^cite_note-385]
