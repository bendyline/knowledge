**Esmaeil Khatib** (1961 – 18 March 2026) was an Iranian cleric and politician who served as the [Minister of Intelligence](501536.md) from August 2021 until his assassination in March 2026 during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md) by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-1] He was the eighth official to hold the post.

## Early life and education

Khatib was born in [Qaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaen), [South Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Khorasan_Province), in 1961.[^cite_note-ncrkia-2][^cite_note-inu21-3] He studied Islamic jurisprudence in [Qom](638742.md).[^cite_note-uani-4] He was a pupil of people such as [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), [Mohammad Fazel Lankarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Fazel_Lankarani), [Naser Makarem Shirazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naser_Makarem_Shirazi), and [Mojtaba Tehrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojtaba_Tehrani).[^cite_note-besa-5]

## Career

From 1991, Khatib was responsible for intelligence in the province of Qom.[^cite_note-6]

Khatib held the title of [Hujjat al-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hujjat_al-Islam), which translates as "proof of Islam" or "authority on Islam".[^cite_note-uani-4] Previous posts of Khatib included the head of the Information Protection Center of the Judiciary and the head of Astan Quds Razavi Security.[^cite_note-inu21-3] He also worked in the [Office](51025254.md) of the [Supreme Leader](36814344.md), Ali Khamenei, as Iran's chief warden, and in the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) intelligence department and the Ministry of Intelligence.[^cite_note-besa-5][^cite_note-inu21-3]

In 1985, Khatib was assigned to the intelligence unit of the IRGC by [Mohsen Rezaee](1862765.md), the first commander-in-chief of the corps.[^cite_note-ncrkia-2] Khatib was in the post until 1991.[^cite_note-ncrkia-2] During the tenure of [Ali Fallahian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fallahian) as Minister of Intelligence, Khatib was appointed head of the ministry's regional branch in [Qom](638742.md) in 1999.[^cite_note-ncrkia-2][^cite_note-besa-5] Khatib was appointed chief warden in the Office of Supreme Leader in 2010.[^cite_note-ncrkia-2]

### Minister of Intelligence

Khatib was nominated as the minister of intelligence in the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ebrahim_Raisi_\(2021%E2%80%93present\)) of president [Ebrahim Raisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebrahim_Raisi) and was confirmed by the [Majlis](383276.md) on 25 August 2021.[^cite_note-besa-5] He received 222 votes in favor.[^cite_note-7] Khatib replaced [Mahmoud Alavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Alavi) in the post.[^cite_note-besa-5]

During the summer of 2023, senior security and intelligence officials, including Khatib, met in [Mashhad](406512.md) to create a coordinated strategy to counter the "coherent and extensive" planning by the West to undermine national stability and purpose.[^cite_note-8]

In June 2025, Iranian state media reported that Iranian intelligence agencies had obtained a large set of sensitive Israeli documents. Khatib said the files included material on Israel’s nuclear facilities, its relations with the United States, Europe, and other countries, as well as its defensive capabilities. He described the collection as a “treasure trove” that would strengthen Iran’s offensive capabilities and said the documents would be made public soon. Israel did not comment on the claims.[^cite_note-9]

### Views

Khatib was one of Ali Khamenei's close allies and was also a supporter of Ayatollah [Ruhollah Khomeini](26232.md). He had close connections with other significant religious figures in Iran, mostly his teachers.[^cite_note-besa-5]

## Assassination

Khatib was targeted in overnight [Israeli airstrikes](82533582.md) on 17–18 March 2026 and was killed, according to Israeli Defense Minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) and the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Iranian president [Masoud Pezeshkian](2650383.md) confirmed Khatib's death on 18 March 2026.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]
