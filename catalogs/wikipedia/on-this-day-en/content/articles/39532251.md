**Middle East respiratory syndrome** (**MERS**) is a viral [respiratory infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_infection) caused by [Middle East respiratory syndrome–related coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome%E2%80%93related_coronavirus) (MERS-CoV).[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] Symptoms may range from none, to mild, to severe depending on age and risk level.[^cite_note-Lancet2015-6][^cite_note-WHO2019-1] Typical symptoms include [fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever), [cough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cough), [diarrhea](53951.md), and [shortness of breath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortness_of_breath).[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] The disease is typically more severe in those with other health problems.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1][^cite_note-Lancet2015-6]

The [first case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_outbreak) was identified in June 2012 by Egyptian physician [Ali Mohamed Zaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Mohamed_Zaki) at the [Dr. Soliman Fakeeh Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Soliman_Fakeeh_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Jeddah](277718.md), Saudi Arabia, and most cases have occurred in the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md).[^cite_note-WHO2019-1][^cite_note-Lancet2015-6] Over 2,600 cases have been reported as of January 2021, including 45 cases in the year 2020.[^cite_note-MERSUpdateMar2021-5] About 35% of those who are diagnosed with the disease die from it.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] Larger outbreaks have occurred in [South Korea in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_outbreak_in_South_Korea) and in [Saudi Arabia in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_outbreak).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-WHO2019-1]

MERS-CoV is a virus in the [coronavirus](201983.md) family believed to be originally from bats.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] However, humans are typically infected from [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camels), either during direct contact or indirectly through respiratory droplets.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] [Spread between humans](63917263.md) typically requires close contact with an infected person.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] Its spread is uncommon outside of hospitals.[^cite_note-Lancet2015-6] Thus, its risk to the global population is currently deemed to be significantly low.[^cite_note-Lancet2015-6] Diagnosis is by [rRT-PCR testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_transcription_polymerase_chain_reaction) of blood and respiratory samples.[^cite_note-CDC2020Diag-3]

There is no specific [vaccine](32653.md) or treatment for the disease,[^cite_note-CDC2020Pro-2] but a number are being developed.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] The [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) recommends that those who come in contact with camels wash their hands and not touch sick camels.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] They also recommend that [camel-based food products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel#Food_uses) be appropriately cooked.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1] Treatments that help with the symptoms and support body functioning may be used.[^cite_note-WHO2019-1]

Previous infection with MERS can confer cross-reactive immunity to [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2) and provide partial protection against [COVID-19](63030231.md).[^cite_note-8] However, [co-infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-infection) with [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2) and MERS-CoV is possible and could lead to a [recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reassortment) event.[^cite_note-9]

## Signs and symptoms



Symptoms of MERS

Early reports compared the [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viruses) to [severe acute respiratory syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome) ([SARS](196741.md)),[^cite_note-zaki8nov2012-10] and it has been referred to as Saudi Arabia's SARS-like virus.[^cite_note-NPR26sep2012-11] The first person, [in June 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_outbreak), had a [fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever), [cough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cough), [expectoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectoration), and shortness of breath.[^cite_note-zaki8nov2012-10] One review of 47 laboratory confirmed cases in Saudi Arabia gave the most common presenting symptoms as fever in 98%, cough in 83%, shortness of breath in 72% and [myalgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myalgia) in 32% of people. There were also frequent gastrointestinal symptoms with diarrhea in 26%, vomiting in 21%, abdominal pain in 17% of people. 72% of people required [mechanical ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_ventilation). There were also 3.3 males for every female.[^cite_note-Assiri9sep2013-12] One study of a hospital-based outbreak of MERS had an estimated [incubation period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubation_period) of 5.5 days (95% [confidence interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval) 1.9 to 14.7 days).[^cite_note-Assiri8jan2013-13] MERS can range from asymptomatic disease to severe [pneumonia](52135.md) leading to [acute respiratory distress syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_respiratory_distress_syndrome) (ARDS).[^cite_note-Assiri9sep2013-12] [Kidney failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_failure), [disseminated intravascular coagulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disseminated_intravascular_coagulation) (DIC), and [pericarditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericarditis) have also been reported.[^cite_note-WHO2nov2013-14]

## Cause

### Virology

Main article: [Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus)



MERS coronaviruses under [electron microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope)

Middle East respiratory syndrome is caused by the [MERS coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus) (MERS-CoV), a species with [single-stranded RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_virus) belonging to the genus [betacoronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus) which is distinct from [SARS coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS_coronavirus) and the common-cold coronavirus.[^cite_note-sciencenews27feb2013-15] Its genomes are phylogenetically classified into two [clades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clades), Clades A and B. Early cases of MERS were of Clade A clusters (EMC/2012 and Jordan-N3/2012), while new cases are genetically different in general (Clade B).[^cite_note-16] The virus grows readily on [Vero cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vero_cells) and LLC-MK2 cells.[^cite_note-promedmail.org-17] In November 2012, Egyptian virologist [Dr. Ali Zaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Mohamed_Zaki) sent a virus sample from the first confirmed case in Saudi Arabia to virologist [Ron Fouchier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Fouchier), a leading coronavirus researcher at the [Erasmus Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Medical_Center) (EMC) in [Rotterdam](26049.md), the Netherlands.[^cite_note-sampledisputeCTV23May2013-18] The second laboratory-proven case was in London, confirmed by the UK [Health Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Protection_Agency) (HPA).[^cite_note-batLu-19][^cite_note-UK23sept2012-20] The HPA named the virus the London1_novel CoV 2012.[^cite_note-UKHPA-21]

### Transmission

See also: [Middle East respiratory syndrome-related coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome-related_coronavirus)

#### Camels

A study performed between 2010 and 2013, in which the incidence of MERS was evaluated in 310 [dromedary camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dromedary), revealed high titers of neutralizing antibodies to MERS-CoV in the blood serum of these animals.[^cite_note-Hemida12dec2013-22] A further study sequenced MERS-CoV from nasal swabs of [dromedary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dromedary) camels in Saudi Arabia and found they had sequences identical to previously sequenced human isolates. Some individual camels were also found to have more than one genomic variant in their [nasopharynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharynx).[^cite_note-Briese29apr2014-23] There is also a 2014 report of a Saudi Arabian man who became ill seven days after applying topical medicine to the noses of several sick camels and later he and one of the camels were found to have identical strains of MERS-CoV.[^cite_note-BBC5jun2014-24][^cite_note-Azhar4jun2014-25] It is still unclear how the virus is transmitted from camels to humans. In 2014, the Saudi Ministry of Agriculture advised people to avoid contact with camels or wear breathing masks when around them.[^cite_note-BBC11may2014-26] In response "some people have refused to listen to the government's advice"[^cite_note-BBC13may2014-27] and kiss their camels in defiance of their government's advice. In 2020, the World Health Organization advised avoiding contact with camels and to eat only fully cooked camel meat, pasteurized camel milk, and to avoid drinking [camel urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_urine).[^cite_note-28]

#### Between people

There has been evidence of limited, but not sustained spread of MERS-CoV from person to person, both in households as well as in health care settings like hospitals.[^cite_note-Assiri8jan2013-13][^cite_note-HPA14mar2013-29] Most transmission has occurred "in the circumstances of close contact with severely ill persons in healthcare or household settings" and there is no evidence of transmission from asymptomatic cases.[^cite_note-WHO8aug2013-30] Cluster sizes have ranged from 1 to 26 people, with an average of 2.7.[^cite_note-Cauchemez13nov2013-31]

## Diagnosis

According to [World Health Organization](33583.md), the interim case definition is that a confirmed case is identified in a person with a positive lab test by "molecular diagnostics including either a positive [polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerase_chain_reaction) (PCR) on at least two specific genomic targets or a single positive target with sequencing on a second".[^cite_note-WHO3jul2013-32]

### World Health Organization

According to the WHO, a probable case is:[^cite_note-WHO3jul2013-32]

- A person with a fever, respiratory infection, and evidence of pneumonia or acute respiratory distress syndrome, where testing for MERS-CoV is unavailable or negative on a single inadequate specimen, and the person has a direct link with a confirmed case

- A person with an [acute febrile respiratory illness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_febrile_respiratory_illness) with clinical, [radiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiology), or histopathological evidence of [pulmonary parenchymal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_parenchyma) disease (e.g., pneumonia or acute respiratory distress Syndrome), an inconclusive MERS-CoV laboratory test (that is, a positive [screening test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screening_test) without confirmation), and a resident of or traveler to Middle Eastern countries where MERS-CoV virus is believed to be circulating in the 14 days before onset of illness

- A person with an acute febrile respiratory illness of any severity, an inconclusive MERS-CoV laboratory test (that is, a positive screening test without confirmation), and a direct epidemiologic link with a confirmed MERS-CoV case

### Centers for Disease Control

In the United States, the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) recommend investigating any person with:[^cite_note-CDC9may2014-33][^cite_note-CDC10apr2015-34]

- Fever and pneumonia or acute respiratory distress syndrome (based on clinical or radiological evidence) and either:&#x20;

  - a history of travel from countries in or near the Arabian Peninsula within 14 days before symptom onset, or

  - close contact with a symptomatic traveler who developed fever and acute respiratory illness (not necessarily pneumonia) within 14 days after traveling from countries in or near the Arabian Peninsula or

  - a member of a cluster of people with severe acute respiratory illness (e.g. fever and pneumonia requiring hospitalization) of unknown cause in which MERS-CoV is being evaluated, in consultation with state and local health departments.

- Fever and symptoms of respiratory illness (not necessarily pneumonia; e.g., cough, shortness of breath) and being in a healthcare facility (as a patient, worker, or visitor) within 14 days before symptom onset in a country or territory in or near the Arabian Peninsula in which recent healthcare-associated cases of MERS have been identified.

- Fever or symptoms of respiratory illness (not necessarily pneumonia; e.g., cough, shortness of breath) and close contact with a confirmed MERS case while the case was ill.

### Medical imaging



Chest X-ray of a case of MERS

[Chest X-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chest_radiograph) findings tend to show bilateral patchy infiltrates consistent with viral pneumonitis and [acute respiratory distress syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_respiratory_distress_syndrome) (ARDS). Lower lobes tend to be more involved. [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) show [interstitial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitial_space_\(biology\)) infiltrates.[^cite_note-HPA14mar2013-29]

### Laboratory testing

MERS cases have been reported to have [low white blood cell count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leucopenia), and in particular [low lymphocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphopenia).[^cite_note-HPA14mar2013-29] For PCR testing, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) recommends obtaining samples from the lower respiratory tract via [bronchoalveolar lavage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchoalveolar_lavage) (BAL), [sputum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputum) sample or [tracheal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachea) aspirate as these have the highest viral loads.[^cite_note-WHOsep2013-35] There have also been studies utilizing upper respiratory sampling via [nasopharyngeal swab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasopharyngeal_swab).[^cite_note-Assiri9sep2013-12]

Several highly sensitive, confirmatory real-time [RT-PCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT-PCR) assays exist for rapid identification of MERS-CoV from patient-derived samples. These assays attempt to amplify upE (targets elements upstream of the E gene),[^cite_note-Corman27sep2012-36] [open reading frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_reading_frame) 1B (targets the ORF1b gene)[^cite_note-Corman27sep2012-36] and open reading frame 1A (targets the ORF1a gene).[^cite_note-Corman6dec2012-37] The WHO recommends the upE target for screening assays as it is highly sensitive.[^cite_note-WHOsep2013-35] In addition, hemi-nested sequencing amplicons targeting RdRp (present in all [coronaviruses](201983.md)) and nucleocapsid (N) gene[^cite_note-Lu1jan2014-38] (specific to MERS-CoV) fragments can be generated for confirmation via sequencing. Reports of potential polymorphisms in the N gene between isolates highlight the necessity for sequence-based characterization.

The WHO recommended testing algorithm is to start with an upE RT-PCR and if positive confirm with ORF 1A assay or RdRp or N gene sequence assay for confirmation. If both an upE and secondary assay are positive it is considered a confirmed case.[^cite_note-WHOsep2013-35]

Protocols for biologically safe [immunofluorescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunofluorescence) assays (IFA) have also been developed; however, antibodies against [betacoronaviruses](201983.md) are known to cross-react within the genus. This effectively limits their use to confirmatory applications.[^cite_note-Corman6dec2012-37] A more specific protein-microarray based assay has also been developed that did not show any cross-reactivity against population samples and serum known to be positive for other betacoronaviruses.[^cite_note-Reusken4apr2013-39] Due to the limited validation done so far with serological assays, WHO guidance is that "cases where the testing laboratory has reported positive serological test results in the absence of PCR testing or sequencing, are considered probable cases of MERS-CoV infection, if they meet the other conditions of that case definition."[^cite_note-WHOsep2013-35]

## Prevention

While the mechanism of spread of MERS-CoV is currently not known, based on experience with prior coronaviruses, such as SARS, the WHO currently recommends that all individuals coming into contact with MERS suspects should (in addition to standard precautions):

- Wear a medical mask

- Wear eye protection (i.e. goggles or a face shield)

- Wear a clean, non sterile, long sleeved gown; and gloves (some procedures may require sterile gloves)

- Perform hand hygiene before and after contact with the person and their surroundings and immediately after removal of [personal protective equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment) (PPE)

  [^cite_note-WHO6may2013-40]

For procedures which carry a risk of aerosolization, such as intubation, the WHO recommends that care providers also:

- Wear a particulate respirator and, when putting on a disposable particulate respirator, always check the seal

- Wear eye protection (i.e. goggles or a face shield)

- Wear a clean, non-sterile, long-sleeved gown and gloves (some of these procedures require sterile gloves)

- Wear an impermeable apron for some procedures with expected high fluid volumes that might penetrate the gown

- Perform procedures in an adequately ventilated room; i.e. minimum of 6 to 12 air changes per hour in facilities with a mechanically ventilated room and at least 60 liters/second/patient in facilities with natural ventilation

- Limit the number of persons present in the room to the absolute minimum required for the person's care and support

- Perform hand hygiene before and after contact with the person and their surroundings and after [PPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment) removal.

  [^cite_note-WHO6may2013-40]

The duration of infectivity is also unknown so it is unclear how long people must be isolated, but current recommendations are for 24 hours after resolution of symptoms. In the SARS outbreak the virus was not cultured from people after the resolution of their symptoms.[^cite_note-Chan2004feb-41]

It is believed that the existing [SARS](196741.md) research may provide a useful template for developing vaccines and therapeutics against a MERS-CoV infection.[^cite_note-Jiang2013-42][^cite_note-Butler3oct2013-43] As of March 2020 there was one ([DNA based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_vaccination)) MERS vaccine which completed [phase I clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research) in humans,[^cite_note-44] and three others in progress all of which are viral vectored vaccines, two [adenoviral vectored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenoviridae#Use_in_gene_therapy_and_vaccination) ([ChAdOx1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChAdOx1)-MERS,[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] BVRS-GamVac[^cite_note-47]) and one [MVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_vaccinia_Ankara) vectored (MVA-MERS-S[^cite_note-48]).[^cite_note-49]

## Treatment

There is no specific [vaccine](32653.md) or treatment for the disease.[^cite_note-CDC2020Pro-2] Using [extra-corporeal membrane oxygenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracorporeal_membrane_oxygenation) (ECMO) seems to improve outcomes significantly.[^cite_note-50]

Neither the combination of antivirals and [interferons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interferon) ([ribavirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribavirin) + interferon alfa-2a or interferon alfa-2b) nor [corticosteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corticosteroids) improved outcomes.[^cite_note-pmid26868298-51]

## Prognosis

About 34.4% of the people diagnosed with MERS die from the condition, across all countries. MERS has a significantly higher [mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate) than it's related viruses, such as [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2).[^cite_note-WHO2019-1]

### Saudi Arabia

MERS was also implicated in an outbreak in April 2014 in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), where it has infected 688 people and 282 MERS-related deaths have been reported since 2012.[^cite_note-NYT20140604-66] In response to newly reported cases and deaths, and the resignation of four doctors at Jeddah's King Fahd Hospital who refused to treat MERS patients for fear of infection, the government removed the Minister of Health and set up three MERS treatment centers.[^cite_note-KhaleejTimes27apr2014-67][^cite_note-Reuters27apr2014-68] Eighteen more cases were reported in early May.[^cite_note-Reuters08may2014-69] In June 2014, Saudi Arabia announced 113 previously unreported cases of MERS, revising the death toll to 282.

A hospital-related outbreak in Riyadh in the summer of 2015 increased fears of an epidemic occurring during the annual [Hajj](23487999.md) pilgrimage that was to begin in late September. After a period of few cases, cases began increasing in the middle of the summer.[^cite_note-70] The [CDC](6811.md) placed the travel health alert to level 2, which calls for taking enhanced precautions.[^cite_note-71]

In May 2019, 14 cases of MERS were reported to the World Health Organization (WHO) by Saudi authorities, of which five were fatal. All those who died had comorbidities and other relatively serious health problems ranging from only [diabetes mellitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus) in one person (aged 35) to complicated combinations of diabetes mellitus, [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension) and [ischemic heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischemic_heart_disease) in two (65 and 80 years old) and diabetes mellitus, hypertension and nephropathy in another one who was 73 years old. Another patient who died and was 64 years old, had diabetes mellitus and hypertension. All those who died were males and three of them were reported to have had contact with, and exposure to, camels. Among the nine persons who survived were two females who were believed to have had contact with a person infected with MERS, one being a 23-year-old healthcare worker. Of the total 14 cases, four were females and 10 were males. All females survived. Reports of fatal cases were from Riyadh, Jeddah, Madinah and Najran. WHO did not recommend screening of travelers upon arrival or traveling restrictions.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

### United States

On 2 May 2014, the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) confirmed the first diagnosis of MERS in the United States at Community Hospital in [Munster, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster,_Indiana). The man diagnosed was a health care worker who had been in Saudi Arabia a week earlier, and was reported to be in good condition.[^cite_note-ABC2may2014-74][^cite_note-WSJ3may2014-75] A second patient who also traveled from Saudi Arabia was reported in Orlando, Florida on 12 May 2014.[^cite_note-CDC12may2014-76][^cite_note-OrlandoSentinel12may2014-77]

### Netherlands

On 14 May 2014, officials in the [Netherlands](21148.md) reported the first case had appeared.[^cite_note-ChicagoTribune14may2014-78]

### South Korea

See also: [2015 Middle East respiratory syndrome outbreak in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_outbreak_in_South_Korea)



2015 MERS outbreak in South Korea

In May 2015, the first case in South Korea was confirmed in a man who had visited Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates and Bahrain.[^cite_note-USAToday2jun2015-79] Another man from South Korea, who was travelling to China, was diagnosed as the first case in China. So far, no Chinese citizen has been found infected.[^cite_note-Xinhua30may2015-80]

As of 27 June 2015, 19 people in South Korea have died from [this outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_outbreak_in_South_Korea), with 184 confirmed cases of infection.[^cite_note-81] There have been at least 6508 quarantined.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-wsj4june-83][^cite_note-Today8Jun2015-84][^cite_note-ID7Jun2015-85][^cite_note-KoreaMW8Jun2015-86][^cite_note-NetEase7Jun2015-87]

In 2018 a case was found in South Korea; the patient had recently returned from Kuwait (via Dubai).[^cite_note-88]

One study found that severe cases of illness had higher viral loads than milder cases, and that concentrations peaked in the second week of illness.[^cite_note-89]

### Philippines

In April 2014, MERS emerged in the [Philippines](23440.md) with a suspected case of a home-bound Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW). Several suspected cases involving individuals who were on the same flight as the initial suspected case are being tracked but are believed to have dispersed throughout the country. Another suspected MERS-involved death in [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat) province caused the Department of Health (DOH) to put out an alert.[^cite_note-GMA24apr2014a-90][^cite_note-GMA24apr2014b-91][^cite_note-MindaNews22apr2014-92][^cite_note-PhilStar18sep2013-93] On 6 July 2015 the [DOH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health) confirmed the second case of MERS in the Philippines. A 36-year-old male foreigner from the Middle East was tested positive.[^cite_note-94]

### United Kingdom

On 27 July 2015, the accident and emergency department at [Manchester Royal Infirmary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Royal_Infirmary) closed after two patients were treated for suspected MERS infection.[^cite_note-95] The facility was reopened later that evening, and it was later confirmed by [Public Health England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Health_England) that the two patients had tested negative for the disease.[^cite_note-96]

### Kenya

In January 2016, a larger outbreak of MERS among camels in Kenya was reported.[^cite_note-97] By 5 February 2016 more than 500 camels had died of the disease.[^cite_note-98] On 12 February 2016, the disease was reported to be MERS.[^cite_note-99] No human cases were identified, although one study found antibodies in healthy humans in Kenya.[^cite_note-100]

## History

Collaborative efforts were used in the identification of the MERS-CoV.[^cite_note-Lu10nov2012-102] Egyptian virologist Dr. Ali Mohamed Zaki isolated and identified a previously unknown coronavirus from the lungs of a 60-year-old Saudi Arabian man with pneumonia and [acute kidney injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_kidney_injury).[^cite_note-zaki8nov2012-10] After routine diagnostics failed to identify the causative agent, Zaki contacted Ron Fouchier, a leading virologist at the [Erasmus Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_MC) (EMC) in Rotterdam, the Netherlands, for advice.[^cite_note-Butler15jan2013-103] Fouchier sequenced the virus from a sample sent by Zaki.

Fouchier used a broad-spectrum "pan-coronavirus" [real-time reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_transcription_polymerase_chain_reaction) (RT-qPCR) method to test for distinguishing features of a number of known [coronaviruses](201983.md) (such as OC43, 229E, NL63, and [SARS-CoV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV)), as well as for [RNA-dependent RNA polymerase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA-dependent_RNA_polymerase) (RdRp), a gene conserved in all [coronaviruses](201983.md) known to infect humans. While the screens for known [coronaviruses](201983.md) were all negative, the RdRp screen was positive.[^cite_note-Lu10nov2012-102]

On 15 September 2012, Dr. Zaki's findings were posted on [ProMED-mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProMED-mail), the Program for Monitoring Emerging Diseases, a public health on-line forum.[^cite_note-sciencenews27feb2013-15]

The United Kingdom's [Health Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Protection_Agency) (HPA) confirmed the diagnosis of severe respiratory illness associated with a new type of coronavirus in a second patient, a 49-year-old Qatari man who had recently been flown into the UK. He died from an acute, serious respiratory illness in a London hospital.[^cite_note-Lu10nov2012-102][^cite_note-UK23sept2012-20] In September 2012, the UK HPA named it the [London1 novel CoV/2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London1_novel_CoV/2012) and produced the virus' preliminary phylogenetic tree, the genetic sequence of the virus[^cite_note-UKHPA-21] based on the virus's [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA) obtained from the Qatari case.[^cite_note-NPR26sep2012-11][^cite_note-BBC24sep2012-104]

On 25 September 2012, the WHO announced that it was "engaged in further characterizing the novel coronavirus" and that it had "immediately alerted all its Member States about the virus and has been leading the coordination and providing guidance to health authorities and technical health agencies".[^cite_note-WHO25sep2012-105] The [Erasmus Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Medical_Center) (EMC) in Rotterdam "tested, sequenced and identified" a sample provided to EMC virologist Ron Fouchier by Ali Mohamed Zaki in November 2012.[^cite_note-sampledisputeCTV23May2013-18]

On 8 November 2012, in an article published in the [New England Journal of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Journal_of_Medicine), Dr. Zaki and co-authors from the Erasmus Medical Center published more details, including a tentative name, Human Coronavirus-Erasmus Medical Center (HCoV-EMC), the virus's genetic makeup, and closest relatives (including SARS).[^cite_note-zaki8nov2012-10]

In May 2013, the Coronavirus Study Group of the [International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Taxonomy_of_Viruses) adopted the official designation, the Middle East Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus (MERS-CoV), which was adopted by WHO to "provide uniformity and facilitate communication about the disease".[^cite_note-WHO16may2013-106] Prior to the designation, WHO had used the non-specific designation 'Novel coronavirus 2012' or simply 'the novel coronavirus'.[^cite_note-WHO23nov2012-107]

## Research

When rhesus macaques were given [interferon-α2b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peginterferon_alfa-2b) and [ribavirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribavirin) and exposed to MERS, they developed less pneumonia than control animals.[^cite_note-Falzarano8sep2013-108] Five critically ill people with MERS in Saudi Arabia with [acute respiratory distress syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_respiratory_distress_syndrome) (ARDS) and on ventilators were given interferon-α2b and ribavirin but all ended up dying of the disease. The treatment was started late in their disease (a mean of 19 days after hospital admission) and they had already failed trials of steroids so it remains to be seen whether it may have benefit earlier in the course of disease.[^cite_note-Al-Tawfiq8jan2014-109][^cite_note-110] Another proposed therapy is inhibition of viral [protease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protease)[^cite_note-Ren3apr2013-111] or [kinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinase) enzymes.[^cite_note-112]

Researchers are investigating a number of medications, including using interferon, [chloroquine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroquine), [chlorpromazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorpromazine), [loperamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loperamide), [lopinavir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopinavir),[^cite_note-Otrompke20oct2014-113] [remdesivir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remdesivir) and [galidesivir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galidesivir) as well as other agents such as [mycophenolic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycophenolic_acid),[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] [camostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camostat)[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] and [nitazoxanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitazoxanide).[^cite_note-pmid28500431-118][^cite_note-pmid8830507-119]
