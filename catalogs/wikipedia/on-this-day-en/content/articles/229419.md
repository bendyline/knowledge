**Rio de Janeiro** (Brazilian Portuguese pronunciation: [\[ˈʁi.u d(ʒi) ʒɐˈne(j)ɾu\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d5/Pt-br_Rio_de_Janeiro.ogg/Pt-br_Rio_de_Janeiro.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d5/Pt-br_Rio_de_Janeiro.ogg/Pt-br_Rio_de_Janeiro.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-6], officially the **State of Rio de Janeiro** (Portuguese: *Estado do Rio de Janeiro*), is one of the [27 federative units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil) of [Brazil](3383.md). It has the second largest economy of Brazil, with the largest being that of the state of [São Paulo](229422.md).[^cite_note-7] The state, which has 8.2% of the Brazilian population, is responsible for 9.2% of the Brazilian [GDP](12594.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The state of Rio de Janeiro is located within the Brazilian geopolitical region classified as the [Southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Region,_Brazil) (assigned by [IBGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Institute_of_Geography_and_Statistics)). Rio de Janeiro shares borders with all the other states in the same Southeast macroregion: [Minas Gerais](222651.md) ([N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North) and [NW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_directions)), [Espírito Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo) ([NE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_direction)) and [São Paulo](229422.md) ([SW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_directions)). It is bounded on the east and south by the [South Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Ocean). Rio de Janeiro has an area of 43,653 km<sup>2</sup> (16,855 sq mi). Its capital and most populous city is the city of [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro,_Rio_de_Janeiro), which was the capital of the Portuguese [Colony of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Brazil) from 1763 to 1815, of the following [United Kingdom of Portugal, Brazil and the Algarves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Portugal,_Brazil_and_the_Algarves) from 1815 to 1822, and of later independent Brazil as a [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realm) and [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) from 1822 to 1960.

The state is divided into 92 municipalities. The state's 22 largest cities are [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), [São Gonçalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Gon%C3%A7alo,_Rio_de_Janeiro), [Duque de Caxias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duque_de_Caxias,_Rio_de_Janeiro), [Nova Iguaçu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Igua%C3%A7u), [Niterói](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niter%C3%B3i), [Campos dos Goytacazes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campos_dos_Goytacazes), [Belford Roxo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belford_Roxo), [São João de Meriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Meriti), [Petrópolis](504174.md), [Volta Redonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta_Redonda), [Magé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mag%C3%A9), [Macaé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maca%C3%A9), [Itaboraí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itabora%C3%AD), [Cabo Frio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabo_Frio), [Armação dos Búzios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arma%C3%A7%C3%A3o_dos_B%C3%BAzios), [Angra dos Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angra_dos_Reis), [Nova Friburgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Friburgo), [Barra Mansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_Mansa), [Barra do Piraí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_do_Pira%C3%AD), [Teresópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teres%C3%B3polis), [Mesquita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesquita,_Rio_de_Janeiro) and [Nilópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil%C3%B3polis).

Rio de Janeiro is the smallest state by area in the Southeast macroregion and one of the smallest in Brazil. It is, however, the [third most populous Brazilian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Brazilian_states_by_population), with a population of over 16 million people at the 2022 Census (making it the [most densely populated state in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Brazilian_states_by_population_density)), and it has the third longest coastline in the country (after those of the states of [Bahia](199070.md) and [Maranhão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranh%C3%A3o)).

In the [Brazilian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Brazil), the state is represented by [Mimosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimosa_\(star\)), the beta star in the [Southern Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crux) (β Cru).

## Etymology

[Guanabara Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanabara_Bay) was first encountered by Europeans on January 1, 1502, when Portuguese explorers [Gaspar de Lemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_de_Lemos) and [Gonçalo Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Coelho)[^cite_note-12] arrived on its shores. According to some historians,[^cite_note-13] the name given by the exploration team to the bay was originally *Ria de Janeiro* ("January [Ria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ria)"). Then confusion took place between the words *ria* and *rio* ("river"). As a result, the name of the bay was soon fixed as *Rio de Janeiro*.

### Demonym

The demonym for the State of Rio de Janeiro is *fluminense*, from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *flumen, fluminis*, meaning "river". While *carioca* is an older term, first attested in 1502, *fluminense* was sanctioned in 1783, twenty years after the city had become the capital of the Brazilian colonies, as the official demonym of the Royal Captaincy of Rio de Janeiro and subsequently of the Province of Rio de Janeiro. From 1783 through the [Imperial Regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil), *carioca* remained an unofficial term which other Brazilians used for the inhabitants of the city as well as the province. During the first years of the Brazilian Republic, *carioca* came to be the name given to those who lived in the city's slums or a pejorative used to refer to the bureaucratic elite of the Federal District. Only when the city lost its status as Federal District (to [Brasília](4752.md)) and became the [State of Guanabara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanabara_\(state\)) in 1960 did *carioca* become an official demonym along with *guanabarino*. In 1975, Guanabara State was incorporated into Rio de Janeiro State, becoming the present [City of Rio de Janeiro](25936.md). *Carioca* became the demonym of the city, while *fluminense* continues to be used for the state as a whole.

## History



[Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), the capital city of the State of Rio de Janeiro

### Hereditary captainships

European presence in Rio de Janeiro dates back to 1502. Rio de Janeiro originated from parts of the captainships of de Tomé and São Vicente. Between 1555 and 1567, the territory was occupied by the French, who intended to install a colony, [France Antarctique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_Antarctique). Aiming to prevent the occupation of the Frenchmen, in March 1565, the city of [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) was established by [Estácio de Sá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1cio_de_S%C3%A1).

In the 17th century, cattle raising and sugar cane cultivation stimulated the city's progress, which was definitively assured when the port started to export gold extracted from [Minas Gerais](222651.md) in the 18th century. In 1763, Rio de Janeiro became the capital of Colonial Brazil. With the flight of the Portuguese royal family from Portugal to Brazil in 1808, the region soon benefited from urban reforms to house the Portuguese. Chief among the promoted changes were the transformation of agencies of public administration and justice, the creation of new churches and hospitals, the foundation of the first bank of the country (the [Bank of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_do_Brasil)) and the Royal Press, with the Gazette do Rio of Janeiro. The following years witnessed the creation of the [Jardim Botânico (Botanical Garden)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro_Botanical_Garden) and the [Royal Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_\(Rio_de_Janeiro\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).

There followed a process of cultural enhancement influenced not only by the arrival of the royal family, but also by the presence of European graphic artists who were hired to record the society and Brazilian natural features. During this same time, [The Royal School of Sciences, Arts, and Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_School_of_Sciences,_Arts,_and_Works?action=edit\&redlink=1) was founded as well.

### The neutral city

In 1834, the city of Rio de Janeiro was transformed into a ["neutral city"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_municipality), remaining as capital of the state, while the captainships became provinces, with headquarters in [Niterói](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niter%C3%B3i), a neighbouring city. In 1889, the city became the capital of the Republic, the neutral city became the federal district and the province a state. In 1894, [Petrópolis](504174.md) became the capital of Rio de Janeiro, until 1902 when Niterói recovered its capital status.[^cite_note-WDL-14] With the relocation of the federal capital to Brasília in 1960, the city of Rio de Janeiro became [Guanabara State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanabara_State). Niterói remained the state capital for Rio de Janeiro state, while Rio de Janeiro served the same status for Guanabara.

### The new state of Rio de Janeiro



[Tiradentes Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiradentes_Palace), seat of the [Legislative Assembly of Rio de Janeiro](39255501.md)



[Guanabara Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanabara_Palace), seat of the state government

In 1975, a further change took place, with the merger of the states of Rio de Janeiro and the state of Guanabara, restoring the then city-state to the status of a municipality, governed by mayors.

In the 1970s, with the transfer of the capital to Brasilia, Rio de Janeiro began to be one of the worst states in the country in terms of economic dynamism. There was no political discussion about the course of the city after it ceased to be the country's capital. "Rio de Janeiro was a job hanger for politicians from all over Brazil. Everyone referred a friend, a relative to take a position in the federal district," explains Marieta de Moraes Ferreira, history professor at [UFRJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFRJ). Left-wing governors and mayors such as [Leonel Brizola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonel_Brizola), [Saturnino Braga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturnino_Braga), [Marcello Allencar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Allencar?action=edit\&redlink=1), [César Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Maia), [Anthony Garotinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Garotinho), and [Benedita da Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedita_da_Silva), among others, helped drive away many industries that still existed in the state. Combined with the incitement to favelas, where Brizola said for example that "the [favela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favela) is not a problem, the favela is a solution", the capital has deteriorated in a few decades, dragging down the State. The state still maintains itself as the largest oil producer in Brazil (80% of the total), where royalties and special participations brought the state 4.2 billion reais in 2014, when the barrel of oil reached \$93.48. But oil revenues, used by the government to repay financing debts, including the payroll of idle workers, fell to 1.6 billion reais. The international price of a barrel fell to USD 50.48 in 2016 and reached a low of USD 28. Petrobras became the target of the largest anti-corruption operation in Brazil, with the participation of governors such as [Sérgio Cabral Filho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9rgio_Cabral_Filho) and [Pezão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Fernando_Pez%C3%A3o), linked to leftist [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md), pushing back investments that were to be at the forefront of the pre-salt exploration. Even when state municipalities received millions of reais in royalties, public spending was used for marginal improvements, for beautification, such as porcelain floors, whale-shaped monuments, without generating revenue or benefit. jobs for the population. Political leaders are blamed for the lack of a unified project for Rio de Janeiro's economy.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Geography



[Serra dos Órgãos National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_dos_%C3%93rg%C3%A3os_National_Park)



[Pico das Agulhas Negras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_das_Agulhas_Negras)

The state is part of the [Mata Atlântica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mata_Atl%C3%A2ntica) [biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome) and is made up of two distinct [morphological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphology) areas: a coastal [plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain), known as *baixada*, and a [plateau](538005.md), which are disposed in parallel fashion from the shoreline on the Atlantic Ocean inland towards [Minas Gerais](222651.md).

The coastline extends 635 kilometers and is formed by the bays of [Guanabara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanabara_Bay), [Sepetiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepetiba), and [Ilha Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilha_Grande). There are prominent [slopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope) near the ocean, featuring also diverse environments, such as [restinga vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_restingas), [bays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay), [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon) and [tropical forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_forest).

Most of the state however consists of highlands, often higher than 1000 m, formed by several mountain chains like the [Serra do Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_do_Mar) which separates Rio from the [state of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo). The highest point of the state, the [Pico das Agulhas Negras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_das_Agulhas_Negras) (Black Needles Peak) is located in the [Serra da Mantiqueira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_da_Mantiqueira) which forms the physical border with neighbouring [Minas Gerais](222651.md).

Its principal rivers are the [Guandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guandu_River_\(Rio_de_Janeiro\)), the [Piraí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pira%C3%AD_River_\(Rio_de_Janeiro\)), the [Paraíba do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para%C3%ADba_do_Sul_River), the [Macaé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maca%C3%A9_River) and the [Muriaé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muria%C3%A9_River).

### Climate



Climate types of Rio de Janeiro

The entire state is located within the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_zone) (the [Tropic of Capricorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Capricorn) passes just a few kilometers south of the state's southernmost point) so a tropical climate is predominant. However, due to altitude, temperatures can drop to freezing point or below in some parts. Frost is not uncommon in some mountainous cities such as Teresópolis, Nova Friburgo and Petrópolis and snow has been reported occasionally in the Itatiaia National Park.

Annual mean temperatures on the coast are around 23 °C (73 °F), 26 °C (79 °F) in summer and 20 °C (68 °F) in winter. In the mountains annual mean temperatures are around 18 °C (64 °F) but can range from 20 °C (68 °F) at an elevation of 600 to 700 metres (2,000 to 2,300 ft) above sea level to 7 °C (45 °F) in the Itatiaia National Park. Summers in these areas tend to be warm but not hot, with averages around 22 °C (72 °F). Winters are quite chilly by Brazilian standards with lows around 8 to 9 °C (46 to 48 °F).

Rains tend to be concentrated in the summer (December to March) with a dry season in the winter (June to September). Most of the state receives between 1,000 and 1,500 millimetres (39 and 59 in) of rainfall in a year.

The last PNAD (National Household Survey) census revealed the following numbers: 6,739,901 [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Brazilian) people (42.0%), 6,682,740 [Pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardo) ([Multiracial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial#Brazil)) people (41.6%), 2,594,253 [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Brazilian) people (16.2%), 21,837 [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Brazilian) people (0.1%), 15,904 [Amerindian](55510.md) people (0.1%).[^cite_note-21]

People of [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Brazilian) ancestry predominate in most of the state. The Brazilian census of 1920 showed that 39,74% of the Portuguese who lived in Brazil lived in Rio de Janeiro. Including all of the Rio de Janeiro, the proportion raised to 46,30% of the Portuguese who lived in Brazil. The numerical presence of the Portuguese was extremely high, accounting for 72% of the foreigners who lived in the capital. Portuguese born people accounted for 20,36% of the population of Rio, and those with a Portuguese father or a Portuguese mother accounted for 30,84%. In other words, native born Portuguese and their children accounted for 51,20% of the inhabitants of Rio, or a total of 267664 people in 1890.[^cite_note-22]

Other [European ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_ethnic_groups), such as [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Brazilian), Finnish and [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Brazilian) settled mostly in the mountainous areas ([Nova Friburgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Friburgo), [Petrópolis](504174.md), etc.) and in the capital. [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Brazilian) and [Spaniards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_immigration_to_Brazil) are also present in the capital as well as in the surrounding cities.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

## Economy



Oil platform [P-51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras) in Rio de Janeiro



[Brasil Kirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasil_Kirin)'s beverage factory in [Cachoeiras de Macacu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachoeiras_de_Macacu)

Much of the economy of the state of Rio de Janeiro is based on the provision of services, with a significant share of industry and little influence on the agricultural sector, which represents only 0.4% of the product gross domestic state.[^cite_note-35]

In the past, [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane) and later, [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) had a considerable impact on the economy of Rio de Janeiro. The area around [Campos dos Goytacazes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campos_dos_Goytacazes) was most affected by the fall of sugar cane: at the beginning of the 20th century, Campos had 27 mills in operation and, throughout the century, it was one of the largest producers in the world. In Brazil, however, as of 2020, only two sugar mills were operating in the city. The state, which harvested about 10 million tonnes in the 1980s, reached only 1 million tonnes in 2017, when it began to recover, harvesting 1.8 million tonnes in the 2019/ 20. In the agricultural field, the State of Rio currently stands out for some products, such as [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), where it is the 4th producer in the country (116 million fruits in 2019), which is produced in the cities of [São Francisco de Itabapoana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Francisco_de_Itabapoana), [São João da Barra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_da_Barra), Campos dos Goytacazes and [Quissamã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quissam%C3%A3). It has small productions of [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) (216 thousand tons in 2019, 20th place in the country), [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana) (76 thousand tons in 2019, 20th place in the country), [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)) (68 thousand tons in 2019, 10th place in the country), [tangerine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerine) (37 thousand tons in 2019, 5th place in the country), [lemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon) (20 thousand tons in 2019, 5th place in the country) and [persimmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persimmon), where it has 6% of national production, being 4th place in the country. The state was also an important producer of [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconuts), in the regions of Quissamã and the Lakes Region, and in 2009 it was the 7th national producer, but between 2010 and 2020 several producers abandoned the activity.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

The [industrial sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector) is the largest component of [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) at 51.6%, followed by the [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector) at 47.8%. [Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) represents 0.6% of [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) (2004). Rio de Janeiro (state) exports: [petroleum](23195.md) 44.8%, [fuel](21188370.md) 17.5%, [siderurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siderurgy) 13%, [chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemicals) 3.6%, nonferrous metals 2.8%, [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicles) 2.1% (2002).

Participation in the Brazilian economy: 15.6% (2004).

There are 3,915,724 vehicles in the state (as of Jan. 2006), 10 million mobile phones, 5.3 million telephones, and 92 cities.

Rio de Janeiro is the city that receives the most foreign tourists in all of Latin America. In Brazil, it receives almost 40% of foreign tourists who visit the country and receives the most tourists in all of Brazil. It was named one of the ten most beautiful cities in the world by global travel guide website Ucityguides. It is the city that receives the most tourists from cruise ships. During the summer, the city receives more than three million tourists, being the best-known Brazilian city abroad. The capital of Rio de Janeiro is internationally known for the beauty of its beaches and hills, in addition to having several sites. According to [Embratur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embratur), it is the most sought-after destination by foreign tourists who visit Brazil for leisure, and second place in business and events tourism.[^cite_note-dadosefatos.turismo.gov.br-45]

[Christ the Redeemer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_the_Redeemer_\(statue\)), elected one of the [seven wonders of the modern world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_7_Wonders_of_the_World), [Sugarloaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarloaf_Mountain) (with its famous cable car), [Rodrigo de Freitas Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_de_Freitas_Lagoon), [Copacabana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copacabana,_Rio_de_Janeiro), [Ipanema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipanema) and [Barra da Tijuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_da_Tijuca) beaches, [Tijuca forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuca_forest), [Quinta da Boa Vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinta_da_Boa_Vista), [Rio de Janeiro Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro_Botanical_Garden), Cinelândia and the [Maracanã stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_stadium) are among the main visiting points. Among the biggest events in the carioca calendar is the [Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Carnival), which, according to the Guinness Book, is the biggest party on the planet with five million revelers playing in the streets of the city, and the [New Year](277922.md)'s party in Copacabana, which is also Listed in Guinness Book as the largest New Year's party in the world, with more than 4 million people spread across the city's beaches, including 2 million on Copacabana Beach alone.[^cite_note-oglobo.globo.com-46][^cite_note-ReferenceA-47][^cite_note-extra.globo.com-48]

Of the 10 most visited cities in the country by leisure tourists, 4 are in the state of Rio. In addition to the capital, in the lake region, [Armação dos Búzios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arma%C3%A7%C3%A3o_dos_B%C3%BAzios), and in the south of the state, [Angra dos Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angra_dos_Reis), both focused on high-end tourism; also in the south of the state, the town of [Paraty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraty), with its colonial architecture and cobbled streets. The beaches of the Lagos region are also very popular, in the towns of [Cabo Frio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabo_Frio) and [Arraial do Cabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arraial_do_Cabo); and [Ilha Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilha_Grande). ## Infrastructure

### Highways



[BR-116](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-116) highway in the mountainous region of Rio de Janeiro

The state of Rio de Janeiro is one of the best in the country in terms of infrastructure, although it has some shortcomings, mainly due to its mountainous terrain. In the highway sector, there are about 800 km of [highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway), the main highway being [BR-101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-101), then [BR-116](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-116). The Presidente Dutra highway, the name given to the BR-116 on the section that connects the country's two main cities (São Paulo to Rio), passing through [Volta Redonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta_Redonda), [Barra Mansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_Mansa) and Resende, was the first [duplicate highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway) in the country, in 1967. The section of the BR-101 is the one that connects the capital to the border with [Espírito Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo), passing through important towns such as [Niterói](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niter%C3%B3i), bordering [Cabo Frio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabo_Frio) and [Búzios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BAzios), then [Macaé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maca%C3%A9) and finally [Campos dos Goytacazes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campos_dos_Goytacazes). The section between [Rio Bonito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Bonito,_Rio_de_Janeiro) and Campos, bidding to have its 176 km duplicated, already has 126 km doubled. BR-116 passes through the state's mountainous interior and connects Rio de Janeiro to the northeastern region of Brazil. The [BR-040](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-040) connects Rio to [Minas Gerais](222651.md) and its capital [Belo Horizonte](82198.md). Also of note is the [Metropolitan Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arco_Metropolitano_do_Rio_de_Janeiro), which surrounds the capital with the aim of removing unnecessary traffic from this area, and which is in the process of being fully completed, with only 23 km of duplication of an old existing section missing. Recently, the Presidente Dutra highway was re-tendered to enable a billion-dollar upgrade project for the Serra das Araras road, the oldest section of the highway, built in the 1920s. Generally, the state's highways have a large movement of freight and passenger vehicles.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

### International airport



Rio de Janeiro-Galeão International Airport

Since August 2004, with the transfer of many flights from [Santos Dumont Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Dumont_Airport), to [Rio de Janeiro-Galeão International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro-Gale%C3%A3o_International_Airport), Rio de Janeiro has second international airport of Brazil. According to data from the official Brazilian travel bureau, Embratur, nearly 40% of foreign tourists who visit Brazil choose Rio as their gateway, meaning Galeão Airport. Besides linking Rio to the rest of Brazil with domestic flights, Galeão has connections to 18 other countries. It can handle up to 15 million passengers a year in two passenger terminals. Located 20 kilometers from downtown Rio, the international airport is served by several quick access routes, such as the Linha Vermelha/Red Line and [Linha Amarela/Yellow Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Line_\(Rio_de_Janeiro\)) freeways and [Avenida Brasil/Brazil Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenida_Brasil_\(Rio_de_Janeiro\)), thus conveniently serving residents of the city's southern, northern and western zones. There are special shuttle buses linking Galeão to Santos Dumont, and bus and taxi service to the rest of the city. The airport complex also has Brazil's longest runway at 4,240 metres (13,910 ft), and one of South America's largest and best equipped cargo logistics terminals.

### Port



Port of Rio de Janeiro

Rio de Janeiro has several important ports, such as the [Port of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rio_de_Janeiro), the Port of [Itaguaí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itagua%C3%AD) and the [Port of Açu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superporto_do_A%C3%A7u), in addition to others such as Niterói and Angra dos Reis. All ports in the state have several rail and road accesses. The Port of Rio de Janeiro is a major exporter of Minas Gerais iron, steel products and vehicles, importing containers, wheat, steel products and zinc concentrate. The Port of Itaguaí exports Minas Gerais iron ore and containerized cargo, importing coal, coal coke, containerized cargo, alumina and steel products. Açu, in 2023, was the third largest private iron ore terminal in Brazil, where it is responsible for 30% of the country's oil exports. In addition, in 2023, it was building the largest thermoelectric park in Latin America, and it houses the largest offshore support base in the world.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]
