**Reno** ([/ˈriː.noʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [*REE-noh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a city in the northwest section of the U.S. state of [Nevada](21216.md), along the Nevada–California border. It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) and most populous city of [Washoe County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_County,_Nevada). Sitting in the High Eastern Sierra foothills, in the [Truckee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_River) valley, on the eastern side of the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada), it is about 23 miles (37 km) northeast of [Lake Tahoe](140899.md). Reno is the [78th most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the United States, the [third most populous city in Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Nevada), and the most populous in Nevada outside the [Las Vegas Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley). It is known as "The Biggest Little City in the World"[^cite_note-4] and had a population of 264,165 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-Pop2020-3]

The city is named after Civil War Union major general [Jesse L. Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_L._Reno), who was [killed in action](368513.md) during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) at the [Battle of South Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_South_Mountain), on [Fox's Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox's_Gap).

The downtowns of Reno and neighboring city [Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparks,_Nevada) reside in a valley known as the [Truckee Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_Meadows), which is sometimes colloquially used as a name for the entire metro area. However, the [Reno–Sparks metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno,_NV_Metropolitan_Statistical_Area) (MSA), the second-largest in Nevada, comprises all of Washoe, [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_County,_Nevada) and [Storey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey_County) Counties. In addition, Reno is part of a [combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) that includes [Carson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_City,_Nevada), [Douglas County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County,_Nevada) and parts of [Placer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placer_County,_California) and [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_County,_California) Counties in California.[^cite_note-CensusQuickFacts-5][^cite_note-Reno_CSA-6]

For much of the 20th century, Reno saw a significant number of people seeking to take advantage of Nevada's relatively lax [divorce laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce_law_in_the_United_States) and the city gained a national reputation as a [divorce mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce_mill). Today, Reno is a tourist destination known for its [casino gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino_gambling) and proximity to Lake Tahoe and the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada). The city is also home to the [University of Nevada at Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada_at_Reno), the state's second-largest university by enrollment and the flagship campus of the University of Nevada system.[^cite_note-7]

Reno was for decades the most populous city in Nevada, before it ceded this place to [Las Vegas](47737.md), with the [1960 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_United_States_census) being the first US census where Las Vegas had more residents than Reno.[^cite_note-8]

## History

### Early history

Archaeological finds place the eastern border of the prehistoric [Martis people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martis_people) in the Reno area.[^cite_note-9] As early as the mid-1850s, a few pioneers settled in the [Truckee Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_Meadows), a relatively fertile valley through which the [Truckee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_River) made its way from [Lake Tahoe](140899.md) to [Pyramid Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_Lake_\(Nevada\)). In addition to [subsistence farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_farming), these early residents could pick up business from travelers along the [California Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Trail), which followed the Truckee westward, before branching off toward [Donner Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donner_Lake), where the formidable obstacle of the Sierra Nevada began.

Gold was discovered in the vicinity of [Virginia City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_City,_Nevada) in 1850, and a modest mining community developed, but the discovery of silver in 1859 at the [Comstock Lode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comstock_Lode) led to a [mining rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_rush), and thousands of emigrants left their homes, bound for the West, hoping to find a fortune.

To provide the necessary connection between Virginia City and the California Trail, Charles W. Fuller built a log toll bridge across the Truckee River in 1859. A small community that served travelers soon grew near the bridge.[^cite_note-VCS_2023/01/11-10] After two years, Fuller sold the bridge to Myron C. Lake, who continued to develop the community by adding a grist mill, kiln, and livery stable to the hotel and eating house. He renamed it "Lake's Crossing". Most of what is present-day western Nevada was formed as the [Nevada Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Territory) from part of [Utah Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Territory) in 1861.

By January 1863, the [Central Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Pacific_Railroad) (CPRR) had begun laying tracks east from [Sacramento, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California), eventually connecting with the [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad) at [Promontory, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promontory,_Utah), to form the [First transcontinental railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad). Lake deeded land to the CPRR in exchange for its promise to build a depot at Lake's Crossing. In 1864, Washoe County was consolidated with [Roop County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roop_County,_Nevada), and Lake's Crossing became the county's largest town. Lake had earned himself the title "founder of Reno".[^cite_note-11] Once the railroad station was established, the town of Reno officially came into being on May 9, 1868.[^cite_note-12] CPRR construction superintendent [Charles Crocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Crocker) named the community after Major General [Jesse Lee Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_L._Reno), a Union officer killed in the Civil War at the [Battle of South Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_South_Mountain).

In 1871, Reno became the county seat of the newly expanded Washoe County, replacing the county seat in [Washoe City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_City,_Nevada). However, political power in Nevada remained with the mining communities, first Virginia City and later [Tonopah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonopah,_Nevada) and [Goldfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldfield,_Nevada).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The extension of the [Virginia and Truckee Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_and_Truckee_Railroad) to Reno in 1872 provided a boost to the new city's economy. In the following decades, Reno continued to grow and prosper as a business and agricultural center and became the principal settlement on the transcontinental railroad between Sacramento and [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City).[^cite_note-15] As the mining boom waned early in the 20th century, Nevada's centers of political and business activity shifted to the non-mining communities, especially Reno and [Las Vegas](47737.md). Nevada is still the third-largest gold producer in the world, after South Africa and Australia; the state yielded 6.9% of the world's supply in 2005 world gold production.[^cite_note-16]

The [Reno Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Arch) was erected on Virginia Street in 1926 to promote the upcoming Transcontinental Highways Exposition of 1927. The arch included the words "Nevada's Transcontinental Highways Exposition" and the dates of the exposition. After the exposition, the Reno City Council decided to keep the arch as a permanent downtown gateway, and Mayor E.E. Roberts asked the citizens of Reno to suggest a slogan for the arch. No acceptable slogan was received until a \$100 prize was offered, and G.A. Burns of Sacramento was declared the winner on March 14, 1929, with "Reno, the Biggest Little City in the World".[^cite_note-shoreno-17]

### The divorce capital of the world

In the early 20th century, Nevada became a popular destination for [migratory divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_divorce) in an era when most states had highly restrictive laws on the subject. Legislation passed in 1931 completed the gradual reduction of the residency requirement from six months to six weeks, and Reno openly advertised itself as the "Divorce Capital of the World". Nevada's laws, which were fairly progressive for the time, allowed numerous grounds for divorce, and Reno's courts quickly gained a reputation for handling cases with both speed and sympathy for those seeking to "untie the knot". From the 1930s through the 1960s, Reno became synonymous with speedy divorce, often referred to colloquially as "the six-week cure". During these decades, the city's reputation drew thousands of divorcees annually, and they in turn became an important part of the local economy. These temporary residents flocked to hotels, [boardinghouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boarding_house), and [hospitality ranches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dude_ranches), many of which catered primarily to those waiting out the six-week residency requirement before their court date.[^cite_note-Brean-18]

Numerous local businesses openly courted these visitors, such as [R. Herz & Bro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Herz_&_Bro), a jewelry store that offered ring resetting services to the recently divorced and the luxurious [El Cortez Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cortez_\(Reno\)), which was built in part to accommodate the more affluent among Reno's six-week guests.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] The majority of those who came to Reno for divorce were women as Nevada did not require both parties in a divorce case to be present in court, and men often could not take that much time off from work. Although new "residents" seeking divorce were required to swear under oath that they intended to make Nevada their permanent home, most left soon after obtaining their divorce decree, which often occurred on the same day as the initial court hearing.[^cite_note-Brean-18]

In addition to ordinary residents, Reno also became a major destination for celebrities and the very wealthy looking to end their marriages as quickly as possible. Some of the many famous personages who got divorced in Reno include [Mary Pickford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Pickford), [Jack Dempsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Dempsey), [General Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur), [Carole Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carole_Lombard), [Tallulah Bankhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallulah_Bankhead), [Adlai Stevenson II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlai_Stevenson_II), [Lana Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Turner), [Nelson Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Rockefeller), [Georges Simenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Simenon), [Rita Hayworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Hayworth), [Gloria Vanderbilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Vanderbilt) and [Cornelius Vanderbilt IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Vanderbilt_IV). The latter was married seven times and had five of his six divorces in Nevada. Mr. Vanderbilt was so taken with Reno that, unlike most migrant divorcees, he eventually settled there permanently.[^cite_note-Smithsonian_Magazine-21][^cite_note-Reno_Divorce_History-22]

In the 1939 film *[The Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Women_\(1939_film\))*, Reno and its divorce culture serve as a backdrop to a significant part of the plot. [Ernie Pyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Pyle) once wrote in one of his columns, "All the people you saw on the streets in Reno were obviously there to get divorces." In [Ayn Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayn_Rand)'s novel *[The Fountainhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fountainhead)*, published in 1943, the New York-based female protagonist tells a friend, "I am going to Reno," which was understood as declaring their intention to get a divorce.[^cite_note-cultureamerica-23]

Reno's divorce industry declined rapidly after the 1970s as other states, including California (which legalized [no fault divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_fault_divorce) in 1969), liberalized their divorce laws, reducing Nevada's legal advantage.[^cite_note-Brean-18]

### Gambling and modern Reno

Reno took a leap forward when the state of Nevada legalized open gambling on March 19, 1931, at the same time as it liberalized its divorce laws. The statewide push for legal Nevada gambling was led by Reno entrepreneur Bill Graham, who owned the Bank Club Casino in Reno, which was on Center Street. No other state offered legalized casino gaming like Nevada did in the 1930s, and [casinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino) such as the Bank Club and Palace were popular.[^cite_note-24] A few states had legal [parimutuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parimutuel_betting) [horse racing](74711.md), but no other state had legal casino gambling.

Within a few years, the Bank Club, owned by [George Wingfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Wingfield), Bill Graham, and Jim McKay, was the state's largest employer and the largest casino in the world. Wingfield owned most of the buildings in town that housed gaming and took a percentage of the profits, along with his rent.[^cite_note-25]

As the divorce industry declined, gambling became the major Reno industry. While gaming pioneers such as "Pappy" and Harold Smith of Harold's Club and Bill Harrah of the soon-to-dominate Harrah's Casino set up shop in the 1930s, the war years of the 1940s cemented Reno as the place to play for two decades.[^cite_note-26] Beginning in the 1950s, the need for economic diversification beyond gaming fueled a movement for more lenient business taxation.[^cite_note-cultureamerica-23]

At 1\:03 pm, on February 5, 1957, two explosions, caused by natural gas leaking into the maze of pipes and ditches under the city, and an ensuing fire, destroyed five buildings in the vicinity of Sierra and First Streets along the Truckee River. The disaster killed two people and injured 49. The first explosion hit under the block of shops on the west side of Sierra Street (now the site of the Century Riverside), the second, across Sierra Street, now the site of the Palladio.[^cite_note-27]

The presence of a main east–west rail line, the emerging interstate highway system, a favorable state tax climate, and relatively inexpensive land created good conditions for warehousing and distribution of goods.[^cite_note-28]

In the 1980s, [Indian gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_gaming) rules were relaxed, and starting in 2000, Californian Native casinos began to cut into Reno casino revenues.[^cite_note-29] Major new construction projects have been completed in the Reno and Sparks areas. A few new luxury communities were built in [Truckee, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee,_California), about 28 miles (45 km) west of Reno on [Interstate 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_in_Nevada). Reno also is an outdoor recreation destination, due to its proximity to the Sierra Nevada, [Lake Tahoe](140899.md), and numerous [ski resorts](179821.md) in the region.[^cite_note-30]

In 2018, the city officially changed its flag after a local contest was held.[^cite_note-31] In recent years, the Reno metro area − spurred by large-scale investments from [Greater Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Seattle) and [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) companies such as [Amazon](90451.md), [Tesla](5533631.md), [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic), [Microsoft](19001.md), [Apple](856.md), and Google − has become a new [major technology center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_technology_centers) in the United States.[^cite_note-bloomberg.com-32]

## Geography

### Geology



Dog Valley, west of Reno, an area of active faulting

Reno is just east of the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada), on the western edge of the [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin) at an elevation of about 4,400 feet (1,300 m) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_sea_level). Numerous [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) exist throughout the region. Most of these are [normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dip-slip_faults) (vertical motion) faults associated with the extension of the earth's [lithosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithosphere) and the uplift of the various mountain ranges, including the Sierra Nevada.

In February 2008, [an earthquake swarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Reno_earthquakes) began to occur, lasting for several months. The largest event in the swarm was measured at [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) 4.9 by the USGS; some seismic analyses suggested it may have approached magnitude 5.0. The earthquakes were centered on the Somersett community in western Reno near [Mogul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogul,_Nevada) and [Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdi,_Nevada). Many homes in these areas were damaged.[^cite_note-33]

Nevada's high desert geology and vast federal lands attract amateur rockhounders and mineral collectors, who commonly find agate, opal, jasper, petrified wood, and fluorescent minerals.[^cite_note-34]

### Environmental considerations



Reno Nevada and the [Truckee Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_Meadows) south west of the Reno Tahoe International Airport has a large herd of [mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustang) horses. These horses nurse and range around the runoff of Steamboat Creek. The mustang is a notable iconic image of the Nevada range land, which includes Reno.

The Reno area is often subject to [wildfires](56106.md) that cause property damage and sometimes loss of life. Roughly 80% of homes in the area have a risk of being impacted by wildfire.[^cite_note-35] In August 1960, the Donner Ridge fire resulted in a loss of electricity to the city for four days.[^cite_note-36] In November 2011, arcing from powerlines [caused a fire in Caughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caughlin_Fire) in southwest Reno that destroyed 26 homes and killed one man. Just two months later, a [fire in Washoe Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_Drive_Fire) sparked by fireplace ashes destroyed 29 homes and killed one woman. Around 10,000 residents were evacuated, and a state of emergency was declared. The fires came at the end of Reno's longest recorded dry spell.[^cite_note-37] In September 2024, the [Davis Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Fire) burned heavily forested regions in Southwest Reno. Fourteen structures burned in the area, and twenty thousand people were evacuated.[^cite_note-38]

### Climate

Reno has a [cold semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) climate ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSk*), bordering a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*) to the west. It experiences moderately cold winters and hot summers; it is influenced by the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) mountains to the west and the more arid [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin) to the east.[^cite_note-Climate-Ohara1-39] It is situated across a varied geographic landscape, which extends from the foothills of the Sierra Nevada into the Truckee River valley. While Reno experiences a [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) effect from the surrounding mountains, its western portions can receive three to four times as much precipitation as those extending eastward.[^cite_note-Climate-Ohara4-40] Annual rainfall patterns in Reno adhere to a Mediterranean climate, with most precipitation occurring in fall, winter, and spring, followed by long, hot, dry summers.[^cite_note-O-41] However, Reno's average annual rainfall is slightly lower than that of Californian cities more typically associated with Mediterranean climates. The area's low [evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration) stemming from its moderate annual average temperature also bears similarity to semi-arid climates found in Nevada's Great Basin.[^cite_note-42]

The monthly daily average temperature ranges from 36.2 °F (2.3 °C) in December to 77.2 °F (25.1 °C) in July, with the [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation) occasionally reaching 40 °F (22 °C) in summer, still lower than much of the high desert to the east. There are 6.0 days of 100 °F (38 °C)+ highs, 65 days of 90 °F (32 °C)+ highs, 1.6 days with 70 °F (21 °C)+ lows, and 1.9 days with sub-10 °F (−12 °C) lows annually; the temperature reaches or dips below the freezing point on 122 days, and does not rise above freezing on only 4.1 of those days.[^cite_note-NOAA-43] The record high temperature is 108 °F (42 °C), which occurred on July 10 and 11, 2002, again on July 5, 2007, and again on July 16, 2023. The record low temperature is −17 °F (−27 °C), which occurred on January 21, 1916; the lowest temperature recorded at the airport is −16 °F (−27 °C), which occurred on four occasions, most recently on February 7, 1989.[^cite_note-NOAA-43] In addition, the region is windy throughout the year; the region's distinctive northwestern wind, known as the 'Washoe Zephyr,' has been noted by regional observers and writers.

Annual precipitation has ranged from 1.55 in (39.4 mm) in 1947 to 13.73 inches (348.7 mm) in 2017. The most precipitation in one month was 6.76 in (171.7 mm) in January 1916, and the most precipitation in 24 hours was 2.71 in (68.8 mm) on January 28, 1903. At [Reno–Tahoe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno%E2%80%93Tahoe_International_Airport), where records go back to 1937, the most precipitation in one month was 5.57 in (141.5 mm) in January 2017, and the most precipitation in 24 hours was 2.29 in (58.2 mm) on January 21, 1943.[^cite_note-NOAA-43]

Most [rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) occurs in winter and spring. Summer thunderstorms can occur between April and October. The eastern side of town and the mountains east of Reno tend to be prone to thunderstorms more often, and these storms may be severe because an afternoon downslope west wind, called a "[Washoe Zephyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_Zephyr)", can develop in the Sierra Nevada, causing air to be pulled down in the Sierra Nevada and Reno, destroying or preventing thunderstorms, but the same wind can push air upward against the Virginia Range and other mountain ranges east of Reno, creating powerful thunderstorms.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

Winter snowfall is usually light to moderate, but can be heavy on some days, averaging 20.9 inches (53 cm) annually. Snowfall varies with the lowest amounts (roughly 19–23 inches annually) at the lowest part of the valley at and east of the airport at 4,404 feet (1,342 m), while the foothills of the [Carson Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_Range) to the west ranging from 4,700 to 5,600 feet (1,400 to 1,700 m) in elevation just a few miles west of downtown can receive two to three times as much annual snowfall. The mountains of the [Virginia Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Range) to the east, meanwhile, can receive more summer thunderstorms and precipitation, and around twice as much annual snowfall above 5,500 feet (1,700 m). However, snowfall increases in the Virginia Range are less dramatic as elevation climbs than in the Carson Range to the west, because the Virginia Range is well within the rain shadow of the Sierra Nevada and Carson Range. The most snowfall in Reno in one winter was 72.3 inches (184 cm) in 1915–1916, with an astonishing 65.7 inches (167 cm) in January, the most in a calendar month, as well as 22.5 inches (57 cm) on January 17, the most in a calendar day; the most snowfall in a calendar year was 82.3 inches (209 cm) in 1916.[^cite_note-NOAA-43]

Reno's population grew 17.3% from 2010 to 2020, more than twice the U.S. rate of 7.4%.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] By 2025, Reno had grown 26% since 2010, to more than 283,000 residents. As of the census of 2010, there were 225,221 people, 90,924 households, and 51,112 families residing in the city. The population density was 2,186.6 inhabitants per square mile (844.3/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 102,582 housing units at an average density of 995.9 per square mile (384.5/km<sup>2</sup>). The city's racial makeup was 74.2% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans), 2.9% [African American](2154.md), 1.3% Native American, 6.3% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.7% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 10.5% some other race, and 4.2% from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) people of any race were 24.3% of the population.[^cite_note-Census_2010_DP-62] [Non-Hispanic Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_Whites) were 62.5% of the population in 2010,[^cite_note-Census_2010_DP-62] down from 88.5% in 1980.[^cite_note-63]



Map of racial distribution in Reno, 2010 U.S. Census. Each dot is 25 people: ⬤ White ⬤ Black ⬤ Asian ⬤ Hispanic ⬤ Other

At the 2010 census, there were 90,924 households, of which 29.8% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 38.4% were headed by married couples living together, 11.8% had a female householder with no husband present, and 43.8% were non-families. 32.1% of all households were made up of individuals, and 9.7% were someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.43, and the average family size was 3.10.[^cite_note-Census_2010_DP-62]

In the city, the 2010 population was spread out, with 22.8% under the age of 18, 12.5% from 18 to 24, 28.2% from 25 to 44, 24.9% from 45 to 64, and 11.7% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 34.6 years. For every 100 females, there were 103.4 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 102.7 males.[^cite_note-Census_2010_DP-62]

In 2011, the city's estimated median household income was \$44,846, and the median family income was \$53,896. Males had a median income of \$42,120 versus \$31,362 for females. The city's per capita income was \$25,041. About 9.6% of families and 14.4% of the population were below the poverty line, including 15.1% of those under age 18 and 12.8% of those age 65 or over.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-GR6-65] The population was 180,480 at the [2000 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census); in 2010, its population had risen to 225,221, making it the third-largest city in the state after [Las Vegas](47737.md) and [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_Nevada), and the largest outside [Clark County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada). Reno lies 26 miles (42 km) north of the Nevada state capital, [Carson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_City,_Nevada), and 22 miles (35 km) northeast of [Lake Tahoe](140899.md) in a [shrub-steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrub-steppe) environment. Reno shares its eastern border with the city of Sparks and is the larger of the principal cities of the [Reno–Sparks, Nevada Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno%E2%80%93Sparks_metropolitan_area) (MSA), a [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_metropolitan_area) that covers [Storey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey_County,_Nevada) and Washoe counties.[^cite_note-66] The MSA had a combined population of 425,417 at the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Census).[^cite_note-67]

There is an [Italian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans) community in Reno.[^cite_note-68]

Until the 1960s, Reno was the gambling capital of the United States, but Las Vegas's rapid growth, [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines)' 2000 buyout of [Reno Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Air), and the growth of [Native American gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_gaming) in California have reduced its gambling economy. Older casinos were torn down ([Mapes Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapes_Hotel), [Fitzgerald's Nevada Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitzgeralds_Gaming), Primadonna, Horseshoe Club, [Harold's Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold's_Club), Palace Club), the Flamingo [Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Hotels_&_Resorts) and smaller casinos like the [Comstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comstock_Hotel_&_Casino), [Sundowner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundowner_\(hotel_and_casino\)), [Golden Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Montage_Reno), [Kings Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Street_Flats), Money Tree, Virginian, and Riverboat were either closed or were converted into residential units.

Because of its location, Reno has traditionally drawn the majority of its California tourists and gamblers from the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) and [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California) while [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley) has historically served more tourists from [Southern California](62520.md) and the [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_metropolitan_area) area.

Several large local hotel casinos have shown significant growth and have moved gaming further away from the downtown core. These larger hotel casinos are the [Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis_Casino_Resort), the [Peppermill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppermill_Reno), and the [Grand Sierra Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Sierra_Resort). The Peppermill was chosen as the most outstanding Reno gaming/hotel property by *Casino Player* and *Nevada* magazines. In 2005, the Peppermill Reno began a \$300 million [Tuscan](21967242.md)-themed expansion.

Reno holds several events throughout the year to draw tourists to the area. They include [Hot August Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_August_Nights)[^cite_note-69] (a classic car convention), Street Vibrations (a motorcycle fan gathering and rally), [the Great Reno Balloon Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Reno_Balloon_Race), a [Cinco de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinco_de_Mayo) celebration, [bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling) tournaments (held in the [National Bowling Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bowling_Stadium)), and the [Reno Air Races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Air_Races).

Several large commercial developments were constructed during the mid-2000s boom, such as [The Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Summit_\(Reno,_Nevada\)) in 2007 and [Legends at Sparks Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlets_at_Legends) in 2008.

Reno is the location of the [corporate headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_headquarters) for several companies, including [Braeburn Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braeburn_Capital), Hamilton, [Server Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_Technology), [EE Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EE_Technologies), [Caesars Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Entertainment_\(2020\)), ITS Logistics,[^cite_note-70] and [Port of Subs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Subs). Companies based in the [Reno metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_metropolitan_area,_Nevada) include [Sierra Nevada Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_Corporation) and [U.S. Ordnance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Ordnance). [International Game Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Game_Technology), [Bally Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bally_Technologies) and GameTech have a development and manufacturing presence.

Other companies headquartered in Reno include [Ormat Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormat_Technologies), a geothermal company, [Figure Technology Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_Technology_Solutions), a financial technology company, and [American Battery Technology Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Battery_Technology_Company), which develops methods for recycling batteries and extracting battery metals.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Positron AI, based in Reno, develops chips for artificial intelligence. In 2026, it raised \$230 million in a financing round that valued the company at \$1 billion.[^cite_note-74]

Since the turn of the 21st century, Greater Reno saw an influx of technology companies entering the area, following major initiatives and investments by investors from [Seattle](11388236.md) & the [Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Area). The first one in 1999 was [Amazon](90451.md) in [Fernley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernley,_Nevada). After the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), the state placed an increased focus on economic development. Thousands of new jobs were created.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-Devine_Intermodal-2017-76][^cite_note-rgj.com-77][^cite_note-rgj.com1-78][^cite_note-bondbuyer.com-79]

The [Tesla](5533631.md) [Gigafactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigafactory_1) at the [Tahoe Reno Industrial Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahoe_Reno_Industrial_Center) is one of the largest buildings in the country, purportedly covering 5.8 million square feet.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-Devine_Intermodal-2017-76][^cite_note-rgj.com-77][^cite_note-rgj.com1-78][^cite_note-bondbuyer.com-79] Although it was originally Tesla's largest factory, it's since been superseded by [Gigafactory Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigafactory_Texas), which has 10 million square feet.[^cite_note-81] It employs roughly 11,000 people, making Tesla larger than any employer in the city of Reno, though the Industrial Center is located just outside of the city.[^cite_note-82] In 2023 Tesla announced a \$3.6 billion expansion[^cite_note-83] of the facility that would incorporate an additional four million square feet, including an all-new plant for [Semis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Semi) and a much larger one for battery development. In 2023, the expansion was projected to create approximately 3,000 new jobs.[^cite_note-84]

The arrival of several data centers at the Tahoe Reno Industrial Center is further diversifying a region that was best known for distribution and logistics outside gaming and tourism. ## Arts and culture



Exterior of [Nevada Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Museum_of_Art)

Reno has several museums. The [Nevada Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Museum_of_Art) is the only [American Alliance of Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Alliance_of_Museums) (AAM) accredited art museum in Nevada and the largest collection of [Land art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_art).[^cite_note-87] The [National Automobile Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Automobile_Museum) contains 200 cars that were from the collection of [William F. Harrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Harrah), including [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley)'s 1973 [Cadillac Eldorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_Eldorado).[^cite_note-88]

Reno also hosts several music venues, such as the [Pioneer Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts), the [Reno Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Philharmonic_Orchestra), and the [Reno Pops Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Pops_Orchestra). The Reno Youth Symphony Orchestra (YSO), affiliated with the Reno Philharmonic, gives talented youth the opportunity to play advanced music and perform nationwide.[^cite_note-89] In 2016, they had the honor of performing at Carnegie Hall. A.V.A. Ballet Theatre is the resident ballet company of the Pioneer Center for the Performing Arts. All of their classical performances are with the Reno Philharmonic Orchestra.

Every July, Reno celebrates [Artown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artown), a visual and performing arts festival that lasts the entire month of July throughout the city. Along with performances, Artown partners with other institutions throughout the Reno Tahoe area to hold workshops, camps, and classes for all ages. All events are free of charge or low cost.[^cite_note-90]

The Reno Rodeo began in 1919 as the "Nevada Round-Up" and took its current name in 1935. For decades during rodeo week, members of the Reno Junior Chamber of Commerce drove through downtown in a paddy wagon called the "Black Maria". They picked up people who were not wearing Western clothes and brought them before a mock court, whose penalties raised money for charity.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] The modern event runs for ten days and draws an estimated 140,000 spectators.[^cite_note-93] In 2026, KTVN reported that the organization had around 900 active volunteers, who worked throughout the year and contributed tens of thousands of hours annually, while it employed about 15 people.[^cite_note-94]

In August, the festival [Hot August Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_August_Nights) is a major car festival in Reno across the city.[^cite_note-95]

Each September, Reno hosts [Northern Nevada Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Nevada_Pride).[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

The [Great Reno Balloon Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Reno_Balloon_Race), also held each September, is the world's largest free balloon race.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

Reno has a public library, a branch of the Washoe County Library System. The Downtown branch of the [Washoe County Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_County_Library) was listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in 2013.[^cite_note-nrhpdoc-101]

## Parks and recreation



Virginia Lake is a popular place for people to walk their dog in Reno.



River rafting on the 4th of July, 2018 on the [Truckee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_River)

Reno is home to a variety of recreational activities, including both seasonal and year-round. In the summer, Reno locals can be found near three major bodies of water: Lake Tahoe, the [Truckee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_River), and Pyramid Lake. The Truckee River originates at Lake Tahoe and flows west to east through the center of downtown Reno before terminating at Pyramid Lake to the north. The river is a major part of [Artown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artown), held in the summer at Wingfield Park. [Washoe Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_Lake) is a popular kite and [windsurfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsurfing) location because of its high wind speeds during the summer.

Skiing and snowboarding are among the most popular winter sports and draw many tourists. There are 18 ski resorts[^cite_note-106] (8 major resorts) as close as 11 miles (18 km) and as far as 98 miles (158 km) from the Reno–Tahoe International Airport, including [Northstar California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northstar_California), [Sierra-at-Tahoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra-at-Tahoe), [Alpine Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Meadows,_California), [Palisades Tahoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palisades_Tahoe), [Sugar Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_Bowl_Ski_Resort), [Diamond Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Peak_\(ski_area\)), [Heavenly Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavenly_Mountain_Resort), and [Mount Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rose_Ski_Tahoe). Other popular Reno winter activities include snowshoeing, ice skating, and snowmobiling. There are many bike paths to ride in the summertime. Lake Tahoe hosts international bike competitions each summer.

## Government

See also: [List of mayors of Reno, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Reno,_Nevada)

Reno has a democratic municipal government. The [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council) is the core of the government, with seven members. Six council members represent districts, with the mayor serving as the at-large representative. In 2023, the city restructured from five districts and two at-large seats to six districts and one at-large seat. In general, the top two vote earners in each ward make the ballot for the citywide election. The mayor serves as the at-large member representing the entire city. A second at-large position existed until the creation of the city’s sixth ward in 2023. The council has several duties, including setting priorities for the city, promoting communication with the public, planning development, and redevelopment.

There is an elected [city attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_attorney) who is responsible for civil and criminal cases. The City Attorney represents the city government in court and prosecutes misdemeanors.

The city's charter calls for a [council-manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council-manager_government) form of government, meaning the council appoints only two positions: the [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager), who implements and enforces the policies and programs the council approves, and the [city clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_clerk). The city manager is in charge of the budget and workforce for all city programs. The city clerk, who records the proceedings of the council, makes appointments for the council, and makes sure efficient copying and printing services are available.

In 2010, there was a ballot question asking whether the Reno city government and the Washoe County government should explore the idea of becoming one combined governmental body.[^cite_note-110] Fifty-four percent of voters approved of the ballot measure to inquire into consolidating the governments.[^cite_note-111]

### Fire department

The Reno Fire Department (RFD) operates 15 fire stations.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

The RFD provides all-risk emergency service to the City of Reno residents. All-risk emergency service is the national model of municipal fire departments, providing the services needed in the most efficient way possible.[^cite_note-114]

The department provides paramedic-level service to the citizens and visitors of Reno.

In addition to responding to fires, the fire department also provides rescue capabilities for almost any type of emergency. This includes emergency medical care for the citizens, a hazardous materials team capable of identifying unknown materials and controlling a release disaster, and management of large-scale incidents.[^cite_note-115]

### Roads

Reno was historically served by the [Victory Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Highway) and a branch of the [Lincoln Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Highway). After the formation of the [U.S. Numbered Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Numbered_Highways) system, [U.S. Route 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_40_in_Nevada) was routed along 4th Street through downtown Reno, before being replaced by [Interstate 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_in_Nevada). The primary north–south highway through Reno is [U.S. Route 395](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_395_in_Nevada)/[Interstate 580](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_580_\(Nevada\)).

### Bus

The [Regional Transportation Commission of Washoe County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Transportation_Commission_of_Washoe_County) (RTC) has a bus system that provides intracity buses, intercity buses to Carson City, and an on-demand shuttle service for disabled persons.[^cite_note-133] The system has its main terminal on 4th Street in downtown Reno and secondary terminals in Sparks and at [Meadowood Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowood_Mall) in south Reno.

Numerous shuttle and excursion services are offered connecting the [Reno–Tahoe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno%E2%80%93Tahoe_International_Airport) to various destinations:

- North Lake Tahoe Express provides connecting shuttle service to North Lake Tahoe Resorts

- South Tahoe Airporter provides connecting shuttle service to [South Lake Tahoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lake_Tahoe,_California) resorts.

- [Eastern Sierra Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Sierra_Transit_Authority) provides shuttles to destinations south along the US-395 corridor in California, such as [Mammoth Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammoth_Mountain) and [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_California)

- Modoc Sage Stage provides shuttles to [Alturas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alturas,_California) and [Susanville, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanville,_California), along the northern US-395 corridor.

- Salt Lake Express provides service to [Las Vegas](47737.md) mainly along the southern US-95 corridor.

[Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) stops at a downtown terminal. [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(North_America\)) stopped at the [Silver Legacy Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Legacy_Reno), but has since discontinued service to Reno.[^cite_note-134]

### Rail



The [*Overland Limited*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overland_Limited_\(UP_train\)) at Reno in 1913



The tram at [Circus Circus Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Circus_Reno)

Reno was historically a stopover along the [First transcontinental railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad); the modern [Overland Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overland_Route_\(Union_Pacific_Railroad\)) continues to run through Reno. Reno was additionally the southern terminus of the [Nevada–California–Oregon Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada%E2%80%93California%E2%80%93Oregon_Railway) (NCO) and the northern terminus of the [Virginia and Truckee Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_and_Truckee_Railroad). Using the NCO depot and right of way, the [Western Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pacific_Railroad) also provided rail service to Reno. In the early 20th century, Reno also had a modest [streetcar system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetcars_in_Reno). Downtown Reno has two historic train depots, the inactive [Nevada-California-Oregon Railroad Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada-California-Oregon_Railroad_Depot) and the active Amtrak depot at [Reno station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_station), originally built by the [Southern Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pacific_Railroad).[^cite_note-135]

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) provides daily passenger service to Reno via the *[California Zephyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Zephyr)* at Reno station and via multiple [Amtrak Thruway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Thruway) buses that connect to trains departing from [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California).

### Air

The city is served by [Reno–Tahoe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno%E2%80%93Tahoe_International_Airport), with general aviation traffic handled by [Reno Stead Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Stead_Airport). Reno–Tahoe International Airport is the second busiest commercial airport in the state of Nevada after [Harry Reid International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Reid_International_Airport) in [Las Vegas](47737.md). Reno was the hub and headquarters of the defunct airline [Reno Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Air).

## Contemporary Development

Reno has experienced significant contemporary development and urban growth in recent decades, transforming from its historical association with gambling and divorce into a diversified modern city. The region has seen expansion in technology industries, with major companies establishing offices and operations in the Reno area. Urban revitalization projects have focused on downtown redevelopment, including the [Truckee Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_Meadows)' waterfront areas and mixed-use developments that blend residential, commercial, and recreational spaces.

Tourism remains a key economic driver, with the city serving as a gateway to [Lake Tahoe](140899.md) and benefiting from outdoor recreation opportunities. The city has invested in infrastructure improvements, including transportation networks and public amenities that enhance its appeal to residents and visitors. Contemporary development initiatives have also emphasized [sustainable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability) practices, including green building standards and environmental conservation efforts in the surrounding region.

The city's proximity to the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) mountains and its elevation of approximately 4,505 feet (1,374 meters) continue to make it an attractive destination for both tourists and residents seeking outdoor recreation. The modernization of Reno's economy has helped diversify its revenue streams while maintaining its historical character and identity.

## Utilities

The [Truckee Meadows Water Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_Meadows_Water_Authority) provides potable water for the city. The [Truckee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truckee_River) is the primary water source. It supplies Reno with 80 million U.S. gallons (300 [Ml](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalitre)) of water a day during the summer, and 40 million U.S. gallons (150 Ml) of water per day in the winter. The two [water treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_treatment) plants are Chalk Bluff and Glendale. The Chalk Bluff plant's main intakes are west of Reno and south of [Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdi,_Nevada), with the water flowing through a series of [flumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flume) and ditches to the plant. Alternative intakes are below the plant along the banks of the Truckee River itself. The Glendale plant is alongside the river, and is fed by a rock and concrete rubble [diversion dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversion_dam) a short distance upstream.[^cite_note-tmwa-136]

[Sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) for most of the Truckee Meadows region takes place at the Truckee Meadows Water Reclamation Facility at the eastern edge of the valley. Treated effluent returns to the Truckee River by way of Steamboat Creek, which also receives water from the historic [Steamboat Ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat_Ditch).[^cite_note-tmwrf-137] In the 1990s, this capacity was increased from 20 to 30 million U.S. gallons (70 to 110 million liters) per day. While treated, the effluent contains suspended solids, [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen), and [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus), aggravating water-quality concerns of the river and its receiving waters of [Pyramid Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_Lake_\(Nevada\)). Local agencies working with the [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) have developed several watershed management strategies to accommodate this expanded discharge. To accomplish this successful outcome, the [DSSAM Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSSAM_Model) was developed and calibrated for the Truckee River to analyze the most cost-effective available management strategy set.[^cite_note-138]

[NV Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NV_Energy), formerly Sierra Pacific, provides electric power and natural gas. Power comes from multiple sources, including Tracy-Clark Station to the east, and the [Steamboat Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat_Springs,_Nevada) [binary cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_cycle) power plants at the southern end of town.[^cite_note-139]
