**Latakia Governorate** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مُحافظة اللاذقية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Muḥāfaẓat al-Lāḏiqiyya*), officially known as **Lattakia Governorate**,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] is one of the fourteen [governorates](690220.md) of [Syria](7515849.md). It is situated in northwestern Syria, bordering [Turkey](11125639.md)'s [Hatay Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatay_Province) to the north, [Idlib](7445043.md) and [Hama Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama_Governorate) to the east, [Tartus Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus_Governorate) to the south, and the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) to the west. Its reported area varies in different sources from 2,297 km<sup>2</sup> (887 sq mi)[^cite_note-4] to 2,437 km<sup>2</sup> (941 sq mi).[^cite_note-Statoids-5] The governorate has a population of 1,008,000 (2011 estimate).

## History



[Triumphal Arch of Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latakia_Tetraporticus) in Latakia, from an 1810 illustration by [Luigi Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Mayer)

The governorate was historically part of the [Alawite State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawite_State), which existed from 1920 to 1936.[^cite_note-L-6]

Tartus Governorate was formerly included as part of Latakia, before being split off circa 1972.[^cite_note-Statoids-5]

The region has been relatively peaceful during the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), being a generally pro-[Assad](364813.md) region that had largely remained under government control. The [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_al-Haffah) Al-Haffah in 2012, and unsuccessfully attempted to wrest control of the province in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Latakia_offensive),[^cite_note-redeploy-7] [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Latakia_offensive)[^cite_note-leithfadel-8] and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Latakia_offensive).[^cite_note-9] In 2016 the [Islamic State](9087364.md) conducted a [bomb attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2016_Jableh_and_Tartous_bombings) on Jableh, resulting in many deaths.[^cite_note-10]

On 8 March 2025, the UK-based [SOHR](34029669.md) reported that [Syrian security forces](27479.md) and pro-government fighters had committed a [massacre](79404646.md) of more than 750 Alawite civilians during [clashes in western Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2025_Western_Syria_clashes).[^cite_note-11]

In January 2026, the Latakia Governorate issued a decree prohibiting female public sector employees from wearing make-up during official work hours.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] However, the governorate clarified that it "aims to regulate professional workplace appearance, not prohibit - by avoiding excessive use of cosmetics."

## Geography

The Latakia Governorate comprises about half of Syria's Mediterranean coastline. The western part of the governorate consists mainly of coastal plains, with the inland eastern parts being mountainous, with the [Syrian Coastal Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Coastal_Mountain_Range) (Nusayriyah Mountains) running north to south.[^cite_note-Syria-profile-14] Its highest peak, Nabi Yunis, is 1,562 meters (5,125 ft) tall with the average elevation only about 1,200 meters.[^cite_note-Syria-profile-14] The western areas of the governorate catch moisture-laden winds from the Mediterranean Sea and are thus more fertile and more heavily populated than the eastern slopes.

The [Orontes River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontes_River) flows north alongside the range on its eastern verge in the [Al-Ghab Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghab_Plain), a 64 kilometres (40 mi) [longitudinal trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench#Geology),[^cite_note-15] and then around the northern edge of the range to flow into the Mediterranean. Another important river is [Nahr al-Kabir al-Shamali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahr_al-Kabir_al-Shamali), a river running from the Turkish [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria-Turkey_border) and to the southwest to flow in the Mediterranean, with the 16 Tishreen dam, one of the most important in the region, being constructed for power generation, storage of rain and river water, and the creation of [Mashqita Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_16_Teshreen).[^cite_note-16]

## Economy



The [Port of Latakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Latakia), Syria's main seaport

The governorate is located on the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), which gave it great economic importance, with the capital of the governorate, [Latakia](195594.md) serving as Syria's main port. Its [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Latakia) was established on 12 February 1950.[^cite_note-21]

Its imported cargo includes clothing, construction materials, vehicles, furniture, minerals, tobacco, cotton, and food supplies such as lintels, onions, wheat, barley, dates, grains and figs; in 2008, the port handled about 8 million tons of cargo.

The governorate is also a popular domestic tourist destination, with the [Cote d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cote_d'Azur,_Syria) B=beach of Latakia being Syria's premier coastal resort, offering water skiing, jet skiing, and windsurfing.[^cite_note-mannheim-22] The city contains eight hotels, two of which have five-star ratings; both the Cote d'Azur de Cham Hotel and Lé Meridien Lattiquie Hotel are located 6 kilometres (3.7 mi) north of the city, at Cote d'Azur. The latter hotel has 274 rooms and is the only international hotel in the city. Latakia is also host to numerous designer-label stores, notably on 8 Azar Street, and the heart of the city's shopping area is the series of blocks enclosed by 8 Azar Street, Yarmouk Street, and Saad Zaghloul Street in the city centre. [Cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_theater) in Latakia include Ugarit Cinema, al-Kindi, and a smaller theater off al-Moutanabbi Street.

As per the [2004 Syrian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Syrian_census) the population was 879,550.[^cite_note-Statoids-5] A 2011 [UNOCHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOCHA) estimate put the population at 1,008,000, though this has likely changed since the start of the war.[^cite_note-23]

At the end of 2021, Latakia's population reached 1,634,000, accounting for 6% of Syria's total population. The majority at 68% are [Alawite Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawite_Muslim), followed by 17% [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim), 15% [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Syria), 0.2% [Twelver Shia Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver_Shia_Muslim) and 0.2% [Ismaili Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili_Muslim).[^cite_note-24]

### Languages

The primary languages of the province are [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) and [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) ([Syrian Turkmen dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Turkmen#Language)). Arabic is spoken in all district centres of the governorate and most, if not all towns and villages surrounding them, with the [North Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Levantine_Arabic) dialect mostly used. An exception is [Kessab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kessab), a historically Armenian-populated town where Armenian is the primary language in it and the surrounding villages such as Sev Aghpyur, Esguran and Duzaghaj,[^cite_note-25] and the [Turkmen Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_Mountain) where Turcoman is spoken primarily, though many Turkmen have fled the area since the start of the civil war.[^cite_note-26]
