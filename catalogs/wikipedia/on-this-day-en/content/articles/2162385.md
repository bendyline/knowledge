The **Beqaa Valley** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): وادي البقاع, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Wādī al-Biqāʿ*, [Lebanese Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Arabic_language): [\[bʔaːʕ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Lebanese_Arabic); also romanized as **Bekaa**, **Bekai**, **Biqâ**, and **Becaa**) is a fertile valley in eastern [Lebanon](17771.md) and its most important [farming region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Lebanon).[^cite_note-Globaleye-1] Industry, especially the country's agricultural industry, also flourishes in Beqaa. The region broadly corresponds to the [Coele-Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coele-Syria) of [classical antiquity](252905.md).[^cite_note-2]

The Beqaa is located about 30 km (19 mi) east of [Beirut](37428.md). The valley is situated between [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) to the west and the [Anti-Lebanon Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Lebanon_Mountains) to the east.[^cite_note-Masri1997-3] It is the northern continuation of the [Jordan Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Rift_Valley), and thus part of the [Great Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley), which stretches from [Syria](7515849.md) to the [Red Sea](26191.md) and then on to southeast Africa. Beqaa Valley is 120 kilometres (75 mi) long and 16 kilometres (9.9 mi) wide on average. It has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) of wet, often snowy winters and dry, warm summers.[^cite_note-4]

## History

### Pre-history

In [Baalbek](58207.md), that is part of a valley to the east of the northern Beqaa Valley, there are evidence of continual habitation dating back almost 8000–9000 years. [Ard Tlaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ard_Tlaili) is a small tell mound with an archaeological site, located on a plain at the foot of the Lebanon Mountain, just 11 km (7 mi) northwest of Baalbeck, in the Beqaa Valley. It dates to around 5780-5710 BC and has the southernmost pottery belonging to the [Halaf Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halaf_culture).[^cite_note-Copeland1969-5]

[Labweh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labweh) is a village at an elevation of 950 metres (3,120 ft) on a foothill of the Anti-Lebanon Mountains in Baalbek District, Baalbek-Hermel Governorate, Lebanon, settled since the [Neolithic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic).[^cite_note-Copeland1969-5]

### Bronze Age

In the [Middle Bronze IIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), the Beqa Valley was a highway between the regional power of [Qatna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatna) in the north and its vassal [Hazor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Hazor) in the south. The Beqaa valley was known as *[Amqu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amqu)* during the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age). The identity of the inhabitants is not known for certain, but the region was part of the [Amorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amorites) Kingdoms of [Amurru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amurru_kingdom) and [Qatna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatna#Kingdom_of_Qatna). To the southwest of Baalbek was [Enišasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni%C5%A1asi), a city or city-state mentioned in the [1350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1350_BC)-[1335 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1335_BC) [Amarna letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letters) [correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_corpus), written by two rulers of the city [Šatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0atiya) and [Abdi-Riša](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdi-Ri%C5%A1a).[^cite_note-6]

### Iron Age

By the early [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age), the Beqaa Valley came to be dominated by [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_language)-and [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic)-speaking populations.

In the 11th and 10th centuries BC, the [Aramaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arameans) founded the kingdom of [Aram-Zobah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zobah) (also Sobah), mentioned in the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible). Many scholars suggest it was located in the Beqa'a valley.[^cite_note-Abousamra2019-7] The precise whereabouts of [Zobah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zobah), a prominent city at the time, remains a subject of scholarly debate. In the 8th and 7th centuries BC, Sobah, now under [Imperial Assyrian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire), served as the residence for an Assyrian governor, Bel-liqbi.[^cite_note-Abousamra2019-7]

According to a surface survey study by German archaeologist L. Marfoe, the northern Beqaa, and thus the Beqaa valley as a whole, was only sparsely inhabited during the [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) period.[^cite_note-8]

### Hellenistic and Roman times

By the time of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), the valley was reportedly inhabited by the [Itureans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itureans), possibly an Arabic or [Aramaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arameans) people. According to the *[Histories of Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_of_Alexander_the_Great)*, the Itureans were Arab peasants living in the hills above [Tyre](30796.md) who slaughtered about 30 [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(ancient_kingdom\)), which prompted Alexander to conduct an expedition against them. Later on, the Itureans broke away from the weakened [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) to form the [Kingdom of Chalcis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Chalcis). From their base in the Bekaa, the Itureans expanded their territory to include the [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) cities of the coast and came close to [Damascus](8914.md). Their territory was eventually absorbed into the rest of [Roman Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Syria).

The valley was of considerable importance to the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) as one of the important agricultural regions in the eastern provinces, and it was known for its [many temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temples_of_the_Beqaa_Valley). The region also gained the attention of [Palmyrene](46709309.md) Queen [Zenobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenobia), who built the [Canalizations of Zenobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canalizations_of_Zenobia), linking the valley with Palmyra.

## Geography

The Beqaa Valley is a long, intermontane plain in eastern Lebanon, situated between the Lebanon Mountains to the west and the Anti Lebanon Mountains to the east. The valley form part of [Great Rift Valley system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley) and extends from the vicinity of [Baalbek](58207.md) in the north towards the upper [Jordan Valley](12105764.md) in the south. Its central section is broad and relatively flat, while the margins rise towards the surrounding ranges. The valley floor lies at an elevation of approximately 800–900 metres above sea level, though this varies along its length.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

### Topography and geology

The valley is structurally associated with the regional fault system that extends through the Levant. It is bounded to the west by the slopes of the Lebanon Mountains and to the east by the Anti Lebanon Mountains, both of which rise sharply above the valley floor, producing marked elevation changes over short distances. The central Baqaa is comparatively level, while the valley narrows and becomes more irregular towards its southern end. The Baqaa and its surrounding mountains are characterised by extensive limestone formations. Variations in the underlying carbonate rocks, combined with thew differences in relief and climate, have produced several types of calcareous soils across the valley and its margins. Erosion is most pronounced on steep mountains slopes, tend to be shallower.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

### Hydrology

The Baqaa forms principal watershed between two major river systems, the [Orontes River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontes_River), which rises in the northern valley and flows north into Syria, and the [Litani River](1920925.md), that rises in the central Baqaa and flows south before turning toward the Mediterranean. The Litani basin is described as the largest river basin located entirely within Lebanon.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] The Litani and its tributaries are fed by precipitation, snowmelt from the surrounding mountains and springs. [Lake Qaraoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Qaraoun), a reservoir created by a dam on the Litani, is described as the largest artificial body of surface water in Lebanon and forms a key part of the river's water management infrastructure. Springs and groundwater also supply irrigation and other water uses to the valley.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### Agriculture and land use

The valley's level terrain and water availability have made the Beqaa one of Lebanon's principal agricultural regions. Cultivated land covers substantial portion of the valley floor, and irrigation allows farming to continue where rainfall alone is insufficient. Crops include cereals, vegetables, fruit and grapes, with their distribution varying according to elevation, soil types and water availability. This agricultural landscape is closely tied to the valley's physical geography, the surrounding mountains shape precipitation and water supply, while the broad valley floor provides extensive areas suited to mechanised and irrigated farming. The combination of fertile land and a semi arid climate distinguishes the Beqaa from Lebanon's more humid coastal and mountainous regions.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Districts and towns



[Zahlé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahl%C3%A9), the capital of the Beqaa Governorate

[Zahle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahle) is the largest city and the administrative capital of the [Beqaa Governorate](866204.md). It lies just north of the main [Beirut](37428.md)–[Damascus](8914.md) highway, which bisects the valley. The majority of Zahle's residents are [Lebanese Christians](4796145.md), the majority being [Melkite Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Christianity_in_Lebanon), [Maronite Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite_Christianity_in_Lebanon), and [Greek Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Christianity_in_Lebanon). The town of [Anjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjar,_Lebanon), situated in the eastern part of the valley, has a predominantly [Armenian Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Lebanon) population and is famous for its 8th-century [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) ruins.[^cite_note-26] Further east, the town of [Majdal Anjar](23093567.md) has a [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Sunni_Muslims) majority.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

The majority of the inhabitants of the northern districts of Beqaa, [Baalbek](58207.md) and [Hermel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermel), are [Lebanese Shiites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam_in_Lebanon), with the exception of the town of [Deir el Ahmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_el_Ahmar), whose inhabitants are [Christians](4796145.md). The Baalbek and Hermel districts have a [Christian](4796145.md) and [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Lebanon) minority, mainly situated further north along the border with Syria.[^cite_note-\:1-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

The western and southern districts of the valley also have a mixed population of [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon), [Christians](4796145.md), and [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_in_Lebanon). The town of [Joub Janine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joub_Jannine) with a population of about 12,000, is situated midway in the valley, and its population is [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Lebanon). Joub Janine is the governmental center of the region known as Western Beqaa, with municipal services like the [serail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saray_\(building\)), which is the main government building in the area, [emergency medical services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services) ([Red Cross](15487.md)), a [fire department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_department), and a [courthouse](58609.md).[^cite_note-\:1-31]



Municipal garden of [Qabb Ilyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qabb_Ilyas)

Other towns in the Western Beqaa district are [Machghara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machghara), Sabghine, [Kamed al Lawz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamid_el-Loz), [Qab Elias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qabb_Ilyas), [Sohmor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohmor), [Yohmor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohmor,_Beqaa). The towns are all a mix of different Lebanese religious confessions. [Rachaiya al Wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashaya), east of the Western Beqaa district, is home to Lebanon's share of [Mount Hermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hermon) and borders Syria also. The district's capital, also Rachaiya al Wadi, not to be confused with [Rachaiya al Foukhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachaya_Al_Foukhar) in South Lebanon, is famous for its old renovated souk and what is known as the castle of independence in which Lebanon's pre-independence leaders were held by French troops before being released in 1943. The southern section of the district is inhabited with Druze and Christian Lebanese, while the other northern section is mainly inhabited by Sunni Lebanese.[^cite_note-\:1-31]

Due to wars and the unstable economic and political conditions Lebanon faced in the past, with difficulties some farmers still face today, many previous inhabitants of the valley left for coastal cities in Lebanon or emigrated from the country altogether, with the majority residing in [North America](21139.md), [South America](26769.md) or [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### Climate

The region receives limited rainfall, particularly in the north, because Mount Lebanon creates a [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) that blocks precipitation coming from the sea. The northern section has an average annual rainfall of 230 millimetres (9.1 in), compared to 750 millimetres (30 in) in the central valley.[^cite_note-38] Nevertheless, two rivers originate in the valley: the [Orontes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontes_River) (Asi), which flows north into Syria and [Turkey](11125639.md), and the [Litani](1920925.md), which flows south and then west to the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md).[^cite_note-Masri1997-3]

From the 1st century BC, when the region was part of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the Beqaa Valley served as a source of grain for the [Roman provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant). Today the valley makes up 40 percent of Lebanon's [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land).[^cite_note-welthungerhilfe-39] The northern end of the valley, with its scarce rainfall and less fertile soils, is used primarily as grazing land by [pastoral nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomadic_pastoralism). Farther south, more fertile soils support crops of wheat, [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), and vegetables, with [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard) and [orchards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard) centered on [Zahlé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahl%C3%A9).&#x20;

The valley also produces [hashish](14415.md) and cultivates [opium poppies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_poppy), which are exported as part of the [illegal drug trade](218449.md).[^cite_note-welthungerhilfe-39]

## Wines

Main article: [Lebanese wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_wine)

The Beqaa Valley, often referred to as the Bekaa Valley in the wine industry, particularly the expansive agricultural regions in its eastern areas, accounts the majority of Lebanon's renowned wine production.[^cite_note-Osseiran-40] Wine making is a tradition that goes back 6000 years in [Lebanon](17771.md). With an average altitude of 1000 m above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level), the valley's climate is very suitable to vineyards. Abundant winter rain and much sunshine in the summer helps the grapes ripen easily. ## Illicit drugs

Further information: [Drug economy in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_economy_in_Lebanon)



Farmers harvesting [cannabis](1481886.md) in the Bekaa Valley, 2021

Drugs have a long tradition in the Beqaa Valley, from the days of the Roman Empire to the present. Cultivators and tribal drug lords have worked with militias to build up a thriving [cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis) trade. The region has been compared to [Peru](170691.md)'s [cocaine](7701.md) producing [Upper Huallaga Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Huallaga_Valley).[^cite_note-Davids2002-46]

During the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War), [cannabis cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_cultivation) was a major source of income in the Beqaa Valley, where most of the country's [hashish](14415.md) and [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium) was produced. The war led various groups to turn to [drug trafficking](218449.md) for income. Syria, which controlled most of the Valley, profited significantly from the trade. [Palestinian](23267.md) militant groups, including the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization), also participated in the hashish trade, making millions of dollars.[^cite_note-Davids2002-46]

The trade collapsed during the [worldwide crackdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_Drugs) on narcotics led by the United States in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-MEI567p19-47] Under pressure from the U.S. State Department, the occupying Syrian Army plowed up the Beqaa's cannabis fields and sprayed them with poison. Prior to 1991 it was estimated that income generated from illicit crops grown in the Beqaa was around \$500 million. According to the [UNDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNDP) the annual per capita income at that time in the [Baalbek and Hermel](53254600.md) district did not exceed \$500. The same agency estimated the figure for the rest of Lebanon was \$2,074.[^cite_note-MEI567p19-47]

Since the mid-1990s, the culture and production of drugs in the Beqaa Valley has been in steady decline. By 2002, an estimated 2,500 hectares[^cite_note-48] of cannabis were limited to the extreme north of the Valley, where government presence remains minimal. Every year since 2001 the [Lebanese Army](17779.md#Lebanese_Ground_Forces) plows cannabis fields in an effort to destroy the crops before harvest.[^cite_note-49] It is estimated that that action eliminates no more than 30% of overall crops. Although important during the civil war, [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium) cultivation has become marginal, dropping from an estimated 30 metric tonnes per year in 1983 to negligible amounts in 2004.

Due to increasing political unrest that weakened the central Lebanese government during the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) and 2007 Opposition boycott of the government, and due to the lack of viable alternatives, [UN](31769.md) promises of irrigation projects and alternative crop subsidies that never materialized, drug cultivation and production have significantly increased.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] They remain a fraction of the civil war era production and are limited north of the town of [Baalbek](58207.md), where the rule of tribal law protecting armed families is still strong.
