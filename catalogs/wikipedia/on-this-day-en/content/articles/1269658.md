[Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) impersonator [Joe Wiegand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Wiegand) performs 27 October 2008 in the [East Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Room) of the [White House](33057.md), during a celebration of Roosevelt's 150th birthday.

An **impersonator** is someone who imitates or copies the behavior or actions of another.[^cite_note-1] There are many reasons for impersonating someone:

- [Living history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_history): After close study of some historical figure, a performer may dress and speak "as" that person for an audience.

  [^cite_note-LARB-2]

  &#x20;Such historical interpretation may be a scripted dramatic performance like *[Mark Twain Tonight!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain_Tonight!)* or an unscripted interaction while staying in character.



Dean Howe impersonating [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley)

- Entertainment: An "[impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionist_\(entertainment\))" impersonates well-known figures in order to entertain an audience. Especially popular objects of impersonation are [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley) (*see [Elvis impersonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_impersonator)*), [Michael Jackson](14995351.md) (*see [Michael Jackson impersonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson_impersonator)*) and [Madonna](142056.md) (see *[Madonna impersonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_impersonator)*). Other uses of impersonation for entertainment include male [drag queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_queens) (previously called "[female impersonators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_impersonators)", although this terminology is now considered outdated.)

- Crime: As part of a [criminal act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime) such as [identity theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_theft).

  [^cite_note-3]

  [^cite_note-4]

  &#x20;This is usually where the criminal is trying to assume the identity of another, in order to commit [fraud](58790.md), such as accessing confidential information, or to gain property not belonging to them. Also known as [social engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_engineering_\(security\)) and [impostors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impostor).

- [Decoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_decoy), used as a form of protection for political and military figures.

  [^cite_note-5]

  &#x20;This involves an impersonator who is employed (or forced) to perform during public appearances, to mislead observers.

- To create a romantic connection with someone, for example [catfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catfishing).

  [^cite_note-6]

## Celebrity



A [Michael Jackson impersonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson_impersonator) for the 25th anniversary of the album *[Thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_\(Michael_Jackson_album\))* at the 2008 [Tribeca Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca_Film_Festival) with performers from [Step It Up and Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Step_It_Up_and_Dance).



Patrick Knight as [Boy George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_George)



An impersonator of [George Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Michael)

**Celebrity impersonators** look similar to [celebrities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity) and dress in such a way as to imitate them. Impersonators are known as sound-alikes, look-alikes, impressionists, imitators and tribute artists.&#x20;

Some interest in celebrity impersonators, may arise from the consumer desire to see a celebrity who has died. The dead celebrity market was estimated to generate \$2.25 billion globally, with celebrity impersonators used in live entertainment shows and [advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising).[^cite_note-7]

One of the most prominent examples of this phenomenon is the case of [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley). [Edward Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Moss_\(impersonator\)) has appeared in movies and sitcoms, impersonating [Michael Jackson](14995351.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

There are other motivations for celebrity impersonation, [Tom Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Jones_\(singer\)) has attracted his share of [impersonators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Jones_impersonator) from different places around the world. From the United States, to South East Asia, to the UK, there are performers who either sound like him or imitate his act.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

A 2010 research study explored how [celebrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity) impersonation performance acts are understood by [audiences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audience), within an interactional frame, where the [performer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts) and audience collaborate by recognising the 'game' of pretending to be a celebrity. It proposed that this type of impersonation goes beyond imitation and is in fact a complex interaction, where the real and artificial coexist comfortably.[^cite_note-14]

## Criminal

A person who impersonates a designated officer in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) faces a prison term not to exceed 51 weeks in [England](9316.md), one year in [Scotland](26994.md), or 6 months in [Northern Ireland](21265.md).[^cite_note-15]

In the United States, it is an offence to impersonate a federal officer.[^cite_note-16] In 2025, there were a number of [arrests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest), due to civilians impersonating [immigration](4599312.md) officials,[^cite_note-17] whilst the [FBI](11127.md) began to probe an effort to access the personal phone of [Susie Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Wiles), the [White House](33057.md) chief of staff, that had involved impersonation.[^cite_note-18]

In a [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Colorado) case, an immigrant was charged with "criminal impersonation" for using another person's [Social Security number](22255742.md) when signing up for a job,[^cite_note-19] some courts have ruled that supplying this wrong information may not be criminal.[^cite_note-20] The ruling hinges on whether there was harm to the other person.[^cite_note-21]

Often, criminal impersonation involves someone impersonating a victim for financial gain. In [Australia](4689264.md), a woman in Melbourne used three victims identities to file ten [fraudulent](58790.md) business activity statements and registered as a tax agent, in order to commit criminal offences.[^cite_note-22]

## Online

The internet has resulted in new forms of impersonators emerging online. This can involve acts such as the impersonation of someone else's identity, across a variety of platforms, such as [social media](5897742.md).[^cite_note-23]

Within [online dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_dating), the phenomenon of [catfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catfishing) has arisen, where individuals impersonate the identity of someone else, whilst forming romantic relationships. Motivations for this can include to check on partner [fidelity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidelity), for monetary gain or simply out of curiosity.[^cite_note-24]

Online impersonation can be used as a [cyberbullying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberbullying) tactic, that in some instances creates a permanent and accessible record that anyone can view. This can have a significant impact, such as hindering employment prospects.[^cite_note-25]

Online impersonation has led to debates around whether identity verification should be a requirement on some online platforms.[^cite_note-26] Some jurisdictions are attempting to introduce new laws to help combat this problem, for example in [Nepal](171166.md).[^cite_note-27]

## Deepfake

Main article: [Deepfake](56641018.md)



Manipulated content: Is it real or fake?

[Audio deepfakes](65679106.md) have been used as part of [social engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_engineering_\(security\)) scams, fooling people into thinking they are receiving instructions from a trusted individual.[^cite_note-Statt-2019-28]

In 2019, a UK based energy firm's CEO was scammed over the phone when he was ordered to transfer €220,000 into a Hungarian bank account by an individual who used audio deepfake technology to impersonate the voice of the firm's holding company's chief executive.[^cite_note-Damiani-2019-29]

The combination advances in deepfake technology, which can clone an individual's voice from a recording of a few seconds to a minute, and new [text generation tools](73291755.md), has enabled automated impersonation scams that can target victims using a convincing digital clone of someone known to the person.[^cite_note-30]

Celebrity impersonation has been conducted using [deepfake](56641018.md) technology, for different purposes. For entertainment purposes, deepfake celebrity impersonation has been used.[^cite_note-31] A number of cases were reported that involved [scam](96637.md) victims believing they were communicating with celebrities.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]
