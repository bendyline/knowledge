The **Embassy of the United States in Kuwait City** is the [diplomatic mission](8970.md) of the [United States of America](3434750.md) in [Kuwait](7515890.md).

## History

Diplomatic relations between the United States and Kuwait were established on September 22, 1961, following Kuwait's full independence from the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1] The U.S. opened its first consulate in Kuwait on June 27, 1951, and began operating publicly on October 15 of the same year with Enoch Duncan as the U.S. Consul.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1] On December 11 and 27, 1960, a bilateral agreement with Kuwait on non-immigrant passport visas was concluded, underscoring the recognition of Kuwait as a sovereign state.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1]

On 12 December 1983, a truck laden with 45 large cylinders of gas connected to [plastic explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_explosive) broke through the front gates of the American Embassy in [Kuwait City](57021.md) and rammed into the embassy's three-story administrative annex, demolishing half the structure. The shock blew out windows and doors in distant homes and shops.[^cite_note-2] A State department spokesman initially declared that damage at the embassy was extensive, and that non-American embassy workers were among the dead and the wounded.[^cite_note-3] According to the US State Department, the attack on the US embassy in Kuwait City led to a "comprehensive review of overseas security and the Department’s establishment of the Bureau of Diplomatic Security (DS) on November 4, 1985".[^cite_note-4]

On August 2, 1990, [Iraq invaded Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_invasion_of_Kuwait). Initially, US diplomats were not to evacuate to publicly defy Iraq's invasion of the country.[^cite_note-\:0-5] The decision was later reversed after Iraq freed approximately 750 US citizens held in Iraq and Kuwait, which allowed American Ambassador [W. Nathaniel Howell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Nathaniel_Howell) and embassy staff to leave Kuwait on December 13, 1990, after 110 days spent in the embassy without power and with scarce rations.[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-6] The United States, alongside a multinational coalition, expelled Iraqi forces from Kuwait in 1991.[^cite_note-policy-history-7][^cite_note-diplomacy.state.gov-8] The embassy reopened on March 1, 1991, following the liberation of Kuwait.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1] Since then, Kuwait has served as an important platform for U.S. and coalition operations, particularly during the [Iraq War](5043324.md) starting in 2003, and played a crucial role during the withdrawal of U.S. combat troops and equipment from Iraq in 2011.[^cite_note-policy-history-7]

In 2026, the embassy was hit by an Iranian missile strike during Iran's retaliatory attacks during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), prompting [US Secretary of State](32293.md) [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) to close the embassy until further notice.[^cite_note-9]
