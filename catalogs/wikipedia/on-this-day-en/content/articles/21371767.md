**Abidjan** ([/ˌæbɪˈdʒɑːn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*AB-ih-JAHN*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), French: [\[abidʒɑ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/ce/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Fabricio_Cardenas\_%28Culex%29-Abidjan.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Fabricio_Cardenas\_%28Culex%29-Abidjan.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/ce/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Fabricio_Cardenas_%28Culex%29-Abidjan.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Fabricio_Cardenas_%28Culex%29-Abidjan.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [N'ko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Ko_script): ߊߓߌߖߊ߲߬) is the seat of government, largest city and former capital of [Ivory Coast](57744.md). As of the [2021 census](5566.md), Abidjan's population was 6.3 million,[^cite_note-census_2021-3] which is 21.5 percent of the overall population of the country, making it the sixth most populous city proper in [Africa](5334607.md), after [Lagos](85232.md), [Cairo](6293.md), [Kinshasa](45773.md), [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), and [Johannesburg](58893.md). A cultural crossroads of [West Africa](67393.md), Abidjan is characterised by a high level of industrialisation and urbanisation. It is the most populous [French-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) city in West Africa.

The city expanded quickly after the construction of a new wharf in 1931, followed by its designation as the capital city of the then-French colony in 1933. The completion of the [Vridi Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vridi_Canal) in 1951 enabled Abidjan to become an important [sea port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_port). Abidjan remained the capital of Ivory Coast after its independence from France in 1960. In 1983, the city of [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro) was designated as the official political capital of Ivory Coast. However, Abidjan has officially been designated as the "economic capital" of the country, because it is the largest city in the country and the centre of its economic activity. Many political institutions and all foreign embassies continue to be located in Abidjan as well. The [Abidjan Autonomous District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abidjan_Autonomous_District), which encompasses the city and some of its suburbs, is one of the 14 [districts of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Ivory_Coast).

## Etymology

According to [oral tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_tradition) of the [Tchaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchaman) as reported in the *Encyclopedic Dictionary of Côte d'Ivoire*,[^cite_note-5] the name "Abidjan" results from a misunderstanding. Legend states that an old man carrying branches to repair the roof of his house met a European explorer who asked him the name of the nearest village. The old man did not speak the language of the explorer, and thought that he was being asked to justify his presence in that place. Terrified by this unexpected meeting, he fled shouting "*min-chan m'bidjan*", which means in the [Ebrié language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebri%C3%A9_language): "I just cut the leaves." The explorer, thinking that his question had been answered, recorded the name of the locale as *Abidjan*.

A slightly different and less elaborate version of the legend: When the first colonists asked a native man the name of the place, the man misunderstood and replied "*M'bi min djan*": "I've just been cutting leaves".[^cite_note-statoids1-6]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Abidjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Abidjan)

### Colonial era



Abidjan boatmen.

Abidjan was originally a small [Atchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchaman) fishing village. In 1896, following a series of deadly [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever) [epidemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemics), French [colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonist) who had initially settled in [Grand-Bassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand-Bassam) decided to move to a safer place and in 1898 chose the current location of Abidjan.[^cite_note-7] In 1903 it officially became a town.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-EB-9] The settlers were followed by the colonial government, created in 1899. But then nearby [Bingerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingerville) became the capital of the French [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony), from 1900 until 1934.

The future Abidjan, situated on the edge of the *lagoon n'doupé* ("the lagoon in hot water"), offered more land and greater opportunities for trade expansion. The wharf in Petit Bassam (now [Port-Bouët](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port-Bou%C3%ABt)) south of town quickly overtook the wharf of [Grand-Bassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand-Bassam) in importance, and became the main point of economic access to the colony. In 1904, the rail terminus was located in the Port-Bouët area of Abidjan.[^cite_note-EB-9] Starting in 1904, when [Bingerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingerville) was not yet complete, Abidjan became the main economic hub of the colony of Ivory Coast and a prime channel for distributing products to the European [hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinterland), particularly through the Lebanese community, which was increasing in importance.

[Henri de Fougères](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Foug%C3%A8res?action=edit\&redlink=1) became governor of [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan) in 1924, and remained governor until his death in 1931. One of the main streets of Abidjan still bears his name.

In 1931, Plateau and what would become [Treichville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treichville) were connected by a floating bridge, more or less where the [Houphouët-Boigny Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houphou%C3%ABt-Boigny_Bridge) stands today. The year 1931 also saw [addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Address_\(geography\)) begin to be assigned to the streets of Abidjan for the first time. The addressing project was temporarily concluded in 1964, under the leadership of Mayor Konan Kanga, then badly redone American-style in 1993.

Abidjan became the third capital of Ivory Coast by a 1934 decree, following [Grand-Bassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand-Bassam) and [Bingerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingerville).[^cite_note-EB-9] The decision to designate Abidjan as the capital spurred from its railroad that would connect the North and South regions of the Ivorian colony.[^cite_note-\:0-10] Several villages in Tchaman were then deserted. The leader of the Tchaman community can still be found in Adjame ("center" or "meeting" in Tchaman), north of the Plateau.

South of the Plateau district (the current central district of the city of Abidjan), the village of *Dugbeo* was moved across the lagoon to Anoumabo, "the forest of fruit bats", which became the neighborhood of [Treichville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treichville) (now known as Commikro, city of clerks). Treichville was renamed in 1934, in honour of [Marcel Treich-Laplène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Treich-Lapl%C3%A8ne) (1860–1890), the first explorer of the Ivory Coast and its first colonial administrator, considered its founder. Instead of Dugbeyo, is the current Treich-Laplénie Avenue, the bus station and water lagoon buses in Plateau, and the Avenue [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) (commonly called Rue du Commerce).

The city was laid out like most colonial towns, on a [grid plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan). [Le Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau,_C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire) ("m'brato" in Tchaman) were inhabited by settlers. In the north, the city was inhabited by the colonized. The two zones were separated by the [Gallieni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallieni) Military Barracks, where now there is the current courthouse. City spaces were often segregated between French colonists and Ivorian natives.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

Near the port, originally named Boulevard de Marseille, settlers became defensive and stole a street sign of a famous street of Marseille renamed the street Canebière, a sand track. This is the legend behind the first Blohorn oil mills, in [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody) and a racetrack was built in the south of the city.

In Le Plateau in the 1940s, the Bardon Park Hotel was built, the first air-conditioned hotel working in [francophone Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophone_Africa).

Under the *Pacte Colonial* ([Colonial Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_pact)), Abidjan served as a port city that would connect the colonial hinterlands that produced raw materials to the industrial centers of France.[^cite_note-\:0-10] The construction of the 15m-deep [Vridi Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vridi_Canal) in 1950 aided in this endeavor by connecting the [Ebrié Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89bri%C3%A9_Lagoon) to the sea.[^cite_note-EB-9][^cite_note-\:0-10] By the late 1950s, Abidjan became the financial center of West Africa, challenging the dominance of [Dakar](44251.md).[^cite_note-EB-9][^cite_note-\:0-10] In 1958, the first bridge to connect [Petit-Bassam Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit_Bassam) with the mainland was completed.[^cite_note-EB-9]

During [World War II](32927.md), France's [Vichy government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Vichy_France) completed construction of the Port in Abidjan to counter British influence. After the war, France competed with other harbors in the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea), and due to increased foreign interest in the capital city, placed [protectionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionism) policies on imports, foreign consulates, and U.S. building contracts.[^cite_note-\:0-10] The Abidjan city council elections of 1945 sparked the beginnings of the nationalist movement that challenged France's authority.[^cite_note-11]

### After independence

When Ivory Coast became independent in 1960, Abidjan became the new country's administrative and economic center. The axis south of Treichville, towards the international airport and the beaches, became the heart of European and middle-class Abidjan. The city saw considerable population growth in the decades following independence, expanding from 180,000 inhabitants in 1960 to 1,269,000 in 1978.[^cite_note-12] Abidjan's skyline dates back to the economic prosperity of this period.[^cite_note-13]

New districts such as the upmarket Cocody were founded during this period; built to a large extent in a colonial style, Cocody has since become home to Ivory Coast's wealthy classes as well as expatriates and foreign diplomats. The district is home to the embassy of France, Hotel Ivoire (which for a long time, was the only African hotel to have a skating rink), and, since 2009, the largest U.S. embassy in West Africa.[^cite_note-14]

Construction on [St. Paul's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_Cathedral,_Abidjan), designed by Italian architect Aldo Spirito, began with the 1980 groundbreaking by [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) and was completed in 1985. From the 1980s, Abidjan's fortunes declined as a result of negligence on the part of officials as well as corruption and general degradation. In 1983, the village of Yamoussoukro became the new political capital of Ivory Coast under the leadership of President Félix Houphouët-Boigny, who was born in Yamoussoukro.

From 2002 to 2007 and especially from 2010 to 2011, Abidjan suffered from the consequences of the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ivorian_Civil_War) and [Second Ivorian Civil Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Ivorian_Civil_War). In November 2004, [armed conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_French%E2%80%93Ivorian_clashes) broke out between French forces and Ivorian forces loyal to [Laurent Gbagbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Gbagbo) after the Ivorian Air Force attacked [French peacekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Licorne) in northern Ivory Coast. After France destroyed Ivory Coast's air capabilities in retaliation, pro-Gbagbo groups staged riots and looting in Abidjan and targeted French homes, schools, and businesses.[^cite_note-15] French peacekeepers moved into the city to calm the situation. Other crises during the first civil war period include [the case of the *Probo Koala* in 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Ivory_Coast_toxic_waste_dump),[^cite_note-16] in which disposed products caused thousands of residents to seek medical attention. Ivory Coast's civil conflicts seriously impacted the security situation in Abidjan. Amidst the anti-French riots in November 2004, 4,000 prisoners in Abidjan escaped from the country's largest prison.[^cite_note-17]

Abidjan was one of the main theaters of the [2010–2011 Ivorian crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%932011_Ivorian_crisis) and the site of major demonstrations against incumbent president Gbagbo, including one on International Women's Day in 2011 that saw several demonstrators killed by Gbagbo's forces. Civil postwar violence persisted in urban districts with high territorial control by Ggabo's supporters. Districts became associated with specific political attributes, which Ggabo's forces used to target residents in [Abobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abobo) and [Yopougon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yopougon). [Ethnic homogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_homogenization) in segregated neighborhoods is suggested to have enabled such territorial turmoil.[^cite_note-\:1-18] The end of the crisis came with Gbagbo's capture in Abidjan in April 2011, following a major offensive by forces loyal to election winner [Alassane Ouattara](1173624.md) with support from France and the UN.[^cite_note-19] Overtime, the intensity of postwar violence has decreased.[^cite_note-\:1-18]

In 2025, a major overhaul of names from the colonial era will be reorganized to more African names.[^cite_note-20]

## Geography



Location map of Abidjan

Abidjan lies on the south-east coast of the country, on the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea). The city is located on the [Ébrié Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89bri%C3%A9_Lagoon). The business district, [Le Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau,_Ivory_Coast), is the center of the city, along with [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody), Deux Plateaux (the city's wealthiest neighborhood and a hub for diplomats), and [Adjamé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjam%C3%A9), a [slum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum) on the north shore of the lagoon. [Treichville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treichville) and [Marcory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcory) lie to the south, [Attecoube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attecoube), Locodjro, Abobo Doume and [Yopougon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yopougon) to the west, and [Île Boulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_Boulay) is located in the middle of the lagoon. Further south lies [Port Bouët](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Bou%C3%ABt), home to the [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix-Houphou%C3%ABt-Boigny_International_Airport) and main [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport). Abidjan is located at 5°25' North, 4°2' West (5.41667, –4.03333).[^cite_note-21]

### Climate



Beach of Vridi

Abidjan experiences a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate), according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): **Aw**), closely bordering a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate). Abidjan has nonconsecutive rainy seasons (precipitation above 60 millimetres (2.4 in) with a long rainy season from March to July and a short rainy season from September to December, and three dry months (January, February and August). Precipitation is abundant during the summer months, except for August, due to activation of the [Benguela Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Current), which reduces the precipitation total throughout the month.[^cite_note-22] The Benguela Current also lowers the mean temperature during August, making it the coolest month of the year, averaging 24.5 °C (76.1 °F). Abidjan has two additional dry months (January and February). Abidjan is generally humid, with average relative humidity above 80% throughout the year.

#### Climate change

A 2019 paper published in [PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) estimates that under [Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Concentration_Pathway#RCP_4.5), a "moderate" scenario of [climate change](5042951.md) where global warming reaches \~2.5–3 °C (4.5–5.4 °F) by 2100, the climate of Abidjan in the year 2050 would most closely resemble the current climate of [Manila](184334.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md). The annual temperature would increase by 1.8 °C (3.2 °F), and the temperature of the coldest month by 1.9 °C (3.4 °F), but the temperature of the warmest month would be 3.4 °C (6.1 °F) higher.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] According to [Climate Action Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Tracker), the current warming trajectory appears consistent with 2.7 °C (4.9 °F), which closely matches RCP 4.5.[^cite_note-29]

Moreover, according to the 2022 [IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report), Abidjan is one of 12 major African cities (Abidjan, [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Algiers](1644.md), [Cape Town](6653.md), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Dakar](44251.md), [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), [Durban](313650.md), [Lagos](85232.md), [Lomé](57067.md), [Luanda](18402.md) and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo)) which would be the most severely affected by the future [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). It estimates that they would collectively sustain cumulative damages of US\$65 billion under RCP 4.5 and US\$86.5 billion for the high-emission scenario RCP 8.5 by the year 2050. Additionally, RCP 8.5 combined with the hypothetical impact from [marine ice sheet instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability) at high levels of warming would involve up to US\$137.5 billion in damages, while the additional accounting for the "low-probability, high-damage events" may increase aggregate risks to US\$187 billion for the "moderate" RCP4.5, US\$206 billion for RCP8.5 and US\$397 billion under the high-end ice sheet instability scenario.[^cite_note-30] Since sea level rise would continue for about 10,000 years under every scenario of climate change, future costs of sea level rise would only increase, especially without adaptation measures.[^cite_note-31] [^cite_note-32]

### Territorial divisions

The Autonomous District of Abidjan (*District Autonome d'Abidjan*), which superseded the former City of Abidjan (*Ville d'Abidjan*) in 2001, is made up of 10 central [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Ivory_Coast), whose territory corresponds to the former City of Abidjan, and 4 outlying sub-prefectures: [Anyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyama), [Bingerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingerville), [Brofodoumé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brofodoum%C3%A9), and [Songon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songon).

The 10 communes of the former City of Abidjan are traditionally divided into northern Abidjan and southern Abidjan, with the Ébrié Lagoon separating both halves of the city.

In 1980, the municipality of Abidjan (*commune d'Abidjan*) was transformed into the City of Abidjan (*Ville d'Abidjan*), and its territory was divided in 10 [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Ivory_Coast). A city council and mayor of the City of Abidjan elected by the population oversaw the entire city, whereas 10 municipal councils also elected by the population managed each commune. Dioulo Emmanuel was elected mayor of the City of Abidjan on 30 November 1980.

In 2001, the City of Abidjan was disbanded and replaced by a larger Autonomous District of Abidjan.[^cite_note-35] The autonomous district overlaps the [Abidjan Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abidjan_Department), which was established in 1969 and has had its current boundaries since 1998. The district is made up of the 10 communes of the former City of Abidjan, and of 4 outlying sub-prefectures: [Anyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyama), [Bingerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingerville), [Brofodoumé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brofodoum%C3%A9), and [Songon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songon). Since the 2000s, the built-up area of Abidjan has largely expanded into these 4 formerly rural sub-prefectures.

As a result of the creation of the Autonomous District of Abidjan in 2001, the post of Mayor of City of Abidjan was replaced by that of District Governor, appointed by the head of state. The position has been occupied by [Ibrahim Bacongo Cissé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Bacongo_Ciss%C3%A9?action=edit\&redlink=1) since 2023. The District Governor manages Abidjan along with an assembly, the Autonomous District Council (*Conseil du District Autonome*), one-third of whose members are appointed by the government of Ivory Coast, and two-third are representatives from the municipal councils of the communes making up the district. Each of the ten communes of Abidjan has its own municipal council, headed by a mayor. The last municipal elections were held in 2018. The sub-prefectures of the autonomous district also have their own mayors and councils.

In Ivory Coast, the enrollment rate is 74%[^cite_note-36] and access to secondary education is limited by an entrance assessment at the 6th stage after which one-third of students are allowed to continue their studies. 24% of the student population resides in Abidjan, which comprises 20% of the total population. Abidjan is the location of the main educational institutions in Ivory Coast. ## Government institutions



Seat of the [National Assembly of Cote d'Ivoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Cote_d'Ivoire)

The majority of Ivorian government institutions are still located in Abidjan and have not been transferred to the political capital, [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro). The President's office is in [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody),[^cite_note-39] and the parliament,[^cite_note-40] the [Constitutional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Council_\(C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire\)), the Supreme Court, and the Chancellery of the National Order are all in [Le Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau,_C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire). Under the presidency of [Laurent Gbagbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Gbagbo) (2000–11), efforts were made to give effect to the project of making the political capital Yamoussoukro in Côte d'Ivoire.[^cite_note-41] This goal continues to be the consensus of the country's political leaders.

Government offices are located in Le Plateau[^cite_note-42] in the administrative district of Abidjan, Boulevard Carde or Angoulvant Boulevard (near the Cathedral of St. Paul of Abidjan), or elsewhere in the great buildings of the town.

## Security

The instability of the 2002–2007 civil war and 2010–2011 crisis saw foreign peacekeeping forces stationed in Abidjan. The United Nations Operation in Côte d'Ivoire ([UNOCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire)), established in April 2004, has its headquarters at the former Hotel Sebroko in Attécoubé. While the size of the force has been reduced since the end of the 2011 crisis, UNOCI's mandate was extended in 2013 and again in 2015. The force remains authorized to maintain up to 5,245 troops, 192 observers, 1,500 police and eight customs officers in the country.[^cite_note-43]

France's peacekeeping operations in Ivory Coast continued under the aegis of [Operation Unicorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Licorne) until 2015 and then as the French forces in Côte d'Ivoire from 2015 onward. In 2014, French defense minister [Jean-Yves Le Drian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Yves_Le_Drian) announced that Abidjan would be reinforced as the entry point for France's military presence and counterterrorism operations in the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) region.[^cite_note-44] After [al-Qaeda militants targeted beachgoers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand-Bassam_shootings) at [Grand-Bassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand-Bassam) in March 2016, the French government announced it would increase its troop deployment in Côte d'Ivoire from 500 to 900.[^cite_note-45] French soldiers are based in Port-Bouet as well as at the Abidjan airport.[^cite_note-46]

In 2007, Pierre Laba's comedy film *Permanent Danger* explored the laxity of the police against banditry in major African cities, specifically in Abidjan.[^cite_note-47]

### Police

Abidjan is divided into districts, each of which has a police station. The municipal police and national police are separate.

All police are trained at the National Police Academy in the town of Cocody. They are recruited by competitive examination held by the police administration. The police include several sections, such as the BAE (Anti Riot Squad), whose base is located in Yopougon north of the city, the CRS (Republican Security Company), whose main base is located in Williamsville (Adjame) and also includes a secondary base called CRS2, which is located in Zone 4 in the town of [Marcory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcory) in the central capital. The Force and CECOS accompany the police.

The main base of the gendarmerie is at Agban (commonly Adjamé). There are others in [Abobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abobo) and [Koumassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koumassi). A training school is in Cocody and the city.

### Drugs

Since 2000, Ivory Coast has experienced a sharp rise in drug trafficking. Drug dens arise mainly in informal settlements and there has been a recent spike in demand among unemployed youth and Ivorian vagrants. The Ivorian police increasingly focus on the fight against trafficking and drug use in the district of Abidjan. A report by the Office of the UN says West Africa plays an increasingly important role in the transit of cocaine between Latin America and Europe.[^cite_note-48] In May 2007, seven drug houses were dismantled and 191 people arrested including a military officer.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### French military base

Abidjan is home to [Port-Bouet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port-Bouet), one of the six French military bases that still exist in Africa ([Dakar](44251.md), [Libreville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libreville), [Bouar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouar) CAR, [N'Djamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Djamena) and [Djibouti](17207794.md)). Thousands of people live there, around the 43rd BIMA which is permanently in place in accordance with established defence agreements on 24 April 1961 linking the two countries.[^cite_note-51]

## Society

The 2021 census recorded Abidjan's population as 6,321,017. As Abidjan is inhabited by people from a wide array of different [ethnicities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity), French is commonly spoken in the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), which is the third largest French-speaking metropolitan area in the world after [Kinshasa](45773.md) and [Paris](22989.md). Colloquial Abidjan French has appeared, which has a different pronunciation and some slang words different from standard French. Abidjan is recognized as one of the few areas in Africa where French (colloquial Abidjan French) is truly a native tongue,[^cite_note-55] along with [Libreville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libreville), Gabon.[^cite_note-56]

Abidjan Centre

By 1950, Abidjan had just exceeded a population of 50,000 (at the end of 1948). Reaching a population of a million by the end of 1975, the city grew at a rate of 10 to 12% per year, doubling every six or seven years. However, this growth underwent a sharp decline due to the crises of the 1980s and 1990s. In the last 20 years of the 20th century, the growth rate dropped to 3–6%. Birth rate growth was supplemented by migration, with the influx being substantial and the outflow only partly offseting the arrivals. The migration, prior to the census of 1988, contributed a growth of about 80,000 with 50,000 people from within Ivory Coast, and about 30,000 from abroad per year.

From within Ivory Coast, the migration pattern was dominated by [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) (South-east, 48%), then [Mandé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mand%C3%A9_peoples) (North-west, 24%) and [Krou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krumen_people) (South-west, 20%). From abroad, the migration was [Burkinabes](3470.md) (30%), [Malians](19127.md) (22%), [Ghanaians](12067.md) (19%), [Nigerien](21373.md) (11%), and [Guineans](12176.md) (9%).[^cite_note-61] Of the non-African migration, [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people_in_Ivory_Coast) migration was highest, followed by European migration, with the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people_in_Ivory_Coast) being largest of this latter group. All in all, people of non-African origin represent 3% of the total population of Abidjan, which remains the highest in the region.

### Languages

The traditional language of the city's region is [Ebrié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebri%C3%A9_language). Since the colonial period, the [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) in Abidjan and throughout Côte d'Ivoire has been [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language). While the official language is a standard variety of French similar to that of Paris, the most commonly spoken form of [French in Abidjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_French#Abidjan_French) is a colloquial dialect known as *français de Treichville* or *français de Moussa* which differs from standard French in pronunciation and in some of its vocabulary. Another form of spoken French in Abidjan is *[Nouchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouchi_language)*, a highly informal variety used as adolescent slang. The historical lingua franca, still spoken and understood by most of the trading population of Abidjan, is [Dioula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioula_language). The city is home to Ivorians from throughout the country and about sixty vernacular languages are spoken, such as [Attié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atti%C3%A9_language), [Baoulé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baoul%C3%A9_language), [Bété](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9t%C3%A9_language), and [Wobé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wob%C3%A9_language). Abidjan is surrounded by more than thirty villages where Baoulé and Ébrié are still commonly spoken.

[Tour F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_F), under construction in 2024 in the central administrative district of [Le Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Plateau,_Abidjan), should be the tallest skyscraper in subsaharan Africa at more than 300 meters (1,000 ft) in height.

Modern districts like [Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Plateau,_Abidjan) or [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody) have developed similar to those in Europe with the construction of large office towers and apartment buildings between the 1970s and the mid-1980s.[^cite_note-63] The [CCIA building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCIA_Building) (Abidjan International Trade Center Building), a skyscraper, was completed in 1982.

Traditional neighborhoods like [Treichville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treichville) or [Marcory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcory) maintained the system of "concessions" where housing is adjacent to the plant and an area of several houses, within the normal organization and multi centuries-old African villages.[^cite_note-64]

The district of [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody) is also home to many single-storey wooden villas, surrounded by vast gardens of lush vegetation, fed by the heavy rains that water the city. Wealthier inhabitants constructed villas in imitation of Greek temples in this district. In addition, many houses were built on stilts on the edge of the [Ébrié Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89bri%C3%A9_Lagoon). #### Problems with urban network



Urban Network in Le Plateau

The urban network is disrupted, as Abidjan has seen its population double every seven years since 1945. Rural people are attracted by the opportunities of the city. Abidjan represents 45% of city dwellers in Ivory Coast and 20% of the overall population. The city is by far the country's largest, dwarfing the second-largest city, [Bouaké](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouak%C3%A9) (which had 542,082 inhabitants in 2014) as well as the political capital, [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro) (207,412 inhabitants).

#### Urban squalor

Since the [2006 Probo Koala toxic waste dumping incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Ivory_Coast_toxic_waste_dump),[^cite_note-65] significant efforts have been made to address waste management issues in Abidjan. In 2006, a contract was signed between [China](5405.md) and Ivory Coast: a Chinese company handling urban waste and industrial plans to treat all waste district this transformation also create daily cleaning.[^cite_note-66]

French nongovernmental organisation [ACTED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agency_for_Technical_Cooperation_and_Development) rolled out a three-part project across three districts of Abidjan in 2016, establishing street cleaning, waste collection and hygiene training. ACTED spokesperson Jérémy Lescot described the project as "a fine success".[^cite_note-67]

#### Urban rehabilitation

The last transportation upgrade in Abidjan led to the repaving of certain roads in the capital in 1994. Since May 2007, the company [Ageroute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageroute?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Road Management Agency) is undertaking major operations in Abidjan and [Anyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyama) relating to badly damaged roads, suffering from lack of maintenance, heavy rain and protests. It is also addressing the gutters and unpaved roads.[^cite_note-68] The District and BNETD plan to rehabilitate and construct 400 to 500 miles (640–800 kilometres), providing them with a quality to last 15–20 years of life. Improvements include the rehabilitation of roads in Abidjan and [Anyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyama), construction of drains, the tarring of gravel roads and sanitation of the city by the construction of sewers. These rehabilitations affect all municipalities in Abidjan:

- In [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody), at a cost of 3 billion [CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc), reconstruction of the [Corniche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corniche), one of the busiest roads in the municipality. Reconstruction has been extended by the dam of the limit, located at the *Carrefour de la vie*, to filter all waste dumped into the lagoon.

- At [Yopougon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yopougon), the rehabilitation nerve Sand continues with the construction of a culvert 200 feet (61 metres) long designed to slow the streaking wastewater, the main cause of the degradation of pathways.

- In [Abobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abobo) the degradation of the roads requires a significant investment in manufacturing and installation of gutters to ensure proper sanitation. The rehabilitation of roads and highways continues.

## Industry

The region near the lagoons is the most industrialized region of the country.

Major industries include [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), [lumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber), automobile manufacturing, textiles, [chemicals](58721.md), and [soap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap). There is also a large [oil refinery](195137.md). Its industries are mainly in construction and maintenance with the presence of major international groups: the furnace SETAO, Colas, [Bouygues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouygues), [Jean Lefebvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Lefebvre), and Swiss [Holcim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holcim).

There are textile industries with the packaging of cotton in the north both for export or for on-site processing of cloth, canvas, batik clothing and miscellaneous. The textile sector is very dynamic, and represents 15.6% of net investment, 13% of turnover and 24% of the value added in Ivorian industry.

There are several offshore oil wells in operation (Côte d'Ivoire is an oil producing country), which leads to the presence of a chemical industry with oil refineries,[^cite_note-74] and an oil port. It also works on stones and precious metals for exportation[^cite_note-75]

The city also has a large wood processing plant at the port by river from the forests of central [Canada](5042916.md). It exports natural [mahogany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahogany), peeled wood, [plywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plywood) and [chipboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paperboard) for the past two centuries.

Food industry mainly include: the production of [palm oil](57561.md), [bergamot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamot_orange) and [Seville oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitter_orange). In the west, rubber is processed as well as manufacturing beverages from [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapples), oranges[^cite_note-76] and [mangoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), producing robusta [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee): Côte d'Ivoire is the third largest producer, behind [Colombia](5222.md) and [Brazil](3383.md)[^cite_note-77] and processing [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean), it is the largest producer in the world ahead of [Ghana](12067.md) and [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-78] (37% of cocoa and 10% of coffee products undergo at least one first local processing). Abidjan is also the first African [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna) port for the European market. This generated 3,000 salaried jobs and is an important source of foreign exchange. As in all countries of the Third World developing countries, much of the city's economy lies in what economists describe as informal economy[^cite_note-79] with its many "odd jobs".[^cite_note-80]

### Tertiary sector

The development of tertiary industry, with the establishment of international commercial banks and the increasing number of service companies of all types, appears to be the trend of recent years. Abidjan is the main financial center of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa). The seat of the common stock exchange of West Africa (BRVM) is located in Abidjan. BRVM is mainly dominated by Ivorian firms.

### Electricity

The thermal power plant Azito, built in 1997 in partnership with [EDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9_de_France), located on the Yopougon on the edge of the lagoon and facing Ébrié Island Boulay, alone provides 300 MW.[^cite_note-81] Two other power plants, built in 1984 and 1985, are installed in [Vridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vridi?action=edit\&redlink=1). Several utilities are installed in Abidjan: [CI Energies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CI_Energies?action=edit\&redlink=1), formerly SOPIE, which is a corporation state,[^cite_note-82] ICE and CIPREL, subsidiaries of the French group SAUR[^cite_note-83] Côte d'Ivoire produces all the electricity it consumes, and exports to neighboring countries, [Ghana](12067.md), [Togo](30138.md), [Benin](3459.md), [Mali](19127.md) and [Burkina Faso](3470.md) through the interconnection.[^cite_note-84]

The production, which increases steadily, and gas storage are provided by two state companies: PETROCI and GESTOCI.[^cite_note-85] A small industry of renewable energy production has also developed.[^cite_note-86]

The District[^cite_note-90] has several paved streets and boulevards linking the towns between them and the District across the country. Abidjan has a network of urban bypasses.

River transport is already consistent with fishing spots and many "boat-buses" connecting the various neighborhoods of the city thrives plan lagoon.

Abidjan is also the bridgehead of the country's only highway linking the city to the administrative and political capital, [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro).

#### Port



Port of Abidjan, 2009

Abidjan has one of the biggest ports in the sub-region (West Africa). It is a container transshipment port and was opened in 1951 in the presence of the Minister of Overseas and Colonies at the time, [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand). The 15 m deep [Vridi Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vridi_Canal), where boats with large draughts can dock in the deep-water port.[^cite_note-91]

Ivory Coast is in the Franc Zone (where the currency is CFA francs), as part of the [West African Economic and Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Economic_and_Monetary_Union) (WAEMU). Abidjan is the economic centre of both the Côte d'Ivoire and of the whole region, including [Burkina Faso](3470.md) and [Mali](19127.md); this is mainly because of its deep-water port. The road network in Abidjan (5,600 km asphalted in 1995, compared to 1,000 km in 1970), which covers the whole region, effectively increases its port activity as 50% of the business from [Burkina Faso](3470.md), [Mali](19127.md) and [Niger](21373.md) pass through it.

Abidjan receives the bulk of the country's industrial activity; its port alone receiving 60% of ivory park industry. Abidjan has ambitions of oil-production—off-shore production and especially [refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refining) and distribution. In 1995 the port received 12 million tonnes of traffic, of which 5.5 million tonnes were petroleum products.

Exports include coffee, [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean), [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber), [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bananas), [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), and [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese).[^cite_note-EB-9]

#### Bridges



Alassane Ouattara Bridge, completed in 2023, is the 5th bridge over the Abidjan lagoon.

The two-halves of the city, Northern Abidjan and Southern Abidjan, are joined by the Houphouët-Boigny, the Charles de Gaulle and the [Henri Konan Bédié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Konan_B%C3%A9di%C3%A9_Bridge) bridges. The two former bridges, built in the 1950s and 1960s respectively, are located between [Treichville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treichville) and [Le Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau,_Ivory_Coast). The [Général-de-Gaulle Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Abidjan_Bridge) extends over the immense Valéry Giscard d'Estaing Boulevard which leads to the airport. Both bridges are often congested, especially during rush hour or on the occasion of police and military roadblocks.

A third 1.5 km (0.93 mi) toll bridge and expressway, the [Henri Konan Bédié Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Konan_B%C3%A9di%C3%A9_Bridge) between [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody) and [Marcory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcory) was completed in December 2014. Project construction began in the second half of 2012 and required the relocation of several homes and businesses opposite the Mille Maquis (the noted restaurant *la Bâche bleue* was moved).[^cite_note-92] According to data reported by the [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank), 2,499 persons were impacted by the bridge construction and were relocated and compensated.[^cite_note-93]

A fourth 1.4 km (0.87 mi) 8-lane toll bridge between [Yopougon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yopougon) and [Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau,_Ivory_Coast) was built between 2018 and 2024.[^cite_note-94]

A fifth 630 m (690 yd) 4-lane toll-free bridge, the Alassane Ouattara Bridge between [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody) and [Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau,_Ivory_Coast), was built between 2019 and 2023.

#### Trains and trams

Train platform, Abidjan, 2014

The city is the foothold for the region's only railway: the Abidjan-[Ouagadougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouagadougou) line, with stations in [Treichville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treichville), [Abobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abobo) and [Adjamé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjam%C3%A9). This line only has two trains, the *Gazelle* and the *Bélier*, and links the two capitals in about 40 hours.[^cite_note-95]

The most recent project in Abidjan is the [Abidjan Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abidjan_Metro) which is promised by the Ivory Society of Railroads (SICF).[^cite_note-96]

The development of infrastructural projects has been envisaged, including a suburban train system in Abidjan which is estimated to cost almost 100 billion CFA francs. This suburban train system would link the North-West to the East and North of the city. The project extends over 25 km of railway tracks which as of 2011 were used by the operator [Sitarail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitarail), a subsidiary company of the [Bolloré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollor%C3%A9) group. The infrastructural works (tracks, electrification, and civil engineers) have been estimated at 40 billion [CFA francs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_francs), financed by the State of Ivory Coast and backers ([World Bank](36752.md) and the French Development Agency). In addition, a BOT concession contract would be given to a private operator, over 20–30 years, to operate the network. This operator will be in charge of the acquisition of railway materials, workshop renovation and the implementation of an organisational system. The concession contract has been estimated to be around 60 billion CFA francs.

### Tourism



A beach in [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody)

Abidjan has features relevant to tourism such as accommodation facilities (such as the Golf Hôtel), sporting facilities, night life, and transport and communication lines. The city is known by nicknames such as "Manhattan of the tropics", "Small Manhattan" and "Pearl of the lagoons".

Abidjan also has beaches around the lagoon, with palm and coconut trees, in the [Vridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vridi?action=edit\&redlink=1) area, which are very popular at weekends with the picturesque sight of the pineapple and coconut sellers. Nevertheless, the rip-tides which affects practically the whole of the Gulf of Guinea's coast, means that in this area swimming is not usually allowed. Generally, in Cote d'Ivoire, tourism has never really been developed as an economic industry; the country is not much of a common holiday destination.

#### Parc du Banco (Banco National Park)



Fanicos, Abidjan's washermen

Converted in 1926, this park has 3,000 hectares and ancient "sacred wood" lies at the entrance to the city, in the [Attécoubé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Att%C3%A9coub%C3%A9) community, which has been conserved as a relic of the first forest which surrounded the lagoon in the past. A tarmaced road goes straight to the lake at the heart of the park and trails go throughout it. The park is inhabited and there are coffee and [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theobroma_cacao) plantations.

At the edge of the park, a small river serves as a wash-house where the *fanicos*, laundry men, work after collecting the laundry from the whole of the city. It is one of the 'small jobs' which exist in Abidjan.

#### Nightlife

The district has an abundance of night-clubs, maquis, out-door areas, and go-go bars. These entertainment platforms provide a musical 'pipeline' encompassing mainly DJs, [Coupé Décalé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup%C3%A9_D%C3%A9cal%C3%A9) and [Zouglou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zouglou), and, in lesser amounts, other local and international varieties.

Formerly containing only local traditional varieties, Congolese music and Western music, Abidjan's night life has experienced a positive cultural disruption in its music since the start of the 2000s, with the arrival of [Coupé Décalé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup%C3%A9_D%C3%A9cal%C3%A9).

This musical genre was introduced in 2002 by [Douk Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douk_Saga) and [La Jet Set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jet_Set?action=edit\&redlink=1), with the help of Sagacité, created a phenomenon which has not stopped spreading and reaching out to the hot nights in the capitals of the sub-region. It has given a globally identifiable cultural identity to entertainment 'made in Côte d'Ivoire'.

The very popular Zouglou additionally benefits "Wôyô" spaces; furnished and dedicated so that the most famous, the Internat at Fitini's and the Lycee at Vieux Gazeur's, attract 'zouglouphiles' the whole weekend.

[Treichville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treichville), with its many maquis, discothèques, and jazz clubs, used to be the liveliest area in the city, but since the end of the 1990s [Youpougon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youpougon?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Marcory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcory), and [Cocody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocody) have taken over this role.

### Music

Abidjan is a turntable for West African music, and a major producer of musical art in Africa. The city attracts the majority of musicians from the Côte d'Ivoire mainly because of its many [dance clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_clubs), which allow artists to make their debut and express themselves, record companies and national media. Here are a few examples of the most popular artists to have made their debut in Abidjan: [Alpha Blondy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Blondy), [Aïcha Koné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFcha_Kon%C3%A9?action=edit\&redlink=1), the group [Magic System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_System), [Meiway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiway), and [Tiken Jah Fakoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiken_Jah_Fakoly), as well as [Gadji Celi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadji_Celi), [Chantal Taiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantal_Taiba?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Nayanka Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayanka_Bell), [DJ Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Arafat). In the 1980s, [South African](17416221.md) musician [Miriam Makeba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Makeba) relocated to Abidjan when she was not allowed back into the country, as did the Congolese singer [Tshala Muana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tshala_Muana).

Since the 1990s, Abidjan has had a significant variety of musical styles being exported throughout Africa and the West. These are [Zoblazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoblazo), [Mapouka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapouka), [Zouglou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zouglou) and, since 2002, the [Coupé-Décalé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup%C3%A9-D%C3%A9cal%C3%A9); the popularity and great variety of these dances make the city a kind of African dance and musical trend capital. The Democratic Republic of the Congo formerly held this title, with [rumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_rumba), [soukous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soukous) and then [ndombolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndombolo). Abidjan is also considered the reggae capital of Africa.

### Media

See also: [Media of Côte d'Ivoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire)

Most mass media in Côte d'Ivoire can be found in Abidjan.

#### Television and radio

The National Council for Audio-visual Communication (CNCA) is the regulating authority of audio-visual material in Côte d'Ivoire.

[Radiodiffusion Television Ivoirienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiodiffusion_Television_Ivoirienne) (RTI) is the radio and television broadcasting authority for Côte d'Ivoire; it is financed by licence fees, publicity and grants. RTI has four television channels and two radio stations: *La Première* (non-specialised), *TV2*, *RTI Music TV*, *RTI Sport TV*, *Radio Côte d'Ivoire*, and *Fréquence 2* (non-specialised radio). *Radio Jam* is a private radio station in Côte d'Ivoire and is most listened to by young people.

With regards to private channels, [Tam-Tam TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tam-Tam_TV?action=edit\&redlink=1) will be the first International Côte d'Ivoire television channel with headquarters in Abidjan, with other studios in the [United States](3434750.md), [Italy](14532.md), [France](5843419.md), Asia, the Middle East.

There are also several Ivory Coast TV channels or radio stations available on the Internet: *Africahit*, *Music TV*, *Abidjan.net TV*, *Bengueshow*, etc.

With the launch in 2015 of DISCOP Africa Abidjan, the city became a hub for TV market professionals from French-speaking Africa.[^cite_note-118]
