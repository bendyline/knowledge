[Diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_Relations) between [South Korea](27019.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) commenced in 1949. The United States helped establish the modern state of South Korea, officially the Republic of Korea, and fought on its [UN](31769.md)-sponsored side in the [Korean War](16772.md) (1950–1953). During the subsequent decades, South Korea experienced tremendous [economic](27024.md), [political](27023.md) and [military](27027.md) growth. To date, the United States recognized the ROK as the sole legitimate government of Korea.

South Korea has a long military alliance with the United States, aiding the U.S. in every war since the [Vietnam War](32611.md), including the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-\:0-1] At the [2009 G20 London summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_G20_London_summit), then-U.S. president [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) called South Korea "one of America's closest allies and greatest friends".[^cite_note-2] In 1987, South Korea was among the first batch of countries to be designated as a [major non-NATO ally](3812445.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] In June 2023, South Korean president [Yoon Suk Yeol](62877952.md) said that he had upgraded the country's alliance with the United States to one that is "nuclear-based" in the face of North Korea's growing military threat.[^cite_note-5]

According to a 2026 Pew Research poll, 45% of South Koreans had a favorable view of the United States, while 55% had a negative view.[^cite_note-\:5-6] According to a 2018 Gallup poll, 77% of Americans had a favorable view of South Koreans, while 22% had a negative view.[^cite_note-7]

## History

### Background



The [Old Korean Legation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Korean_Legation_Museum) in Washington, D.C. is where the Korean legation was housed from 1889 to 1905.

Following the [United States expedition to Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_expedition_to_Korea) in 1871, the United States and [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) established diplomatic relations under the 1882 [Treaty of Peace, Amity, Commerce, and Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Korea_Treaty_of_1882). In 1883, Joseon sent the first ever [Korean special mission to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1883_Korean_special_mission_to_the_United_States), also known in Korean as *Bobingsa* (보빙사; 報聘使).[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Bobingsa2-8] However, Japan [assumed direction over Korean foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Korea_Treaty_of_1905) in 1905 and in 1910 began a 35-year period of [colonial rule over Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule).[^cite_note-US_DOS-9]

In 1945, [Japan](15573.md) surrendered to the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) at the end of [World War II](32927.md). The United States proposed to the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) that they could share responsibility of the Korean peninsula, [dividing the Korean peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Korea) at the 38th parallel into two occupation zones, with the United States in the South and the Soviet Union in the North. This was intended to be a temporary measure.[^cite_note-\:1-10]

Each half was polarized politically: the USSR-backed North was led by the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Korea), while U.S. General [John Hodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Hodge) handpicked far-right [Syngman Rhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syngman_Rhee) to lead the South.[^cite_note-\:1-10] Initial talks in 1945–6 to achieve a unified, independent Korea were not successful. The U.S. petitioned the United Nations to find a resolution, and the UN concluded that elections should be held.[^cite_note-\:1-10] In reality, only the South held [an election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_South_Korean_Constitutional_Assembly_election), as the North refused to allow UN officials entry to supervise the election. Syngman Rhee was officially elected after ruling the South for several years already, and in turn [Stalin's regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin's_regime) appointed Communist [Kim Il-Sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung) without an election.[^cite_note-Malkasian2001-11]

In 1948, two separate nations were established: the Republic of Korea (ROK) in the South, and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK) in the North. On January 1, 1949, the United States officially recognized the Republic of Korea as the sole legitimate government of Korea and established diplomatic relations on March 25 of that year.[^cite_note-US_DOS-9][^cite_note-12]

### Korean War 1950-1953

Main articles: [Division of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Korea) and [Korean War](16772.md)

Cross-border skirmishes and raids at the 38th Parallel escalated into open warfare when the North Korean forces started to [invade South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pokpung) on June 25, 1950. In response, 16 member countries of the [United Nations](31769.md), led by the United States, came to the defense of South Korea. It was the first significant armed conflict of the [Cold War](325329.md) with extensive deployment of U.S. and other troops. The Korean War was a major [Cold War](325329.md) military clash fought up and down the peninsula of Korea, finally leading to a stalemate in 1953 that restored the boundaries to nearly what they were at the start, along the 38th parallel. The war was initiated by [Kim Il-sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il-sung) of the [Democratic People's Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_People's_Republic_of_Korea) (North Korea), with assistance from China and the Soviet Union\[1] were arrayed against the Republic of Korea (South Korea), supported by the United States of America and a multinational United Nations force.[^cite_note-13]

The war began with an invasion by North Korea in June 1950, followed by an unexpected American entry. North Korean forces had pushed the South Koreans and Americans back into a small perimeter around the port of Pusan. In September 1950, an amphibious landing at Inchon, followed by a breakout from the Pusan Perimeter, turned the tide. The North Korean People's Army (NKPA) disintegrated as the allies moved north, with UN approval, to unify the country. Unexpectedly the Chinese then sent in large numbers of infantry, and in the bitter cold of November-January 1950-51 pushed the UN forces out of the north. Communist supply lines were fragile, especially in the face of heavy American bombing, so the lines stabilized close to the 38th parallel in 1951. Two more years of static warfare followed, with the issue of returning reluctant Communist prisoners of war held by the UN the major sticking point. Finally an armistice was reached in summer 1953; the prisoners were exchanged and fighting ended in an uneasy truce that continues into the 21st century.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

The war was limited in size and scope, but casualties were heavy on both sides. In the U.S., the political consequences of the war contributed to the defeat of the Truman administration and his Democratic party in the 1952 election of General Dwight D. Eisenhower, the Republican candidate who promised to end the war. For Americans and Chinese it is a "forgotten war", neglected on the timeline between the twin cataclysms of World War II and Vietnam. For the Koreans it is the central event of their modern history, and efforts to reunify the land continue.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Origins of the South Korea–United States alliance

General Douglas MacArthur and Syngman Rhee, Korea's first President, warmly greet one another upon the General's arrival at Kimpo Air Force Base alt text

General [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur) and [Syngman Rhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syngman_Rhee), Korea's first President

In 1953, following the end of the Korean War, the United States established a [bilaterial alliance](429154.md) pact with South Korea.[^cite_note-\:1-10]

Moreover, the "U.S. alliance with South Korea would consequently have three functions. First, it would serve as part of a network of alliances and military installations designed to ring the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) threat in the Pacific. Second, it would deter a second North Korean attack, with U.S. ground troops serving as the 'tripwire' guaranteeing U.S. involvement. Third, it would restrain the South from engaging in adventurism".[^cite_note-18]

The United States and South Korea are allies under the [1953 Mutual Defense Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_Defense_Treaty_Between_the_United_States_and_the_Republic_of_Korea). Under the agreement, U.S. military personnel have maintained a continuous presence on the Korean peninsula.

### Issues and recent history

#### U.S. military and the sex trade

See also: [United States military and prostitution in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_and_prostitution_in_South_Korea)

In 1953, at the end of the Korean War, the number of prostitutes in South Korea was estimated as about 350,000, with about 60 percent working near U.S. military camps.[^cite_note-Clough163-19] In the post-Korean War period, the U.S. military continued to contribute significantly to the South Korean economy, providing an estimated 1 percent of the South Korean GNP in 1991, including the sex industry.[^cite_note-1997Moon76-20] Despite the world-wide growth of women's human rights advocacy since the 1990s, and the shift towards foreign workers providing sex services for U.S. troops, (particularly women trafficked from the Philippines and the former Soviet Union), prostitution via "juicy bars" remains an issue near U.S. bases in South Korea.[^cite_note-Moon_2009-21][^cite_note-22]

#### 1992 Yun Geum-i murder

Main article: [Yun Geum-i murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yun_Geum-i_murder)

In 1992, [Yun Geum-i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yun_Geum-i_murder), a 26-year-old woman, was brutally killed by a U.S. serviceman, Private Kenneth L. Markle, in [Dongducheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongducheon).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-joong201109302-24] In August 1993, the U.S. government compensated the victim's family with a payment of about US\$72,000.[^cite_note-Hankyoreh200610282-25] Markle was sentenced by a South Korean court to life imprisonment, later reduced to 15 years. Professor Katharine Moon notes that the murder was not unique, and did not spark a national debate about the presence of U.S. forces. However, it did become a "call to action" for some Koreans, and led to the establishment of the "National Campaign for the Eradication of Crimes by U.S. troops."

#### Environmental degradation

Main article: [McFarland incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McFarland_incident)

In July 2000, the [Eighth U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_United_States_Army) apologized for an incident where [formaldehyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaldehyde), a toxic fluid, was released into the [Han River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Korea\)) in February of that year.[^cite_note-26] In a report released in 2017 detailing spill incidents from 1995 to 2015 at the [US garrison in Yongsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongsan_Garrison), South Korean environmentalist groups expressed concern about the lack of transparency and the possibility of continued water contamination, as well as who would take responsibility for cleanup of the site.[^cite_note-27]

#### Yangju highway incident

Main article: [Yangju highway incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangju_highway_incident)

On 13 June 2002, two 14-year-old South Korean schoolgirls were crushed to death by a 50-ton [United States Army](32087.md) vehicle in [Yangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangju). [Anti-Americanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Americanism) was pervasive after the driver and the navigator of the vehicle were both acquitted in U.S. [courts-martial](58625.md) on charges of [negligent homicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negligent_homicide). There was resentment from protesters towards the [U.S.–South Korea Status of Forces Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93South_Korea_Status_of_Forces_Agreement), which restricted South Korea from having jurisdiction over alleged crimes that occurred when American soldiers were on official duty. South Korean presidential candidate [Lee Hoi-chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hoi-chang) called on United States President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) to "apologize to soothe the pain of the Korean people and to prevent any escalation in anti-American sentiment". American [ambassador to South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_South_Korea) [Thomas C. Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Hubbard) apologized on behalf of Bush.[^cite_note-28]

#### 2008 beef protests in South Korea

Further information: [2008 US beef protest in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_US_beef_protest_in_South_Korea)

The Government of South Korea banned imports of U.S. beef in 2003 in response to a case of [mad cow disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_cow_disease) in [Washington state](13015878.md). In 2008, protests were held against U.S. beef that were reminiscent of the student "pro-democracy" movements of the 1980s. Nevertheless, by 2010, South Korea had become the world's third largest U.S. beef importer. With its strong import growth, South Korea surpassed [Japan](15573.md) for the first time to become the largest market for U.S. beef in [Asia](689.md); in 2016, U.S. beef imports in Korea reached a value of \$1 billion.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

#### 2015 attack on the United States Ambassador



President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) is awarded the [Grand Order of Mugunghwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Order_of_Mugunghwa) in October 2025, the first U.S. President to ever receive this award.

At around 7\:40 a.m. on March 5, 2015, [United States Ambassador to South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_South_Korea) [Mark Lippert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Lippert) was attacked by a knife-wielding man at a restaurant attached to [Sejong Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejong_Center) in downtown Seoul, where he was scheduled to give a speech at a meeting of the Korean Council for Reconciliation and Cooperation.[^cite_note-NYT-31] The assailant, Kim Ki-jong, is a member of Uri Madang, a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_South_Korea) cultural organization opposed to the [Korean War](16772.md).[^cite_note-32] He inflicted wounds on Lippert's left arm as well as a four-inch cut on the right side of the ambassador's face, requiring 80 stitches.[^cite_note-NYT-31] Lippert underwent surgery at [Yonsei University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonsei_University)'s [Severance Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_Hospital) in Seoul. While his injuries were not life-threatening, doctors stated that it would take several months for Lippert to regain use of his fingers.[^cite_note-NYT-31] A police official said that the knife used in the attack was 10 inches (25 cm) long[^cite_note-NYT-31] and Lippert later reported that the blade penetrated to within 2 cm of his carotid artery.[^cite_note-33] ABC News summarized the immediate aftermath of the attack as follows: "Ambassador Lippert, an Iraq war veteran, defended himself from the attack. Lippert was rushed to a hospital where he was treated for deep cuts to his face, his arm, and his hand. ... \[He] kept his cool throughout the incident."[^cite_note-34]

During the attack and while being subdued by security, Kim screamed that the rival Koreas should be unified and told reporters that he had attacked Lippert to protest the [annual United States–South Korean joint military exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foal_Eagle).[^cite_note-NYT-31] Kim has a record of militant [Korean nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_nationalism) activism; he attacked the Japanese ambassador to South Korea in 2010 and was sentenced to a three-year suspended prison term.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] On September 11, 2015, Kim was sentenced to twelve years in prison for the attack.[^cite_note-39]

#### Opposition to THAAD

Main articles: [Terminal High Altitude Area Defense § Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_High_Altitude_Area_Defense#Opposition), and [South Korea-China relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea-China_relations)

The rollout of the Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) has been met with domestic opposition in South Korea. The opposition has been on the grounds that the North Korean threat has gone, and on environmental grounds.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-\:02-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-\:2-43] THAAD was deployed under the administration of ROK President [Park Geun-hye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Geun-hye). Her opponents accused her of "bow\[ing] too readily to America's requests."[^cite_note-\:2-43] According to [South China Morning Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Morning_Post), when Prime Minister [Hwang Kyo-ahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwang_Kyo-ahn) visited Seongju to appease the local backlash against THAAD, demonstrators blocked Hwang's buses and pelted him with eggs and water bottles. The progressive People's Party also opposes the deployment.[^cite_note-\:2-43]

#### 2025 Trade Agreement

As part of President [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s [Liberation Day tariffs](79617151.md) in April 2025, the United States announced a trade deal with South Korea that called for tariffs of up to 25% on their goods.[^cite_note-44] Rather than retaliating with tariffs of their own, South Korea pursued a proactive negotiation strategy, sending officials frequently to Washington to create a framework for a deal that would reduce the US tariff rate. As a result, the US lowered their tariffs on South Korea to 15%, while South Korea pledged a \$350bn investment fund for US-owned companies within their country.[^cite_note-45]

Following the September [2025 Georgia Hyundai plant immigration raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Georgia_Hyundai_plant_immigration_raid) where 300 Korean workers were detained, President [Lee Jae Myung](50305655.md) warned that concerns over the treatment of Korean technicians in the United States were making businesses hesitant to invest directly, further complicating the trade negotiations. “We will not make a decision that goes against our national interests,” he said, adding, “We will not engage in negotiations that are not rational or just.”[^cite_note-46]

#### Reduction of yearly joint military exercises

In August 2026, US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) ordered the Pentagon to reduce yearly [Ulchi-Freedom Guardian](16576103.md) joint exercises with South Korea, saying that the ally's refused to help with the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md) and citing his good relationship with North Korean leader [Kim Jong Un](5962511.md) and the high cost of the exercises.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] On 26 August, the [U.S. Pacific Command](797909.md) announced that the [United States](3434750.md) will conduct trilateral [military exercises](1225714.md) with [South Korea](27019.md)'s [armed forces](27027.md) and [Japan](15573.md)'s [self-defense forces](2785204.md) despite U.S. president Trump scaling back on the recent [Ulchi-Freedom Guardian](16576103.md) exercises.[^cite_note-49]

## The United States military in Korea



US Secretary of Defense [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin) lays a wreath at the [War Memorial of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Memorial_of_Korea)



President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) greets General [Park Chung Hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee), November 1961

See also: [Major non-NATO ally](3812445.md), [United States Forces Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Korea), and [Mutual Defense Treaty Between the United States and the Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_Defense_Treaty_Between_the_United_States_and_the_Republic_of_Korea)

South Korea and the United States agreed to a military alliance in 1953.[^cite_note-50] They called it "the relationship forged in blood".[^cite_note-51] In addition, roughly 29,000 [United States Forces Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Korea) troops are stationed in South Korea. In 2009, South Korea and the United States pledged to develop the alliance's vision for future defense cooperation.[^cite_note-52] Currently, South Korean forces would fall under United States control should the war resume. As of September 2023, the transfer of this war time control to South Korea is on an indefinite “conditions-based” timeline.[^cite_note-53] Proposals to modernize the alliance include increasing South Korea’s defense budget to 3.5–3.8% of GDP, enhancing cost-sharing for U.S. Forces Korea, and maintaining a clear transfer plan for wartime operational control while ensuring continued U.S. military presence and extended nuclear deterrence.[^cite_note-54]

At the request of the [United States](3434750.md), President [Park Chung Hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee) [sent troops to Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_in_the_Vietnam_War) to assist American troops during the [Vietnam War](32611.md), maintaining the second largest contingent of foreign troops after the [United States](3434750.md). In exchange, the United States increased military and economic assistance to South Korea. In 2004, President [Roh Moo-hyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roh_Moo-hyun) authorized dispatching a [small contingent of troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaytun_Division) to [Iraq](7515928.md) at the request of U.S. President [George W. Bush](3414021.md).[^cite_note-\:0-1]

Since 2009, air forces of South Korea and the U.S.A. have conducted annual joint exercises under the name "Max Thunder". In 2018, the drills began on May 11 and continued until May 17.[^cite_note-55]

At a Cabinet meeting in Seoul on 10 July 2018, the government decided not to hold that year's Ulchi drill, scheduled for June 2018. The Government said the decision was made in line with recent political and security improvements on the peninsula and the suspension of South Korea-U.S. joint military exercises.[^cite_note-56]

Former South Korean President [Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Jae-in), elected in May 2017, has said he supports the continuation of sanctions against North Korea if it is aimed at bringing North Korea out of its state of isolation and to the negotiating table. He also argued, at the same time, that he was against a "sanctions-only" approach toward North Korea.[^cite_note-57] His approach to North Korea is similar to Kim Dae-jung's [Sunshine Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Policy), which only continued up to the Roh Mu-hyun's administration.

In 2018, there were several rounds of talks regarding sharing the cost of U.S. forces in South Korea. These reflect Washington's desire for South Korea to share a "greater burden" of the costs of the military deployment.[^cite_note-58]

On 12 June 2018, during the [2018 Trump–Kim summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_North_Korea%E2%80%93United_States_Singapore_Summit), U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and [Kim Jong Un](5962511.md) signed a [Joint Statement](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Joint%20Statement%20of%20President%20Donald%20J.%20Trump%20of%20the%20United%20States%20of%20America%20and%20Chairman%20Kim%20Jong%20Un%20of%20the%20Democratic%20People%E2%80%99s%20Republic%20of%20Korea%20at%20the%20Singapore%20Summit) which reaffirmed the [Panmunjom Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panmunjom_Declaration).[^cite_note-59]

The U.S. Ambassador to South Korea, [Harry B. Harris Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_B._Harris_Jr.), arrived in Seoul on July 7, 2018. The post had been vacant since President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) took office in January 2017. Harris, a former head of the U.S. military's Pacific Command, has expressed his resolve to work as an ambassador to strengthen the alliance between the United States and South Korea.[^cite_note-nhk-60]

On February 10, 2019, South Korea and the United States confirmed that a year long deal for keeping American troops, numbering 28,500, in South Korea had been made. This was in exchange for South Korea paying 925 million dollars to the United States.[^cite_note-61]

In terms of American leadership, Bill Clinton and George W. Bush both emphasized the Middle East over North Korea. Clinton had deep emotional ties with Israel but neglected North Korea issues and never built strong personal relations with South Korean leaders. Bush, whose religious fundamentalism led him to divide the world into good and evil, had a personalized hatred for North Korean leader Kim Jong Il, but he also had frosty relations with South Korean leaders.[^cite_note-62]

In his 2021 New Year's press conference on January 18, President Moon Jae-in stated that the two Koreas might be able to discuss the U.S.-South Korea military drills through a joint inter-Korean military committee. However, U.S. officials expressed that it is not a topic to be decided by Pyongyang, and that scaling down the exercises was not desirable.[^cite_note-63]



President Joe Biden greets President Yoon Suk Yeol, U.S. Army Commander Gen. [Paul LaCamera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_LaCamera) and Lieutenant General [Sung Chun Choi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sung_Chun_Choi?action=edit\&redlink=1) at the Air Operations Center of [Osan Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osan_Air_Base) in Seoul, South Korea on 22 May 2022

In May 2022, President Joe Biden and President Yoon Suk Yeol agreed in talks to begin discussions on restarting and potentially expanding joint military training on and around the Korean Peninsula. The move was a signal that Biden was changing course from former President Donald Trump's positions in Asia.[^cite_note-64]



State visit by US President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) at the [Blue House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_House)

In April 2023 a visit by Yoon to Washington produced a nuclear deterrence plan aimed at North Korea. [Joe Biden](145422.md) said the deal allowed nuclear submarines to dock in South Korea. This capability had not been seen since 1991. Biden said: "A nuclear attack by North Korea against the United States or its allies and partners is unacceptable, and will result in the end of whatever regime were to take such an action." The so-called Washington Declaration came [after nearly 50 years had passed since Korea signed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parties_to_the_Treaty_on_the_Non-Proliferation_of_Nuclear_Weapons) the [Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Non-Proliferation_Treaty) and gave up its nuclear ambitions.[^cite_note-apaamad-65][^cite_note-ajazus-66][^cite_note-cbcap-67]

In August 2024 The US and South Korea South Korea initiated military exercises, to boost their joint readiness to fend off North Korea's weapons and cyber threats.[^cite_note-68]

On 9 October 2024, South Korean Defence Minister Kim Yong-hyun called for enhanced joint military drills with the U.S. to ensure a strong defence against North Korean threats. In talks with U.S. Admiral Samuel Paparo, they agreed to hold a second "Freedom Edge" exercise and expressed concerns about the security risks posed by North Korea-Russia military collaboration.[^cite_note-69]

On November 6, 2024, South Korea's presidential office said on Wednesday that it will build a "perfect" security partnership with a new U.S. administration. Seoul is a strong U.S. ally in the region, with around 28,500 American troops stationed in South Korea.[^cite_note-70]

In February 2025, the U.S. and South Korean militaries conducted their first joint air drill of [President Donald Trump's second term](78298108.md), featuring at least one U.S. [B-1B bomber](4952.md) along with South Korean [F-35](11812.md) and [F-15 jets](11715.md) and [U.S. F-16s](11642.md). [Germany](11867.md), US, South Korean, and [Japanese](15573.md) diplomats reaffirmed military cooperation and sanctions to counter [North Korea](21255.md)’s nuclear ambitions. North Korea condemned the USA, South Korea, and Japan for pursuing denuclearization, vowing to expand its nuclear arsenal under [Kim Jong Un's leadership.](5962511.md)[^cite_note-71]

On March 2, 2025, the U.S. aircraft carrier [USS Carl Vinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Carl_Vinson) arrived in [Busan](68115.md), marking the first such visit since President Donald Trump's second term began in January. The visit, part of extended deterrence efforts, was accompanied by the [USS Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Princeton_\(CG-59\)) and [USS Sterett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Sterett_\(DDG-104\)). Rear Admiral Michael S. Wosje emphasized joint training to strengthen ties. North Korea, which recently tested strategic cruise missiles, has condemned U.S. military deployments.[^cite_note-72]

## Nuclear and missile diplomacy



South Korean President [Chun Doo Hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chun_Doo_Hwan) with U.S. President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md), November 1983

Between 1958 and 1991, the United States based a variety of nuclear weapons in South Korea. The number reached a peak of 950 warheads in 1967. Since 1991, when [President George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_George_H._W._Bush) announced the withdrawal of all tactical nuclear weapons based abroad, the Korean peninsula has seen ongoing efforts by the U.S. to negotiate an end to North Korea's own nuclear and missile development. These efforts have been characterized by "stalemates, crises and tentative progress." Despite the ongoing tensions, the U.S. has not redeployed nuclear weapons, although one recent press report suggests a majority of South Koreans are in favor of developing their own nuclear weapons. South Korea announced the deployment of the [Terminal High Altitude Area Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_High_Altitude_Area_Defense) anti-ballistic missile defense system at the end of 2017.[^cite_note-73]

On 21 September 2026, U.S. Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) met South Korean Foreign Minister [Cho Hyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cho_Hyun_\(diplomat\)) in New York. The two countries, together with [Japan](15573.md), reaffirmed security cooperation, discussed economic security and critical technologies, and reiterated their commitment to the denuclearization of [North Korea](21255.md).[^cite_note-74]

## Opinion polls

According to [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) in 2026, 45% of South Koreans have a favorable view towards the United States, while 55% have an unfavorable opinion.[^cite_note-\:5-6] On the political side, the United States supported South Korea after 1945 as a "staunch bastion against communism", even when the ROK itself was ruled by a US-backed dictatorship.[^cite_note-75] According to Pew Research Center in 2026, 77% of South Koreans have little or no confidence on U.S. President Donald Trump, while 22% have some or a lot of confidence.[^cite_note-\:5-6] In 2025, a poll by the [Korea Institute for National Unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Institute_for_National_Unification) showed that 65.7% of South Koreans agreed that the United States "does not consider South Korea’s interests".[^cite_note-76] According to a KStatResearch poll in October 2025, 56% of South Koreans had favorable opinions of the United States, while 40% had a negative opinion; most commonly cited reasons for negative opinions were Trump administration's erratic policies (46%), economic burden imposed by the US (26%), and the America First policy (20%). Additionally, only 27% of respondents said that "some national interests should be sacrificed" for the sake of the alliance with the United States, while 68% responded that "national interests should be the top priority".[^cite_note-\:4-77]

As relations with Korea waxed hot and cold under President Donald Trump, American public opinion regarding North Korea likewise fluctuated sharply, and no clear picture emerges.[^cite_note-78]

## Economic relations

US companies were actively involved in the development of the South Korean automotive industry. After [Hyundai](42257.md) entered the automobile business in 1967, [Ford](30433662.md) provided technical assistance.[^cite_note-\:07-79]<sup>: 416 </sup> A [joint venture](538782.md) between [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) and [Shinjin Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjin_Motors) began car production in 1972.[^cite_note-80]

In the 1980s, there were dozens of agreements in which US semiconductor companies sold or licensed chip technology to South Korean companies. The transfers occurred amidst the dominance of Japanese companies and a slump in the industry.[^cite_note-81]

In 2011, President [Lee Myung-bak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Myung-bak) signed the [United States–Korea Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Korea_Free_Trade_Agreement).[^cite_note-82]

US policy under [Joe Biden](145422.md) such as the 2022 [Inflation Reduction Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_Reduction_Act) (IRA) and [CHIPS and Science Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHIPS_and_Science_Act) attracted South Korean businesses in industries such as electric vehicles, batteries, semiconductors, and solar panels to build production facilities in the US.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-\:12-84] These investments were mainly in southern states.[^cite_note-\:12-84] Although the IRA initially created discord in relations,[^cite_note-85] the US government accommodated South Korean corporate interests.[^cite_note-86] [Donald Trump](4848272.md), however, revoked most Biden-era tax credits in the [One Big Beautiful Bill Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act), replacing them with tariffs.[^cite_note-\:03-87] Various joint ventures between US automakers and South Korean battery makers were consequently dissolved.[^cite_note-88] In September 2025, the US government detained hundreds of South Korean nationals in a raid of a Hyundai-LG battery plant in [Georgia](48830.md) state. A poll found that a majority of South Koreans surveyed found the crackdown to be excessive.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-KT610-90]

> “Halt investments in the U.S. immediately. Restore the dignity of the workers. If not, this will inevitably happen again,” said Um Mi-kyung, the KCTU’s acting secretary general. “We will fight to the very end to put an end to these U.S. investments that are destroying Korea’s domestic economy and dismantling our manufacturing industry.”[^cite_note-KT610-90]

The South Korean government's response to [Coupang](61730814.md)'s 2025 data breach created tension with the US government.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

South Korea's export-driven economy and competition with domestic U.S. producers in certain fields of products have led to some trade friction with the United States. For example, imports of certain steel and non-steel products have been subject to U.S. anti-dumping and countervailing duty investigations. A total of 29 U.S. imports from South Korea have been assessed.[^cite_note-93] There have also been major trade disputes between South Korea and the U.S. in areas such as [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication), [automotive industry](163778.md), [intellectual property rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_Property_Rights) issues, [pharmaceutical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical) industry, and the [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) industry.[^cite_note-fpc.state.gov-94]
