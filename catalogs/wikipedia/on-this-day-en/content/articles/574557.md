The **Bell AH-1 SuperCobra** is a twin-engined [attack helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopter) that was developed on behalf of, and primarily operated by, the [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) (USMC). The twin Cobra family, itself part of the larger [Huey family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huey_family), includes the **AH-1J SeaCobra**, the **AH-1T Improved SeaCobra**, and the **AH-1W SuperCobra**.

The Super Cobra was derived from the single-engined [AH-1 Cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_AH-1_Cobra), which had been developed during the mid-1960s as an interim [gunship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunship) for the U.S. Army. The USMC had quickly taken an interest in the type, but sought a twin-engined arrangement for greater operational safety at sea, along with more capable armaments. While initially opposed by the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense), who were keen to promote commonality across the services, in May 1968, an order for an initial 49 twin-engine AH-1J SeaCobras was issued to Bell. The type entered service during the final months of the US's involvement in the [Vietnam War](32611.md), seeing limited action in the theatre as a result.

The USMC promptly sought greater payload capacity than that provided by the original Sea Cobra; thus the AH-1T, equipped with the dynamic systems of the [Model 309](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_309_KingCobra) and a lengthened fuselage, was produced by Bell during the 1970s. In the following decade, in response to the denial of funding to procure the [Boeing AH-64 Apache](37746.md) attack helicopter, the USMC opted to procure a more capable variant of the AH-1T; equipped with revised fire control systems compatible with new munitions, such as the [AGM-114 Hellfire](38151.md) anti-tank missile. The new model, designated AH-1W, commenced delivery in 1986. Seeking to further develop the type, Bell opted to develop the extensively redesigned and modernised [Bell AH-1Z Viper](10805384.md) during the 1990s and 2000s.

The Sea Cobra was involved in multiple major operations during the latter half of the twentieth century, such as during the [United States invasion of Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Grenada) in 1983. During the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) of the 1980s, [Iranian](14653.md) Sea Cobras were intensely used, proving itself to be capable in both anti-armor and anti-aircraft warfare. [Turkey](11125639.md), who operated numerous Cobras and Super Cobras, used the type on multiple occasions against [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK) insurgents. On numerous occasions in the 1990s, USMC AH-1s were deployed during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) of the early 1990s, as well as for the [United States invasion of Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uphold_Democracy) in 1994, and the US intervention in the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars) in the late 1990s. In the twenty-first century, the type also saw action in the multi-decade [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md), and the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md). During October 2020, the USMC withdrew the last of its AH-1Ws in favor of exclusively operating the AH-1Z.

## Design and development



AH-1J SeaCobra flying off [Hawaii](13270.md), circa 1971

The AH-1 Cobra was developed in the mid-1960s as an interim [gunship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunship) for the U.S. Army for use during the [Vietnam War](32611.md). The Cobra shared the proven transmission, rotor system, and the [Lycoming T53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_T53) [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft) engine of the prolific [UH-1 "Huey"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_UH-1_Iroquois) utility helicopter.[^cite_note-mod_battle-2] By June 1967, the first AH-1G HueyCobras had been delivered. Between 1967 and 1973, Bell manufactured 1,116 AH-1Gs for the U.S. Army; these Cobras chalked up in excess of one million operational hours in the Vietnam theatre.[^cite_note-mod_battle-2]

The [U.S. Marine Corps](17349325.md) became particularly interested in the AH-1G Cobra, but expressed its preference for a twin-engined configuration that would provide improved safety in over-water operations.[^cite_note-popmech_2020-3] Furthermore, the service also sought a more potent turret-mounted weapon. Initially, the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense) had balked at providing the Marines with a twin-engine version of the Cobra in the belief that commonality with the Army's AH-1Gs outweighed the advantages of a different engine arrangement. However, the Marines won out; thus, during May 1968, Bell received a contract for 49 twin-engine AH-1J SeaCobras. The AH-1J was also equipped with a more powerful gun turret in the form of the three-barrel 20 mm [XM197](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M197_Gatling_gun) cannon, which was based on the six-barrel [M61 Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M61_Vulcan) cannon.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4] As an interim measure, the U.S. Army transferred 38 AH-1Gs to the Marines in 1969.[^cite_note-AH-1J_vector-5]

During the 1970s, the Marine Corps requested greater load carrying capability in high temperatures for the Cobra. Bell used systems from its [Model 309](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_309_KingCobra) to develop a new model, the AH-1T, that was provisioned with a lengthened tailboom and fuselage as well as the upgraded transmission and engines from the Model 309. Bell designed the AH-1T to be more reliable and easier to maintain in the field. The version was given full [TOW missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGM-71_TOW) capability with targeting system and other sensors. An advanced version, known as the AH-1T+, that was equipped with more powerful [T700-GE-700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_T700) engines and advanced avionics, was proposed to Iran in the late 1970s, but the [overthrow of the Shah of Iran](347268.md) in the late 1970s resulted in the sale being canceled.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4]



An AH-1T Sea Cobra being launched from the amphibious assault ship [*Iwo Jima*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Iwo_Jima_\(LPH-2\)), July 1985.

In the early 1980s, the Marine Corps sought a new navalized helicopter; accordingly, it evaluated the [Boeing AH-64 Apache](37746.md) attack helicopter over a two-week period in September 1981, which included shipboard operation tests. Furthermore, various concepts were studied at this time.[^cite_note-Richardson_Peacock_p60-61-6][^cite_note-Donald_p150-7] However, the service's request for funding to purchase the AH-64 was denied by Congress that same year.[^cite_note-Donald_p170-8] As an alternative option, the Marines procured a more powerful version of the AH-1T.[^cite_note-9] Other changes included modified fire control systems to carry and fire [AIM-9 Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) and [AGM-114 Hellfire](38151.md) missiles.[^cite_note-10] The new version, which was funded by Congress, received the AH-1W designation.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4][^cite_note-popmech_2020-3] During March 1986, deliveries of the AH-1W SuperCobra commenced, eventually totaling 179 new-built helicopters along with the upgrading of 43 existing AH-1Ts.[^cite_note-11]



On the right an AH-1W showing its twin exhaust, compared to the single on the AH-1F (*left*)

During the late 1990s, Bell was negotiating to acquire the [Romanian](25445.md) state-managed helicopter manufacturer [IAR Brașov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industria_Aeronautic%C4%83_Rom%C3%A2n%C4%83) with the intention of establishing an overseas production line for the AH-1W for multiple export customers.[^cite_note-12] In May 1997, the company signed an agreement with the Romanian State Ownership Fund to buy the government's 70 percent stake in IAR Brașov.[^cite_note-13] However, during November 1999, following protracted negotiations, Bell announced that it had abandoned its acquisition efforts, and thus the overseas production initiative, after the Romanian government had allegedly ceased responding to its proposals.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

The AH-1T+ demonstrator and AH-1W prototype were later tested with a new experimental composite four-blade main rotor system. The new configuration offered better performance, reduced noise, and improved battle damage tolerance. Lacking a USMC contract, Bell developed this new design into the AH-1Z with its own funds. By 1996, the Marines were again prevented from ordering the AH-64.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4] Developing a Marine version of the Apache would have been expensive and it was likely that the Marine Corps would be its only customer.[^cite_note-mod_battle-2] Instead, the service signed a contract for the [upgrading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-1_upgrade_program) of AH-1Ws into AH-1Zs.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4][^cite_note-16]

The [Bell AH-1Z Viper](10805384.md) retained much of the AH-1W's design, but also features several major changes.[^cite_note-popmech_2020-3] The AH-1Z's two redesigned wing stubs are longer with each adding a wingtip station for a missile such as the AIM-9 Sidewinder. Each wing has two other stations for 70 mm (2.75 in) Hydra rocket pods, or AGM-114 Hellfire quad missile launcher. The [AN/APG-78 Longbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/APG-78_Longbow) radar can be mounted on a wingtip station.[^cite_note-mod_battle-2][^cite_note-17] During October 2020, the last AH-1Ws were phased out of service with the Marine Corps in favor of the AH-1Z.[^cite_note-military20oct20-1][^cite_note-popmech_2020-3]

## Operational history

### United States



AH-1W launches a Sidewinder missile, 1987



A [rocket pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_pod) being loaded on an AH-1W's stub wing

During the closing months of the United States' involvement in the Vietnam War, the Marine Corps embarked the AH-1J SeaCobra assigned to HMA-369 (now HMLA-369) aboard [*Denver*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Denver_\(LPD-9\)), [*Cleveland*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Cleveland_\(LPD-7\)), and later [*Dubuque*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Dubuque_\(LPD-8\)), for sea-based interdiction of the Ho Chi Minh Trail in North Vietnam in the vicinity of Hon La (Tiger) Island. These were termed Marine Hunter-Killer (MARHUK) Operations and lasted from June to December 1972.[^cite_note-Verier_p104-18] As such, the type participated in the final American combat operations in Vietnam. Several AH-1Js were present to cover the [evacuation of the US Embassy in Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Saigon#Fall_of_Saigon_and_Operation_Frequent_Wind) in April 1975.[^cite_note-history_navymilAH-1J-19] During the Vietnam War, 306 AH-1 were destroyed.[^cite_note-roush-20]

During 1983, Marine Cobras took part in the [invasion of Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Urgent_Fury). They were typically used to perform close air support and helicopter escort missions; a total of two AH-1Ts were shot down and three crew members killed.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4] That same year, Marine AH-1s were also deployed off the coast of [Beirut](37428.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), amid the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) in support of the Multinational Peacekeeping Force.[^cite_note-21] In this theatre, the AH-1s were typically armed with Sidewinder missiles and guns, which were intended to be used as an emergency air defense measure to counter the threat of light civil aircraft being piloted by [suicide bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bomber).[^cite_note-22]

During the mid-1980s, the AH-1W variant entered service with the USMC.[^cite_note-23] Between, 1986 to 1999, the service took delivery of 179 Super Cobras.[^cite_note-military20oct20-1]

In the late 1980s, in response to the [Tanker War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanker_War) of the wider [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), USMC Cobras were dispatched for [Operation Earnest Will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Earnest_Will) in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) to escort shipping and deter attacks upon them.[^cite_note-efraimkarsh-24] It was during these missions that Cobras sank three Iranian [patrol boats](536773.md) while losing a single AH-1T to Iranian anti-aircraft fire.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4] USMC Cobras from [*Saipan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Saipan_\(LHA-2\)) flew "top cover" during an evacuation of U.S. and other foreign citizens from Liberia in 1990.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4]

During the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), 78 Marine SuperCobras were deployed, flying a total of 1,273 sorties in Iraq.[^cite_note-25] While no combat losses were reportedly incurred, three AH-1s were lost to accidents either during or after the combat operations. The AH-1W units were credited with destroying 97 tanks, 104 armored personnel carriers and vehicles, and two anti-aircraft artillery sites during the 100-hour ground campaign.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4][^cite_note-history_navymilAH-1J-19]

USMC Cobras were also used in various other operations throughout the 1990s.[^cite_note-Huey_Cobra-4] The type provided support for the U.S. humanitarian intervention in Somalia, [Operation Restore Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Restore_Hope), during 1992–1993, where they were used during the [Bloody Monday raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloody_Monday_raid) on 12 July 1993.[^cite_note-26] They were also employed during the [U.S. invasion of Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uphold_Democracy) during 1994. USMC Cobras were used in U.S. military interventions in the former Yugoslavia in the 1990s; specifically, two AH-1Ws assisted in the rescue of USAF Captain [Scott O'Grady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_O'Grady), after his F-16 was shot down by a SAM in June 1995.[^cite_note-timeres-27][^cite_note-nyet-28]



A Super Cobra flies past [USS *Fort McHenry*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Fort_McHenry) during a Search and Seizure (VBSS) drill in 2009.



An AH-1W before its final flight prior to retirement

During the twenty-first century, USMC Cobras participated in [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) in Afghanistan and in [Operation Iraqi Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iraqi_Freedom) in the [conflict in Iraq](5043324.md). While new replacement aircraft were considered as an alternative to major upgrades of the AH-1 fleet, Marine Corps studies showed that an upgrade was the most affordable, most supportable and most effective solution for the Marine Corps light attack helicopter mission.[^cite_note-29]

During the opening phase of the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md#Battle_of_Karbala), SuperCobras were deployed on the front lines, often flying in hunter-killer teams with Bell UH-1 Iroquois utility helicopters and other coalition aircraft.[^cite_note-30] Reportedly, 46 of the 58 USMC AH-1s deployed sustained battle damage, which was mostly from infantry-type weapons.[^cite_note-rand-31] AH-1Ws were involved in the rescue of [Private First Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_first_class) [Jessica Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lynch) from an Iraqi hospital.[^cite_note-32]

In late August 2016, Marine AH-1W Cobras flying from [USS *Wasp*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wasp_\(LHD-1\)) started flying combat missions over [Sirte, Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte,_Libya) against the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_of_Iraq_and_the_Levant_in_Libya), providing close air support for friendly militias on the ground.[^cite_note-33] In the later stages of the operation, AH-1Ws flew combat missions from the deck of [USS *San Antonio*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_San_Antonio) after that ship replaced *Wasp* in October 2016.[^cite_note-34]

In October 2020, the U.S. Marine Corps retired the last of its AH-1Ws after 34 years of service, having replacing the type with the Bell AH-1Z Viper. By the time of their retirement, the model had collectively accumulated 933,614 flight hours.[^cite_note-military20oct20-1][^cite_note-35]

### Iran



[Iran Army Aviation](45381390.md) AH-1J Cobra

In 1971, [Iran](14653.md) purchased 202 examples of an improved AH-1J, named "AH-1J International", from the United States.[^cite_note-36] This model featured improvements such as the uprated [P\&WC T400-WV-402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PT6T#Variants) engine and a strengthened drivetrain. Furthermore, recoil damping gear was fitted to the 20 mm [M197](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M197_electric_cannon) gun turret, while the gunner was provided with a stabilized sight along with a stabilized seat. Of the AH-1Js operated by the [Imperial Iranian Army Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Iranian_Army_Aviation), 62 were capable of using the [TOW missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOW_missile).[^cite_note-37]

Iranian AH-1Js participated in the Iran–Iraq War—which saw the most intensive use of helicopters in any conventional war.[^cite_note-38] Iranian AH-1Js (particularly the TOW-capable ones) were "exceptionally effective" in anti-armor warfare, inflicting heavy losses on Iraqi armored and vehicle formations. In operations over the barren terrain in [Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan) and later in southern Iraq, beside the standard tactics, Iranian pilots developed special, effective tactics, often in the same manner as the Soviets did with their [Mi-24s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi-24).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Due to the post-Revolution weapons sanctions, Iranians had to make do with what was at hand: they equipped the AH-1Js with [AGM-65 Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-65_Maverick) missiles and used them with some success in several operations.[^cite_note-airvectors.net-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Starting from October 1980, the AH-1Js engaged in [air-to-air combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogfight) with Iraqi [Mil Mi-24](384591.md) helicopters on several, separate occasions during the Iran–Iraq War. The results of these engagements are disputed. One document cited that Iranian AH-1Js took on Iraqi [Mi-8](957233.md) and Mi-24 helicopters.[^cite_note-44] Sources report that the Iranian AH-1 pilots achieved a 10\:1 kill ratio over the Iraqi helicopter pilots during these engagements (1\:5). Additionally, one source states that ten Iranian AH-1Js were lost in the war, compared to six Iraqi Mi-24s lost. The skirmishes are described as fairly evenly matched in another source.[^cite_note-45] The Mi-24 was faster and more powerful, but the AH-1J was more agile.[^cite_note-airvectors.net-41] There were even engagements between Iranian AH-1Js and Iraqi fixed-wing aircraft. The AH-1Js scored three confirmed kills against [MiG-21s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21), claimed a [Su-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-20), and shared in the destruction of a [MiG-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-23)—all using their 20 mm M197 cannon.[^cite_note-46]



Iranian AH-1J during a live fire exercise, 2016

During early 1984, an Iranian AH-1J Sea Cobra was shot down by an Iraqi [Pilatus PC-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-7) during [Operation Kheibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Kheibar) (Iranian pilots Reza Moghadam and Mohammad Yazdi were rescued).[^cite_note-47] About half of the AH-1Js were lost during the conflict to combat, accidents, and simple wear and tear.[^cite_note-airvectors.net-41] [Ali Akbar Shiroodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Akbar_Shiroodi?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Ahmad Keshvari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Keshvari) were two distinguished Iranian Cobra pilots during Iran-Iraq War and are considered wartime heroes in Iran. During 1988, two Soviet [MiG-23s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-23) shot down a pair of Iranian AH-1Js[^cite_note-48] that had strayed into western Afghan airspace.

Into the twenty-first century, Iranian AH-1Js remain in service with the Islamic Republic of Iran Army Aviation and have undergone indigenous [upgrade programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panha_2091). In 2010, Iran has unveiled its own AH-1J variants called Toufan I. In 2013, another improved variant called [Toufan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Aviation_Industries_Toufan) was unveiled.

By the 2020s, there was an increasing expectation that, amid a combination of factors such as accumulated [fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue), increasingly outdated capabilities, and a shortage of spare components, Iran will probably seek to replace its AH-1Js before too long, possibly with Russian-sourced Mi-24s.[^cite_note-49]

On 18 June 2025, during the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War), the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) stated that it destroyed eight AH-1s at a military airbase in [Kermanshah](1489244.md).[^cite_note-50]

### Taiwan



ROCA crew talking with pilot of AH-1W 543 while helicopter warms up

During 1984, [Taiwan](25734.md) announced its requirement for attack helicopters; it subsequently evaluated the [MBB Bo 105](1716038.md) and [MD 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Helicopters_MD_500) helicopters. The requirement resulted in an order for 42 AH-1W SuperCobras being placed during 1992. Deliveries of this batch ran from 1993 to 1997. During 1997, it was announced that an additional 21 AH-1Ws had been ordered.[^cite_note-jan2021_supportdeal-51] The [Ministry of National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defense_\(Republic_of_China\)) assigned the helicopters to the [ROC Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_Army) Aviation Training Centre and a pair of Army Aviation attack helicopter brigades.[^cite_note-Donald_p195-52] During the 2010s, Taiwan began supplementing its SuperCobras with 30 newly purchased AH-64Es.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] Into the 2020s, Taiwan continues to operate its SuperCobras; during January 2021, a NT\$339.24 million (US\$11.91 million) contract with the US was signed for the provision of technical support and spare parts the AH-1W fleet through to September 2027.[^cite_note-jan2021_supportdeal-51]

### Turkey

During the early 1990s, Turkey bought ten AH-1W SuperCobras; these were supplemented by 32 ex-US Army AH-1 Cobras.[^cite_note-Donald_p195-52] The sale was politically controversial in the United States; in April 1996, the purchase of ten additional AH-1Ws by Turkey was blocked by the [Clinton administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bill_Clinton).[^cite_note-55] During late 2011, Turkey requested the purchase of three AH-1Ws from the USMC inventory.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

The AH-1s have been repeatedly used in combat against [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (*PKK*) insurgents.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] On 13 May 2016, PKK militants shot down a Turkish Army AH-1W SuperCobra using a [9K38 Igla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K38_Igla) (SA-18 Grouse) MANPADS; in the published video, the missile severed the tail section from the rest of the helicopter, causing it to spin, fragment in midair and crash, killing the two pilots on board. The Turkish government initially stated that it had descended due to technical failure, though it later became clear that the helicopter had been shot down.[^cite_note-60] Amid the [2016 Turkish coup d'état attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt), Turkish Cobras were alleged to have fired upon several police vehicles.[^cite_note-61]

During April 2022, all of the Turkish Army's remaining AH-1Ws were transferred to the Turkish Navy; they are the first type of attack helicopter to be operated by the service.[^cite_note-turk_transfer2022-62] Being already appropriately suited to maritime operations, the AH-1Ws are to be used on board the [TCG Anadolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCG_Anadolu) [amphibious assault ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship); as such, the type has been speculated to have displaced a planned navalised version of the indigenously built [TAI/AgustaWestland T129 ATAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI/AgustaWestland_T129_ATAK) attack helicopter.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]
