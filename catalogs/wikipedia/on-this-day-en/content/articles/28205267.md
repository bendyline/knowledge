# Petroleum industry in Iran

This article is about the petroleum industry in Iran. For the industry's economic effects, see [Economy of Iran](64704.md).

[Iran](14653.md) is considered an [energy superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_superpower), largely due to its [petroleum industry](994887.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] In 2004, Iran produced 5.1 percent of the world's total [crude oil](23195.md) (3.9 million barrels (620,000 m<sup>3</sup>; 160 million US gal) per day), which generated revenues of US\$25 billion to US\$30 billion and was the country's primary source of [foreign currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_reserves).[^cite_note-loc1-5][^cite_note-6] At 2006 levels of production, oil proceeds represented about 18.7% of [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP). However, the importance of the [hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) sector to [Iran's economy](64704.md) has been far greater. The oil and gas industry has been the engine of economic growth, directly affecting public development projects, the [government's annual budget](50913538.md#Public_finance_and_fiscal_policy), and most [foreign exchange sources](3961876.md#Foreign_reserves).[^cite_note-loc1-5]

In FY 2009, the sector accounted for 60% of total government revenues and 80% of the total annual value of both exports and foreign currency earnings. Oil and gas revenues are affected by the value of crude oil on the international market. It has been estimated that at the [Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_the_Petroleum_Exporting_Countries) (OPEC) quota level (December 2004), a one-dollar change in the price of crude oil on the international market would alter [Iran's oil revenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_\(Iran\)#Revenues_from_crude_oil) by US\$1 billion.[^cite_note-loc1-5]

In 2012, Iran, which exported around 1.5 million barrels of crude oil a day, was the second-largest exporter among the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries.[^cite_note-EIAApr2613-7] In the same year, officials in Iran estimated that annual oil and gas revenues could reach \$250 billion by 2015.[^cite_note-oil-8] The industry was subsequently disrupted by an [international embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo_against_Iran) from July 2012 to January 2016.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Despite ongoing sanctions, Iran's petroleum exports have increased in recent years. According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration, Iran exported an average of 1.4 million barrels per day of crude oil and condensate in 2023, rising to approximately 1.5 million barrels per day during the first eight months of 2024.[^cite_note-EIA2024-11] Petroleum product exports, including liquefied petroleum gas, fuel oil, and diesel, exceeded 1 million barrels per day in 2023, up from around 820,000 barrels per day in 2022. The majority of Iranian crude exports were directed to China, while other export destinations included Syria, the United Arab Emirates, and Venezuela.[^cite_note-EIA2024-11]

## History

Main articles: [Anglo-Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iranian_Oil_Company) and [1953 Iranian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Iranian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

### The era of international control, 1901–1979

Further information: [National Iranian Oil Company § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company#History), [Nationalization of the Iranian oil industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization_of_the_Iranian_oil_industry), [Consortium Agreement of 1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium_Agreement_of_1954), [1973 Sale and Purchase Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Sale_and_Purchase_Agreement), and [1970s energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_energy_crisis)



[Abadan Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abadan_Refinery)

Iran's oil industry began in 1901, when the [British](31717.md) speculator [William D'Arcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Knox_D'Arcy) received a concession from Iran to explore and develop southern Iran's oil resources. The exploration in Iran was led by George Reynolds. The discovery of oil on May 26, 1908[^cite_note-12] led to the formation in 1909 of the London-based [Anglo-Persian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iranian_Oil_Company) (APOC). By purchasing a majority of the company's shares in 1914, the British government gained direct control of the Iranian oil industry, which it would not relinquish for 37 years. After 1935 the APOC was called the [Anglo-Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iranian_Oil_Company) (AIOC). A 60-year agreement signed in 1933 established a flat payment to Iran of four British pounds for every ton of crude oil exported and denied Iran any right to control oil exports.[^cite_note-loc1-5]

In 1950, ongoing popular demand prompted a vote in the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_of_Iran) to nationalize the petroleum industry. In 1951, the government of Prime Minister [Mohammad Mosaddeq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Mosaddeq) formed the [National Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company) (NIOC). A [1953 coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Iranian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) led by [British](31717.md) and [U.S. intelligence](5183633.md) agencies ousted the Mosaddeq government and paved the way for a new oil agreement.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In 1954, [a new agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium_Agreement_of_1954) divided profits equally between the NIOC and a multinational consortium that had replaced the AIOC, which at this time had been renamed [The British Petroleum Company](18998720.md). In 1973, after the Shah threatened that the agreement would not be extended unless the consortium doubled oil production or handing over oil production entirely to Iranians, Iran signed [a new 20-year concession with the consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Sale_and_Purchase_Agreement), which handed over oil production entirely to NIOC while giving the consortium privileged priority to purchase oil from NIOC.[^cite_note-loc1-5]

Beginning in the late 1950s, many of Iran's international oil agreements did not produce the expected outcomes. Even those oil companies that managed to extract oil in their designated areas contributed very little to the country's total oil production. By the time of the [Islamic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Revolution) of 1978–79, the five largest international companies that had agreements with the NIOC accounted for only 10.4 percent of total oil production. During this period, [Iran's oil industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_of_Iran#Oil,_gas_and_petrochemicals) remained disconnected from other industries, particularly manufacturing. This separation promoted inefficiencies in [the country's overall industrial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_of_Iran).[^cite_note-loc1-5]

In 1973, at a time when Iran was the second-largest oil exporter in the world and the [Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War) of October 6–25 was pressurizing the price of oil, the [Shah of Iran](13877.md) told the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)*,[^cite_note-15] "Of course the world price of oil is going to rise. ... Certainly! And how ...; You \[Western nations] increased the price of wheat you sell us by 300%, and the same for sugar and cement ...; You buy our crude oil and sell it back to us, refined as petrochemicals, at a hundred times the price you've paid to us ...; It's only fair that, from now on, you should pay more for oil. Let's say ten times more."

### The era of nationalized oil, 1979–present

See also: [Economic history of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_Iran)



The [Arak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arak,_Iran) Petrochemical complex

#### Isolation and stagnation under Khomeini

Following the 1979 Revolution, the NIOC took control of Iran's petroleum industry and canceled Iran's international oil agreements. In 1980, the exploration, production, sale, and export of oil were delegated to the [Ministry of Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_\(Iran\)). Initially Iran's post-revolutionary oil policy was based on foreign currency requirements and the long-term [preservation of the natural resource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Iran). Following the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), this policy was replaced by a more aggressive approach: maximizing exports and accelerating [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth). From 1979 until 1998, Iran did not sign any oil agreements with foreign oil companies.

#### Khatami era

Early in the first administration of President [Mohammad Khatami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Khatami), in office 1997–2005, the government paid special attention to developing Iran's oil and gas industry. Oil was defined as inter-generational capital and an indispensable foundation of [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_Iran#Review_of_Iran's_successive_economic_plans_\(1991-2010\)). Thus, between 1997 and 2004 Iran invested more than US\$40 billion in expanding the capacity of existing oil fields and discovering and exploring new fields and deposits. These projects were financed either in the form of [joint investments with foreign companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment_in_Iran) or domestic contractors or through direct investment by the NIOC.[^cite_note-loc1-5]

In accordance with the law, foreign investment in oil discovery was possible only in the form of [buyback agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment_in_Iran#Buy-back) under which the NIOC was required to reimburse expenses and retain complete ownership of an oil field. Marketing of crude oil to potential buyers was managed by the NIOC and by a government enterprise called [Nicoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company#Subsidiary_companies). Nicoo marketed Iranian oil to Africa, and the NIOC marketed to Asia and Europe.[^cite_note-loc1-5] According to IHS CERA estimate, Iran's oil revenue will increase by a third to US\$100 billion in 2011 even though Iran is under an extended [period of U.S. sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Iran).[^cite_note-FPdec192011-16]

#### Post-JCPOA era

in July 2015, the signing of the [JCPOA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JCPOA) by [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani) and [Javad Zarif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javad_Zarif) with [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) heralded a new era of détente in foreign relations. With it, Iran was once again allowed to sell its oil unencumbered on the world stage. The withdrawal by [Donald Trump from the JCPOA in May 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_withdrawal_from_the_Joint_Comprehensive_Plan_of_Action) was an ill wind for Iran because he decided to re-apply the sanctions regime that had caused such difficulties to [Ali Khamenei](385653.md). For example, the [*Grace 1* oil tanker](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian%20Darya%201) was captured near [Gibraltar](7607314.md) in July 2019 at the request of the U.S. by the armed forces of the UK. The incident was one of several that have been labelled as the 2019 Persian Gulf crisis, but the crisis soon widened to encompass more distant seas where Iranian arms are impotent.[^cite_note-sabiti-17]

In May 2019, the Iranian tanker *Happiness 1* suffered difficulties near [Jiddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiddah). In August the tanker *Helm* encountered difficulties while transiting the [Red Sea](26191.md), and in October the Iranian tanker *[Sabiti](22596135.md)* was attacked by two missiles of unknown origin, again in the Red Sea.[^cite_note-sabiti-17] The fully laden *Sabiti* had been destined for the "Eastern Mediterranean".[^cite_note-bnnp-18]

[China](5405.md) is a significant supporter of the Iranian petroleum industry. In 2017, 64% of an export total \$16.9 billion with China was labelled "crude oil".[^cite_note-oecic-19] Because the [National Iranian Tanker Company](22596135.md) cannot defend the product on the oceans, it most often leaves port under the flag of another nation which is able to defend its own interests.[^cite_note-nyts-20] The case of a [Shanghai](27643.md)-based operator of oil tankers, Kunlun Holdings is illustrative: in early June it was operating with a shipment of Iranian crude oil as the *Pacific Bravo* before its transponder disappeared on 5 June in the [Straits of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Malacca). When it reappeared on 18 July, the ship had been relieved of its load and was renamed the *Latin Venture*. The U.S., which had warned ports not to offload the \$120 million cargo of the *Pacific Bravo*, was unhappy about the failure of its sanctions regime.[^cite_note-np42-21] Kunlun Holdings is owned by the Bank of Kunlun, which is owned by the [(SOE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/\(SOE\)) [CNPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNPC). The Bank "has long been the financial institution at heart of China-Iran bilateral trade".[^cite_note-cnbc1-22] [Chinese foreign ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) spokesperson [Geng Shuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geng_Shuang) "resolutely opposes" American unilateral sanctions.[^cite_note-bnb-23]

## Oil production and reserves

Main articles: [National Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company) and [Oil reserves in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves_in_Iran)



Iran's oil and gas production, 1970–2009 data, 2010–2030 projected



Iran's Petroleum production and consumption, 1977–2010

Iran's total oil production reached a peak level of 6.6 Mbbl/d (1,050,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) in 1976. By 1978, Iran had become the second-largest [OPEC](166346.md) producer and exporter of crude oil and the fourth-largest producer in the world. After a lengthy decline in the 1980s, caused by the [Iraq-Iran War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq-Iran_War), economic sanctions and the decline of mature oil fields,[^cite_note-24] production of crude oil began to increase steadily in 1987. In 2008 Iran produced 3.9-million-barrels (620,000 m<sup>3</sup>) per day (bpd) and exported 2.4 Mbbl/d (380,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-25]

Accounting for 5 percent of world production, it returned to its previous position as OPEC's second-largest producer. In 2005, Iran had the estimated capacity to produce 4.5 Mbbl/d (720,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d); it was believed that production capacity could increase to 5 Mbbl/d (790,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) to 7 Mbbl/d (1,100,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d), but only with a substantial increase in [foreign investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment_in_Iran).[^cite_note-26]

Iran's long-term sustainable oil production rate is estimated at 3.8 Mbbl/d (600,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-loc1-5] According to the Iranian government, Iran has enough reserves to produce oil for the next 100 years while oil reserves of other Middle Eastern countries will be depleted in the next 60 years and most other oil-rich countries will lose their reserves within the next 30 years.

In 2006 Iran reported crude oil reserves of 132.5 billion barrels (2.107×10<sup>10</sup> m<sup>3</sup>), accounting for about 15 percent of OPEC's proven reserves and 11.4 percent of [world proven reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_proven_oil_reserves). While the estimate of world crude oil reserves remained nearly steady between 2001 and 2006, at 1,154 billion barrels (1.835×10<sup>11</sup> m<sup>3</sup>), the estimate of [Iran's oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company) was revised upward by 32 percent when a [new field was discovered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIOC_Recent_Discoveries) near [Bushehr](205669.md). Market value of Iran's total [oil](4207510.md) reserves at [international crude price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_of_petroleum) of \$75 per barrel stands at \~US\$10 trillion.[^cite_note-27]

In the early 2000s, leading international oil firms from [China](5405.md), [France](5843419.md), [India](14533.md), [Italy](14532.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md), [Norway](21241.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Spain](26667.md), and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) had agreements to develop [Iran's oil and gas fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company). In 2004 China signed a major agreement to buy oil and gas from Iran, as well as to develop Iran's [Yadavaran oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadavaran_Field). The value of this contract was estimated at US\$150 billion to US\$200 billion over 25 years.[^cite_note-loc1-5][^cite_note-28]

In 2009, [China National Petroleum Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Petroleum_Corp) (CNPC) signed a deal with the National Iranian Oil Company with CNPC taking ownership of a 70% stake upon promising to pay 90 percent of the development costs for the South [Azadegan oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadegan_oil_field), with the project needing investment of up to \$2.5 billion. Earlier that year, CNPC also won a \$2 billion deal to develop the first phase of the North Azadegan oilfield.[^cite_note-29]

A more modest yet important agreement was signed with [India](14533.md) to explore and produce oil and natural gas in southern Iran. In 2006 the rate of production decline was 8 percent for Iran's existing onshore oil fields (furnishing the majority of oil output) and 10 percent for existing offshore fields. Little exploration, upgrading, or establishment of new fields occurred in 2005–6.[^cite_note-loc1-5] However, the [threat of American retaliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-Libya_Sanctions_Act) kept the investment way below the desired levels.[^cite_note-30] It only allowed Iran to continue to keep its oil export at or below its OPEC determined quota level.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Today, much of the equipment needed for oil industry are being produced by [local manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company#NIOC's_major_domestic_contractors) in Iran. Besides, Iran is among the few countries that has reached the technology and "know-how" for drilling in the deep waters.[^cite_note-33]

Along with other Middle East countries where oil reserves are easy to extract, Iran has relatively low production costs around \$10 per barrel, making most oil sales prices profitable. By comparison, Canada and United States have production costs around \$40 to \$60 per barrel.[^cite_note-dom-34]

## Oil refining and consumption

Main articles: [National Iranian Oil Refining and Distribution Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Refining_and_Distribution_Company), [Iranian targeted subsidy plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_targeted_subsidy_plan), and [Fuel smuggling in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Gasoline_Rationing_Plan_in_Iran#Fuel_smuggling)



Iran's refining capacity, 2007–2013 estimate

In 2011 [Iran's refineries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Refining_and_Distribution_Company#Installations_and_capabilities) had a combined capacity of 1.457 Mbbl/d (231,600 m<sup>3</sup>/d). The largest refineries have the following capacities: Abadan, 350,000 bbl/d (56,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d); Esfahan, 284,000 bbl/d (45,200 m<sup>3</sup>/d); Bandar-e Abbas, 232,000 bbl/d (36,900 m<sup>3</sup>/d); Tehran, 220,000 bbl/d (35,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d); Arak, 170,000 bbl/d (27,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d); and Tabriz, 100,000 bbl/d (16,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-IEAIran-35]

In 2004, pipelines conveyed 69 percent of total refined products; trucks, 20 percent; rail, 7 percent; and tankers, 4 percent. Oil refining produces a wide range of oil products, such as [liquefied petroleum gas](361384.md) (LPG), [gasoline](23639.md), [kerosene](16992.md), [fuel oil](392547.md), and [lubricants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubricants).[^cite_note-loc1-5] As of 2011 Iran is a net exporter of petroleum products thanks to large exports of residual fuel oil, but the refineries cannot meet domestic demand for lighter distillates such as gasoline.[^cite_note-IEAIran-35]

Between 1981 and 2010, domestic consumption of oil products increased from 0.6 Mbbl/d (95,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) to 1.8 Mbbl/d (290,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d)[^cite_note-IEAIran-35]—an average annual growth rate of 3.7 percent. Between 1981 and 2004, consumption of gasoline grew by 6 percent annually, but domestic production met only 75 percent of demand for this product. In 2004 the country imported US\$1.6 billion worth of gasoline. By 2006 it imported 41 percent of its gasoline, but by 2010 imports were down to 19.5% of gasoline consumption[^cite_note-IEAIran-35] and heavy investment in new refining capacity may see Iran exporting gasoline by 2015.[^cite_note-IEAIran-35] Refining capacity increased 18% in 2010 and the target is to increase refining capacity to 3.5 million barrels per day.[^cite_note-36]

## Trade in oil and oil products

See also: [Iranian Oil Bourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Oil_Bourse), [National Iranian Tanker Company](22596135.md), and [Oil and gas revenues in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_\(Iran\)#Revenues_from_crude_oil)

In 2006, exports of crude oil totaled 2.5 Mbbl/d (400,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d), or about 62.5 percent of Iran's crude oil production. The direction of crude oil exports changed after the [Revolution](347268.md) because of the [U.S. trade embargo on Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-Libya_Sanctions_Act) and the marketing strategy of the [NIOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company). Initially, Iran's post-revolutionary crude oil export policy was based on foreign currency requirements and the need for long-term preservation of the natural resource. In addition, the government expanded oil trade with other developing countries.[^cite_note-loc2-37]

While the shares of Europe, Japan, and the United States declined from an average of 87 percent of oil exports before the Revolution to 52 percent in the early 2000s, the share of exports to East Asia, excluding Japan, increased significantly. In addition to crude oil exports, Iran exports oil products. In 2006 it exported 282,000 barrels (44,800 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil products, or about 21 percent of its total oil product output.[^cite_note-loc2-37] Iran plans to invest a total of \$500 billion in the oil sector before 2025.[^cite_note-nitc.co.ir-38][^cite_note-heritage-39]

In 2010, Iran, which exports around 2.6 million barrels of crude oil a day, was the second-largest exporter among the [Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Petroleum_Exporting_Countries).[^cite_note-WSJDec192011-40] Several major emerging economies depend on Iranian oil: 10% of South Korea's oil imports come from Iran, 9% of India's and 6% of China's.[^cite_note-FPdec192011-16] Iranian oil makes up 7% of Japan's and 30% of all Greece's oil imports.[^cite_note-FPdec192011-16] Iran is also a major oil supplier to Spain and Italy.[^cite_note-WSJDec192011-40] In 2010, officials in Iran estimate that Iran's annual oil and gas revenues could reach \$250 billion by 2015 once the current projects come on stream.[^cite_note-oil-8]

According to a 2010 estimate, Iran's oil revenue will increase by a third to US\$100 billion in 2011, even though Iran is under an extended period of U.S. sanctions.[^cite_note-FPdec192011-16] As of January 2012, Iran exports 22% of its oil to China, 14% to Japan, 13% to India, 10% to South Korea, 7% to Italy, 7% to Turkey, 6% to Spain and the remainder to France, Greece (and other European countries), Taiwan, Sri Lanka, South Africa.[^cite_note-41]

In 2020, Iranian exports of petrochemicals and petroleum products were worth almost \$20 billion.[^cite_note-reuters-20210917-42] By 2025, Iran exports 80% of its oil to China.[^cite_note-dom-34]

## Pipeline transport

According to the [Global Energy Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Energy_Monitor) website, in 2023, the top five countries in terms of developing pipelines, proposed and under construction, are the United States, India, Iraq, Iran, and Tanzania. Iran has ranked first in terms of oil pipelines under construction. Ministry of Petroleum Iran is among the five parent companies developing oil pipelines.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

## Natural gas

Main articles: [National Iranian Gas Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Gas_Company) and [Natural gas in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_in_Iran)



In 2010, Iran held 10% of the world's proven [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves) and 15% of its [gas](22131.md). It is [OPEC](166346.md)'s second largest exporter and the world's fourth largest oil producer.

[^cite_note-EIA-45]

[^cite_note-46]



Iran's natural gas production (red) and exports (black), 1960–2012

In addition to the [natural gas](22131.md) associated with oil exploration and extraction, an estimated 62 percent of Iran's 32.3 trillion cubic meters of proven natural gas reserves in 2006 were located in independent natural gas fields, an amount second only to those of Russia.[^cite_note-47] In 2006 annual production reached 105 billion cubic meters, with fastest growth occurring over the previous 15 years. In 2006 natural gas accounted for about 50 percent of [domestic energy consumption](10580575.md), in part because domestic [gas prices were heavily subsidized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_targeted_subsidy_plan).[^cite_note-loc2-37] Natural gas production will reach 700 million cubic meter/day by 2012[^cite_note-48] and 900 million cubic meter/day by 2015.

Since 1979, infrastructure investment by Iranian and foreign oil firms has increased pipeline[^cite_note-49] capacity to support the Iranian gas industry. Between 1979 and 2003, pipelines to transport natural gas to refineries and to domestic consumers increased from 2,000 kilometers to 12,000 kilometers. In the same period, natural gas distribution pipelines increased from 2,000 kilometers to 45,000 kilometers in response to growing domestic consumption.[^cite_note-payvand.com-50]

Gas processing plants are located at Ahvaz, Dalan, Kangan, and Marun, in a corridor along the northern [Persian Gulf](24761.md) close to the major gas fields. [South Pars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pars), Iran's largest natural gas field, has received substantial foreign investment. With its output intended for both export and domestic consumption, South Pars is expected to reach full production in 2015. However, delays and lower production in the Iranian side due to sanctions is resulting in migration of gas to the Qatari part and a loss for Iran.[^cite_note-payvand.com-50]

The output of South Pars is the basis of the [Pars Special Economic Energy Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asalouyeh#PSEEZ), a complex of petrochemical and [natural gas processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_processing) plants and port facilities established in 1998 on the Persian Gulf south of Kangan.[^cite_note-loc2-37] Iran is able to produce all the parts needed for its gas refineries.[^cite_note-51] Iran is now the third country in the world to have developed [gas to liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_to_liquids) (GTL) technology.[^cite_note-52]

In the 1980s, Iran began to replace oil, coal, charcoal, and other fossil-fuel energy sources with natural gas, which is environmentally safer. The share of natural gas in Iranian household energy consumption, which averaged 54 percent in 2004, was projected to increase to 69 percent by 2009. Overall, natural gas consumption in Iran was expected to grow by more than 10 percent per annum between 2005 and 2009.[^cite_note-loc2-37]

With international [oil prices](5137675.md) increasing and projected to continue increasing, international demand for natural gas and investment in production and transportation of natural gas to consumer markets both increased in the early 2000s. Iran set a goal of increasing its natural gas production capacity to 300 billion cubic meters by 2015 while keeping oil production stable.[^cite_note-53] To achieve this capacity, the government has planned a joint investment worth US\$100 billion in the oil and gas industry through 2015.

In 2010, the market value of Iran's total [natural gas](22131.md) reserves at a comparative international energy price of \$75 per barrel of [oil](4207510.md) stood at \~US\$4 trillion.[^cite_note-International_Monetary_Fund-54] In 2011, following the discovery of a large gas deposit in the Caspian sea, The *Sardar Jangal* gas field, estimated at 50 trillion cubic feet, some 1.4 trillion cubic meters, Iran could rank first in the world in terms of gas reserves.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] [Iranian media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Iran) have reported in 2012 the discovery of gas field "as big as South Pars gas field" in the oil-rich [Khuzestan province](448079.md).[^cite_note-57]

In 2011, Iran signed a [contract with Baghdad and Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-Iraq-Syria_pipeline) in order to export Iran's gas to [Iraq](7515928.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), the Mediterranean region and eventually Europe.[^cite_note-58] [Oman](22316.md) and [Kuwait](7515890.md) were negotiating an import agreement in 2014.[^cite_note-bl-20140602-59] Once all pipelines become operational, Iran can potentially export a total of more than 200 million cubic meters of natural gas every year to Iraq, India, Pakistan, and Oman.[^cite_note-60]

### Major foreign projects

See also: [Foreign direct investment in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment_in_Iran) and [Sanctions against Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Iran)



Projected [Iran–Pakistan–India gas pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_pipeline).

In 2004, Iran signed a contract with France and Malaysia for production and export of natural gas and another agreement with European and Asian companies for expansion and marketing of its natural gas resources. In 2005, Iran exported natural gas to [Turkey](11125639.md) and was expected to expand its market to Armenia, China, Japan, other East Asian countries, India, Pakistan, and Europe. In spring 2007, the first section of a new line to Armenia opened, as a much-discussed [major pipeline to India and Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_pipeline) remained in the negotiation stage.[^cite_note-loc2-37]

In 2007, Switzerland's energy company EGL, signed a 25-year LNG export deal with Iran's National Iranian Gas Export Company, reportedly valued at 18 billion. [Switzerland](26748.md) will buy 5.5 billion cubic meters of Iranian natural gas each year, beginning in 2011. In April 2007, [OMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OMV), the [Austrian](26964606.md) partially state-owned energy company, signed letters of intent with Iran, worth an estimated \$22.8 billion (22 billion euros), for Iran to supply Europe with gas. The United States has expressed strong opposition to both the Swiss and Austrian deals with Iran.[^cite_note-Congress1-61]

The [National Iranian Gas Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Gas_Company) (NIGC) is expected to finalize a natural gas export deal with [Pakistan](23235.md), with exports set to begin in 2011. The gas would be transported through a "[Peace pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_pipeline)", worth about \$7.4 billion. The plan initially also included exporting gas to [India](14533.md), but negotiations have stalled over pricing. Iran also is discussing a gas production and export deal with [Turkey](11125639.md). Under the plan, Turkey would assist in developing Iran's South Pars field in exchange for cash or natural gas. Gas would be shipped from Iran to Turkey and Europe via a new pipeline that Turkey plans to build.[^cite_note-Congress1-61]

Other notable petroleum sector development deals include those with [Russia](25391.md) and [China](5405.md). On February 19, 2008, Russian state gas company [Gazprom](320489.md) announced a deal to establish a joint venture company to develop the offshore Iranian [South Pars gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pars_gas_field). A [China National Offshore Oil Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Offshore_Oil_Corporation) (CNOOC) investment deal, valued at \$16 billion, to develop Iran's [North Pars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pars) gas field and to build a [liquid natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_natural_gas) (LNG) plant, was supposed to be signed on February 27, 2008 but has been delayed.[^cite_note-62] The state-operated National Iranian Oil Company (NIOC) and CNOOC signed a memorandum of understanding in December 2006 for the project, under which CNOOC would purchase 10 million metric tons per year of LNG for 25 years.[^cite_note-Congress1-61]

## Petrochemicals

Main article: [National Petrochemical Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Petrochemical_Company)



The Abadan Petrochemical Complex

In the early 2000s, an ambitious state [petrochemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrochemicals) project called for expansion of annual output in that industry from 9 million tons in 2001 to 100 million tons in 2015.[^cite_note-63] In 2006, output capacity was estimated at 15 million tons. The goal of this expansion is to increase the percentage of Iran's processed petroleum-based exports, which are more profitable than raw materials. In 2005, Iran exported US\$1.8 billion of petrochemical products, about one-third of total non-oil exports in that year. Receiving 30 percent of Iran's petrochemical exports between them, [China](5405.md) and [India](14533.md) were the major trading partners in this industry.[^cite_note-loc3-64]

Iran's domestic resource base gives it a unique comparative advantage in producing petrochemicals when international crude oil prices rise. The gain has been greatest in those plants that use liquid gas as their main input. For FY 2006, the petrochemical industry's share of GDP was projected to be about 2 percent.[^cite_note-loc3-64] Iran plans to invest a total of \$500 billion in the oil sector before 2025.[^cite_note-nitc.co.ir-38][^cite_note-heritage-39]

Iran's petrochemical industries have absorbed a large amount of private and public investment. In the early 2000s, 43 percent of these investments was financed by [Iran's National Petrochemical Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Petrochemical_Company), a subsidiary of the [Ministry of Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_of_Iran), which administers the entire petrochemical sector. Another 53 percent is owned by foreign creditors (more than 100 foreign banks and foreign companies), 3 percent by banks, and 1 percent by the capital market. Most of the petrochemical industry's physical capital is imported, and the industry does not have strong backward linkages to manufacturing industries.[^cite_note-loc3-64]

In 2006, new petrochemical plants came online at [Marun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marun_petrochemical_complex) and [Asaluyeh](3393482.md), and construction began on three others.[^cite_note-loc3-64] The Iran National Petrochemical Company's output capacity will increase to over 100 million tpa by 2015 from an estimated 50 million tpa in 2010, becoming the world's second-largest chemical producer globally after [Dow Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Chemical), with Iran housing some of the world's largest chemical complexes.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]
