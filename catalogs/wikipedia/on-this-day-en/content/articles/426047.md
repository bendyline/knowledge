**Gyeongju** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 경주; pronounced [\[kjʌŋ.dʑu\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean)), historically known as **Sŏrabŏl** (서라벌; pronounced [\[sʰʌ.ɾa.bʌɭ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean)), is a coastal city in the far southeastern corner of [North Gyeongsang Province](314848.md), South Korea.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Gyeongju_1-3][^cite_note-Xinhua-4] It is the second largest city by area in the province after [Andong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andong), covering 1,324 km<sup>2</sup> (511 sq mi) with a population of 264,091 people as of December 2012.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Gyeongju_1-3][^cite_note-Gyeongbuk_area-5] Gyeongju is 370 km (230 mi) southeast of [Seoul](19159283.md),[^cite_note-Brisbane-6] and 55 km (34 mi) east of [Daegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu).[^cite_note-Kyŏngju-7] The city borders [Cheongdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheongdo) and [Yeongcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongcheon) to the west, [Ulsan](323111.md) to the south and [Pohang](326500.md) to the north, while to the east lies the coast of the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan).[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Gyeongju_1-3] Numerous low mountains—outliers of the [Taebaek range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taebaek_Mountains)—are scattered around the city.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Nature-8]

Gyeongju was the capital of the ancient kingdom of [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla) (57 BC – 935 AD), which ruled about two-thirds of the Korean peninsula at its height between the 7th and 9th centuries, for close to one thousand years, where it was known as Sŏrabŏl. According to [Samguk yusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samguk_yusa), Sŏrabŏl had more than 170,000 households or between 800,000 and 900,000 inhabitants.

[Later Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Silla) was a prosperous and wealthy country,[^cite_note-9] and Gyeongju[^cite_note-10] was the fourth largest city in the world.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] A vast number of archaeological sites and cultural properties from this period remain in the city. Gyeongju is often referred to as "the museum without walls".[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Travel_Blackboard-16] Among such historical treasures, [Seokguram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seokguram) grotto, [Bulguksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulguksa) temple, [Gyeongju Historic Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Historic_Areas) and [Yangdong Folk Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangdong_Folk_Village) are designated as [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage) Sites by [UNESCO](21786641.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Yangdong_2010-18] The many major historical sites have helped Gyeongju become one of the most popular [tourist destinations in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_South_Korea).[^cite_note-Kyŏngju-7][^cite_note-19]

The city of Gyeongju was united with the nearby rural Gyeongju County in 1995 and is now an urban–rural complex.[^cite_note-20] It is similar to 53 other small and medium-sized cities with a population under 300,000 people in South Korea.[^cite_note-21] As well as its rich historical heritage, Gyeongju today is affected by the economic, demographic, and social trends that have shaped modern [South Korean culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_South_Korea). Tourism remains the major economic driver, but manufacturing activities have developed due to its proximity to major industrial centers such as Ulsan and Pohang. Gyeongju is connected to the nationwide rail and highway networks, which facilitate industrial and tourist traffic.[^cite_note-Industry-22][^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23][^cite_note-Tamásy-24]

## History

Main article: [History of Gyeongju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Gyeongju)

The early history of Gyeongju is closely tied to that of the [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla) kingdom, of which it was the capital. Gyeongju first enters non-Korean records as [Saro-guk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saro-guk), during the [Samhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhan) period.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25] Korean records, probably based on the dynastic chronicles of Silla, record that Saro-guk was established in 57 BCE, when six small villages in the Gyeongju area united under [Bak Hyeokgeose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bak_Hyeokgeose_of_Silla). As the kingdom expanded, it changed its name to Silla.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Silla-26] During the Silla period, the city was called "Sŏrabŏl" (this toponym would eventually evolve into a Korean word for "capital city" as in [Seoul](19159283.md)),[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25] "Gyerim" (lit. Rooster's forest), or "Geumseong" (lit. City of Gold or Metal *i.e.* Impenetrable Fortress).[^cite_note-Lee_KB_p.78-27]

&#x20;After the [unification of the peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Silla#Unification) up to [Taedong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taedong_River)[^cite_note-28] in 668 AD, Gyeongju became the center of Korean political and cultural life.[^cite_note-Robinson_et_al._2007._p.28-29] The city was home to the Silla court and the great majority of the kingdom's elite. Its prosperity became legendary, and was reported as far away as [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) according to the 9th century book *[The Book of Roads and Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Roads_and_Kingdoms_\(ibn_Khordadbeh\))*.[^cite_note-30] The historical text *[Samguk yusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samguk_yusa)* give the city's population in its peak period as 178,936 households,[^cite_note-Lee_KB_p.78-27] suggesting that the total population was almost one million.[^cite_note-nfm.go.kr-31][^cite_note-Breen_82-32][^cite_note-33] Many of Gyeongju's most famous sites date from this [Unified Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Silla) period, which ended in the beginning of 10th century by [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo) (918–1392).[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25][^cite_note-EncyKorea_Silla-26]



A portrait of the last king of Silla, [King Gyeongsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongsun_of_Silla) (r. 927–935). After his surrender to [King Taejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taejo_of_Goryeo), Gyeongju lost its status as capital city.

In 940, the founder of Goryeo, [King Taejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taejo_of_Goryeo), changed the city's name to "Gyeongju",[^cite_note-Kim,_Chang-hyun_p.1-6-34] which literally means "Congratulatory district".[^cite_note-35] In 987, as Goryeo introduced a system in which three additional capitals were established in politically important provinces outside Gaegyeong (nowadays [Kaesong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaesong)), and Gyeongju was designated as "Donggyeong" ("East Capital"). However, that title was removed in 1012, the third year of [King Hyeongjong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeonjong_of_Goryeo)'s reign, due to political rivalries at that time,[^cite_note-Kim,_Chang-hyun_p.1-6-34][^cite_note-36] though Gyeongju was later made the seat of [Yeongnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongnam) Province.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25] It had jurisdiction over a wide area, including much of central eastern Yeongnam,[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25] although this area was greatly reduced in the 13th century.[^cite_note-Kim,_Chang-hyun_p.1-6-34] Under the subsequent [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) (1392–1910) dynasties, Gyeongju was no longer of national importance, but remained a regional center of influence.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25] In 1601, the city ceased to be the provincial capital.[^cite_note-Doosan_History-37]

Over these centuries, the city suffered numerous assaults. In the 13th century, [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) forces destroyed a nine-story wooden pagoda at [Hwangnyongsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwangnyongsa).[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25][^cite_note-38] During the [Japanese invasions of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasions_of_Korea_\(1592%E2%80%931598\)), the Gyeongju area became a heated battlefield,[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25] and Japanese forces burned the wooden structures at [Bulguksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulguksa).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Guardian_Cole-40] Not all damage was due to invasions, however. In the early Joseon period, a great deal of damage was done to Buddhist sculptures on [Namsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan_\(Gyeongju\)) by [Neo-Confucian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confucianism) radicals, who hacked arms and heads off [statuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue).[^cite_note-41]

In the 20th century, the city remained relatively small, no longer ranking among the major cities of Korea.[^cite_note-KBS_Kim_Dong-ni-42] During the early 20th century, many archaeological excavations were conducted, particularly inside the tombs which had remained largely intact over the centuries.[^cite_note-43] A museum, the forerunner of the present-day [Gyeongju National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_National_Museum), was inaugurated in 1915 to exhibit the excavated artifacts.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Museum-44]

Gyeongju emerged as a railroad junction in the later years of the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), as the [Donghae Nambu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghae_Nambu_Line) and [Jungang line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungang_line) were established in preparation for the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) and to exploit the rich resources of the eastern part of the Korean peninsula.[^cite_note-Donghae_Nambu_Line-45][^cite_note-Junagang_Line-46] Following liberation in 1945, Korea was plunged into turmoil, and Gyeongju was no exception. Returnees from abroad were numerous; a village for them was constructed in present-day [Dongcheon-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongcheon-dong,_Gyeongju).[^cite_note-47] In a period marked by widespread conflict and unrest, the Gyeongju area became particularly notorious for the level of [guerrilla](12720.md) activity in the mountains.[^cite_note-48]

Despite the outbreak of the [Korean War](16772.md) in 1950, most of Gyeongju was spared from the fighting, and remained under South Korean control throughout the conflict. However, for a brief time in late 1950 portions of the city stood on the front lines, as North Korean forces pushed the [Pusan Perimeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pusan_Perimeter) southward from Pohang.[^cite_note-49]

In the 1970s, Korea saw substantial industrial development, much of it centered in the Yeongnam region of which Gyeongju is a part.[^cite_note-Lee,_Sungkyun-50][^cite_note-Kim,_Won_Bae-51] The [POSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POSCO) steel mill in neighboring Pohang commenced operations in 1973,[^cite_note-52] and the chemical manufacturing complex in Ulsan emerged in the same year.[^cite_note-53] These developments helped to support the emergence of Gyeongju's manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-Industry-22]

## Geography and climate

A distant view of a wooden shrine surrounded by a thick forest on the slopes of a mountain in autumn.

[Seokguram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seokguram) grotto on the slopes of [Tohamsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohamsan)

[^cite_note-54]

Gyeongju lies in the southeastern corner of North Gyeongsang Province, and is bounded by the metropolitan city of [Ulsan](323111.md) on the south. Within the province, its neighbors include [Pohang](326500.md) on the north, [Cheongdo County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheongdo_County) on the southwest, and [Yeongcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongcheon) on the northwest.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Gyeongju_1-3] Gyeongju is located about 50 kilometers (31 mi) north of [Busan](68115.md).[^cite_note-Xinhua-4] To the east, it has no neighbor but the sea.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Gyeongju_1-3]

Most of Gyeongju lies in the [Gyeongsang Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongsang_Basin?action=edit\&redlink=1), but a few areas of the city belong to the [Pohang Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pohang_Basin?action=edit\&redlink=1), such as Eoil-ri and Beomgok-ri in [Yangbuk-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangbuk-myeon), and part of [Cheonbuk-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonbuk-myeon,_Gyeongju). The Gyeongsang Basin areas consist of Bulguksa intrusive rock penetrating layers of [sedimentary rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock), mainly [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and [porphyry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_\(geology\)). By contrast, the Pohang Basin areas are made up of [stratum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratum) that formed in the [Tertiary period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_period) of the [Cenozoic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic_era), which consists of [igneous rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock), [aqueous rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueous_rock), porphyry, [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone), and [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff).[^cite_note-Encyber_City-55]

Low mountains are widespread throughout Gyeongju. The highest of these are the [Taebaek Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taebaek_Mountains), which run along the city's western border. Gyeongju's highest point, [Munboksan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munboksan) (문복산), is 1,015 meters (3,330 ft) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). This peak lies in [Sannae-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sannae-myeon,_Gyeongju), on the border with [Cheongdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheongdo_County).[^cite_note-56] East of the Taebaek range, other western peaks such as Danseok Mountain lie within the [Jusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jusa_Mountains) subrange.[^cite_note-57] The city's eastern peaks, including [Tohamsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohamsan), belong to the Haean Mountains and [Dongdae Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongdae_Mountains).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

A river runs from south to north through the region, draining most of it. Half the tallest mountains are on the southern border; other mountains are mostly in the west, clustered in the northwest.

Principal mountains and drainage patterns of Gyeongju. Mountains of 500 to 700 m (1,600 to 2,300 ft) are in green, those taller than 700 m (2,300 ft) in violet. The rest three in gray are under 500 m (1,600 ft).

Gyeongju's drainage patterns are shaped by these lines of mountains.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Nature-8] The Dongdae Mountains divide a narrow foothills area on their east, and various internal river systems to the west. Most of the city's interior is drained by the small [Hyeongsan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeongsan_River), which flows north from Ulsan and meets the sea at Pohang Harbor. The Hyeongsan's chief tributaries include the Bukcheon and Namcheon, which join it in [Gyeongju Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Basin).[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Nature-8] The southwestern corner of Gyeongju, on the far side of the Taebaek range, drains into the [Geumho River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geumho_River), which then flows into the [Nakdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakdong_River). A small area of the south, just west of the Dongdae range, drains into the [Taehwa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taehwa_River), which flows into the Bay of Ulsan.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

The Gyeongju coastline runs for 36.1 kilometers (22.4 mi) between [Pohang](326500.md) in the north and [Ulsan](323111.md) in the south.[^cite_note-2008_Tonggye_2-62] There are no islands or large bays, only the small [indentations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcove_\(architecture\)) made by the small streams flowing off the Dongdae ridgeline. Because of this, the city has no significant ports, though there are 12 small harbors.[^cite_note-gyeongju1-63] One such harbor in Gyeongju's southeast corner is home to the Ulsan base of the National Maritime Police. This base is responsible for security over a wide area of South Korea's east-central coast.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] The coastline of Gyeongju is also notable for its well-developed marine terraces, which provide important evidence of tectonic uplift and sea-level changes during the Quaternary period. These landforms, particularly around the Yangnam area, serve as a significant geological resource for studying the paleogeography of the East Sea coast.[^cite_note-67]

### Climate

Gyeongju has a cooler version of a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*), closely bordering a hot-summer [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) [Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfa*) using the 0°C isotherm. Due to its coastal location, Gyeongju has a slightly milder climate than the more inland regions of Korea. In general, however, the city's climate is typical of South Korea. It has hot summers and cool winters, with a [monsoon](57630.md) season between late June and early August. As on the rest of Korea's east coast, autumn [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) are not uncommon. The average annual rainfall is 1,091 millimeters (43.0 in), and the average annual high temperatures range from 8.6–31.1 °C (47–88 °F).[^cite_note-68]

Gyeongju's historic city center lies on the banks of the Hyeongsan in Gyeongju Basin. This low-lying area has been subject to repeated [flooding](50482.md) throughout recorded history, often as a result of typhoons. On average, chronicles report a major flood every 27.9 years, beginning in the 1st century.[^cite_note-69] Modern flood control mechanisms brought about a dramatic reduction in flooding in the later 20th century. The last major flood occurred in 1991, when the Deokdong Lake reservoir overflowed due to [Typhoon Gladys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Pacific_typhoon_season#Typhoon_Gladys).[^cite_note-70]

## Government

In front of a low and ivory building, a parking lot and cars are shown.

Gyeongju City Hall in [Dongcheon-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongcheon-dong,_Gyeongju)

The executive branch of the government is headed by a mayor and vice-mayor. As in other South Korean cities and counties, the mayor is elected directly, while the vice-mayor is appointed.[^cite_note-73] As of 2019, the mayor is Joo Nak-young, who was elected on 13 June 2018, in the [local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_South_Korean_local_elections).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] He is Gyeongju's fifth mayor to be directly elected, the sixth to preside over the city in its present form, and the 31st mayor since 1955.[^cite_note-76] Like most heads of government in the [Yeongnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongnam) region, he is a member of the conservative [Liberty Korea Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Korea_Party).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

The legislative branch consists of the Gyeongju City Council, with 21 members as of 2009.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] The present City Council was formed from the merger of the old Gyeongju City Council with the Wolseong County Council in 1991. Most subdivisions of Gyeongju elect a single member to represent them in the council, but [Angang-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angang-eup) is represented by two members because of its large population, and two of the representatives serve combined districts composed of two *dong*. Like the mayor, the council members were last elected in 2006, except for a small number elected in more recent [by-elections](155434.md).

The central administration is composed of a City Council committee, five departments, two subsidiary organs, a chamber (the auditor), and six business offices. The five departments are the Departments of Planning and Culture, Autonomous Administration, Industry and Environment, Construction and Public Works, and the National Enterprise Committee; these oversee a total of 29 subdivisions. The two subsidiary organs are the Health Care Center and Agro-technology Center; these belong directly to the central administration and have a total of 4 subdivisions. In addition, there are 23 local administrative subdivisions. Each of these subdivisions has a local office with a small administrative staff.[^cite_note-82] As of December 2008, the city government employed 1,462 people.[^cite_note-83]

## Subdivisions

Main article: [Subdivisions of Gyeongju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Gyeongju)

The city is divided into 23 administrative districts: 4 *[eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eup_\(administrative_division\))*, 8 *[myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myeon_\(administrative_division\))*, and 11 *[dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_\(administrative_division\))*.[^cite_note-Status_quo-84][^cite_note-85] These are the standard [subdivisions of cities and counties in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_subdivisions_of_South_Korea). The *dong* or neighborhood units occupy the area of the city center, which was formerly occupied by Gyeongju-eup. *Eup* are typically substantial villages, whereas *myeon* are more rural.[^cite_note-2008_Tonggye_2-62][^cite_note-86]

The city's boundaries and designation changed several times in the 20th century. From 1895 to 1955, the area was known as Gyeongju-gun ("Gyeongju County"). In the first decades of the century, the city center was known as Gyeongju-myeon, signifying a relatively rural rea. In 1931, the downtown area was designated Gyeongju-eup, in recognition of its increasingly urban nature. In 1955, Gyeongju-eup became Gyeongju-si ("Gyeongju City"), the same name as today, but with a much smaller area. The remainder of Gyeongju-gun became "Wolseong County." The county and city were reunited in 1995, creating Gyeongju City as it is today.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_History-25]

## Demographics

When the Silla kingdom reached the peak of its development, Gyeongju was estimated to have a million residents, four times the city's population in 2008.[^cite_note-EncyKorea_Gyeongju_1-3][^cite_note-Breen_82-32] In recent years, Gyeongju has followed the same trends that have affected the rest of South Korea. Like the country as a whole, Gyeongju has seen its population age and the size of families shrink. For instance, the mean household size is 2.8 people. Because this has fallen in recent years, there are more households in the city as of 2008 (105,009) than there were in 2003, even though the population has fallen.[^cite_note-gyeongju2-87]

Like most of South Korea's smaller cities, Gyeongju has seen a steady drop in population in recent years. From 2002 to 2008, the city lost 16,557 people.[^cite_note-status_quo-88] This is primarily due to the migration of workers seeking employment in the major South Korean cities. In 2007, about 1,975 more people moved away from the city each year than moved in.[^cite_note-89] During the same period, births exceeded deaths by roughly 450 per year, a significant number but not enough to offset the losses due to migration.[^cite_note-90]

Gyeongju has a small but growing population of non-Koreans. In 2007, there were 4,671 foreigners living in the city. This number corresponds to 1.73% of the total population, more than double the figure from 2003. The growth was largely in immigrants from other Asian countries, many of whom were employed in the automotive parts industry. Countries of origin whose numbers have risen include the [Philippines](23440.md), China, [Taiwan](25734.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Vietnam](202354.md). The number of residents from Japan, the United States, and Canada fell significantly in the 2003–2007 period.[^cite_note-91]

### Dialect

The city has a distinctive [dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect) which it shares with northern portions of [Ulsan](323111.md). This dialect is similar to the general [Gyeongsang dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongsang_dialect), but retains distinctive features of its own. Some linguists have treated the distinctive characteristics of the Gyeongju dialect as vestiges of the [Silla language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla_language). For instance, the contrast between the local dialect form "소내기" (*sonaegi*) and the standard "[소나기](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%EC%86%8C%EB%82%98%EA%B8%B0)" (*sonagi,* meaning "rainshower") has been seen as reflecting the ancient [phonemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoneme) character of the Silla language.[^cite_note-92]

## Culture and people

### Cultural properties



[A gold crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Silla) excavated from Gold Crown Tomb. National Treasures of South Korea No. 87.



Divine [Bell of King Seongdeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_of_King_Seongdeok)

Gyeongju is the main destination in South Korea for visitors interested in the cultural heritage of Silla and the architecture of Joseon. The city has 31 [National Treasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Treasure_\(South_Korea\)), and [Gyeongju National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_National_Museum) houses 16,333 artifacts.[^cite_note-Brit_Edu-93] There are four broad categories of relics and historical sites: [tumuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus) and their artifacts; Buddhist sites and objects; fortresses and palace sites; and ancient architecture. [Prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Korea) remains including [Mumun pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumun_pottery_period) have been excavated in central Gyeongju, in the Moa-ri and Oya-ri villages of the [Cheonbuk-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonbuk-myeon,_Gyeongju) district, and in the Jukdong-ri village of the [Oedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oedong) district. [Dolmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmen) are found in several places, especially in [Gangdong-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangdong-myeon,_Gyeongju) and Moa-ri. [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) relics found in Angye-ri village of Gangdong-myeon, Jukdong-ri and Ipsil-ri villages of Oedong-eup and graveyards in the Joyang-dong district represent the [Samhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhan) confederacy period of around the 1st century BC to the 3rd century AD.[^cite_note-relics-94]

There are 35 royal tombs and 155 tumuli in central Gyeongju, and 421 tumuli in the outskirts of the city.[^cite_note-95] Silla burial mounds built after the period of the Three Kingdoms are found in central Gyeongju, including tumuli in the districts of Noseo-dong, Nodong-dong, Hwangnam-dong, Hwango-dong and Inwang-dong. Western Gyeongju has the tomb of [King Muyeol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muyeol_of_Silla) in Seoak-dong, nearby tumuli in Chunghyo-dong and the tomb of [Kim Yu-sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yu-sin). The tombs of [Queen Seondeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Seondeok_of_Silla), [King Sinmun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinmun_of_Silla), [King Hyogong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyogong_of_Silla) and [King Sinmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinmu_of_Silla) are at the base of [Namsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan_\(Gyeongju\)) mountain while the tombs of [King Heongang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heongang_of_Silla), [King Jeonggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeonggang_of_Silla), [King Gyeongmyeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongmyeong_of_Silla) and [King Gyeongae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongae_of_Silla) are on the slopes of the mountain. In addition to the tombs, tumuli have been found surrounding Namsan mountain and in the western part of Geumgang mountain. Artifacts excavated from the tombs of [Geumgwanchong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geumgwanchong) (gold crown tomb), [Seobongchong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seobongchong?action=edit\&redlink=1) (western phoenix tomb), [Cheonmachong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonmachong) (heavenly horse tomb) and northern and southern parts of Tomb No. 98 are good examples of Silla culture.[^cite_note-relics-94]

### Notable people

See also: [Category\:People from Gyeongju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:People_from_Gyeongju)

Yi Che-hyŏn (1287–1367), an early Korean Neo-Confucian scholar, whose bongwan was Gyeongju.

[Yi Che-hyŏn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Che-hy%C5%8Fn) (1287–1367)

Gyeongju has produced notable individuals throughout its history. As the capital of Silla, Gyeongju was a center of culture in its heyday.[^cite_note-Breen_82-32] Notable Gyeongju residents in the Silla period included most of the kingdom's leading figures, not only rulers but scholars such as [Seol Chong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seol_Chong) and [Ch'oe Ch'i-wŏn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'oe_Ch'i-w%C5%8Fn),[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] and generals like [Kim Yu-sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yu-sin), the leader of the [Hwarang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwarang) warriors.[^cite_note-99] The city continued to contribute to [traditional Korean thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_philosophy) in subsequent dynasties. Relatives of Ch'oe Ch'i-wŏn such as [Ch'oe Ŏn-wi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'oe_%C5%8En-wi) and [Ch'oe Hang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'oe_Hang_\(Goryeo_civil_minister\)) played an important role in establishing the structures of early Goryeo.[^cite_note-Kim,_Chang-hyun_p.1-6-34][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] In the Joseon period, Gyeongju joined the rest of Gyeongsang in becoming a hotbed of the conservative *[Sarim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarim)* faction. Notable Gyeongju members of this faction included the 15th-century intellectual [Yi Ŏnjŏk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_%C5%8Enj%C5%8Fk). He has been enshrined in the [Oksan Seowon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oksan_Seowon,_Gyeongju) since 1572.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-Angang-104] In modern times, the city produced writers such as [Kim Dongni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Dongni) and [Pak Mok-wol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pak_Mok-wol), both of whom did a great deal to popularize the region's culture,[^cite_note-KBS_Kim_Dong-ni-42][^cite_note-Kim_&_Fulton-105][^cite_note-106] as well as [Choe Jun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choe_Jun), a wealthy businessman who established the [Yeungnam University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeungnam_University) Foundation.[^cite_note-107]

Some Korean family clans trace their origins to Gyeongju, often to the ruling elites of Silla. For example, the [Gyeongju Kim clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Kim_clan) claims descent from the rulers of later Silla.[^cite_note-108] The [Gyeongju Park clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Park_clan?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Gyeongju Seok clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Seok_clans?action=edit\&redlink=1) trace their ancestry to Silla's earlier ruling families. These three royal clans played a strong role in preserving the historical precincts of Gyeongju into modern times.[^cite_note-109] The [Gyeongju Choi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Choi_clan) and [Gyeongju Lee clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Lee?action=edit\&redlink=1) also trace their ancestry to the Silla elites. Prominent members of the Gyeongju Lee clan include Goryeo period scholar [Yi Che-hyŏn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Che-hy%C5%8Fn), and Joseon period scholars [Yi Hwang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Hwang) and [Yi Hang-bok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Hang-bok?action=edit\&redlink=1). A contemporary notable figure from the Gyeongju Lee clan is [Lee Byung-chul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Byung-chul), the founder of [Samsung Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Group).[^cite_note-110] However, not all Gyeongju clans date to the Silla period; for instance, the Gyeongju Bing clan was founded in the early Joseon period.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

### Religion

The city remains an important centre of [Korean Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Buddhism). East of the downtown area lies [Bulguksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulguksa), one of South Korea's largest Buddhist temples; nearby is [Seokguram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seokguram), a famed Buddhist shrine. Traditional prayer locations are found on mountains throughout Gyeongju. Such mountains include [Namsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan_\(Gyeongju\)) near the city center,[^cite_note-113] Danseok-san and Obong-san in the west, and the low peak of Hyeong-san on the Gyeongju-Pohang border.[^cite_note-114] Namsan in particular is often referred to as "the sacred mountain" due to the Buddhist shrines and statues which cover its slopes.[^cite_note-115] In addition, Gyeongju is the birthplace of [Cheondoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheondoism), an indigenous religion to Korea based on [Korean shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_shamanism), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) and Korean Buddhism, with elements drawn from [Christianity](5211.md). The religion evolved from [Donghak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghak) (lit. East learning) disciplines established by [Choe Je-u](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choe_Je-u). His birthplace of [Yongdamjeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongdamjeong), located in [Hyeongok-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeongok-myeon), is regarded as a sacred place to followers of Cheondogyo.[^cite_note-Angang-104][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

### Cuisine

See also: [Korean cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_cuisine)

24 buns in golden brown are put in a white rectangular box. The buns are arranged like an abacus.

[Gyeongju bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_bread), a local speciality

The cuisine of Gyeongju is generally similar to other areas of [Gyeongsang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongsang_Province): spicy and salty.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-KTO_Local_foods-119][^cite_note-Daegu_Bank-120] However, it has distinctive tastes according to region and several local specialties known nationwide.[^cite_note-Daegu_Bank-120] The most famous of these is "[Gyeongju bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_bread)" or "Hwangnam bread", a red-bean pastry first baked in 1939 and now sold throughout the country.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] *[Chalboribbang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalboribbang)*, made with locally produced [glutinous barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutinous_barley), is also a pastry with a filling of [red bean paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_bean_paste).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-idaegu_2007-124] Local specialties with a somewhat longer pedigree include *[beopju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beopju)*, a traditional Korean liquor produced by the Gyeongju Choe in [Gyo-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyo-dong,_Gyeongju). The brewing skill and distill master were designated as [Important Intangible Cultural Properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Intangible_Cultural_Properties_of_Korea) by South Korea government.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

An array of about 10 small side dishes, a bean curd stew, and leaf vegetables on a table.

*[Ssambap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ssambap)*, a rice dish served with vegetable leaves, various small side dishes and condiments

Other local specialties include *[ssambap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ssambap)*, *[haejangguk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haejangguk)*, and *[muk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muk_\(food\))*.[^cite_note-128] *Ssambap* refers to a rice dish served with vegetable leaves, various *[banchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banchan)* (small side dishes) and condiments such as *[gochujang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gochujang)* (chili pepper paste) or *[ssamjang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ssamjang)* (a mixture of [soybean paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doenjang) and *gochujang*) to wrap them together. Most *ssambap* restaurants in Gyeongju are gathered in the area of Daenuengwon or Grand Tumuli Park.[^cite_note-129] *Haejangguk* is a kind of [soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guk) eaten as a [hangover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangover) cure, and means "soup to chase a hangover".[^cite_note-Dong-a-130] A street dedicated to *haejangguk* is located near [Gyeongju National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_National_Museum), where 20 *haejangguk* restaurants are gathered to serve the Gyeongju-style *haejangguk*. The soup is made by boiling [soybean sprout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongnamul), sliced *[memilmuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memilmuk)* (buckwheat starch jelly), sour [kimchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimchi) (pickled vegetables) and [gulfweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfweed) in a clear broth of dried anchovy and [Alaska pollack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_pollack).[^cite_note-131]

The east district of Gyeongju, [Gampo-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gampo-eup) town, is adjacent to the sea, so fresh seafood and *[jeotgal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeotgal)* (fermented salted seafood) are abundant. There are over 240 seafood restaurants in Gampo Harbor offering various dishes made with seafood caught in the sea, such as *[hoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoe_\(dish\))* (raw fish dishes), *jeonboktang* (an abalone soup), grilled seafood and others.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

## Sports

People cheering their teams with colorful flags for track and field games in a stadium

2008 Gyeongju Citizens' Athletics Festival held at Gyeongju Public Stadium

As of 2007, Gyeongju had two stadiums, two gymnasiums, two tennis courts, one swimming pool and others as public sport facilities as well as various registered private sports venues.[^cite_note-Sports_facilities-135][^cite_note-문화_Culture-136] Many of public sport facilities are located in [Hwangseong Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwangseong_Park) with an area of 1,022,350 m<sup>2</sup> (11,004,500 sq ft) including a luxuriant pine trees forest.[^cite_note-tour.go.kr-137] The site was originally the location of the artificial forest of Doksan which was established for *[feng shui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_shui)* purposes during the [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla) period. It was also used as a training ground for [hwarang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwarang) warriors and hunting spot for Silla kings, and was reported to be [King Jinpyeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinpyeong_of_Silla)'s favorite location.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] In 1975, Hwangseong Park was designated a "city neighborhood park" and it currently consists of the multi-purpose Gyeongju Public Stadium, Football Park (with seven football fields and one futsal field), and one [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gym), as well as Horimjang field for *[gukgung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gukgung)* or Korean traditional [archery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archery) and a [ssireum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ssireum) wrestling ring.[^cite_note-140] In addition, it contains a gateball field, an inline skating rink, jogging courses, and cycling roads.[^cite_note-Doosan-141] The Gyeongju Public Stadium was completed in 1982[^cite_note-Sports_facilities-135] and can accommodate 20,000 people at capacity.[^cite_note-tour.go.kr-137]

In 2013, [Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Korea_Hydro_&_Nuclear_Power_FC) relocated from Daejeon to Gyeongju, playing home matches at [Gyeongju Civic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Civic_Stadium).[^cite_note-142] In 2017, the club established a [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Korea_Hydro_&_Nuclear_Power_WFC) to compete in the [WK League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WK_League), using Pitch 3 of Gyeongju Football Park as their home ground.[^cite_note-143]

Angang Field Hockey Stadium, located in the district of [Angang-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angang-eup), is home to Gyeongju City Hockey, which is one of four professional women's [field hockey](10886.md) teams in South Korea.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-Allstar-145] The team was formed in 1994,[^cite_note-Pohang_KBS-146] and is governed by the Sport and Youth Division of Gyeongju City.[^cite_note-shock-147] Although not an initial successful team, Gyeongju City Hockey won the first trophies both at National Division Hockey Championships and National Sports Festival in 2000. In 2002, Gyeongju City Hockey took first prize and three second prizes,[^cite_note-Pohang_KBS-146] and in 2008, the team won the first prize at the 51st National Division Hockey Championships.[^cite_note-148]

The city plays host to two annual [marathon](49132.md) events. The [Gyeongju International Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_International_Marathon), held in October, garners elite level competition while the larger Gyeongju Cherry Blossom Marathon caters more for amateur [fun runners](8836052.md). The Cherry Blossom Marathon has been held each year in Gyeongju since 1992, usually in April, to improve relations with [Japan](15573.md) (a country with a long history of marathon running).[^cite_note-149] The race, mainly sponsored by Gyeongju and the district, attracted 13,600 participants in 2009 including about 1,600 foreigners.[^cite_note-150]

## Economy

See also: [Economy of South Korea](27024.md)

Two boats anchor in a harbor on the right. Blue skies and the sea are clear and tranquil.

Gampo Port

The economy of Gyeongju is diverse.[^cite_note-Industry-22][^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23] Although tourism is important to the economy, most residents work in other fields. Over 27,000 are employed in manufacturing compared to roughly 13,500 in the hospitality industry. The number involved in tourism has remained constant over recent years, while the manufacturing sector added about 6,000 jobs from 1999 to 2003.[^cite_note-151] The manufacturing sector is closely tied to nearby cities, utilizing Gyeongju's transit links with [Ulsan](323111.md), [Pohang](326500.md), and [Daegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu).[^cite_note-Industry-22][^cite_note-Tamásy-24][^cite_note-152] As in Ulsan and Daegu the automotive parts industry plays an important role.[^cite_note-Lee,_Sungkyun-50] Of the 1,221 businesses incorporated in Gyeongju almost a third are involved in auto-parts manufacture.[^cite_note-153]

Fishing takes place in coastal towns, especially in [Gampo-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gampo-eup) in the city's northeast, with 436 registered fishing craft in the city.[^cite_note-gyeongju1-63] Fishing industry in Gyeongju is generally in a declined status due to relatively inconvenient transport conditions and lacks of subordinate facilities.[^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23] Much of the catch from these boats goes direct from the harbor to Gyeongju's many seafood restaurants. Mainly, [sauries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauries), anchovies and [rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batoidea) are harvested and a small number of [abalone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abalone) and [wakame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakame) farming takes place. Local specialties include *[myeolchijeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myeolchijeot)* (fermented anchovy), abalone, wakame, and squid.[^cite_note-Eup_Myeon-154]

Yellow paddy fields and green hills during autumn

Paddy fields in Gyeongju

Agriculture is still important, particularly in the outlying regions of Gyeongju. According to the 2006 statistical yearbook of Gyeongju, rice fields occupy an area of 169.57 km<sup>2</sup> (65.47 sq mi), which is 70% of the total cultivated acreage of 24,359 km<sup>2</sup> (9,405 sq mi). The remaining 74.02 km<sup>2</sup> (28.58 sq mi) consists of fields under other crops and farmsteads. Crop production is centered in the fertile river basins near the [Hyeongsan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeongsan_River). The main crops are rice, barley, beans and corn. Vegetables such as [radish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radish) and [napa cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napa_cabbage) and fruits are also important crops. Apples are mainly produced in the districts of [Geoncheon-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoncheon-eup), [Gangdong-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangdong-myeon,_Gyeongju) and [Cheonbuk-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonbuk-myeon,_Gyeongju) and [Korean pear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_pear) are cultivated in Geoncheon-eup and [Angang-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angang-eup). The city plays a leading role in the domestic production of [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef) and [mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom). [Button mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Button_mushroom) harvested in Geoncheon-eup are canned and exported.[^cite_note-Industry-22] The cultivated acreage and the number of households engaging in agriculture is however declining.[^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23]

A small amount of [quarrying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarry) activity takes place in the city, with 46 active mines and quarries in Gyeongju. Most are engaged in the extraction of [kaolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaolin), [fluorspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorspar) and [Agalmatolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agalmatolite)[^cite_note-155] and Kaolin is exported.[^cite_note-Doosan_Industry-156]

A female customer browsing a fruit shop. Banana and grapes are displayed on the front.

A fruit shop at [Seongdong Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongdong_Market)

As the capital of Silla, commerce and trading in Gyeongju developed early on. *[Samguk sagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samguk_sagi)* has records on the establishment of *Gyeongdosi* (capital area market) in March, 490 during [King Soji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soji_of_Silla)'s reign, and *Dongsi* (East Market) in 509, during [King Jijeung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jijeung_of_Silla)'s reign. In the 1830s, Gyeongju had five five-day markets which remained very active until the late 1920s. Due to its size *Gyeongju Bunaejang* (Gyeongju village market) was referred to as one of the two leading markets in the [Yeongnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongnam) area, along with *[Daegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu) Bunaejang*. Transportation developed in the late period of the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), as the [Jungang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungang_Line) and the [Daegu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu_Line) and the connecting route between [Pohang](326500.md) and the northwestern part of Japan were set up, leading to increasing population and developing commerce. After the 1960s, traditional periodic markets gradually transformed into regular markets as the city was flourishing. In periodic markets, agricultural and marine products, industrial products, living necessaries, wild edible greens, herbs, and cattle are mainly traded. As of 2006, Gyeongju had eight regular markets, nine periodic markets and the Gyeongju department store. Traditional periodic markets declined and have become token affairs these days.[^cite_note-Industry-22][^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23]

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Gyeongju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Gyeongju)

A stone pagoda with elaborated tiers, a small lion status, and stairs. Blue skies and a roof of a building and trees are shown on the background

[Dabotap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabotap) pagoda at [Bulguksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulguksa) temple

Gyeongju is a major [tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist) destination for South Koreans as well as foreign visitors. It boasts 1000 years of Silla heritage with vast number of ancient ruins and archaeological sites found throughout the city,[^cite_note-Robinson_et_al._2007._p.28-29] which help to attract 6 million visiting tourists including 750,000 foreigners per year.[^cite_note-Brisbane-6] The city government has parlayed its historic status into a basis for other tourism-related developments such as conferences, festivals, and resorts.[^cite_note-Tourism-157]

Many Silla sites are located in [Gyeongju National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_National_Park) such as the Royal Tomb Complex, the [Cheomseongdae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheomseongdae) observatory that is one of the oldest surviving [astronomical observatories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_observatories) in [East Asia](19605700.md),[^cite_note-158] the [Anapji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapji) royal pond garden,[^cite_note-Tourism-157] and the [Gyerim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyerim) forest.[^cite_note-159] [Gyeongju National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_National_Museum) hosts many important artifacts and national treasures that have been excavated from sites within the city and surrounding areas.[^cite_note-Tourism-157]

A huge three-story pagoda stands against blue skies. The pagoda is made with bricks of dark gray stone. Colorful lanterns are lined up.

[Bunhwangsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunhwangsa) pagoda, [National Treasure of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Treasures_of_South_Korea) No. 30

Much of Gyeongju's heritage are related to the Silla kingdom's patronage of [Buddhism](3267529.md). The grotto of [Seokguram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seokguram) and the temple of [Bulguksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulguksa) were the first Korean sites to be included on the UNESCO [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_List) in 1995.[^cite_note-Tourism-157][^cite_note-160] In addition, the ruins of the old [Hwangnyongsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwangnyongsa) temple, said to have been Korea's largest, are preserved on the slopes of [Tohamsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohamsan). Various Silla-era stone carvings of Buddhas and [Bodhisattvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva) are found on mountainsides throughout the city, particularly on [Namsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan_\(Gyeongju\)).[^cite_note-Tourism-157]

A significant portion of Gyeongju's tourist traffic is due to the city's promotion of itself as a site for various festivals, conferences, and competitions. Every year since 1962, the Silla cultural festival has been held in October to celebrate and honour the dynasty's history and culture. It is one of the major festivals of Korea.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] It features athletic events, folk games, music, dance, literary contests and Buddhist religious ceremonies. Other festivals include the Cherry Blossom Marathon in April,[^cite_note-164] the Korean Traditional Liquor and Cake festival in March,[^cite_note-Travel_Blackboard-16] and memorial ceremonies for the founders of the Silla Dynasty and General [Kim Yu-sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yu-sin).[^cite_note-문화_Culture-136]

There were 15 hotels including [Hilton Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Hotel), Gyeongju Chosun Hotel, and 276 lodging facilities, and 2,817 restaurants in Gyeongju in 2006.[^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23]

Gyeongju's emerging tourist attraction is the shopping street [Hwangnidan-gil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwangnidan-gil). The address of Hwangnidan-gil is 1080, Poseok-ro, Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province.[^cite_note-\:0-165] There are about 400 stores,[^cite_note-\:1-166] including restaurants, cafes, bookstores, and gift shops. Hwangnidan-gil became popular through social networking sites, and neighboring Gyeongju's historical site is designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The advantage of the Hwangnidan-gil is the result of voluntary efforts by merchants without help from local governments.[^cite_note-\:0-165]

## Media

Gyeongju has two main local newspapers; the *[Gyeongju Sinmun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Sinmun)* and the *Seorabeol Sinmun*.[^cite_note-168] Both are weekly newspapers providing news via online as well and their headquarters are located in the neighborhood of [Dongcheon-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongcheon-dong,_Gyeongju).[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] The Gyeongju Sinmun was founded in 1989 and provides various news and critics on anything concerning Gyeongju.[^cite_note-171] Its online newspaper, Digital Gyeongju Sinmun opened in December 2000 to provide live local news out of the limit as a weekly newspaper and to establish mutual information exchanges from Gyeongju locals. In 2001, Gyeongju Sinmun started to present Gyeongju Citizen Awards to people who try to develop the local industry and economy, culture and education, and welfare service. The *Seorabeol Sinmun* was established in 1993,[^cite_note-gynews-173] however, from 15 November 2000, to 10 November 2005, its publication was stopped for financial difficulties after the 1997 Asian economic crisis had left a strong impact on the nationwide economy.[^cite_note-174] Since 2006, Seorabeol Sinmun presents Serabeol Awards to people having devouring to develop Gyeongju.[^cite_note-gynews-173][^cite_note-175]

In 2009, the filming of the *[Queen Seondeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Seondeok_\(TV_series\))*, a popular [MBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munhwa_Broadcasting_Corporation) TV series took place in a studio at [Silla Millennium Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla_Millennium_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) located in [Bomun Lake Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomun_Lake_Resort).[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

## Education

See also: [Education in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_South_Korea)

Gyeongju is strongly associated with the education tradition of [Hwarangdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwarangdo) ("Way of the Flower of Young Men") which was established and flourished during the Silla period. It is a military and philosophical code that offered the basis of training to [Hwarang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwarang), a military cadet of youths from the aristocratic class. The training equally emphasized practicing academic and martial arts based on Buddhism and patriotism. A number of Silla's greatest generals and military leaders such as [Kim Yu-sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yu-sin) were Hwarang who played a central role in Silla unification of the Korean peninsula. As Silla was integrated into the next ruling dynasty, [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo), the system declined and was officially disbanded in the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) period. However, the spirit and discipline were revived in the second half of the 20th century as a form of Korean martial arts with the same name.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

On a sunny day, a Korean traditional wooden building painted with white and dark red stands on a grass field. Luxuriant trees are seen on the right while a gate is shown at a distance.

A building of the [Gyeongju Hyanggyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Hyanggyo)

Formal education has a longer history in Gyeongju than anywhere else in South Korea. The *[Gukhak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gukhak)*, or national academy, was established here in 682, at the beginning of the [Unified Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Silla) period.[^cite_note-Gyeongju_City-184] Its curriculum focused on the Confucian classics for local officials.[^cite_note-Robinson_et_al._2007._p.28-29] After the fall of [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla) in the 10th century, the [Gukhak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gukhak) closed. However, due to Gyeongju's role as a provincial center under the [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo) and early [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon_Dynasty) dynasties, the city was home to state-sponsored provincial schools (*[hyanggyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyanggyo)*) under both dynasties such as [Gyeongju Hyanggyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_Hyanggyo). During the later Joseon dynasty there were several [seowon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seowon), or private Confucian academies, were set up in the city such as [Oksan Seowon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oksan_Seowon,_Gyeongju) and [Seoak Seowon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoak_Seowon).[^cite_note-Education-185]

The education system of Gyeongju is the same as elsewhere in the country. Schooling begins with [preschools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool); there are 65 in the city. This is followed by six years in [elementary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_school); Gyeongju has 46. Subsequently, students pass through three years of [middle school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school). There are 19 middle schools in Gyeongju. High school education, which lasts for three years, is not [compulsory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education), but most students attend and graduate from high school. Gyeongju is home to 21 high schools,[^cite_note-Education-185] of which 11 provide specialized technical training. At each of these levels, there is a mix of public and private institutions. All are overseen by the Gyeongju bureau of North Gyeongsang's Provincial Office of Education.[^cite_note-186] Gyeongju is home to a school for the [mentally disabled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentally_disabled), which provides education to students from preschool to adult age.[^cite_note-Brit_Edu-93]

A night scene of a building complex.

Campus of [Dongguk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguk_University) in Gyeongju at night

Gyeongju is home to four institutions of [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education).[^cite_note-Brit_Edu-93] [Sorabol College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorabol_College?action=edit\&redlink=1) is a technical college in the district of Chunghyo-dong that offers majors specializing in tourism, leisure, health care and cosmetic treatments.[^cite_note-187]

Each of Gyeongju's three universities reflects the city's unique role. [Dongguk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguk_University) and [Uiduk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uiduk_University) universities are Buddhist institutions,[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189] reflecting that religion's link to the city.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191] [Gyeongju University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_University), formerly Korea Tourism University, is strongly focused on tourism, reflecting its importance in the region.[^cite_note-192]

## Infrastructure

### Healthcare

A large seven-story hospital complex on a slope that consists of about two buildings. The wall of one on the left is covered with blue glasses, and the other building with round corners is covered with beige bricks. Large green vertical signs are attached on the wall of the latter. The signs say "동국대학교 부속병원 한방병원". In front of the hospital, a black car coming down from the slope.

Dongguk University Gyeongju Hospital

According to the 2008 yearbook of Gyeongju, the total number of medical institutions was 224 with 3,345 beds, including two general hospitals, thirteen hospitals, 109 clinics, five nursing homes, forty-two dental hospitals, two [Korean traditional medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_traditional_medicine) hospitals and 50 Korean traditional medicine clinics.[^cite_note-193] There are also twenty-eight medical institutions related to Gyeongju Health Center affiliated to the Gyeongju City government.[^cite_note-Brit_Edu-93]

The two general hospitals are associated with two major universities in Gyeongju and nearby [Daegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu). One is the [Dongguk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguk_University) Gyeongju Hospital, located in the district of Seokjang-dong, which is affiliated with Dongguk University Medical School and Center. The Gyeongju Hospital was opened in a seven-story building in 1991 to provide Gyeongju locals with a quality medical service and train medical specialists in the region.[^cite_note-194] After various renovations the hospital currently has 24 departments including a [radiation](25856.md) [oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology) center and 438 beds.[^cite_note-195] It is also assigned as a teaching and learning hospital and in partnership with Dongguk University Oriental Hospital.[^cite_note-196] The other general hospital is a branch of [Keimyung University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keimyung_University), Dongsan Medical Hospital in Daegu. It is the successor of Gyeongju Christianity Hospital founded in 1962, and was reborn as the current general hospital in 1991. The Gyeongju Dongsan Hospital is located in the district of Seobu-dong and has 12 departments in a three-story building.[^cite_note-197]

### Utilities

A vast and tranquil river in a clear day of fall. Apartment blocks and buildings under construction are seen at a distance.

A view of [Hyeongsan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeongsan_River) from Dong Bridge. The river is one of water sources of Gyeongju.

Water supply and sewage disposal are municipal services which are respectively handled by the Water Supply Office and Water Quality and Environment Office. Water comes from the [Hyeongsan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeongsan_River), the multi-purpose [Deokdong Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deokdong_Dam?action=edit\&redlink=1) and several streams. The city is divided into seven water districts, with eight filtration plants and seven sewage treatment plants.[^cite_note-198] One of the sewage treatment plants, Angang Sewage Disposal Plant began operating in April 2005 by the co-investment of the governments of North Gyeongsang and Gyeongju with a fund of 44,300,000,000 [won](298884.md) to install facilities to prevent the pollution of the [Hyeongsan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeongsan_River), which is a main water source for Gyeongju and [Pohang](326500.md) residents. The plant is located on a spacious site with 39,000 m<sup>2</sup> (420,000 sq ft) in Homyeong-ri, [Gangdong-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangdong-myeon,_Gyeongju) in Gyeongju where nature friendly facilities provide recreational venues for the locals. Through 56.1 km (34.9 mi) of sewer pipes and 14 [pumping stations](851965.md), the plant has a capacity of 18,000 tonnes of [domestic sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_sewage) per day that comes from [Angang-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angang-eup), and [Gangdong-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangdong-myeon,_Gyeongju). The facilities have high-powered disposal equipment developed by related industrial companies to maintain the discharged water at the first or second degree in quality, so that it is used as [river maintenance flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_maintenance_flow) and [agricultural water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_water) in case a [drought](37335.md) occurs.[^cite_note-199]

The city had managed its own recycling service, but privatized it since 1 July 2009.[^cite_note-200]

Other utilities are provided by private entities or South Korean government-owned companies. Seorabeol City Gas, an affiliate of [GS Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GS_Group), provides gas to the Gyeongju residents,[^cite_note-201] while, electrical power is supplied by the public enterprises, [Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Hydro_&_Nuclear_Power) via the [Wolseong Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolseong_Nuclear_Power_Plant). The plant is known for the only nuclear power plant operating [PHWRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHWR) (Pressurized Heavy Water Reactor) in South Korea[^cite_note-Industry-22] and supplies about 5% of South Korea's electricity.[^cite_note-202] The owner, Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power[^cite_note-Star-203] began to build the Wolseong 1 in the districts of [Yangnam-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangnam-myeon), [Yangbuk-myeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangbuk-myeon) and [Gampo-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gampo-eup) in 1976. Since 1983, the power plant has been providing commercial service[^cite_note-Star-203] and operating with the PHWRs that has a capacity of 678,000 kW. As the construction of each Wolseong 2, 3 and 4 with a capacity of 70,000 kW were completed respectively in 1997, 1998 and 1999, Wolseong Nuclear Power plant site has been successfully operating the four PHWRs plants.[^cite_note-Industry-22] New project, Sinwolseong No. 1 and No. 2 are currently under construction which is estimated to be completed until 2011–12.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205] The [Wolseong Low and Intermediate Level Radioactive Waste Disposal Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolseong_Low_and_Intermediate_Level_Radioactive_Waste_Disposal_Center), which treats and stores low and intermediate level [radioactive waste](37257.md) from the local power plants,[^cite_note-Park,_T;_Choi,_J-206] is overseen and inspected by the Korea Institute of Nuclear Safety (KINS).[^cite_note-FNCA-207]

### Transportation

A front view of a one-story building with a Korean traditional roof.

Gyeongju train station

The city lies at the junction of two minor lines operated by the [Korean National Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_National_Railroad). The [Jungang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungang_Line) runs from Seoul to Gyeongju and carries trains from the [Daegu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu_Line), which originates in [Dongdaegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongdaegu_Station).[^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23] In Gyeongju, the Jungang line connects to the [Donghae Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghae_Line) which runs between [Busan](68115.md) and [Yeongdeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongdeok_County).[^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23] The [Gyeongbu Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_Expressway), which runs from Seoul to Busan, passes through Gyeongju,[^cite_note-Brit_Transport-23] and Provincial Highway 68, aided by the South Korean government, connects [Seocheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seocheon) in [South Chungcheong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Chungcheong_Province) to Gyeongju.[^cite_note-208] Additionally national highways such as Route 4,[^cite_note-209] 7,[^cite_note-210] 14,[^cite_note-211] 20,[^cite_note-212] 28,[^cite_note-213] 31,[^cite_note-214] and 35[^cite_note-215] crisscross the city. Since the city is a popular tourist destination, nonstop bus services are available from most major cities in South Korea.[^cite_note-216]

[High-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) does not serve central Gyeongju, but the [KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Train_Express) [Gyeongbu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_Line) stops at the nearby [Gyeongju station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongju_station), in [Geoncheon-eup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoncheon-eup), west of Gyeongju's city center.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]
