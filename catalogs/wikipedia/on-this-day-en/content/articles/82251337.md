Beginning in late January 2026, the [United States](3434750.md) carried out its largest military buildup in the [Middle East](19323.md) since the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md), deploying air, naval, and missile defense assets amid [escalating tensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93United_States_relations_during_the_second_Trump_administration) with [Iran](14653.md), which the United States has attributed to [Iran's nuclear program](721807.md), the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md), and a [government crackdown that killed thousands of demonstrators](82036668.md).[^cite_note-Cancian-1][^cite_note-2] On 28 February 2026, this culminated in a series of joint military strikes in Iran by the United States and [Israel](9282173.md), sparking the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Trump held off from immediate military intervention for the protesters, reportedly due to pressure from Israel and the [Gulf Arab states](3088555.md) and as an apparent reaction to Iranian officials holding off on carrying out executions of prisoners arrested after protests.[^cite_note-6] The planning for a proposed series of attacks also started in co-ordination with Israeli intelligence agencies.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera_English-2026a-7] In late January, Gulf states reportedly blocked military base and airspace access to the US over fears of Iranian retaliation.[^cite_note-Ward-8][^cite_note-Heinrich-9]

Assets deployed included the [US Navy](20518076.md)'s [Carrier Strike Group 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_3) and [Carrier Strike Group 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_12), led by [USS *Abraham Lincoln*](199106.md) and [USS *Gerald R. Ford*](4050542.md) respectively. These groups support cruise missiles, [F-35C Lightning II](11812.md) and [F/A-18E Super Hornet](845012.md) fighters, and other aircraft. The [US Air Force](32090.md) deployed additional [F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) and [F-15E Strike Eagle](495724.md) fighters to bases in [Israel](9282173.md) and [Jordan](7515964.md) respectively, as well as large numbers of [tanker aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tanker_aircraft).

On 31 March 2026, [Carrier Strike Group 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_10) departed [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md) for deployment to the Middle East, following ongoing combat operations by the US towards Iran. The Carrier Strike Group had arrived in [CENTCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CENTCOM)'s area of responsibility on April 23, 2026, bringing the amount of Carrier Strike Group's in the area to 3. This is the first time in decades since three aircraft carriers were operating in the Middle East.[^cite_note-10]

## Background

Further information: [2025–2026 Iran–United States negotiations](79685207.md), [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md), [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md), and [Reactions to the 2025–2026 Iranian protests](82038636.md)

### Iran's nuclear program

The United States has maintained a military presence in the [Middle East](19323.md) since the early [Cold War](325329.md) era, expanding it significantly after the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) and the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md), maintaining military facilities across at least nineteen sites in the Middle East, in countries including [Bahrain](162036.md#Bahrain), [Iraq](162036.md#Iraq), [Jordan](162036.md#Jordan), [Kuwait](162036.md#Kuwait), [Qatar](162036.md#Qatar), [Saudi Arabia](162036.md#Saudi_Arabia), and the [United Arab Emirates](162036.md#United_Arab_Emirates).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

In the context of the [Treaty on the Non-proliferation of Nuclear Weapons](22107.md), a 2006 report of the Director General of the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) demonstrated a security concern that the IAEA were at that time unable to satisfactorily demonstrate that the government of Iran's nuclear programme did not include a military nuclear dimension.[^cite_note-UN16962006-14] In the context of the continuation of [diplomatic relations](28165911.md),[^cite_note-15] subsequent [UN resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_resolution) on the matter of the *Iran Nuclear Issue* were made from 2007–2010, and in 2015.[^cite_note-16]

U.S. pressure on Iran intensified during [Trump's first term](52231341.md). In May 2018, the Trump administration announced that the United States would exit the [Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Comprehensive_Plan_of_Action) and apply a policy of [maximum pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_pressure_campaign) on Iran's economy, centering the strategy on comprehensive economic sanctions.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] On 4 February 2025, at the start of his [second term](78298108.md), Trump signed a [National Security Presidential Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_directive) restoring the maximum pressure policy, directing the [Department of the Treasury](53667.md) to impose maximum economic sanctions on Iran and the [Department of State](31975.md) to lead a campaign aimed at driving Iranian oil exports to zero.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Some degree of limitation of the activities of the IAEA was brought into place via the Iranian government in 2021.[^cite_note-21] In 2024 a statement the Iranian government official [Kamal Kharrazi](186801.md) stated that Iran was prepared to change its policy on nuclear armament, and that [Khamenei](385653.md)'s [fatwa against nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatwa_against_nuclear_weapons) was the only thing stopping them from doing so.[^cite_note-22]

### Escalation of tensions in 2025–2026

Tensions escalated sharply in late 2025. On 28 December 2025, protests erupted after a sharp collapse of [Iran's currency](708656.md), amid soaring inflation and worsening living conditions, beginning with shopkeepers in [Tehran](57654.md)'s [Grand Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Bazaar,_Tehran) before spreading across the country.[^cite_note-23] Iranian authorities responded with a crackdown, and on 8 January 2026, [cut all internet access to the country](82063825.md). [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) found evidence of a coordinated escalation in the use of [lethal force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadly_force) beginning 8 January, with protesters and bystanders shot in the head and torso.[^cite_note-24] The death toll remained contested due to the communications blackout. Iran's government put the figure at 3,117; human rights activists counted at least 6,126 dead, while acknowledging the true number could not be independently verified.[^cite_note-25]

On 13 January, speaking in [Detroit](8687.md), Trump told Iranian protesters to "keep protesting" and that "help is on its way," vowing to punish security forces responsible for the killings and announcing he had canceled all meetings with Iranian officials until the crackdown stopped.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The U.S. then began reinforcing its military presence in the region.[^cite_note-Roth-28]

The [Gang of Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_of_Eight_\(intelligence\)), a group of eight leaders in the US Congress who are commonly briefed on classified intelligence matters, was briefed on the attack prior to its commencement.[^cite_note-29] Also at the time, [Bloomberg News](3057255.md) reported that [NATO](21133.md) Airborne Warning and Control System ([AWACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control)) craft were surveilling Iran out of [Konya Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konya_Airport),[^cite_note-30] though Turkey later denied reports it was aiding US and Israeli strikes on Iran.[^cite_note-31]

The Trump administration stated in February 2026 that Iran had restarted its nuclear programme and was developing missiles with enough range for an attack on the US.[^cite_note-auto12-32] According to an anonymous US source, Trump authorized the strike after the US received intelligence that Iran was planning to preemptively launch missiles.[^cite_note-auto11-33][^cite_note-auto3-34] The administration did not provide any evidence that Iran was planning to preemptively strike US assets,[^cite_note-auto4-35] and an unspecified [Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) source told Congress in closed-door briefings that there was no intelligence suggesting Iran was planning to attack US forces first.[^cite_note-Phil_01032026-36][^cite_note-auto7-37] In March 2026, it was reported by [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(United_States\)) that the U.S. "recently acquired information that as of early February 2026, Iran allegedly aspired to conduct a surprise attack using unmanned aerial vehicles from an unidentified vessel off the coast of the United States Homeland, specifically against unspecified targets in California, in the event that the US conducted strikes against Iran."[^cite_note-38]

## Deployment

### Pre-buildup



U.S. Navy ship deployments to the Middle East since 2023

Since the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) in 2023, the United States has maintained at least one aircraft carrier in the Middle East or [eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean), with two carriers present in the region for [Operation Rough Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rough_Rider) from March to May of 2025. Coverage briefly lapsed in late 2025 when [USS *Gerald R. Ford*](4050542.md) was reassigned to the [Caribbean](18956035.md) to take part in [Operation Southern Spear](81593523.md), but by January 2026 the US naval presence in and around the Middle East was reported to be the largest since the [regional crisis](75895273.md) began in 2023.[^cite_note-Cancian-1]

### January 2026

In mid-January 2026, [USS *Abraham Lincoln*](199106.md) was ordered to [CENTCOM](338482.md)'s area of operations and arrived on 26 January with its strike group, including [guided-missile destroyers](8779.md), [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser), support vessels, and embarked aircraft such as [F/A-18E Super Hornets](845012.md), [EA-18G Growlers](833647.md), [E-2 Hawkeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_E-2_Hawkeye), [MH-60 helicopters](202031.md#MH-60S), and [F-35C Lightning IIs](11812.md).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Additional U.S naval assets reported in the region included warships positioned in the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), the [Red Sea](26191.md), and the Mediterranean, as well as [littoral combat ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship) operating in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). Air components were reinforced by [F-15E Strike Eagle](495724.md) and other fighter aircraft relocated from [RAF Lakenheath](856907.md) to [Muwaffaq Salti Air Base](32592197.md) in [Jordan](7515964.md). Allied forces, including [Eurofighter Typhoons](167667.md) belonging to the [Royal Air Force](25679.md), were also stationed at [al-Udeid Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Udeid_Air_Base) in [Qatar](8238258.md).

### February 2026

&#x20;Throughout February 2026, U.S. officials signaled plans for an expanded carrier presence. [The Wall Street Journal](173070.md) and other outlets reported preparations for deploying [USS *George H. W. Bush*](156573.md), which was completing drills off the coast of [Virginia](32432.md) and indicated additional carriers might be sent to reinforce operations.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]



Map of U.S. bases in the Middle East

On 13 February 2026, sources stated that USS *Gerald R. Ford* was en route to the Middle East, creating an uncommon two-carrier deployment in the theater at a time of [heightened tensions with Iran](82533582.md).[^cite_note-43] Reports from multiple outlets noted that this concentration of U.S. air and naval assets constituted one of the most significant force postures in the region since the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md).[^cite_note-44] On 20 February, it was reported that the ship was seen off the coast of [Gibraltar](7607314.md).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The arrival of USS *Gerald R. Ford* and its strike group marks the largest deployment of naval assets to the region since five carrier battle groups assembled at the beginning of [Operation Iraqi Freedom](201936.md).[^cite_note-Cancian-1]

On 24 February 2026, it was reported that twelve [F-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) fighter jets had been deployed to [Ovda Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovda_Airbase) in southern Israel, which had available [hardened aircraft shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardened_aircraft_shelter).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] This was the first US deployment of offensive weaponry in Israel.[^cite_note-50] [Fox News](11121.md) and *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* had previously reported that the US had been blocked by [Gulf allies](3088555.md) from using their bases and airspace for strikes on Iran over fears of retaliation.[^cite_note-Ward-8][^cite_note-Heinrich-9][^cite_note-51]

On 26 February 2026, Fox News reported that the [US Fleet Headquarters in Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain) had been reduced to fewer than 100 mission critical personnel. Satellite photos revealed that all US ships based in Bahrain had left port. Both steps had previously been taken as defensive measures in 2025 ahead of the [United States strikes on Iranian nuclear sites](80262844.md).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

On 27 February, USS *Gerald R. Ford* was initially deployed off the coast of Israel.[^cite_note-54] The total number of [United States Air Force](32090.md) refueling tankers deployed at Israel's [Ben Gurion Airport](113149.md) rose to 14, enabling the Ford's [carrier air wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_air_wing) enough range to be able to reach Iran.[^cite_note-55]

#### Incidents

On 3 February 2026, the [US Central Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Central_Command) said that elements of Iran's [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy](4676345.md) attempted to intercept and board the U.S.-flagged tanker MV *Stena Imperative* in the Strait of Hormuz. The vessel continued on its course toward the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) under the escort of the guided-missile destroyer [USS *McFaul*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_McFaul) after U.S. naval forces intervened.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] During the same day, a U.S. F-35C assigned to USS *Abraham Lincoln* shot down an Iranian [Shahed-139](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahed_139) drone that was flying toward the carrier in the Arabian Sea; U.S. Central Command characterized the drone's approach as a potential threat and stated there were no casualties or damage to U.S assets in the encounter. Iranian state media said that the drone had been completing a surveillance mission in international waters.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

On 5 February, Iranian state media reported that the [IRGC](1285472.md) seized two foreign-flagged oil tankers near [Farsi Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsi_Island) in the Persian Gulf, transferring them to the port of [Bushehr](205669.md) and alleging the vessels were engaged in fuel smuggling.[^cite_note-60]

According to Defense Express, a [Northrop Grumman MQ-4C Triton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_MQ-4C_Triton) disappeared over the Strait of Hormuz after sending a [7700 code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_transponder_codes#7700) that signifies an emergency.[^cite_note-61]

### Iran war

Main article: [2026 Iran war](82533582.md)

On 28 February, the United States and Israel reported that they had conducted military strikes on Iran. Explosions were reported in central Tehran, according to the semiofficial Iranian news station and agency Fars.[^cite_note-62] Israeli [Channel 12 News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hevrat_HaHadashot) revealed shortly after the strikes began that information about the US F-22 deployment was leaked as part of a joint deception operation to hide that strike preparations were more advanced at other bases. This led to large groups of senior Iranian officials going ahead and gathering together, including [Iranian Supreme Leader](36814344.md) [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), who were then targeted in opening strikes of the war.[^cite_note-63] Days later it was revealed in a Fox News interview of [IDF Spokesperson](5257972.md) [Brigadier General](206220.md) [Effie Defrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effie_Defrin) that there were months of joint US and Israeli "strategic and operation deception" of satellite imagery leading up to the strike. This encompassed both planes and other facilities. The night of the strike he and [IDF Chief of Staff](3011266.md) [Lieutenant General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluf) [Eyal Zamir](61877756.md) had taken their official cars home from [IDF Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaKirya). They came back without them so that leading up to the strike satellite imagery would show that their base did not appear full nor warplanes appear armed and ready for a strike.[^cite_note-64]

## Reactions

### Iran

Iran's response to the buildup combined military signaling with hardened rhetoric. On 22 January, IRGC commander Gen. [Mohammad Pakpour](53188360.md) issued a written statement marking the Guards' annual commemoration day, warning the United States and Israel "to avoid any miscalculation" and declaring that the force stood "more ready than ever, finger on the trigger, to execute the orders and measures of the supreme commander-in-chief."[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Chief of Staff of the [Iranian Armed Forces](14305018.md) [Abdolrahim Mousavi](5072731.md) separately announced that Iran had revised its military doctrine from defensive to offensive following the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

On 16 February, the IRGC began live-fire naval drills in the Strait of Hormuz, announcing a temporary closure of sections of the waterway, a rare move not seen since the 1980s.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] The following day, Supreme Leader [Ali Khamenei](385653.md) warned that US warships deployed to the Gulf "could be sunk to the bottom of the sea," stating that the "strongest army in the world may receive a slap it cannot recover from."[^cite_note-Fatima-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-depose-74] Iran's UN ambassador said all US bases and assets in the region would be considered legitimate targets in the event of an attack. Foreign Minister [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md) warned [Europe](9239.md) it was "fanning the flames" of a potential wider war, and called the [European Union](9317.md)'s designation of the IRGC as a [terrorist organization](63578.md) a "major strategic mistake." [Iran's parliament](383276.md) retaliated by designating the armed forces of EU member states as terrorist organizations under domestic law.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-BBC_News-76][^cite_note-aj0102-77]

On 26 February, it was reported the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Aerospace Force](5613577.md) conducted drills simulating missile attacks on [Al Dhafra Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Dhafra_Air_Base) in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).[^cite_note-78] Satellite imagery reported by [The New York Times](30680.md) showed Iran accelerating repairs at missile sites while making limited repairs at its nuclear facilities which had been damaged by the [United States strikes on Iranian nuclear sites](80262844.md), indicating a focus on missile infrastructure.[^cite_note-79] The Chinese YLC-8B UHF-Band 3D long-range anti-stealth surveillance radar was reportedly deployed in preparation for potential strikes.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] State media said the deployment of the [Khorramshahr-4](54338983.md) ballistic missile into IRGC underground facilities "coincides with the announced shift in the armed forces' doctrine from defensive to offensive and carries a clear message to regional and extra-regional adversaries."[^cite_note-84] Security Commissioner [Yahaya Safavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Rahim_Safavi) threatened the [White House](33057.md) with a missile attack.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

### United States

The US administration maintained that military options remained on the table while pursuing parallel diplomacy. [White House Press Secretary](284101.md) [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md) told reporters that "President Trump's first option is always diplomacy," while adding that he "is willing to use the lethal force of the United States military if necessary."[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

[Politico](9172097.md) reported that some senior White House advisers opposed direct US military action, citing a lack of political support for American casualties, and that a large campaign focused on regime change risked depleting munitions stockpiles. The advisers argued it would be preferable for Israel to strike first, calculating that Iranian retaliation against US assets in the region would then provide a clearer political justification for American involvement.[^cite_note-POLITICO-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt responded that "the media may continue to speculate on the president's thinking all they want, but only President Trump knows what he may or may not do."[^cite_note-POLITICO-90][^cite_note-93]

[Secretary of State](32293.md) [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) stated that the United States was prepared to engage in talks with Iran but stressed that any meaningful negotiations would need to address Iran's ballistic missile program, its support for regional militant groups, and other core security concerns, further adding that Iran's refusal to discuss its ballistic missile program as a "big problem" and an "unsustainable threat" to the US homeland. Rubio expressed skepticism about the likelihood of reaching a successful deal, even as [Washington](108956.md) signaled readiness to pursue diplomacy.[^cite_note-94]

The United States imposed new sanctions in late January 2026 targeting Iranian political and economic figures, including Interior Minister [Eskandar Momeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskandar_Momeni) and businessman [Babak Zanjani](38586496.md), in response to the government's crackdown on protests and to encourage compliance with international norms.[^cite_note-95] [Secretary of the Treasury](43338.md) [Scott Bessent](38532044.md) said that senior Iranian leaders were moving tens of millions of dollars out of the country, describing the financial outflows as evidence that the regime was "abandoning ship" amid ongoing protests and economic collapse, and that the Treasury was tracking these transfers into banks and financial institutions abroad.[^cite_note-96]

The U.S. Virtual Embassy in Iran issued security alerts in January and February 2026, urging American citizens to leave the country amid unrest and travel disruptions, and recommending departure via land through open borders to neighboring countries.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] The U.S. State Department ordered the departure of non-emergency personal and families from Lebanon on 23 February. It told those in Israel "to leave today" if they wanted to on 27 February.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

In his [2026 State of the Union Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_State_of_the_Union_Address) President Trump stated that the Iranian regime is rebuilding its "sinister" nuclear weapons program.[^cite_note-102]

In May 2026, Congress began considering major changes to integrate the US and Israeli military, and defense industries, as part of the 2027 [National Defense Authorization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act). [^cite_note-103]

### European Union

On 29 January, EU foreign ministers unanimously voted to designate the IRGC as a terrorist organization, placing it on the same list as [Al-Qaeda](1921.md), [Hamas](13913.md), and the [Islamic State](9087364.md).[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-BBC_News-76] EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas framed the decision as a direct response to the crackdown on protesters, saying "repression cannot go unanswered." France had reportedly been hesitant, but shifted after new data on the scale of the crackdown became available, and Italy formally proposed the designation days before the vote.[^cite_note-105] The designation triggers [asset freezes](5847461.md), [travel bans](26647363.md), and a prohibition on funding IRGC-affiliated entities. Iran condemned the move as illegal and politically motivated.[^cite_note-aj0102-77] The German [Bundeswehr](288188.md) later warned of increased Iranian related espionage and cyber attacks.[^cite_note-106]

### Regional countries

[Crown Prince of Saudi Arabia](40373823.md) [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md) told Iranian President [Masoud Pezeshkian](2650383.md) that the kingdom would not permit the use of its airspace for any strike on Iran. However, in a closed-door briefing with think tank experts and Jewish organization representatives in Washington, Saudi Defense Minister Prince [Khalid bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_bin_Salman_Al_Saud) said that if Trump did not follow through on his threats, it would "only embolden the regime."[^cite_note-107] Israel sent military intelligence officials to Washington to share target data and expressed support for strikes on Iranian leadership.[^cite_note-108] Qatar, the UAE, and [Turkey](11125639.md) all publicly stated they would not permit their territory or airspace to be used for an attack. An Arab Gulf diplomat said regional leaders were in contact with both Tehran and Washington to avert war, warning that conflict could cause a severe spike in oil prices.[^cite_note-109]

## Negotiations

Further information: [2025–2026 Iran–United States negotiations § 2026 negotiations](79685207.md#2026_negotiations), and [Prelude to the 2026 Iran war § Negotiations](82272270.md#Negotiations)

Diplomatic contacts between the United States and Iran resumed in early February 2026, as both governments sought to avert a military confrontation while maintaining sharply incompatible positions. [Oman](22316.md) served as mediator throughout, with Foreign Minister Badr bin Hamad Al-Busaidi conveying messages between the delegations and hosting or attending each session.[^cite_note-150]

The first round of indirect talks took place on 6 February in [Muscat](57053.md), Oman. The US delegation included special envoy [Steve Witkoff](40901053.md), [Jared Kushner](6191053.md), and CENTCOM commander Admiral [Brad Cooper](67555995.md), the first time a senior US military commander had been present at negotiations with Iran.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] Iran was represented by Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. The talks were structured as indirect discussions, with Al-Busaidi relaying positions between rooms. Araghchi described the session as "a good start," while noting that negotiations must take place "without tension and without threats." Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md) publicly voiced frustration over the format, telling [Megyn Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megyn_Kelly)'s podcast that negotiating with a country "when you can't even talk to the person who's in charge" was "bizarre" and made diplomacy "very, very difficult," a reference to Khamenei's absence from the table.[^cite_note-153] Rubio said after the round that he was "not sure you can reach a deal with these guys."[^cite_note-154]

A second round was held in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) on 17 February. A US official said Iran would return with detailed proposals to address remaining gaps, and characterized the chances of imminent military conflict as low.[^cite_note-155] Araghchi said the two sides had reached a tentative understanding on the broad principles guiding further discussions, though no substantive agreement had been reached.[^cite_note-156] That same day, Khamenei delivered a speech in which he rejected the US demand for zero enrichment, dismissed US conditions as "foolish," and warned that American warships could be sunk.[^cite_note-Fatima-72][^cite_note-depose-74]

A third round took place in Geneva on 26 February. The session ran in two formats: indirect, with Al-Busaidi passing messages between the sides, and at some points directly between the US and Iranian negotiators.[^cite_note-Ravid-157] [IAEA](14984.md) Director-General [Rafael Grossi](62195943.md) also participated. Araghchi said "good progress was achieved" and that the parties had entered "a serious review of the elements of an agreement, both in the nuclear field and on sanctions," with a fourth round expected within about a week.[^cite_note-158] Al-Busaidi described the outcome as "significant progress" and said technical discussions between nuclear experts would take place in [Vienna](55866.md) the following week.[^cite_note-159]

The core positions of the two sides remained far apart throughout. The US demanded that Iran completely halt [uranium enrichment](37555.md), surrender its [existing stockpile](721807.md), roll back its [long-range missile program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran's_missile_program), and end support for regional armed groups. Iran maintained that talks must remain limited to nuclear issues and that its right to enrichment was non-negotiable.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] The US came into the third round demanding that any future deal remain in effect indefinitely and that Iran give up its stockpile of approximately 10,000 kilograms of enriched uranium, while showing some flexibility on enrichment itself if Iran could prove no path to a bomb existed.[^cite_note-Ravid-157] On the eve of the third round, the Trump administration imposed new sanctions targeting 14 vessels and entities linked to Iran's oil exports, framing the move as part of the ongoing maximum pressure campaign.[^cite_note-162]

### Pleas against talks

On 8 February, it was reported that Iranians inside Iran pleaded with Trump not to negotiate with the Islamic Republic after the [massacres in the country](82036668.md).[^cite_note-163] Likewise, an Iranian man from [Bushehr](205669.md), Pouria Hamidi, posted a video appealing to Trump and Western governments to refrain from any deals with the Islamic Republic, before [taking his own life](16831059.md).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] Among Iranians inside Iran as well as across the [Iranian diaspora](82164645.md), calls grew for American military strikes on Iran.[^cite_note-166] Similarly, during the 26 February negotiations in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), Iranian demonstrators protested against the Islamic Republic outside the [United Nations Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_at_Geneva).[^cite_note-167] United States Senator [Lindsey Graham](363279.md) warned against any Iran deal, while also stating, "the best and only long term answer is to stand with the Iranian people and demand regime change."[^cite_note-168] After the [8 April ceasefire](82888027.md), rallies continued to be held worldwide against the Islamic Republic, with protesters voicing opposition to any potential deal with the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-169]
