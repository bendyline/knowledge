# Greenhouse gas emissions



Greenhouse gas emissions per person in the highest-emitting countries (as of 2023).

[^cite_note-GHGemissionsByCountry-1]

&#x20;Areas of rectangles represent total emissions for each country.

**Greenhouse gas emissions** (**GHG emissions**) from human activities intensify the [greenhouse effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect) which contributes to [climate change](5042951.md). [Carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) (CO<sub>2</sub>) from burning [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) (such as [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [oil](23195.md), and [natural gas](22131.md)) is the main cause of climate change. The [largest annual emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_locations_and_entities_by_greenhouse_gas_emissions) are from [China](61886740.md) followed by the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States), which has [higher emissions per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_greenhouse_gas_emissions_per_capita). The main producers fueling the emissions globally are [large oil and gas companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Oil). Emissions from human activities have increased [atmospheric carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_in_the_atmosphere_of_Earth) by about 50% over pre-industrial levels. The growing levels of emissions have varied, but have been consistent among all [greenhouse gases](21350772.md). Over 60 billion tons were emitted in 2025, higher than any year before.[^cite_note-2] Total cumulative emissions from 1870 to 2022 were 703 GtC (2575 GtCO<sub>2</sub>), of which 484±20 GtC (1773±73 GtCO<sub>2</sub>) from [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) and industry, and 219±60 GtC (802±220 GtCO<sub>2</sub>) from [land use change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use,_land-use_change,_and_forestry). [Land-use change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_change), such as [ deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_and_climate_change), caused about 31% of cumulative emissions over 1870–2022, [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) 32%, oil 24%, and gas 10%.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

Carbon dioxide is the main greenhouse gas resulting from human activities. It accounts for more than half of warming. [Methane](18582230.md) (CH<sub>4</sub>) emissions have almost the same short-term impact.[^cite_note-ch4-vs-co2-5] [Nitrous oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide) (N<sub>2</sub>O) and [fluorinated gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorinated_gases) (F-gases) play a lesser role in comparison. Emissions of carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide in 2023 were all higher than ever before.[^cite_note-6]

[Electricity generation](9540.md), heat and [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) are major emitters; overall energy is responsible for around 73% of emissions.[^cite_note-by-sector-7] A growing concern for electricity usage is the expansion of artificial intelligence (AI), which is projected to significantly increase global energy demand.[^cite_note-8] [Deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and other changes in land use also emit carbon dioxide and [methane](18582230.md). The largest source of anthropogenic [methane emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_emissions) is [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture), closely followed by [gas venting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_venting) and [fugitive emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugitive_emissions) from the [fossil-fuel industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_industry). The largest agricultural methane source is [livestock](25160767.md). [Agricultural soils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_soil_science) emit [nitrous oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide) partly due to [fertilizers](37401.md). Similarly, [fluorinated gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorinated_gases) from [refrigerants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerant) play an outsized role in total human emissions.

The current CO<sub>2</sub>-equivalent emission rates, averaging 4.9 tonnes per person per year,[^cite_note-\:5-9] are well over twice the estimated rate of 2.3 tons[^cite_note-\:7-10][^cite_note-\:8-11] required to stay within the 2030 Paris Agreement increase of 1.5 °C (2.7 °F) over pre-industrial levels.[^cite_note-\:9-12] Annual per capita emissions in the industrialized countries are typically as much as ten times the average in developing countries.[^cite_note-grubb_kyoto_protocol-13]

&#x20;The [carbon footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint) (or *greenhouse gas footprint*) serves as an indicator to compare the amount of greenhouse gases emitted over the entire [life cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_assessment) from the production of a good or service along the [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain) to its final consumption.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:6-15] [Carbon accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_accounting) (or greenhouse gas accounting) is a framework of methods to measure and track how much [greenhouse gas](21350772.md) an organization emits.[^cite_note-\:3-16] This accounting is used to help understand the various effects of emissions on positive and negative impacts they have on the climate including on human mortality.[^cite_note-17]

## Relevance for greenhouse effect and global warming

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Greenhouse effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Greenhouse_effect\&action=edit)]

The [greenhouse effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect) occurs when [heat-trapping gases in a planet's atmosphere](21350772.md) prevent the planet from losing heat to space, raising its surface temperature. Surface heating can happen from an internal heat source (as in the case of [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter)) or come from an external source, such as a [host star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Host_star). In the case of [Earth](9228.md), the [Sun](26751.md) emits [shortwave radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortwave_radiation) ([sunlight](27979.md)) that passes through greenhouse gases to heat the Earth's surface. In response, the Earth's surface emits [longwave radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outgoing_longwave_radiation) that is mostly [absorbed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_\(electromagnetic_radiation\)) by greenhouse gases, reducing the rate at which the Earth can cool off.

Without the greenhouse effect, the Earth's average surface temperature would be as cold as −18 °C (−0.4 °F).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Greenhouse_effect_IPCC4_ch012-19] This is much less than the 20th century average of about 14 °C (57 °F).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Greenhouse_effect_clchgtrack-21] In addition to naturally present greenhouse gases, burning of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) has increased amounts of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) and [methane](18582230.md) in the atmosphere.[^cite_note-Greenhouse_effect_ipcc-AR4WG1-22][^cite_note-Greenhouse_effect_budget-23] As a result, at 2023,[^cite_note-24] [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) of about 1.2 °C (2.2 °F) has occurred since the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution),[^cite_note-25] with the global average surface temperature increasing at a rate of 0.18 °C (0.32 °F) per decade since 1981.[^cite_note-26]

## Overview of main sources

### Relevant greenhouse gases

See also: [Carbon dioxide in Earth's atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_in_Earth's_atmosphere) and [Atmospheric methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_methane)

The major anthropogenic (human origin) sources of greenhouse gases are [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) (CO<sub>2</sub>), [nitrous oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide) (N<sup></sup>\
<sub>2</sub>O), [methane](18582230.md) and three groups of fluorinated gases ([sulfur hexafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_hexafluoride) (SF<sup></sup>\
<sub>6</sub>), [hydrofluorocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofluorocarbons) (HFCs) and [perfluorocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfluorocarbons) ([PFCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfluorinated_compound), [sulphur hexafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulphur_hexafluoride) (SF<sub>6</sub>), and [nitrogen trifluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_trifluoride) (NF<sub>3</sub>)).[^cite_note-27] Though the [greenhouse effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect) is heavily driven by [water vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_vapor_feedback),[^cite_note-28] human emissions of water vapor are not a significant contributor to warming.

Although [CFCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFCs) are greenhouse gases, they are regulated by the [Montreal Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Protocol) which was motivated by CFCs' contribution to [ozone depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_depletion) rather than by their contribution to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming). Ozone depletion has only a minor role in greenhouse warming, though the two processes are sometimes confused in the media. In 2016, negotiators from over 170 nations meeting at the summit of the [United Nations Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme) reached a legally binding accord to phase out [hydrofluorocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofluorocarbons) (HFCs) in the [Kigali Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali_Amendment) to the [Montreal Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Protocol).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The use of CFC-12 (except some essential uses) has been phased out due to its [ozone depleting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_depletion) properties.[^cite_note-32] The phasing-out of less active [HCFC-compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haloalkane) will be completed in 2030.[^cite_note-33]

## Calculations and reporting

Further information: [Carbon accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_accounting), [Carbon footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint), and [Greenhouse gas inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_inventory)

### Variables

There are several ways of measuring greenhouse gas emissions. Some variables that have been reported include:[^cite_note-sapiens.revues.org-55]

- Definition of measurement boundaries: Emissions can be attributed geographically, to the area where they were emitted (the territory principle) or by the activity principle to the territory that produced the emissions. These two principles result in different totals when measuring, for example, electricity importation from one country to another, or emissions at an international airport.

- Time horizon of different gases: The contribution of given greenhouse gas is reported as a CO

  2

  &#x20;equivalent. The calculation to determine this takes into account how long that gas remains in the atmosphere. This is not always known accurately and calculations must be regularly updated to reflect new information.

- The measurement protocol itself: This may be via direct measurement or estimation. The four main methods are the emission factor-based method, mass balance method, predictive [emissions monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_monitoring) systems, and [continuous emissions monitoring systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_emissions_monitoring_system). These methods differ in accuracy, cost, and usability. Public information from [space-based measurements of carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_measurements_of_carbon_dioxide) by [Climate Trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Trace) is expected to reveal individual large plants before the [2021 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference).

  [^cite_note-56]

These measures are sometimes used by countries to assert various policy/ethical positions on climate change.[^cite_note-banuri-57]<sup>: 94 </sup>The use of different measures leads to a lack of comparability, which is problematic when monitoring progress towards targets. There are arguments for the adoption of a common measurement tool, or at least the development of communication between different tools.[^cite_note-sapiens.revues.org-55]

### Reporting

Emissions may be tracked over long time periods, known as historical or cumulative emissions measurements. Cumulative emissions provide some indicators of what is responsible for greenhouse gas atmospheric concentration build-up.[^cite_note-iea-58]<sup>: 199 </sup>

### National accounts balance

See also: [Carbon leakage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_leakage)



&#x20;Substantial land-use change contributions to emissions have been made by Latin America, Southeast Asia, Africa, and Pacific Islands. Area of rectangles shows total emissions in 2019 for that region.

[^cite_note-IPCCwg3Fig2c-59]

The national accounts balance tracks emissions based on the difference between a country's exports and imports. For many richer nations, the balance is negative because more goods are imported than they are exported. This result is mostly due to the fact that it is cheaper to produce goods outside of developed countries, leading developed countries to become increasingly dependent on services and not goods. A positive account balance would mean that more production was occurring within a country, so more operational factories would increase carbon emission levels.[^cite_note-holtz-eakin-60]

Emissions may also be measured across shorter time periods. Emissions changes may, for example, be measured against the base year of 1990. 1990 was used in the [United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change](31898.md) (UNFCCC) as the base year for emissions, and is also used in the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) (some gases are also measured from the year 1995).[^cite_note-grubb_kyoto_protocol-13]<sup>: 146, 149 </sup> A country's emissions may also be reported as a proportion of global emissions for a particular year.

Another measurement is of per capita emissions. This divides a country's total annual emissions by its mid-year population.[^cite_note-world_bank_emissions_data-61]<sup>: 370 </sup> Per capita emissions may be based on historical or annual emissions.[^cite_note-banuri-57]<sup>: 106–107 </sup>

### Embedded emissions

See also: [Embedded emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_emissions)

One way of attributing greenhouse gas emissions is to measure the [embedded emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_emissions) (also referred to as "embodied emissions") of goods that are being consumed. Emissions are usually measured according to production, rather than consumption.[^cite_note-62] For example, in the main international [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) on climate change (the [UNFCCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNFCCC)), countries report on emissions produced within their borders, e.g. the emissions produced from burning fossil fuels.[^cite_note-world_energy_outlook_2009-63]<sup>: 179 </sup>[^cite_note-davis_consumption_emissions-64]<sup>: 1 </sup> Under a production-based accounting of emissions, embedded emissions on imported goods are attributed to the exporting, rather than the importing, country. Under a consumption-based accounting of emissions, embedded emissions on imported goods are attributed to the importing country, rather than the exporting, country.

A substantial proportion of CO<sub>2</sub> emissions is traded internationally. The net effect of trade was to export emissions from China and other emerging markets to consumers in the US, Japan, and Western Europe.[^cite_note-davis_consumption_emissions-64]<sup>: 4 </sup>

### Carbon footprint

This section is an excerpt from [Carbon footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Carbon_footprint\&action=edit)]

A [carbon footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint) (or greenhouse gas footprint) is a calculated value or index that makes it possible to compare the total amount of [greenhouse gases](21350772.md) that an activity, product, company or country adds to the atmosphere. Carbon footprints are usually reported in tonnes of emissions ([CO<sub>2</sub>-equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_potential)) per unit of comparison. Such units can be, for example, *tonnes CO<sub>2</sub>-eq per year*, *per kilogram of protein for consumption*, *per kilometer travelled*, *per piece of clothing* and so forth. A product's carbon footprint includes the emissions for the entire [life cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_assessment). These run from the production along the [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain) to its final consumption and disposal.

Similarly, an organization's carbon footprint includes the direct as well as the indirect emissions that it causes. The [Greenhouse Gas Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_accounting) (for [carbon accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_accounting) of organizations) calls these *Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions*. There are several methodologies and online tools to calculate the carbon footprint. They depend on whether the focus is on a country, organization, product or individual person. For example, the carbon footprint of a product could help consumers decide which product to buy if they want to be [climate aware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_movement). For [climate change mitigation](2119179.md) activities, the carbon footprint can help distinguish those economic activities with a high footprint from those with a low footprint. So the carbon footprint concept allows everyone to make comparisons between the climate impacts of individuals, products, companies and countries. It also helps people devise strategies and priorities for reducing the carbon footprint.

&#x20;\
&#x20;Consumption-based accounting is therefore more comprehensive. This comprehensive carbon footprint reporting including Scope 3 emissions deals with gaps in current systems. Countries' [GHG inventories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_inventory) for the [UNFCCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNFCCC) do not include international transport.[^cite_note-Carbon_footprint_\:14-65] Comprehensive carbon footprint reporting looks at the final demand for emissions, to where the consumption of the goods and services takes place.[^cite_note-Carbon_footprint_\:15-66]

### Emission intensity

Further information: [Emission intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_intensity)

Emission intensity is a ratio between greenhouse gas emissions and another metric, e.g., gross domestic product (GDP) or energy use. The terms "carbon intensity" and "[emissions intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_intensity)" are also sometimes used.[^cite_note-67] Emission intensities may be calculated using [market exchange rates](12594.md#Cross-border_comparison_and_purchasing_power_parity) (MER) or [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) (PPP).[^cite_note-banuri-57]<sup>: 96 </sup>

Cumulative anthropogenic (i.e., human-emitted) emissions of CO<sub>2</sub> from fossil fuel use are a major cause of [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming),[^cite_note-77] and give some indication of which countries have contributed most to human-induced climate change. In particular, CO<sub>2</sub> stays in the atmosphere for at least 150 years and up to 1000 years,[^cite_note-78] whilst methane disappears within a decade or so,[^cite_note-79] and nitrous oxides last about 100 years.[^cite_note-80] The graph gives some indication of which regions have contributed most to human-induced climate change.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-hohne_2010_regional_contribution_to_global_warming-82]<sup>: 15 </sup> When these numbers are calculated per capita cumulative emissions based on then-current population the situation is shown even more clearly. The ratio in per capita emissions between industrialized countries and developing countries was estimated at more than 10 to 1.

Non-[OECD](33853117.md) countries accounted for 42% of cumulative energy-related CO<sub>2</sub> emissions between 1890 and 2007.[^cite_note-world_energy_outlook_2009-63]<sup>: 179–80 </sup> Over this time period, the US accounted for 28% of emissions; the EU, 23%; Japan, 4%; other OECD countries 5%; Russia, 11%; China, 9%; India, 3%; and the rest of the world, 18%.[^cite_note-world_energy_outlook_2009-63]<sup>: 179–80 </sup>The European Commission adopted a set of legislative proposals targeting a reduction of the CO<sub>2</sub> emissions by 55% by 2030.

Overall, developed countries accounted for 83.8% of industrial CO<sub>2</sub> emissions over this time period, and 67.8% of total CO<sub>2</sub> emissions. Developing countries accounted for industrial CO<sub>2</sub> emissions of 16.2% over this time period, and 32.2% of total CO<sub>2</sub> emissions.

However, what becomes clear when we look at emissions across the world today is that the countries with the highest emissions over history are not always the biggest emitters today. For example, in 2017, the UK accounted for just 1% of global emissions.[^cite_note-auto-51]

In comparison, humans have emitted more greenhouse gases than the [Chicxulub meteorite impact event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicxulub_crater) which caused the [extinction of the dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous%E2%80%93Paleogene_extinction_event).[^cite_note-Specktor_2019-83]

Transport, together with [electricity generation](9540.md), is the major source of greenhouse gas emissions in the [EU](9317.md). Greenhouse gas emissions from the transportation sector continue to rise, in contrast to power generation and nearly all other sectors. Since 1990, transportation emissions have increased by 30%. The transportation sector accounts for around 70% of these emissions. The majority of these emissions are caused by passenger [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle) and vans. Road travel is the first major source of greenhouse gas emissions from transportation, followed by aircraft and maritime.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-\:2-85] Waterborne transportation is still the least [carbon-intensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_intensity) mode of transportation on average, and it is an essential link in [sustainable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability) [multimodal freight supply chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain).[^cite_note-86]

Buildings, like industry, are directly responsible for around one-fifth of greenhouse gas emissions, primarily from [space heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_heater) and hot water consumption. When combined with power consumption within buildings, this figure climbs to more than one-third.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-by-sector-7][^cite_note-88]

Within the EU, the [agricultural sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy) presently accounts for roughly 10% of total greenhouse gas emissions, with methane from livestock accounting for slightly more than half of 10%.[^cite_note-89]

Estimates of total CO<sub>2</sub> emissions do include [biotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biota_\(ecology\)) carbon emissions, mainly from deforestation.[^cite_note-banuri-57]<sup>: 94 </sup> Including biotic emissions brings about the same controversy mentioned earlier regarding carbon sinks and land-use change.[^cite_note-banuri-57]<sup>: 93–94 </sup> The actual calculation of net emissions is very complex, and is affected by how carbon sinks are allocated between regions and the dynamics of the [climate system](5565588.md).

Carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) is the dominant emitted greenhouse gas, while methane (CH<sub>4</sub>) emissions almost have the same short-term impact.[^cite_note-ch4-vs-co2-5] Nitrous oxide (N<sub>2</sub>O) and fluorinated gases (F-gases) play a lesser role in comparison.

Greenhouse gas emissions are measured in [CO<sub>2</sub> equivalents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_potential#Carbon_dioxide_equivalent) determined by their [global warming potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_potential) (GWP), which depends on their lifetime in the atmosphere. Estimations largely depend on the ability of oceans and land sinks to absorb these gases. [Short-lived climate pollutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-lived_climate_pollutants) (SLCPs) including methane, [hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofluorocarbon), [tropospheric ozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropospheric_ozone) and [black carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_carbon) persist in the atmosphere for a period ranging from days to 15 years; whereas carbon dioxide can remain in the atmosphere for millennia.[^cite_note-\:1-95] Reducing SLCP emissions can cut the ongoing rate of global warming by almost half and reduce the projected [Arctic warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_warming) by two-thirds.[^cite_note-96]

Greenhouse gas emissions in 2019 were estimated at 57.4 GtCO<sub>2</sub>e, while CO<sub>2</sub> emissions alone made up 42.5 Gt including land-use change (LUC).[^cite_note-Trends2-97]

While mitigation measures for [decarbonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decarbonization) are essential on the longer term, they could result in weak near-term warming because sources of carbon emissions often also co-emit [air pollution](10934212.md). Hence, pairing measures that target carbon dioxide with measures targeting non-CO<sub>2</sub> pollutants – short-lived climate pollutants, which have faster effects on the climate, is essential for climate goals.[^cite_note-98]

### Nitrous oxide (N<sup></sup> <sub>2</sub>O)

N<sub>2</sub>O has a high GWP and significant Ozone Depleting Potential. It is estimated that the global warming potential of N<sub>2</sub>O over 100 years is 265 times greater than CO<sub>2</sub>.[^cite_note-103] For N<sub>2</sub>O, a reduction of more than 50% would be required for a stabilization.

Most emissions (56%) of nitrous oxide comes from agriculture, especially meat production: cattle (droppings on pasture), fertilizers, animal manure.[^cite_note-Olivier_2020-99]<sup>: 12 </sup>Further contributions come from combustion of fossil fuels (18%) and [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel)[^cite_note-104] as well as industrial production of [adipic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adipic_acid) and [nitric acid](21655.md).

### F-gases

Fluorinated gases include [hydrofluorocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halocarbon) (HFC), perfluorocarbons (PFC), [sulfur hexafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_hexafluoride) (SF<sub>6</sub>), and [nitrogen trifluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_trifluoride) (NF<sub>3</sub>). They are used by switchgear in the power sector, semiconductor manufacture, aluminum production and a largely unknown source of SF<sub>6</sub>.[^cite_note-Olivier_2020-99]<sup>: 38 </sup> Continued phase down of manufacture and use of HFCs under the [Kigali Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali_Amendment) to the Montreal Protocol will help reduce HFC emissions and concurrently improve the energy efficiency of appliances that use HFCs like air conditioners, freezers and other refrigeration devices.[^cite_note-105]

### Hydrogen

[Hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) leakages contribute to indirect global warming.[^cite_note-106] When hydrogen is oxidized in the atmosphere, the result is an increase in concentrations of greenhouse gases in both the troposphere and the stratosphere.[^cite_note-107] Hydrogen can leak from [hydrogen production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_production) facilities as well as any infrastructure in which hydrogen is transported, stored, or consumed.[^cite_note-108]

### Black carbon

[Black carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_carbon) is formed through the incomplete combustion of fossil fuels, [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel), and [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass). It is not a greenhouse gas but a [climate forcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_forcing) agent. Black carbon can absorb sunlight and reduce [albedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo) when deposited on snow and ice. Indirect heating can be caused by the interaction with clouds.[^cite_note-109] Black carbon stays in the atmosphere for only several days to weeks.[^cite_note-Ramanathan_&_Carmichael_2008-110] Emissions may be mitigated by upgrading coke ovens, installing particulate filters on diesel-based engines, reducing [routine flaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Routine_flaring), and minimizing open burning of biomass.

## Emissions by sector

See also: [Climate change mitigation § Mitigation by sector](2119179.md#Mitigation_by_sector)



Contributions to climate change broken down by economic sector as of 2019

Global greenhouse gas emissions can be attributed to different [sectors of the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sector). This provides a picture of the varying contributions of different types of economic activity to climate change, and helps in understanding the changes required to [mitigate climate change](2119179.md).

Greenhouse gas emissions can be divided into those that arise from the combustion of fuels to produce energy, and those generated by other processes. Around two thirds of greenhouse gas emissions arise from the combustion of fuels.[^cite_note-\:0-111]

Energy may be produced at the point of [consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_\(economics\)), or by a generator for consumption by others. Thus emissions arising from energy production may be categorized according to where they are emitted, or where the resulting energy is consumed. If emissions are attributed at the point of production, then electricity generators contribute about 25% of global greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-112] If these emissions are attributed to the final consumer then 24% of total emissions arise from manufacturing and construction, 17% from transportation, 11% from domestic consumers, and 7% from commercial consumers.[^cite_note-113] Around 4% of emissions arise from the energy consumed by the energy and fuel industry itself.

The remaining third of emissions arise from processes other than energy production. 12% of total emissions arise from agriculture, 7% from land use change and forestry, 6% from industrial processes, and 3% from waste.[^cite_note-\:0-111]

### Electricity generation

See also: [Life-cycle greenhouse gas emissions of energy sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_greenhouse_gas_emissions_of_energy_sources)



Emissions attributed to specific power stations around the world, color-coded by type of fuel used at the station. Lower half focuses on Europe and Asia

[^cite_note-Guevara2024-114]

[Coal-fired power stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal-fired_power_station) are the single largest emitter, with over 20% of global greenhouse gas emissions in 2018.[^cite_note-115] Although much less polluting than coal plants, natural [gas-fired power plants](12438990.md) are also major emitters,[^cite_note-116] taking electricity generation as a whole over 25% in 2018.[^cite_note-117] Notably, just 5% of the world's power plants account for almost three-quarters of carbon emissions from electricity generation, based on an inventory of more than 29,000 fossil-fuel power plants across 221 countries.[^cite_note-118] In the 2022 IPCC report, it is noted that providing modern energy services universally would only increase greenhouse gas emissions by a few percent at most. This slight increase means that the additional energy demand that comes from supporting decent living standards for all would be far lower than current average energy consumption.[^cite_note-119]

In March 2024, the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) (IEA) reported that in 2023, global CO<sub>2</sub> emissions from energy sources increased by 1.1%, rising by 410 million tonnes to a record 37.4 billion tonnes, primarily due to coal. Drought-related decreases in hydropower contributed to a 170 million tonne rise in emissions, which would have otherwise led to a decrease in the electricity sector's emissions.[^cite_note-\:10-120] The implementation of [clean energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_energy) technologies like [solar](13690575.md), [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), nuclear, [heat pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pump), and [electric vehicles](279350.md) since 2019 has significantly tempered emissions growth, which would have been threefold without these technologies.[^cite_note-\:10-120]

### Agriculture, forestry and land use

#### Agriculture

See also: [Methane emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_emissions)

This section is an excerpt from [Greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture\&action=edit)]

[Greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture) are large: the agriculture, forestry and land use sectors contribute between 13% and 21% of global greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture_\:0-121] *Direct* greenhouse gas emissions include those from [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and [livestock](25160767.md) farming.[^cite_note-122] *Indirect* emissions from the conversion of non-agricultural land such as [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) into agricultural land are also very important.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] With regard to direct emissions, [nitrous oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide) and [methane](18582230.md) make up over half of total greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture.[^cite_note-Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture_FAO2020-125] A 2023 review emphasizes that emissions from agricultural soils are shaped by factors such as soil type, climate, and management practices. It also highlights several mitigation strategies, including conservation tillage, precision agriculture, improved water use, and the application of biochar, that can reduce emissions and enhance soil carbon storage.[^cite_note-126]

Furthermore, there is also [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) consumption for transport and [fertilizer](37401.md) production. For example, the [manufacture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haber_process) and use of [nitrogen fertilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_fertilizer) contributes around 5% of all global greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-127] [Livestock farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impacts_of_animal_agriculture) is a major source of greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-128]

Farm animals' digestive systems can be put into two categories: [monogastric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogastric) and [ruminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruminant). Ruminant cattle for beef and dairy rank high in greenhouse gas emissions. In comparison, monogastric, or pigs and poultry-related foods, are lower. The consumption of the monogastric types may yield less emissions. Monogastric animals have a higher feed-conversion efficiency and also do not produce as much methane.[^cite_note-Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture_strategies-129] Non-ruminant livestock, such as poultry, emit much less greenhouse gas.[^cite_note-130]

##### Land-use change

Main article: [Greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_from_agriculture)

Land-use change, e.g., the clearing of forests for agricultural use, can affect the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere by altering how much carbon flows out of the atmosphere into [carbon sinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sink).[^cite_note-133] Accounting for land-use change can be understood as an attempt to measure "net" emissions, i.e., gross emissions from all sources minus the removal of emissions from the atmosphere by carbon sinks.[^cite_note-banuri-57]<sup>: 92–93 </sup>

There are substantial uncertainties in the measurement of net carbon emissions.[^cite_note-134] Additionally, there is controversy over how carbon sinks should be allocated between different regions and over time.[^cite_note-banuri-57]<sup>: 93 </sup> For instance, concentrating on more recent changes in carbon sinks is likely to favour those regions that have deforested earlier, e.g., Europe.

In [1997, human-caused Indonesian peat fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Southeast_Asian_haze) were estimated to have released between 13% and 40% of the average annual global carbon emissions caused by the burning of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

[Low-energy buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-energy_house) are designed to be highly efficient with low total energy consumption and carbon emissions—a popular type is the [passive house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_house).[^cite_note-Urge-150]

The construction industry has seen marked advances in building performance and energy efficiency over recent decades.[^cite_note-152] [Green building practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_building) that avoid emissions or capture the carbon already present in the environment, allow for reduced footprint of the construction industry, for example, use of [hempcrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hempcrete), [cellulose fiber insulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose_insulation), and [landscaping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscaping).[^cite_note-153]

According to a report by [UNEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme), the sector represents more than 35% of total global CO2 emissions.[^cite_note-154] In 2019, the building sector was responsible for 12 GtCO<sub>2</sub>-eq emissions. More than 95% of these emissions were carbon, and the remaining 5% were CH<sub>4</sub>, N<sub>2</sub>O, and halocarbon.[^cite_note-155]

The largest contributor to building sector emissions (49% of total) is the production of electricity for use in buildings.[^cite_note-\:04-156]

Of global building sector GHG emissions, 28% are produced during the manufacturing process of building materials such as [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_frame), [cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement) (a key component of [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete)),[^cite_note-157] and glass.[^cite_note-\:04-156] The conventional process inherently related to the production of steel and cement results in large amounts of CO<sub>2</sub> emitted. For example, the production of steel in 2018 was responsible for 7 to 9% of the global CO<sub>2</sub> emissions.[^cite_note-158]

The remaining 23% of global building sector GHG emissions are produced directly on site during building operations.[^cite_note-\:04-156]

#### Embodied carbon emissions in construction sector

[Embodied carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embodied_carbon_emissions), or upfront carbon emissions (UCE), are the result of creating and maintaining the materials that form a building.[^cite_note-\:03-159] As of 2018, "Embodied carbon is responsible 11% of global greenhouse gas emissions and 28% of global building sector emissions ... Embodied carbon will be responsible for almost half of total new construction emissions between now and 2050."[^cite_note-160]

GHG emissions which are produced during the mining, processing, manufacturing, transportation and installation of building materials are referred to as the *embodied carbon of a material*.[^cite_note-161] The embodied carbon of a construction project can be reduced by using low-carbon materials for building structures and finishes, reducing demolition, and reusing buildings and construction materials whenever possible.[^cite_note-\:04-156]

### Industrial processes

See also: [Environmental impact of concrete § Carbon dioxide emissions and climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_concrete#Carbon_dioxide_emissions_and_climate_change)

As of 2020 [Secunda CTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunda_CTL) is the world's largest single emitter, at 56.5 million tonnes CO<sub>2</sub> a year.[^cite_note-162]

#### Mining

[Flaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_flare) and venting of [natural gas](22131.md) in oil wells is a significant source of greenhouse gas emissions. Its contribution to greenhouse gases has declined by three-quarters in absolute terms since a peak in the 1970s of approximately 110 million metric tons/year, and in 2004 accounted for about 1/2 of one percent of all anthropogenic carbon dioxide emissions.[^cite_note-163]

The [World Bank](36752.md) estimates that 134 billion cubic meters of natural gas are flared or vented annually (2010 datum), an amount equivalent to the combined annual gas consumption of [Germany](11867.md) and [France](5843419.md) or enough to supply the entire world with gas for 16 days. This flaring is highly concentrated: 10 countries account for 70% of emissions, and twenty for 85%.[^cite_note-164]

#### Steel and aluminum

Steel and aluminum are key economic sectors where CO<sub>2</sub> is produced. According to a 2013 study, "in 2004, the steel industry along emits about 590M tons of CO<sub>2</sub>, which accounts for 5.2% of the global anthropogenic GHG emissions. CO<sub>2</sub> emitted from steel production primarily comes from energy consumption of fossil fuel as well as the use of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) to purify [iron oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_oxide)."[^cite_note-165]

More recent estimates suggest that global steel production emitted around 2,280 million tons of CO<sub>2</sub> in 2017, indicating the scale has increased significantly. A 2022 study found that across bulk material sectors—steel, aluminum, cement, and paper—total emissions reached approximately 8.4 billion tons of CO<sub>2</sub> equivalent.[^cite_note-166]

#### Plastics

Plastics are produced mainly from fossil fuels. It was estimated that between 3% and 4% of global GHG emissions are associated with plastics' life cycles.[^cite_note-167] The EPA estimates[^cite_note-168] as many as five mass units of carbon dioxide are emitted for each mass unit of [polyethylene terephthalate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene_terephthalate) (PET) produced—the type of plastic most commonly used for beverage bottles,[^cite_note-Plastic_Pollution_and_Climate_Change-169] the transportation produce greenhouse gases also.[^cite_note-What_Is_the_Carbon_Footprint_of_a_Plastic_Bottle?-170] [Plastic waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_waste) emits carbon dioxide when it degrades. In 2018 research claimed that some of the most common plastics in the environment release the greenhouse gases methane and [ethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene) when exposed to sunlight in an amount that can affect the earth climate.[^cite_note-Study_Finds_New_Reason_to_Ban_Plastic:_It_Emits_Methane_in_the_Sun-171]

Due to the lightness of plastic versus glass or metal, plastic may reduce energy consumption. For example, packaging beverages in PET plastic rather than glass or metal is estimated to save 52% in transportation energy, if the glass or metal package is [single-use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_product), of course.

In 2019 a new report "Plastic and Climate" was published. According to the report, the production and incineration of plastics will contribute in the equivalent of 850 million tonnes of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) (CO<sub>2</sub>) to the atmosphere in 2019. With the current trend, annual life cycle greenhouse gas emissions of plastics will grow to 1.34 billion tonnes by 2030. By 2050, the life cycle emissions of plastics could reach 56 billion tonnes, as much as 14 percent of the Earth's remaining [carbon budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_budget).[^cite_note-172] The report says that only solutions which involve a [reduction in consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_\(economics\)) can solve the problem, while others like biodegradable plastic, ocean cleanup, using renewable energy in plastic industry can do little, and in some cases may even worsen it.[^cite_note-173]

#### Pulp and paper

Further information: [Environmental effects of paper § Greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_effects_of_paper#Greenhouse_gas_emissions)

The global print and paper industry accounts for about 1% of global carbon dioxide emissions.[^cite_note-174] Greenhouse gas emissions from the [pulp and paper industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_and_paper_industry) are generated from the combustion of fossil fuels required for raw material production and transportation, wastewater treatment facilities, purchased power, paper transportation, printed product transportation, disposal and recycling.

### Various services

#### Digital services

See also: [Streaming media § Greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media#Greenhouse_gas_emissions), [Data center § Greenhouse gas emissions](579730.md#Greenhouse_gas_emissions), and [Cryptocurrency § Environmental effects](36662188.md#Environmental_effects)

In 2020, [data centers](579730.md) (excluding cryptocurrency mining) and data transmission each used about 1% of world electricity.[^cite_note-\:02-175] The [digital sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_economy) produces between 2% and 4% of global GHG emissions,[^cite_note-176] a large part of which is from [chipmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_device_fabrication).[^cite_note-177] However the sector reduces emissions from other sectors which have a larger global share, such as transport of people,[^cite_note-178] and possibly buildings and industry.[^cite_note-179]

Mining for [proof-of-work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof-of-work) [cryptocurrencies](36662188.md) requires enormous amounts of electricity and consequently comes with a large [carbon footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint).[^cite_note-180] Proof-of-work blockchains such as [Bitcoin](28249265.md), [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum), [Litecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litecoin), and [Monero](45714713.md) were estimated to have added between 3 million and 15 million tonnes of carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) to the atmosphere in the period from 1 January 2016 to 30 June 2017.[^cite_note-181] By the end of 2021, Bitcoin was estimated to produce 65.4 million tonnes of CO<sub>2</sub>, as much as [Greece](12108.md),[^cite_note-182] and consume between 91 and 177 terawatt-hours annually (0.3% to 0.6% of world electricity). Bitcoin is the least energy-efficient cryptocurrency, using 707.6 kilowatt-hours of electricity per transaction.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

A study in 2015 investigated the global electricity usage that can be ascribed to [Communication Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_Technology) (CT) between 2010 and 2030. Electricity usage from CT was divided into four principle categories: (i) consumer devices, including personal computers, mobile phones, TVs and home entertainment systems; (ii) network infrastructure; (iii) data center computation and storage; and lastly (iv) production of the above categories. The study estimated for the worst-case scenario, that CT electricity usage could contribute up to 23% of the globally released greenhouse gas emissions in 2030.[^cite_note-186]

#### Health care

The healthcare sector produces 4.4–4.6% of global greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-187]

Based on the 2013 life cycle emissions in the health care sector, it is estimated that the GHG emissions associated with US health care activities may cause an additional 123,000 to 381,000 DALYs annually.[^cite_note-188]

#### Water supply and sanitation

This section is an excerpt from [WASH § Reducing greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WASH#Reducing_greenhouse_gas_emissions).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=WASH\&action=edit)]

Solutions exist to reduce the greenhouse gas emissions of water and sanitation services.[^cite_note-WASH_\:110-189] These solutions into three categories which partly overlap: Firstly "reducing water and energy consumption through lean and efficient approaches"; secondly "embracing [circular economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_economy) to produce energy and valuable products"; and thirdly by "planning to reduce GHG emissions through strategic decisions".[^cite_note-WASH_IWAbook2022-190]<sup>: 28 </sup> The mentioned *lean and efficient approaches* include for example finding ways to reduce water loss from water networks and to reduce infiltration of rainwater or groundwater into sewers.[^cite_note-WASH_IWAbook2022-190]<sup>: 29 </sup> Also, incentives can to encourage households and industries to [reduce their water consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_demand_management) and their energy requirements for [water heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_heating).[^cite_note-WASH_IWAbook2022-190]<sup>: 31 </sup> There is another method to reduce the energy requirements for the treatment of raw water to make drinking water out of it: protecting the quality of the source water better.[^cite_note-WASH_IWAbook2022-190]<sup>: 32 </sup>

#### Tourism

According to UNEP, global tourism is a significant contributor to the increasing concentrations of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, with air travel accounting for a substantial share of tourism's carbon footprint—up to 70% in some countries like Iceland.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-\:11-192]

Recent studies show that tourism produces around 1 kg of CO<sub>2</sub> per dollar spent—about 25% higher than the global economic average—highlighting its substantial carbon intensity.[^cite_note-\:11-192]

### By type of energy source



Life-cycle greenhouse gas emissions of electricity supply technologies, median values calculated by [IPCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC)

[^cite_note-IPCC_2014_Annex_III-193]

This section is an excerpt from [Life-cycle greenhouse gas emissions of energy sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_greenhouse_gas_emissions_of_energy_sources).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Life-cycle_greenhouse_gas_emissions_of_energy_sources\&action=edit)]

Greenhouse gas emissions are one of the [environmental impacts of electricity generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_electricity_generation). Measurement of [life-cycle greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_greenhouse_gas_emissions_of_energy_sources) involves calculating the [global warming potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_potential) (GWP) of energy sources through [life-cycle assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_assessment). These are usually sources of only electrical energy but sometimes sources of heat are evaluated.[^cite_note-194] The findings are presented in units of global warming potential per unit of electrical energy generated by that source. The scale uses the global warming potential unit, the [carbon dioxide equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_equivalent) (CO<sub>2</sub>e), and the unit of electrical energy, the [kilowatt hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt_hour) (kWh). The goal of such assessments is to cover the full life of the source, from material and fuel mining through construction to operation and waste management.

In 2014, the [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change](15030.md) harmonized the [carbon dioxide equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_equivalent) (CO<sub>2</sub>e) findings of the major electricity generating sources in use worldwide. This was done by analyzing the findings of hundreds of individual scientific papers assessing each energy source.[^cite_note-Life-cycle_greenhouse_gas_emissions_of_energy_sources_NREL-LCA1-195] [Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) is by far the worst emitter, followed by [natural gas](22131.md), with solar, wind and nuclear all low-carbon. Hydropower, biomass, geothermal and ocean power may generally be low-carbon, but poor design or other factors could result in higher emissions from individual power stations.

The largest disproportionality was identified to be in the domain of transport, where e.g. the top 10% consume 56% of vehicle fuel and conduct 70% of vehicle purchases.[^cite_note-209] However, wealthy individuals are also often [shareholders](146081.md) and typically have more influence[^cite_note-bbc-20200618-210] and, especially in the case of [billionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire), may also direct lobbying efforts, direct financial decisions, and/or control companies.

Based on a study in 32 developed countries, researchers found that "seniors in the United States and Australia have the highest per capita footprint, twice the Western average. The trend is mainly due to changes in expenditure patterns of seniors".[^cite_note-211]

## Methods for reducing greenhouse gas emissions

See also: [Methane emissions § Approaches to reduce emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_emissions#Approaches_to_reduce_emissions)

[Governments](12229.md) have taken action to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to [mitigate climate change](2119179.md). Countries and regions listed in Annex I of the [United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change](31898.md) (UNFCCC) (i.e., the OECD and former planned economies of the Soviet Union) are required to submit periodic assessments to the UNFCCC of actions they are taking to address climate change.[^cite_note-2011_UNFCC_synthesis_of_annex_I_communications-212]<sup>: 3 </sup> Policies implemented by governments include for example national and regional targets to reduce emissions, promoting [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use), and support for an [energy transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition).

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Climate change mitigation](2119179.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Climate_change_mitigation\&action=edit)]

[Climate change mitigation](2119179.md), also called decarbonisation, is an action to limit the [greenhouse gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gases) in the atmosphere that cause [climate change](5042951.md). Climate change mitigation actions include [conserving energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) and [replacing fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out) with [clean energy sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy). Secondary mitigation strategies include changes to land use and [removing carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration) from the atmosphere.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] 2022 assessments emphasize that global greenhouse gas emissions must peak before 2025 and decline by about 43% by 2030 to limit warming to 1.5 °C, requiring rapid transitions in energy, transport, and land-use systems.[^cite_note-215]

The adopted climate change mitigation policies are insufficient, as they contribute to some changes but fail to accelerate transitions at the scale and speed required,[^cite_note-Climate_change_mitigation_Stechemesser2024-216][^cite_note-Climate_change_mitigation_Suzuki2023-217] and would still result in global warming of about 2.7 °C by 2100,[^cite_note-Climate_change_mitigation_Ritchie2022-218] significantly above the 2015 [Paris Agreement](30242372.md)'s[^cite_note-219] goal of limiting global warming to below 2 °C.[^cite_note-Climate_change_mitigation_theguardian.com-220][^cite_note-Climate_change_mitigation_United_Nations-221] Recent research shows that demand-side climate solutions—such as shifts in transportation behavior, [dietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_\(nutrition\)) change, improved building energy efficiency, and reduced material consumption—could reduce global greenhouse gas emissions by 40% to 70% by 2050 while improving human well-being.[^cite_note-222]

The model results are far from encouraging. In no case did aggregate energy-related carbon emissions ever dip below 2022 levels (see figure 3 plot). This section is an excerpt from [Climate change mitigation § Needed emissions cuts](2119179.md#Needed_emissions_cuts).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Climate_change_mitigation\&action=edit)]

The annual "Emissions Gap Report" by [UNEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme) stated in 2022 that it was necessary to almost halve emissions. "To get on track for limiting global warming to 1.5°C, global annual GHG emissions must be reduced by 45 per cent compared with emissions projections under policies currently in place in just eight years, and they must continue to decline rapidly after 2030, to avoid exhausting the limited remaining atmospheric [carbon budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_budget)."[^cite_note-Climate_change_mitigation_UNEP-2022-226]<sup>: xvi </sup> The report commented that the world should focus on broad-based economy-wide transformations and not incremental change.[^cite_note-Climate_change_mitigation_UNEP-2022-226]<sup>: xvi </sup>

In 2022, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) released its [Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report) on climate change. It warned that greenhouse gas emissions must peak before 2025 at the latest and decline 43% by 2030 to have a good chance of limiting global warming to 1.5 °C (2.7 °F).[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228] Or in the words of Secretary-General of the United Nations [António Guterres](471806.md): "Main emitters must drastically cut emissions starting this year".[^cite_note-229]

According to a joint press release published by the [German Physical society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Physical_Society) and the German meteorological society in September 2025, if the current trends will continue, global temperature can rise to 3°C above pre-industrial level by the year 2050, and by 5°C by the year 2100. The scientists emphasised the need to urgently implement already existing solutions.[^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231]

### United States



Though the U.S.'s *per capita* and per GDP emissions have declined significantly, the raw numerical decline in emissions is much less substantial.

[^cite_note-EPA_GHGemissions_1990_-235]

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Greenhouse gas emissions by the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States\&action=edit)]

The [United States](3434750.md) produced 7 billion [metric tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_tons) of [carbon dioxide equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_equivalent) greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in 2025,[^cite_note-Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States_\:2-236] the second largest in the world after [greenhouse gas emissions by China](61886740.md) and among the [countries with the highest greenhouse gas emissions per person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_greenhouse_gas_emissions_per_capita). In 2025 China is estimated to have emitted 29% of world [GHG](21350772.md), followed by the United States with 12%, then India with 7%.[^cite_note-Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States_\:2-236] In total the United States has emitted a quarter of world GHG, more than any other country.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] Annual emissions are over 15 tons per person and, amongst the top eight emitters, it is the highest [country by greenhouse gas emissions per person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_greenhouse_gas_emissions_per_capita).[^cite_note-239]

### China

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Greenhouse gas emissions by China](61886740.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_China\&action=edit)]

The total greenhouse gas emissions of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) are the world's highest, accounting for 35% of the world's total, in 2023, according to the [International Energy Agency](285292.md). The country's emissions may have peaked in 2024[^cite_note-240] or 2025.[^cite_note-241]

### India

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Climate change in India § Greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_India#Greenhouse_gas_emissions).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Climate_change_in_India\&action=edit)]

Greenhouse gas emissions by [India](14533.md) are the third largest in the world and the main source is coal.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_India_CarbonBrief-242] In 2019 [China](5405.md) is estimated to have emitted 27% of world GhG, followed by the US with 11%, then India with 6.6%.[^cite_note-243] India emitted 2.8 Gt of [CO<sub>2eq</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_equivalent) in 2016 (2.5 including [LULUCF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use,_land-use_change,_and_forestry)).[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-Climate_change_in_India_\:9-245] 79% were CO<sub>2</sub>, 14% methane and 5% [nitrous oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide).[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_India_\:9-245] India emits about 3 gigatonnes ([Gt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigatonne)) [CO<sub>2eq</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_equivalent) of [greenhouse gases](21350772.md) each year; about two tonnes per person,[^cite_note-246] which is half the world average.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_India_\:3-247] the [UNEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme) forecasts that by 2030 they will be between 3 and 4 tonnes.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_India_UN2019-248]

According to data from the Climate Change Tracker, India has contributed 169,898.56 megatonnes of carbon dioxide since 1851, accounting for 4.62% of the historic total. India's per capita emissions are rated "low", at 1.85 tonnes per capita per year. However, this reflects the country's large population which dilutes the per capita footprint despite high overall emissions.[^cite_note-249] Furthermore, India's energy use per capita increased by 11% from 2014 to 2019.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_India_\:1-250]

### Impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic

Main article: [Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the environment § Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_the_environment#Climate_change)

In 2020, carbon dioxide emissions fell by 6.4% or 2.3 billion tonnes globally.[^cite_note-\:21-251] In April 2020, [NO<sub>*x*</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOx) emissions fell by up to 30%.[^cite_note-\:18-252] In China, [lockdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdown_in_Hubei) and other measures resulted in a 26% decrease in coal consumption, and a 50% reduction in nitrogen oxide emissions.[^cite_note-\:92-253] Greenhouse gas emissions rebounded later in the pandemic as many countries began lifting restrictions, with the direct impact of pandemic policies having a negligible long-term impact on climate change.[^cite_note-\:21-251][^cite_note-effectspaper-254]
