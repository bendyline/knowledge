The **_Bundeswehr_** (German: [\[ˈbʊndəsˌveːɐ̯\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/72/De-Bundeswehr2.ogg/De-Bundeswehr2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/72/De-Bundeswehr2.ogg/De-Bundeswehr2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, lit. *Federal Defence*) are the armed forces of the [Federal Republic of Germany](11867.md). The *Bundeswehr* is divided into a military part (armed forces or *Streitkräfte*) and a civil part. The military part consists of the four armed forces: [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army), [German Navy](12960.md), [German Air and Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_and_Space_Force) and [Cyber and Information Domain Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_and_Information_Domain_Service_\(Germany\)), which are supported by the [Bundeswehr Joint Support Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeswehr_Joint_Support_Command).

As of 31 January 2026, the *Bundeswehr* had a strength of 186,221 active-duty military personnel and 81,205 civilians,[^cite_note-bundeswehr-4] placing it among the 30 largest military forces in the world, and making it the second largest in the [European Union](9317.md) behind [France](10724.md). In addition, the *Bundeswehr* has approximately 860,000 reserve personnel (2025).[^cite_note-Reserve-5] With the German military budget at \$127 billion (€108.2 billion) for 2026,[^cite_note-Haushalt_2026-7] the *Bundeswehr* is the fourth-highest-funded military in the world. Germany's defence spending had averaged approximately 1.2% of GDP over the preceding decade, well below the NATO guideline of 2%, which Germany first met in 2024.[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-11] NATO's revised spending guidelines call for 3.5% of GDP allocated to defence and an additional 1.5% to critical infrastructure resilience, a threshold endorsed at the [2025 Hague summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Hague_summit).[^cite_note-12] Germany is aiming to expand the *Bundeswehr* to around 203,000 soldiers by 2031 to better cope with increasing responsibilities.[^cite_note-auto-13]

Following concerns from the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Germany announced a major shift in policy, pledging a €100 billion (\$116.344 billion) special fund for the Bundeswehr – to remedy years of underinvestment – along with raising the budget to above 2% [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP).[^cite_note-commits-14] In 2025, the [German constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany) was amended, exempting military and [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Intelligence_Service) spending above 1% GDP from the *[Schuldenbremse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuldenbremse)* (debt limit).[^cite_note-15]

### Founding principles

The name *Bundeswehr* was first proposed by former *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* general and [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_of_Germany) politician [Hasso von Manteuffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasso_von_Manteuffel).[^cite_note-16] The [Iron Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Cross) *(Eisernes Kreuz)*, a symbol that has a long association with the military of Germany, is its official emblem. The *Schwarzes Kreuz* is derived from the [black cross insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Cross_\(Teutonic_Order\)) of the medieval [Teutonic knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_knights); since 1813 the symbol has been used to denote a military decoration for all ranks.

When the *Bundeswehr* was established in 1955, its founding principles were based on developing a completely new military force for the defence of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany). In this respect the *Bundeswehr* did not consider itself to be a successor to either the *[Reichswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichswehr)* (1921–1935) of the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) or [Hitler's](2731583.md) *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* (1935–1945), and did not adhere to the traditions of any former German military organization. Its official ethos is based on three major themes:[^cite_note-17]

- The aims of the military reformers at the beginning of the 19th century such as [Scharnhorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_von_Scharnhorst), [Gneisenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Neidhardt_von_Gneisenau), and [Clausewitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_von_Clausewitz)

- The conduct displayed by members of the [military resistance against Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_resistance_to_Nazism), especially the attempt of [Claus von Stauffenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_von_Stauffenberg) and [Henning von Tresckow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henning_von_Tresckow) to assassinate him.

- Its own military traditions set in 1955.



Generals [Adolf Heusinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Heusinger) and [Hans Speidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Speidel) being sworn into the newly founded *Bundeswehr* by [Theodor Blank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Blank) on 12 November 1955



A *[Großer Zapfenstreich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro%C3%9Fer_Zapfenstreich)* at the Federal Ministry of Defense in Bonn in 2002

One of the most visible traditions of the modern *Bundeswehr* is the *[Großer Zapfenstreich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro%C3%9Fer_Zapfenstreich)*. This is a form of [military tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tattoo) that has its origins in the *[landsknecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsknecht)* era. The [FRG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) reinstated this formal military ceremony in 1952, three years before the foundation of the *Bundeswehr*. Today it is performed by a military band with 4 fanfare [trumpeters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpet) and [timpani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timpani), a corps of drums, up to two escort companies of the *Bundeswehr*'s *[Wachbataillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wachbataillon)* (or another deputized unit) and torchbearers. The *Zapfenstreich* is only performed during national celebrations or solemn public commemorations. It can honour distinguished persons present such as the German Federal President, or provide the conclusion to large military exercises.

Another important tradition in the modern German armed forces is the *[Gelöbnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_oath_of_the_Bundeswehr)*: the solemn oath made by serving professional soldiers, and recruits ([and formerly conscripts](1285284.md)) during basic training. There are two kinds of oath: a pledge for recruits, and a solemn vow for full-time personnel.

The pledge is made annually on 20 July, the date on which a group of *Wehrmacht* officers attempted to [assassinate Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_20_plot) in 1944. Recruits from the *Bundeswehr*'s *Wachbataillon* make their vow at the [Bendlerblock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendlerblock) in Berlin. This was the headquarters of the resistance and also where the officers were summarily executed following the failure of the assassination attempt on Hitler. National commemorations are held nearby within the grounds of the [*Reichstag*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_\(building\)). Similar events also take place across the German Republic. Since 2011, when conscription was suspended, the wording of the ceremonial vow for full-time recruits and volunteer personnel is:

"*Ich gelobe, der Bundesrepublik Deutschland treu zu dienen und das Recht und die Freiheit des deutschen Volkes tapfer zu verteidigen.*"&#x20;

"I pledge to serve the Federal Republic of Germany loyally and to defend the right and the freedom of the German people bravely."

Serving *Bundeswehr* personnel replace "*Ich gelobe*, ..." with "*Ich schwöre*, ..." ("I swear...").

### Cold War: 1955–1990

See also: [French–German enmity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French%E2%80%93German_enmity)



The Federal Republic of Germany joined [NATO](21133.md) in 1955.

After [World War II](32927.md) the responsibility for the security of Germany as a whole rested with the four occupying [Allied Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II): the United States, the United Kingdom, France and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). Germany had been without armed forces since the *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* was dissolved following World War II. When the Federal Republic of Germany was founded in 1949, it was without a military. Germany remained completely demilitarized and any plans for a German military were forbidden by Allied regulations.

Some naval [mine-sweeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minesweeping) units such as the [German Mine Sweeping Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Mine_Sweeping_Administration) (*Deutscher Minenräumdienst*) continued to exist, but they remained unarmed and under Allied control and did not serve as a national defence force. The [Federal Border Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesgrenzschutz) (*Bundesgrenzschutz*), a mobile, lightly armed police force of 10,000 men, was formed on 14 March 1951 and expanded to 20,000 men on 19 June 1953. A proposal to integrate West German troops with soldiers of France, Belgium, the Netherlands, [Luxembourg](17515.md) and Italy in a [European Defence Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Defence_Community) was proposed but never implemented.

There was a discussion among the United States, the United Kingdom and France over the issue of a revived (West) German military. In particular, France was reluctant to allow Germany to rearm in light of recent history (Germany had invaded France twice in living memory, in [World War I](4764461.md) and [World War II](32927.md), and also defeated France in the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War) of 1870/71). However, after the project for a [European Defence Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Defence_Community) failed in the [French National Assembly](299616.md) in 1954, France agreed to West German accession to [NATO](21133.md) and rearmament.



[Leopard 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_2) tanks

With growing tensions between the Soviet Union and the West, especially after the [Korean War](16772.md), this policy was to be revised. While the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic) (East Germany) was already secretly rearming, the seeds of a new West German force started in 1950 when former high-ranking German officers were tasked by Chancellor [Konrad Adenauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer) to discuss the options for [West German rearmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_German_rearmament). The results of a meeting in the monastery of [Himmerod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himmerod_Abbey) formed the conceptual base to build the new armed forces in West Germany.

The *[Amt Blank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amt_Blank)* (Blank Agency, named after its director [Theodor Blank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Blank)), the predecessor of the later [Federal Ministry of Defence](2563857.md), was formed the same year to prepare the establishment of the future forces. [Hasso von Manteuffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasso_von_Manteuffel), a former general of the *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* and [Free Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_\(Germany\)) politician, submitted the name *Bundeswehr* for the new forces. This name was later confirmed by the West German *Bundestag*.

The *Bundeswehr* was officially established on the 200th birthday of [Scharnhorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_von_Scharnhorst) on 12 November 1955. In personnel and education terms, the most important initial feature of the new German armed forces was to be their orientation as citizen defenders of a democratic state, fully subordinate to the political leadership of the country.[^cite_note-Erler_Politik_1962-18] A personnel screening committee was created to make sure that the future colonels and generals of the armed forces were those whose political attitude and experience would be acceptable to the new democratic state.[^cite_note-Abenheim_CitizenInUniform_1990-19] There were a few key reformers, such as General [Ulrich de Maiziere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_de_Maiziere), General [Graf von Kielmansegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_von_Kielmansegg), and [Graf von Baudissin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Graf_von_Baudissin),[^cite_note-Bogowicz_PolishArmedForces_2000-20] who reemphasised some of the more democratic parts of Germany's armed forces history in order to establish a solid civil-military basis to build upon.

A flying MiG 29 seen from above. The cross-shaped roundel of the Luftwaffe is painted on the left wing.

The *Bundeswehr* was the first NATO member to use the Soviet-built [MiG 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG_29) jet, taken over from the former [East German Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Forces_of_the_National_People's_Army) after reunification.

After an amendment of the [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany) in 1955, West Germany became a member of NATO. The first public military review took place at [Andernach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andernach), in January 1956.[^cite_note-Large_GermansToFront_1996-21] In 1956, [conscription](5735.md) for all men between the ages of 18 and 45 was reintroduced, later augmented by a civil alternative with longer duration (see [Conscription in Germany](1285284.md)). In response, [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) formed its own military force, the *[Nationale Volksarmee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Army)* (NVA), in 1956, with conscription being established only in 1962. The *[Nationale Volksarmee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Army)* was eventually dissolved with the [reunification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Germany) in 1990. Compulsory conscription was suspended – but not completely abolished as an alternative – in January 2011.

During the [Cold War](325329.md) the *Bundeswehr* was the backbone of NATO's conventional defence in [Central Europe](5188.md). It had a strength of 495,000 military and 170,000 civilian personnel. Although Germany had smaller armed forces than France and the United States, Cold War historian [John Lewis Gaddis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis_Gaddis) assesses the *Bundeswehr* as "perhaps (the) world's best army".[^cite_note-Gaddis_ColdWar_2005-22] The Army consisted of three corps with 12 divisions, most of them heavily armed with tanks and APCs. The *Luftwaffe* owned significant numbers of tactical combat aircraft and took part in [NATO's integrated air defence (NATINAD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATINAD). The Navy was tasked and equipped to defend the [Baltic Approaches](3335.md), to provide escort reinforcement and resupply shipping in the [North Sea](21179.md) and to contain the [Soviet Baltic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet).

Further information: [Operation Summer Rain (Cold War)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Summer_Rain_\(Cold_War\))

During the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) in the 1980s, German special forces of the Bundeswehr were deployed as part of a covert operation. During this time, [Operation Summer Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Summer_Rain_\(Cold_War\)) played a significant role. The [German Federal Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Federal_Intelligence_Service) (BND) conducted this secret mission, where special forces were infiltrated from [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) to [Pakistan](23235.md) and then into [Afghanistan](737.md).

The primary task of these special units was to clandestinely acquire Soviet weapon technology, including armor for combat helicopters, vehicles, landmines, modern ammunition such as uranium rounds, rocket warheads, night vision devices, and navigation technology. Collaboration with the insurgent Mujahideen was central to this covert operation.

During this time the *Bundeswehr* did not take part in combat operations. However, there were a number of large-scale training exercises resulting in operational casualties. The first such incident was in June 1957, when 15 paratroop recruits drowned in the [Iller river, Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iller).[^cite_note-23]

### German Reunification 1990



At a festive event on German Unity Day, Federal Defense Minister [Gerhard Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Stoltenberg) takes over command of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Army) of the former GDR.

At the time of reunification, the German military boasted a manpower of some 585,000 soldiers.[^cite_note-auto-13] As part of the [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) process, under the [Treaty on the Final Settlement with Respect to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Final_Settlement_with_Respect_to_Germany) (Two-Plus Four Treaty), which paved the way for reunification, the *Bundeswehr* was to be reduced to 370,000 personnel, of whom no more than 345,000 were to be in the Army and Air Force.[^cite_note-Bundesregierung_ArmeeVerkleinerung_1990-24] This would be Germany's contribution to the [Treaty on Conventional Armed Forces in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_Conventional_Armed_Forces_in_Europe), and the restrictions would enter into force at the time the CFE treaty would. As a result, the *Bundeswehr* was significantly reduced, and the former East German *[Nationale Volksarmee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationale_Volksarmee)* (NVA) was disbanded at the moment of [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification), with a portion of its personnel and materiel being absorbed into the *Bundeswehr*.

A [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) of the [*Luftwaffe*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force)

About 50,000 *Volksarmee* personnel were integrated into the *Bundeswehr* on 2 October 1990. This figure was rapidly reduced as conscripts and short-term volunteers completed their service. A number of senior officers (but no generals or admirals) received limited contracts for up to two years to continue daily operations. Personnel remaining in the *Bundeswehr* were awarded new contracts and new ranks, dependent on their individual qualification and experience. Many were granted and accepted a lower rank than previously held in the *Volksarmee*.

In general, the unification process of the two militaries – under the slogan "*Armee der Einheit*" (or "Army of Unity") – has been seen publicly as a major success and an example for other parts of the society.

With the reduction, a large amount of the [military hardware](90815.md) of the *Bundeswehr*, as well as of the *Volksarmee*, had to be disposed of. Most of the [armoured vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_armour) and [fighter jet aircraft](10929.md) (the *Bundesluftwaffe* – due to reunification – was the only air force in the world that flew both Phantoms and MIGs) were dismantled under international disarmament procedures. Many ships were scrapped or sold, often to the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) or [Indonesia](14579.md) (the latter received 39 former *Volksmarine* vessels of various types).

With reunification, all restrictions on the manufacture and possession of conventional arms that had been imposed on the *Bundeswehr* as a condition for West German rearmament were lifted.[^cite_note-Duffield_WorldPowerForsaken_1998-25]

Since 1996, Germany also has its own [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces), the *[Kommando Spezialkräfte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kommando_Spezialkr%C3%A4fte)* (Special Forces Command). It was formed after German citizens had to be rescued from the [Rwandan genocide](676659.md) by [Belgian Para-Commandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immediate_Reaction_Cell) as the [Special Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSG_9) of the [Federal Police](1826396.md) were not capable of operating in a war zone.

### Reorientation

A major event for the German military was a series of defense spending cuts and the suspension of the compulsory [conscription](1285284.md) for men in 2011. These were introduced by Chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) and Finance Minister [Wolfgang Schäuble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Sch%C3%A4uble) as part of [austerity](684037.md) measures in response to the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession) and the [European debt crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_debt_crisis).[^cite_note-26] In 2011/12, a major reform of the *Bundeswehr* was announced under [Thomas de Maizière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_de_Maizi%C3%A8re), further limiting the number of military bases and soldiers. The land forces of the Bundeswehr would have three large units at divisional level. There are currently five. The number of brigades decreased from eleven to eight.[^cite_note-zukunft-27]

German military expenditures are lower than comparable countries such as the United Kingdom, or countries of the [European Union](9317.md) such as France, especially when taking into account Germany's larger population and economy. This discrepancy is often criticized by Germany's [NATO](21133.md) allies, as far back as [Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama)-era [US Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Secretary_of_Defense) [Robert M. Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Gates).[^cite_note-nytdim-28][^cite_note-ustsp-29]

As one result of the [2014 NATO Wales summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NATO_Wales_summit) which was attended by both Merkel and [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) in September 2014,[^cite_note-alozius21-30] the *Bundeswehr* acknowledged in October chronic equipment problems that rendered its armed forces "unable to deliver its defensive NATO promises". Among the problems cited were dysfunctional weapons systems, armored vehicles, aircraft, and naval vessels unfit for immediate service due to a neglect of maintenance, and serious equipment and spare parts shortages. The situation was so dire in 2016 that it was acknowledged that most of Germany's [fighter aircraft](10929.md) and [combat helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopter) were not in deployable condition,[^cite_note-foreignaffairs-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] although the Air Force had almost 38,000 soldiers,[^cite_note-zukunft-27] and von der Leyen's daycare system.[^cite_note-hug1-34][^cite_note-hug2-35]

In 2015, as a result of the [Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), Germany announced what was termed "a major increase" in defense spending. In May 2015, the German government approved an increase in defense spending, at the time 1.3% of GDP, by 6.2% over the following five years, allowing the Ministry of Defense to fully modernize the army.[^cite_note-36] The 2015 reform set a required strength of 185,000 soldiers.[^cite_note-Chambers02072017-37] Plans were also announced to significantly expand the tank fleet to a potential number of 328, order 131 more [Boxer armored personnel carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_\(armoured_fighting_vehicle\)), increase the submarine fleet, and to develop a new fighter jet to replace the [Panavia Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Germany considered increasing the size of the army,[^cite_note-Chambers02072017-37] and in May 2016 it announced it would spend €130 billion on new equipment by 2030 and add nearly 7,000 soldiers by 2023 in the first German military expansion since the end of the Cold War.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] In February 2017, the German government announced another expansion, which would increase the number of its professional soldiers by 20,000 by 2024.[^cite_note-44]

As of May 2025, the Bundeswehr is permanently stationing a full armored brigade abroad for the first time in its postwar history. The [45th Panzer Brigade "Litauen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Panzer_Brigade_\(Bundeswehr\)) based in Lithuania, is part of Germany's broader *Zeitenwende* strategy to reinforce NATO's eastern flank and transition from rotational deployments to structural forward presence.[^cite_note-\:1-45] The brigade is expected to include 2,000 personnel by 2026, with additional supporting infrastructure provided jointly with Lithuania.

### Coordination with European partners

As a consequence of improved Dutch-German cooperation, since 2014 two of the three [Royal Netherlands Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Army) Brigades are under German Command. In 2014, the [11th Airmobile Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airmobile_Brigade_\(Netherlands\)) was integrated into the German Division of fast forces (DSK). The Dutch [43rd Mechanized Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_Mechanized_Brigade_\(Netherlands\)) will be assigned to the [1st *Panzer* Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Panzer_Division_\(Bundeswehr\)) of the German army, with the integration starting at the beginning of 2016, and the unit becoming operational at the end of 2019.[^cite_note-janes16-46] In February 2016 it was announced that the [*Seebatallion*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Battalion_\(Germany\)) of the German Navy would start to operate under [Royal Dutch Navy](198478.md) command.[^cite_note-duint-47] The Dutch-German military cooperation was seen in 2016 by von der Leyen and Dutch Minister of Defence [Jeanine Hennis-Plasschaert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeanine_Hennis-Plasschaert) as an example for setting up a European defense union.[^cite_note-duint-47]

According to a policy dictated by von der Leyen in February 2017, the *Bundeswehr* is to play a greater role as "anchor army" for smaller [NATO](21133.md) states, by improving coordination between its divisions and smaller members' [Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade).[^cite_note-anchorarmy-48]

A further proposal by Minister of Defence [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), to allow non-German [EU nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_European_Union) to join the *Bundeswehr*, was met in July 2016 by strong opposition, even from her own party.[^cite_note-49]

It was announced in February 2017 that the [Czech Republic](5321.md)'s [4th Rapid Deployment Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Rapid_Deployment_Brigade_\(Czech_Republic\)) and [Romania](25445.md)'s [81st Mechanized Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81st_Mechanized_Brigade_\(Romania\)) would be integrated into Germany's [10 Armoured Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Armoured_Division_\(Bundeswehr\)) and Rapid Response Forces Division.[^cite_note-czroint-50] The 4 RDB-10 PD link is not an isolated Czech–German initiative.[^cite_note-51] It sits beside:

- Netherlands – three brigades integrated into German divisions since 2016.

- Romania – 9th Mechanised Brigade tied to the Bundeswehr's Rapid Response Forces Division.

- Lithuania (2025) – standing up Panzer Brigade 45 under 10 PD for the new German permanent brigade in Rūdninkai.

Taken together, Germany is slowly contributing to a pre-integrated divisional structure.

### Consequences of 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine

As of 31 December 2022, the number of active military personnel in the *Bundeswehr* was 183,051.[^cite_note-bundeswehr-4] Military expenditure in Germany was at \$52.8 billion in 2020, roughly 1.3% of the country's GDP.[^cite_note-SIPRI-2020-52]

At the end of February 2022, in light of [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) announced a plan to increase the power of the German military, pledging €100 billion (\$112.7 billion) of the 2022 budget for the armed forces and repeating his promise to reach the 2% of gross domestic product spending on defense in line with (as editorialized by *[Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle)*) NATO requirements.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-commits-14]

According to information from defense politicians of the federal German parliament, representatives of the armaments industry and other experts, in October 2022 the Bundeswehr only had enough ammunition in stock for one or two days during wartime.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] A report made by the Ministry of Defence revealed problems in the *Bundeswehr* such as limited preparedness and lack of equipment. In the letter accompanying the report which was sent to the federal [German parliament](3768.md), the Minister of Defence noted that the situation would improve but "closing the gaps takes time".[^cite_note-SZ_BundeswehrSchwaechen_2022-56]

In 2025, the new government under Chancellor [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) passed laws to further significantly expand the Bundeswehr, both in materiel and personnel. The annual military budget of Germany is to grow from \~2% of its GDP in 2025 to 3.5% in 2029.[^cite_note-BMF_Budget2026_2025-57] In October 2025, the Federal Ministry of Defence consolidated its procurement planning into a single framework totalling approximately €377 billion across roughly 320 programmes spanning air, naval, land, air defence, ammunition, and C4ISR domains.[^cite_note-Grosswald_IndustrialDeterrence_2025-58] A new push to gain more volunteers and mechanisms for compulsory drafting in case these efforts are insufficient ("[Nordic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_model)") have been announced to come into force on 1 January 2026. The goal is to reach a number of 260,000 of active Bundeswehr personnel, a 30% increase from its 2025 number.[^cite_note-Tagesschau_Wehrdienst_2025-59]

In April 2026, Germany unveiled its first standalone military strategy since the Second World War, titled "Verantwortung für Europa" (Responsibility for Europe), presented by Defence Minister Boris Pistorius on 22 April. The strategy package included a new capability profile, a personnel growth plan, a revised reserve strategy, and an administrative reform agenda of 153 concrete measures. Russia was identified as the primary threat, and the strategy adopted a "one theatre approach" treating NATO territory, the Middle East, and the Indo-Pacific as interconnected security spaces.[^cite_note-60]

## Organisation

### History of organisation

With the growing number of missions abroad it was recognized that the *Bundeswehr* required a new command structure. A reform commission under the chairmanship of the former President [Richard von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_von_Weizs%C3%A4cker) presented its recommendations in spring 2000.

In October 2000 the [Joint Support Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Support_Service_\(Germany\)), the *[Streitkräftebasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streitkr%C3%A4ftebasis)*, was established to concentrate logistics and other supporting functions such as military police, supply and communications under one command. 2025, the Joint Support Service was dissolved and its commands and departments reassigned to the [Joint Support Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeswehr_Joint_Support_Command). Medical support was reorganised with the establishment of the [Joint Medical Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeswehr_Joint_Medical_Service). In 2016, the *Bundeswehr* created its youngest branch the [Cyber and Information Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_and_Information_Space_Command_\(Germany\)).

### Senior leadership

The Minister of Defence is supported by the Chief of Defense (CHOD, *[Generalinspekteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_General_of_the_Bundeswehr)*) and the service chiefs (*Inspekteure*: [Inspector of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_of_the_Army), [Inspector of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_of_the_Air_Force_\(Germany\)), [Inspector of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_of_the_Navy)) and their respective staffs in his or her function as commander-in-chief. The CHOD and the service chiefs form the Military Command Council (*Militärischer Führungsrat*) with functions similar to those of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) in the United States. Subordinate to the CHOD is the [Armed Forces Operational Command (*OpFüKdoBw*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeswehr_Operational_Command). For smaller missions one of the service HQs (e.g. the Fleet Command) may exercise command and control of forces in missions abroad. The *[Bundestag](3768.md)* must approve any foreign deployment by a simple majority. This has led to some discontent with Germany's allies about troop deployments e.g. in Afghanistan since parliamentary consent over such issues is relatively hard to achieve in Germany.

### Combat forces

The combat forces of the Army are organised into three combat [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\)) and participate in multi-national command structures at the [corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps) level. The Air and Space Force maintains three divisions and the Navy is structured into two flotillas. The Joint Support Command and the Joint Medical Service are both organized in four regional commands of identical structure. All of these services also have general commands for training, procurement, and other general issues.



A [German Navy](12960.md) [Sachsen-class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsen-class_frigate)

### Operational Command

The Armed Forces Operational Command (*[Einsatzführungskommando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzf%C3%BChrungskommando) der Bundeswehr*) is the only joint military command of the Bundeswehr. It controls all missions abroad. The command is located at Henning von Tresckow Kaserne ([Schwielowsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwielowsee)) near [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam) and is headed by a [Generalleutnant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalleutnant) (3-star general).

### Mission



[German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army) soldiers [in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)) in front of [Dingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATF_Dingo) infantry mobility vehicles, 2009

The role of the *Bundeswehr* is described in the [Constitution of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Germany) (Art. 87a) as absolutely defensive only. Its only active role before 1990 was the *Katastropheneinsatz* (disaster control). Within the *Bundeswehr*, it helped after natural disasters both in Germany and abroad. After 1990, the international situation changed from east–west confrontation to one of general uncertainty and instability.

After a ruling of the [Federal Constitutional Court](34781059.md) in 1994 the term "defence" has been defined to not only include protection of the borders of Germany, but also crisis reaction and conflict prevention, or more broadly as guarding the security of Germany anywhere in the world.[^cite_note-61] According to the definition given by Defence Minister [Peter Struck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Struck_\(politician\)) (2002 to 2005), it may be necessary to defend Germany even at the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush). This requires the *Bundeswehr* to take part in operations outside of the borders of Germany, as part of NATO or the [European Union](9317.md) and mandated by the UN.

## Appearance

### Uniforms

See also: [Military uniform § Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_uniform#Germany)



German Army signallers in service uniforms

The service uniform is theoretically the standard type of *Bundeswehr* uniform for general duty and off-post activity,[^cite_note-loc-87] but is most associated with ceremonial occasions. The army's service uniform consists of a light grey, single-breasted coat and darker grey trousers, worn with a light blue shirt, black tie, and black shoes.[^cite_note-loc-87] The peaked, visored cap has been replaced by the [beret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_beret#Germany) as the most common form of headgear.[^cite_note-loc-87] Dress uniforms featuring dinner jackets or double-breasted coats are worn by officers for various social occasions.[^cite_note-loc-87]

The battle and work uniform consists of *[Flecktarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flecktarn)* camouflage fatigues, which are also worn on field duty. In practice, they are also used for general duty and off-post at least at barracks where there is also field duty even by others, and for the way home or to the post, and generally regarded as the *Heer* uniform.[^cite_note-88] In all three services, light sand-coloured uniforms are available for duty in warmer climates.[^cite_note-loc-87] In 2016 a new *[Multitarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multitarn)* pattern was launched, similar to the [MultiCam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiCam) uniforms of the [British Army](4887.md) or [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army).[^cite_note-Bundeswehr_Multitarn_2016-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

A different, traditional variety of the service uniform is worn by the *[Gebirgsjäger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebirgsj%C3%A4ger)* (mountain infantry), consisting of ski jacket, stretch trousers, and ski boots. Instead of the beret, they wear the grey "mountain cap". The field uniform is the same, except for the (optional) metal *[Edelweiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edelweiss)* worn on the forage cap.



A German infantryman stands at the ready with his [Heckler & Koch G36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_G36) during a practice exercise in 2004 as U.S. troops watch in the background. All rifles in the photo are equipped with [blank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_\(cartridge\)) firing adapters.

The traditional arm-of-service colours appear as lapel facings and as piping on shoulder straps.[^cite_note-loc-87] Generals wear an inner piping of gold braid; other officers wear silver piping.[^cite_note-loc-87] Lapel facings and piping are maroon for general staff, green for infantry, red for artillery, pink for armour, black for engineers, yellow for communications, dark yellow for reconnaissance and various other colors for the remaining branches.[^cite_note-loc-87] Combat troops wear green (infantry), black (armour), or [maroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroon_beret) (airborne) berets.[^cite_note-loc-87] Logistics troops and combat support troops, such as artillery or engineers, wear red berets.[^cite_note-loc-87] A gold or silver badge on the beret denotes the individual branch of service.[^cite_note-loc-87]

The Navy wear the traditional navy blue, double-breasted coat and trousers; enlisted personnel wear either a white shirt or a navy blue shirt with the traditional navy collar.[^cite_note-loc-87] White uniforms provide an alternative for summer.[^cite_note-loc-87] The officer's dress cap is mounted with a gold anchor surrounded by a wreath.[^cite_note-loc-87] The visor of the admiral's cap bears a double row of oak leaves.[^cite_note-loc-87] [U-boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat) captains wear the traditional white hat.

The Air and Space Force service uniform consists of a blue jacket and trousers with a light blue shirt, dark blue tie, and black shoes.[^cite_note-loc-87] Olive battle dress similar to the army fatigue uniform is worn in basic training and during other field duty.[^cite_note-loc-87] Flying personnel wear [wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator_badge#After_1955) on their right breast.[^cite_note-loc-87] Other Air and Space Force personnel wear a modified wing device with a symbol in its centre denoting service specialisation.[^cite_note-loc-87] These *Tätigkeitsabzeichen* come in bronze, silver, or gold, depending on one's length of service in the specialty.[^cite_note-loc-87] Wings, superimposed over a wreath, in gold, silver, or bronze, depending on rank, are also worn on the service or field cap.[^cite_note-loc-87]

### Ranks

Main article: [Command and obedience in the Bundeswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_obedience_in_the_Bundeswehr)

In general, officer ranks are those used in the Prussian and pre-1945 German armies. Officer rank insignia are worn on shoulder straps or shoulder boards.[^cite_note-loc-87] Army (*Heer*) and Air and Space Force (*Luftwaffe*) junior officers' insignia are four pointed silver stars while field grade officers wear silver (black or white on camouflage uniforms) stars and an oak wreath around the lowest star. The stars and wreath are gold for general officers. In the case of naval (*Marine*) officers, rank is indicated by gold stripes on the lower sleeve of the blue service jacket and on shoulder boards of the white uniform.[^cite_note-loc-87]

Soldier and NCO ranks are similar to those of the Prussian and pre-1945 German armies. In the army and air force, a *[Gefreiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gefreiter)* corresponds to the NATO rank OR-2 and *[Obergefreiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obergefreiter)* as well as *[Hauptgefreiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauptgefreiter)* to OR-3, while OR-4 stands for *[Stabsgefreiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabsgefreiter)* and *[Oberstabsgefreiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberstabsgefreiter)*. An *[Unteroffizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unteroffizier)* is the lowest-ranking sergeant (OR-5), followed by *Stabsunteroffizier* (also OR-5), *[Feldwebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldwebel)* and *[Oberfeldwebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberfeldwebel)* (OR-6), *[Hauptfeldwebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauptfeldwebel)* (OR-7/8), *Stabsfeldwebel* (OR-8) and *Oberstabsfeldwebel* (OR-9). Ranks of army and air and space force enlisted personnel are designated by stripes, chevrons, and "sword knots" worn on rank slides.[^cite_note-loc-87]

Naval enlisted rank designations are worn on the upper (OR 1–5) or lower (OR-6 and above) sleeve along with a symbol based on an anchor for the service specialization (rating).[^cite_note-loc-87] Army and air force officer candidates hold the separate ranks of *[Fahnenjunker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahnenjunker)* (OR-5), *[Fähnrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4hnrich)* (OR-6) and *Oberfähnrich* (OR-7/8), and wear the appropriate rank insignia plus a silver cord bound around it. Officers candidates in the navy *Seekadett* (sea cadet; equivalent to OR-5) and *Fähnrich zur See* (midshipman second class; OR-6) wear the rank insignia of the respective enlisted ranks but with a gold star instead of the rating symbol, while an *Oberfähnrich zur See* (midshipman first class; OR-7/8) wears an officer type thin rank stripe.

Medical personnel of all three services wear a version of the traditional caduceus (staff with entwined serpents) on their shoulder straps or sleeve.[^cite_note-loc-87] The officers' ranks have own designations differing from the line officers, the rank insignias however are basically the same.

### Women

Women have served in the medical service since 1975. From 1993 they were also allowed to serve as enlisted personnel and non-commissioned officers in the medical service and the [army bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_music). In 2000, in a lawsuit brought up by [Tanja Kreil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanja_Kreil), the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) issued a ruling allowing women to serve in more roles than previously allowed. Since 2001 they can serve in all functions of service without restriction, but they are not subject to conscription. There are presently around 25,417 women on active duty[^cite_note-bundeswehr-4] and a number of female reservists who take part in all duties including [peacekeeping](84617.md) missions and other operations. In 1994, [Verena von Weymarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verena_von_Weymarn) became *Generalarzt der Luftwaffe* (Surgeon General of the Air Force), the first woman ever to reach the rank of general in the armed forces of Germany.

For women, lower physical performance requirements are required in the basic fitness test, which must be completed at the time of recruitment and later on annually.[^cite_note-92]

## Recruitment

With the suspension of compulsory [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) service in 2011 and the reorientation of the Bundeswehr, the military district recruiting offices were dissolved effective 30 November 2012. Their tasks were taken over by the newly created career centers of the Bundeswehr. The career centers of the Bundeswehr are the armed forces main way of presenting itself as a nationwide employer for both military and civilian careers.[^cite_note-96]

In the structure of the Bundeswehr's personnel recruitment organization adopted in 2019, there are five large career centers in [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz), [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf), [Munich](19058.md) and [Berlin](3354.md) with assessment centers. There are 16 smaller, regional career centers, of which only those in [Wilhelmshaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmshaven), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) and [Erfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erfurt) have an assessment center. The 110 career counseling offices belonging to the career centers are combined with 86 location teams of the career development service to form 113 counseling offices.[^cite_note-97] The Bundeswehr offers numerous career paths:

- Voluntary military service (FWD) in Germany is an employment relationship for soldiers in a career of the lower rank Bundeswehr personnel. It lasts at least 7 and at most 23 months. Its legal status is similar to that of conscripts.

  [^cite_note-98]

- A temporary soldier (abbreviated SaZ, colloquially called Zeitsoldat) is a soldier who voluntarily agrees to perform military service for a limited time. A SaZ can enter all three categories (enlisted, non-commissioned officers and officers). SaZ recruited as NCOs and officers undergo general military, career and specialty training. The regular commitment period is a minimum of 2 and a maximum of 25 years, but may not extend beyond the age of 62.

  [^cite_note-99]

  [^cite_note-100]

- Professional soldiers for life (*Berufssoldat*) are selected from the group of temporary soldiers. In contrast to temporary soldiers, professional soldiers don't have contractual commitment periods but serve until retirement. The age of retirement varies with rank. It is possible for a professional soldier to ask for early discharge or to revert to temporal service.

- Career in the Reserve: There are multiple career paths in the reserve of the armed forces for officers, NCOs, and enlisted personnel, as well as for civilians who have no prior military training.

  [^cite_note-101]
