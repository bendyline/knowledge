The **Kentucky Derby** ([/ˈdɜːrbi/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is an American [Grade I stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_stakes_race) [race](67745.md) run at [Churchill Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Downs) in [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky). The race is run by three-year-old [Thoroughbreds](18842022.md) at a distance of 1+1⁄4 miles (10 furlongs; 2,012 metres). [Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt_\(horse\)) and [geldings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelding) carry 126 pounds (57 kilograms) and [fillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillies) 121 pounds (55 kilograms).[^cite_note-3]

Held annually on the first Saturday in May, the Derby is the first leg of the [Triple Crown](19116721.md) and will be the first race in the planned [Thoroughbred Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_Championship_Series?action=edit\&redlink=1) from Churchill Downs and New York Racing Association. It is preceded by the two-week-long [Kentucky Derby Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Derby_Festival).[^cite_note-4] The race is known as "The Run for the Roses", as the winning horse is draped in a blanket of roses.[^cite_note-Kleber-5][^cite_note-Rossingh-6] Lasting approximately two minutes, the Derby has been alternately called "The Most Exciting Two Minutes in Sports",[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] "The Fastest Two Minutes in Sports",[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] or "The Greatest Two Minutes in Sports", coined by Churchill Downs president [Matt Winn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Winn).[^cite_note-Sowers-11][^cite_note-12] At least two of these descriptions are thought to be derived from the words of sportswriter [Grantland Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grantland_Rice), when in 1935 he said "Those two minutes and a second or so of derby running carry more emotional thrills, per second, than anything sport can show."[^cite_note-Rossingh-6][^cite_note-13]

The race was first run in 1875. Unlike the other, older races of the Triple Crown—the [Preakness Stakes](62370.md) and the [Belmont Stakes](62398.md)—along with the [Travers Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travers_Stakes) (the oldest comparable stakes race in the US), the Kentucky Derby and its sibling race, the [Kentucky Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Oaks), have been run every year since inception. Further, the Derby and the Oaks are the oldest major sporting events in the US held annually since their beginning, although they were twice rescheduled within the same year, the first time due to [World War II](32927.md) in [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_Kentucky_Derby), and the second time due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Kentucky_Derby).[^cite_note-Kleber-5][^cite_note-14] Among thoroughbred stakes races, they are the oldest that have been held annually on the same track every year.[^cite_note-Kleber-5]

The Derby is the most-watched and [most-attended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_thoroughbred_racing_top_attended_events) horse race in the United States. The [152nd](82792534.md) took place on Saturday, May 2, 2026, with [Golden Tempo](83090873.md) winning the event.

## History

See also: [Churchill Downs § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Downs#History)

In 1872, Col. [Meriwether Lewis Clark Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriwether_Lewis_Clark_Jr.), grandson of [William Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clark) of the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition), traveled to England, visiting [Epsom](174203.md) in Surrey where [The Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsom_Derby) had been running annually since 1780.[^cite_note-15] From there, Clark went on to [Paris](22989.md) where a group of racing enthusiasts had formed the [French Jockey Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jockey-Club_de_Paris) in 1863. They had organized the [Grand Prix de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_de_Paris) at [Longchamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longchamp_Racecourse), which at the time was the greatest race in France. Returning home to Kentucky, Clark organized the Louisville Jockey Club and Driving Park Association to raise money for building quality racing facilities just outside the city.[^cite_note-16] First known as the Louisville Jockey Club grounds,[^cite_note-17] seven years later the track was commonly referred to as Churchill Downs, named for John and Henry Churchill, who provided the land for the racetrack.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The naming went official in 1937.[^cite_note-ch-20]

A sepia-toned, black and white photograph showing the horse Aristides galloping down the track of the first Kentucky Derby. The horse was ridden by Jockey Oliver Lewis, who was a black man.

[Alan-A-Dale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan-a-Dale_\(horse\)), ridden by jockey [Jimmy Winkfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Winkfield) won the Derby in [1902](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Kentucky_Derby).

The Kentucky Derby was first run at 1+1⁄2 miles (12 [furlongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furlong); 2.4 km) the same distance as the Epsom Derby, before changing lengths in 1896 to its current 1+1⁄4 miles (10 furlongs; 2 km). On May 17, 1875, in front of an estimated crowd of 10,000 people, a field of 15 three-year-old horses contested the first Derby. Under jockey [Oliver Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Lewis), a colt named [Aristides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristides_\(horse\)), who was trained by future [Hall of Famer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Racing_and_Hall_of_Fame) [Ansel Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansel_Williamson), won the inaugural Derby. Later that year, Lewis rode Aristides to a second-place finish in the Belmont Stakes.

In these early decades, Black jockeys were very influential at the Derby.[^cite_note-HISTORY-2025-21] Horses, including race horses, had been cared for, trained and exercised by Blacks in the ante-bellum slave-holding states and this expertise laid the groundwork for future racing standards.[^cite_note-22] Jockeying was seen as activity unsuitable for Whites during that era and in the decades after the Civil War when it was becoming lucrative.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Black jockeys dominated the Derby in all the years before 1894, except for one.[^cite_note-25] In 1886 the track, which had been successful, ran into financial difficulties when a protracted, [gambling-related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parimutuel_betting) [horseman boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Ali_\(horse\)#Wagering_monopoly_and_boycott) removed it from the upper echelons of racing until just after the turn of the 20th century.[^cite_note-fadedglory-26] In 1894 the New Louisville Jockey Club was incorporated with new capital and improved facilities.[^cite_note-fundinguniverse.com-27] The rise of on-track betting and increasing audience sizes brought larger purse sizes, and this began to attract White jockeys to the profession.[^cite_note-HISTORY-2025-21][^cite_note-The_Press_Democrat-2006-28] White jockeys on tracks everywhere began to use violence to attack and intimidate Black jockeys and the horses they rode.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] This caused horse owners to stop hiring Black jockeys.[^cite_note-Menderski-31] Though they were consistent Derby winners, Black jockeys began to disappear from the Derby after 1894.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-Menderski-31] Jimmy Winkfield was the last Black jockey to win the derby and Black jockeys were gone by 1911.[^cite_note-33] But they had instituted innovations now universal in the sport.[^cite_note-The_Press_Democrat-2006-28] [Willie Simms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Simms) won the Derby in 1896 and 1898 on the shortened stirrups he evolved from those used by Black jockeys before him.[^cite_note-34] After his racing career, Oliver Lewis began collecting and analyzing racing data, developing a system very much like the ones used today.[^cite_note-35]

Initially a successful venue, the track ran into financial difficulties due to a protracted, gambling-related horseman boycott removing it from the upper echelons of racing that would last until just after the turn of the 20th century.[^cite_note-fadedglory-26] In 1894 the New Louisville Jockey Club was incorporated with the new capital and improved facilities.[^cite_note-fundinguniverse.com-27] Despite this, the business floundered until 1902, when a syndicate led by [Col. Matt Winn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Winn) of Louisville acquired the facility. Under Winn, Churchill Downs prospered, and the Kentucky Derby then became the preeminent stakes race for three-year-old thoroughbred horses in North America.[^cite_note-36]

Thoroughbred owners began sending their successful Derby horses to compete in two other races. These two are the Preakness Stakes at the [Pimlico Race Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimlico_Race_Course), in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), and the Belmont Stakes in [Elmont, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmont,_New_York). The three races offered large purses, and in [1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_Kentucky_Derby), [Sir Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Barton) became the first horse to win all three races. However, the term "[Triple Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_of_Thoroughbred_Racing)" did not come into use for another eleven years. In [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_Kentucky_Derby), when [Gallant Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallant_Fox) became the second horse to win all three races, sportswriter [Charles Hatton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hatton_\(journalist\)) brought the phrase into American usage. Fueled by the media, public interest in the possibility of a "superhorse" that could win the Triple Crown began in the weeks leading up to the Derby. Two years after the term went in use, the race (until that time ran in mid-May since inception) changed the date to the first Saturday in May. This change allows for a specific schedule for the Triple Crown races. Since 1931, the order of Triple Crown races has been the Kentucky Derby first, followed by the Preakness Stakes and then the Belmont Stakes. Before 1931, eleven times the Preakness was run before the Derby. On May 12, 1917, and again on May 13, 1922, the Preakness and the Derby took place on the same day. On eleven occasions the Belmont Stakes was run before the Preakness Stakes, and in 2020, the Belmont was run first, then the Kentucky Derby, and the Preakness Stakes last.



[Churchill Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Downs) in 1901

On May 16, 1925, the first live radio broadcast of the Kentucky Derby aired on [WHAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHAS_\(AM\)) as well as on [WGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WGN_\(AM\)) in Chicago.[^cite_note-37] On May 7, 1949, the first television coverage of the Kentucky Derby took place, produced by [WAVE-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WAVE-TV), the NBC affiliate in Louisville. This coverage was aired live in the Louisville market and sent to NBC as a kinescope newsreel recording for national broadcast. On May 3, 1952, the first national television coverage of the Kentucky Derby took place, aired from then-[CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) affiliate [WHAS-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHAS-TV).[^cite_note-38] In 1954, the purse exceeded US\$100,000 for the first time. In 1968, [Dancer's Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancer's_Image) became the first horse to win the race and then face disqualification. A [urine test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinalysis) revealed traces of [phenylbutazone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenylbutazone) (an [anti-inflammatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsteroidal_anti-inflammatory_drug) painkiller drug) inside Dancer's Image. [Forward Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Pass_\(horse\)) won after a protracted legal battle by the owners of Dancer's Image (which they lost). Forward Pass thus became the eighth winner for [Calumet Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calumet_Farm). Unexpectedly, the regulations at Kentucky thoroughbred race tracks were changed some years later, allowing horses to run on phenylbutazone. In 1970, [Diane Crump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Crump) became the first female jockey to ride in the Derby, finishing 15th aboard Fathom.[^cite_note-39]

The fastest time ever run in the Derby was in [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Kentucky_Derby) at 1\:59.4 minutes, when [Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_\(horse\)) broke the record set by [Northern Dancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Dancer) in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Kentucky_Derby). Also during that race, Secretariat did something unique in Triple Crown races: for each successive quarter run, his times were faster. Although the races do not record times for non-winners, in 1973 Sham finished second, two and a half lengths behind Secretariat in the same race. Using the thoroughbred racing convention of one length equaling one-fifth of a second to calculate [Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_\(horse\))'s time, he also finished in under two minutes. Another sub-two-minute finish, only the third, was set in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Kentucky_Derby) by [Monarchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchos) at 1\:59.97, the first year the race used hundredths of seconds instead of fifths in timing.[^cite_note-40]

In 2005, the [purse distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purse_distribution) for the Derby changed, so that horses finishing fifth would henceforth receive a share of the purse; previously only the first four finishers did so.[^cite_note-41]

The Kentucky Derby began offering \$3 million in purse money in 2019. Churchill Downs officials have cited the success of [historical race wagering terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_Racing) at their Derby City Gaming facility in Louisville as a factor behind the purse increase. The Derby first offered a \$1 million purse in 1996; it was doubled to \$2 million in 2005.[^cite_note-42]

In 2020, the Derby was postponed from May 2 to September 5 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-43] This was the second time in history the race had been postponed, the other being in 1945.[^cite_note-Kleber-5] Churchill Downs used a new singular 20-stall starting gate for the 2020 Kentucky Derby, replacing the previous arrangement that used a standard 14-stall gate and an auxiliary six-stall gate.[^cite_note-gate20-44] The old setup contributed to congestion at the start of the race, especially in the gap between the two gates.[^cite_note-gate20-44]

[Rich Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Strike), a [reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_\(sport\)) who only made it into the final field after a late scratching, won the [race in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kentucky_Derby) at final odds of 80\:1 and parimutuel betting payouts were even larger.[^cite_note-45]



Churchill Downs sign for the [2024 Kentucky Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kentucky_Derby), its 150th running, with one of the twin spires in the background

In January 2024, the purse for the Kentucky Derby was increased to \$5 million.[^cite_note-Frakes-2024-2]

In the [2026 Kentucky Derby](82792534.md), [Cherie DeVaux](83090954.md) became the first woman trainer to ever win the Kentucky Derby, as the trainer of [Golden Tempo](83090873.md).[^cite_note-46]

On August 5, 2026, Churchill Downs Incorporated and the [New York Racing Association](673662.md) (NYRA) announced the [Thoroughbred Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_Championship_Series?action=edit\&redlink=1) (TCS), a six-race series for three-year-old thoroughbreds that is set to begin in 2027. The Kentucky Derby was designated as the first race of the new series.[^cite_note-47]

### Attendance

Millions of people worldwide bet at various live tracks and online sportsbooks.[^cite_note-48] In 2017, a crowd of 158,070 watched Always Dreaming win the Derby, making it the seventh biggest attendance in the history of the racetrack. The track reported a wagering total of \$209.2 million from all the sources on all the races on the Kentucky Derby Day program. It was a 9% increase compared to the total of \$192.6 million in 2016 and an increase of 8% over the previous record set in 2015 of \$194.3 million.[^cite_note-49] TwinSpires, a platform for betting online and a partner of the Kentucky Derby and the Breeders' Cup, recorded \$32.8 million in handle on the Churchill Down races for the Kentucky Derby Day program. This record was a 22% increase over the preceding year. On the Kentucky Derby race alone, the handle of TwinSpires was \$20.1 million, which is a 22% rise compared to the prior year.[^cite_note-50]

The race often draws celebrities. HM [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II), on a visit to the United States, joined the racegoers at Churchill Downs in 2007.[^cite_note-51]

### Sponsorship

The 2004 Kentucky Derby marked the first time that jockeys—as a result of a [court order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_order)—were allowed to wear corporate advertising [logos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo) on their clothing.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

Norman Adams has been the designer of the Kentucky Derby Logo since 2002. On February 1, 2006, the Louisville-based [fast-food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast-food) company [Yum! Brands, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yum!_Brands,_Inc.) announced a corporate sponsorship deal to call the race "The Kentucky Derby presented by Yum! Brands."[^cite_note-54] In 2018, [Woodford Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodford_Reserve) replaced Yum! Brands as the presenting sponsor.[^cite_note-Woodford-1]

The Swiss watchmaker [Longines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longines) is the official sponsor timekeeper of the Kentucky Derby.[^cite_note-55]

## Traditions

In addition to the race itself, several traditions play a significant role in the Derby atmosphere. The [mint julep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_julep)—an iced drink consisting of [bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_whiskey), [mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentha), and [sugar](27712.md) syrup—is the traditional beverage of the race. The historic beverage comes served in an ice-frosted silver julep cup. However, most Churchill Downs patrons sip theirs from souvenir glasses (first offered in 1939 and available in revised form each year since) printed with all previous Derby winners.[^cite_note-56] Also, [burgoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgoo), a thick stew of beef, chicken, pork, and vegetables, is a popular Kentucky dish served at the Derby.[^cite_note-57]



[Louisville Clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville_Clock) (often called the Louisville Derby Clock), which was dismantled in 2015

The [infield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infield)—a spectator area inside the track—offers general admission prices but little chance of seeing much of the race, particularly before the [jumbotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumbotron) installation in 2014.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Instead, revelers show up in the infield to party with abandon. By contrast, "Millionaire's Row" refers to the expensive box seats that attract the rich, the famous and the well-connected. Women appear in elegant outfits lavishly accessorized with large, elaborate hats. Following the [Call to the Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_to_the_Post) played on bugle by Steve Buttleman, as the horses start to parade before the grandstands, the [University of Louisville Cardinal Marching Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Louisville_Cardinal_Marching_Band) plays [Stephen Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Foster)'s "[My Old Kentucky Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Old_Kentucky_Home)" while sung by the [University of Louisville Cardinal Singers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Louisville_Cardinal_Singers), except in 2020 when the song was played on bugle by Buttleman. This song is a tradition which began in 1921.[^cite_note-60] The event attracts spectators from a large area, flying in hundreds of private aircraft to [Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport](264229.md).[^cite_note-ain2015-05-05-61]

The Derby is frequently referred to as "*The Run for the Roses*", because a lush blanket of 554 red [roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose) is awarded to the Kentucky Derby winner each year.[^cite_note-Tran-62] New York sports columnist and future Churchill Downs president [Bill Corum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Corum) in 1925 began describing the race thusly,[^cite_note-Rossingh-6] but the tradition originated in 1883 when New York City socialite [E. Berry Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evander_Berry_Wall) presented roses to ladies at a post-Derby party. The Churchill Downs founder and president, Col. Meriwether Lewis Clark Jr., attended that event. This gesture is believed to have led Clark to the idea of making the rose the race's official flower. However, it was not until 1896 that any recorded account referred to draping roses on the Derby winner.[^cite_note-Tran-62] The [Governor of Kentucky](253074.md) and the Chairman of [Churchill Downs Incorporated](3648388.md) present the garland and the [Kentucky Derby Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Derby_Trophy) to the winner. Pop vocalist [Dan Fogelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Fogelberg) composed the song "[Run for the Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_for_the_Roses_\(song\))", released in time for the 1980 running of the race.[^cite_note-63]

### Riders Up!

"Riders Up!" is the traditional command from the Paddock Judge for jockeys to mount their horses in advance of the upcoming race. This command typically occurs across from Stall 1 in the Saddling Paddock around 19 minutes in advance of the start of the race. After the command, jockeys receive a leg up on their mounts and immediately turn right to the Paddock Runway, which leads to the racetrack. Since 2012, a celebrity attendee recites this phrase.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

### Festival

Main article: [Kentucky Derby Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Derby_Festival)

In the weeks preceding the race, numerous activities took place for the Kentucky Derby Festival. [Thunder Over Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Over_Louisville)—an airshow and fireworks display—generally begins the festivities in earnest two weeks before the Derby.

### Horse records

Secretariat set the record for speed in 1973 with a time of 1\:59.4. During its first two decades when the Derby was run at 1+1⁄2 miles, the record was 2\:34.5, set by [Spokane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane_\(horse\)) in 1889.

Four different horses won by 8 lengths: [Old Rosebud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Rosebud) in 1914, [Johnstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnstown_\(horse\)) in 1939, [Whirlaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlaway) in 1941, and [Assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_\(horse\)) in 1946.

The highest odds of a winning horse were 91 to 1 for [Donerail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donerail) in 1913. The second-highest odds occurred in 2022, when Rich Strike went off at 80 to 1 and won the race.

Three horses have won the Kentucky Derby without competing as a two-year-old: [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_\(horse\)) (1882), [Justify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justify_\(horse\)) (2018), and [Mage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mage_\(horse\)) (2023).[^cite_note-NYT-67]

### Jockey records

107 [jockeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jockeys) have won the Kentucky Derby, with 27 doing so multiple times. [Isaac Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Burns_Murphy) (1890–91), Jimmy Winkfield (1901–02), [Ron Turcotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Turcotte) (1972–73), [Eddie Delahoussaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Delahoussaye) (1982–83), [Calvin Borel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Borel) (2009–10), and [Victor Espinoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Espinoza) (2014–15) are the only jockeys to win the Derby in back-to-back years. Borel is the only jockey with three wins in a four-year span (2007, 2009–10).[^cite_note-68]

## Sire lines

Winners of the Kentucky Derby can be connected to each other due to the practice of [arranging horse breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_breeding_theories) based on their previous success. All of the horses can be traced back to the three foundational sires, with [Godolphin Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godolphin_Arabian) the ancestor of 7 winners, [Byerley Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byerley_Turk) the ancestor of 11 winners, and [Darley Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darley_Arabian) the ancestor of 134 winners, including all winners since 1938.

The [Curlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curlin) direct sire line has produced 3 of the last 5 Kentucky Derby winners, most recently [Golden Tempo](83090873.md) in 2026.

The [Into Mischief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_Mischief) direct sire line has produced 4 of the last 7 Kentucky Derby winners, most recently [Sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty_\(horse\)) in 2025.

### Darley Arabian line

The Darley Arabian (1700c) sire line (all branched through the [Eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_\(horse\)) (1764) line)[^cite_note-75] produced 134 Derby winners (126 colts, 5 geldings, 3 fillies), including all winners from 1938 to present.[^cite_note-KDSL-76] The main branches of this sire line are:

- the [King Fergus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fergus) (1775) branch (all branched through the [Voltigeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltigeur_\(horse\)) (1847) line), produced 14 winners. His sire line continued primarily through his son [Vedette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedette_\(horse\)) (1854) with 12 winners, due to his sons [Speculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculum_\(horse\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1865) with 6 winners (nearly exclusively through [Sundridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundridge_\(horse\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1898) with 5 winners, most recently Count Turf in 1951) and [Galopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galopin) (1872) with 6 winners (exclusively through [St. Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Simon_\(horse\)) (1881), most recently Go for Gin in 1994).

  &#x20;produced 120 winners (all branched through the [Waxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxy_\(horse\)) (1790) line), including all winners from 1995 to present. In turn, the primary branch continues through [Sir Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Hercules) (1826), which has produced 93 winners (including all winners since 2006), and then the [Birdcatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdcatcher) (1833) branch
