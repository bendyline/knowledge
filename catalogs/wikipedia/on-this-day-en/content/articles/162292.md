**Renault S.A.**, commonly referred to as **Groupe Renault** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˈrɛnoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*REN-oh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/rəˈnoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*rə-NOH*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]; French: [\[ʁəno\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French), also known as the **Renault Group** in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)), is a French [multinational corporation](214491.md) and [automobile manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_manufacturer) established in 1899.[^cite_note-LAE-9] The company produces a range of cars and vans; it has previously manufactured [trucks](31456.md), [tractors](152692.md), [tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank), [buses](4146.md)/[coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(bus\)), [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) and aircraft engines, as well as [autorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autorail) vehicles.

Headquartered in [Boulogne-Billancourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulogne-Billancourt), a suburb of [Paris](22989.md), the Renault group is made up of the namesake Renault marque along with subsidiaries [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles_Alpine), [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Dacia) from Romania, and [Mobilize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilize_\(marque\)). It has been part of the [Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault%E2%80%93Nissan%E2%80%93Mitsubishi_Alliance) (previously Renault–Nissan Alliance) since 1999. The French state and [Nissan](21240.md) of Japan each own a 15% share of the company.

Renault also has other [subsidiaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary) such as [RCI Banque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCI_Banque) (automotive financing), Renault Retail Group (automotive distribution), and [Motrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motrio) (automotive parts). Renault has various joint ventures, including [Horse Powertrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_Powertrain) (engine development), [Oyak-Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyak-Renault) (Turkish manufacturing), [Renault Nissan Automotive India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Nissan_Automotive_India) (Indian manufacturing), and [Renault Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Korea) (previously Renault Samsung Motors, South Korean manufacturing). [Renault Trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trucks), previously known as Renault Véhicules Industriels, has been part of the [Volvo Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Group) since 2001. [Renault Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Agriculture) became fully owned by German agricultural equipment manufacturer [CLAAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLAAS) in 2008.

Renault is known for its role in [motor sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_sport), particularly [rallying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallying), [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_F1) and [Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAMS#Formula_E). The company's early work on mathematical curve [modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3d_modeling) for car bodies is significant in the history of [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics).[^cite_note-SWOT_2018-10]

## History

### Founding and early years (1898–1918)



[Louis Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Renault_\(industrialist\)) (1877–1944) in 1903

The Renault corporation was founded on 25 February 1899 as *Société Renault Frères* by [Louis Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Renault_\(industrialist\)) and his brothers [Marcel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Renault) and [Fernand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_Renault).[^cite_note-LAE-9][^cite_note-11] Louis was a bright, aspiring young engineer who had already designed and built several prototypes before teaming up with his brothers, who had honed their business skills working for their father's [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) firm. While Louis handled design and production, Marcel and Fernand managed the business.[^cite_note-AIM-12]

The first Renault car, the [Renault Voiturette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Voiturette) 1CV, was sold to a friend of Louis' father after giving him a test ride on 24 December 1898.[^cite_note-13]



1901 Voiturette Renault Type D Série B



1911 Renault CC

In 1903, Renault began to manufacture its own engines; until then it had purchased them from [De Dion-Bouton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Dion-Bouton). The first major volume sale came in 1905 when Société des Automobiles de Place bought Renault AG1 cars to establish a fleet of taxis.[^cite_note-TEMBEF-14] These vehicles were later used by the French military to transport troops during [World War I](4764461.md) which earned them the nickname "[Taxi de la Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Taxi_de_la_Marne)."[^cite_note-15] By 1907, a significant percentage of London and Paris taxis had been built by Renault.[^cite_note-TEMBEF-14] Renault was also the best-selling foreign brand in [New York](645042.md) in 1907 and 1908.[^cite_note-16] In 1908 the company produced 3,575 units, becoming the country's largest car manufacturer.[^cite_note-TEMBEF-14]

The brothers recognised the value of publicity that participation in [motor racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_racing) could generate for their vehicles. Renault made itself known through succeeding in the first city-to-city races held in Switzerland, producing rapid sales growth. Both Louis and Marcel raced company vehicles, but Marcel was killed in an accident during the 1903 [Paris-Madrid race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Madrid_race).[^cite_note-17] Although Louis never raced again, his company remained very involved, including [Ferenc Szisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Szisz) winning the first [Grand Prix motor racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motor_racing) event[^cite_note-18] in a [Renault AK 90CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_AK_90CV) [in 1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_French_Grand_Prix).

Louis took full control of the company as the only remaining brother in 1906 when Fernand retired for health reasons.[^cite_note-AIM-12] Fernand died in 1909 and Louis became the sole owner, renaming the company *Société des Automobiles Renault* (Renault Automobile Company).[^cite_note-LAE-9][^cite_note-TEMBEF-14]

Renault fostered its reputation for innovation from very early on. At the time, cars were luxury items manufactured without assembly line advances. The price of the smallest Renaults at the time was 3000 [francs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franc) (₣); an amount equal to ten years pay for the average worker. In 1905, the company introduced [mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production) techniques and [Taylorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylorism) in 1913.[^cite_note-ROH-19] In 1911, Renault visited Henry Ford at the [Highland Park factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park_Ford_Plant) and adopted some of the manufacturing principles from his trip.[^cite_note-20]

Renault manufactured [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses) and commercial cargo vehicles in the pre-war years. The first real [commercial truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_truck) from the company was introduced in 1906.[^cite_note-TT-21] Renault were also an important pre-war manufacturer of [aircraft engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_engine). The firm entered the business in 1907 with the first of what would become a series of [air-cooled V8 engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_50/60_hp).[^cite_note-\:SYHartmann2-22] In 1911 the [Renault 90 hp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_90_hp) became the world's first [V12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V12_engine) aircraft engine when it was exhibited at the Salon de l’Aéronautique at the [Grand Palais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Palais) in [Paris](22989.md).[^cite_note-\:SY1-23]

During World War I, the company branched out into [ammunition](18940621.md)[^cite_note-TEMBEF-14] and [military vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_vehicle) such as the revolutionary [Renault FT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_FT) [tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank).[^cite_note-ROH-19] Production of aero engines also ramped up with additional licensed production of the firms' products being undertaken by various companies, including [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Limited) who got their start in the aircraft engine business producing a batch of [Renault 70 hp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_70_hp) air-cooled V8s.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Renault's most successful aircraft engine was the [300 hp 12Fe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_12Fe) with around 5,300 built during the war.[^cite_note-\:SYHartmann2-22] The company's military designs were so successful that Louis was awarded the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour) for his company's contributions.[^cite_note-26] The company exported engines to American automobile manufacturers for use in such automobiles as the [GJG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GJG), which used a Renault 26 [horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower) (19 kW) or 40 hp (30 kW) four-cylinder engine.[^cite_note-27]

### Interwar years (1919–1938)



[Renault Celtaquatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Celtaquatre), 1935

Louis Renault enlarged Renault's scope after 1918, producing [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_machinery) and [industrial machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_machinery). The war from 1914 until 1918 led to many new products.[^cite_note-IDCH-28] The first Renault tractor, the Type GP was produced between 1919 and 1930. It was based on the FT tank.[^cite_note-29] Renault struggled to compete with the increasingly popular small, affordable "people's cars," while problems with the United States stock market and the workforce slowed the company's growth. Renault also had to find a way to distribute its vehicles more efficiently. In 1920, Louis signed one of its first distribution contracts with [Gustave Gueudet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gueudet), an entrepreneur from [Amiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens), France.

The pre-First World War cars had a distinctive front shape caused by positioning the [radiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiator_\(engine_cooling\)) behind the engine to give a so-called "[coal scuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_scuttle)" [bonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hood_\(vehicle\)). This continued through the 1920s.[^cite_note-30] Only in 1930 did all models place the radiator at the front. The bonnet badge changed from circular to the familiar and continuing diamond shape in 1925. The practice of installing the radiator behind the engine against the firewall continued during the 1950s and 1960s on vehicles where the engine was installed longitudinally in the rear of the vehicle.

Renault introduced new models at the [Paris Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Motor_Show), which was held in September or October of the year. This led to confusion about model years. For example, a "1927" model was mostly produced in 1928.

Renault cars during this time period had two model lines; the economy four-cylinder engine models that in the 1930s had the suffix "Quatre" and the luxury six-cylinder models that were sold with the suffix "-six", later becoming "Stella." For example, in 1928, when Renault produced 45,809 cars, its seven models started with a 6CV, a 10CV, the [Monasix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Monasix), 15CV, the [Vivasix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Vivasix), the 18/22CV and the [40CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_40CV). Renault offered eight body styles. The longer [rolling chassis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_chassis) were available to [coachbuilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachbuilder). The smaller were the most popular while the least produced was the 18/[24CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_24CV). The most expensive body style in each range was the closed car. Roadsters and tourers (torpedoes) were the cheapest.



1932 [Renault Reinastella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Reinastella)

The London operation was important to Renault in 1928. The UK market was quite large and North America also received exports for the luxury car market. Lifted suspensions, enhanced cooling, and special bodies were common on vehicles sold abroad. Exports to the US by 1928 had declined to near-zero from their high point prior to WWI. A [Type NM 40CV Tourer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_40CV) had a US list price of over US\$4,600 (\$86,250 in 2025 dollars[^cite_note-inflation-US-31]), about the same as a [Cadillac V-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_V-12), [Packard Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packard_Eight), [Fiat 520](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_520), or [Delahaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delahaye). Closed 7-seat limousines like the [Renault Suprastella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Suprastella) started at US\$6,000 (\$112,500 in 2025 dollars[^cite_note-inflation-US-31]).

Cars were conservatively engineered and built. The [Renault Vivasix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Vivasix), model PG1, was sold as the "executive sports" model beginning in 1927. Lighter weight factory steel bodies powered by a 3,180 [cubic centimetre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_centimetre) (cc) six-cylinder motor provided a formula that lasted until the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).

"de Grand Luxe Renaults", those with a [wheelbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelbase) over 12-foot (3.7 m), were produced in small numbers in two major types – six- and eight-cylinder. The 1927 six-cylinder Grand Renault models NM, PI and PZ introduced the new three spring rear suspension that considerably aided stability that was needed since some vehicles surpassed 90 mph (140 km/h).

The straight 8-cylinder [Reinastella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Reinastella) was introduced in 1929 and expanded to a range culminating in 1939 [Suprastella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Suprastella). Coachbuilders included [Kellner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellner-B%C3%A9chereau), [Labourdette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Henri_Labourdette?action=edit\&redlink=1), [J. Rothschild et Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A9ment-Rothschild) and Renault bodies. Closed car Renault bodies were often trimmed with interior woodwork by Rothschild.



[Renault Viva Grand Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Viva_Grand_Sport) and [Hélène Boucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9l%C3%A8ne_Boucher). During the 1930s, Renault settled several speed world records with Caudron planes, thanks to its 6-cylinders engines and aerodynamic designs.

[^cite_note-Renault_2015-32]

In 1928, Renault introduced an upgraded specification to its "Stella" line. The [Vivastella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Vivastella)'s and Grand Renaults had upgraded interior fittings and a small star fitted above the front hood logo. This proved to be a winning differentiator and in the 1930s all cars changed to the Stella suffix from the previous two alpha character model identifiers.

The Grand Renaults were built using a considerable amount of [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium). Engines, brakes, transmissions, floor and running boards and all external body panels were aluminium. Of the few that were built, many went to scrap to aid the war effort.

In 1931, Renault introduced [diesel engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine) for its commercial vehicles.[^cite_note-TT-21]

Renault was one of the few French vehicle manufacturers that pursued the production of [aircraft engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_engine) after World War I. In the late 1920s, it attempted to produce a high-power military engine to compete with the American [Pratt & Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney) units, which proved unsuccessful, although its civil engines achieved better results.[^cite_note-AERO-33] In the 1930s, the company took over the aircraft manufacturer [Caudron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudron), focusing its production in small airplanes,[^cite_note-AERO-33] acquired a stake in [Air France](14998012.md) and partnered to establish the [airmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airmail) company [Air Bleu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Bleu).[^cite_note-INSEE-34] Renault Caudron airplanes settled several speed world records during the 1930s. Renault continued developing tanks as part of France's rearming effort, including the [D1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Char_D1) and the FT's replacement, the [R 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_R_35).[^cite_note-35]

During the late 1920s and early 1930s, Renault was surpassed by [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn) as the largest car manufacturer in France. Citroën models at the time were more innovative and popular than Renault's.[^cite_note-36] However, by mid-1930s the French manufacturers were hit by the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression). Renault could initially offset losses through its tractor, railroad and weaponry businesses, while Citroën filed for [bankruptcy](4695.md), and was later acquired by [Michelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin).[^cite_note-INSEE-34] Renault became again the largest car manufacturer, a position it would keep until the 1980s.[^cite_note-INSEE-34]

Renault was finally affected by the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) economic crisis in 1936. The company spun off [Caudron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudron) and its foundry and aircraft engine divisions into related but autonomous operations, keeping its core automotive business.[^cite_note-INSEE-34] Between 1936 and 1938, a series of labour disputes, strikes, and worker unrest spread throughout the French automobile industry.[^cite_note-Siedman1-37] The disputes were eventually quashed by Renault in a particularly intransigent way, and over 2,000 people lost their jobs.[^cite_note-Siedman1-37][^cite_note-Saltmarsh1-38]

### World War II and aftermath (1939–1944)

After the [French capitulation in 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Armistice_at_Compi%C3%A8gne), [Louis Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Renault_\(industrialist\)) refused to produce tanks for [Nazi Germany](21212.md), which took control of his factories. As Renault was manufacturing the [Renault UE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_UE) tank for the Allies, he produced trucks instead. On 3 March 1942, the British [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF) launched 235 low-level bombers at the [Île Seguin, Billancourt, Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_Seguin) plant, the largest number aimed at a single target during the war.[^cite_note-39] 460 metric tons (450 long tons; 510 short tons) of bombs were dropped on the plant and the surrounding area, causing extensive damage along with heavy civilian casualties.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40] Renault resolved to rebuild the factory as quickly as possible, but bombardments continued a year later, on 4 April, this time delivered by the Americans, and on 3 and 15 September 1943.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40]

A few weeks after the [Liberation of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Paris), at the start of September 1944, the factory gates at Renault's Billancourt plant reopened.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40] Operations restarted slowly, in an atmosphere poisoned by plotting and political conspiracy.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40] In 1936, the Billancourt factory had been the scene of violent political and industrial unrest that had surfaced under [Léon Blum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Blum)'s [Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_\(France\)) government. The political jostling and violence that followed liberation ostensibly reflected the rivalries between [capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalist) collaboration and [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) resistance; many of the scores settled predated the invasion.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40]

Responding to the chaotic situation at Renault, a 27 September 1944 meeting of the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_France) ([fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil%20des%20ministres%20\(France\))) took place under [de Gaulle's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) presidency. Postwar European politics had quickly become polarised between communists and anti-communists, and in France de Gaulle was keen to resist [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party) attempts to monopolise the political dividends available to [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_resistance) heroes: politically Billancourt was a communist stronghold. The government decided to "requisition" the Renault factories.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40] A week later, on 4 October, [Pierre Lefaucheux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Lefaucheux), a resistance leader with a background in engineering and top-level management, was appointed provisional administrator of the firm, assuming his responsibilities at once.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40]

Meanwhile, [provisional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_the_French_Republic) accused Louis Renault of collaborating with the Germans. In the frenzied atmosphere of those early post-liberation days, with many wild accusations, Renault was advised by his lawyers to present himself to a judge. He appeared before Judge Marcel Martin, on 22 September 1944[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40] and was arrested on 23 September 1944, as were several other French automobile-industry leaders.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40] Renault's harsh handling of the 1936–1938 strikes had left him without political allies and no one came to his aid.[^cite_note-Saltmarsh1-38] He was incarcerated at [Fresnes prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnes_prison) where he died on 24 October 1944 under unclear circumstances,[^cite_note-shame-41] while awaiting trial.[^cite_note-ogre-42][^cite_note-43]

On 1 January 1945, by de Gaulle's decree, the company was posthumously expropriated from Louis Renault. On 16 January 1945, it was formally nationalised as *Régie Nationale des Usines Renault*.[^cite_note-Automobilia1940-46-40] Renault's were the only factories permanently expropriated by the French government.[^cite_note-Berliet_Renault-44] In subsequent years, the Renault family tried to have the nationalisation rescinded by French courts and receive compensation. In 1945, and again in 1961, the Courts responded that they had no authority to review the government's actions.[^cite_note-Saltmarsh1-38]

### Postwar resurgence (1945–1971)



[Renault 4CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_4CV)

Under the leadership of [Pierre Lefaucheux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Lefaucheux), Renault experienced both a commercial resurgence and [labor unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_unrest), that was to continue into the 1980s.

In the early 1950s, Renault assembled at least two Frégate variants in England, the "Standard Saloon" and the "De Luxe Saloon".[^cite_note-45]



[Renault Frégate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Fr%C3%A9gate), 1951

In secrecy during the war, Louis Renault had developed the [rear engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_engine) [4CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_4CV)[^cite_note-Autoworld197404-46] which was subsequently launched under Lefacheux in 1946. Renault debuted its flagship model, the largely conventional 2-litre 4-cylinder [Renault Frégate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Fr%C3%A9gate) (1951–1960), shortly thereafter. The 4CV proved a capable rival for cars such as the [Morris Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Minor) and [Volkswagen Beetle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Beetle); its sales of more than half a million ensured its production until 1961.



[Renault Dauphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Dauphine)

After the success of the 4CV, Lefacheux continued to defy the postwar French [Ministry of Industrial Production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul-Marie_Pons), which had wanted to convert Renault solely to truck manufacture,[^cite_note-50jahre-47] by directing the development of its successor. He oversaw the prototyping of the [Dauphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Dauphine) (until [his death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Lefaucheux#Death)), enlisting the help of artist [Paule Marrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paule_Marrot) in [pioneering the company's textile and color division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Dauphine#Paule_Marrot).

The [Dauphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Dauphine) sold well as the company expanded production and sales further abroad, including Africa and North America.[^cite_note-48] The Dauphine sold well initially in the US, although it subsequently became outdated against increased competition, including from the country's nascent domestic compacts such as the [Chevrolet Corvair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Corvair). Renault also sold the [Renault Caravelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Caravelle) roadster, which was called the Floride outside North America.



[Renault Floride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Floride), 1958



Launched in 1964, the [Renault R8 Gordini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_R8_Gordini) was the first sportive compact car for a public consumption price.

[^cite_note-49]

During the 1950s, Renault absorbed two small French heavy vehicle manufacturers ([Somua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somua) and [Latil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latil)) and in 1955 merged them with its own truck and bus division to form the [Société Anonyme de Véhicules Industriels et d'Equipements Mécaniques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saviem) (Saviem).[^cite_note-CBTT-50]



1966 [Renault 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_4)

Renault then launched two successful cars – the [Renault 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_4) (1961–1992), a practical competitor for the likes of the [Citroën 2CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_2CV), and the rear-engined [Renault 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_8).[^cite_note-IDCH-28] The larger [Renault 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_10) followed the success of the Renault 8, and was the last rear-engined Renault. The company achieved success with the more modern and more upmarket [Renault 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_16), a pioneering [hatchback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatchback) launched in 1966, followed by the smaller [Renault 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_6).



1969 [Renault 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_12)

On 16 January 1970, the manufacturer celebrated the 25th anniversary of its 1945 rebirth as the nationalised *Régie Nationale des Usines Renault*. The 1960s had been a decade of aggressive growth: a few months earlier, in October 1969, the manufacturer had launched the [Renault 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_12), combining the engineering philosophy of its hatchbacks with the more conservative "three-box" design. The four-door Renault 12 model slotted between the [Renault 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_6) and [Renault 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_16). The model was a success. 1970 was also the first year during which Renault produced more than a million cars in a single year, building 1,055,803.[^cite_note-Automobilia1970-51]

### Modern era (1972–1980)



Renault 5 Turbo



1972 [Renault 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_17) TS coupe



The [Renault Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Fuego) won 8 consecutive championships in the Argentinian [TC 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TC_2000_Championship) touring car racing series between 1986 and 1993.

The company's compact and economical [Renault 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_5) model, launched in January 1972,[^cite_note-Automobilia1972-52] was another success, anticipating the [1973 energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_energy_crisis).[^cite_note-IDCH-28] The [Renault 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_18) was introduced in 1978 and the larger [Renault 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_20) in 1975.

During the mid-seventies, the already broad-based company diversified into more industries and continued to expand globally, including [South East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Asia). The energy crisis led Renault to again attempt to attack the North American market. Despite the Dauphine's success in the United States in the late 1950s and an unsuccessful assembly project in [Saint-Bruno-de-Montarville, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Bruno-de-Montarville,_Quebec) (1964–72), Renault began to disappear from North America at the end of the decade.

Renault acquired a controlling stake in [Automobiles Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles_Alpine) in 1973, and over the decades, Renault developed a collaborative partnership with [Nash Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_Motors) [Rambler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_Rambler) and its successor [American Motors Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Motors_Corporation) (AMC). From 1962 until 1967, Renault assembled [complete knock down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_knock_down) (CKD) kits of the [Rambler Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambler_Classic) sedans in its factory in Belgium.[^cite_note-53] Renault did not have large or luxury cars in its product line and the "Rambler Renault" was positioned as an alternative to the [Mercedes-Benz "Fintail"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_Heckflosse) cars. Later, Renault continued to make and sell a hybrid of AMC's [Rambler American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambler_American) and Rambler Classic called the [Renault Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Torino) in Argentina (sold through IKA-Renault). Renault partnered with AMC on other projects, such as a rotary concept engine in the late 1960s.

In the late 1960s and 1970s, the company established subsidiaries in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), most notably [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia_\(car\)) in Romania, and [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrias_Kaiser_Argentina) (many of which remain active) and forged technological cooperation agreements with [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo) and [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot),[^cite_note-DTI-54] (for instance, for the development of the [PRV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRV_engine) [V6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V6) engine, which was used in [Renault 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_30), [Peugeot 604](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_604), and [Volvo 260](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_200_series) in the late 1970s). In the mid-1960s, Renault Australia was set up in Melbourne. The company produced and assembled models including the R8, R10, R12, R16, sporty R15, R17 coupes, R18, and R20. The unit closed in 1981 and the factory closed with LNC Industries taking over import and distribution of Renaults in Australia.[^cite_note-55]

When Peugeot acquired Citroën and formed [PSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupe_PSA), the group's collaboration with Renault was reduced, although established joint production projects were maintained. Prior its merging with Peugeot, Citroën sold to Renault the truck and bus manufacturer [Berliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berliet)[^cite_note-DTI-54] in December 1974,[^cite_note-56] merging it with its subsidiary Saviem in 1978 to create [Renault Véhicules Industriels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trucks), which became the only French manufacturer of heavy commercial vehicles.[^cite_note-TT-21][^cite_note-CBTT-50] In 1976, Renault reorganised the company into four business areas: automobiles (for car and [light commercial vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_commercial_vehicles) or LCVs), finance and services, commercial vehicles (coaches and trucks over 2.5 [tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_ton) [GVW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_vehicle_weight_rating)), and minor operations under an industrial enterprises division (farm machinery, plastics, foundry, etc.). In 1980, Renault produced 2,053,677 cars and LCVs. The cars at the time were the Renault 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, and 30; the LCVs were the 4, 5, and 12 Société and the Estafette. The company added 54,086 buses/coaches and trucks.[^cite_note-DTI-54]

In North America, Renault partnered with [American Motors Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Motors_Corporation) (AMC), lending AMC operating capital and buying a minority 22.5% stake in the company in late 1979. The first Renault model sold through AMC's dealerships was the R5, renamed Renault Le Car. [Jeep](15658.md) was keeping AMC afloat until new products, particularly the [XJ Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep_XJ), could be launched. When the bottom fell out of the [four-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-wheel_drive) (4×4) truck market in early 1980, AMC was in danger of bankruptcy. To protect its investment, Renault bailed AMC out with cash – at the price of a controlling 47.5% interest.[^cite_note-TCAMAIH-57] Renault replaced some AMC executives, and [Jose Dedeurwaerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Dedeurwaerder) of Renault became President of AMC.[^cite_note-IDCH-28]

The partnership resulted in the marketing of Jeep vehicles in Europe.[^cite_note-IDCH-28] The [Jeep Cherokee (XJ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep_Cherokee_\(XJ\)) Motor vehicle may have been a joint AMC/Renault project since some early sketches of the XJ series were made in collaboration by Renault and AMC engineers (AMC insisted that the XJ Cherokee was designed by AMC personnel; even though a former Renault engineer designed the Quadra-Link front suspension for the XJ series).[^cite_note-58] The Jeep also used wheels and seats from Renault. Part of AMC's overall strategy was to save manufacturing costs by using Renault's parts and engineering expertise when practical. This led to the improvement of the venerable [AMC inline six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Straight-6_engine) – a Renault/Bendix-based port electronic [fuel injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_injection) system (usually called [Renix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renix)) transformed it into a modern, competitive powerplant with a jump from 110 to 177 hp (82 to 132 kW) with less displacement (from 4.2 to 4.0 litres). The XJC Cherokee concept, which was conceived in 1983 as a successor to the XJ series, was also a joint collaboration with AMC and Renault engineers until the design was inherited by the [Chrysler Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Corporation) in late 1987 after Renault divested AMC – which debuted in 1989 as the Jeep Concept 1 (evolving into the [Jeep Grand Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep_Grand_Cherokee) in April 1992).

The Renault-AMC marketing effort in passenger cars was unsuccessful compared to the popularity of Jeep vehicles. This was because, by the time the Renault range was ready, the [second energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_energy_crisis) was over, taking with it much of the desire for economical, compact cars. One exception was the [Renault Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Alliance) (an Americanised version of the [Renault 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_9)), which debuted for the 1983 model year. Assembled at AMC's [Kenosha, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenosha,_Wisconsin) plant,[^cite_note-TCAMAIH-57] the Alliance received *[Motor Trend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Trend)*'s domestic [Car of The Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Trend_Car_of_the_Year) award in 1983.[^cite_note-Renault_car_of_the_year_USA-59] The Alliance's 72% US content allowed it to qualify as a domestic vehicle, making it the first car with a foreign nameplate to win the award. (In 2000, *Motor Trend* did away with separate awards for domestic and imported vehicles.) A surprising side effect of the AMC linkup was that Renault felt the effects of the [Arab League boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_League_boycott_of_Israel) of companies doing business with [Israel](9282173.md), as AMC built Jeeps there under license. Plans to sell the Renault 9 in the Middle East were mothballed as a result.[^cite_note-AG78a-60]

Introductions in the US during the 1980s included the Renault Alliance GTA and GTA convertible – an automatic-top convertible with a 2.0 L engine – big for a car of its class and the [Renault Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Fuego) coupé. The Alliance was followed by the [Encore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Encore) (US version of the [Renault 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_11)), an Alliance-based hatchback.[^cite_note-TCAMAIH-57] In 1982, Renault become the second European automaker to build cars in the US, after [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen). However, bland styling and poor product quality proved insurmountable.[^cite_note-61]

Eventually, Renault sold AMC to [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Corporation) in 1987 after the assassination of Renault's chairman, [Georges Besse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Besse) by [Action directe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_directe_\(armed_group\)).[^cite_note-IDCH-28] The [Renault Medallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Medallion) (Renault 21 in Europe) sedan and wagon was sold from 1987 until 1989 through [Jeep-Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep-Eagle) dealerships. Jeep-Eagle was the division Chrysler created out of the former AMC. Renault imports ended after 1989. A completely new [full-sized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-size_car) 4-door sedan, the [Eagle Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Premier), was developed during the partnership between AMC and Renault. The Premier design, as well as its state-of-the-art manufacturing facility in [Bramalea, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramalea,_Ontario), Canada, were the starting point for the sleek [LH sedans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_LH_platform) such as the [Eagle Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Vision) and [Chrysler 300M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_300M).

In early 1979, as part of its attempts to expand into the US market, Renault bought a 20% stake in truck manufacturer [Mack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mack_Trucks).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-RCICIUSJWE-63] The aim of this operation was to make use of the company's extensive dealership network to distribute light trucks.[^cite_note-TMC-64] In 1983, Renault increased its stake in Mack to 44.6%.[^cite_note-RCICIUSJWE-63][^cite_note-TMC-64] In 1987, it transferred the ownership of a 42% stake to Renault Véhicules Industriels.[^cite_note-65]

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, Renault increased its involvement in motorsport, with novel inventions such as [turbochargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbochargers) in its [Formula One](10854.md) cars. Renault's Head of Engines, Georges Douin, orchestrated the installation of turbocharged engines across much of the Renault range beginning in 1980. 10% of all turbocharged European cars in 1984 were Renaults.[^cite_note-Car9.84-66] The company's road car designs were revolutionary in other ways also – the [Renault Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace) was one of the first [minivans](20828.md) and was to remain the most well-known minivan in Europe for the next two decades. The second-generation Renault 5, the European Car of the Year-winning [Renault 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_9), and the most luxurious Renault yet, the aerodynamic [25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_25), were all released in the early 1980s. At the same time, poor product quality damaged the brand. The ill-fated [Renault 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_14) may have been the culmination of these problems in the early 1980s.



1985 [Renault Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace), the first European multi-purpose vehicle



[Renault Twingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Twingo) popularized the city car in Europe from 1992. Six years later, most of its rivals began to enter the city car market.

### Restructuring (1981–1995)

Renaults were somewhat successful on both road and track, including the 1984 [Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace) launch, which was Europe's first multi-purpose vehicle, a dozen years before any competitor. However, Renault was losing a billion francs a month totaling ₣12.5 billion in 1984. The government intervened and [Georges Besse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Besse) was installed as chairman; he set about cutting costs dramatically, selling many of Renault's non-core assets (Volvo stake, [Gitane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitane), Eurocar, and Renix), withdrawing almost entirely from [motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsports), and laying off many employees.[^cite_note-R1985-67] This halved the deficit by 1986, but Besse was murdered by the communist [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) group *[Action Directe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_Directe_\(terrorist_group\))* in November 1986. He was replaced by Raymond Lévy, who continued Besse's initiatives, slimming the company enough that by the end of 1987, Renault was more or less financially stable. However, while Besse was convinced that Renault needed a presence in the North American market and wanted to push forward with restructuring AMC, Lévy, facing domestic losses from Renault at home, and losses from AMC in the US, along with the political climate that led to Besse's assassination, decided to sell AMC to [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler) that same year.

The [Renault 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_9), a small four-door family saloon, was voted [European Car of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Car_of_the_Year) on its 1981 launch. It sold well in France, but was eventually eclipsed by its sister vehicle, the [Renault 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_11) hatchback, as the hatchback body style became more popular in this size of the car. The [Renault 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_5) entered its second generation in 1984 and continued to sell well. The long-running [Renault 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_18) was replaced by the [Renault 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_21) early in 1986, adding a seven-seater estate badged as the Nevada or Savanna depending on where it was sold. Renault's top-of-the-range model in the 1980s was the [Renault 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_25), launched at the end of 1983.

In 1990, Renault strengthened its collaboration with Volvo by signing an agreement that allowed both companies to reduce vehicle conception costs and purchasing expenses. Renault had access to Volvo's expertise in upper market segments and in return, Volvo exploited Renault's designs for low and medium segments. In 1993, the two companies announced their intention to merge operations by 1 January 1994 and increased their cross-shareholding. The French accepted the merger, while Volvo shareholders rejected it.[^cite_note-R1985-67]

A revitalised Renault launched successful new cars in the early 1990s, accompanied by an improved marketing effort on European markets,[^cite_note-R1985-67] including the 5 replacement, the [Clio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Clio) in May 1990.[^cite_note-IDCH-28] The Clio was the first new model of a generation that replaced numeric identifiers with traditional nameplates. The Clio was voted [European Car of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Car_of_the_Year) soon after its launch, and was one of Europe's best-selling cars in the 1990s, proving even more popular than its predecessor. Other important launches included the third-generation [Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace) in 1996 and the innovative [Twingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Twingo) in 1992, the first car to be marketed as a city car MPV ([multi-purpose vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_vehicle)). The Twingo was roomier than any prior cars of its size range. Twingo sales reached 2.4 million in Europe, even though the original was only built for (Continental) [left-hand drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-_and_right-hand_traffic) markets.[^cite_note-68]



[Renault Scénic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Sc%C3%A9nic), awarded Car of the year in Europe in 1997 and the first

[^cite_note-ScenicLeaderMPV-69]

&#x20;car to be marketed as a compact MPV, is the most popular MPV in Europe for 20 years.

### Privatisation and the alliance era (1996–2019)

It was eventually decided that the company's state-owned status was a detriment. By 1994, plans to sell shares to public investors were officially announced.[^cite_note-R1985-67] The company was [privatised](24661.md) in 1996.[^cite_note-IDCH-28] This new freedom allowed the company to venture once again into markets in Eastern Europe and South America, including a new factory in Brazil and upgrades for its infrastructure in Argentina and Turkey. In December 1996, [General Motors Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_Europe) and Renault began to collaborate in the development of LCVs, starting with the second generation [Trafic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trafic) (codenamed X83).[^cite_note-70]



[Carlos Ghosn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ghosn) led Renault's cost cutting effort in 1998–2000.

Renault's financial problems were not all fixed by the privatisation, and Renault's president, [Louis Schweitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Schweitzer_\(businessman\)) gave to his then deputy, [Carlos Ghosn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ghosn), the task of confronting them. Ghosn elaborated a plan to cut costs for the period 1998–2000, reducing the workforce, revising production processes, standardising vehicle parts and pushing the launch of new models. The company also undertook organisational changes, introducing a [lean production system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_manufacturing) with delegate responsibilities inspired by Japanese systems (the "Renault Production Way"), reforming work methods, and centralising [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) at its Technocentre to reduce vehicle conception costs while accelerating such conception.[^cite_note-R1985-67]

After Volvo's exit, Renault searched for a new partner to cope with an industry that was consolidating. Talks with [BMW](3772.md), [Mitsubishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi), [Nissan](21240.md), PSA and others were held and yielded a relationship with Nissan, whose negotiations with Daimler had stalled.[^cite_note-71] Starting on 27 March 1999, the Renault–Nissan Alliance is the first of its kind involving a Japanese and a French company, including [cross-ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_ownership). Renault initially acquired a 36.8% stake at a cost of £2.7 billion in Nissan, while Nissan, in turn, took a 15% [non-voting stake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-voting_stock) in Renault.[^cite_note-72] Renault continued to operate as a stand-alone company, but with the intent to collaborate with its alliance partner to reduce costs. The same year, Renault bought a 51% majority stake of the Romanian company [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Dacia) for £408.5 million,[^cite_note-DEVELOPING-73] thus returning after 30 years, in which time the Romanians had built over 2 million cars that primarily consisted of local versions of the Renault 8, 12 and 20. In 2000, Renault acquired a [controlling stake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlling_interest) of the newly formed South Korean [Samsung Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Group)'s automotive division for £59.5 million.[^cite_note-74]

In Japan, Renault was formerly licensed by [Yanase Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanase_\(car_dealership\)), Japan's premier seller of imported cars. However, as a result of Renault's purchase of an interest in Nissan in 1999, Yanase cancelled its licensing contract with Renault in the spring of 2000, and Nissan took over as the sole licensee, hence sales of Renault vehicles in Japan were transferred from *Yanase Store* locations to *[Nissan Red Stage Store](21240.md#Japan)* locations.

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, Renault sold various assets to finance its inversions and acquisitions,[^cite_note-SELLOFF-75] refocusing itself as a car and van manufacturer. In 1999, the company sold its [industrial automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_automation) subsidiary, Renault Automation, to [Comau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comau) and its engine parts division to TWR Engine Components.[^cite_note-SELLOFF-75] In 2001, Renault sold its 50% stake in bus/coach manufacturer [Irisbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iveco_Bus) to co-owner [Iveco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iveco) and its [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) subsidiary, CAT France, to Global Automotive Logistics.[^cite_note-SELLOFF-75] Following the sale of Renault Véhicules Industriels to Volvo in 2001, the company retained a minority (but controlling) stake (20%) in the Volvo Group. In 2010 Renault reduced its shareholding to 6.5% and in December 2012 sold its remaining shares.[^cite_note-76] In 2004, Renault sold a 51% majority stake in its agricultural machinery division, Renault Agriculture, to CLAAS. In 2006, CLAAS increased its ownership to 80% and in 2008 took full control.[^cite_note-77]



The [Renault Avantime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Avantime) three-door MPV showcases Renault's distinctive design in early 2000s.

In the twenty-first century, Renault developed a reputation for distinctive, outlandish design. The second generation of the [Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Laguna) and [Mégane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_M%C3%A9gane) featured ambitious, angular designs that turned out to be successful, The 2000 Laguna was the second European car to feature ["keyless" entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyless_entry) and [ignition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyless_ignition).[^cite_note-78] Less successful were the company's more upmarket models. The [Avantime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Avantime), a unique [coupé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup%C3%A9) [multi-purpose vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_vehicle), sold poorly and was quickly discontinued while the luxury [Vel Satis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Vel_Satis) model also disappointed. However, the design inspired the lines of the second-generation Mégane, the maker's most successful car. As well as its distinctive styling, Renault was to become known for its car safety by the independent company Euro NCAP[^cite_note-Renault-safety-leader-NCAP-79] Thus, in 2001, the Laguna achieved a five-star rating,[^cite_note-Renault-safety-leader-NCAP-79] followed in 2004 by the [Modus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Modus),[^cite_note-80] and acquired control of [AvtoVAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AvtoVAZ) in 2008.[^cite_note-81]

In April 2010, Renault–Nissan announced an alliance with Daimler. Renault supplied Mercedes-Benz with its brand new 1.6-litre [turbo-diesel engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo-diesel) and Mercedes-Benz provided a 2.0 litre four-cylinder petrol engine to Renault–Nissan.[^cite_note-82] The resulting new alliance was to develop a replacement for the [Smart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_\(automobile\)) based on the Twingo.[^cite_note-83]

In February 2010, Renault opened a new production factory near [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier), Morocco, with an annual output capacity of 170,000 vehicles.[^cite_note-84] Initially, it manufactured the [Dacia Lodgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia_Lodgy) and [Dacia Dokker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia_Dokker) models followed in October 2013[^cite_note-85] by the second generation [Dacia Sandero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia_Sandero). The output capacity increased to 340,000 vehicles per year with the inauguration of a second production line.[^cite_note-86] The site is located in a dedicated [free trade area](10883.md), neighboring Tanger Automotive City.[^cite_note-87] According to Renault, the new factory emits zero carbon and industrial liquid discharges.[^cite_note-88] Over 100,000 vehicles were produced there in 2013. Renault expects to eventually increase production at the Tangier plant to 400,000 vehicles per year.[^cite_note-89]

In the 2010s, Renault increased its efforts to gain market share in the Chinese market. In 2013, it formed a joint venture with [Dongfeng Motor Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongfeng_Motor_Group) named as [Dongfeng Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongfeng_Renault), based on a failed previous venture with the Chinese company Sanjiang.[^cite_note-CHADAY-90] In December 2017, it signed an agreement with [Brilliance Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brilliance_Auto) to create a new joint venture (Renault Brilliance Jinbei) aimed at producing light commercial vehicles and minivans under the Renault, [Jinbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinbei_\(marque\)) and Huasong marques.[^cite_note-Wards-91] In December 2018, Renault announced it would acquire a "significant" stake in [JMCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JMCG)'s electric vehicle subsidiary [JMEV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JMEV).[^cite_note-92] In July 2019, Renault took a 50% majority stake from JMEV through capital increase.[^cite_note-nbd2-93] In April 2020, Renault announced it planned to withdraw from the Dongfeng Renault venture, transferring its stake to Dongfeng.[^cite_note-94]

In December 2012, the Algeria's National Investment Fund (FNI), the [Société Nationale de Véhicules Industriels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNVI) (SNVI), and Renault signed an agreement to establish a factory near the city of [Oran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oran), Algeria, with the aim of manufacturing Symbol units from 2014 onwards. The production output was estimated at 25,000 vehicles. The Algerian State has a 51% stake in the facility.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

In September 2013, Renault launched its brand in Indonesia, the world's fourth most populous country, with the aim of becoming one of the top European brands there until 2016. The model range at the time of the launch consisted of the [Duster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia_Duster) (locally assembled), the [Koleos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Koleos) and the [Mégane RS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9gane_Renault_Sport).[^cite_note-97] Later, the [Clio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Clio) and the [Captur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Captur) were also added.[^cite_note-98]

In April 2015, the [French government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_des_participations_de_l'%C3%89tat) upped its stake in Renault from 15% to 19.73% with the aim of blocking a resolution at the next annual general meeting that could reduce its control over the company.[^cite_note-99] In 2017, the government sold back shares and returned to a 15% stake as agreed with Renault.[^cite_note-100]

During 2016, Renault changed position on the viability of small ([B-segment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-segment)) diesel cars in Europe, as they become significantly more expensive when re-engineered to comply with [new emissions regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro6) as a result of the [Volkswagen emissions scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_emissions_scandal). Renault believes that all small and some mid-size ([C-segment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-segment)) will no longer be diesels by 2020.[^cite_note-101] However, on Friday, 13 January 2017, Renault shares fell as the [Paris prosecutor](1619543.md) started an investigation into possible exhaust emissions cheating.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] The company later recalled 15,000 cars for [emission testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_testing) and fixing.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] Renault, along with several other automobile companies, has been accused of manipulating the measurement equipment for NO<sub>*x*</sub> pollution from diesel cars. Independent tests carried out by the German car club [ADAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADAC) proved that, under normal driving conditions, diesel vehicles, including the [Renault Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace), exceeded legal [European emission limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_emission_limits) for nitrogen oxide (NO<sub>*x*</sub>) by more than 10 times.[^cite_note-adac2015-107][^cite_note-independent.co.uk_2015-10-01-108][^cite_note-theguardian.com_2015-10-01-109] Renault denied any foul play, stating compliance with French and European standards.[^cite_note-110]

In November 2018, Renault's CEO Ghosn was arrested by Japanese officials for allegedly underreporting his Nissan's salary, following an internal review conducted by the Japanese company. Renault traded shares fell more than 15% after the arrest was known.[^cite_note-111] After Ghosn's arrest, the [chief operating officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer) and company deputy chief [Thierry Bolloré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Bollor%C3%A9) became the acting CEO and the board director Philippe Lagayette the acting chairman.[^cite_note-112] In January 2019, following Ghosn's resignation, Renault announced it had appointed [Jean-Dominique Senard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Dominique_Senard) as chairman and the acting CEO Bolloré as CEO.[^cite_note-113] In October 2019, Bolloré was fired and replaced by Renault's [CFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_financial_officer) [Clotilde Delbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clotilde_Delbos) as acting CEO. Bolloré said his dismissal was a "coup" by Senard.[^cite_note-114] In January 2020, Renault announced it had named Italian [Luca de Meo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_de_Meo) as its new CEO, with him taking his post on 1 July. Delbos was named as his deputy.[^cite_note-115]

### COVID-19, Russo-Ukrainian war, and Alliance reforms (2019–present)

In May 2020, Renault announced a cost-cutting plan aimed at eliminating 15,000 jobs worldwide, about 10% of the company's workforce, due to falling sales and the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-116]

In January 2021, as part of a company revamp, Renault said it would divide its automotive division into four business units: Renault, Dacia and [Lada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada), Alpine, and Mobilize (the latter for new "new mobility services").[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

In April 2021, Renault said that its revenue fell by 1.1% from the beginning of 2021 until March and it will reduce car production and focus on models with higher margins.[^cite_note-119]

Following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), many international, particularly Western companies pulled out of Russia. Unlike most of its Western competitors, Renault was slow to announce divestment or scaling back of its operations in Russia, drawing criticism.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] On 21 March 2022, after a brief halt, Renault resumed production at the Renault Russia car plant near Moscow.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] In May 2022, Renault sold 100% of its shares in Renault Russia to the city of Moscow and its 67.69% interest in AvtoVAZ to the Russian Central Research and Development Automobile and Engine Institute for a symbolic one [rouble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble).[^cite_note-124] The agreement provides an option for Renault to buy back its interest in AvtoVAZ within six years.[^cite_note-125]

In November 2022, Renault said it plans to spin off the electric car development into a subsidiary company tentatively called [Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere_\(company\)).[^cite_note-powersplit-126] It also plans to spin off its powertrain production and development operations (including internal combustion engines and hybrid systems) into a [joint venture company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_project) named [Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_\(company\)) with [Geely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geely) as co-owner.[^cite_note-powersplit-126][^cite_note-127]

In January 2023, Renault said it intended to transfer almost 30% of its controlling stake in Nissan to a French trust (pending approval by both companies), reducing its shares with voting rights to a minority 15% and, in doing so, making Nissan shares in Renault to gain voting rights. The shareholding and voting ratio of both companies is set to be fixed in the future. The agreement also included Nissan investing in Ampere and projects in various markets.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] In February 2023, both companies approved the going-ahead for the shareholding changes. Final details and regulatory clearances for the transaction were set to be completed by the first quarter of 2023 and it would be done by the fourth quarter. The companies also approved joint projects and Nissan's Ampere investment.[^cite_note-130] The share transfer was completed in November 2023.[^cite_note-share-131]

On March 31, 2025, Renault announced plans to acquire Nissan’s 51% stake in its Indian manufacturing unit[^cite_note-132]

In June 2025, Renault announced [Luca de Meo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_de_Meo) had resigned as its CEO.[^cite_note-133] Renault began production of [drones](38212231.md) in Ukraine in 2025.[^cite_note-134] The decision was opposed by the [Confédération Genérale du Travail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Confederation_of_Labour_\(France\)) and [Force Ouvrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Force) unions.[^cite_note-135]

In July 2026, amid the Dieselgate scandal, British courts dismissed the allegations against five manufacturers, including Renault, ruling in their favor.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

## Motorsport

Main articles: [Renault Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Sport) and [Formula Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Renault)



1907 Renault-built Replica of its [French Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Grand_Prix) winner, one of 4 known to exist

Renault took part in motorsport at the beginning of the 20th century, promoted by [Marcel Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Renault)'s racing interests and over the years acquired companies with a sporting connection such as [Gordini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordini) and [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_\(car\)).

In the 1970s, Renault set up a dedicated motorsport division called [Renault Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Sport), and, in 1978, won the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md) with the [Renault Alpine A442](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Alpine_A442). Renault has also achieved success in both [rallying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallying) and in [Formula One](10854.md) over the past few decades.



Renault has won twelve F1 Championships as engine manufacturer in Formula One.

[^cite_note-144]

&#x20;Nigel Mansell, Damon Hill, Michael Schumacher, Alain Prost, Fernando Alonso, Sebastian Vettel and Jacques Villeneuve won eleven F1 driver's titles with cars powered by Renault engines.

The company has also backed various one-make single-seater series such as [Formula Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Renault) and the [Formula Renault 3.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Series_by_Renault). These two racing series were a step in the career of thousands of drivers, including Formula One champions [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso), [Sebastian Vettel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Vettel), [Kimi Räikkönen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimi_R%C3%A4ikk%C3%B6nen)[^cite_note-145] and [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton),[^cite_note-146] as well as [IndyCar](30446232.md) champion [Will Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Power).[^cite_note-147]

Renault Sport develops and manufactures the Renault Sport-badged cars,[^cite_note-148] as the Renault Clio RS (for Renault Sport) and the [Renault Mégane RS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9gane_Renault_Sport), which own the world records in their categories, such as the Nürburgring,[^cite_note-149] and the Suzuka circuit and awards from *[What Car?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Car%3F)*,[^cite_note-150] *[Evo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evo_\(magazine\))*, and other magazines.

### Formula One

Main article: [Renault in Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One)

Renault introduced the [turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbocharger) engine to Formula One when it debuted its first car, the [Renault RS01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_RS01) at [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverstone_Circuit) in 1977. The Renault team continued until 1986. From 1989 Renault supplied engines for the successful [Williams-Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams-Renault) car.

Renault took over the [Benetton Formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benetton_Formula) team in 2000[^cite_note-151] for the [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1_2001) season and renamed it [Renault F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_F1) in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1_2002). In [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Formula_One_season) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Formula_One_Season) the team won the Constructors' and Drivers' titles (with Fernando Alonso).[^cite_note-152] At the [2005 French Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_French_Grand_Prix) [Carlos Ghosn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ghosn) set out his policy regarding the company's involvement in motorsport:

"We are not in Formula One out of habit or tradition. We're here to show our talent and that we can do it properly ... Formula One is a cost if you don't get the results. Formula One is an investment if you do have them and know how to exploit them."

Renault powered the winning 2010 [Red Bull Racing](1173193.md) team, and took a similar role with its old team in December 2010, when it sold its final stake to the investment group [Genii Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genii_Capital), the main stakeholder[^cite_note-153] since December 2009,[^cite_note-154] ending Renault's direct role in running a F1 team for the second time.[^cite_note-155]

Renault bought the Enstone-based team for the 2016 season, rebranding it Renault.[^cite_note-RSPRRSPC-156][^cite_note-F1RTN-157] In 2021, the team was renamed [Alpine F1 Team](65240620.md) and became part of the new Alpine business unit, with Renault retained as the engine nameplate. Renault will stop manufacturing F1 engines at [Viry-Châtillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viry-Ch%C3%A2tillon) after the [2025](72768531.md) season.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

### Rallying

Renault has been involved in [rallying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallying) from an early era. Marcel Renault won the 1902 Rallye Paris-Vienna, but lost his life while competing in the 1903 Paris-Madrid rally.[^cite_note-RALL02-160]

During the 1950s and 1960s, Renault manufactured several small cars with [rear wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_wheel_drive) in some cases, as the [4CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_4CV), the [R8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_R8) or the [Dauphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Dauphine). These cars were well-adapted to the rally of the time, and the tuner [Amedee Gordini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amedee_Gordini) collaborated with its performance.[^cite_note-RALL02-160] In the 1950s the Renault Dauphine won several international rallies, including the 1956 [Mille Miglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mille_Miglia) and the 1958 [Monte Carlo Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Rally).[^cite_note-RALL01-161]

In 1973, Renault took control of [Automobiles Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_\(car_maker\)), a related company for several years, which was responsible for building successful rally cars such as the [A110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_A110).[^cite_note-RALL03-162] A highly evolved A110 won the first [World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rally_Championship), representing Alpine-Renault.[^cite_note-RALL02-160]

In 1976, the Alpine's competition department and the Gordini factory at Viry-Chatillon were merged into Renault Sport.[^cite_note-RALL03-162] The focus shifted to Formula One, although Renault achieved several victories including the 1981 Monte Carlo Rally with the [Renault 5 Turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_5_Turbo)[^cite_note-RALL01-161] before retirement from the world rally in late 1994.[^cite_note-RALL02-160]

Renault cars also participate of [cross-country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-roading) races, most prominently the [Dakar Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Rally). The Marreau brothers won the [1982 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Paris%E2%80%93Dakar_Rally) driving a [Renault 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_20) [Turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbocharger) [4x4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-wheel_drive) prototype.[^cite_note-163]

Later, Renault provided a Renault Megane platform and sponsored the [Schlesser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Schlesser)-Renault [Elf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elf_Aquitaine) [buggies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buggy_\(automobile\)) that won the 1999[^cite_note-164] and 2000 editions.[^cite_note-165] The 1999 car was the first [two-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-wheel_drive) Dakar's winner.[^cite_note-166]

Renaults won the [European Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Rally_Championship) four times, in 1970, 1999, 2004 and 2005.[^cite_note-167]

## Vehicle design

### Design

#### "Pre-design" era

During its early years, Renault only manufactured the cars' [chassis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chassis), while the bodywork was completed by [coachbuilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachbuilder). The first car with Renault's bodywork was the "Taxi de la Marne" introduced in 1905.[^cite_note-D1-217] Most Renault-made bodyworks were simple and [utilitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilitarianism) until the Reinastella unveiling in 1928. In the 1930s, Renault developed streamlined cars such as the Viva Grand Sport. In the 1950s, the company worked with [Ghia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrozzeria_Ghia) designers.[^cite_note-DMD-218]

#### Renault Styling

In 1961, with the assistance of the independent designer [Philippe Charbonneaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Charbonneaux) (responsible for the R8), the company created Renault Styling as a design department, led by [Gaston Juchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Juchet) since 1963.[^cite_note-DMD-218][^cite_note-GC-219] In 1975, [Robert Opron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Opron) was named chief designer[^cite_note-GC-219] and Renault Styling was divided into Interior, Exterior and Advanced Design groups.[^cite_note-D1-217]

In the 1960s, an in-house [computer-aided design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_design) (CAD) [computer-aided manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_manufacturing) (CAM) system called [UNISURF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNISURF) was introduced, led by [Pierre Bézier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_B%C3%A9zier) (who popularised [Bézier curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9zier_curve) and worked at Renault from 1933 until 1975).

#### Industrial Design Department

In 1987, Renault named [Patrick le Quément](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_le_Qu%C3%A9ment) as chief designer[^cite_note-D2-220] and created the Industrial Design Department to replace Renault Styling. The new division incorporated a new management system, with more technology and personnel. Renault gave it the same importance as Engineering and Product Planning, participating in product development.[^cite_note-D1-217][^cite_note-GC-219]

Le Quément was responsible for bold designs such as the Mégane II and the Vel Satis,[^cite_note-221] giving Renault a more coherent and stylish image.[^cite_note-222] In 1995, Design and Quality were merged under le Quément's direction.[^cite_note-D1-217][^cite_note-D2-220] Later, the new department moved to [Guyancourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyancourt)'s Technocentre, which also became the base for Engineering and Product Planning. The group was organised in three sections: Automobile Design; Truck, LCV and Bus Design; and Concept Cars and Advanced Design. During the next years, satellite centres opened in Spain (1999), Paris (2000), South Korea (2003), Romania (2007), India (2007),[^cite_note-D1-217][^cite_note-GC-219] Brazil (2008)[^cite_note-223] and China (2019).[^cite_note-224]

At the end of 2009, le Quément was replaced by [Laurens van den Acker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurens_van_den_Acker),[^cite_note-D2-220] who introduced the "cycle of life" concept to Renault's design.[^cite_note-225]

### Engineering and Product Planning



Renault [twin-turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin-turbo) engine

Most of Renault engineering was decentralised until 1998, when the Technocentre became the main Renault's engineering facility.[^cite_note-R1985-67][^cite_note-TECHNO-226] Satellite centres exist, including Renault Technologies Americas (with branches in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia and Mexico), Renault Technologies Romania (branches in Morocco, Russia, Slovenia and Turkey) and Renault Technologies Spain (branch in Portugal).[^cite_note-227] As of 2013, Renault's engineering section had over 6,500 employees worldwide, of which 34% were engineers and 63% technicians.[^cite_note-228] Engine development is in charge of a specific division, Renault Powertrains, with nearly 65 engineers.[^cite_note-229] Overseas engineering is increasing and research and design teams are in charge of adjusting existing vehicles to local needs and budgets.[^cite_note-230]

As of 2014, Engineering, and Product Planning, are directed by Gaspar Gascon Abellan[^cite_note-231] and [Philippe Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Klein)[^cite_note-232] respectively.

### Technocentre



View of the Technocentre from the Jardin des Gogottes

The Renault Technocentre (French pronunciation: [\[ʁəno tɛknɔˈsɑ̃tʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) is the main research and development facility. It is located in [Guyancourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyancourt). It covers 150 hectares (370 acres)[^cite_note-233] and integrates all departments involved in developing products and industrial processes (design, engineering and product planning) as well as supplier representatives. The Technocentre gathers more than 8,000 employees[^cite_note-234] and comprises three main sections: The Advance Precinct, The Hive and the prototype build centre. The Advance Precinct, a stepped structure surrounded by a lake, has design studios and other departments related to early design stages. The Hive is the tallest structure and includes research and engineering facilities dedicated to the development process of new vehicles. The prototype build centre is an extension of The Hive. The three main structures are accompanied by smaller technical buildings.[^cite_note-TECHNO-226]

The Technocentre was one of the first enterprises to have real-time life-size 3D modelling systems.[^cite_note-235]

### Renault Tech

Renault Tech is a division of [Renault Sport Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Sport), headquartered in [Les Ulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Ulis). It was established in 2008 and is in charge of modifying cars and vans for special purposes ([mobility cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_car), driver's school cars, and [business fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_vehicle)).[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

### Alliances

#### Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi

Main article: [Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault%E2%80%93Nissan%E2%80%93Mitsubishi_Alliance)

For many years, Renault had a 43.4% stake in Nissan, thereby giving it effective control *de jure*, and Nissan held a 15% stake (with no voting rights) in Renault.[^cite_note-261] As of November 2023 Renault owns 15% shares with voting rights. It has more shares in a French trust which it can use for voting in a few limited situations.[^cite_note-share-131]

As well as sharing engines and joint-development of zero-emissions technology, Nissan increased its presence in Europe by badging various Renault van models such as the [Renault Kangoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Kangoo)/[Nissan Kubistar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Kubistar), [Renault Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Master)/[Nissan Interstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Interstar) and the [Renault Trafic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trafic)/[Nissan Primastar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Primastar). Some passenger cars have also been badge-engineered, such as the Renault Clio-based [Nissan Platina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Platina) in Brazil. The "Renault Production System" standard used by all Renault factories borrowed extensively from the "Nissan Production Way" and resulted in Renault productivity improving by 15%. The alliance led to the loss of 21,000 jobs, and the closure of three assembly and two powertrain plants.[^cite_note-262]

In March 2010 the Renault-Nissan alliance opened its first joint facility in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), India, investing 45 billion rupees (US\$991.1 million).[^cite_note-263] The facility builds the [Nissan Micra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Micra). The Renault Fluence and [Renault Koleos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Koleos) are intended to be assembled there from [completely knocked-down units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knock-down_kit). As a result of opening its own factory, Renault ended its five-year [Mahindra Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_Renault_Limited) joint venture with [Mahindra & Mahindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_&_Mahindra) company to make and sell the [Renault Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Logan) in India.[^cite_note-264]

#### Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi and Daimler alliance

On 7 April 2010 Ghosn and [Daimler AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimler_AG) CEO [Dieter Zetsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Zetsche) announced a partnership between the three companies.[^cite_note-265] Daimler acquired a 3.10 per cent stake in Renault-Nissan and Renault and Nissan each took a 1.55 per cent stake in Daimler.[^cite_note-266]

#### Geely alliance

Main articles: [Horse Powertrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_Powertrain) and [Renault Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Korea)

In January 2022, Renault and Chinese manufacturer [Geely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geely) signed an agreement by which Renault's South Korean subsidiary, [Renault Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Korea), would produce vehicles based on Geely [Compact Modular Architecture platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Modular_Architecture_platform),[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268] initially intended for the domestic market. In December 2022, Geely acquired a 34% stake of Renault Korea through capital increase as part of their partnership, although the company would continue to be majority owned by Renault and a consolidated subsidiary of it.[^cite_note-Geelyshare2-269][^cite_note-270]

In May 2024, Renault and Geely established a [joint venture](538782.md) [holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parent_company) aimed at producing [powertrains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powertrains), including [internal combustion engines](41228673.md) (ICE) and [hybrid systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_vehicle).[^cite_note-271] The venture dates back to an agreement between the two companies in November 2022.[^cite_note-272] Both Geely and Renault were set to transfer their [intellectual property](14724.md) for ICEs and hybrid systems to the venture holding, and plans to supply engines to [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Dacia) (part of Renault Group), [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Cars), [Lynk & Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynk_&_Co), [Proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_Holdings) (part of Geely)[^cite_note-273] as well as [Nissan](21240.md) and [Mitsubishi Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Motors) from the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance.[^cite_note-auto-274]

In June 2025, Renault, [Geely Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geely_Auto) and [Geely Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geely) announced to establish a joint venture to manufacture electric vehicle in Brazil. The JV company would be owned 73.57% by Renault, 21.29% by Geely Auto, 5.11% by Geely Holding, and 0.03% by an independent third party.[^cite_note-\:2-275] The company would produce and distribute vehicles under the Renault and Geely brands in Brazil, with shared distribution infrastructure and a nationwide dealer network.[^cite_note-276]

#### American Motors

In 1979, Renault entered into an agreement with [American Motors Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Motors_Corporation) (AMC) to sell cars in the US.[^cite_note-277] A year later, Renault acquired a 22.5% interest in AMC.[^cite_note-accent-278] This was not the first time the two companies had worked together. In the early 1960s, Renault assembled [CKD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_knock_down) kits and marketed Ramblers in France.[^cite_note-279] In 1982, Renault increased its stake in AMC to 46.4%.[^cite_note-280] The Renault Alliance/Encore (a modified version of the Renault 9 and 11) entered production in the US, but following AMC's continued decline, Renault withdrew from the US in 1987 and sold its share to Chrysler.[^cite_note-281]

#### Proposed alliances

On 30 June 2006, the media reported that General Motors convened an emergency board meeting to discuss a proposal by shareholder [Kirk Kerkorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_Kerkorian) to form an alliance with Renault-[Nissan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Motor_Co.,_Ltd.). However, GM CEO [Richard Wagoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Wagoner) felt that an alliance would disproportionately benefit Renault's shareholders and that GM should receive compensation accordingly. Talks between GM and Renault ended on 4 October 2006.[^cite_note-282]

In 2007, Renault-Nissan entered talks with Indian manufacturer [Bajaj Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto) to develop a new ultra-low-cost car along the lines of the [Tata Nano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Nano).[^cite_note-283] Renault's existing partner in India, Mahindra, was not interested in the project. The proposed joint venture did not come to fruition and in late 2009 the companies announced that Bajaj would develop and manufacturer the vehicle and supply Renault-Nissan with completed cars.[^cite_note-284]

On 7 October 2008 a Renault executive said the company was interested in acquiring or partnering with Chrysler.[^cite_note-285] On 11 October 2008, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), Nissan and Renault had all been in discussions over the past month with Chrysler's owner [Cerberus Capital Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerberus_Capital_Management) about acquiring Chrysler.[^cite_note-286]

In May 2019, [Fiat Chrysler Automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Chrysler_Automobiles) proposed merging its business with Renault.[^cite_note-287] The proposal was later withdrawn.[^cite_note-288]

## Marketing and branding

Renault markets its products under the marques Renault, Dacia and Alpine.[^cite_note-300] Renault founded in 2021 the Mobilize brand, which started in 2024 with the marketing of electric mobility services and vehicles.

### Renault badge

Renault's first badge was introduced in 1900 and consisted of the Renault brothers' intertwined initials. When the company started mass production in 1906, it adopted a gear-shaped logo with a car inside it. After World War I the company used a logo depicting an FT tank. In 1923 it introduced a new circle-shaped badge, which was replaced by the "diamond" or lozenge in 1925.[^cite_note-Dinesh-301] The [lozenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozenge_\(shape\)) of Renault means a diamond that expresses the brand's firm desire to project a strong and consistent corporate image.[^cite_note-EDM-302]

The Renault diamond logo has been through many iterations.[^cite_note-Dinesh-301] To modernise its image, Renault asked [Victor Vasarely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Vasarely) to design its new logo in 1972. The transformed logo maintained the diamond shape. The design was later revised to reflect the more rounded lines of the brand's new styling cues.[^cite_note-Renault.com-303] The current badge has been in use since 1992.

The logo for web and print use was updated three times thereafter. In 2002 a more realistic representation inside a yellow rectangle was made which is still used as the [Renault Trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trucks) logo albeit in red. In 2004 the logo received the *Renault Identité* typeface. In 2007, Saguez & Partners produced a version with the wordmark and logo inside a square.[^cite_note-Dinesh-301][^cite_note-304]

In April 2015, Renault introduced new designs to differentiate the company from the product brand, as part of the 'Passion for life' campaign. The new brand logo replaced the yellow background with a yellow stripe. A new typeface was also introduced. A corporate logo was unveiled at the 2015 Annual General Meeting, incorporating Renault, Dacia and Renault Samsung Motors.[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306]

January 2021 saw the introduction of a new flat diamond logo alongside the Renault 5 Prototype electric concept car. The logo received so much positive feedback that Renault officially introduced the new symbol as its logo in March 2021, according to Renault's design director [Gilles Vidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_Vidal), who joined the group in 2020. They plan to introduce the new diamond on many online platforms in June 2021 and the first model with the new logo featured will be revealed in 2022.[^cite_note-307]

The yellow associated with the company appeared initially in the diamond badge of 1946, when Renault was nationalised.[^cite_note-Dinesh-301][^cite_note-EDM-302]

### Typeface

#### Renault MN

Both the Renault logo and its documentation (technical as well as commercial) historically used *Renault MN,* a custom [typeface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface) developed by British firm [Wolff Olins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolff_Olins). This type of family is said to have been designed mainly to save costs at a time when the use of typefaces was costly.

A retail version of the font family was sold by URW++ as Renault.[^cite_note-310]

#### Renault Identité

In 2004, French typeface designer [Jean-François Porchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Fran%C3%A7ois_Porchez) was commissioned to design a replacement. This was shown in October of that year and was called *Renault Identité*.[^cite_note-311] The OpenType font family was developed from the Renault logotype created by Éric de Berranger.[^cite_note-312][^cite_note-313]

#### Helvetica

Since 2007, as part of the Saguez & Partners revamp, all graphic advertising makes use of [Helvetica Neue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetica_Neue) Condensed.[^cite_note-314]

#### Renault Life

The Renault Life font family was built by Fontsmith Limited, based on the foundry's FS Hackney font family.[^cite_note-316]

The family consists of six fonts in three weights (Life, Regular, and Bold) and one width, with complimentary italic.

### L'Atelier Renault Paris

Renault's flagship showroom, L'Atelier Renault (French pronunciation: [\[latəlje ʁəno\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)), is located on the [Champs-Élysées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champs-%C3%89lys%C3%A9es) in Paris, with other manufacturers such as Peugeot, Citroën and Toyota. It opened in November 2000, located on the site of Pub Renault, which operated from 1963 until 1999. The first Renault venue at the location was the *Magasin Renault* in 1910, a pioneering car showroom.[^cite_note-317][^cite_note-318]

L'Atelier features a Renault Boutique as well as regular exhibitions featuring Renault and Dacia cars. An upmarket restaurant is located on the second floor, looking out onto the Champs-Élysées. The ground floor can hold up to five exhibitions at any one time. As of March 2009, 20 million visitors had visited L'Atelier Renault.[^cite_note-319][^cite_note-320][^cite_note-321]

### Renault Classic

Main article: [Renault Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Classic)

Renault Classic is a department within Renault that seeks to collect, preserve and exhibit notable vehicles from the company's history. Originally named Histoire & Collection, the collection was assembled in 2002 and its workshops formally opened on 24 April 2003.[^cite_note-Smith2008-322]

### Music

Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, Renault's European advertising made extensive use of [Robert Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Palmer)'s song "[Johnny and Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_and_Mary)".[^cite_note-323] Television advertisements initially used Palmer's original version, while a range of special recordings in different styles were produced during the 1990s, most famously the acoustic interpretation by [Martin Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Taylor_\(guitarist\)) that he released on his album *Spirit of Django*.

### Sponsorship

Renault has sponsored films as an advertising technique since 1899. A Renault Voiturette Type A, driven by Louis Renault, appeared in one of the [Lumières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_and_Louis_Lumi%C3%A8re)' early films.[^cite_note-324][^cite_note-COLCIN-325] Between 1914 and 1940, the company commissioned a series of documentary films to promote its industrial activities.[^cite_note-326] Renault also backed some films set in Africa during the 1920s to promote the reliability of its products on tough conditions.[^cite_note-COLCIN-325] Since 1983, the company sponsors the [Cannes Film Festival](101460.md)[^cite_note-327] and it has also sponsored other festivals as the [Venice Film Festival](32497.md), the Marrakech Film Festival[^cite_note-328] and the [BFI London Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI_London_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-329]

Through its foundations and institutes, Renault funds projects around the world that focus on: education through scholarships,[^cite_note-330] road safety[^cite_note-331] and diversity.
