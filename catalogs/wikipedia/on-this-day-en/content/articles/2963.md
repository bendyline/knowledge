**Assassination** is the willful killing, by a sudden or secret attack, of a person—especially a [prominent or important](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_important_person) one—typically for political, social, or ideological reasons.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Assassinations may be ordered by individuals or organizations, and carried out by themselves or their hired accomplices. Acts of assassination have been performed since [ancient times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history). A person who carries out an assassination is called an **assassin**.[^cite_note-4]

## Etymology

Main article: [Order of Assassins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Assassins)



[Masyaf Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masyaf_Castle) in [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama), [Syria](7515849.md). It was the headquarters of the Assassins in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant). Picture taken in 2017

*Assassin* comes from medieval Italian and French *Assissini* or *Assassini*, believed to derive from the word *[hashshashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashshashin)* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حشّاشين, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ḥaššāšīn*),[^cite_note-5] and shares its etymological roots with *[hashish](14415.md)* ([/hæˈʃiːʃ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst\_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst\_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> or [/ˈhæʃiːʃ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/1c/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst\_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish\_%28alt%29.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst\_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish\_%28alt%29.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/1c/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish_%28alt%29.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-hashish_%28alt%29.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; from حشيش *_ḥašīš_*).[^cite_note-OED-6][^cite_note-The_Assassins:_a_radical_sect_in_Islam-7] It referred to a group of [Nizari Ismailis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizari_Isma'ilism) known as the [Order of Assassins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Assassins) who worked against various political targets.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

Founded by [Hassan-i Sabbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan-i_Sabbah), the Assassins were active in the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East) from the 11th to the 13th centuries. The group killed members of the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_dynasty), [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire), [Fatimid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate), and [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) elite for political and religious reasons.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

Although it is commonly believed that members of the Order of Assassins were under the influence of [hashish](14415.md) during their killings or during their indoctrination, there is debate as to whether these claims have merit, with many Eastern writers and an increasing number of Western academics coming to believe that drug-taking was not the key feature behind the name.[^cite_note-C\:AH-9]

The term "assassinare" (to assassinate) was used in [Medieval Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin) from the mid 13th century.[^cite_note-OED-6]

The earliest known use of the verb "to assassinate" in printed English was by [Matthew Sutcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Sutcliffe) in *A Briefe Replie to a Certaine Odious and Slanderous Libel, Lately Published by a Seditious Jesuite*, a pamphlet printed in 1600, five years before it was used in *[Macbeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macbeth)* by [William Shakespeare](32897.md) (1605).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Use in history

Main article: [History of assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_assassination)

### Ancient to medieval times

Assassination is one of the oldest tools of [power politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_politics). It dates back at least as far as recorded history.[^cite_note-12]

The Egyptian [pharaoh](23294.md) [Teti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teti), of the [Old Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kingdom_of_Egypt) [Sixth Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) (23rd century BC), is thought to be the earliest known victim of assassination, though written records are scant and thus evidence is circumstantial. Two further ancient Egyptian monarchs are more explicitly recorded to have been assassinated; [Amenemhat I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amenemhat_I) of the [Middle Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Kingdom_of_Egypt) [Twelfth Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) (20th century BC) is recorded to have been assassinated in his bed by his palace guards for reasons unknown (as related in the *[Instructions of Amenemhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instructions_of_Amenemhat)*); meanwhile [contemporary judicial records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Papyrus_of_Turin) relate the assassination of [New Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt) [Twentieth Dynasty](28830264.md) monarch [Ramesses III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_III) in 1155 BC as part of a [failed coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harem_conspiracy). Between 550 BC and 330 BC, seven Persian kings of the [Achaemenid Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) were murdered. *[The Art of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_War)*, a 5th-century BC Chinese military treatise, mentions tactics of assassination and its merits.[^cite_note-13]

In the [Hebrew Bible (Tanakh)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible), King [Joash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehoash_of_Judah) of [Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah) was assassinated by his own servants;[^cite_note-14] [Joab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joab) assassinated [Absalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absalom), [King David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_David)'s son;[^cite_note-15] King [Sennacherib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sennacherib) of Assyria was assassinated by his own sons;[^cite_note-16] and [Jael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jael) assassinated [Sisera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisera).[^cite_note-17]

[Chanakya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanakya) (c. 350–283 BC) wrote about assassinations in detail in his political treatise *[Arthashastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthashastra)*. His student [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya), the founder of the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire), later made use of assassinations against some of his enemies.[^cite_note-18]

Some famous assassination victims are [Philip II of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon) (336 BC), the father of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), and Roman dictator [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar) (44 BC).[^cite_note-19] A number of [Roman emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperors) were assassinated, as did many of the Muslim [Shia Imams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Imam) hundreds of years later. Three successive Rashidun caliphs ([Umar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar), [Uthman Ibn Affan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman_Ibn_Affan), and [Ali ibn Abi Talib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_ibn_Abi_Talib)) were assassinated in early civil conflicts between Muslims. The practice was also well known in ancient China, as in [Jing Ke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing_Ke)'s failed assassination of [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)) king [Ying Zheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang) in 227 BC. Whilst many assassinations were performed by individuals or small groups, there were also specialized units who used a collective group of people to perform more than one assassination. The earliest were the [sicarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicarii) in 6 AD, who predated the Middle Eastern [Assassins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Assassins) and Japanese [shinobis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinobi) by centuries.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Ross-21]

During the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), [tyrannicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrannicide) became very common in Western Europe.[^cite_note-22]

### Modern history



18th-century depiction of the [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Silent#Assassination) of [William the Silent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Silent) by [Balthasar Gérard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balthasar_G%C3%A9rard) on 10 July 1584

During the 16th and 17th centuries, international lawyers began to voice condemnation of assassinations of leaders. [Balthazar Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balthazar_Ayala) has been described as "the first prominent jurist to condemn the use of assassination in foreign policy".[^cite_note-Thomas2000-23] [Alberico Gentili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberico_Gentili) condemned assassinations in a 1598 publication where he appealed to the self-interest of leaders: (i) assassinations had adverse short-term consequences by arousing the ire of the assassinated leader's successor, and (ii) assassinations had the adverse long-term consequences of causing disorder and chaos.[^cite_note-Thomas2000-23] [Hugo Grotius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Grotius)'s works on the law of war strictly forbade assassinations, arguing that killing was only permissible on the battlefield.[^cite_note-Thomas2000-23] In the modern world, the killing of important people began to become more than a tool in power struggles between rulers themselves and was also used for political symbolism, such as in the [propaganda of the deed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_of_the_deed).[^cite_note-Gillen-24]

In Japan, a group of assassins called the [Four Hitokiri of the Bakumatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Hitokiri_of_the_Bakumatsu) killed a number of people, including [Ii Naosuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ii_Naosuke) who was the head of administration for the Tokugawa shogunate, during the [Boshin War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boshin_War).[^cite_note-25] Most of the assassinations in Japan were committed with bladed weaponry, a trait that was carried on into modern history. A video-record exists of the [assassination of Inejiro Asanuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Inejir%C5%8D_Asanuma), using a sword.[^cite_note-chun-26]

In 1895, a group of Japanese [Rōnin assassins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C5%8Dnin) [killed the Korean queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Empress_Myeongseong) (and posthumously empress) Myeongseong.[^cite_note-27]

In the United States, from 1865 to 1963, four presidents—[Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abraham_Lincoln), [James A. Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_James_A._Garfield), [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_William_McKinley) and [John F. There have been at least [20 known attempts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_presidential_assassination_attempts) on U.S. presidents' lives.[^cite_note-28]

In Austria, the [assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand) and his wife [Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie,_Duchess_of_Hohenberg) was carried out in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914, by [Gavrilo Princip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavrilo_Princip), a Serbian nationalist. He is blamed for igniting [World War I](4764461.md). [Reinhard Heydrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Heydrich) died after an attack by British-trained Czechoslovak soldiers on behalf of the Czechoslovak government in exile in [Operation Anthropoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Anthropoid),[^cite_note-29] and knowledge from decoded transmissions allowed the United States to carry out [a targeted attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Isoroku_Yamamoto), killing Japanese [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [Isoroku Yamamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoroku_Yamamoto) while he was travelling by plane.[^cite_note-McNaughton2006-30]

During the 1930s and 1940s, [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)'s [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD) carried out numerous assassinations outside of the Soviet Union, such as the killings of [Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Ukrainian_Nationalists) leader [Yevhen Konovalets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevhen_Konovalets), [Ignace Poretsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignace_Poretsky), [Fourth International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_International) secretary Rudolf Klement, [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky), and the Workers' Party of Marxist Unification ([POUM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POUM)) leadership in [Catalonia](6822.md).[^cite_note-31] India's "Father of the Nation", [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi), was [shot to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Mahatma_Gandhi) on January 30, 1948, by [Nathuram Godse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathuram_Godse).[^cite_note-32]

The African-American civil rights activist [Martin Luther King Jr.](20076.md) was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Martin_Luther_King_Jr.) on April 4, 1968, at the Lorraine Motel (now the [National Civil Rights Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Civil_Rights_Museum)) in [Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee), Tennessee. Three years prior, another African-American civil rights activist, [Malcolm X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_X), was assassinated at the [Audubon Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audubon_Ballroom) on February 21, 1965.[^cite_note-33]

### Cold War to present

See also: [Cold War](325329.md) and [War on terror](13425800.md)



[Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi)'s blood-stained [sari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari) and belongings at the time of her assassination. She was the [prime minister of India.](24452.md)

Most major powers repudiated [Cold War](325329.md) assassination tactics, but many allege that was merely a smokescreen for political benefit and that covert and illegal training of assassins continues today, with Russia, Israel, the U.S., [Argentina](18951905.md), Paraguay, Chile, and other nations accused of engaging in such operations.[^cite_note-34] After the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) of 1979, the new Islamic government of Iran began an international campaign of assassination that lasted into the 1990s. At least 162 killings in 19 countries have been linked to the senior leadership of the [Islamic Republic of Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-35] The campaign came to an end after the [Mykonos restaurant assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos_restaurant_assassinations) because a German court publicly implicated senior members of the government and issued arrest warrants for [Ali Fallahian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fallahian), the head of Iranian intelligence.[^cite_note-36] Evidence indicates that Fallahian's personal involvement and individual responsibility for the murders were far more pervasive than his current indictment record represents.[^cite_note-37]

In India, Prime Ministers [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi) and her son [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi) (neither of whom was related to [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi), who had himself been assassinated in 1948), were assassinated in 1984 and 1991 in what were linked to [separatist](286527.md) movements in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) and northern [Sri Lanka](26750.md), respectively.[^cite_note-38]

On October 19, 1984, 37-year-old Father [Jerzy Popiełuszko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko), a Roman Catholic priest in [Warsaw](32908.md), was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko) by three [Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Service_\(Poland\)) agents in [Włocławek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82oc%C5%82awek), Poland.

In 1994, the [assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira) during the [Rwandan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Civil_War) sparked the [Rwandan genocide](676659.md).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

In Israel, Prime Minister [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin) was assassinated on November 4, 1995, by [Yigal Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yigal_Amir), who opposed the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

On September 2, 2022, a 35 year old Brazilian national attempted to assassinate the then vice-president of Argentina, [Cristina Fernández de Kirchner](1644780.md). However, the attempt was unsuccessful because the assassin's gun jammed.[^cite_note-43]

On September 10, 2025, American right-wing political activist and media personality [Charlie Kirk](53245776.md) was [assassinated](81040139.md) while speaking at a [Turning Point USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Point_USA) rally at [Utah Valley University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Valley_University).[^cite_note-44]

#### United States government killing of citizens

In 2012, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* revealed that the Obama administration maintained a "[kill list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_list)" containing terrorism suspects.[^cite_note-45] The list is sometimes referred to as a "disposition matrix", and President Obama made a final decision on whether anyone listed would be killed, without court oversight and without trial.[^cite_note-46] In September 2011, American citizens [Anwar Al-Awlaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Al-Awlaki) and [Samir Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samir_Khan) were assassinated in [Yemen](350939.md) by the United States government via drone strikes. Two weeks later, Awlaki's 16-year-old son, also an American citizen, was killed in a strike targeting [Ibrahim al-Banna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_al-Banna), a senior operative in [Al-Qaeda](1921.md).[^cite_note-legalethical-47][^cite_note-48] Al-Banna was not killed in the strike.[^cite_note-legalethical-47]

## Further motivations

### As a military and foreign policy doctrine

See also: [Manhunt (military)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhunt_\(military\)), [Decapitation (military strategy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapitation_\(military_strategy\)), and [Covert operation](613453.md)



The functions of the [ninja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninja) included espionage, [sabotage](29462.md) and assassination.

Assassination for military purposes has long been espoused: [Sun Tzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Tzu), writing around 500 BC, argued in favor of using assassination in his book *[The Art of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_War)*. Over 2000 years later, in his book *[The Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince)*, [Machiavelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machiavelli) also advises rulers to assassinate enemies whenever possible to prevent them from posing a threat.[^cite_note-49]

For similar and additional reasons, assassination has also sometimes been used in the conduct of [foreign policy](233404.md). The costs and benefits of such actions are difficult to compute. It may not be clear whether the assassinated leader gets replaced with a more or less competent successor, whether the assassination provokes ire in the state in question, whether the assassination leads to souring domestic public opinion, and whether the assassination provokes condemnation from third-parties.[^cite_note-iraja-50][^cite_note-Thomas2000-23] One study found that perceptual biases held by leaders often negatively affect decision making in that area, and decisions to go forward with assassinations often reflect the vague hope that any successor might be better.[^cite_note-iraja-50]

In both military and foreign policy assassinations, there is the risk that the target could be replaced by an even more competent leader, or that such a killing (or a failed attempt) will prompt the masses to contemn the killers and support the leader's cause more strongly. Faced with particularly brilliant leaders, that possibility has in various instances been risked, such as in the attempts to kill the Athenian [Alcibiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcibiades) during the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War). A number of additional examples from [World War II](32927.md) show how assassination was used as a tool:

- The [assassination of Reinhard Heydrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Anthropoid) in [Prague](23844.md) on May 27, 1942, by the British and Czechoslovak government-in-exile. That case illustrates the difficulty of comparing the benefits of a foreign policy goal (strengthening the legitimacy and influence of the [Czechoslovak government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_government-in-exile) in London) against the possible costs resulting from an assassination (the [Lidice massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidice_massacre)).

  [^cite_note-iraja-50]

- The American interception of Admiral [Isoroku Yamamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoroku_Yamamoto)'s plane during World War II after his travel route had been decrypted.

- [Operation Gaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gaff) was a planned British commando raid to capture or kill the German field marshal [Erwin Rommel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Rommel), also known as "The Desert Fox".

  [^cite_note-Skor-51]

Use of assassination has continued in more recent conflicts:

- During the [Vietnam War](32611.md), the US engaged in the [Phoenix Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Program) to assassinate [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong) leaders and sympathizers. It killed between 6,000 and 41,000 people, with official "targets" of 1,800 per month.

The assassination of the military leaders was part of escalating tensions between the US and Iran and the [American-led intervention in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American-led_intervention_in_Iraq_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)).

### As a tool of insurgents

The [Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republican_Army_\(1917%E2%80%9322\)) guerrillas in 1919 to 1921 killed many [Royal Irish Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Constabulary) Police intelligence officers during the [Irish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_War_of_Independence). [Michael Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Collins_\(Irish_leader\)) set up a special unit, [the Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Squad_\(IRA_unit\)), for that purpose, which intimidated many policemen into resigning from the force. The Squad's activities peaked with the killing of 14 British agents in [Dublin](8504.md) on [Bloody Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloody_Sunday_\(1920\)) in 1920.[^cite_note-58]

The tactic was used again by the [Provisional IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_IRA) during [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles) in Northern Ireland (1969–1998). Assassination of [unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionism_in_the_United_Kingdom) politicians and activists was one of a number of methods used in the [Provisional IRA campaign 1969–1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_IRA_campaign_1969%E2%80%931997). The IRA also attempted to assassinate British Prime Minister [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher) by [bombing the Conservative Party Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_hotel_bombing) in a [Brighton](49796.md) hotel.[^cite_note-59] [Loyalist paramilitaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_loyalism) retaliated by killing Catholics at random and assassinating [Irish nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalist) politicians.[^cite_note-60]

[Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_people) separatists [ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_\(separatist_group\)) in Spain assassinated many security and political figures since the late 1960s, notably the president of the [Francoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist) government of Spain, [Luis Carrero Blanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Carrero_Blanco), 1st Duke of Carrero-Blanco Grandee of Spain, in 1973. In the early 1990s, it also began to target academics, journalists and local politicians who publicly disagreed with it.[^cite_note-61]

The [Red Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Brigades) in Italy carried out assassinations of [political figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician) and, to a lesser extent, so did the [Red Army Faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_Faction) in [Germany](11867.md) in the 1970s and the 1980s.[^cite_note-62]

In the [Vietnam War](32611.md), communist insurgents routinely assassinated government officials and individual civilians deemed to offend or rival the revolutionary movement. Such attacks, along with widespread military activity by insurgent bands, almost brought the [Ngo Dinh Diem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngo_Dinh_Diem) regime to collapse before the US intervened.[^cite_note-63]

## Psychology

A major study about assassination attempts in the US in the second half of the 20th century came to the conclusion that 80% of perpetrators spend copious amounts of time planning and preparing for their attempts, and almost all (97%) did so when their attempts were successful. Assassinations are thus rarely "impulsive" actions, mainly motivated by the desire to "save the world" (+40% of assassination attempts), to become notorious (38%) or to get killed (22%).[^cite_note-SS-64]

However, about 25% of the actual attackers were found to be [delusional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delusion), a figure that rose to 60% with "near-lethal approachers" (people apprehended before reaching their targets). That shows that while mental instability plays a role in many modern assassinations, the more delusional attackers are less likely to succeed in their attempts. The report also found that around two-thirds of attackers had previously been arrested, not necessarily for related offenses; 44% had a history of serious depression, and 39% had a history of substance abuse.[^cite_note-SS-64]

The report found that most perpetrators were men (86%) or were unemployed (52%), and almost half of them had some form of college degree (46%) or served in military (42%). Women were more likely than men to carry out their attacks before getting arrested.[^cite_note-SS-64]

## Techniques

### Modern methods

Explosives, especially the [car bomb](155282.md), become far more common in modern history, with [grenades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenades) and remote-triggered land mines also used, especially in the Middle East and the Balkans; the initial attempt on [Archduke Franz Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria)'s life was with a grenade. With heavy weapons, the [rocket-propelled grenade](26286.md) (RPG) has become a useful tool given the popularity of armored cars (discussed below), and Israeli forces have pioneered the use of aircraft-mounted missiles.[^cite_note-65]



[Carcano Model 38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_assassination_rifle) of [Lee Harvey Oswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Harvey_Oswald), the assassin of President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy)



[Derringer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derringer) of [John Wilkes Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilkes_Booth), the assassin of President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln)

A [sniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper) with a precision rifle is often used in fictional assassinations; however, certain pragmatic difficulties attend long-range shooting, including finding a hidden shooting position with a clear line of sight, detailed advance knowledge of the intended victim's travel plans, the ability to identify the target at long range, and the ability to score a first-round lethal hit at long range, which is usually measured in hundreds of meters. A dedicated [sniper rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper_rifle) is also expensive, often costing thousands of dollars because of the high level of precision machining and hand finishing required to achieve extreme accuracy.

Despite their comparative disadvantages, handguns are more easily concealable and so are much more commonly used than rifles. Of the 74 principal incidents evaluated in a major study about assassination attempts in the US in the second half of the 20th century, 51% were undertaken by a handgun, 30% with a rifle or shotgun, 15% used knives, and 8% explosives (the use of multiple weapons/methods was reported in 16% of all cases).[^cite_note-SS-64]

In the case of state-sponsored assassination, poisoning can be more easily denied. [Georgi Markov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Markov), a dissident from [Bulgaria](3415.md), was assassinated by [ricin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricin) poisoning. A tiny pellet containing the poison was injected into his leg through a specially designed [umbrella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_umbrella). Widespread allegations involving the Bulgarian government and the [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) have not led to any legal results. However, after the fall of the Soviet Union, it was learned that the KGB had developed an umbrella that could inject ricin pellets into a victim, and two former KGB agents who defected stated that the agency assisted in the murder.[^cite_note-66] The [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) made several [attempts to assassinate Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_attempts_on_Fidel_Castro); many of the schemes involving poisoning his cigars. In the late 1950s, the KGB assassin [Bohdan Stashynsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohdan_Stashynsky) killed Ukrainian nationalist leaders [Lev Rebet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Rebet) and [Stepan Bandera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Bandera) with a spray gun that fired a jet of poison gas from a crushed [cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanide) ampule, making their deaths look like heart attacks.[^cite_note-67] A 2006 case in the UK concerned the [assassination of Alexander Litvinenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisoning_of_Alexander_Litvinenko) who was given a lethal dose of radioactive [polonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonium)-210, possibly passed to him in aerosol form sprayed directly onto his food.[^cite_note-68]

## Targeted killing

Main article: [Targeted killing](33928336.md)



[Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Atomics_MQ-1_Predator) [combat drone](337755.md); sometimes used in targeted killings

[Targeted killing](33928336.md) is the intentional killing by a government or its agents of a civilian or "[unlawful combatant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlawful_combatant)" who is not in the government's custody. The target is a person asserted to be taking part in an armed conflict or terrorism, by bearing arms or otherwise, who has thereby lost the immunity from being targeted that he would otherwise have under the [Third Geneva Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Geneva_Convention).[^cite_note-Solis_Targeting_Combatants_and_Others-69]

On the other hand, [Gary D. Solis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_D._Solis), a professor at [Georgetown University Law Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University_Law_Center), in his 2010 book *The Law of Armed Conflict: International Humanitarian Law in War*,[^cite_note-70] wrote, "Assassinations and targeted killings are very different acts."[^cite_note-Solis_Targeting_Combatants_and_Others-69] The use of the term "assassination" is opposed, as it denotes murder (unlawful killing), but the terrorists are targeted in self-defense, which is thus viewed as a killing but not a crime ([justifiable homicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justifiable_homicide)).[^cite_note-HOV-71] [Abraham D. Sofaer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_D._Sofaer), former federal judge for the [US District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York), wrote on the subject:

> When people call a targeted killing an "assassination", they are attempting to preclude debate on the merits of the action. Assassination is widely defined as murder, and is for that reason prohibited in the United States ... U.S. officials may not kill people merely because their policies are seen as detrimental to our interests ... But killings in self-defense are no more "assassinations" in international affairs than they are murders when undertaken by our police forces against domestic killers. Targeted killings in self-defense have been authoritatively determined by the federal government to fall outside the assassination prohibition.[^cite_note-sfgate2004-72]

Author and former U.S. Army Captain Matthew J. Morgan argued that "there is a major difference between assassination and targeted killing ... targeted killing \[is] not synonymous with assassination. Assassination ... constitutes an illegal killing."[^cite_note-73] Similarly, [Amos Guiora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Guiora), a professor of law at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah), wrote, "Targeted killing is ... not an assassination."[^cite_note-74] [Steve David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_R._David), professor of international relations at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), wrote, "There are strong reasons to believe that the Israeli policy of targeted killing is not the same as assassination." [Syracuse Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University_College_of_Law) William Banks and [GW Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University_Law_School) Peter Raven-Hansen wrote, "Targeted killing of terrorists is ... not unlawful and would not constitute assassination."[^cite_note-75] Rory Miller writes: "Targeted killing ... is not 'assassination.'"[^cite_note-google3-76] Eric Patterson and Teresa Casale wrote, "Perhaps most important is the legal distinction between targeted killing and assassination."[^cite_note-77]

On the other hand, the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) also states on its website, "A program of targeted killing far from any battlefield, without charge or trial, violates the constitutional guarantee of [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process). It also violates [international law](8195726.md), under which [lethal force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_force) may be used outside armed conflict zones only as a last resort to prevent imminent threats, when non-lethal means are not available. Targeting people who are suspected of terrorism for execution, far from any war zone, turns the whole world into a battlefield."[^cite_note-78]

Yael Stein, the research director of [B'Tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Tselem), the Israeli Information Center for Human Rights in the Occupied Territories, also stated in her article "By Any Name Illegal and Immoral: Response to 'Israel's Policy of Targeted Killing'":[^cite_note-Stein-79]

> The argument that this policy affords the public a sense of revenge and retribution could serve to justify acts both illegal and immoral. Clearly, lawbreakers ought to be punished. Yet, no matter how horrific their deeds, as the targeting of Israeli civilians indeed is, they should be punished according to the law. David's arguments could, in principle, justify the abolition of formal legal systems altogether.

Targeted [killing](20171.md) has become a frequent tactic of the United States and Israel in [asymmetric warfare](46863.md).[^cite_note-Solis_Targeting_Combatants_and_Others-69][^cite_note-nytimes2-80] The tactic can raise complex questions and lead to contentious disputes as to the legal basis for its application, who qualifies as an appropriate "hit list" target, and what circumstances must exist before the tactic may be used.[^cite_note-Solis_Targeting_Combatants_and_Others-69] Opinions range from people considering it a legal form of self-defense that decreases terrorism to people calling it an [extrajudicial killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing) that lacks due process and leads to further violence.[^cite_note-Solis_Targeting_Combatants_and_Others-69][^cite_note-sfgate2004-72][^cite_note-autogenerated1-81][^cite_note-google1426-82] Methods used have included firing [Hellfire missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellfire_missile) from [Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-1_Predator) or [Reaper](4769988.md) [drones](58900.md) (unmanned, remote-controlled planes), detonating a cell phone bomb, and long-range [sniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper) shooting. Countries such as the US (in Pakistan and Yemen) and Israel (in the West Bank and Gaza) have used targeted killing to eliminate members of groups such as [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) and [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-Solis_Targeting_Combatants_and_Others-69] In early 2010, with President Obama's approval, [Anwar al-Awlaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_al-Awlaki) became the first [US citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_citizen) to be publicly approved for targeted killing by the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md). Awlaki was killed in a [drone strike](38212231.md) in September 2011.[^cite_note-latimes3-83][^cite_note-washingtonpost3-84]

United Nations investigator [Ben Emmerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Emmerson) said that US drone strikes may have violated [international humanitarian law](1093604.md).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] *The Intercept* reported, "Between January 2012 and February 2013, [U.S. special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_special_operations_forces) airstrikes \[in northeastern Afghanistan] killed more than 200 people. Of those, only 35 were the intended targets."[^cite_note-87]

## Countermeasures

### Early forms



A bodyguard who was killed by an [IED](265112.md) during [Sheik Abdul Sattar Abu Risha's assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Sattar_Abu_Risha#Death) in 2007.

One of the earliest forms of defense against assassins was employing [bodyguards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodyguards), who act as a shield for the potential target; keep a lookout for potential attackers, sometimes in advance, such as on a parade route; and putting themselves in harm's way, both by simple presence, showing that physical force is available to protect the target,[^cite_note-SS-64][^cite_note-88] and by shielding the target if any attack occurs. Failure to realize divided loyalties allowed the assassination of Indian Prime Minister [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi), who was assassinated by two [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) bodyguards in 1984.[^cite_note-89]

### Modern strategies



[Assassination attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Ronald_Reagan) on President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md)



[Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) in a modified [Mercedes-Benz M-Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_M-Class) [Popemobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popemobile) in São Paulo, Brazil

The methods used for protection by famous people have sometimes evoked negative reactions by the public, with some resenting the separation from their officials or major figures. One example might be traveling in a car protected by a bubble of clear [bulletproof glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletproof_glass), such as the [MRAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRAP)-like [Popemobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popemobile) of [Pope John Paul II](23805.md), built following an attempt at his life. Politicians often resent the need for separation and sometimes send their bodyguards away from them for personal or publicity reasons. US President [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McKinley) did so at the public reception in which he was assassinated.[^cite_note-Report-90]

Other potential targets go into seclusion and are rarely heard from or seen in public, such as writer [Salman Rushdie](28722.md). A related form of protection is the use of [body doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_decoy), people with similar builds to those they are expected to impersonate. These people are then [made up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makeup) and, in some cases, undergo [plastic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_surgery) to look like the target, with the body double then taking the place of the person in high-risk situations. According to Joe R. Reeder, Under Secretary of the Army from 1993 to 1997, [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro) used body doubles.[^cite_note-FOX-91]

[US Secret Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Secret_Service) protective agents receive training in the psychology of assassins.[^cite_note-92]
