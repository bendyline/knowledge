The **Pyrenees**[^cite_note-1] is a [mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) in southwestern [Europe](9239.md), straddling the [France–Spain border](47280458.md). The Pyrenees extend nearly 500 kilometres (310 mi) from their union with the [Cantabrian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantabrian_Mountains) to [Cap de Creus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_de_Creus) on the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) coast, reaching a maximum [elevation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevation) of 3,404 metres (11,168 ft) at the peak of [Aneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneto), in [Huesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Huesca), [Spain](26667.md).[^cite_note-2]

The main crest mostly forms a political divide between the states of Spain and France, with the [microstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstate) of [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra) sandwiched in between. Historically, the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) and the [Kingdom of Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Navarre) extended on both sides of the mountain range.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Etymology



Ancient peoples of the Pyrenees.

In [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), [Pyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrene_\(daughter_of_Bebryx\)) is a princess who [gave her name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) to the Pyrenees. The [Greek historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_historian) [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) says Pyrene is the name of a town in [Celtic Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Europe).[^cite_note-5] According to [Silius Italicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silius_Italicus),[^cite_note-6] she was the virgin daughter of [Bebryx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebryx), a king in [Mediterranean Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narbonensis) by whom the hero [Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules) was given [hospitality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitality) during his [quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quest) to steal the cattle of [Geryon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geryon)[^cite_note-7] during his famous [Labours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labours_of_Hercules). Hercules, characteristically drunk and lustful, violates the sacred code of hospitality and rapes his host's daughter. Pyrene gives birth to a serpent and runs away to the woods, afraid that her father will be angry. Alone, she pours out her story to the trees, attracting the attention of wild beasts who tear her to pieces.

After his victory over Geryon, Hercules passes through the kingdom of Bebryx again, finding the girl's lacerated remains. As is often the case in stories of this hero, the sober Hercules responds with heartbroken grief and remorse at the actions of his darker self, and lays Pyrene to rest tenderly, demanding that the surrounding geography join in mourning and preserve her name:[^cite_note-8] "struck by Herculean voice, the mountaintops shudder at the ridges; he kept crying out with a sorrowful noise 'Pyrene!' and all the rock-cliffs and wild-beast haunts echo back 'Pyrene!' ... The mountains hold on to the wept-over name through the ages." [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) connects the story of Hercules and Pyrene to [Lusitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitania), but rejects it as *fabulosa*, highly fictional.[^cite_note-9]

Other classical sources derived the name from the Greek word for fire, [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): πῦρ (IPA: /pŷːr/).[^cite_note-10] According to Greek historian [Diodorus Siculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diodorus_Siculus) "in ancient times, we are told, certain herdsmen left a fire and the whole area of the mountains was entirely consumed; and due to this fire, since it raged continuously day after day, the surface of the earth was also burned and the mountains, because of what had taken place, were called the Pyrenees."[^cite_note-11]

## Geography

### Political divisions

The Spanish Pyrenees are part of the following [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain), from east to west: [Girona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girona_Province), [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Province_\(Spain\)) and [Lleida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lleida_Province) (all in [Catalonia](6822.md)), [Huesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huesca_Province) and [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Zaragoza) (both in [Aragon](39443.md)), [Navarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarra) (in [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre)) and [Gipuzkoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gipuzkoa) (in the [Basque Country](20514897.md)).

The French Pyrenees are part of the following *[départements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9partement)*, from east to west: [Pyrénées-Orientales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Orientales) (also known as [Northern Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Catalonia)), [Aude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aude), [Ariège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari%C3%A8ge_\(department\)), [Haute-Garonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Garonne), [Hautes-Pyrénées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hautes-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es), and [Pyrénées-Atlantiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Atlantiques) (the latter two of which include the [Pyrenees National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenees_National_Park)).

The independent principality of [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra) is sandwiched in the eastern portion of the mountain range between the Spanish Pyrenees and French Pyrenees.

### Physiographical divisions



[Monte Perdido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Perdido), [Aragon](39443.md) (Spain)

[Physiographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_geography), the Pyrenees may be divided into three sections: the Atlantic (or Western), the Central, and the Eastern Pyrenees. Together, they form a distinct physiographic province of the larger Alpine System division.

In the Western Pyrenees, from the [Basque Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Mountains) near the [Bay of Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Biscay) of the Atlantic Ocean, the average elevation gradually increases from west to east.

The Central Pyrenees extend eastward from the [Somport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somport) pass to the [Aran Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aran_Valley), and they include the highest summits of this range:[^cite_note-EB1911-12]

- [Pico de Aneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_de_Aneto) 3,404 metres (11,168 ft) in the Maladeta ridge,

  [^cite_note-EB1911-12]

- [Pico Posets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_Posets) 3,375 metres (11,073 ft),

  [^cite_note-EB1911-12]

- [Monte Perdido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Perdido) 3,355 metres (11,007 ft).

  [^cite_note-EB1911-12]

In the Eastern Pyrenees, with the exception of one break at the eastern extremity of the *Pyrénées Ariégeoises* in the [Ariège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari%C3%A8ge_\(department\)) area, the mean elevation is remarkably uniform until a sudden decline occurs in the easternmost portion of the chain known as the [Albères](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alb%C3%A8res).[^cite_note-EB1911-12]

#### Foothills

Main article: [Pre-Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Pyrenees)

Most [foothills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothill) of the Pyrenees are on the Spanish side, where there is a large and complex system of ranges stretching from Spanish [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre), across northern Aragon and into Catalonia, almost reaching the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) coast with summits reaching 2,600 m (8,500 ft).[^cite_note-13] At the eastern end on the southern side lies a distinct area known as the [Sub-Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Pyrenees).[^cite_note-14]

On the French side the slopes of the main range descend abruptly and there are no foothills except in the [Corbières Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corbi%C3%A8res_Massif) in the northeastern corner of the mountain system.[^cite_note-15]

## Geology

See also: [Geology of the Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_Pyrenees)

The Pyrenees are older than the [Alps](981.md): their [sediments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) were first deposited in coastal basins during the [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic) and [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) eras. During [Ediacaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediacaran) to [Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician) times, Pyrenees were located at the Northwest margin of [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana), where they formed a lateral continuity of neighbouring areas, such as the [Montagne Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montagne_Noire) and the [Mouthoumet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouthoumet) massifs and Southwestern territory of [Sardinia](29376.md).[^cite_note-maxime2022-16] Between 100 and 150 million years ago, during the Early [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) Period, the [Bay of Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Biscay) [fanned out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafloor_spreading), pushing present-day Spain against France and applying intense [compressional pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_\(geology\)) to large layers of [sedimentary rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock). The intense pressure and uplifting of the Earth's crust first affected the eastern part and moved progressively to the entire chain, culminating in the [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) Epoch.

The eastern part of the Pyrenees consists largely of [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and [gneissose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gneiss) rocks, while in the western part the granite peaks are flanked by layers of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone). The massive and unworn character of the chain comes from its abundance of granite, which is particularly resistant to [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion), as well as weak [glacial](12463.md) development.

The upper parts of the Pyrenees contain low-relief surfaces forming a [peneplain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peneplain). This peneplain originated no earlier than in [Late Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) times. Presumably it formed at height as extensive sedimentation raised the local [base level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_level) considerably.[^cite_note-17]

## Landscape

Conspicuous features of Pyrenean scenery are:

- the absence of great lakes, such as those that fill the lateral valleys of the Alps

  [^cite_note-EB1911-12]

- the rarity and relative high elevation of usable [passes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_pass)

  [^cite_note-EB1911-12]

- the large number of the mountain torrents locally called *[gaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gave_\(stem\))*, which often form lofty [waterfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall), surpassed in Europe only by those of [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia)

  [^cite_note-EB1911-12]

- the frequency with which the upper end of a valley assumes the form of a semicircle of precipitous cliffs, called a [cirque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirque).

  [^cite_note-EB1911-12]

The highest [waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall) is [Gavarnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavarnie_Falls) (422 m or 1,385 ft),[^cite_note-18] at the head of the [Gave de Pau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gave_de_Pau); the [Cirque de Gavarnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirque_de_Gavarnie), in the same valley,[^cite_note-EB1911-12] together with the nearby Cirque de Troumouse and [Cirque d'Estaubé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirque_d'Estaub%C3%A9), are notable examples of the [cirque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirque) formation.

Low passes are lacking, and the principal roads and the railroads between France and Spain run only in the lowlands at the western and eastern ends of the Pyrenees, near sea level. The main passes of note are:

- [Col de la Perche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_la_Perche?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1,581 m (5,187 ft)), towards the east, between the valley of the [Têt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%AAt_River) and the valley of the [Segre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Segre),

- [Col de Puymorens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_Puymorens) (1,920 m (6,300 ft)), on [European route E09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E09) between France and Spain.

- The nearby [Pas de la Casa or Port d'Envalira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pas_de_la_Casa), the highest road pass in the Pyrenees at 2,408 m (7,900 ft), and one of the highest points of the European road network, which provides the route from France to Andorra,

- The [Port de la Bonaigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_de_la_Bonaigua) (2,070 m (6,790 ft)), in the middle of the range at the head of the Aran Valley.

- Plan de Beret (1,870 m (6,140 ft))

- [Col du Pourtalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_du_Pourtalet) (1,794 m (5,886 ft)).

- The Col de [Somport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somport) or Port de Canfranc (1,632 m (5,354 ft)), where there were old [Roman roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_road).

- [Col de la Pierre St Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_la_Pierre_St_Martin) (1,766 m (5,794 ft))

- [Puerto de Larrau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_de_Larrau?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1,578 m (5,177 ft))

- The [Roncevaux Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roncevaux_Pass) (1,057 m (3,468 ft)), entirely in [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre) (Spain) is an important point on the [Camino de Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camino_de_Santiago) pilgrimage route.

Because of the lack of low passes a number of tunnels have been created, beneath the passes at Somport, Envalira, and Puymorens and new routes in the center of the range at [Bielsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielsa) and [Vielha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vielha_Tunnel).

A notable visual feature of this [mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) is [La Brèche de Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Br%C3%A8che_de_Roland), a gap in the ridge line, which – according to legend – was created by [Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland).

## Natural resources

The metallic [ores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore) of the Pyrenees are not in general of much importance now, though there were [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) mines at several locations in [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Andorra#Natural_resources), as well as at [Vicdessos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicdessos) in Ariège, and the foot of [Canigó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canig%C3%B3) in [Pyrénées-Orientales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Orientales) long ago. [Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) deposits capable of being profitably worked are situated chiefly on the Spanish slopes, but the French side has beds of [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite).[^cite_note-EB1911-12] The open pit of Trimoun near the commune of [Luzenac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzenac) (Ariège) is one of the greatest sources of [talc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talc) in Europe.



Various samples of Pyrenean marbles

There are many marble quarries in the Pyrenees, most of which were opened by the Romans in ancient times. Quarried intermittently, they provided prestigious marbles such as [Grand Antique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Antique_marble) (used in Rome and Constantinople by the Romans), statuary white marbles as well as coloured marbles used to decorate the royal palaces of the Louvre and Versailles in France and the Royal Palace of Madrid in Spain.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

[Mineral springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_spring) are abundant and remarkable, and especially noteworthy are the [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring). The hot springs, among which those of [Les Escaldes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Escaldes) in Andorra, [Panticosa](13170088.md) and [Lles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lles_de_Cerdanya) in Spain, [Ax-les-Thermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ax-les-Thermes), [Bagnères-de-Luchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagn%C3%A8res-de-Luchon) and [Eaux-Chaudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eaux-Chaudes) in France may be mentioned, are [sulfurous](27127.md) and mostly situated high, near the contact of the granite with the stratified rocks. The lower springs, such as those of [Bagnères-de-Bigorre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagn%C3%A8res-de-Bigorre) ([Hautes-Pyrénées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hautes-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es)), [Rennes-les-Bains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes-les-Bains) ([Aude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aude)), and [Campagne-sur-Aude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campagne-sur-Aude) (Aude), are mostly selenitic and not hot.[^cite_note-EB1911-12]

## Climate

The amount of [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) the range receives, including rain and snow, is much greater in the western than in the eastern Pyrenees[^cite_note-EB1911-12] because of the moist air that blows in from the Atlantic Ocean over the [Bay of Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Biscay). After dropping its moisture over the western and central Pyrenees, the air is left dry over the eastern Pyrenees. The winter average temperature is −2 °C (28 °F).

Sections of the mountain range vary in more than one respect. There are some [glaciers](12463.md) in the western and snowy central Pyrenees, but there are no glaciers in the eastern Pyrenees because there is insufficient snowfall to cause their development. Glaciers are confined to the northern slopes of the central Pyrenees, and do not descend, like those of the Alps, far down into the valleys but rather have their greatest lengths along the direction of the mountain chain. They form, in fact, in a narrow zone near the crest of the highest mountains. Here, as in the other great mountain ranges of central Europe, there is substantial evidence of a much wider expanse of glaciation during the [glacial periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_period). The best evidence of this is in the valley of Argeles Gazost, between Lourdes and Gavarnie, in the *_département_* of Hautes-Pyrénées.[^cite_note-EB1911-12]

The annual snow-line varies in different parts of the Pyrenees from about 2,700 to 2,800 metres (8,900 to 9,200 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-EB1911-12] On average the seasonal snow is observed at least 50% of the time above 1,600 metres (5,200 ft) between December and April.[^cite_note-21]

## Flora and fauna

### Flora

Main category: [Flora of the Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Flora_of_the_Pyrenees)

Main article: [Pyrenees conifer and mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenees_conifer_and_mixed_forests)

See also: [Forests of the Iberian Peninsula - Eurosiberian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forests_of_the_Iberian_Peninsula#The_Eurosiberian_region)



Aigualluts cascade in Benasque Valley, [Aragon](39443.md) (Spain)

A still more marked effect of the preponderance of rainfall in the western half of the chain is seen in the vegetation. The lower mountains in the extreme west are wooded, but the extent of forest declines as one moves eastwards. The eastern Pyrenees are peculiarly wild and barren, all the more since it is in this part of the chain that granitic masses prevail. Also moving from west to east, there is a change in the composition of the flora, with the change becoming most evident as one passes the centre of the mountain chain from which point the [Corbières Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corbi%C3%A8res_Massif) stretch north-eastwards towards the central plateau of France. Though the difference in latitude is only about 1°, in the west the flora resembles that of central Europe while in the east it is distinctly Mediterranean in character. The Pyrenees are nearly as rich in [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) species as the Alps, and among the most remarkable instances of that [endemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) is the occurrence of the monotypic genus *[Xatardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xatardia)* (family [Apiaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apiaceae)), which grows only on a high alpine pass between the Val d'Eynes and [Catalonia](6822.md). Other examples include *[Arenaria montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arenaria_montana)*, *[Bulbocodium vernum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulbocodium_vernum)*, and *[Ranunculus glacialis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranunculus_glacialis)*. The genus most abundantly represented in the range is that of the [saxifrages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxifrage), several species of which are endemic here.[^cite_note-EB1911-12]

### Fauna

In their [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) the Pyrenees present some striking instances of [endemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism). The [Pyrenean desman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenean_desman) is found only in some of the streams of the northern slopes of these mountains; the only other [desman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desman), the [Russian desman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_desman), is confined to the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga) river basin in southern Russia, Kazakhstan and Ukraine. The [Pyrenean brook salamander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenean_brook_salamander) (*Calotriton asper*), an endemic amphibian, also lives in streams and lakes located at high altitudes. Among other peculiarities of Pyrenean fauna are blind insects in the [caverns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave) of Ariège, the principal genera of which are *[Anophthalmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anophthalmus)* and *Adelops*.[^cite_note-EB1911-12]

The [Pyrenean ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenean_ibex), an endemic subspecies of the [Iberian ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_ibex), became extinct in January 2000; another subspecies, the [western Spanish ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Spanish_ibex), was introduced into the area, with the population numbering over 400 individuals as of 2020. The native [brown bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_bear) population was hunted to near-extinction in the 1990s, but its numbers rebounded in 1996 when three bears were brought from [Slovenia](27338.md). The bear population has bred successfully, and there are now believed to be about 15 brown bears in the central region around [Fos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fos,_Haute-Garonne), with only four native ones still living in the [Aspe Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspe_Valley).

## Demographics and culture



Some [Blonde d'Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blonde_d'Aquitaine) on summer pasture near the [Pic du Midi d'Ossau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pic_du_Midi_d'Ossau)

The Pyrenean region possesses a varied ethnology, [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) and history: see [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra); [Aragon](39443.md); [Ariège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari%C3%A8ge_\(department\)); [Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(historical_territory\)); [Béarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9arn); [Catalonia](6822.md); [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarre); [Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roussillon). For their history, see also [Almogavars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almogavars), [Marca Hispanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marca_Hispanica).

The principal languages spoken in the area are Spanish, French, [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragonese_language), [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) (in [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra) and in [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Catalonia) and [Southern Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Catalonia)), and [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language). Also spoken, to a lesser degree, is the [Occitan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language), consisting of the [Gascon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gascon_language) and [Languedocien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedocien) dialects in France and the [Aranese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranese) dialect in the [Aran Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aran_Valley).

An important feature of rural life in the Pyrenees is '[transhumance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transhumance)', the moving of livestock from the farms in the valleys up to the higher grounds of the mountains for the summer.[^cite_note-ariegecom-23] In this way the farming communities could keep larger herds than the lowland farms could support on their own. The principal animals moved were [cows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cows) and [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep), but historically most members of farming families also moved to the higher pastures along with their animals, so they also took with them [pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs), [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horses)[^cite_note-chevalEnPyrenees-24] and [chickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chickens).[^cite_note-ariegecom-23] Transhumance thus took the form of a mass biannual migration, moving uphill in May or June[^cite_note-guide2midipyrenees-25] and returning to the farms in September or October. During the summer period, the families would live in basic stone cabins[^cite_note-ariegecom-23] in the high mountains.

Nowadays, industrialisation and changing agriculture practices have diminished the custom. However, the importance of transhumance continues to be recognised through its celebration in popular festivals.[^cite_note-chevalEnPyrenees-24][^cite_note-guide2midipyrenees-25][^cite_note-26]

## Scientific facilities

### Pic du Midi Observatory



The observatory on the [Pic du Midi de Bigorre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pic_du_Midi_de_Bigorre).

The [Pic du Midi Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pic_du_Midi_de_Bigorre) is an astronomical observatory located at 2877 metres on top of the Pic du Midi de Bigorre in the French Pyrenees. Construction of the observatory began in 1878 and the 8-metre dome was completed in 1908.

The observatory housed a powerful mechanical equatorial reflector which was used in 1909 to formally discredit the Martian canal theory. A 1.06-metre (42 in) telescope was installed in 1963, funded by [NASA](18426568.md) and was used to take detailed photographs of the surface of the Moon in preparation for the Apollo missions. Other studies conducted in 1965 provided a detailed analysis of the composition of the atmospheres on Mars and Venus, this served as a basis for [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory) scientists to predict that these planets had no life.

Since 1980, the observatory has had a 2-metre telescope, which is the largest telescope in France. Overtaken by the giant telescopes built in recent decades, today the observatory is widely open to amateur astronomy.

### Odeillo solar furnace



[Odeillo solar furnace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeillo_solar_furnace).

The [Odeillo solar furnace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeillo_solar_furnace) is the world's largest solar furnace. It is situated in Font-Romeu-Odeillo-Via, in the department of [Pyrénées-Orientales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Orientales), in south of France. Built between 1962 and 1968, it is 54 metres (177 ft) and 48 metres (157 ft) wide, and includes 63 heliostats. The site was chosen because of the length and the quality of sunshine with direct light (more than 2,500 h/year) and the purity of its atmosphere (high elevation and low average humidity).

This furnace serves as a science research site studying materials at very high temperatures. Temperatures above 3,500 °C (6,330 °F) can be obtained in a few seconds; in addition, it provides rapid temperature changes and therefore allows studying the effect of thermal shocks.

The Pyrenees mountain range seen from Toulouse.

## Sports and leisure



Ski Center, Cerler (Spain)

Both sides of the Pyrenees are popular spots for winter sports such as [alpine skiing](39576.md) and [mountaineering](20341.md). The Pyrenees are also a good place for athletes to do high-elevation training in the summer, such as by bicycling and cross-country running.

In the [summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer) and the [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn), the Pyrenees are usually featured in two of cycling's grand tours, the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France) held annually in July and the [Vuelta a España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuelta_a_Espa%C3%B1a) held in September. The stages held in the Pyrenees are often crucial legs of both tours, drawing hundreds of thousands of spectators to the region.

Three main [long-distance footpaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_footpath) run the length of the mountain range: the [GR 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GR_10) across the northern slopes, the [GR 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GR_11_\(Spain\)) across the southern slopes, and the [HRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute_Randonn%C3%A9e_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9enne) which traverses peaks and ridges along a high elevation route. In addition, there are numerous marked and unmarked trails throughout the region.

*[Pirena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirena_\(race\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)* is a dog-[mushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushing) competition held in the Pyrenees.
