The **New Space Race**[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-\:3-3] or **Second Space Race**[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-\:2-5] describes the renewed competition in [space exploration](28431.md) and [technological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_technology) development in the 21st century, primarily between the [space programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_programs) of [China](343674.md) and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_policy_of_the_United_States), as well as other nations, and [private spaceflight](752732.md). This modern race is characterized by a broad range of objectives, including large [satellite constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_constellation),[^cite_note-\:5-6] advancements in [reusable launch vehicles](336014.md) and [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reusable_spacecraft),[^cite_note-\:2-5] a return to [crewed Moon landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_landing), establishing a permanent [moonbase](8420417.md),[^cite_note-\:3-3][^cite_note-\:7-7] and [crewed missions to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mission_to_Mars).[^cite_note-\:8-8][^cite_note-\:9-9]

The [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race) was part of the [Cold War](325329.md), a rivalry between the US and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and saw the realization of [satellites](27683.md), crewed Moon landings, [space stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_station), and [interplanetary spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_spaceflight). In the 21st century, the [China Manned Space Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Manned_Space_Program) saw its [first crewed flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_5), [two prototype space stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_program), and a continuously-occupied modular space station, [*Tiangong*](2481401.md); the [China National Space Administration](344618.md) sent orbiters, landers, and rovers [to the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program) and [to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1). In the same period, NASA's human spaceflight centered on the [International Space Station](15043.md); crewed lunar hardware was consolidated under the [Artemis program](60758751.md) in 2017, centered on the [Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System) and [Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_\(spacecraft\)) spacecraft; long-term NASA [probes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_probe) [explored the Martian surface](32506368.md#Missions), [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_\(spacecraft\)), and [Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassini%E2%80%93Huygens). Humans returned to [cislunar space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cislunar_space) during NASA's [Artemis II](41391511.md) flight in 2026, for the first time [since 1972](1971.md). The US currently targets a crewed lunar landing with [Artemis IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_IV) in 2028. China plans a [Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10)-launched crewed lunar landing in 2029, and [a robotic Mars sample return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-3) launch in 2028.

Some have argued that the New Space Race is part of a [Second Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Cold_War) between China and the US.[^cite_note-\:12-10] The New Space Race is seen as more [multipolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarity_\(international_relations\)) than the original. The US-led [Artemis Accords](63968963.md) involve 68 nations, including [space agencies of Europe](10363.md), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Space), [Japan](30764115.md), [Canada](328618.md), and the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Rashid_Space_Centre). China and Russia's [Roscosmos](406573.md) lead the [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station) effort.[^cite_note-\:6-11] Collaborations include ESA's Orion [European Service Module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Service_Module), alongside other contributions made by five other space agencies in the cancelled NASA-led [Lunar Gateway](53648310.md) space station.

The concept also includes the "[billionaire space race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire_space_race)": increasing involvement of private [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) companies, such as [SpaceX](832774.md), [Blue Origin](887418.md), and [Virgin Galactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Galactic). These have transformed [launch services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_launch_market_competition) via the [Falcon 9](2647515.md), the [satellite internet constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_internet_constellation) market with [Starlink](45111627.md), and [space tourism](67896.md) via [Dragon 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2), [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard), and [SpaceShipTwo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceShipTwo).

## Participants and objectives



[Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System) with the [Orion spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_\(spacecraft\)) launching from [Kennedy Space Center Launch Complex 39B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Launch_Complex_39B) for [Artemis II](41391511.md)

The primary race is between the [government space agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_space_agency) of the [United States](18426568.md) and [China](344618.md).[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-\:1-13] The US uses its [Artemis program](60758751.md) and China uses its [Chinese Lunar Exploration Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

In the 2020s, both the US and China are engaged in an effort to establish a [permanent presence on the Moon](8420417.md), with an emphasis on the [lunar south pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole). Further objectives include large [satellite constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_constellation),[^cite_note-\:5-6] advancements in [reusable launch vehicles](336014.md) and [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reusable_spacecraft),[^cite_note-\:2-5] establishing a permanent a return to [crewed Moon landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_landing), and [crewed missions to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mission_to_Mars).[^cite_note-\:8-8][^cite_note-\:9-9]

Other nations, such as [Russia](406573.md), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO),[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:4-16][^cite_note-17] [Japan](716184.md),[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-18] as well as [European countries](10363.md),[^cite_note-19] are also active participants.

## Primary missions

### Lunar missions



Concept art of [Artemis Base Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_Base_Camp) at the [lunar south pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole)

Most missions associated with the New Space Race have focused on [lunar exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_exploration). A particular emphasis has been placed on the [lunar south pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole), where a large fraction of [lunar water ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_water) may be accessible. These missions are intended to demonstrate technologies and capabilities for sustained lunar exploration in preparation for the eventual establishment of [permanent human outposts](8420417.md), as well as to serve as a proving ground and stepping stone for future [crewed missions to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mission_to_Mars).[^cite_note-\:8-8][^cite_note-\:9-9]

In 2019, China became the first country to achieve a [soft landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_landing) on the [far side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon) with [Chang'e 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_4), which deployed [*Yutu*-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutu-2), which remains operational as the longest-lived lunar rover ever.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-planetary_success-26] In 2024, China became the first country to return lunar samples from the far side of the Moon with [Chang'e 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_6).[^cite_note-27] China plans a [Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10)-launched crewed landing in 2029, likely late in the year.[^cite_note-28]

In April 2026, NASA's [Artemis II](41391511.md) mission returned astronauts to the vicinity of the Moon for the first time since the [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program), including the first non-American to travel beyond [low Earth orbit](47568.md) ([Canadian astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_astronaut) [Jeremy Hansen](22811438.md)), and broke the record previously set by [Apollo 13](1770.md) for the farthest distance humans have ever traveled from Earth.[^cite_note-Graf_2026-04-06-29][^cite_note-space.com_timeline-30][^cite_note-31] The Artemis program's [Human Landing System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Landing_System), either a [Blue Moon Mark 2](54252087.md) or [Starship HLS](64020556.md), will be selected following orbital testing during [Artemis III](50493439.md) in 2027, while [Artemis IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_IV) is planned to land a crew on the surface in 2028.[^cite_note-32]

India has already made significant progress with missions such as [Chandrayaan-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrayaan-3), which successfully landed near the lunar south polar region on 23 August 2023, making it one of the few nations to achieve a soft landing on the Moon and the first to land near the lunar south pole.[^cite_note-\:4-16]

### Martian missions



Launch concept of an interplanetary [Mars sample-return mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_sample-return_mission)

Mars remains a longer-term objective of the New Space Race. Unlike the Moon, Mars has not yet been visited by humans, and its greater distance makes crewed missions substantially more difficult. Robotic exploration has therefore continued to serve as a precursor to potential crewed missions.

In 2021, China became only the second country (after the United States) to successfully operate a lander and rover on Mars with [*Tianwen*-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1) and [*Zhurong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\)).[^cite_note-33]

Robotic [Mars sample-return missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_sample-return_mission) have been planned [by China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-3), as well as [by NASA and the European Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA-ESA_Mars_Sample_Return). However, as of August 2026, following [US congressional budget cuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_federal_budget#January_2026_negotiations) and ESA member proposals to cancel the Earth Return Orbiter (ERO)—a key element of the sample return[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]—the joint mission is considered virtually cancelled.[^cite_note-36]

### Space stations



Rendering of China's [*Tiangong* space station](2481401.md) with a [*Tianzhou* spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianzhou_spacecraft) docked aft and two [*Shenzhou* spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_spacecraft) docked to the forward and nadir (Earth facing) ports

The New Space Race has also expanded into the development of space stations and commercial destinations in low Earth orbit.

Since 2021, China has operated [*Tiangong*](2481401.md), the only space station in orbit alongside the [International Space Station](15043.md), and the third to use [modular design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_design#In_space_stations) following *[Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir)* and the ISS.[^cite_note-37]

## Governance

See also: [Space law](743972.md)



The growth of all tracked objects in space over time showing a recent increase of active satellites

[^cite_note-38]

Some have argued the New Space Race is a component of the [Second Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Cold_War) concept,[^cite_note-\:12-10] pointing to the alignment under the US-led [Artemis Accords](63968963.md) with Europe, India, and Japan, contrasted with the China and Russia-led [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station) (ILRS).[^cite_note-\:6-11] As of May 2026, 67 nations have joined the Artemis Accords, predominantly in the [Global North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_North) and [Latin America](18524.md), while 13 nations have joined the ILRS, predominantly in the [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South). China and Russia initially hoped to attract major European states to the project, but the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) was seen as ending these efforts.[^cite_note-\:62-39]

Some scholars have argued the substance of the Accords is "uncontentious" and represent a "significant political attempt to codify key principles of space law" for governing nations' space activities.[^cite_note-Newman_2020-40] China and Russia have criticized the Accords' framework for [lunar mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_mining) as too favorable to US interests, with China describing them as "akin to European colonial [enclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclosure) land-taking methods."[^cite_note-The_Diplomat_2020-41][^cite_note-pmid33033208-42]

## Private sector involvement

Main articles: [Private spaceflight](752732.md) and [Billionaire space race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire_space_race)



Renderings of the [Human Landing System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Landing_System) spacecraft: [SpaceX](832774.md)'s [Starship HLS](64020556.md) (*left*) and [Blue Origin](887418.md)'s [Blue Moon Mark 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_Mark_2) (*right*)

Unlike the original [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race) during the [Cold War](325329.md) era, private [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) companies play a massive role in the New Space Race, with [SpaceX](832774.md) and [Blue Origin](887418.md) developing [launch technology](1306157.md) and services.[^cite_note-\:3-3][^cite_note-\:1-13] This [private spaceflight](752732.md) race involves sending privately developed rockets and vehicles to various destinations in space, often in response to government programs or even to develop the [space tourism](67896.md) sector.[^cite_note-43]

SpaceX's [Falcon 9](2647515.md) has the [most total launches of an active vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_orbital_launch_systems) worldwide,[^cite_note-44] its in-development [Starship](59118602.md) is the [most powerful rocket flown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_heavy-lift_launch_vehicle),[^cite_note-45] [Starlink](45111627.md) comprises 75% of active maneuverable satellites in orbit as of March 2026;[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-S-1-47] Blue Origin's [New Glenn](51585988.md) is the most powerful reusable rocket to reach orbit.[^cite_note-48]

In October 2025, NASA announced that it would reopen competition to develop its [Human Landing System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Landing_System) for the [Artemis III](50493439.md) mission, planning to use Space X's [Starship HLS](64020556.md) or Blue Origin's [Blue Moon Mark 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_Mark_2), or both, depending on which was ready. NASA's acting administrator [Sean Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Duffy) said, "SpaceX has the contract to build the HLS which will get U.S. astronauts there on Artemis III. But, competition and innovation are the keys to our dominance in space so NASA is opening up HLS production to Blue Origin and other great American companies."[^cite_note-49]
