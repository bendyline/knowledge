**Teesta River** ([Limbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbu_language): ᤋᤡᤘᤠ᤺, **Teewaa**; in Classical Limbu) is a 414 km (257 mi) long river that rises in the [Pauhunri Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauhunri) of eastern [Himalayas](37966.md), flows through the [Indian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_states) of [Sikkim](140372.md) and [West Bengal](34040.md) and subsequently enters [Bangladesh](3454.md) through [Rangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur_Division) division. In Bangladesh, it merges with [Jamuna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamuna_River) which after meeting some other major rivers of the [Bengal delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_delta) finally falls into the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md).[^cite_note-Mullick_al2011-1] It drains an area of 12,540 km<sup>2</sup> (4,840 sq mi).[^cite_note-Rahaman_WP2020-2] In [India](14533.md), the Teesta River flows through [Mangan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sikkim_district), [Gangtok District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sikkim_district), [Pakyong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakyong_District), [Kalimpong district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimpong_district), [Darjeeling District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darjeeling_District), [Jalpaiguri District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalpaiguri_District), and [Cooch Behar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooch_Behar_District) as well as the cities of [Rangpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpo), [Jalpaiguri](2186661.md), and [Mekhliganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekhliganj). In Bangladesh, it flows through [Lalmonirhat District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalmonirhat_District), [Rangpur District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur_District), [Kurigram District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurigram_District) and [Gaibandha District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaibandha_District). It joins the [Jamuna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamuna_River) at [Phulchhari Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phulchhari_Upazila) in Bangladesh. 305 km (190 mi) of the river lies in India and 109 km (68 mi) in Bangladesh. The Teesta is the largest river of Sikkim and second largest river of West Bengal after the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges).

## Course

Teesta River near Gangtok

Teesta River near [Gangtok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangtok)



[National Highway 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_10_\(India\)) winds along the banks of the Teesta River near [Kalimpong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimpong)

The Teesta River originates from [Teesta Khangtse Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesta_Khangtse_Glacier), west of [Pahunri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauhunri) (or Teesta Kangse) glacier above 5,400 m (17,700 ft), and flows southward through gorges and rapids in the [Sikkim](140372.md) Himalaya.[^cite_note-Meetei2007-3]

It is fed by streams from [Tso Lhamo Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tso_Lhamo_Lake), [Gurudongmar Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurudongmar_Lake) and [rivulets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivulet) arising in the [Thangu Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thangu_Valley), [Yumthang Valley of Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yumthang_Valley_of_Flowers), [Dikchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikchu) and Donkha mountain ranges. The river then flows past the towns of [Chungthang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chungthang), [Singhik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhik), [Mangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangan,_India), [Dikchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikchu) and [Makha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makha,_Sikkim), where some major tributaries like Kanaka river join it, and reaches [Singtam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singtam), where it is spanned by the scenic Indreni Bridge. Also in Singtam, a large tributary of the Teesta called [Ranikhola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranikhola) joins and then descends towards Bardang, [Majitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majitar), Mining where it is spanned by Rangpo - Mining Teesta Bridge and reaches the city of [Rangpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpo) where [Rangpo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpo_River), the second largest tributary of the Teesta joins. From here river Teesta forms the border between Sikkim and [West Bengal](34040.md) up to [Teesta Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesta_Bazaar) via [Melli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melli). Just before the Teesta Bridge, where the roads from [Kalimpong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimpong) and [Darjeeling](186729.md) join, the river is met by its largest [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary), the [Rangeet River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangeet_River).[^cite_note-Chaudhuri2015-4]

After this point, Teesta River changes its course southwards flowing into West Bengal and some more tributaries like [Relli River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relli_River), Riyang River, Geil Khola etc joins. Than the river hits the plains at [Sevoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevoke), 22 km (14 mi) northeast of [Siliguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri), where it is spanned by the [Coronation Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_Bridge) and [Sevoke Railway Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevoke_Railway_Bridge) linking the northeast states to the rest of [India](14533.md). After crossing Sevoke, the river is fed by some small tributaries like Chel Khola, Neora Khola, Leesh Khola etc, and then reaches [Gajoldoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajoldoba) where there is Teesta Barrage. The river then flows through [Jalpaiguri](2186661.md), where Jalpaiguri Teesta Bridge links Northeastern States. The river flows further down through [Mekhliganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekhliganj) and [Haldibari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldibari,_India) in [Cooch Behar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooch_Behar_district), where it is spanned by [Joyee Setu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyee_Setu), the longest roadway bridge of West Bengal with length 3.8 kilometres.

Here the Teesta completes its journey in India and enters [Bangladesh](3454.md) in [Dahagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahagram), [Rangpur Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur_Division). The river descends towards Barakhata, where there is Teesta Barrage of Bangladesh. Further it reaches [Rangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur_District) and [Lalmonirhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalmonirhat) town in [Lalmonirhat District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalmonirhat_District) and [Nilphamari District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilphamari_District). After crossing Tepamadhupur Bazaar, Nagrakura Bazaar, Sundarganj and Bozra Bazaar, river Teesta joins [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra) River at Haripur Port near [Gaibandha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaibandha), [Phulchhari Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phulchhari_Upazila) in [Kurigram District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurigram_District) of Bangladesh.[^cite_note-Joshi2004-5]

Through its course, the Teesta River has carved out [ravines](2014227.md) and [gorges](78478.md) in Sikkim meandering through the hills with the [hill station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_station) of Kalimpong lying just off the river and the city of Siliguri at the foothills of Himalaya. Variegated vegetation can be seen along this route. At lower elevations, [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) trees and shrubs cover the surrounding hills; [alpine vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) is seen at the upper altitudes. The river is flanked by white sand which is used by the construction industry in the region. Large boulders in and around the waters make it ideal for [rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafting).

During the [monsoon](57630.md), Teesta River distends its banks, both in size and turbulence. [Landslides](37208.md) in this region often dam up parts of the river in this season. It has also caused severe flooding, multiple times during monsoon in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Changes in course of rivers



James Rennell's 1776 map shows an earlier flow of the Teesta meeting the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) in three channels before a devastating flood in 1787 changed its course.

In the past, the Teesta flowed south from Jalpaiguri in three channels: the [Punarbhaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punarbhaba_River), [Atrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrai_River) and [Karatoya Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karatoya_River). The Teesta changed course as a result of the flooding of 1787, turning southeast to become part of the [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River).[^cite_note-Majumdar1971-8]

## Water sharing challenge

Disputes over the appropriate allocation and development of the water resources of the river have remained a subject of conflict between India and Bangladesh for almost 35 years, with several bilateral agreements and rounds of talks failing to produce results.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Negotiations have been going on since 1983.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-\:0-30][^cite_note-31] In 1983, a temporary arrangement was reached: Bangladesh would receive 36%, India 39% while, and the remainder would remain unallocated.[^cite_note-\:0-30] Both countries signed a water sharing treaty in 1996 which would look into water sharing between the two countries in general following the [Ganges water dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_water_dispute).[^cite_note-\:0-30] The water sharing remains a challenge.[^cite_note-\:0-30]

In recent developments, Bangladesh's interim government water advisor, [Syeda Rizwana Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizwana_Hasan), told news agency PTI (Press Trust of India) on September 1, 2024, that both upper and lower riparian countries must follow international water-sharing principles. If a treaty remains out of reach, it could mark a significant shift in India-Bangladesh relations if diplomacy fails.[^cite_note-32]

## Seismic concerns

Teesta River area is in the seismically active Zone-V and has experienced micro-seismic activity. According to India's Ministry of Environment & Forests, the Teesta River dam projects have been approved with the requirement that they adopt suitable seismic coefficient in the design for the dam, tunnel, surge shaft and power house. The projects are cascaded over the length of the river, do not store large amounts water, have small reservoirs, and therefore the projects are expected to have very low risk from the reservoir induced seismicity in the area.[^cite_note-moef.gov.in-10]

## Climate and tectonics

The Teesta River has preserved good imprints of climatic and tectonics along its valleys and catchments. The interrelationship between climate, erosion, deposition and tectonic activities is not properly understood to date. These are being studied.[^cite_note-Meetei2007-3][^cite_note-Mukul2000-33][^cite_note-34]
