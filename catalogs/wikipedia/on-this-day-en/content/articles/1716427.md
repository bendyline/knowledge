The **Île-de-France** ([/ˌiːl də ˈfrɒ̃s/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); French: [\[il də fʁɑ̃s\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0e/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-%C3%8Ele-de-France.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-%C3%8Ele-de-France.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0e/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-%C3%8Ele-de-France.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-%C3%8Ele-de-France.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; lit. 'Island of France') is the most populous of the eighteen [regions of France](58846.md), with an official estimated population of 12,271,794 residents on 1 January 2023.[^cite_note-pop2022-1] Containing the capital city of France, [Paris](22989.md), it is located in the north-central part of the country and often called the **Paris Region**[^cite_note-iledefrance.fr_Essentials2018-3] (French: *Région parisienne*, pronounced [\[ʁeʒjɔ̃ paʁizjɛn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)). Île-de-France is densely populated and retains a prime economic position on the national stage. It covers 12,012 square kilometres (4,638 square miles), about 2% of [metropolitan French territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France). Its 2017 population was nearly one-fifth of the national total.[^cite_note-auto1-4]

The region is made up of eight administrative [departments](38523.md): Paris, [Essonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essonne), [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis), [Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Marne), [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne), [Val-d'Oise](90616.md) and [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines). It was created as the "District of the Paris Region" in 1961. In 1976, when its status was aligned with the French administrative regions created in 1972, it was renamed after the historic province of Île-de-France. Residents are sometimes referred to as *Franciliens*, an administrative word created in the 1980s. The GDP of the region in 2019 was nearly one-third of the total for France,[^cite_note-cci_paris2022-5]<sup>: 12 </sup> and 5% of the European Union's.[^cite_note-cci_paris2022-5]<sup>: 12 </sup> It has the highest per capita GDP of any French region.[^cite_note-oecdatlas-6]

Beyond the city limits of Paris, the region has many other important historic sites, including the palaces of [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles) and [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Fontainebleau), as well as the most-visited tourist attraction in France, [Disneyland Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland_Paris). Although it is the richest French region, a significant number of residents live in poverty. The official poverty rate in the Île-de-France was 15.9% in 2015. The region has witnessed increasing income inequality in recent decades, and rising housing prices have pushed the less affluent outside Paris.[^cite_note-7]

## Etymology

Further information: [History of Île-de-France § Early history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_%C3%8Ele-de-France#Early_history)

Although the modern name Île-de-France literally means Island of France, its etymology is unclear. Despite its name, the region itself is not an island. The "island" may refer to the land between the rivers [Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oise_\(river\)), [Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marne_\(river\)) and [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine), or it may also have been a reference to the [Île de la Cité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_de_la_Cit%C3%A9), where the French royal palace and cathedral were located.

Alternatively, the name may refer to the [lands that were under the direct rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_lands_of_France) of the [Capetian kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_dynasty) during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages); thus, the lands were an "island" in a sea of various [feudal territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism) ruled by [vassals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal) of the king.[^cite_note-8]

## History

Main article: [History of Île-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_%C3%8Ele-de-France)



Historic extent of the Île-de-France

The Île-de-France was inhabited by the *[Parisii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parisii_\(Gaul\))*, a sub-tribe of the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celt) [Senones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senones), from around the middle of the 3rd-century BC.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArbois_de_JubainvilleDottin1889132-9][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECunliffe2004201-10] One of the area's major north–south trade routes crossed the Seine on the [île de la Cité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_de_la_Cit%C3%A9); the meeting place of land and water trade routes gradually became an important trading centre.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELawrenceGondrand201025-11] The Parisii traded with many river towns (some as far away as the Iberian Peninsula) and minted their own coins for that purpose.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchmidt200965–70-12]

The [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) conquered the area in 52 BC and began their settlement on Paris's [Left Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rive_Gauche).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchmidt200988–104-13] It became a prosperous city with a forum, baths, temples, theatres, and an amphitheatre.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchmidt2009154–167-14] [Christianity](5211.md) was introduced in the middle of the 3rd century AD by [Saint Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Denis_of_Paris), the first Bishop of Paris. According to legend, when Denis refused to renounce his faith before Roman authorities, he was beheaded on the hill that became known as *Mons Martyrum* (Latin "Hill of Martyrs"), later "[Montmartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montmartre)". The legend further states that Denis walked headless from this hill to the north of the city. The place that he finally fell and was buried became an important religious shrine, the [Basilica of Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint-Denis).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchmidt2009210–11-15]

[Clovis the Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_the_Frank), the first king of the [Merovingian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty), made the city his capital in 508. As the Frankish domination of Gaul began, there was a gradual immigration by the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) to Paris and the Parisian [Francien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francien_language) dialects were born. Fortification of the Île de la Cité failed to avert [sacking by Vikings in 845](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_\(845\)), but Paris's strategic importance—with its bridges preventing ships from passing—was established by successful defence in the [Siege of Paris (885–86)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_\(885%E2%80%9386\)). In 987, [Hugh Capet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Capet), [Count of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Paris) (*comte de Paris*) and [Duke of the Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_the_Franks) (*duc des Francs*), was elected [King of the Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_monarchs) (*roi des Francs*). Under the rule of the [Capetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Capet) kings, Paris gradually became the largest and most prosperous city in France.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchmidt2009210–11-15]

The Kings of France enjoyed getting away from Paris and hunting in the game-filled forests of the region. They built palatial hunting lodges, most notably [Palace of Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Fontainebleau) and the [Palace of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles). From the time of Louis XIV to the French Revolution, Versailles was the official residence of the Kings and the seat of the French government. Île-de-France became the term used for the territory of Paris and the surrounding province, which was administered directly by the King.

During the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), the royal provinces were abolished and divided into departments, and the city and region were governed directly by the national government. After World War II, as Paris faced a major housing shortage, hundreds of massive apartment blocks for low-income residents were built around the edges of Paris. In the 1950s and the 1960s, thousands of immigrants settled in the communes bordering the city. In 1959, under President [Charles De Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_De_Gaulle), a new region was created out of six departments, which corresponded approximately with the historic region, with the name *District de la région de Paris* ("District of the Paris Region"). On 6 May 1976, as part of the process of [regionalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalism_\(politics\)), the district was reconstituted with increased administrative and political powers and renamed the Île-de-France region.

## Geography

Île-de-France is in the north of France, neighboring [Hauts-de-France](45093681.md) to the north, [Grand Est](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Est) to the east, [Bourgogne-Franche-Comté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgogne-Franche-Comt%C3%A9) to the southeast, [Centre-Val-de-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-Val_de_Loire) to the southwest, and [Normandy](21724.md) to the west.

### Departments

Île-de-France has a land area of 12,011 km<sup>2</sup> (4,637 sq mi). It is composed of eight [departments](38523.md) centred on its innermost department and capital, Paris. Around the department and municipality of Paris, urbanisation fills a first concentric ring of three departments commonly known as the *petite couronne* ("small ring"); it extends into a second outer ring of four departments known as the *grande couronne* ("large ring"). The [former department of Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_\(department\)), abolished in 1968, included the city proper and parts of the *petite couronne*.

The *petite couronne* consists of the departments of [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine), [Paris](22989.md), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) and [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne); the *grande couronne* consists of those of [Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Marne), [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines), [Essonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essonne) and [Val-d'Oise](90616.md). Politically, the region is divided into 8 departments, 25 arrondissements, 155 cantons and 1,276 communes, out of the total of 35,416 in [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France).[^cite_note-auto1-4]

### Topography

The outer parts of the Île-de-France remain largely rural. Agricultural land, forest and natural spaces occupy 78.9 percent of the region, and 28 percent of the region's land is in urban use.[^cite_note-17]

The River [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine) flows through the middle of the region, which is crisscrossed by its tributaries and sub-tributaries, including the Rivers [Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marne_\(river\)), [Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oise_\(river\)) and [Epte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epte). The River [Eure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eure_\(river\)) does not cross the region but receives water from several rivers in the Île-de-France, including the Drouette and the Vesgre. The major rivers are navigable, and, because of the modest variations of altitude in the region (between 10 metres (33 ft) and 200 metres (660 ft)), they have a tendency to meander and curve. They also create many lakes and ponds, some of which have been transformed into recreation areas, including Moisson-Mousseaux, Cergy-Neuville and [Villeneuve-Saint-Georges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeneuve-Saint-Georges).

## Economy



Paris as an engine of the global economy: the skyscrapers of [La Défense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_D%C3%A9fense), the largest purpose-built business district of Europe, with 3.35 million m² (36 million sq. ft) of office space.

[^cite_note-18]

Île-de-France produced €742 billion ([gross domestic product](12594.md))[^cite_note-cci_paris2022-5] or around 1/3 of the [economy of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_France) in 2019.[^cite_note-cci_paris2022-5]<sup>: 12 </sup> The regional economy has gradually shifted toward high-value-added service industries (finance, IT services etc.) and high-tech manufacturing (electronics, optics, aerospace etc.).[^cite_note-INSEE_201210-19] In 2014, industry represented just under five percent of active enterprises in the region, and 10.2 percent of salaried workers. Commerce and services account for 84 percent of the business establishments in the region, and have 83.3 percent of the salaried employees.[^cite_note-20] Financial services and insurance are important sectors of the regional economy; the major French banks and insurance companies, including [BNP Paribas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNP_Paribas), [Société Générale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale) and [Crédit Agricole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9dit_Agricole), all have their headquarters in the region. The region also hosts the headquarters of the top French telecom companies and utilities, including [Orange S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_S.A.), [Veolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veolia) and [EDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9_de_France). The French stock market, the [Bourse de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourse_de_Paris), now known as [Euronext Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronext_Paris), occupies a historical building in the center of Paris and is ranked fourth among global stock markets, after New York, Tokyo and London.[^cite_note-auto3-21][^cite_note-22] Other major sectors of the regional economy include energy companies ([Orano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orano), [Engie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engie), [Électricité de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9_de_France) and [Total S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_S.A.)). The two major French automobile manufacturers, [Renault](162292.md), in [Flins-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flins-sur-Seine), and [Groupe PSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupe_PSA), in [Poissy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poissy), do much of their assembly work outside France but still have research centre and large plants in the region. The leading French and European aerospace and defense companies, including [Airbus](26220236.md), [Thales Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group), [Dassault Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Aviation), [Safran Aircraft Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safran_Aircraft_Engines), the [European Space Agency](10363.md), [Alcatel-Lucent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcatel-Lucent), and [Arianespace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianespace), have a large presence in the region.[^cite_note-auto3-21]

The region is served by [Charles de Gaulle Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport). The airport is also the hub of [Air France](14998012.md). In 2024, the airport handled 70,290,260 passengers and 466,543 aircraft movements, making it the [world's fourteenth busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic) and [Europe's third busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Europe) airport (after [Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport) and [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_Airport)) in terms of passenger numbers.[^cite_note-\:1-23] Charles de Gaulle is the busiest airport within the [European Union](9317.md). In terms of cargo traffic, the airport is the second busiest in Europe, after [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport), handling 1,914,681 tonnes of cargo in 2024.[^cite_note-\:1-23] As of 2025, it was the airport served by the second highest number of [airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlines), after [Suvarnabhumi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvarnabhumi_Airport), with 105 airlines operating from it.[^cite_note-24] [Orly Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orly_Airport) also remains the busiest French airport for domestic traffic and the [second busiest French airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_France) overall in passenger traffic, with 33,123,027 passengers in 2024.

The energy sector is also well established in the region. The nuclear power industry, with its major firm being [Orano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orano), has its headquarters in Île-de-France, as does the main French oil company [Total S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_S.A.), the top French company in the Fortune Global 500, and the main electric utility, [Électricité de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9_de_France). The energy firm [Engie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engie) also has its main offices in the region at [La Défense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_D%C3%A9fense).

### Employment

In 2018 just 7.2 percent of employees in the region were engaged in industry; 62.3 percent were engaged in commerce and market services; 25.5 percent in non-market services, including government, health and education; 4.8 percent in construction; and 0.2 percent in agriculture.[^cite_note-auto-25]

The largest non-government employers in the region as of the end of 2015 were the airline [Air France](14998012.md) (40,657); the [SNCF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNCF) (French Railways, 31,955); the telecom firm [Orange S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_S.A.) (31,497); the bank [Société Générale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale) (27,361); the automotive firm [Groupe PSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupe_PSA) (19,648); [EDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9_de_France) (Electricité de France, 18,199); and [Renault](162292.md) (18,136).[^cite_note-cci_paris2018_a-26] While the Petite Couronne, or departments closest to Paris, previously employed the most industrial workers, the largest number is now in the Grande Couronne, the outer departments.[^cite_note-auto-25]

The unemployment rate in the region stood at 8.6% at the end of 2016. It varied within the region from 7.8 percent in the city of Paris, to a high of 12.7 percent in [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis), and 10 percent in [Val-d'Oise](90616.md); to regional lows of 7.4 percent in [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines); 7.5 percent in [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine); 7.7 percent in [Essonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essonne); 7.9 percent in [Seine et Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_et_Marne), and 8.8 percent in [Val de Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_de_Marne).[^cite_note-27]

### Agriculture

In 2018, 48 percent of the land of the Île-de-France was devoted to agriculture; 569,000 hectares were cultivated. The most important crops are grains (66 percent), followed by beets (7 percent), largely for industrial use, and grass for grazing. In 2014, 9,495 hectares were devoted to bio-agriculture. However, the number of persons employed in agriculture in the region dropped 33 percent between 2000 and 2015 to just 8,460 persons in 2015.[^cite_note-28]

## Tourism

The Île-de-France is one of the world's top tourist destinations, with a record 23.6 million hotel arrivals in 2017, and an estimated 50 million visitors in all types of accommodation. The largest number of visitors came from the United States, followed by England, Germany and China.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The top tourist attraction in the region in 2017 was [Disneyland Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland_Paris), which received 14.8 million visitors in 2017, followed by the [Cathedral of Notre-Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris) (est. 12 million) and the [Basilica of Sacre-Coeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sacre-Coeur) at Montmartre (est. 11.1 million visitors).[^cite_note-32]

## Sports

The Île-de-France is home to numerous high level sports clubs competing across France’s top levels. These include [Paris Saint-Germain FC](357488.md) and [Paris FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_FC) in [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1), the top flight of [French football](1963109.md), as well as [Red Star FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_FC), currently competing in [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2), the nation’s second division.[^cite_note-34]

In [rugby union](25405.md), the Île-de-France is home to [Racing 92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_92) and [Stade Français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Fran%C3%A7ais) of the [Top 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_14), the top level of [French rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_France).[^cite_note-35]

The region currently hosts two clubs competing in the [LNH Division 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LNH_Division_1), the highest level of handball in France, [Paris Saint-Germain Handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Handball) and [Tremblay Handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremblay-en-France_Handball).[^cite_note-36]

The Île-de-France has also been the site of many notable sporting events, including the final of the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), played at the [Stade de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_France) in [Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis),[^cite_note-37] as well as the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics), held in Paris.[^cite_note-38]

Seat of the regional council of Île-de-France in Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine (2021)

The Regional Council is the legislative body of the region. Its seat is in [Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine), at 2 rue Simone-Veil. On 15 December 2015, a list of candidates of the Union of the Right, a coalition of centrist and [right-wing](56522.md) parties, led by [Valérie Pécresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val%C3%A9rie_P%C3%A9cresse), narrowly won the regional election, defeating the Union of the Left, a coalition of socialists and ecologists. The socialists had governed the region for the preceding 17 years.

Since 2016 the regional council has 121 members from the Union of the Right, 66 from the Union of the Left and 22 from the [far-right](221220.md) National Front.[^cite_note-39]

## Demographics



Île-de-France population pyramid in 2023

### Population density

As of 1 January 2017, the population density of the region was 1010.9 inhabitants per square kilometer. The densest department is Paris itself, with 21,066 inhabitants per square kilometer. The least dense département is [Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Marne) with 239 residents per square kilometer.[^cite_note-cci_paris2018_b-40]

### Wealth and poverty

As of 2015 according to the official government statistics agency INSEE, 15.9 percent of residents of the region had an income below the poverty level; for residents of the city of Paris, this proportion was 16.2 percent. Poverty was highest in the departments of [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) (29 percent), [Val-d'Oise](90616.md) (17.1 percent), and [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne) (16.8 percent). It was lowest in [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines) (9.7 percent); [Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Marne) (11.8 percent), [Essonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essonne) (12.9 percent), and [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine) (12.4 percent). The department of Hauts-de-Seine is the wealthiest in France in terms of per capita GDP.[^cite_note-41]

At the 2019 census, 75.1% of the inhabitants of Île-de-France were natives of [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France), 1.7% were born in [Overseas France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_France), and 23.1% were born in foreign countries.[^cite_note-immig_1-44] A quarter of the immigrants living in the Île-de-France were born in Europe (38% of whom in Portugal), 29% were born in the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) and 22% in the rest of Africa (in particular [West](67393.md) and [Central Africa](539606.md)), 3% were born in Turkey and 15% in the rest of Asia, 5% were born in the Americas (not counting those born in the [French overseas departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_regions_of_France) in the Americas, who are not legally immigrants), and 0.1% in Oceania (not counting those born in the French territories of the South Pacific, who are not legally immigrants).[^cite_note-immig_2-45]

In 2013, roughly 2,206,000 residents of the Île-de-France were immigrants, born outside of France. This amounted to 18.5% of the population of the region, twice the national average. Four out of ten immigrants living in France reside in the region. The immigrant population of the Île-de-France has a higher proportion of non-Europeans, as well as a higher proportion of immigrants with an advanced level of education, than the rest of France. The population of immigrants is more widely distributed throughout the region than it was in the early 2000s, but the concentrations remain high in certain areas, particularly Paris and the department of Seine-Saint-Denis. The proportion of residents born outside of Metropolitan France rose between the 1999 (19.7%) and 2019 censuses (24.9%).[^cite_note-immig_3-46][^cite_note-immig_1-44]

In 2024, 152,548 babies were born in Île-de-France. 69,602 or 45.6% were born to both parents that were also born in France, 16,103 or 10.6% had a French-born mother and a foreign-born father, 14,338 or 9.4% had a foreign-born mother and a French-born father, and 52,505 or 34.4% had both parents that were born abroad.[^cite_note-47]

### *Petite Couronne*

"Petite Couronne" redirects here. For the municipality in Upper Normandy, see [Petit-Couronne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit-Couronne).



Map of the *Petite Couronne* with Paris



Locator map showing the municipalities in which the *Petite Couronne* is divided. Paris is divided into its [20 arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Paris).

The ***Petite Couronne***[^cite_note-49] (literally "Little Crown", or inner ring) is formed by the three [departments](38523.md) bordering Paris, forming a geographical *crown* around it. These departments, until 1968 part of the disbanded [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_\(department\)) department, are [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) and [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne). The most populated towns of the *Petite Couronne* are [Boulogne-Billancourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulogne-Billancourt), [Montreuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreuil,_Seine-Saint-Denis), [Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis), [Nanterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanterre) and [Créteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9teil).

The [Métropole du Grand Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Paris) is an administrative structure that comprises Paris and the three departments of the *Petite Couronne*, plus seven additional communes in the *Grande Couronne*.

### *Grande Couronne*

"Grande Couronne" redirects here. For the municipality in Upper Normandy, see [Grand-Couronne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand-Couronne).

The ***Grande Couronne***[^cite_note-51] (literally Large Crown, or outer ring) includes the outer four departments of Île-de-France, which do not border Paris. They are [Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Marne) (77), [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines) (78), [Essonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essonne) (91) and [Val-d'Oise](90616.md) (95). The last three departments formed the [Seine-et-Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Oise) department until it was disbanded in 1968. The city of [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles,_Yvelines) is part of the area.
