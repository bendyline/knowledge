**SoFi Stadium** ([/ˈsoʊfaɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*SOH-fy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-15] is a [multi-purpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_stadium) [indoor stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_stadium) in [Inglewood, California](107640.md), United States, a suburb of [Los Angeles](18110.md). SoFi occupies the former site of the [Hollywood Park Racetrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Park_Racetrack) and neighbors the [Kia Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Forum) and [Intuit Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Dome).

Opened in September 2020, the stadium has a capacity of 70,240, expandable to over 100,000 for major events, and a translucent roof made of [ETFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETFE) panels allowing for sunlight and climate control.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] It is home to the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL)'s [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) and [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers). The stadium hosted [Super Bowl LVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVI) in 2022, the [2023 College Football Playoff National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_College_Football_Playoff_National_Championship), [WrestleMania 39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_39) in 2023, the [2023 CONCACAF Gold Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup_final), and the [LA Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Bowl) from 2021 to 2025. It hosted eight matches during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), including the [United States](582488.md) opening match against [Paraguay](804025.md).[^cite_note-19] It will host [Super Bowl LXI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LXI) in 2027, and the opening ceremony and swimming events for the [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md).

SoFi Stadium is one of two stadiums currently shared by a pair of NFL teams, the other being [MetLife Stadium](5476983.md) shared by the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) and [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets). It is the first stadium complex outside of the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) to host two NFL teams concurrently. The stadium lies within the [Hollywood Park](#Hollywood_Park) complex, a [mixed-use neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-use_neighborhood) on the site of the former racetrack. [Hollywood Park Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Park_Casino) re-opened in a new building on the property in October 2016, becoming the development's first establishment to open.[^cite_note-casino-20]

## Design



Southwest entrance into American Airlines Plaza between the stadium and the [YouTube Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_Theater)



East entrance



The escalators at SoFi Stadium; September 18, 2022

SoFi Stadium was designed by [HKS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HKS,_Inc.) and consists of the stadium itself, a pedestrian plaza, and a performance venue. Above the stadium is an independently supported translucent canopy which covers the stadium proper, the adjacent pedestrian plaza, and the attached performance venue. The million-square-foot canopy is made up of 302 [ETFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETFE) panels, 46 of which can be opened to provide ventilation, supported by a cable net.[^cite_note-21] The canopy has 27,000 embedded LED pucks, which can display images and video that can also be seen from airplanes flying into [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md).[^cite_note-22] The stadium bowl has open sides and seats 70,240 spectators for most events, with the ability to expand by 30,000 seats for larger events.[^cite_note-23] Despite the roof, the open sides of the stadium still make it vulnerable to lightning delays, with the first such delay in an NFL game between the Chargers and the [Las Vegas Raiders](22312.md) on October 4, 2021.[^cite_note-24] The attached music and theatre venue, known as [YouTube Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_Theater), has a capacity of 6,000 seats. The stadium and performance center are considered to be separate facilities under one roof.[^cite_note-25] While the stadium has a skylight roof on top, it is an open-air facility that has often been mistaken as a traditional climate-controlled domed stadium due to how its structure looks from afar and/or on television broadcasts.

Another component of the stadium's design is the Infinity Screen by [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung), an ovular, double-sided [4K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4K_resolution) [HDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-dynamic-range_video) [video board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumbotron), the first of its kind, that is suspended from the roof over the field. Formerly known as "the Oculus" before a name change, the structure weighs 2.2 million pounds (1,000 t) and displays 80 million pixels.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The Infinity Screen also houses the stadium's 260-speaker [audio system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_array) by [JBL professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JBL) as well as 56 [5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G) wireless antennas.[^cite_note-\:0-29]

Outdoor sports in California are usually played on [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass) due to the state's highly favorable climate. However, a grass field is very difficult to maintain to an acceptable standard when it is used by more than one gridiron football team. Because SoFi Stadium was intended from the outset to be used by two NFL teams, the designers opted not to install a natural playing surface. The stadium joined [California Memorial Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Memorial_Stadium) and [Valley Children's Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Children's_Stadium) as the only major sports facilities in California currently in use to have [artificial turf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_turf) installed.

## History

### Background

Main article: [Hollywood Park Racetrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Park_Racetrack)

The stadium site was previously home to **Hollywood Park**, later sold and referred to as **Betfair Hollywood Park**, which was a [thoroughbred](18842022.md) [race course](74711.md) from 1938 until it was shut down for racing and training in December 2013. Most of the complex was demolished in 2014 to make way for new construction with the rest demolished in late 2016 after the Hollywood Park Casino, which remained open after the track itself closed, moved to a new building. The current stadium was not the first stadium proposed for the site. The site was almost home to an NFL stadium two decades earlier. In May 1995, after the departure of the Rams for [St. Louis](27687.md), the NFL team owners approved, by a 27–1 vote with two abstentions, a resolution supporting a plan to build a \$200 million, privately funded stadium on property owned by Hollywood Park for the [Los Angeles Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Los_Angeles_Raiders). [Al Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Davis), who was then the Raiders owner, balked and refused the deal over a stipulation that he would have had to accept a second team at the stadium.[^cite_note-34]

On January 31, 2014, the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* reported that [Stan Kroenke](5336231.md), owner of the [St. Louis Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_St._Louis_Rams), purchased a 60-acre (24 ha) parcel of land just north of the Hollywood Park site in the area that had been studied by the NFL in the past for the 1995 Raiders proposal and that the league at one point attempted to purchase.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-autogenerated1-36] This set off immediate speculation as to what Kroenke's intentions were for the site: After the site's former Hollywood Park owners gave up on getting an NFL stadium for the site in the mid-2000s it was sold and planned to be a [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) Supercenter; however, in 2014, most of the speculation centered on the site as a possible stadium site or training facility for the Rams.[^cite_note-37] NFL Commissioner [Roger Goodell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Goodell) represented that Kroenke informed the league of the purchase. As an NFL owner, any purchase of land in which a potential stadium could be built must be disclosed to the league. Speculation about [the Rams' returning to their home of nearly fifty years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_National_Football_League_in_Los_Angeles) had already been discussed when Kroenke was one of the finalists in bidding for ownership in the [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md), but speculation increased when the news broke that the Rams owner had a possible stadium site in hand.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]



2015 aerial view of former racetrack and complex site, with the [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles) skyline in background

Nearly a year went by without a word from Kroenke about his intentions for the land, as he failed to ever address the St. Louis media, or the Hollywood Park Land Company, about what the site may be used for. There was, however, speculation about the future of the Rams franchise until it was reported that the National Football League would not be allowing any franchise relocation for the 2015 season.[^cite_note-40]

On January 5, 2015, [Stockbridge Capital Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockbridge_Capital_Group), the owners of the Hollywood Park Land Company, announced that it had partnered with [Kroenke Sports & Entertainment](5336313.md) to add the northern 60-acre (24 ha) parcel to the rest of the development project and build a multi-purpose 70,240-seat stadium designed for the NFL.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-autogenerated1-36][^cite_note-Piper-43] The project would include the stadium and a performance arts venue attached to the stadium with up to 6,000 seats. The previously approved Hollywood Park development was reconfigured to fit the stadium, and included plans for up to 900,000 square feet (84,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of retail, 800,000 square feet (74,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of office space, 2,500 new residential units, a luxury [hotel](14276.md) with over 300 rooms, 25 acres (10 ha) of public parks, playgrounds, open space, a lake, and pedestrian, bicycle, and mass-transit access for future services.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-36][^cite_note-Piper-43] On February 24, 2015, the Inglewood City Council approved plans with a 5–0 unanimous vote to combine the 60-acre (24 ha) plot of land with the larger Hollywood Park development and rezone the area to include sports and entertainment capabilities. This essentially cleared the way for developers to begin construction on the venue as planned in December 2015.[^cite_note-nbclosangeles1-44][^cite_note-nbcsports1-45][^cite_note-46]

It was reported in early February 2015, that "earth was being moved" and the site was being graded in preparation for the construction that would begin later in the year.[^cite_note-47]

The project was competing directly with a rival proposal. On February 19, 2015, the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Oakland_Raiders) and the [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_San_Diego_Chargers) announced plans for a [privately financed \$1.85 billion stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Los_Angeles_NFL_stadiums#Carson_Stadium_\(2015\)) that the two teams would have built in [Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson,_California) if they were to move to the Los Angeles market.[^cite_note-48] The project was, like the Inglewood project, also approved to move forward and cleared for development.[^cite_note-49] The two projects spent the remainder of 2015 jockeying for the right to get approved by the NFL.[^cite_note-50]

### Financing

The stadium was built privately,[^cite_note-51] but as of 2015, the developer was seeking significant tax breaks from Inglewood.[^cite_note-52] At the commencement of construction, the cost of the stadium was estimated at \$2.66 billion. But internal league documents, produced by the NFL in March 2018, indicated a need to raise the debt ceiling for the stadium and facility to a total of \$4.963 billion. Team owners voted to approve this new debt ceiling at a meeting that same month.[^cite_note-53] Another \$500 million in loans was approved by the NFL in May 2020, putting the total cost at \$5.5 billion and making it the [most expensive stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_expensive_stadium) ever built.[^cite_note-nbc-9][^cite_note-54]

### Construction



2016 aerial view of the stadium construction site, adjacent to [The Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forum_\(Inglewood,_California\)). The new [Hollywood Park Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Park_Casino) is in the foreground.

The NFL approved the Inglewood proposal and the Rams' relocation back to Los Angeles, 30–2, on January 12, 2016, over the rival Carson proposal.[^cite_note-55] On July 14, 2016, it was announced that [Turner Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Construction) and [AECOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AECOM) Hunt would oversee construction of the stadium and that the architectural firm [HKS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HKS,_Inc.) would design the stadium.[^cite_note-56] On October 19, 2016, the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) determined that a 110-foot (34 m) tall LB 44 rotary drill rig would not pose a hazard to air navigation, so it approved the first of several pieces of heavy equipment to be used during construction. The stadium design had been under review by the FAA for more than a year because of concerns about how the structure would interact with radar at nearby [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md) (LAX).[^cite_note-57] On December 16, 2016, it was reported in *[Sports Business Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Business_Journal)* that the FAA had declined to issue permits for cranes needed to build the structure. "We're not going to evaluate any crane applications until our concerns with the overall project are resolved," said FAA spokesman Ian Gregor.[^cite_note-58] The FAA had previously recommended building the stadium at another site because of the risks posed to LAX—echoing concerns raised by former [United States Secretary of Homeland Security](212926.md) [Tom Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Ridge).[^cite_note-59] The [Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) held the groundbreaking construction ceremony at the stadium site on November 17, 2016. The ceremony featured [NFL Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Commissioner) [Roger Goodell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Goodell) and Rams' owner Stan Kroenke.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] On December 23, 2016, the FAA approved the large construction [cranes](318378.md) to build the stadium.[^cite_note-62]

On May 18, 2017, developers announced that record rainfall in the area had postponed the stadium's completion and opening from 2019 until the [2020 NFL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NFL_season).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] On August 8, 2017, the LA Stadium Premiere Center opened in [Playa Vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_Vista,_California), featuring interactive multimedia displays and models showcasing the design and features of the new stadium (with a particular focus on prospective buyers of premium suites and seats at the facility).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

In March 2018, the NFL announced that it would relocate its NFL Media unit (which manages the NFL's in-house media units, including [NFL Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Network), digital properties, and [NFL Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Films) among other units) from [Culver City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culver_City) to a new 200,000-square-foot (19,000 m<sup>2</sup>) facility neighboring the stadium in the Hollywood Park development including a studio capable of hosting audiences, as well as an outdoor studio. The new facility was completed in 2021.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] On June 26, 2018, the new stadium was ceremonially [topped out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topping_out).[^cite_note-69]

As of August 2019, one year before the planned opening, Rams [chief operating officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer) Kevin Demoff stated that the stadium was 75 percent complete.[^cite_note-70]



The almost-completed stadium in July 2020

In January 2020, Demoff announced that construction was 85 percent complete, with roof and oculus work, as well as seat installation, still in progress.[^cite_note-71] In February 2020, a large crane collapsed—no one was injured.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Amidst the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_California) and [stay-at-home orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stay-at-home_order) issued by the California state government in March 2020, construction (exempted as a critical infrastructure project) continued with [social distancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_distancing) and heightened health and safety standards. Demoff acknowledged that there was a possibility that its completion could be delayed, explaining that it was "not the time you want to be finishing a stadium, in this environment as you prepare", but that "our stadium, and I believe the [Raiders' stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegiant_Stadium) as well, will both be amazing when they are finished and when they will begin play, which will certainly happen in the near future, whether that's in July, August, September, in 2021".[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Five construction workers were reported to have tested positive, including an ironworker who had worked in an assembly area away from the structure, and a [backfill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backfill) operator who had worked in an "isolated area outside the building" and had not entered it.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] On June 5, 2020, construction on the facility was temporarily halted after an ironworker fell to his death through a hole in the roof created by the removal of a panel for maintenance.[^cite_note-LAT_2020-09-04-78][^cite_note-79] On June 9, 2020, construction on the facility resumed everywhere but the roof.[^cite_note-80]

### Canceled or postponed opening events, first events



Interior of SoFi Stadium in November 2021

The entirety of the NFL preseason was also cancelled; the Rams held their first practice at the stadium on August 22, 2020.[^cite_note-81] On August 25, the Rams and Chargers announced that all games at the stadium would be held [behind closed doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behind_closed_doors_\(sports\)) "until further notice".[^cite_note-82] An official ribbon-cutting ceremony was hosted on September 8, ahead of its first NFL event on September 13—featuring the Rams hosting and defeating the [Dallas Cowboys](8121.md) 20–17 in the first [Sunday Night game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sunday_Night_Football) of the season.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] The Chargers would have their first game at the stadium a week later, though they would fall to the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) 23–20. The first athletic contest with spectators present occurred on May 15, 2021, with [LA Giltinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Giltinis) defeating [Utah Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Warriors), 38–27, in a [Major League Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Rugby) match before 4,880 spectators.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] The [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) hosted the [Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Bears) for the first NFL regular-season game at the stadium with fans in attendance on September 12, 2021, a 34–14 Rams win before a crowd of 70,445. A week later, the [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers) would hold their first regular season game at the stadium with fans in attendance, though they would fall to the Dallas Cowboys 20–17 before a crowd of 70,240. The first [playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_playoffs) game at the stadium took place on January 17, 2022, a 34–11 Rams victory against the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals) before a crowd of 70,625.

Two months later, it was announced that the stadium would play host to Monster Jam World Finals 23 on May 18, 2024. Monster Jam returned to the stadium on May 17, 2025, and on April 11, 2026.

The stadium hosted City Year Los Angeles Spring Break Fundraiser on May 4, 2024, with a performance by [John Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Legend).[^cite_note-95] On February 1, 2025, the stadium hosted the [Honda Battle of the Bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Battle_of_the_Bands), the first on the West Coast.

## Naming

On September 15, 2019, it was announced that the [San Francisco](49728.md)–based financial services company [SoFi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoFi) had acquired the [naming rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_rights) to the new stadium under a 20-year deal valued at over \$30 million per-year, a record for any naming rights for a sports venue.[^cite_note-96] The company became an official partner of both the Rams and the Chargers, as well as a partner of the performance venue and surrounding entertainment district.[^cite_note-97]

The covered open space formerly known as *Champions Plaza* between the playing field and the performance venue within the stadium was officially named [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines) Plaza, as the airline has a hub at nearby [LAX](18131.md). The airline was named the first founding partner on August 6, 2019.[^cite_note-98]

The performance venue was officially named [YouTube Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_Theater) ([the company](3524766.md) is based in [San Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bruno,_California)) on June 28, 2021.[^cite_note-99]

For the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), the stadium was referred to "Los Angeles Stadium" in accordance with FIFA's policy on corporate-sponsored names.[^cite_note-100] Likewise, for the [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md), the stadium will be referred to as "2028 Stadium" for the duration of the Games.[^cite_note-101]

## Teams

The [St. Louis Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_St._Louis_Rams) were first to commit to moving to the stadium, as NFL approval for their relocation to Los Angeles was obtained on January 12, 2016. The approval, as part of a concession made by Kroenke to get the stadium project and Rams relocation approved, also gave the [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_San_Diego_Chargers) the first option to relocate to Los Angeles and share the stadium with the Rams, conditioned on a negotiated lease agreement between the two teams. The option would have expired on January 15, 2017, at which time the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Oakland_Raiders) would have acquired the same option.[^cite_note-102]

On January 29, 2016, the Rams and the Chargers came to an agreement in principle to share the stadium. Both teams would contribute a \$200 million stadium loan from the NFL and [personal seat license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_seat_license) fees to the construction costs and would pay \$1 per year in rent to the facility's controlling entity, StadCo LA, LLC.[^cite_note-2016agreement-103][^cite_note-SoFiStadCoRent-104][^cite_note-StadCoLA-3] The same day, Chargers chairman and CEO [Dean Spanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Spanos) announced the team would remain in [San Diego](28504.md) for the [2016 NFL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NFL_season), while continuing to work with local government on a new stadium.[^cite_note-Spanos2016-105] Measure C (the Chargers stadium proposal) did not receive the requisite number of votes required for passage.

On January 12, 2017, the Chargers exercised their option and announced plans to relocate to Los Angeles for the 2017 season, making the Chargers the second tenant at the stadium and returning them to the market where they played their inaugural season in 1960.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] The Chargers exercising of their option led the Raiders [to move to Las Vegas, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Raiders_relocation_to_Las_Vegas), instead, and they began play at [Allegiant Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegiant_Stadium) in 2020.

The Rams' and the Chargers' move into the stadium marked the return of major professional sports to Inglewood for the first time since the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) and [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) left [The Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Forum) for the [Staples Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto.com_Arena) (now Crypto.com Arena) in Downtown Los Angeles in October 1999.

In November 2025, it was reported that the [UCLA Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Bruins_football) were exploring a move from the [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\)) in [Pasadena](92408.md) to SoFi Stadium.[^cite_note-108]

#### Super Bowl LVI



Pre-game warmups before Super Bowl LVI

Main article: [Super Bowl LVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVI)

SoFi Stadium hosted [Super Bowl LVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVI) on February 13, 2022, marking the first Super Bowl to be played in the Los Angeles area since [Super Bowl XXVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVII) in 1993. The stadium was originally awarded [Super Bowl LV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LV) (2021) at an NFL owners' meeting in May 2016;[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] in May 2017, because the stadium's opening was delayed to 2020, the NFL chose to re-award Super Bowl LV to [Raymond James Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_James_Stadium) in Tampa (which was the remaining city in a pool of four used to determine the hosts of [Super Bowl LIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIII) through LV), and award LVI to Los Angeles instead. Because issues may need to be addressed in an inaugural season, the NFL does not allow a stadium to host the Super Bowl during its first season of operation.[^cite_note-111]

In 2022, SoFi Stadium became the first stadium to host a conference championship game and the Super Bowl in the same year. With the Rams winning the 2021 NFC Championship Game, they became only the second team behind the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers) to play a Super Bowl in their home stadium, although the Rams were the away team, since the AFC ([Cincinnati Bengals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Bengals)) was the designated home team. Following the Rams' 23–20 victory against the Bengals, it also became the second stadium to see its main tenant win the Super Bowl after Raymond James Stadium.

#### 2026 FIFA World Cup

Main article: [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)



Inside SoFi Stadium during the quarter-final match between [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_national_football_team) and [Belgium](679716.md) at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).

The local bid for Los Angeles in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) was organized by private businesses led by [AEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschutz_Entertainment_Group) with assistance from the Los Angeles Sports and Entertainment District Commission, [Los Angeles FC](44255607.md), the [LA Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Galaxy), and [Rose Bowl Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Stadium). The Los Angeles City Council approved the bid after private businesses showed support and offered to pay hosting costs.[^cite_note-discoverlosangeles.com-123] SoFi Stadium was initially not selected as a bidding venue in the winning [Canada–Mexico–United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_2026_FIFA_World_Cup_bid) bid because the organizing committee left unbuilt venues out of its final evaluations, but it was later added to the proposal.[^cite_note-124] The United bid to host the World Cup was selected by [FIFA](11049.md) on June 13, 2018,[^cite_note-discoverlosangeles.com-123] and SoFi stadium was confirmed to host multiple matches during the tournament.[^cite_note-125] SoFi Stadium was one of sixteen venues set to host matches. It was also one of eleven US venues being used and was one of two venues in California which hosted matches, the other being [Levi's Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi's_Stadium) in the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area).

In June 2026, workers at SoFi Stadium voted 96 percent in favor of authorizing a strike in early June 2026 after working for a year without a contract. [Unite Here Local 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNITE_HERE), representing nearly 2,000 food service workers employed by [Legends Hospitality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legends_Hospitality), cited stalled negotiations over pay and safety protections related to potential [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement) activity at the stadium. The union had asked FIFA and stadium operators to bar ICE from the venue and filed complaints with California regulators over FIFA's accreditation process, which collected workers' nationality and country of birth. On June 1, [Los Angeles County Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Sheriff's_Department) Robert Luna said federal officials had told him that civil immigration enforcement would not take place at World Cup games or events in Los Angeles.[^cite_note-126] A tentative agreement was reached on June 8, three days before the stadium's first match, preserving a contractual right for workers to strike if the union determines that federal immigration enforcement threatens worker safety during a match.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

The stadium lacked a regulation-sized [soccer field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_field) due to its narrow width, which has been criticized by FIFA officials. To satisfy FIFA requirements, several field level seats would be removed and the turf surface replaced by a grass field.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] In September 2023, [Stan Kroenke](5336231.md) threatened to pull hosting from the stadium until a dispute regarding revenue sharing between him and FIFA could be resolved, though it was confirmed in January 2024 that the stadium would still be hosting matches. On February 4, 2024, it was announced that the stadium would host the opening match for the [United States](582488.md) on June 12, 2026, as well as their third group match on June 25, 2026. SoFi Stadium hosted eight matches: five group stage matches, two [Round of 32](36581929.md#Round_of_32) matches and a [quarterfinal](36581929.md#Quarter-finals) match.[^cite_note-131]

Due to FIFA's clean stadium policy, SoFi Stadium was temporarily renamed to Los Angeles Stadium for the tournament.[^cite_note-132]

### 2028 Summer Olympics and Paralympics

Main articles: [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md) and [2028 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Summer_Paralympics)

SoFi Stadium (which, per prior precedent, will be renamed for the duration of the Games according to [sponsorship rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambush_marketing)) will host the [opening and closing ceremonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games_ceremony) of [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md) and [Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Summer_Paralympics) (with organizers confirming a split format for the Olympics that would also incorporate the [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum)).[^cite_note-138]

The stadium will host [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_Summer_Olympics) events with a planned configuration seating 38,000 spectators, making it the largest swimming venue in Olympic history. The pool will be constructed prior to the opening ceremony, and covered by the staging. To facilitate the reconfiguration process following the opening ceremony, swimming will notably take place during the second week of the Games, rather than the first as is in the several previous games.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] As part of their planning, the stadium sent a team to review how another NFL stadium – the [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts)' [Lucas Oil Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Oil_Stadium) – successfully installed the pool to host the [2024 US Olympic swimming trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Olympic_trials_\(swimming\)).[^cite_note-141]

[Warner Bros. Studios’ The Ranch Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Ranch) is planned to be the site of the [International Broadcast Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Broadcast_Centre) for the Games, while [Hollywood Park Studios](#Hollywood_Park) by SoFi Stadium will host the Main Press Center.[^cite_note-142]

## Hollywood Park



NFL Los Angeles campus and [Kia Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Forum)

The stadium and the adjacent [Lake Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Park_\(California\)) are central to Hollywood Park, an entertainment complex and master-planned neighborhood named after the former horse racing track that sat on the site. Hollywood Park consists of over 8.5 million square feet (790,000 m<sup>2</sup>) that is used for [office space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_park) and [condominiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium), a 12-screen [Cinépolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cin%C3%A9polis) multiplex movie theater featuring [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX), ballrooms, outdoor spaces for community programming, retail, a [fitness center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gym), a 13-story luxury hotel, a brewery, an 18,000 square feet (1,700 m<sup>2</sup>) [soundstage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_studio) named **Hollywood Park Studios**,[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] five restaurants and a shopping and entertainment complex.[^cite_note-170] Adjacent to the stadium is an artificial lake colloquially known as [Rivers Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivers_Lake),[^cite_note-NBCOlympics-171][^cite_note-StudioMLA-172][^cite_note-ABC7-173][^cite_note-Turf_Show_Times-174] with a waterfall and fountain. The [YouTube Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_Theater), which seats 6,000, is under the southeast corner of the stadium canopy.[^cite_note-175]

The Kali Hotel and Rooftop, a 300-room, [Marriott Autograph Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autograph_Collection_Hotels) property developed by KPC Development Company, is under construction at Hollywood Park.[^cite_note-Kali_Hotel_Tops_Out_Main_Structure-176] Designed by LJC Design & Engineering and built by Clayco, the hotel broke ground in October 2024, topped out in September 2025, and is expected to open in September 2026.[^cite_note-177] It is the only lodging property permitted within the Hollywood Park Specific Plan.[^cite_note-Kali_Hotel_Tops_Out_Main_Structure-176]

The first establishment to open in Hollywood Park was the [Hollywood Park Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Park_Casino), which opened on October 21, 2016.[^cite_note-casino-20] The NFL opened a new west coast campus in a seven-story office tower at Hollywood Park next to the stadium on September 8, 2021. Replacing a facility in [Culver City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culver_City,_California), "NFL Los Angeles" consists of 214,063 square feet (19,887.1 m<sup>2</sup>) of leased office space and a 74,992 square feet (6,967.0 m<sup>2</sup>) studio, and features 20 conference rooms, five soundstages and an outdoor studio. One of its main tenants is NFL Media—which operates [NFL Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Films), [NFL Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Network), [NFL RedZone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_RedZone), and the league's digital properties from the building.[^cite_note-nflmediacampus-178][^cite_note-179] The league has also used the facility as a secondary hub for replay reviews.[^cite_note-180] On August 6, 2025, as part of [ESPN, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN,_Inc.)'s acquisition of the [NFL Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Network), and [NFL RedZone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_RedZone), ESPN took over the lease for the studio space that the NFL Network occupied.[^cite_note-181] On July 1, 2026, ESPN announced that they would be relocating their Los Angeles operations to Inglewood.[^cite_note-182]

## Public transportation&#x20;

The **SoFi Stadium Express Shuttle** is a free [shuttle bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttle_bus) service operated by the [Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Metropolitan_Transportation_Authority) (Metro), providing transportation between SoFi Stadium and the [LAX/Metro Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX/Metro_Transit_Center). From there, passengers can connect to the Metro [C Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) and [K Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)).[^cite_note-183] The shuttle operates during pre-season, regular-season, and [post-season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_playoffs) [NFL](21211.md) games, as well as select special events.[^cite_note-\:3-184] Service runs every five to eight minutes beginning three hours before kickoff and continuing until 90 minutes after the conclusion of the event.[^cite_note-185]

[GTrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTrans) operates the [7X Stadium Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTrans#7X), a Sunday-only shuttle route connecting the [Harbor Gateway Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Gateway_Transit_Center) in [Gardena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardena,_California) to SoFi Stadium. The route also provides a transfer to the Metro C Line at the [Hawthorne/Lennox station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne/Lennox_station).[^cite_note-186]

SoFi Stadium is also served year-round by several local bus routes, including Metro lines: [115](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Bus#115), [117](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Bus#117), and [212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Bus#212), as well as [Torrance Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance_Transit) line [10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance_Transit#10).[^cite_note-\:3-184]

The city of [Inglewood](107640.md) proposed building the [Inglewood Transit Connector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inglewood_Transit_Connector), a 1.6-mile (2.6 km) automated people mover that would have linked the [Downtown Inglewood station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Inglewood_station) with the Hollywood Park area. However, the project was later canceled in 2024 though it has since been revived.[^cite_note-187]
