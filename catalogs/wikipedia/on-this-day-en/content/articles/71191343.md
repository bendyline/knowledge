# Legality of conversion therapy



Map of jurisdictions that have bans on sexual orientation and gender identity change efforts with minors as of October 2026:&#x20;

&#x20; Criminal prohibition against conversion therapy on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity

&#x20; Only medical professionals are banned from performing conversion therapy

&#x20; No ban on conversion therapy

[Conversion therapy](26410.md) is the [pseudoscientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscientific) practice of attempting to change a person's [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation) or [gender identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_identity).[^cite_note-1] As of October 2026, twenty-eight countries have bans on conversion therapy. Fourteen of them ban the practice by any person: [Belgium](3343.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Cyprus](5593.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Iceland](14531.md), [Malta](19137.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Netherlands](21148.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Norway](21241.md), [Portugal](23033.md) and [Spain](26667.md).&#x20;

Eight ban its practice by medical professionals (or, in some cases, those who receive monetary compensation without necessarily being medical experts) only: [Albania](738.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Greece](12108.md), [India](14533.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Taiwan](25734.md) and [Vietnam](202354.md).

Another seven, namely [Argentina](18951905.md), [Fiji](10707.md), [Nauru](21302.md), [Paraguay](23420.md), [Samoa](27238.md), [Switzerland](26748.md) and [Uruguay](31843.md), have bans for diagnoses based solely on sexual orientation or gender identity, without specifically banning conversion therapy. This effectively amounts to a ban on health professionals since they would not generally engage in therapy without a diagnosis. In addition, jurisdictions within [Australia](4689264.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) also ban conversion therapy.[^cite_note-2]

At a supranational level, the [European Union](9317.md) is considering banning conversion therapy across [its member states](10890716.md). Similarly, [citizens' initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Citizens'_Initiative) started collecting signatures in May 2024 calling on the European Commission to outlaw such practices.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] On January 29, 2026, the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) adopted a resolution urging [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Council_of_Europe) to ban conversion therapy.[^cite_note-5]

In 1993, the Superior Court of San Francisco's Family Court placed 15-year-old lesbian [Lyn Duff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyn_Duff) under the guardianship of a foster couple after her mother committed her to Rivendell Psychiatric Center in West Jordan, Utah, where she allegedly endured physical abuse under the guise of conversion therapy. Lyn Duff's petition to leave her mother was granted without court opinion.[^cite_note-Lyn_Duff-191][^cite_note-Molnar1997-192][^cite_note-193][^cite_note-Orly-194]

In 1997, the [United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Ninth_Circuit) addressed conversion therapy in the context of an asylum application. A Russian citizen "had been apprehended by the Russian militia, registered at a clinic as a 'suspected lesbian', and forced to undergo treatment for lesbianism, such as '[sedative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedative) drugs' and hypnosis. ... The Ninth Circuit held that the conversion treatments to which Pitcherskaia had been subjected constituted mental and physical torture." The court rejected the argument that the treatments to which Pitcherskaia had been subjected did not constitute persecution because they had been intended to help her, not harm her, and stated "human rights laws cannot be sidestepped by simply couching actions that torture mentally or physically in benevolent terms such as 'curing' or 'treating' the victims".[^cite_note-Gans-195]

On 25 June 2015, a New Jersey jury found the Jewish conversion therapy organization JONAH guilty of consumer fraud in the case *[Ferguson v. JONAH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson_v._JONAH)* for promising to be able to change its clients' sexual urges. The jury determined its commercial practices to be unconscionable.[^cite_note-196]
