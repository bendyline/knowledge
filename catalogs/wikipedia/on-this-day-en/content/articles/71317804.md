**Melissa Jefferson-Wooden** ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Jefferson**; born February 21, 2001) is an American track and field sprinter who competes in the [100 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_meters) and [200 m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/200_m). She rose to prominence at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metres), winning bronze in the 100 metres.&#x20;

At the [2025 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships), she became the first American woman—and the second woman ever—to achieve the sprint triple, capturing gold in the 100 m, 200 m, and 4 × 100 m relay. Her winning 100 m time of 10.61 s set a new championship record and made her the fourth-fastest woman of all time. She continued her form into the 2026 season, winning the sprint double at the inaugural [2026 World Ultimate Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Ultimate_Championship_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metres). Her winning 200 m time of 21.47 s made her the third-fastest woman of all time over the distance.&#x20;

Jefferson-Wooden finished the 2025 and 2026 seasons as the world's top-ranked female sprinter in both the 100 m and 200 m. She has won gold in the 4 × 100 m relay in Tokyo 2025 World Athletics Championships, in the [4 × 100 metres relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_%C3%97_100_metres_relay) at the 2024 Olympic Games, as well as the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_Championships) and [2023 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships), and the [2024 World Athletics Relays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Athletics_Relays).

## Career

### 2022: U.S. Champion and 4 × 100 m World championships gold

Jefferson competed at [Coastal Carolina University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Carolina_University) and won the [NCAA Indoor 60 m Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NCAA_Division_I_Indoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) in 2022.[^cite_note-4] She won the 100 metres at the [2022 USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) to qualify for the [2022 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_Championships) held in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon). Jefferson reached the final and finished eighth.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] She won gold as part of the [4 × 100 metres relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metres_relay) team.[^cite_note-7]

### 2023: 4 × 100 m World Championships gold

In 2023, she placed fifth at the [2023 USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) and was selected for the relay team. The team won the 4 × 100 m relay at the [2023 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships) held in [Budapest](36787.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### 2024: 100 m Olympic bronze, 4 × 100 m gold

In 2024, Jefferson was a member of the USA team which won the 4 × 100 m relay at the [2024 World Athletics Relays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Athletics_Relays) in [Nassau, Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau,_Bahamas).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] She won the 100m at the [LA Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Grand_Prix) meet in May.[^cite_note-12] In June, she finished second in the 100 metres at the [U.S. Olympic trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Olympic_trials_\(track_and_field\)) in Eugene, Oregon, in a new personal best of 10.80 seconds.[^cite_note-13] At the [Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics), she won a bronze medal in the 100 m with a time of 10.92 s.[^cite_note-14] She won gold as part of the 4 × 100 m relay team.[^cite_note-15]



Jefferson (right) and her training partner, [Twanisha Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twanisha_Terry) (left), at the [2024 US Olympic trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Olympic_trials_\(track_and_field\))

### 2025: Triple World Champion

In September 2024, it was announced that Jefferson had signed up for the inaugural season of the [Michael Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Johnson_\(sprinter\)) founded [Grand Slam Track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_Track) taking place in 2025.[^cite_note-16] She won both the 100 metres and 200 metres races in the Short Sprints category, ahead of [Jenna Prandini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenna_Prandini) and [Jacious Sears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacious_Sears), at the inaugural [Grand Slam Track Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Grand_Slam_Track_Kingston) meeting on April 5–6, 2025, in [Kingston, Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Jamaica).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Jefferson ran 10.75 (+2.4 wind) to win the women's 100 metres race at the second slam event in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Slam) on May 2, 2025.[^cite_note-19] The following day, she became the first athlete to claim back-to-back titles as third place in the 200 metres race, which meant she finished a point clear of 200 m winner [Gabby Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrielle_Thomas) in the two-race format.[^cite_note-20] She ran a personal best of 21.99 seconds to win the 200 metres race at the [2025 Philadelphia Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Philadelphia_Slam) on May 31, 2025, beating the 2024 Olympic champion in the 200m, [Gabby Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabby_Thomas).[^cite_note-21] The following day, she moved into the top-ten all-time for the 100 metres, running a personal best and current world lead of 10.73 seconds.[^cite_note-22] On July 5, She ran 10.75 seconds to beat Olympic champion [Julien Alfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Alfred) into second in the 100 metres at the [2025 Prefontaine Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Prefontaine_Classic) into a headwind (−1.5 m/s).[^cite_note-23]

She ran 10.65 seconds (+0.4) to win 100 metres at the [2025 USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships), in Eugene, with the time making her the joint-fifth-fastest woman in history.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] She also ran a personal best to win the 200 meters at the championships in 21.84 seconds.[^cite_note-26] Later that month, she ran 10.66 seconds (+0.1) to win the 100 metres at the Diamond League event in Silesia, and 10.76 (−0.3) to win at the [2025 Memorial Van Damme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Memorial_Van_Damme) in the Diamond League, in Brussels, Belgium.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

At the [2025 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships), she won the [100 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metres) in a new championship record time of 10.61 (+0.3), and became the fourth fastest woman in history. She also won the [women's 200 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_200_metres) at the championships, running a world lead of 21.68 seconds.[^cite_note-29] She also won the gold medal with the American team in the [women's 4 x 100 metres relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metres_relay) at the championships, completing the sprint triple.[^cite_note-30]

### 2026: World Ultimate double champion and American 200 m champion

Jefferson-Wooden opened her season on June 4, when she was second to Julien Alfred in 22.17 seconds in the women's 200 metres at the [2026 Diamond League](81417246.md) event in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Golden_Gala).[^cite_note-31] On June 7, in the Diamond League, Jefferson-Wooden won over 100 metres in 10.84 seconds (+0.8), at the [Bauhausgalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bauhausgalan) in Stockholm, her fastest season opener over the distance.[^cite_note-32] On July 4, she won the 100 metres at the [Prefontaine Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Prefontaine_Classic), winning in a photo finish ahead of [Sha'Carri Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha'Carri_Richardson) and [Adaejah Hodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaejah_Hodge) in 10.78 seconds (+0.0).[^cite_note-33] Choosing to only compete in the 200 metres at the [U.S. Outdoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) in a season without USA team trials, she won the title in 21.69 seconds ahead of [Cambrea Sturgis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrea_Sturgis) and [Camryn Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camryn_Dickson).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

On August 23, she won over 100 metres equalling her season’s best time of 10.78 seconds (0.6m/s) to win ahead of Alfred and Richardson in the Diamond League at the [2026 Kamila Skolimowska Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Kamila_Skolimowska_Memorial) in Silesia, Poland.[^cite_note-37] On August 27, she ran 10.70 seconds but was edged out in a photo-finish by Alfred in the 100 metres at the [Weltklasse Zürich](84064248.md), her first defeat over 100 metres since the 2024 Olympic final after a run of 11 consecutive victories at the distance.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] On September 12, Jefferson-Wooden won the [100 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Ultimate_Championship_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metres) ahead of Alfred at the inaugural [World Ultimate Championship](81879269.md) in Budapest, running a season's best 10.62 seconds.[^cite_note-40] The following day, she returned to complete the sprint double by winning the [200 metres final](84170480.md), with her time of 21.47 seconds making her the third-fastest woman on the all-time list.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Following the race, Jefferson-Wooden expressed shock at her time, stating, "I was thinking 21.6 low, 21.5. I skipped all over 21.5 and went 21.4. I mean I'll take it!"[^cite_note-44]

## Personal life

She married Rolan Wooden II in March 2025 in [Florida](18933066.md). The ceremony featured [Isaiah Likely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Likely) as best man and [Twanisha “Tee Tee” Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twanisha_Terry) as a bridesmaid, with fellow Olympians [Sha'Carri Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha'Carri_Richardson), [Kenny Bednarek](60913941.md) and [Christian Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Coleman) in attendance. The couple live in Florida.[^cite_note-48]
