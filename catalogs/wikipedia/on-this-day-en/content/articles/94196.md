**Northwood** is a town mostly within the [London Borough of Hillingdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hillingdon), located 14.5 miles (23 kilometres) northwest of [Charing Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charing_Cross). Northwood was part of the ancient parish of [Ruislip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip), [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex). The majority of the town is situated in the London Borough of Hillingdon but most of the northern part of the town ([Parts of Moor Park and Eastbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moor_Park_and_Eastbury)) is situated in [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire). Northwood shares post towns with parts of Hertfordshire. Since the [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex) side of the town being incorporated into [Greater London](45367389.md) in 1965, has been on the Greater London boundary with that county. It has also been within the [Metropolitan Police District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_District) [since 1840](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_District#1840_revision). The [Northwood Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_Headquarters) is on the Hertfordshire side.

The area consists of the elevated settlement of Northwood and Northwood Hills, both of which are served by stations on the [Metropolitan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_line) of the [London Underground](17839.md). At the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_United_Kingdom_census), the population of Northwood was 10,949, down from 11,068 in 2008,[^cite_note-northwood-2] while the population of Northwood Hills was 11,578, up from 10,833 in 2001.[^cite_note-3]

Northwood adjoins [Ruislip Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip_Woods) National Nature Reserve. The area was used for location filming of the Goods' and Leadbetters' houses and surrounding streets in the [BBC](19344654.md) TV sitcom *[The Good Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Life_\(1975_TV_series\))*, standing in for [Surbiton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surbiton).[^cite_note-4]

## History

### Toponymy

Northwood was first recorded in 1435 as *Northwode*, formed from the [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) 'north' and 'wode', meaning 'the northern wood', in relation to [Ruislip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip) and [Ruislip woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip_Woods).[^cite_note-5]

### Early developments

In the [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book) of 1086, the Northwood-embracing parish of [Ruislip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip) had immense woodland, sufficient to support one parish with 1,500 pigs per year, and a park for wild beasts (parcus ferarum).[^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6]

The hamlet of Northwood grew up along the north side of the [Rickmansworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickmansworth)-[Pinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinner) road which passes across the north-east of the parish. Apart from this road and internal networks in areas of scattered settlement to the east and west, Ruislip had only three ancient roads of any importance of which Ducks Hill Road was the only one in the Northwood hamlet. This followed the course of the modern road from its junction with the Rickmansworth road in the northwest corner of the parish. It then ran south through Ruislip village as Bury Street and continued through the open fields as Down Barns Road (now West End Road) to West End in [Northolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northolt).[^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6]

Northwood had a [manorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism) [grange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastic_grange) in 1248, which may have occupied the site of the later Northwood Grange. The monks of the [Bec Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bec_Abbey) who lived at [Manor Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_Farm,_Ruislip) in Ruislip in the 11th century owned this grange.[^cite_note-Bowlt_60-7] A few cottages at Northwood are mentioned in the 1565 manorial survey. Two hundred years later the shape of the hamlet, composed of a few farms and dwellings scattered along the Rickmansworth road, had altered little except for the addition of Holy Trinity church.[^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6] [Robert Cecil, 1st Earl of Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Cecil,_1st_Earl_of_Salisbury) had 568 acres (230 ha) of Ruislip cleared of forest.[^cite_note-Bowlt_60-7]

Northwood, however, elevated and separated from the rest of the parish by a belt of woodland, took until the 19th century to form a village — 350 acres (140 ha) in the manor of St. Catherine's were inclosed under the [Ruislip Inclosure Act 1769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip_Inclosure_Act_1769) ([9 Geo. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_Geo._3). c. *67* Pr.) privatizing land which lay west of Ducks Hill Road, including West Wood (now *Mad Bess Wood*) which was [common ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_land). A further 3,000 acres (1,200 ha) of Ruislip parish were inclosed by the [Ruislip Inclosure Act 1804](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip_Inclosure_Act_1804) ([44 Geo. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/44_Geo._3). c. *45* Pr.).[^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6] The character of the area in providing for Northwood and Northwood Hills to have the majority of open spaces as opposed to housing land was begun by transfers of open space land to the public as early as 1899.[^cite_note-8] The open nature of the district attracted three hospitals to move or establish in this part of the parish: [Mount Vernon Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon_Hospital), St. Vincent's Orthopaedic Hospital and Northwood, Pinner and District Hospital.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6]

### Urban development



Late 20th-century houses in Northwood

A land survey of Northwood conducted in 1565 by [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), the new lords of the manor of Ruislip, recorded ten houses and several farms.[^cite_note-Bowlt_60-7]

By 1881, the population of Northwood had reached 257, with 62 houses recorded[^cite_note-Bowlt_65-10] from 41 people in 1841.[^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6] David Carnegie owned the large [Eastbury Park Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbury_Park,_Northwood) in the north of the area in 1881. In 1887, the [Metropolitan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Railway) was extended from [Harrow-on-the-Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow-on-the-Hill) to [Rickmansworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickmansworth) and Carnegie sold his land to Frank Carew[^cite_note-11] for development for £59,422.[^cite_note-Bowlt_65-10] [Northwood station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_station) opened in August that year.[^cite_note-Bowlt_60-7] Carew stipulated the prices for the new housing he had built, with the cottages along the west side of the High Street priced at £120. He had hoped these would be owned by the staff of the larger houses. The High Street itself had been a track leading on from Rickmansworth Road to Gate Hill Farm.[^cite_note-Bowlt_69-12] The first shops opened in 1895 on the east side of the road, and included a hairdresser, butchers and a fishmongers.[^cite_note-Bowlt_70-13] Carew sold the majority of the estate to George Wieland in 1892.[^cite_note-Bowlt_60-7]

By 1902, the population had reached 2,500 in 500 houses and running 36 shops.[^cite_note-Bowlt_60-7] In 1904, the Emmanuel Church opened in Northwood Hills, designed by Sir [Frank Elgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Minshull_Elgood), a local architect. It had been built in 1895, originally to serve as a school.[^cite_note-14] Elgood later served as chairman of the [Ruislip-Northwood Urban District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip-Northwood_Urban_District).[^cite_note-Bowlt_70-13]



Northwood and Pinner Cottage Hospital, built in 1926

[Northwood and Pinner Cottage Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_and_Pinner_Cottage_Hospital) was built in 1926 as a memorial to the [First World War](4764461.md), using donations from the Ruislip Cottagers' Allotments Charity.[^cite_note-15]

Northwood is home to [Northwood Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_Headquarters), in the grounds of Eastbury Park, the estate purchased by David Carnegie in 1857. The [Royal Air Force](25679.md) took over the site in 1939 for the use of [RAF Coastal Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Coastal_Command) which made use of Eastbury house and also created a network of underground bunkers and operations blocks, at which time the house was used as the leading Officers' Mess, though was subsequently damaged by fire.[^cite_note-16] The RAF vacated the site in 1969, and it is now the location of the [British Armed Forces](31730.md) [Permanent Joint Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Joint_Headquarters) (PJHQ) for planning and controlling overseas [military operations](666301.md), together with the [NATO](21133.md) Maritime Command.[^cite_note-17]

A new community centre on the town's high street, replacing an older building, was officially opened by the local MP [Nick Hurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Hurd) in September 2012. The new building was named the Kate Fassnidge Community Centre after the Uxbridge landowner who donated some of her land to the borough, and replaced a derelict dining club that had originally been a Ritz cinema.[^cite_note-18]

Northwood was listed as the UK’s most expensive place to buy property because of its suburban setting and good transport links to Central London.[^cite_note-19]

## 1948 air disaster

Main article: [1948 Northwood mid-air collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Northwood_mid-air_collision)

On 4 July 1948, a [Scandinavian Airlines](162868.md) [Douglas DC-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-6) on a flight from [Amsterdam](844.md) to [RAF Northolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Northolt) collided with an RAF [Avro York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_York) coming from [Malta](19137.md) over Northwood. Both aircraft crashed, killing all 39 people on both aircraft.[^cite_note-20]

## Governance

Northwood was part of the ancient parish of [Ruislip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip) and became part of the [Ruislip-Northwood Urban District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip-Northwood_Urban_District) in 1904.[^cite_note-Vision_of_Britain-21] The urban district was abolished in 1965 and merged with others to become part of the [London Borough of Hillingdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hillingdon) in [Greater London](45367389.md). Northwood is part of the [Northwood ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_\(ward\)) for elections to [Hillingdon London Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillingdon_London_Borough_Council).[^cite_note-22]

The [Member of Parliament](3776360.md) (MP) for the constituency of [Ruislip, Northwood and Pinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip,_Northwood_and_Pinner_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) is currently [David Simmonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Simmonds), who was elected in the [December 2019 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_United_Kingdom_general_election) with 55.6% of the vote.[^cite_note-23] The current voting constituency was created from the former [Ruislip-Northwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip-Northwood_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and parts of the [Harrow West constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_West_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), for the 6 May 2010 [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_election).

## Geography

> The rural nature of Northwood, with extensive views over the rest of the parish, has attracted much expensive building, particularly in and around Copse Wood Way, along Green Lane and at the northern end of Ducks Hill Road.

— *A History of Middlesex*, 1971

[^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6]

Northwood is in both [Hillingdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hillingdon) and [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire). The town centre is situated in Greater London; however, the northern part of the town is situated in Hertfordshire. A triangular area of Northwood including the old High Street, Chester Road and Hallowell Road is a place of Local Architectural Special Interest, a restriction to protect the ornate Victorian houses made of high-quality brickwork.[^cite_note-24] Dotted across the area are 22 [listed buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building) (for their architecture).[^cite_note-25]



Northwood border of Hillingdon & Hertfordshire

Elevations range between 177 feet (54m) to 374 feet (114m) [AOD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_Ordnance_Datum), with many ridges and folds in the land creating an undulating terrain.[^cite_note-os-26]

## Localities

### Northwood Hills



Early 21st-century houses in Northwood Hills

**Northwood Hills** includes **Haste Hill** and is separated by [green buffers](489870.md) on almost all sides, though touches [Eastcote Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastcote_Village) to the south and had a population of 11,441 in 2008 according to the [Office for National Statistics](324029.md).[^cite_note-focus-29]

The land on which Northwood Hills, Haste Hill Golf Club and most of Northwood now stand was once the Great [Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_land) Wood. This covered 860 acres (350 ha) in the 16th century, which residents would use for grazing their livestock and collecting firewood. [Robert Cecil, 1st Earl of Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Cecil,_1st_Earl_of_Salisbury) achieved [inclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclosure) from Parliament and sold 568 acres (230 ha) of the wood in 1608 for £4000. The remaining woodland became Copse Wood, part of the [Ruislip Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip_Woods), a national nature reserve.[^cite_note-os-26][^cite_note-30]

Northwood Hills has intermixed in its area the only social housing estates beyond one street of the area;[^cite_note-focus-29] much of its private housing stock was built during the 1930s by the Belton Estates company, led by Harry Peachey,[^cite_note-Our_town-31] while Harry Neal was responsible for building the shopping parade in Joel Street. Its name was chosen in a competition by a woman from [North Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Harrow), as the land was split between Northwood, North Harrow and Ruislip parishes.[^cite_note-Around_Ruislip-32] The first houses were built in Potter Street.[^cite_note-Around_Ruislip-32]



The Northwood Hills public house

The Namaste Lounge Restaurant opposite the tube station was formerly the Northwood Hills public house, known previously as the Northwood Hills Hotel. It is cited as the venue where [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John) first performed professionally.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] A picture of the pub appears on one of his album covers.[^cite_note-35]

[Northwood secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_School_\(London\)) and sixth form is located in Potter Street (the former name of the school). The [Olympic](22576.md) boxer [Audley Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audley_Harrison) and *[Big Brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother_\(UK\))* contestant [Nikki Grahame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikki_Grahame) are [alumni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumni) of the school.[^cite_note-Our_town-31]

## Landmarks

Northwood Grange incorporates a 15th-century block with a crown-post roof, a cross-wing of the same date, and a long range of about 1600.[^cite_note-boltonkingwyld-6][^cite_note-36] This is now the **Hall School**, Northwood.

[Denville Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denville_Hall), formerly Northwood Hall, this has since 1925 been a retirement home for actors, actresses and members of other theatrical professions.[^cite_note-37]

The [London School of Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Theology) is an English interdenominational evangelical theological college.[^cite_note-38]

## Transport

[London Underground](17839.md) stations on the [Metropolitan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_line) that serve the area are [Northwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_tube_station), [Northwood Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_Hills_tube_station),[^cite_note-39] and [Moor Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moor_Park_tube_station).

The area is also served by [Transport for London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_London) [contracted bus routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses) [282](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses_route_282), [331](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses_route_331) and [H11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses_route_H11), connecting the area to [Ruislip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruislip), [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Harrow), [Northolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northolt), [Denham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denham,_Buckinghamshire), [Greenford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenford), [Uxbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uxbridge) and [Ealing Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ealing_Hospital).

The area is also served by Red Rose Travel routes 328, 346 connecting the area to [Watford](349407.md) via [South Oxhey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Oxhey) and [Carpenders Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenders_Park), and by Red Eagle Buses, which terminate at [Mount Vernon Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon_Hospital) with the R1 to [Maple Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Cross) and the R2 to [Chorleywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorleywood) – through [Harefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harefield) and [Rickmansworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickmansworth).
