***Yantar*** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Янтарь) is a special purpose [intelligence collection ship](2521578.md) built for the [Russian Navy](383258.md).[^cite_note-PM-2] The ship has been operated by [the Russian Navy's Main Directorate of Underwater Research (GUGI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Directorate_of_Deep-Sea_Research) since 2015 and is reportedly a [spy ship](2521578.md).[^cite_note-PM-2][^cite_note-BBC180103-3] The vessel's home port is [Severomorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severomorsk), where it is attached to the [Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet).[^cite_note-4] It is the [lead ship](855210.md) of the [Project 22010 class](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%BA%D0%B5%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5%20%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%BB%D1%8C%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5%20%D1%81%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%20%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%82%D0%B0%2022010), with two [sister ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_ship): *Almaz* (planned launch of 2019 but delayed), intended to serve with the [Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fleet_\(Russia\)),[^cite_note-7] and *[Vice-Admiral Burilichev](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B5-%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BB%20%D0%91%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D0%B2%20\(%D1%81%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%BE\))* ([laid down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laid_down) 2021).

## Design and construction

*Yantar* was designed by the CMDB [Almaz Design Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaz_Design_Bureau) in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md), and the hull was [laid down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keel_laying) on 8 July 2010.[^cite_note-GS-8] It was [launched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_ship_launching) in December 2012, and concluded its [sea trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_trial) in May 2015. The ship has a length of 108 metres (354 ft) and a [full displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_\(ship\)) of 5,736 tons. It uses [diesel-electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel-electric) propulsion for a top speed of approximately 15 [knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_\(unit\)) (28 km/h; 17 mph). It officially has a [complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship's_company) of 60. The ship was built at the [Yantar Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantar_Shipyard) in [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad).[^cite_note-GS-8]

## Roles

*Yantar* can act as a [mothership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_ship#Maritime_craft) to [mini-subs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midget_submarine). The [United States Navy](20518076.md) has stated that the submersibles are able to sever cables miles beneath the ocean's surface.[^cite_note-PM-2][^cite_note-NYT-9] The submersibles are reportedly capable of operating at depths of up to 6,000 metres (20,000 ft). The submersibles are reportedly the project 16810 *Rus*-class submersible[^cite_note-GS-8] and the project 16811 [*Konsul*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konsul-class_submersible) submersible.[^cite_note-BBC180103-3]

According to [Alexei Burilichev](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D0%B2,%20%D0%90%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%B5%D0%B9%20%D0%92%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87), head of the [Russian Defence Ministry's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Minister_of_Defence) [deepwater research department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Directorate_of_Deep-Sea_Research) from 2005 to 2020, *Yantar* is an oceanic research complex.[^cite_note-NYT-9]

## Activities

*Yantar* has been reported in position near [undersea telecommunications cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_communications_cable).[^cite_note-PM-2][^cite_note-BBC180103-3][^cite_note-NYT-9][^cite_note-10]

In September 2015, *Yantar* was spotted off the coast of [Guantánamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guant%C3%A1namo_Bay), [Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-PM-2][^cite_note-NYT-9]

In late October 2015, US intelligence sources reported that the vessel was inside Norwegian [territorial waters](496983.md) for the first time, heading north along the [Norwegian coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline_of_Norway). The [NJHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Joint_Headquarters) stated that they were aware of the vessel and were monitoring all traffic along the coast.[^cite_note-NRK151026-11]

Summer 2016, *Yantar* was anchored outside [Nuuk](53303.md), [Greenland](12118.md).[^cite_note-arktisknyt20171130-12][^cite_note-13]

In 2017, *Yantar* was active in the eastern [Mediterranean](19006.md), near an undersea cable linking [Israel](9282173.md) to [Cyprus](5593.md).[^cite_note-PM-2][^cite_note-BBC180103-3] It was also reportedly used to recover and/or destroy "secret equipment" from crashed [Su-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-33) and [MiG-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-29) aircraft.[^cite_note-BBC180103-3][^cite_note-IrishTimes210818-14]

On 23 November 2017, upon an order of Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), *Yantar* and the specialists of the Russian Navy's 328th expedition search and rescue unit were sent to Argentina's coast to search for the Argentine submarine [ARA *San Juan*](38834392.md) that went missing on 15 November 2017.[^cite_note-BBC180103-3][^cite_note-15]

In Summer 2018, she was deployed to Mediterranean off the Syrian coast.

In November 2019, *Yantar* visited [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md).[^cite_note-16]

In February 2020, *Yantar* was found near the [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) submarine cables by the [Brazilian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinha_do_Brasil). The crew evaded questions about their intentions and turned off the ship's identification systems.[^cite_note-17] In late March, *Yantar* was anchored off the [Baie de la Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baie_de_la_Seine), a few weeks before the *[Suffren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_submarine_Suffren)* first sea trial from Cherbourg.[^cite_note-18]

In August 2021, *Yantar* was spotted off the coast of [Ireland](147575.md), running parallel to [AEConnect-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEConnect) and the expected route of the Celtic Norse [submarine communications cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_communications_cable), as well as taking up a stationary position between them for most of the day.[^cite_note-IrishTimes210818-14][^cite_note-NavalNews210819-19][^cite_note-IBT210819-20][^cite_note-BBC210828-21] *Yantar* subsequently entered the [English Channel](9230.md) in mid-September.[^cite_note-UKDJ210913-22]

On 11 and 12 September 2023, while being followed by the [Norwegian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Coast_Guard) vessel [NoCGV *Barentshav*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NoCGV_Barentshav) in the [Fram Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fram_Strait), *Yantar* closely followed [*Kronprins Haakon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronprins_Haakon) for 16.5 hours.[^cite_note-23] The Russian ship was operating without [AIS](1825702.md) and would overtly copy all of the research vessel's stops and movements, at one point closing to a distance of 200–370 metres. While the [Norwegian Polar Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Polar_Institute) characterised the incident as "[harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harassment)", and the [Royal Norwegian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Navy) reportedly keeps a close eye on the Russian intelligence vessel, all relevant Norwegian authorities assessed that *Yantar* acted in accordance with [international maritime law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_maritime_law).[^cite_note-NRK231030-24]

In November 2024, she was escorted out of the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea) by the Irish offshore patrol vessel [LÉ *James Joyce*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%89_James_Joyce). Beneath this area of sea, south-west of the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man) and east of [Dublin](8504.md), lie cables carrying data for [Microsoft](19001.md) and [Google](1092923.md).[^cite_note-25]

In January 2025, *Yantar* resupplied in [Algiers](1644.md) and attended a high level meeting in Algeria, and then operated on the location of the sunk [MV *Ursa Major*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Ursa_Major). *Yantar* was also spotted in British waters, with the [Royal Navy](26061.md) deploying [HMS *Somerset*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Somerset_\(F82\)) and [HMS *Tyne*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Tyne_\(P281\)) to monitor the ship. Its location, close to Cornwall, is believed to possess off-shore infrastructure.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

On 19 November 2025, [British Defence Secretary](302332.md) [John Healey](434625.md), stated that the intelligence-gathering *Yantar* had entered the United Kingdom’s wider waters north of Scotland during the previous weeks,[^cite_note-\:2-28][^cite_note-\:3-29] and was allegedly engaging in espionage and the mapping of UK's [undersea cables](79454.md).[^cite_note-\:2-28][^cite_note-\:3-29] The vessel came within 45 miles of the British coast.[^cite_note-30] In response, the UK deployed a Royal Navy [frigate](11464.md) and RAF [P-8 Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft](731169.md) to monitor and track the ship, during which *Yantar* reportedly directed [Dazzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazzler_\(weapon\)) lasers at the pilots.[^cite_note-\:3-29][^cite_note-\:2-28][^cite_note-31] Healey described the Russian actions as "deeply dangerous", noting that this was the second visit of *Yantar* to UK waters in the same year, and warning that Britain was prepared to respond if the vessel attempted to travel further south.[^cite_note-\:2-28][^cite_note-\:3-29]

## Sister ships

### *Almaz*

A [Project 22010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_22010_research_vessel) [sister ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_ship) *Almaz* (01604) was laid down at the [Yantar Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantar_Shipyard) in [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad) on 9 June 2016.[^cite_note-BBC180103-3][^cite_note-TASS191028-32][^cite_note-NavyRecog2016-33] The [Russian state news](45060755.md) agency [TASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TASS) reported that after a technical launch in early October 2019, the vessel was intended to monitor [rocket launches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_launch) of the [Vostochny Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostochny_Cosmodrome) from the Pacific Ocean.[^cite_note-TASS191028-32][^cite_note-NavyRecog2016-33]

### *Burilichev*

On 5 February 2021, the third ship of the series was reportedly laid down in the [Vyborg Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg_Shipyard) under the name [*Vice-Admiral Burilichev*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B5-%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BB_%D0%91%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D0%B2_\(%D1%81%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%BE\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), to honour [Alexey Vitalyevich Burilichev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Vitalyevich_Burilichev?action=edit\&redlink=1), who headed [GUGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Directorate_of_Deep-Sea_Research) for 15 years and died in November 2020 due to [COVID-19](63030231.md).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]
