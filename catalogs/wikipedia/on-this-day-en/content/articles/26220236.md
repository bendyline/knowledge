**Airbus SE** ([/ˈɛərbʌs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*AIR-buss*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); French: [\[ɛʁbys\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c1/Airbus2.ogg/Airbus2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c1/Airbus2.ogg/Airbus2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; German: [\[ˈɛːɐ̯bʊs\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/07/Airbus3.ogg/Airbus3.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/07/Airbus3.ogg/Airbus3.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; Spanish: [\[ˈejɾβus\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a [European](9239.md)[^cite_note-7] [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) corporation. While the company's primary business is the design and manufacture of commercial aircraft, it also operates separate divisions for [Defence and Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Defence_and_Space) and [Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Helicopters). Airbus has long been the world's leading helicopter manufacturer and, in 2019, emerged as the world's largest manufacturer of airliners.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-About_Airbus-9]

Airbus originated from **Airbus Industrie GIE**, a [consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium) of European aerospace companies established in 1970 to produce [a wide-body aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300) to compete with American-built airliners. In 2000, the consortium's parent firms—[Aérospatiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale)-[Matra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra) of France, [DASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DASA) of Germany, and [CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA) of Spain—merged to form the **European Aeronautic Defence and Space Company** (**EADS**), which later acquired full ownership of Airbus Industrie. EADS [rebranded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebranded) as Airbus SE in 2015. Reflecting its multinational origins, the company operates offices and assembly plants in [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Spain](26667.md), and the [United Kingdom](31717.md), along with more recent additions in [Canada](5042916.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), the [United States](3434750.md), [Morocco](19291.md), and [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Airbus's headquarters are legally registered in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden), [Netherlands](21148.md), while day-to-day management is conducted from the company's main office in [Blagnac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blagnac), France.[^cite_note-12] The *SE* in its name denotes *[Societas Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societas_Europaea)*, a public company under European Union corporate law.[^cite_note-13] Airbus is led by CEO [Guillaume Faury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Faury) and is included in the [EURO STOXX 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EURO_STOXX_50) stock market index.[^cite_note-14] Since its inception in 2000, the company's shares have been listed on the [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Stock_Exchange), [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Stock_Exchange), and four Spanish stock exchanges, including the [Bolsa de Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsa_de_Madrid).[^cite_note-15]

## History

This section is an excerpt from [History of Airbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Airbus).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=History_of_Airbus\&action=edit)]

Airbus is the product of several historical forces: the desire of [European](9239.md) governments to create an [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) and [defence manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_manufacturing) large enough to compete with major [American](3434750.md) firms; the consolidation of the sector following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) and the ensuing "peace dividend", which raised concerns that companies could be acquired by non-European firms; and efforts to privatise state-owned companies.

The company traces its origins to the formation of the *[Airbus Industrie GIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Industrie_GIE)*, a consortium of European aerospace companies established in 1970 to produce a [wide-body aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300) to compete with American-built airliners. France's state-owned [Aérospatiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale) owned 37.9% of Airbus Industrie, West Germany's Deutsche Airbus also owned 37.9%, [British Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace) held 20%, and Spain's largely state-owned [CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)) owned 4.2%.

In 2000, the European Aeronautic Defence and Space Company (EADS) was established through the merger of Aérospatiale and [Matra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra) of France, [DASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DASA) (the successor to Deutsche Airbus) of Germany, and CASA. EADS held 100% of *Eurocopter* *S.A.*, established in 1992 and later renamed [Airbus Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Helicopters), along with 80% of Airbus Industrie GIE, which was quickly reorganised as *Airbus S.A.S.*, a simplified [joint-stock company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-stock_company). In 2006, EADS acquired the remaining 20% of Airbus S.A.S. from [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) (the successor of British Aerospace).[^cite_note-16] EADS was renamed *Airbus Group N.V.* in 2014 and *Airbus Group S.E.* in 2015.[^cite_note-History_of_Airbus_ft20140102-17][^cite_note-History_of_Airbus_Airbus_Group_SE-18][^cite_note-19]

Airbus S.A.S. and Airbus Group S.E. were merged in January 2017, and in April 2017 the combined entity adopted the name Airbus SE. Since then, Airbus SE has owned the commercial aircraft manufacturing operations while also serving as the parent company of the other two divisions, [Airbus Defence and Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Defence_and_Space) and Airbus Helicopters.

## Products

### Civilian



[Airbus A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300), the company's first airliner and the world's first [wide-body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-body), [twinjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinjet) aircraft

The Airbus product line started with the [A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A300) in 1972, the world's first [wide-body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-body), [twinjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinjet) aircraft. Initial sales were slow, leading to calls to end the Airbus program.[^cite_note-Champagne_and_Drought-20] However, the aircraft would greatly benefit from the 1976 introduction of the [ETOPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETOPS) 90 rule, which allowed twinjet aircraft to operate up to 90 minutes—an increase from 60 minutes—away from the [nearest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversion_airport). Under the new rule, the A300 was able to operate over the [North Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic), the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md), and the [Indian Ocean](14580.md),[^cite_note-AirbusETOPS3-21] <sup>: 14 </sup> making it a more efficient alternative to the [trijets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trijet) and [four-engined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-engined_jet_aircraft) aircraft offered by competitors, leading to renewed airline interest and a sharp increase in sales.[^cite_note-airbus3-22][^cite_note-\:1-23]

An Airbus A310 taking off from London Heathrow airport.

An [Airbus A310](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A310)

Airbus then developed the [A310](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A310) to meet demand for a shorter-body but longer-range version of the A300. The aircraft also introduced a [glass cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cockpit), which eliminated the need for a [flight engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_engineer), and a redesigned rear fuselage that used space more efficiently, creating additional capacity. The A310 was manufactured from 1981 to 1998. These design changes were later incorporated into the A300, resulting in the A300-600.&#x20;



[Airbus A320](164933.md), the company's best-selling airliner

Building on its success with the A300, Airbus developed the narrow-body [A320](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A320) aircraft that was launched in 1987, which became and remains a major commercial success. The A320 was the first commercial jet to use a digital [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire) control system. All Airbus models developed since have adopted cockpit systems similar to the A320, making it easier for crews to transition between aircraft types. The success of the A320 led Airbus to introduce a lengthened version, the [A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A321), in 1993, followed by the shorter [A319](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A319) in 1995 and the even shorter [A318](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A318) in 2002. In 2016, Airbus re-engineered the narrow-body family under a programme called the [A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A320neo) (*new engine option*).

An A340-600

[Airbus A340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340) at [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport)

Airbus also embarked on an effort to develop new wide-body aircraft derived from the cross-section of the A300, but with greater range and seating capacity and integrating technologies developed from the A320 programme. This effort led to the introduction of the four-engine [A340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A340) in 1991 and the twinjet [A330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A330) in 1992. At that time, Airbus wanted to offer a [four-engined jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-engined_jet_aircraft) to allow for [transpacific flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transpacific_flights?action=edit\&redlink=1). However, during the aircraft's development, new ETOPS rules extended twinjet operations to 120 minutes in 1986 and 180 minutes in 1989. Although these changes hurt sales of the A340, they greatly benefited the A330. Production of the A340 ended in 2011, while the A330 was re-engineered as the [A330neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A330neo) (*new engine option*) in 2018.



An [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) taking off from [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport)

In 2005, Airbus introduced the world's largest passenger airliner: the [A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A380) is a four-engine aircraft with two full-length passenger seating decks. Intended to challenge the dominance of the [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) in the [long-haul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-haul) market, the A380 was ultimately a money-losing venture for Airbus due to large development costs and limited sales arising from high operating costs, and production ended in December 2021.

The [A350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A350), a wide-body, twinjet aircraft, was introduced in 2015. The A350 was the first Airbus aircraft that was largely produced from [carbon-fiber-reinforced polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-fiber-reinforced_polymers). It is longer and wider than the fuselage used on the A300, A310, A330, and A340.

A second narrow-body jet was added to the product list in 2018 when Airbus gained control of the [Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Aviation) [CSeries programme](1567474.md#Development), and rebranded it as the [A220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A220). The jet offers five-abreast seating compared to the six-abreast seating on the A320.

In December 2024, it was reported that the total aircraft delivery in the year for Airbus has reached 643 units, with 84 planes delivered in November 2024 alone.[^cite_note-24] Subsequently, in January 2025, it was reported that Airbus had successfully delivered 766 aircraft to its customers out of 878 orders in the entire 2024, marking a 4% increase from the previous year.[^cite_note-25] In October 2025, the A320 overtook the [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) as the most delivered airliner.[^cite_note-26]

### Corporate jets

Main article: [Airbus Corporate Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Corporate_Jets)



VIP aircraft [Airbus A330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330) of [Qatar Amiri Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Amiri_Flight) taxiing at [Zagreb Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Airport)

Airbus Corporate Jets markets and modifies new aircraft for private and corporate customers. It has a model range that parallels the commercial aircraft offered by the company. Following the entry of the 737-based [Boeing Business Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Business_Jet), Airbus joined the business jet market with the A319 Corporate Jet in 1997. Although the term Airbus Corporate Jet was initially used only for the A319CJ, it is now often used for all models, including VIP widebodies. As of December 2008, 121 corporate and private jets are operating, and 164 aircraft have been ordered.[^cite_note-49]

The company also owns 10% of [Dassault Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Aviation), which builds the [Falcon family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon) of smaller business jets.

### Military

Main article: [Airbus Defence and Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Defence_and_Space)



The first [A400M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A400M) in Seville, 26 June 2008

&#x20;Airbus Defence and Space markets and either builds or modifies new aircraft for military use. Airbus became increasingly interested in developing and selling to the [military aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aviation) market in the late 1990s. It embarked on two main fields of development: [aerial refuelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_refuelling) with the [Airbus A310 MRTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A310_MRTT) (Multi-Role Tanker Transport) and later the [Airbus A330 MRTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330_MRTT), and [tactical airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_airlift) with the [Airbus A400M Atlas](371265.md). The company has also continued to market and assemble some military aircraft previously offered by the companies that formed Airbus, notably [CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)).

The A310 and A330-based MRTT aircraft are conversions of civilian airliners. The aircraft are called multi-role tanker transports because, in addition to their aerial refuelling capability, the aircraft can also be configured for troop transport, medevac, and cargo transportation.

The [A400M Atlas](371265.md) is a four-engine, [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop)-powered tactical transport aircraft. The A400M is sized between the American-made [C-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130) and the [C-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17) transports, and while it can carry heavier loads than the C-130, its turboprop engines allow it to retain the ability to use rough landing strips. The A400M was developed for European [NATO](21133.md) members Belgium, France, Germany, Luxembourg, Spain, Turkey, and the UK, as an alternative to relying on foreign aircraft. During development, the A400M programme faced delays and cost overruns;[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] with customer nations stepping in to offer additional subsidies.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-FTscrap-53] The first aircraft was delivered to the [French Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_Force) in 2013, and by 2023, more than 100 aircraft had been built.

The Defence and Space division also markets and assembles the [EADS CASA C295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA_C-295), a smaller tactical transport aircraft, that was designed and initially manufactured by the [Spanish](26667.md) aerospace company [CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)).

The company is also a 50% owner of the [ArianeGroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArianeGroup) joint venture which builds the [Ariane 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariane_5) space launch vehicle, a 46% owner of the [Eurofighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurofighter_GmbH) joint venture which builds the [Typhoon](167667.md) fighter jet, a 42.5% owner of the [Panavia Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Aircraft) joint venture which built the [Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado) fighter jet, a 37.5% owner of the [MBDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA) joint venture which builds missiles, and formerly a 10% owner of Dassault Aviation which builds the [Rafale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafale) fighter jet, and previously, the [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_2000) fighter.

### Helicopters

Main article: [Airbus Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Helicopters)

Airbus Helicopters builds and markets new [rotorcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotorcraft) for civilian and military use. The division was founded formed in 1992 as the Eurocopter Group, through the merger of the helicopter divisions of [Aérospatiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale) and [DASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DASA) (two of the founding companies of Airbus). Airbus Helicopters is the foremost player in the turbine helicopter industry in terms of both revenue and deliveries.

The division's civilian products include the single-engine [H125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H125) and [H130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H130), the light twin-engine [H135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H135) and [H145](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H145), the medium twin-engine [H155](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H155) and [H160](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H160), the super medium twin-engine [H175](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H175), and the heavy twin-engine [H215](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H215) and [H225](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H225).

Military products include the [Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_Tiger) attack helicopter, along with militarised versions of the H125, H135, H145, H160, H175, H215, and H225.

The company owns 62.5% of [NHIndustries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHIndustries) joint venture, which builds the [NH90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NH90) military utility helicopter.

Airbus, however, has a number of other plants in different European locations, reflecting its foundation as a consortium.

For aircraft assembled in Europe, aircraft parts often move between the different factories and the assembly lines via the use of the [Beluga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Beluga) and [BelugaXL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_BelugaXL), a fleet modified aircraft capable of carrying entire sections of fuselage. For aircraft assembled in China and the United States, the parts needed to build an aircraft meet in a single European location where they are loaded onto ships for the final journey to the assembly line.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

Airbus opened an assembly plant for the A320 family of aircraft in [Tianjin](50821.md), China in 2009.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-eads_20061026_pr-75] Airbus started constructing a \$350 million component manufacturing plant in [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin), China in July 2009.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] It was fully operational by early 2011,[^cite_note-78] the 30,000 square metre plant manufactures composite parts and assembles composite work-packages for the A350 XWB, A320 families and future Airbus programmes. Harbin Aircraft Industry Group Corporation, Hafei Aviation Industry Company Ltd, [AviChina Industry & Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AviChina_Industry_&_Technology) and other Chinese partners hold an 80% stake in the plant while Airbus controls the remaining 20%.[^cite_note-China_Daily-79] In 2022, the Tianjin plant finished upgrading works to allow for production of A321.[^cite_note-80] In 2023, the Tianjin final assembly plant started construction to be expanded with a second production line.[^cite_note-81]

North America plays a crucial role for Airbus, both in terms of aircraft sales and suppliers. Of the approximately 5,300 Airbus jetliners sold worldwide, 2,000 are ordered by North American customers. These orders span Airbus' entire product line, from the compact A318 to the massive A380, accommodating 107 to 565 passengers. Notably, US contractors contribute significantly, supporting around 120,000 jobs and generating an estimated \$5.5 billion in business. For instance, the A380 boasts 51% American content in terms of work share value.&#x20;

Plans for a [Mobile, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama) aircraft assembly plant were unveiled by Airbus CEO [Fabrice Brégier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrice_Br%C3%A9gier) from the Mobile Convention Centre on 2 July 2012. The plans include a \$600 million factory at the [Mobile Aeroplex at Brookley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Aeroplex_at_Brookley) for the assembly of the [A220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A220), A319, A320 and [A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A321) aircraft. It could employ up to 1,000 full-time workers when operational. Construction began on 8 April 2013, and became operable by 2015,[^cite_note-82] producing up to 50 aircraft per year by 2017.[^cite_note-apconfirm-83][^cite_note-bbcconfirm-84]

On 16 December 2024, it was reported that Airbus had leased 650,000 sq ft of office space in [Bengaluru](44275267.md)'s [Whitefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitefield,_Bengaluru) to build its Global Capacity Centre. The lease is for 10 years and valued at Rs 500 crore, securing the entire building in Titanium Tech Park. In this way, Airbus intends to strengthen its position in [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-85]

In October 2005 the British [Ministry of Defence](203793.md) warned European politicians to stop, as it saw it, interfering in the corporate governance of EADS. On 4 April 2006, DaimlerChrysler announced its intention to reduce its shareholding from 30% to 22.5%. The company placed a value on the stake at "approximately €2.0 billion."[^cite_note-DaimlerReduce-99] Lagardère was to reduce its holding by an identical amount. However, [Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caisse_des_D%C3%A9p%C3%B4ts_et_Consignations), a unit of the French government, acquired 2.25% of EADS. At issue, as a result, is the fact that the German and French shareholdings were now in imbalance.[^cite_note-iht_20060411-100]

On 30 August 2006, shortly after the stock price decline caused by the A380 delivery delays, more than 5% of EADS stock was reportedly purchased by the Russian state-owned [Vneshtorgbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTB_Bank),[^cite_note-vneshtorgbank_yahoo-101][^cite_note-102] bringing its share to nearly 6%. In December 2007, Vneshtorgbank sold EADS shares to another state-controlled bank, [Vnesheconombank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vnesheconombank_\(Russia\)). EADS shares were to be delivered by Vneshekonombank to the charter capital of JSC [United Aircraft Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Aircraft_Corporation) in 2008.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

On 3 October 2006, shortly after EADS admitted further delays in the Airbus 380 programme would cost the company 4.8 billion euros in lost earnings in 2010, EADS shares, traded on the Paris arm of Euronext, were suspended after they surpassed the 10% loss limit. Trading resumed later in the day with the one-day loss holding at 7%.

In 2007, [Dubai Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Holding) acquired 3.12% of EADS stock, making the Dubai buy-out fund one of the largest institutional shareholders.[^cite_note-105]

In 2008, EADS had arms sales equivalent to \$17.9 billion, which constituted 28% of total revenue.[^cite_note-SIPRItable-106]

In April 2013, Daimler sold its shares in EADS.[^cite_note-107]

As of 31 December 2024, 73.7% of Airbus Group stock is publicly traded on six European stock exchanges ([Euronext Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronext_Paris) in France, the [Frankfurt Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Stock_Exchange) in Germany, and the four regional stock exchanges [Bolsa de Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsa_de_Madrid), [Borsa de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borsa_de_Barcelona), Bolsa de Valencia and Bolsa de Bilbao in Spain). 0.6% of the shares are treasury shares owned by Airbus, while the remaining 25.7% are owned by a "Contractual Partnership". As of 31 December 2024, the partnership is owned by [SOGEPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOGEPA) (10.8%), GZBV (10.8%) and [SEPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPI) (4.1%).[^cite_note-108] SOGEPA is owned by the French State, GZBV is majority owned by the German state-owned investment and development bank [KfW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KfW), and SEPI is a Spanish state holding company.

In April 2020, Airbus announced that it had cut aircraft production by a third due to the COVID-19 outbreak. According to Guillaume Faury, the company was "bleeding cash at an unprecedented speed". The recession put its survival at stake and presented the need for deep job cuts throughout all Airbus departments. 3,000 workers in France were involved in government-assisted furlough schemes.[^cite_note-109]

Airbus was the first aerospace business to become [ISO 14001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_14001) certified, in January 2007; this is a broader certification covering the whole organisation, not just the aircraft it produces.[^cite_note-111]

### Co-development of biofuels

In association with [Honeywell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell) and [JetBlue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue), Airbus has developed an [aviation biofuel](17903761.md) to reduce pollution and dependence on fossil fuels, claiming that this has the potential to replace up to a third of the world's aviation fuel. Algae-based biofuel absorbs carbon dioxide during growth and does not compete with food production. This alternative may be commercially available by 2030 but algae and other vegetation-based fuels are in an early stage of development, and fuel-bearing algae have been expensive to develop.[^cite_note-112] Airbus offers delivery flights to airlines using a 10% biofuel blend in standard engines. The fuel does not cut carbon emissions but is free of sulphur emissions, which demonstrates that the fuel could be used in commercial flights in unmodified engines.[^cite_note-113]

On 22 July 2024, at the [2024 Farnborough International Airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_International_Airshow), Airbus and Airports Council International (ACI) World association signed a cooperation agreement to support the industry's efforts to reduce the environmental impact of aviation, including the adoption of sustainable aviation fuel (SAF).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

### Hydrogen powered aircraft concepts announced

In September 2020, Airbus unveiled three [liquid hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_hydrogen)-fueled "ZEROe" concept aircraft that it claims could become the first commercial zero-emission aircraft, entering service by 2035.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] The design includes an aircraft with six eight-bladed removable motors.[^cite_note-118]

At the Airbus Summit in March 2025, Airbus delivered updates on its development of the ZEROe hydrogen powered aircraft.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

## Controversies

### Government subsidies

[Boeing](18933266.md) has continually protested over "launch aid" and other forms of government aid to Airbus, while Airbus has argued that Boeing receives illegal subsidies through military and research contracts and tax breaks.[^cite_note-123]

In July 2004, former Boeing CEO [Harry Stonecipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Stonecipher) accused Airbus of abusing a 1992 bilateral EU-US agreement providing for disciplines for large civil aircraft support from governments. Airbus is given reimbursable launch investment (RLI), called "launch aid" by the US, from European governments, with the money being paid back with interest plus indefinite royalties, but only if the aircraft is a commercial success.[^cite_note-124] Airbus contends that this system is fully compliant with the 1992 agreement and [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTO) rules. The agreement allows up to 33% of the programme cost to be met through government loans, which are to be fully repaid within 17 years with interest and royalties. These loans are held at a minimum interest rate equal to the cost of government borrowing plus 0.25%, which would be below market rates available to Airbus without government support.[^cite_note-125] Airbus claims that since the signature of the EU-US agreement in 1992, it has repaid European governments more than US\$6.7 billion and that this is 40% more than it has received.

Airbus argues that the military contracts awarded to Boeing, the second largest US defence contractor, are in effect a form of subsidy, such as the controversy surrounding the Boeing [KC-767](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-767) military contracting arrangements. The significant US government support of technology development via [NASA](18426568.md) also provides significant support to Boeing, as do the large tax breaks offered to Boeing, which some people claim are in violation of the 1992 agreement and [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTO) rules. In its recent products such as the [787](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner), Boeing has also been offered direct financial support from local and state governments.[^cite_note-126]

In January 2005 the European Union and United States trade representatives, [Peter Mandelson](216970.md) and [Robert Zoellick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Zoellick) respectively, agreed to talks aimed at resolving the increasing tensions.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] These talks were not successful with the dispute becoming more acrimonious rather than approaching a settlement.[^cite_note-129]

WTO ruled in August 2010 and in May 2011 that Airbus had received improper government subsidies through loans with below market rates from several European countries.[^cite_note-130] In a separate ruling in February 2011, WTO found that Boeing had received local and federal aid in violation of WTO rules.[^cite_note-131]

### Cluster bomb allegation

In 2005 the [Government Pension Fund of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Pension_Fund_of_Norway) recommended the exclusion of several companies producing [cluster bombs](79745.md) or components. EADS and its sister company EADS Finance BV were among them, arguing that EADS manufactures "key components for cluster bombs". The criticism was centred around TDA, a joint venture between EADS and Thales S.A. TDA produced the [mortar](324499.md) ammunition PR Cargo, which can be considered cluster ammunition, however this definition has since been successfully battled by EADS. EADS and its subsidiaries are now regarded as fulfilling all the conditions of the [Ottawa Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Treaty). According to the new point of view, no product of EADS or its [subsidiaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiaries) falls into the category of [antipersonnel mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipersonnel_mine) as defined by the Ottawa Treaty ("[landmines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmine) under the Ottawa Treaty"). In April 2006, the fund declared that the basis for excluding EADS from investments related to the production of [cluster munitions](79745.md) is no longer valid, however its shareholding of MBDA means the fund still excludes EADS due to its indirect involvement in [nuclear weapons](21785.md) production.[^cite_note-132]

### Insider trading investigation

On 2 June 2006 co-CEO Noël Forgeard and Airbus CEO [Gustav Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Humbert) resigned following the controversy caused by the June 2006 announcement that deliveries of the A380 would be delayed by a further six months. Forgeard was one of a number of executives including [Jean-Paul Gut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Gut) who exercised [stock options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_option) in November 2005 and March 2006. He and twenty-one other executives are under investigation as to whether they knew about the delays in the Airbus A380 project which caused a 26% fall in EADS shares when publicised. The French government's actions were also under investigation; The state-owned bank Caisse des Dépots et Consignations (CDC) bought part of Lagardère's 7.5% stake in EADS in April 2006, allowing that latter to partially escape the June 2006 losses.[^cite_note-economist11oct2007-133]

### Investment in Chinese firm supplying Myanmar military

In September 2024, Airbus received negative press attention for its links with [Aviation Industry Corporation of China](20138455.md) (AVIC), a Chinese company that provides aircraft to the [Myanmar junta](66589972.md).[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] In a joint report, NGOs [Justice for Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_for_Myanmar) and Info Birmanie outlined how the supplied aircraft and weapons were used by the Myanmar military to commit war crimes in the [Myanmar civil war](13299430.md). The report called on Airbus to use its influence to pressure the Chinese firm to end its arms sales to the Myanmar junta.[^cite_note-136]

In response to the report, Airbus stated its collaboration focused exclusively on the civil domain, and that its conduct complied with all laws and regulations.[^cite_note-137] In June 2025, Justice for Myanmar reported that Airbus had completed its divestment from [AviChina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AviChina_Industry_&_Technology), AVIC's publicly listed subsidiary, by 1 April 2025.[^cite_note-138]

### Bribery allegations

#### South Africa

In 2003 [Tony Yengeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Yengeni), former [chief whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_whip) of South Africa's [African National Congress](2503.md), was convicted of fraud worth around US\$5 billion relating to an arms deal with South Africa, in which Airbus (formerly EADS) were major players.[^cite_note-139] It was claimed that Airbus had admitted that it had "rendered assistance" to around thirty senior officials, including defence force chief General [Siphiwe Nyanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siphiwe_Nyanda), to obtain luxury vehicles.[^cite_note-140] In March 2003, South Africa withdrew all charges of bribery against the former head of EADS South Africa,[^cite_note-141] and in September 2004, the prosecutor's office dismissed the bribery charges against Yengeni.

#### Saudi Arabia

In August 2012 the UK's [Serious Fraud Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serious_Fraud_Office_\(United_Kingdom\)) opened a criminal investigation into an EADS subsidiary, GPT Special Project Management Ltd, regarding bribery allegations made by GPT's former programme director, Ian Foxley. Foxley alleged that luxury cars were bought for senior Saudis, and that millions of [pounds sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pounds_sterling) were paid to mysterious [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands) companies, possibly to secure a £2 billion contract to renew the [Saudi Arabian National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian_National_Guard)'s military telecommunications network.[^cite_note-142] Foxley's allegations were supported by two other GPT employees.[^cite_note-143] The later agreement between Airbus and the SFO on 31 January 2020 excluded the settlement of this case.[^cite_note-SFO2mar2020-144]

#### British and French investigations

The French [National Financial Prosecutor's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Financial_Prosecutor's_Office) (PNF), the UK [Serious Fraud Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serious_Fraud_Office_\(United_Kingdom\)) (SFO) and the US Department of Justice (DoJ) had been jointly investigating irregularities in Airbus marketing practices since 2016, in particular the activities of agents Saudi Arabia, Kazakhstan, the Philippines, Indonesia and Austria,[^cite_note-146] but also China, the United Arab Emirates, South Korea, Japan, Taiwan, Kuwait, Turkey, Russia, Mexico, Brazil, Vietnam, India, Colombia and Nepal.[^cite_note-SFO2mar2020-144]

In July 2016, SFO opened a criminal investigation into "suspicions of fraud, bribes and corruption" after Airbus informed British authorities of a failure to disclose the role played by some intermediaries facilitating the sale of aircraft. Airbus was required to provide this information in order to benefit from export credits, which the British, French and German governments had suspended. In March 2017, the PNF subsequently opened a preliminary investigation into "suspicions of fraud and corruption in civil aviation activities" in cooperation with the SFO.[^cite_note-SFO2mar2020-144]

The allegations included that from 2012 onwards Airbus was responsible for recruiting and remunerating intermediaries to influence the award of civil and military contracts. Payments worth hundreds of millions of euros in alleged secret commissions were made and numerous sales including in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Philippines](23440.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), [Austria](26964606.md), [China](5405.md) and [Mauritius](19201.md) were under suspicion of bribery.[^cite_note-mediapart1aug2017-145][^cite_note-FT31jan2020-147]

The investigation focused on the Airbus, Strategy and Marketing Organization (SMO), the department responsible for negotiating sales contracts and which, La Tribune reported as having "a network and an incredible influence around the world". Directed successively by [Jean-Paul Gut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Gut) and [Marwan Lahoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_Lahoud), the SMO was dissolved in 2016 under the new executive director, Thomas Enders, as part of a "clean hands" operation.[^cite_note-148]

In 2014, in a case referred to as the Kazakhgate affair, a search at [Airbus Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Helicopters) by French authorities found emails confirming that Airbus had agreed in principle to pay €12 million in bribes to the Prime Minister of Kazakhstan to facilitate the sale of helicopters. Officers from the Central Anti-Corruption Office (OCLCIFF) then searched the home of [Marwan Lahoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_Lahoud) on 8 February 2016.[^cite_note-149] This revealed that two Turkish intermediaries had claimed payment of commissions due in connection with the sale of 160 aircraft to China valued at US\$10 billion. A message by Lahoud suggested that the commissions could reach US\$250 million. The SMO was to conceal these commissions as false invoices for a fictitious Caspian pipeline project.[^cite_note-150]

In January 2020, French, British and American courts validated three agreements between Airbus and the PNF,[^cite_note-151] the UK SFO,[^cite_note-SFO2mar2020-144][^cite_note-152] and the US DoJ.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] Airbus recognised the charges and agreed to pay fines of €2.1 billion in France, €984 million in the United Kingdom and €526 million in the United States. The penalties were the highest ever issued by the French and British bodies.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157]

These settlements close the prosecution of Airbus regarding the Kazakhstan case but not allegations of misconduct in Saudi Arabia, China and Turkey, which Airbus denies.[^cite_note-158] Airbus managers may still be pursued as private individuals.[^cite_note-159]

### Export Control breaches

In January 2020, the US State Department reached a settlement with Airbus to resolve alleged violation of the Arms Export Control Act (AECA) and International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR). The charges included providing false statements on authorization requests and failure to maintain records involving ITAR-controlled transactions; and the unauthorized re-export and retransfer of defense articles. Under the terms of the settlement, paid a civil penalty of \$10 million, with \$5 million of this amount suspended on the condition the funds were used for remedial compliance measures. Airbus was required to appoint an external Special Compliance Official to oversee the Agreement, and to conduct two external audits of its compliance program during the Agreement term as well as implement additional compliance measures.[^cite_note-160]

In July 2026, Airbus announced that it had paid a fine of £6.4 million to UK revenue and ‌customs authorities to settle a two-year-old investigation into export control violations. The investigation focused on anomalies over the cross-border transfer of parts for the A400M military airlifter that came to light during an audit in 2022.[^cite_note-161]

According to HMRC sources, the breaches included:[^cite_note-162]

- Failing to keep accurate records of transfers of controlled technology as per the conditions of three of their open general export licences (OGELs)

- Failing to keep registers in relation to OGELs

- Failing to keep accurate records contrary to the conditions of one of their OGELs

- A failure of licence conditions on a standard individual export licence (SIEL)
