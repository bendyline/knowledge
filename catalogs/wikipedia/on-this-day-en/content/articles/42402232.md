# Switzerland at the FIFA World Cup

For the women's team, see [Switzerland at the FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_at_the_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).



Switzerland vs Costa Rica match in [Nizhny Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Novgorod), Russia (2018)

The **[FIFA World Cup](11370.md)**, sometimes called the **Football World Cup**, but usually referred to simply as the **World Cup**, is an international [association football](10568.md) competition contested by the [men's national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_national_association_football_teams) of the members of Fédération Internationale de Football Association ([FIFA](11049.md)), the sport's global governing body. The championship has been awarded every four years since the first tournament in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), except in 1942 and 1946 due to [World War II](32927.md).

The tournament consists of two parts, the [qualification phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) and the final phase (officially called the *World Cup Finals*). The qualification phase, which currently take place over the three years preceding the Finals, is used to determine which teams qualify for the Finals. The current format of the Finals involves 48 teams competing for the title, at venues within the host nation (or nations) over a period of about a month. The World Cup final is the most widely viewed sporting event in the world, with an estimated 715.1 million people watching the [2006 tournament final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_final).

[Switzerland](681862.md) have appeared in the World Cup on twelve occasions. They have reached the quarter-finals four times, in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), [1954](60523.md), and [2026](36581929.md).

### [1966 FIFA World Cup](61629.md)

Despite securing back-to-back qualification for the [FIFA World Cups](11370.md), Switzerland's performance in the [1966 edition](61629.md) was far more abysmal. They lost all of their matches once more, including a 5-0 walloping by eventual runners-up [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in their opening game. To date, this remains as Switzerland's worst performance.

This was also Switzerland's last [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) campaign in 28 years, as their next appearance at the tournament would come in the [1994 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup).

### [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)



The Swiss line-up against China, just before World Cup 2006

The [World Cup 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Germany](11867.md) was the first World Cup for Switzerland since their participation at the [World Cup 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup). After finishing second behind [France](250197.md) in [qualifying group 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_UEFA_Group_4), they defeated [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) in the [play-off round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA_play-off\)) 2–0 and 4–2 to qualify for the main tournament.

In the group stage, they played again against [France](250197.md). The game played in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) ended in a goalless draw. After defeating [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo_national_football_team) 2–0 in [Dortmund](149349.md) and [South Korea](1018627.md) also 2–0 in [Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannover), they finished first in [group G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) and qualified for the knockout stage. In the second round of the tournament, they faced [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team) in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne). [The game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Switzerland_vs_Ukraine) had to be decided in a penalty shoot-out since no goal was scored after 120 minutes. Ukraine won the shoot-out 3–0. Switzerland was the only team in tournament not to have conceded a goal during regulation time in their matches. Switzerland's top scorer at the tournament was [Alexander Frei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Frei) with two goals. When Switzerland lost 3–0 on penalties, that was the first time that a team lost on penalties without scoring a single goal in the penalties. &#x20;

### 2014 FIFA World Cup

At the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), Switzerland were drawn in Group E along with [Ecuador](1016797.md), [France](250197.md), and [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team). They opened their campaign with a 2–1 victory over Ecuador in [Brasília](4752.md). However, in their next match, they suffered a 5–2 defeat to France. Despite the initial setback, a 3–0 victory in their final game against [Honduras](13394.md), courtesy of a hat-trick by [Xherdan Shaqiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xherdan_Shaqiri) sent them into the round of 16, where they faced the two-time world champions and eventual runners-up Argentina.

The game was goalless and nearly heading to penalties when [Ángel Di María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Di_Mar%C3%ADa) scored a 118th-minute extra time goal to send Argentina into the quarter-finals. Despite being eliminated in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage), it was Switzerland's best performance in eight years.[^cite_note-9]
