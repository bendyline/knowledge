The **Embassy of the United States in Islamabad** is the [diplomatic mission](8970.md) of the United States in [Pakistan](23235.md) at Ramna 5, [Diplomatic Enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_Enclave,_Islamabad), [Islamabad](51153.md), 44000. The embassy in [Islamabad](51153.md) is one of the largest [U.S. embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._embassies) in the world, in terms of personnel, and houses a [chancery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancery_\(diplomacy\)) and complex of office buildings.[^cite_note-\:1-1] The embassy complex also houses a contingent of military officials and intelligence personnel in addition to diplomatic and non-diplomatic staff.[^cite_note-\:1-1] [U.S. Department of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_State) also maintains Consulates in [Karachi](41909779.md), [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_General_of_the_United_States,_Lahore) and [Peshawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_General_of_the_United_States,_Peshawar).[^cite_note-maintains_Consulates-2]

The American diplomatic mission is headed by Ambassador [Donald Blome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Blome). The first Embassy of the United States to Pakistan was located in the city of [Karachi](17123.md), then the capital of Pakistan. The embassy was relocated to Islamabad after the city was made the new capital in 1960, and rebuilt after [being burned down in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_U.S._embassy_burning_in_Islamabad) by [Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami_Pakistan) agitators. In 2015, a new embassy complex was completed at a cost of \$736 million.[^cite_note-\:2-3]

## History

The first [U.S. embassy](6654039.md) in Pakistan was established on August 15, 1947, in [Karachi](17123.md), then-capital of Pakistan. When the capital was moved to [Islamabad](51153.md) in 1960, a new embassy was constructed there.

### 1979

Main article: [1979 U.S. embassy burning in Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_U.S._embassy_burning_in_Islamabad)

After being [burned to the ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_U.S._embassy_burning_in_Islamabad) by [Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami_Pakistan) extremists in 1979 following rumours of American involvement in the [Grand Mosque seizure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mosque_seizure), security at the rebuilt embassy was heightened.[^cite_note-4]

### Post 9/11

Security was again significantly increased in the wake of the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001.

### 2011

In 2011, the new complex began construction.[^cite_note-5]

### 2013

On 9 August 2013, the [U.S. State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_State_Department) evacuated most diplomats and all non-emergency staff from the [consulate in Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_General_of_the_United_States,_Lahore), and U.S. citizens were warned not to travel there due to terror concerns.[^cite_note-6]

### 2015

In August 2015, a new embassy complex was inaugurated in the Diplomatic Enclave which would house the embassy, replacing the previous building. The complex was built at a cost of \$736 million,[^cite_note-\:2-3] with \$85 million invested into the local economy by the purchase of construction supplies from Pakistani contractors and suppliers.[^cite_note-\:0-7] The embassy is reported to be the second-most expensive U.S. diplomatic mission, after the [U.S. embassy in Baghdad](4754335.md).[^cite_note-8] The embassy was designed to accommodate a staff of 2,500 people.[^cite_note-Davis-9]

### 2025

This section is an excerpt from [2025 Tehreek-e-Labbaik Pakistan protests](81307395.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2025_Tehreek-e-Labbaik_Pakistan_protests\&action=edit)]

On 9 October 2025, the Islamist political party [Tehreek-e-Labbaik Pakistan](55761171.md) (TLP) launched a protest march from [Lahore](125315.md) and [Faisalabad](401880.md) toward [Islamabad](51153.md) under the banner of the [Labbaik Ya Aqsa Million March](81307395.md). The movement called for a rally outside the US Embassy in Islamabad in solidarity with the [Palestinians](23267.md) amid the ongoing [Gaza genocide](75668427.md). The planned march triggered a major security response: authorities imposed [Section 144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_144) in [Rawalpindi](343524.md) to restrict public gatherings, suspended 3G/4G mobile data services in [Islamabad](51153.md) and Rawalpindi, and placed [shipping containers](22849124.md) alongside a heavy police deployment to seal off the [Red Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Zone_\(Islamabad\)) around the capital. Clashes erupted in Lahore when Punjab Police raided the TLP headquarters on Multan Road to arrest party leader [Saad Rizvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saad_Rizvi). Police used [tear gas](1202646.md) and live fire to disperse demonstrators. According to reports, at least 11 TLP workers were killed and dozens more were injured during the live fire and shelling by the police.[^cite_note-10] The government maintained that the TLP had not obtained the required permission for the march, attributing the unrest to internal party disputes.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-2025_Tehreek-e-Labbaik_Pakistan_protests_auto-12][^cite_note-2025_Tehreek-e-Labbaik_Pakistan_protests_auto3-13][^cite_note-2025_Tehreek-e-Labbaik_Pakistan_protests_auto1-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] [Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf](1651925.md) member [Sheikh Waqas Akram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Waqas_Akram) has compared the incident to [Model Town Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Town_Massacre) and has said the government could not carry out a transparent probe into the massacre, hence independent observers and human rights organisations must be included to ensure a fair investigation.[^cite_note-18]

### 2026

Main article: [Protests against the 2026 Iran war § Pakistan](82544763.md#Pakistan)

Nationwide protests erupted in Pakistan on 1 March 2026, primarily among [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam_in_Pakistan) communities, in response to the killing of [Ayatollah Ali Khamenei](385653.md) in joint [US-Israeli strikes](82533582.md).[^cite_note-\:3-19][^cite_note-\:4-20] Protestors accused the Pakistani government of siding with the US during the conflict. The protests in [Islamabad](51153.md) led authorities to block roads to the [Red Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Zone_\(Islamabad\)) and increase security around the US Embassy. Amid calls for demonstrations, at least 3 protesters were killed near the embassy.[^cite_note-\:3-19][^cite_note-\:4-20][^cite_note-21]
