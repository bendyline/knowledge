**T24** is a Turkish [online newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_newspaper).

## History

T24 was launched on 1 September 2009, initially staffed with former *Tempo24* employees.[^cite_note-Founders_(1)-1]

On 29 December 2012, T24 started their [YouTube](3524766.md) channel and uploaded their first video on 12 April 2013. In 2013, author and columnist [Hasan Cemal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Cemal) joined T24.[^cite_note-Hasan_Cemal_Hiring-5] In September 2013, T24 announced "T24 Okur Fonu" their [crowdfunding venture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdfunding) that planned to collect funds from its readers.[^cite_note-T24_Okur_Fonu_(1)-6][^cite_note-T24_Okur_Fonu_(2)-7] In September 2013, led by Hasan Cemal, Doğan Akın, [Yavuz Baydar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavuz_Baydar), Yasemin Çongar, Andrew Finkel, Hazal Özvarış and Murat Sabuncu, T24 staff established **Punto24**, a self-proclaimed "independent press platform".[^cite_note-Puntı24_Establishment-8]

On 14 January 2015, T24 published an entire instalment of [Charlie Hebdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hebdo), which included cartoons depicting [the Prophet Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_the_Prophet), following the 2015 [Charlie Hebdo shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hebdo_shooting).[^cite_note-Charlie_Hebdo_Cartoons-9] In February 2015, T24 started their online [literary criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_criticism) initiative **Kitap Kültür Kritik 24** (K24).[^cite_note-K24_Kickoff-10][^cite_note-K24_Nomination-11] Between 30 October and 13 November 2015, [Nazlı Ilıcak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazl%C4%B1_Il%C4%B1cak) published her columns as a "guest columnist" at T24.[^cite_note-2015_Ilicak_Guest_(1)-12][^cite_note-2015_Ilicak_Guest_(2)-13]

In March 2016, columnist and literary critic [Murat Belge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murat_Belge) joined T24.[^cite_note-Murat_Belge_Hiring-14]

Following his departure from [Doğan Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C4%9Fan_Media_Group) in September 2018, Mehmet Y. Yılmaz joined T24.[^cite_note-Yilmaz_Hiring_2018_(1)-15] On 19 October 2018, T24 announced the arrival of Yılmaz.[^cite_note-Yilmaz_Hiring_2018_(2)-16] On 23 October 2018, following the cancellation of her contract with [CNN Türk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_T%C3%BCrk), co-administrator Şirin Payzın joined T24.[^cite_note-Sirin_Payzın_Hiring-17] On 25 November 2018, a new periodical feature was introduced, "Şirin Payzın'la Ne Oluyor?", hosted by Şirin Payzın and first aired on T24's YouTube channel.[^cite_note-Feature_-_Ne_Oluyor-18] On 17 December 2018, T24 publicised their weekly electronic outlook **T24 Pazar**, which was to be online-published every Sunday.[^cite_note-T24_Pazar_Launch-19]

In 2020 chief editor Doğan Akın, one of the founders of the website, was prosecuted for "Support to a Terrorist Organization without having affiliation", as reporting the news based on tweets posted by [Fuat Avni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuat_Avni), an anonymous [Twitter](9988187.md) account, which caused number of detentions of alleged persons affiliated [FETÖ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FET%C3%96) (formerly referred as *"[Gülen Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BClen_Movement)"* until colloquially known [17-25 December Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_corruption_scandal_in_Turkey) taken place).[^cite_note-2020_FETO_Trial-20] Akın was acquitted on 2 July 2020.[^cite_note-2020_FETO_Case_Result_(1)-21][^cite_note-2020_FETO_Case_Result_(2)-22]

On 17 May 2020, T24 announced the arrival of senior journalist and politician and writer [Altan Öymen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altan_%C3%96ymen).[^cite_note-Oymen_Hiring_2020-23]

On September 30, 2026, a court in Istanbul ordered the blocking of the website and its social media accounts for allegedly spreading ["LGBTQ propaganda"](1601956.md).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Opposition leader [Özgür Özel](49050282.md) criticised the block as against freedom of information.[^cite_note-26]

## Controversies

In June 2013, T24 columnist [Alper Görmüş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alper_G%C3%B6rm%C3%BC%C5%9F), who formerly worked at [Nokta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokta), [Taraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraf) and [Yeni Aktüel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeni_Akt%C3%BCel), accused the [Gezi Park protestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Gezi_Park_protests) of being *"[pro-coup d'état](25914154.md)"* in his T24 column.[^cite_note-24062013_T24-27][^cite_note-2013_Gezi_Accusations-28]

Chief editor Doğan Akın, a former [Doğan Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C4%9Fan_Media_Group) employee before joining T24, was accused by Zekeriya Say in 2019, a columnist at [Yeni Akit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeni_Akit) newspaper, of a [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) due to his allegedly neutrality-periling relations with 3rd parties, alleged misuse of his connections in order to obtain [funding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funding) for the newspaper.[^cite_note-COI_Accusation-29]

Despite its claims of political neutrality, T24 was labelled as a [left-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) media outlet, by [Nihat Genç](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihat_Gen%C3%A7).[^cite_note-Liberal_labeling-30]
